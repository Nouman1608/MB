Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry (0620) is a foundation course in chemistry,
taking students from the particle model and atomic structure through
reactions, the Periodic Table and metals to environmental and organic
chemistry and laboratory analysis. The syllabus described here covers the
2026-2028 examination series. Candidates follow either the Core or the
Extended route and sit three papers: a multiple-choice paper worth 30%, a
theory paper worth 50%, and one practical component worth 20%.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus has 12 topics. Although they are not formally staged, they
fall naturally into four groups.

**Particles, structure and quantities (Topics 1 to 3).** States of
matter and diffusion; atoms, elements and compounds, including atomic
structure, isotopes and ionic, covalent and metallic bonding; and
stoichiometry, covering formulae, relative masses and the mole.

**Reactions and energy (Topics 4 to 7).** Electrochemistry (electrolysis
and hydrogen–oxygen fuel cells), chemical energetics (exothermic and
endothermic reactions), chemical reactions (rates, reversible reactions
and equilibrium, and redox), and acids, bases and salts.

**Elements and materials (Topics 8 to 10).** The Periodic Table (Group I,
Group VII, transition elements and noble gases), metals (properties,
uses, alloys, the reactivity series, corrosion and extraction), and
chemistry of the environment (water, fertilisers, and air quality and
climate).

**Organic chemistry and practical chemistry (Topics 11 and 12).** Organic
chemistry from naming and fuels through alkanes, alkenes, alcohols and
carboxylic acids to polymers, and experimental techniques and chemical
analysis, including experimental design, titrations, chromatography,
separation and identification of ions and gases.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Route | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paper 1 | Multiple Choice (Core) | Core | 45 minutes | 40 | 30% |
| Paper 3 | Theory (Core) | Core | 75 minutes | 80 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | Multiple Choice (Extended) | Extended | 45 minutes | 40 | 30% |
| Paper 4 | Theory (Extended) | Extended | 75 minutes | 80 | 50% |
| Paper 5 | Practical Test | Both | 75 minutes | 40 | 20% |
| Paper 6 | Alternative to Practical | Both | 60 minutes | 40 | 20% |

Core candidates take Papers 1 and 3, and Extended candidates take Papers
2 and 4. Whichever route you follow, you also sit one of Paper 5 or
Paper 6, not both; only the one you sit counts towards your total. The
qualification is component-based, with your three papers taken in the
same series. Your school chooses your route and practical option, so
confirm both early.

## How the parts fit together

Chemistry at this level is cumulative. The mole and formulae from
Stoichiometry are needed for calculations in electrolysis, energetics,
titrations and the preparation of salts. Bonding from Topic 2 explains
the properties of metals, the behaviour of Group I and Group VII
elements and the structure of polymers. Redox from Chemical reactions
reappears in electrolysis, the extraction of metals and corrosion. The
reactivity series links metals back to displacement and electrochemistry.

Topic 12, Experimental techniques and chemical analysis, is best treated
as a thread rather than a final chapter. Separation methods, titrations,
chromatography and tests for ions and gases all depend on content from
other topics, and they are exactly the skills the practical component
assesses, whether through Paper 5 at the bench or Paper 6 on paper.

Two skills run through every topic and every paper. The first is writing
chemistry precisely: correct formulae, balanced equations and accurate
use of terms such as element, compound, ion and isotope. The second is
working with numbers, from relative masses and moles to rates and
titration results. Because the multiple-choice paper samples the whole
syllabus and the theory paper carries half the qualification, weaknesses
in either skill show up quickly, so it is worth practising both a
little every week rather than in occasional long sessions.

## Planning your study

A sensible order follows the syllabus groups:

- **First term or two:** Topics 1 to 3, with regular practice of
  formulae, equations and simple mole calculations.
- **Rest of the first year:** Topics 4 to 7, linking rates, equilibrium,
  redox and acids to practical work.
- **Second year:** Topics 8 to 11, from the Periodic Table and metals to
  environmental and organic chemistry.
- **Throughout:** Topic 12 practical skills, built up through experiments
  attached to each topic, followed by whole-syllabus revision.

Extended candidates should check that their notes cover the extra depth
expected on their route. Bring in past-paper questions as each group of
topics is finished; leave timed full papers and paper-specific technique
for revision, when the exam preparation page is the right resource.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which papers do Extended candidates take in IGCSE Chemistry 0620?**
Extended candidates take Paper 2 (Multiple Choice, Extended), Paper 4 (Theory, Extended) and one practical component, either Paper 5 or Paper 6.

**Do I sit both the Practical Test and the Alternative to Practical?**
No. You sit one of Paper 5 (Practical Test, 75 minutes) or Paper 6 (Alternative to Practical, 60 minutes). Each is worth 20%, and only the one you sit counts.

**How many topics does Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry have?**
There are 12 topics, from States of matter to Experimental techniques and chemical analysis.

**How much is the theory paper worth?**
Paper 3 (Core) and Paper 4 (Extended) each last 75 minutes, carry 80 marks and are worth 50%.

**Which years does this 0620 syllabus cover?**
This syllabus covers the 2026-2028 examination series.
