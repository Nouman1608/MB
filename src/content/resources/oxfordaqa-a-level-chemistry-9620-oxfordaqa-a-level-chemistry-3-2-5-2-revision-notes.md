## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ligand substitution | Replacement of one or more ligands in a complex by others; oxidation state unchanged |
| Monodentate ligand | Forms one co-ordinate bond (H₂O, NH₃, Cl⁻) |
| Bidentate ligand | Forms two co-ordinate bonds (H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂, C₂O₄²⁻) |
| Multidentate ligand | Forms several co-ordinate bonds (EDTA⁴⁻ forms six) |
| Chelate effect | Bidentate and multidentate ligands replace monodentate ligands from complexes |
| Haem | An iron(II) complex with a multidentate ligand, found in haemoglobin |

## Substitutions to learn

| Reaction | Equation | Co-ordination number | Colour change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Co²⁺ + excess NH₃ | [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 6NH₃ → [Co(NH₃)₆]²⁺ + 6H₂O | 6 → 6 | pink → pale brown (straw) |
| Cu²⁺ + excess NH₃ | [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4NH₃ → [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ + 4H₂O | 6 → 6 (incomplete) | blue → deep blue |
| Co²⁺ + conc HCl | [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CoCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O | 6 → 4 | pink → blue |
| Cu²⁺ + conc HCl | [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CuCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O | 6 → 4 | blue → yellow (green mixture) |
| Fe³⁺ + conc HCl | [Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [FeCl₄]⁻ + 6H₂O | 6 → 4 | → yellow |

**Why NH₃ keeps 6:** NH₃ and H₂O are similar in size and both uncharged.

**Why Cl⁻ gives 4:** Cl⁻ is larger, so only four fit; shape changes from octahedral to tetrahedral; the charge on the complex changes because each Cl⁻ is 1−.

**Reversibility:** adding water to [CoCl₄]²⁻ shifts the equilibrium back and the pink colour returns.

## Chelating ligands

| Ligand | Donor atoms | Bonds formed | Number needed for co-ordination number 6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂ | 2 N | 2 | 3 |
| C₂O₄²⁻ | 2 O | 2 | 3 |
| EDTA⁴⁻ | 6 | 6 | 1 |

Example equations:
- [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + EDTA⁴⁻ → [Cu(EDTA)]²⁻ + 6H₂O (2 → 7 particles)
- [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 3H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂ → [Co(H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂)₃]²⁺ + 6H₂O (4 → 7 particles)

## The chelate effect explained

1. **ΔH ≈ 0**: the same number of similar co-ordinate bonds are broken and formed.
2. **ΔS > 0**: the number of particles increases.
3. **ΔG = ΔH − TΔS < 0**: the reaction is feasible, driven by entropy.

## Haem and carbon monoxide

- O₂ forms a co-ordinate bond to Fe(II) in haemoglobin, allowing oxygen transport in the blood.
- CO is toxic because it replaces the O₂ co-ordinately bonded to Fe(II), forming a more stable complex, so less oxygen is carried.

## Charge bookkeeping

Overall charge of a complex = oxidation state of metal + sum of ligand charges.

| Complex | Metal | Ligand charges | Overall charge |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ | +2 | 0 | 2+ |
| [CuCl₄]²⁻ | +2 | 4 × (−1) | 2− |
| [FeCl₄]⁻ | +3 | 4 × (−1) | 1− |
| [Fe(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻ | +3 | 3 × (−2) | 3− |
| [Cu(EDTA)]²⁻ | +2 | −4 | 2− |
| [Co(H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂)₃]²⁺ | +2 | 0 | 2+ |

Use the same sum in reverse to find an unknown oxidation state.

## Particle counts for the chelate effect

| Substitution | Particles before | Particles after | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6H₂O replaced by 3 bidentate ligands | 4 | 7 | +3 |
| 6H₂O replaced by 1 EDTA⁴⁻ | 2 | 7 | +5 |
| 6NH₃ replaced by 3 bidentate ligands | 4 | 7 | +3 |

## Exam traps

- Writing [Cu(NH₃)₆]²⁺ instead of [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺.
- Forgetting that the charge changes when Cl⁻ replaces H₂O.
- Saying a substitution changes the oxidation state.
- Using bond strength alone to explain the chelate effect.
- Missing the six released water molecules when counting particles.
- Calling EDTA⁴⁻ bidentate.
- Saying CO "blocks the blood" rather than replacing O₂ on Fe(II).

## Quick self-test

1. Name the three monodentate ligands in this specification point.
2. Why does NH₃ replace H₂O without a change in co-ordination number?
3. Write the equation for [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ with excess ammonia.
4. What colour change accompanies the reaction in Question 3?
5. Why does the co-ordination number fall when Cl⁻ replaces H₂O?
6. Write the equation for [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ with concentrated hydrochloric acid and give the colour change.
7. State the oxidation state of iron in [FeCl₄]⁻.
8. What is a bidentate ligand? Give the two examples named in the specification.
9. State the co-ordination number of copper in [Cu(EDTA)]²⁻.
10. How many particles are on each side when three H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂ replace six H₂O around Co²⁺?
11. Explain why carbon monoxide is toxic.
12. Explain the chelate effect in terms of enthalpy and entropy.

### Answers

1. H₂O, NH₃ and Cl⁻.
2. They are similar in size and both uncharged.
3. **[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4NH₃ → [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ + 4H₂O**
4. **Blue to deep blue.**
5. Cl⁻ is larger than H₂O, so only four fit around the metal ion.
6. **[Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CoCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O**; pink to blue.
7. x + 4(−1) = −1, so **+3**.
8. A ligand that forms two co-ordinate bonds to the metal ion; ethane-1,2-diamine (H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂) and ethanedioate (C₂O₄²⁻).
9. **6**.
10. **4** on the left and **7** on the right.
11. CO replaces the oxygen co-ordinately bonded to Fe(II) in haemoglobin, so the blood carries less oxygen.
12. ΔH is close to zero because similar bonds are broken and made; the number of particles increases, so ΔS is positive; therefore ΔG is negative and the substitution is feasible.
