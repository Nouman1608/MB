## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Financial intermediary | Institution linking savers with borrowers |
| Retail bank | Serves households and small firms |
| Commercial bank | Lends to and takes deposits from businesses |
| Investment bank | Issues and trades securities, advises large clients |
| Pension fund | Invests contributions to pay retirement income |
| Insurance fund | Pools risk and pays claims from premiums |
| Hedge fund | Pursues high returns, often with leverage |
| Bond | Tradable IOU with a fixed coupon and face value |
| Inter-bank lending | Short-term loans between banks |
| Collateral (security) | Asset a lender can seize if the loan is not repaid |
| Real economy | Output, jobs and incomes, as opposed to financial assets |

## Institutions and their macro effects

| Institution | Main macro channel | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| Retail bank | Lending to households raises consumption | Household debt, mortgage defaults |
| Commercial bank | Lending to firms funds investment | Credit crunch when lending stops |
| Investment bank | Raises long-term capital, moves asset prices | Speculation, trading losses |
| Pension fund | Long-term saving into bonds and shares | Asset falls cut future pensions |
| Insurance fund | Pools risk so firms will take it on | Correlated events |
| Hedge fund | Trades that help set prices | Leverage and forced selling |

Chain for any institution: **action** (lends, invests, trades) → **change in a variable** (credit, asset prices, wealth, confidence) → **component of AD or AS** (C, I, LRAS) → **outcome** (output, employment, prices).

## Markets

**Stock market.** Firms raise equity; prices drive the wealth effect. Reminder: households hold 600m cu of shares and prices fall 15%. Wealth loss = 600 × 0.15 = 90m cu. If 5% of lost wealth is cut from spending, consumption falls 90 × 0.05 = 4.5m cu.

**Bond market.** Current yield = coupon ÷ price. Price and yield move in opposite directions.

```
Coupon 3 on face value 100
Price  96 → 3/96  = 3.125%
Price 120 → 3/120 = 2.5%
```

Method for a bond price after market rates change: price = coupon ÷ new yield. A bond with coupon 2 when the market yield is 2.5% is worth 2 ÷ 0.025 = 80, a fall of 20% from 100.

**Foreign exchange market.** A depreciation makes exports cheaper abroad and imports dearer at home; an appreciation does the opposite. Capital inflows tend to push the rate up and outflows to push it down. The effect on the trade balance depends on elasticities.

## Inter-bank lending

- Banks with surplus funds lend short-term to banks with a gap after daily payments.
- It supports liquidity and gives a benchmark rate linked to the central bank's policy rate.
- If trust breaks down, short-term funding stops and credit to the real economy falls.

Method for interest on an inter-bank loan: amount × annual rate × days ÷ 365.

```
80m cu for 7 days at 3.5% = 80,000,000 × 0.035 × 7/365 = 53,698.63 cu
```

## Forms of borrowing: quick comparison

| Form | Secured? | Cost | Best use | Main drawback |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mortgage | Yes, on property | Low | Buying a home | Lose home if unpaid; negative equity |
| Unsecured loan | No | Medium | One-off purchase | Higher rate than secured |
| Overdraft | No | Low for days, high for months | Short gap in cash | Easy to rely on |
| Credit card | No | High on unpaid balances | Short-term convenience | Compounding debt |
| Pay day loan | No | Very high | Emergency small sum | Rollover and debt spiral |

**Loan-to-value (LTV).** LTV = loan ÷ property value. House 250,000 cu, loan 200,000 cu: LTV = 80%. A 25% fall in price gives a value of 187,500 cu, and equity of 187,500 − 200,000 = −12,500 cu.

**Mortgage payment sensitivity.** Interest-only loan of 120,000 cu. At 5.4%, monthly interest = 120,000 × 0.054 ÷ 12 = 540 cu. At 7.4% it is 740 cu, an extra 200 cu a month. This is a fall in disposable income for the household, so consumption falls.

**Unsecured loan.** 2,000 cu at 10% over 24 months (reducing balance) costs 92.29 cu a month. Total repaid is 2,214.96 cu, so interest is 214.96 cu.

**Overdraft cost.** 1,000 cu for 90 days at 20% a year: 1,000 × 0.20 × 90 ÷ 365 = 49.32 cu.

**Credit card.** Balance 1,500 cu at 1.8% a month: interest = 27 cu a month. Nominal annual rate = 21.6%. Effective rate = (1.018)¹² − 1 = 23.9%.

**Pay day loan.** Borrow 200 cu, repay 230 cu after 14 days. Cost = 30 ÷ 200 = 15%. Simple annualised = 15 × 365 ÷ 14 = 391%. Compare it with other borrowing only after annualising.

## Risk, security and interest rates

- **Risk premium.** Higher chance of default → higher rate to compensate.
- **Security.** Collateral lets the lender recover part of the loan, so the rate can be lower.
- **Other factors.** Term, loan size, borrower history, and the base rate set by the central bank.

Method for the required rate when default probability is p, no recovery, and target return t:

```
(1 − p)(1 + r) = 1 + t
Example: t = 4%, p = 10%  →  1 + r = 1.04/0.90  →  r = 15.56%
```

With recovery share s of the loan: (1 − p)(1 + r) + p × s = 1 + t.

Ranking of typical rates, lowest to highest: government bonds, secured mortgage, unsecured loan, credit card, pay day loan.

## Other worked reminders

**Bank margin.** A bank lends 100,000 cu at 6.5% and pays depositors 1.5%. Interest received is 6,500 cu, interest paid is 1,500 cu, margin is 5,000 cu, which covers costs, bad debts and profit.

**Insurance.** Claim chance 0.5%, claim size 40,000 cu. Expected claim = 200 cu. Premium 260 cu gives a 60 cu loading (30% above expected claims).

**Leverage.** Capital 50, assets 400. Leverage = 8. A 10% fall in assets loses 40, which is 80% of capital. The fall that wipes out capital is 50 ÷ 400 = 12.5%.

**Price-earnings.** Share price 60, earnings per share 4: ratio = 15. Dividend 1.5 on price 60: dividend yield = 2.5%.

## Evaluating the financial sector in the real economy

For: transfers saving to investment, spreads risk, smooths consumption, funds growth, lets firms hedge.

Against: credit booms and busts, asset bubbles, contagion, household over-indebtedness, resources used in trading rather than production.

Method for a long evaluation answer: (1) define the term, (2) give two or three effects, each as a chain to AD, AS or jobs, (3) give limits or counter-arguments, (4) weigh them using the data, (5) conclude.

Judgement factors: size of leverage, quality of lending, trust between institutions, time period (long-run gains, short-run instability) and the context in the data. End with a verdict, and state what would change it.

## Must-know distinctions

- Retail (households) versus commercial (businesses) versus investment (securities).
- Pension (long-term saving) versus insurance (risk pooling) versus hedge fund (high-return, leveraged).
- Coupon (fixed payment) versus yield (return at the current price).
- Secured (collateral) versus unsecured borrowing.
- Nominal rate versus effective annual rate.
- Overdraft (flexible within a limit) versus loan (fixed sum).

## Quick self-test

1. A bond has a coupon of 5 and a face value of 100. Its price is 125. Find the current yield.
2. Bond prices fall. Do yields rise or fall?
3. House 300,000 cu, deposit 45,000 cu. Find the LTV.
4. Borrow 500 cu overnight between banks at 4.0% a year. What is the interest for one night to the nearest 0.01 cu?
5. Pay day loan: borrow 100 cu, repay 118 cu. State the cost over the loan period.
6. Name one reason a mortgage has a lower rate than a credit card.
7. State the effect on export prices abroad of a depreciation.
8. Capital 20, assets 160. What fall in asset values wipes out capital?
9. Give one macro risk of a hedge fund using leverage.
10. A lender wants a 6% return, default chance 5%, no recovery. Find the required rate to one decimal place.

### Answers

1. 5 ÷ 125 = 4.0%.
2. Yields rise.
3. Loan 255,000; 255,000 ÷ 300,000 = 85%.
4. 500 × 0.04 ÷ 365 = 0.05 cu.
5. 18 ÷ 100 = 18%.
6. It is secured on property, so the lender can recover its loss.
7. Export prices fall abroad.
8. 20 ÷ 160 = 12.5%.
9. Forced selling can push asset prices down and pass losses to lenders.
10. 1.06 ÷ 0.95 = 1.1158, so 11.6%.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying bond prices rise when market interest rates rise.
- Comparing pay day and bank rates without putting them on the same time basis.
- Describing what a fund does but not linking it to AD, AS or employment.
- Treating all banks as one type.
- Leaving out security when explaining why rates differ.
- Confusing coupon with yield.
- Writing percentage points as percent.
- Evaluating with a list rather than weighing which point matters most in the context.
- Forgetting the exchange rate direction: a depreciation lowers the foreign price of exports.
