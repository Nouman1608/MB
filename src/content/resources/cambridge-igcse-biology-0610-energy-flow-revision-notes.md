These notes condense sub-topic **19.1 Energy flow** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Both outcomes are **Core**; there is no Extended content in this sub-topic.

For full explanations and worked examples, read the [energy flow study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-energy-flow/) first, then test yourself with the [energy flow practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-energy-flow-practice/). The [organisms and their environment revision notes](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-organisms-and-their-environment-revision-notes/) cover the rest of topic 19.

## Core recall: the one-sentence statement

> The **Sun** is the **principal source of energy input** to biological systems.

"Principal" means main. Almost every food chain starts with a producer that captures light energy.

## Core recall: the flow in order

1. **Light energy** from the Sun is absorbed by **chlorophyll** in producers.
2. **Photosynthesis** transfers it to **chemical energy** in glucose; glucose is used to make starch, cellulose, proteins and fats.
3. **Feeding** passes chemical energy from producer to herbivore to carnivore.
4. **Respiration** in every organism releases energy from glucose for life processes.
5. Energy is eventually **transferred to the environment as heat**.
6. Energy in **dead material and waste** passes to **decomposers**, which respire and release it as heat.

## Core recall: why not all energy passes on

| Where energy goes | Why it does not reach the next organism |
|---|---|
| Parts not eaten (roots, bones, wood) | never enters the consumer |
| Faeces | food not digested or absorbed; goes to decomposers |
| Urine (urea) | excreted; goes to decomposers |
| Respiration | energy released and transferred to surroundings as heat |
| Movement, keeping warm | ends up as heat in the surroundings |
| Body tissue | **only this** is available to the next consumer |

## Core recall: uses of energy from respiration

Muscle contraction, protein synthesis, cell division, active transport, growth, nerve impulses, maintaining a constant body temperature. All of it is eventually transferred to the surroundings as heat.

## Energy vs nutrients

| | Energy | Nutrients (carbon, nitrogen) |
|---|---|---|
| Path | one-way flow | cycle |
| Enters as | light | carbon dioxide, ions |
| Leaves as | heat | returned to air and soil by decomposers |
| Reused by producers? | no | yes |

**Rule:** energy flows; matter cycles.

## Reading an energy-flow diagram

- Arrows into an organism show chemical energy in food eaten.
- Arrows out show faeces, urine, heat from respiration and energy stored in new tissue.
- **Energy stored = energy in food − (faeces + urine + heat)**.
- **Percentage stored = energy stored ÷ energy in food × 100**.
- Only the energy stored in tissue can pass to the next consumer.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Principal energy source | The Sun: the main source of energy input to biological systems |
| Light energy | Energy from the Sun absorbed by chlorophyll |
| Chemical energy | Energy stored in organic compounds such as glucose, starch, fats and proteins |
| Photosynthesis | Process that transfers light energy into chemical energy in glucose |
| Respiration | Process that releases energy from glucose in every living cell |
| Producer | Organism that makes its own organic nutrients, usually using energy from sunlight |
| Decomposer | Organism that gets its energy from dead or waste organic material |
| Heat (thermal energy) | The form in which energy is eventually transferred to the environment |

## Exam traps

- **Energy "made" or "used up".** Always say energy is **transferred**.
- **Respiration "makes energy".** It **releases** energy.
- **Plants get energy from soil.** Soil gives water and mineral ions, not energy.
- **"Light energy passes to the rabbit."** Energy passes along a chain as **chemical** energy in food.
- **Energy recycled by decomposers.** Decomposers recycle nutrients; energy leaves as heat.
- **Only animals lose heat.** Producers and decomposers respire and transfer heat too.
- **Calculations.** Subtract every listed loss; give kJ as the unit.

## Command-word reminders

- **State**: the Sun is the principal source of energy input to biological systems.
- **Describe**: light → chemical (photosynthesis) → feeding → respiration → heat.
- **Explain** a loss: say where the energy goes and why the next organism cannot use it.

## Self-test

1. State the principal source of energy input to biological systems.
2. Name the pigment that absorbs light energy in producers.
3. In what form is energy stored in a potato tuber?
4. Name the process that transfers light energy into chemical energy.
5. In what form does energy pass from a caterpillar to the bird that eats it?
6. Name the process in all organisms that releases energy from glucose.
7. In what form is energy finally transferred to the environment?
8. Give two reasons why a sheep does not gain all the energy in the grass it eats.
9. What happens to the energy in the faeces of a sheep?
10. Why is a compost heap warm?
11. Explain why energy cannot be recycled by producers.
12. A goat eats food containing 20 000 kJ. It loses 11 000 kJ in faeces and urine and 7 500 kJ as heat. Calculate the energy stored in new tissue.

### Answers

1. The Sun.
2. Chlorophyll.
3. Chemical energy (in starch).
4. Photosynthesis.
5. Chemical energy in food.
6. Respiration.
7. Heat (thermal energy).
8. Any two: some grass parts are not eaten; some food is not digested and is lost in faeces; energy is released in respiration and transferred as heat; energy is lost in urine.
9. Decomposers feed on the faeces, respire and transfer the energy to the environment as heat.
10. Decomposers in it are respiring and releasing heat.
11. Energy leaves organisms as heat, and producers can only capture light energy, not heat.
12. 20 000 − 11 000 − 7 500 = 1 500 kJ.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out the change from light to chemical energy.
- Not saying how energy finally leaves (heat to the environment).
- Using "energy is produced" in a description.
- Forgetting decomposers when describing what happens to energy in dead material.
- Omitting the unit in a calculation.
