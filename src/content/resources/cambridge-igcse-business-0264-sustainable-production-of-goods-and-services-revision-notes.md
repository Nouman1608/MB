Condensed notes for section 4.3 (Sustainable production of goods and services) of the Cambridge IGCSE Business 0264 syllabus for examination in 2027, 2028 and 2029, content statement 4.3.1. A three-option hotel decision and a packaging calculation are worked through in the [sustainable production study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-business-0264-sustainable-production-of-goods-and-services/). Test yourself with the [sustainable production practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-business-0264-sustainable-production-of-goods-and-services-practice/). The wider [technology and production notes](/resources/igcse-business-0264-technology-and-production-of-goods-and-services-and-related-topics-revision-notes/) cover section 4.2 too.

All examples are invented. Money is in dollars.

## The six methods named in the syllabus

| Method | What it means | Factory example | Service example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Using renewable energy | Power from sources that are naturally replaced, such as sun and wind | Wind turbine powering a mill | Solar panels on a school roof |
| Using fewer resources / less waste | Producing the same output with less material, energy or water | Precise cutting of metal sheets | Digital receipts instead of paper |
| Reusing | Using an item again as it is | Returnable crates for deliveries | Washable cups in a café |
| Recycling | Turning waste into material for new products | Scrap plastic melted into new parts | Office paper collected for pulping |
| Environmentally friendly products | Products that do less harm in making, use or disposal | Long-lasting, repairable kettles | A car-sharing service instead of private hire cars |
| Environmentally friendly packaging | Less packaging, or packaging that is recyclable, compostable or made from recycled material | Card trays instead of plastic | Paper bags for takeaway food |

**Memory hook:** energy, less, reuse, recycle, product, packaging.

### Distinctions

- **Reusing vs recycling:** reusing keeps the item whole; recycling breaks it down to make something new.
- **Fewer resources vs recycling:** fewer resources prevents waste; recycling deals with waste already made.
- **Section 4.3 vs 6.3:** 4.3 is about how production is carried out; 6.3 is about environmental effects of business and legal controls.

## Advantages of becoming more sustainable

- Lower running costs once energy, water and material use falls.
- Better reputation; may attract customers who care about the environment.
- May be able to charge a higher price for a sustainable product.
- Easier to meet current and future laws; less risk of fines.
- Employees may be more motivated and the firm may attract better recruits.
- Less dependence on fossil fuel prices once renewable energy is installed.

## Disadvantages

- High start-up costs for equipment, redesign and training.
- Sustainable materials and packaging can cost more per unit, permanently.
- Savings may take years to cover the cost.
- Higher costs may mean higher prices and lost price-sensitive customers.
- Results depend on others: guests reusing towels, customers returning containers.
- Claims must be true; exaggerated claims damage reputation.

## Two calculations you may meet

**Extra cost of a change** = extra cost per unit x number of units.

Worked reminder: paper straws cost $0.02 more than plastic; a café uses 30,000 a year.

```
0.02 x 30,000 = $600 extra a year
```

**Time for savings to cover a cost** = cost of the change / yearly saving.

Worked reminder: insulation costs $12,000 and saves $3,000 a year on heating.

```
12,000 / 3,000 = 4 years
```

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sustainable production | Producing goods and services in ways that do not use up resources or damage the environment for the future |
| Renewable energy | Energy from sources that are naturally replaced, such as solar, wind and water |
| Waste | Materials or energy used in production that do not end up in the product sold |
| Reusing | Using an item again in its existing form |
| Recycling | Processing waste into material for new products |
| Environmentally friendly product | A product designed to do less harm to the environment in its making, use or disposal |
| Environmentally friendly packaging | Packaging that is reduced, recyclable, compostable or made from recycled material |

## Exam traps

- Do not say "recycle" when the case describes refilling or returning containers: that is reusing.
- Services can be sustainable too; give service examples for service businesses.
- Sustainability does not always save money. Say which costs rise and which fall.
- A recommendation needs a reason from the case: budget, type of customer, how much energy the business uses.
- Do not drift into pressure groups or laws unless the question asks about them.

## Quick self-test

1. List the six ways a business can be more sustainable named in the syllabus.
2. What is the difference between reusing and recycling?
3. Give an example of a hairdresser using fewer resources.
4. Give one example of environmentally friendly packaging.
5. State two advantages to a business of becoming more sustainable.
6. State two disadvantages to a business of becoming more sustainable.
7. A bakery switches to compostable boxes costing $0.05 more each, on 40,000 boxes a year. Calculate the extra yearly cost.
8. A wind turbine costs $90,000 and saves $15,000 a year. How many years until the savings equal the cost?
9. Why might a business that mainly sells on low prices be cautious about switching to dearer sustainable materials?
10. Give one reason why reusing usually saves more resources than recycling.
11. Which method is a printer using when it changes to solar power?
12. Give one reason why the yearly saving from solar panels might be lower than expected.

### Answers

1. Using renewable energy; using fewer resources / less waste; reusing; recycling; developing environmentally friendly products; using environmentally friendly packaging.
2. Reusing uses an item again in its existing form; recycling breaks it down to make new material or products.
3. Any one: using less water per wash with efficient taps; buying products in large refillable containers; switching off dryers when not in use.
4. Any one: recycled card instead of plastic; compostable wrappers; less packaging per item.
5. Any two: lower running costs; better reputation; may attract customers or charge more; easier to meet laws; more motivated staff.
6. Any two: high start-up costs; higher material costs; slow savings; may need to raise prices; depends on customers taking part.
7. 0.05 x 40,000 = $2,000 a year.
8. 90,000 / 15,000 = 6 years.
9. Higher costs may force a price rise, and its customers buy mainly on price, so they may switch to cheaper rivals.
10. The item does not need energy and processing to turn it back into material.
11. Using renewable energy.
12. Any one: less sunshine than expected; electricity prices fall; panels need repair or maintenance.
