## Section A

**1.** Which chamber of the heart has the thickest muscular wall?

A: left atrium

B: left ventricle

C: right atrium

D: right ventricle

**[1]**

**2.** What is the function of the coronary arteries?

A: to carry blood from the heart to the lungs

B: to carry blood from the lungs to the heart

C: to supply the heart muscle with oxygen and glucose

D: to stop blood flowing backwards

**[1]**

**3.** Name the vessel that carries blood away from the left ventricle. **[1]**

**4.** State **two** ways in which the activity of the heart may be monitored. **[2]**

**5.** State **two** risk factors for coronary heart disease. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A diagram of the heart shows the left ventricle (L), the right ventricle (R), the septum (S), an atrioventricular valve (V) and a semilunar valve (W).

**(a)** State the function of the septum. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the wall of L is thicker than the wall of R. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why the walls of the atria are thinner than the walls of the ventricles. **[2]**

**7.** Describe the action of the valves during one heartbeat, starting when the ventricles contract. **[4]**

**8.** A student counted a pulse of 22 beats in 15 seconds at rest and 41 beats in 15 seconds straight after exercise.

**(a)** Calculate the heart rate at rest, in beats per minute. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in heart rate after exercise. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the heart rate increases during exercise. **[3]**

**(d)** State **one** way to make the pulse measurement more reliable. **[1]**

**9.** Describe how coronary heart disease is caused. **[4]**

**10.** Discuss how diet and exercise can reduce the risk of coronary heart disease. **[4]**

**11.** At rest, a heart beats 72 times per minute and pumps 70 cm cubed of blood with each beat. Calculate the volume of blood pumped per minute, in dm cubed. **[2]**

**12.** A heart rate monitor recorded the heart rate of a student: 70 beats per minute at rest, 150 during hard exercise and 85 five minutes after stopping.

**(a)** Suggest why the heart rate was still above the resting rate five minutes after stopping. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the change in heart rate from rest to exercise as a ratio, in the form exercise : rest, simplified to the nearest whole numbers. **[1]**

**13.** Explain why a blocked coronary artery can cause a heart attack, even though the heart chambers are full of blood. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** C [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Aorta [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Any two of: electrocardiogram (ECG); pulse rate; listening to the sounds of the valves closing [1 each]. **[2]**

**5.** Any two of: diet; sedentary lifestyle; stress; smoking; genetic predisposition; age; gender [1 each]. **[2]**

**6.**

**(a)** It separates the left and right sides of the heart, so oxygenated and deoxygenated blood do not mix [1].

**(b)** The left ventricle pumps blood to the whole body [1], which is a long way, so it must produce a high pressure [1]. The right ventricle pumps blood only to the lungs, where a lower pressure is enough and a high pressure could damage the capillaries [1].

**(c)** The atria only push blood a short distance into the ventricles [1], so they do not need to produce as much force or pressure [1]. **[6]**

**7.** The ventricles contract and the pressure rises above that in the atria, so the atrioventricular valves shut [1]. This stops blood flowing back into the atria [1]. The pressure rises above that in the arteries, so the semilunar valves open and blood leaves the heart [1]. When the ventricles relax, the semilunar valves shut, which stops blood flowing back into the ventricles [1]. **[4]**

**8.**

**(a)** 22 x 4 [1] = 88 beats per minute [1].

**(b)** After exercise: 41 x 4 = 164 beats per minute [1]. Percentage increase = (164 - 88) divided by 88 x 100 = 86 per cent [1].

**(c)** The muscles respire faster [1] and need more oxygen and glucose, and make more carbon dioxide [1]. A faster heart rate delivers these more quickly and removes the carbon dioxide [1].

**(d)** Repeat the measurement and take a mean (or count for a longer time) [1]. **[8]**

**9.** The coronary arteries become narrowed by fatty deposits [1]. Blood clots may block the artery [1]. Part of the heart muscle then receives too little oxygen and glucose [1] and cannot respire aerobically, so it may be damaged or die (a heart attack) [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Any four of: a diet low in saturated fat reduces the build-up of fatty deposits [1]; less salt lowers blood pressure [1]; more fibre, fruit and vegetables help [1]; regular exercise strengthens the heart [1]; exercise lowers blood pressure and helps control body mass [1]; they reduce the risk but cannot remove it, because age, gender and genes also matter [1]. **[4]**

**11.** 72 x 70 = 5040 cm cubed per minute [1] = 5.04 dm cubed per minute [1]. **[2]**

**12.**

**(a)** The muscles had made lactic acid during the exercise [1], and the heart kept beating faster to carry it to the liver and to supply oxygen to break it down [1].

**(b)** 150 : 70 = 15 : 7 [1]. **[4]**

**13.** The heart muscle gets its oxygen and glucose from the blood in the coronary arteries, not from the blood in the chambers [1]. If the artery is blocked, part of the muscle gets too little oxygen and glucose [1], so it cannot respire aerobically and may stop working or die [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying that the right ventricle is thinner because it is smaller.
- Describing valves as closed by muscles rather than by pressure differences.
- Forgetting to multiply by 4 when a pulse is counted for 15 seconds.
- Saying that the coronary arteries are inside the heart chambers.
- Stating that diet and exercise remove the risk of coronary heart disease completely.
