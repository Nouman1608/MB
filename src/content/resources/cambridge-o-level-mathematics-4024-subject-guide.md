Cambridge O Level Mathematics (4024) is a single-tier secondary mathematics course in which every candidate studies the same content and sits the same two papers. Paper 1 is a non-calculator paper and Paper 2 a calculator paper; each lasts 2 hours, is marked out of 100 and is worth 50% of the qualification. A calculator is not allowed in Paper 1, while a scientific calculator is required for Paper 2. Both papers are externally assessed, and the course is examined in June and November. This overview explains how the content is organised, how the two papers relate and how to plan a route through the syllabus.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus groups the content into nine areas:

1. Number
2. Algebra and graphs
3. Coordinate geometry
4. Geometry
5. Mensuration
6. Trigonometry
7. Transformations and vectors
8. Probability
9. Statistics

Three of these areas are worth looking at more closely because of how much they contain.

**Algebra and graphs** runs from an introduction to algebra and algebraic manipulation, through algebraic fractions, indices, equations and inequalities, to sequences, proportion, graphs in practical situations, graphs of functions, sketching curves and functions. It is the backbone of the course and supports almost every other area.

**Statistics** covers classifying and interpreting statistical data, averages and measures of spread, statistical charts and diagrams, scatter diagrams, cumulative frequency diagrams and histograms. Much of it is about reading and interpreting information as well as calculating.

**Transformations and vectors** brings together transformations, vectors in two dimensions, the magnitude of a vector and vector geometry, linking shape work with algebraic thinking.

The remaining areas, Number, Coordinate geometry, Geometry, Mensuration, Trigonometry and Probability, cover the core skills of calculation, shape, space and chance that you will use throughout.

## How it's assessed

The assessment is linear and not tiered. Both papers are taken together at the end of the course.

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting | Calculator |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Non-calculator | 2 hours | 100 | 50% | Not allowed |
| Paper 2 | Calculator | 2 hours | 100 | 50% | Scientific calculator required |

Because there is only one tier, every topic in the syllabus is relevant to every candidate. The papers are equal in length, marks and weighting, so the non-calculator paper matters exactly as much as the calculator one. The syllabus covers the 2025-2027 examination window, with June and November series, and was first examined in 2025.

## How the parts fit together

The two papers test the same syllabus in different conditions, and that shapes how each topic should be learned.

On Paper 1, you need reliable written and mental methods: working with fractions and decimals, simplifying expressions, solving equations exactly and estimating sensibly. On Paper 2, a scientific calculator handles the arithmetic, so questions can involve less convenient numbers and longer multi-step problems, and accuracy in using the calculator becomes the priority. Any content area can appear on either paper, so each needs to be practised both ways.

The content areas also connect. Number skills feed into every other area. Algebra links to coordinate geometry through the equations of lines and graphs of functions, and to geometry and trigonometry whenever an unknown length or angle has to be found. Mensuration draws on geometry, and statistics and probability both depend on confident handling of fractions, decimals and percentages. Vectors combine coordinate ideas with geometric reasoning.

Seeing these links helps with the longer, multi-part questions that ask you to move from one area to another within a single problem.

## Planning your study

A sensible order begins with Number and the opening topics of Algebra and graphs, since nearly everything else depends on them. Coordinate geometry, Geometry and Mensuration can follow, then Trigonometry once similarity and right-angled triangles are secure. Transformations and vectors, Probability and Statistics fit naturally in the later part of the course.

In general terms:

- **First stage:** number skills, algebraic manipulation, indices, equations and inequalities.
- **Second stage:** sequences, graphs and functions, coordinate geometry, geometry and mensuration.
- **Third stage:** trigonometry, transformations and vectors, probability and statistics.
- **Final stage:** mixed revision across all nine areas.

Two habits make this plan work better. First, keep a running list of methods you find difficult, with a worked example of each, and return to it every few weeks rather than only before the examinations. Second, when you finish a topic, try a few questions that combine it with something learned earlier, such as an algebra problem set in a geometry diagram, so that the links between areas become familiar long before you meet them in a full paper.

Keep earlier skills fresh with short mixed practice, and always include some non-calculator work, even when you are studying topics that feel calculator-heavy. Start exam-style questions as each topic is completed and move to full past papers, under timed conditions, in the final months. For paper-by-paper advice, use the dedicated exam preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge O Level Mathematics 4024?**
There are two: Paper 1 Non-calculator and Paper 2 Calculator. Each lasts 2 hours, is marked out of 100 and is worth 50%.

**Can I use a calculator in Paper 1?**
No. A calculator is not allowed in Paper 1. A scientific calculator is required for Paper 2.

**Is O Level Mathematics 4024 tiered?**
No. The syllabus is not tiered, so all candidates sit the same two papers.

**When is 4024 examined?**
The 2025-2027 syllabus is examined in the June and November series.

**What content areas does 4024 cover?**
Number, Algebra and graphs, Coordinate geometry, Geometry, Mensuration, Trigonometry, Transformations and vectors, Probability, and Statistics.
