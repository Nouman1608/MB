## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State which business document is described in each case.

**(a)** Sent by a buyer to tell the seller which invoices a payment covers. **[1]**
**(b)** Sent by a seller to reduce the amount a buyer owes after goods are returned. **[1]**
**(c)** Signed by a person who has been given a small sum of cash for a business expense. **[1]**
**(d)** Sent by a seller at the end of a month, showing the balance a customer owes. **[1]**

**2.** Explain two purposes of business documents. **[4]**

**3.** Distinguish between trade discount and cash discount. **[2]**

**4.** Goods with a list price of 1,240.00 are sold with 15% trade discount. Calculate the amount due on the invoice. **[2]**

**5.** State the book of original entry in which each document is first recorded by the business named.

**(a)** A credit note received by a buyer. **[1]**
**(b)** A sales invoice issued by a seller. **[1]**
**(c)** A petty cash voucher. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** On 8 June 2027 Larchmont Ceramics sold goods on credit to Pebble Bay Gifts, order PB-77: 60 mugs at 4.50 each and 24 vases at 11.25 each. Trade discount is 10%. Terms are 2.5% cash discount if paid within 10 days, otherwise net 30 days.

Prepare the sales invoice, number 3315. **[6]**

**7.** On 12 June Pebble Bay Gifts returned 5 mugs that were chipped. It also found that the vases had been charged at 11.25 although 10.75 had been agreed.

**(a)** Calculate the amount of the credit note. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why the credit note must use the same trade discount as the invoice. **[2]**

**8.** Using your answers to questions 6 and 7, Pebble Bay Gifts pays on 17 June, within the discount period.

**(a)** Calculate the cash discount and the amount paid. **[3]**
**(b)** Prepare the remittance advice. **[3]**

**9.** On 1 July 2027 Raymond Stores owed Delmar Wholesale 2,140.00. During July: 5 July invoice 880 for 1,365.00; 9 July credit note 52 for 145.00; 14 July Raymond paid 2,086.50 by bank, taking cash discount of 53.50 on the opening balance; 26 July invoice 897 for 720.00.

Prepare Delmar Wholesale's statement of account for Raymond Stores at 31 July 2027, with a running balance. **[6]**

**10.** Raymond Stores' own account for Delmar Wholesale in its payables ledger shows a balance of 1,220.00 at 31 July, while the statement shows a different figure.

**(a)** Calculate the difference. **[1]**
**(b)** Suggest one reason for the difference that matches the amount. **[2]**

**11.** A purchases clerk receives an invoice for 2,800.00 from a supplier. The purchase order was for 200 units at 14.00 each with 20% trade discount.

**(a)** Calculate the correct amount due. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the overcharge and explain what the clerk should do before recording the invoice. **[3]**

**12.** Explain the purpose of each document and state who keeps a copy.

**(a)** Petty cash voucher. **[3]**
**(b)** Cash receipt. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) Remittance advice [1]. (b) Credit note [1]. (c) Petty cash voucher [1]. (d) Statement of account [1]. **[4]**

**2.** Any two, each with explanation. Evidence [1]: proves a transaction took place, its date and amount, which helps settle disputes [1]. Source for records [1]: entries in the books of original entry are made from documents, so nothing is recorded without support [1]. Also acceptable: checking before payment; authorisation of spending; audit trail through numbering. **[4]**

**3.** Trade discount is a reduction from list price given to trade customers and deducted on the invoice [1]. Cash discount is a reduction for paying within a stated period and is only taken when payment is made [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Trade discount = 1,240.00 × 15% = 186.00 [1]. Amount due = 1,240.00 − 186.00 = **1,054.00** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** (a) Purchases returns book [1]. (b) Sales day book [1]. (c) Petty cash book [1]. **[3]**

**6.**

```
SALES INVOICE No. 3315                    8 June 2027
Larchmont Ceramics to Pebble Bay Gifts    Order ref: PB-77
60 mugs     @ 4.50                         270.00
24 vases    @ 11.25                        270.00
Total at list price                        540.00
Less trade discount 10%                     54.00
Amount due                                 486.00
Terms: 2.5% cash discount if paid within 10 days; net 30 days
```

Heading, number, date and both names [1]. Mugs 270.00 [1]. Vases 270.00 [1]. Total 540.00 [1]. Trade discount 54.00 and amount due 486.00 [1]. Terms stated, cash discount not deducted [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Deducting the 2.5% on the invoice loses the last mark and gives the wrong amount due.

**7. (a)** Returns: 5 × 4.50 = 22.50 [1]. Overcharge: 24 × (11.25 − 10.75) = 24 × 0.50 = 12.00 [1]. Total before discount 34.50, less 10% = 3.45 [1]. Credit note **31.05** [1].
**(b)** The goods were invoiced net of 10% trade discount [1], so crediting at list price would reduce the debt by more than was charged, overstating the credit [1]. **[6]**

**8. (a)** Amount owed = 486.00 − 31.05 = 454.95 [1]. Cash discount = 454.95 × 2.5% = **11.37** (to the nearest cent) [1]. Amount paid = 454.95 − 11.37 = **443.58** [1].
**(b)**

```
REMITTANCE ADVICE                        17 June 2027
Pebble Bay Gifts to Larchmont Ceramics
Invoice 3315                               486.00
Less credit note                            31.05
                                           454.95
Less cash discount 2.5%                     11.37
Payment enclosed                           443.58
```

Heading, date and names [1]. Invoice and credit note listed [1]. Discount and payment shown [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Cash discount is calculated on 454.95, the amount owed after the credit note, not on 486.00.

**9.**

```
STATEMENT OF ACCOUNT    Delmar Wholesale to Raymond Stores
31 July 2027                       Debit      Credit     Balance
1 Jul   Balance b/d                                     2,140.00
5 Jul   Invoice 880             1,365.00               3,505.00
9 Jul   Credit note 52                      145.00     3,360.00
14 Jul  Bank                              2,086.50
        Discount                             53.50     1,220.00
26 Jul  Invoice 897               720.00               1,940.00
Amount now due                                         1,940.00
```

Heading, date and names [1]. Opening balance [1]. Invoices on the debit side [1]. Credit note on the credit side [1]. Payment and discount on the credit side [1]. Running balance and amount due 1,940.00 [1]. **[6]**

**10. (a)** 1,940.00 − 1,220.00 = **720.00** [1].
**(b)** Invoice 897 for 720.00, dated 26 July, has not yet been received or recorded by Raymond Stores [1], so it is in transit and will be entered when it arrives, after which the balances agree [1]. **[3]**

**11. (a)** 200 × 14.00 = 2,800.00; less 20% (560.00) [1] = **2,240.00** [1].
**(b)** Overcharge = 2,800.00 − 2,240.00 = **560.00** [1]; the supplier has left out the trade discount [1]. The clerk should not record or pay the invoice as it stands, but should contact the supplier and ask for a credit note for 560.00 (or a corrected invoice) [1]. **[5]**

**12. (a)** It authorises and records one small payment from petty cash [1], provides evidence for the entry in the petty cash book [1], and is kept by the petty cashier with the receipt attached [1].
**(b)** It proves that cash was received [1], giving the payer evidence in case of dispute [1]; the payer keeps the original and the receiver keeps a copy as evidence of the receipt [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Deducting cash discount on the invoice.
- Ignoring trade discount on a credit note.
- Calculating cash discount on the invoice total when a credit note has been issued.
- Putting payments or credit notes in the debit column of a statement.
- Leaving out document numbers, dates or the names of both parties.
- Explaining a purpose with a single word such as "evidence" and no reason.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is cash discount ever shown on an invoice?**
The terms are printed on the invoice, but the discount is not deducted from the amount due. It is taken only if the customer pays within the stated period.

**Why might a statement of account not agree with the buyer's records?**
Items can be in transit, such as an invoice sent but not yet received, or one side may have made an error. Comparing the two line by line finds the cause.
