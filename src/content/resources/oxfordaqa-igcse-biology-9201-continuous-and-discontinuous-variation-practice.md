These questions cover section **3.6.1 Continuous and discontinuous variation** of the OxfordAQA International GCSE
Biology (9201) specification, Version 5.2, for exams in May/June 2018 onwards. The qualification is untiered. All data
are invented for practice. A calculator is useful for questions 5 to 7.

Learn the content first in the [Continuous and discontinuous variation study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-biology-9201-continuous-and-discontinuous-variation/)
and the [Variation, natural selection and the origin of species revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-biology-variation-natural-selection-and-the-origin-of-species-revision-notes/).
For mixed questions, use the [Variation, natural selection and the origin of species practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-biology-variation-natural-selection-and-the-origin-of-species-practice/).

## Section A

**1.**

**(a)** Give the **two** causes of variation named in the specification. **[2]**
**(b)** Give the **two** sources of genetic variation named in the specification. **[2]**

**2.** Give an example of an acquired characteristic in a plant or an animal. **[1]**

**3.** State whether each characteristic shows continuous or discontinuous variation. **[4]**

**(a)** The height of adult humans.
**(b)** Human blood group (A, B, AB or O).
**(c)** The mass of seeds from a plant.
**(d)** The flower colour (purple or white) in pea plants.

**4.** State the type of graph that should be used for data on each of these. **[2]**

**(a)** The number of people in each blood group.
**(b)** The heights of students, grouped in ranges of 5 cm.

## Section B

**5.** Cuttings were taken from one plant, so they are genetically identical. Four were grown in a bright room and four in a dim room. The length of the longest leaf was measured after six weeks.

| Cutting | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bright room (cm) | 22.5 | 24.0 | 21.0 | 23.5 |
| Dim room (cm) | 14.0 | 15.5 | 13.5 | 15.0 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean leaf length in each room. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage by which the mean leaf length in the dim room is smaller than in the bright room. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain what the results show about the cause of the difference between the two rooms. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest what could cause the differences between cuttings in the same room. **[1]**

**6.** The heights of 40 students were measured.

| Height (cm) | 145 up to 150 | 150 up to 155 | 155 up to 160 | 160 up to 165 | 165 up to 170 | 170 up to 175 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 3 | 7 | 14 | 10 | 4 | 2 |
| Mid-point (cm) | 147.5 | 152.5 | 157.5 | 162.5 | 167.5 | 172.5 |

**(a)** State the modal class. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate an estimate of the mean height. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[3]**
**(c)** Describe the shape of the distribution and state what type of variation it shows. **[2]**

**7.** The blood groups of 50 people were recorded.

| Blood group | A | B | AB | O |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Number of people | 16 | 9 | 4 | 21 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of people who are in blood group O. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage in blood group AB. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why a bar chart with gaps between the bars is suitable for these data. **[1]**

**8.** Two identical twins have the same genes. One twin is much heavier than the other. Explain how this can happen. **[2]**

**9.** A weightlifter develops large muscles through training. He says his children will inherit large muscles. Evaluate his statement. **[2]**

**10.** Explain how sexual reproduction produces genetic variation. **[2]**

**11.** A student wants to investigate variation in the length of holly leaves from one bush. Describe how to collect data that are reliable, and how to show the results. **[4]**

**12.** Describe how you could find out whether differences in the height of bean plants are caused by genes, by the environment, or by both. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Genetic variation [1]; environmental variation [1]
**(b)** Mutation [1]; reproduction [1]

**2.** Any one of the following [1]: a scar; a suntan; a plant growing taller in shade; an animal being smaller because of a poor diet

**3. (a)** Continuous [1]
**(b)** Discontinuous [1]
**(c)** Continuous [1]
**(d)** Discontinuous [1]

**4. (a)** Bar chart (with gaps between the bars) [1]
**(b)** Histogram (bars touching) [1]

**5. (a)** Bright: (22.5 + 24.0 + 21.0 + 23.5) ÷ 4 = 91.0 ÷ 4 = **22.75 cm** [1]; dim: (14.0 + 15.5 + 13.5 + 15.0) ÷ 4 = 58.0 ÷ 4 = **14.5 cm** [1]
**(b)** (22.75 − 14.5) ÷ 22.75 × 100 [1] = 8.25 ÷ 22.75 × 100 = **36.3%** [1]
**(c)** The cuttings are genetically identical, so there is no genetic variation between the groups [1]; the difference must be caused by the environment (the amount of light) [1]
**(d)** Any one of the following [1]: small differences in other environmental conditions such as position, water or soil; differences in the size of the cutting at the start

**6. (a)** 155 up to 160 cm [1]
**(b)** sum of frequency × mid-point = 3 × 147.5 + 7 × 152.5 + 14 × 157.5 + 10 × 162.5 + 4 × 167.5 + 2 × 172.5 = 442.5 + 1067.5 + 2205.0 + 1625.0 + 670.0 + 345.0 = 6355.0 [1]; divide by 40 [1] = 158.875, so **159 cm** [1]
**(c)** Most students are in the middle groups, with fewer at the extremes (a bell shape) [1]; continuous variation [1]

**7. (a)** 21 ÷ 50 × 100 = **42%** [1]
**(b)** 4 ÷ 50 × 100 = **8%** [1]
**(c)** The groups are distinct categories with no values in between [1]

**8.** Identical twins have the same genes, so the difference is caused by the environment [1]; for example, differences in diet or amount of exercise [1]

**9.** The large muscles are an acquired characteristic caused by training (the environment) [1]; acquired characteristics are not usually passed on in the genes, so the statement is not supported [1]

**10.** Offspring receive alleles from two parents [1]; so each offspring has a different combination of alleles (mutation can also create new alleles) [1]

**11.** Any four of the following, 1 mark each [4]: pick leaves at random from the bush (a large sample, such as 30 or more); measure each leaf from the base to the tip with a ruler, in the same way each time; record the lengths and group them into ranges; calculate the mean (and find the modal class); draw a histogram with touching bars; repeat with a second sample to check that the results are similar

**12.** Marking points, 1 mark each [6]:
- genes: use genetically identical plants (clones, or seeds from a pure-breeding line) and grow them in different conditions
- environment: use the same conditions for plants of different genetic make-up
- control the other variables, such as water, temperature and light
- grow a large number of plants in each group
- measure the height of every plant at set times, and calculate means for each group
- conclusion: if genetically identical plants differ in different conditions, the environment is a cause; if plants of different genes differ in the same conditions, genes are a cause; if both differ, both contribute

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying that acquired characteristics are inherited.
- Writing that clones differ because of their genes.
- Using the wrong graph: a bar chart for continuous data in ranges, or a histogram for categories.
- Stating a mean for grouped data without saying it is an estimate.
- Giving only one cause of variation when the question asks for both.
- Forgetting that mutation and reproduction are the sources of genetic variation.
