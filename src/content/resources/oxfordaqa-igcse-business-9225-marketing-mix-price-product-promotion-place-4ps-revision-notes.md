Condensed recall for section 3.5.4 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Business (9225). For full explanations and worked examples, use the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-9225-marketing-mix-price-product-promotion-place-4ps/). Figures are invented.

## What the specification asks

- **Price**: skimming, penetration, competitive, loss-leader, cost-plus; factors that influence pricing.
- **Product**: new products, design, image and brand, differentiation, USP, **product life cycle** and extension strategies, **product portfolio**.
- **Promotion**: methods; factors influencing the promotional mix.
- **Place**: channels of distribution, intermediaries (retailers, wholesalers, vending machines, telesales, e-commerce, direct selling).
- **E-commerce** and mobile commerce.
- How the four elements **work together** and **change over time**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Marketing mix | Product, price, promotion and place (4Ps) |
| Mark-up | Percentage added to cost to set a selling price |
| USP | Unique selling point: a feature rivals do not offer |
| Brand image | The identity and reputation customers associate with a product |
| Product life cycle | Pattern of sales over a product's life |
| Extension strategy | Action to extend the life of a product |
| Product portfolio | Range of products a business sells |
| Channel of distribution | Route a product takes from producer to consumer |
| Intermediary | A business between the producer and consumer (wholesaler, retailer) |
| E-commerce | Buying and selling online; m-commerce uses a mobile phone |

## Pricing methods

| Method | Summary | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Cost-plus | Cost + percentage mark-up | Reliable profit per item |
| Competitive | Similar to rivals | Crowded markets |
| Penetration | Low launch price | Winning market share fast |
| Skimming | High launch price, lowered later | New or innovative products with little competition |
| Loss-leader | Sold below cost to attract shoppers | Shops with many products |

Cost-plus example: $8.00 + 40% x $8.00 = $11.20.

**Factors affecting price:** production costs, competition, stage of the life cycle, customers' willingness to pay, brand image, business objectives, economies of scale.

## Product life cycle

| Stage | Sales | Actions |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | Low | Heavy promotion; limited distribution; skimming or penetration price |
| Growth | Rising fast | Build brand; widen distribution |
| Maturity | Peak, levelling | Defend share; extension strategy |
| Decline | Falling | Cut costs, withdraw or extend |

**Extension strategies:** new flavours or features, new packaging, new markets, new uses, price cuts or promotions.

## Product portfolio and new products

- A balanced portfolio has products at different life cycle stages; mature products fund new ones.
- Benefits of new products: fresh sales, new markets, keeping up with rivals.
- Risks: high cost of development; failure.
- Differentiation and a **USP** help a product stand out; a strong brand allows a higher price.

## Promotion

| Method | Benefit | Drawback |
|---|---|---|
| Advertising | Wide reach | Costly; hard to measure |
| Sales promotion | Quick boost to sales | Cuts profit |
| Personal selling | Answers questions | Costly; few reached |
| PR and sponsorship | Credible image | Less control |
| Direct marketing | Targeted | May seem junk mail |
| Social media | Cheap; young audience | Hard to control |

**Promotional mix depends on:** target market, budget, competitors' activity, nature of the product, life cycle stage.

Cost per customer = campaign cost / customers won. $9,000 / 2,250 = $4.

## Place

| Channel | Route |
|---|---|
| Direct | Producer to consumer (website, own shop, telesales, vending machine) |
| Retailer | Producer to retailer to consumer |
| Wholesaler | Producer to wholesaler to retailer to consumer |

Each intermediary adds a margin. Example: $10 x 1.25 = $12.50; $12.50 x 1.40 = $17.50.

**E-commerce:** large reach, always open, customer data; but delivery costs, competition, no trying the product, security risks.

## Integration of the mix

- All four Ps must fit the **target market** and each other.
- Luxury: high price, premium product, upmarket promotion, exclusive shops.
- Budget: low price, wide distribution, promotion on value.
- The best mix **changes over time** with the life cycle, technology and competition.

## Exam traps

- **Skimming** = start high; **penetration** = start low.
- Marketing is more than advertising: use all four Ps.
- Say **why** a method suits the market.
- Intermediaries raise the final price.
- Extension strategies are for maturity or decline, not launch.
- Use the case: target market, budget, competitors, stage of life cycle.

## Model answer outline: a 9-mark "evaluate" question

Question style: "Evaluate whether a new speaker should be launched using skimming or penetration pricing."

- **Skimming:** high profit per unit, premium image, suits a unique product; but few sales and rivals may enter.
- **Penetration:** quick market share and large sales; but low profit per unit and hard to raise prices later.
- **Calculation:** compare total profit for each, for example $120,000 against $175,000.
- **Context:** how strong the USP is, how much competition there is and what the business wants to achieve.
- **Judgement:** choose one, then say how the other three Ps must fit the choice (promotion, product quality and place).

## Revision checklist

- I can name the 4Ps and explain each.
- I can describe five pricing methods and calculate cost-plus prices.
- I can list factors that influence price.
- I can explain differentiation, USP, brand and the product portfolio.
- I can draw on the stages of the product life cycle and give extension strategies.
- I can describe six promotion methods and choose a promotional mix.
- I can compare channels of distribution and e-commerce.
- I can explain why the mix must be consistent.

## Self-test

1. List the 4Ps.
2. Define penetration pricing.
3. Define skimming pricing.
4. A product costs $20. Calculate the price with a 30% mark-up.
5. Give one reason for using a loss-leader.
6. Name the four stages of the product life cycle.
7. Give two extension strategies.
8. Define USP.
9. State two factors that influence the choice of promotional mix.
10. Give one benefit and one drawback of selling through a wholesaler.
11. A campaign costs $5,000 and wins 1,250 customers. Calculate the cost per customer.
12. Explain one benefit of e-commerce for a business.

### Answers

1. Product, price, promotion, place.
2. A low launch price to win customers and market share quickly.
3. A high launch price to earn high profit from early buyers, which may be lowered later.
4. 30% x $20 = $6; price = **$26**.
5. To attract customers into the shop, who then buy other goods at normal prices.
6. Introduction, growth, maturity, decline.
7. Any two: new flavour or feature; new packaging; new market; new use; price promotion.
8. A feature of a product that competitors do not offer.
9. Any two: target market; budget; competitors; product type; life cycle stage.
10. Benefit: sells in bulk to one buyer, saving selling effort. Drawback: the wholesaler takes a share, so the price rises or profit falls.
11. $5,000 / 1,250 = **$4**.
12. It reaches a much larger market, including other countries, without needing new shops.
