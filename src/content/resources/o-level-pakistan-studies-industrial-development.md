**Industrial development is Section 5 of Paper 2 in Cambridge O Level Pakistan Studies 2059.** It
defines the vocabulary of industry, then studies ten named processing and manufacturing industries
and tourism: what they make, where they are, why they are there, and how government, the formal
sector and the informal sector help them grow. The exam rewards a named place and a named location
factor for every industry.

## How does Section 5 connect to the rest of Paper 2?

Industry turns the raw materials of Sections 2 and 4 (limestone, gypsum, cotton, cane, natural gas)
into goods, and it depends on Section 3's power supply and Section 7's transport links. Its exports
feed Section 6 and its jobs feed Section 8. A 25-mark Paper 2 question often starts with a map of
industrial locations or a photograph of a workshop, then moves to a part (d) judgement such as
whether tourism or export zones can create jobs.

## What do the key terms mean, using one product chain?

Follow cotton from field to shirt to see every term at once:

1. **Raw material:** seed cotton picked in Sanghar or Vehari.
2. **Processed:** ginned to separate lint from seed, then spun into yarn.
3. **Manufactured:** yarn woven or knitted into cloth, then cut and sewn into garments.
4. **Value-added:** each stage raises the price; a kilogram of garments earns far more export
   income than a kilogram of raw lint.
5. **Refined** applies to other chains: crude oil into petrol, raw cane juice into white sugar.

**Infrastructure** is the base a factory needs: roads, rail, ports, power, gas, water, telecoms.
**Services** are activities that support people and firms, such as banking, insurance, transport
and repair. **Primary** industry extracts (farming, mining, fishing), **secondary** processes or
manufactures, and **tertiary** provides services.

**Scale in Pakistan.** Official statistics treat "large-scale manufacturing" as factories
registered under the Factories Act and covered by the census of manufacturing, generally with ten
or more workers using power-driven machinery. **Small-scale** units are smaller workshops with a
few hired workers, such as a Gujranwala metal workshop. **Cottage** industry is carried out at home
by family members with simple tools, such as embroidery or rilli quilt-making in rural Sindh.

## Where are the ten industries and why?

| Industry | Example locations | Decisive location factors | Market |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cement | Dera Ghazi Khan, Daudkhel and Chakwal (Punjab), Pezu and Hattar (KP), Thatta and Rohri (Sindh) | Heavy limestone and gypsum quarried nearby; power; road and rail | Domestic; exports to Afghanistan and by sea |
| Cotton: ginning | Rahim Yar Khan, Multan, Sanghar, Hyderabad | Seed cotton is bulky, so ginneries sit in the cotton belt | Lint to domestic mills |
| Cotton: spinning to clothing | Faisalabad, Lahore, Karachi, Multan | Labour, capital, power; port for exports and imported machinery | Domestic and the largest export group |
| Sugar refining | Rahim Yar Khan, Jhang, Mardan, Badin, Thatta | Cane loses sugar within a day or two of cutting | Domestic; molasses exported for ethanol |
| Crafts | Multan blue pottery; Hala lacquer work and ajrak; Chiniot furniture; Lahore carpets | Inherited skills; local materials; tourist trade | Domestic, tourists and export |
| Fertiliser (urea) | Daharki and Mirpur Mathelo (Sindh), Sadiqabad, Multan, Sheikhupura | Natural gas is both feedstock and fuel; on gas fields or pipelines | Domestic |
| Iron and steel | Pakistan Steel Mills at Pipri, Port Qasim; private mills in Lahore and Karachi; ship-breaking scrap at Gadani | Imported ore and coking coal land at the port; scrap from Gadani | Domestic |
| Brick | Kilns on the edge of every city, e.g. around Lahore and Peshawar | River-plain clay, cheap labour, local market; bricks are heavy and cheap | Domestic |
| Oil refining | Karachi (National and Pakistan refineries), Morgah near Rawalpindi (Attock Refinery), Mahmood Kot (Pak-Arab), Hub | Coastal sites for imported crude; Potwar sites for local crude | Domestic |
| Sports goods and surgical instruments | Sialkot, with cutlery at Wazirabad | Skilled workforce, export firms, Sialkot dry port and airport | Mainly export |

Pakistan Steel Mills was built with Soviet help and began producing in the early 1980s; it has been
largely idle since about 2015, so private re-rolling mills using imported billets and Gadani scrap
now supply much of the steel. Sialkot footballs have been used at FIFA World Cups, a good example
of export-led small-scale industry.

## Formal and informal sectors: what is the difference and why do both matter?

| | Formal sector | Informal sector |
|---|---|---|
| Registration | Registered, taxed, inspected | Unregistered, untaxed |
| Jobs | Contracts, regular wages, some benefits | Casual, irregular pay, no protection |
| Examples | Textile mills in Faisalabad, refineries, cement plants | Roadside tyre repair, rickshaw workshops, home stitching, street food |
| Capital | Banks, shareholders, foreign investors | Family savings, moneylenders |

**Services the informal sector provides:** cheap repairs, transport, recycling of scrap, small
retail and domestic work. **Advantages:** jobs for people without formal qualifications, low start-up
cost, cheap goods and services, flexible subcontracting for exporters. **Disadvantages:** low pay,
child labour (a long-standing concern in brick kilns), unsafe conditions, no tax revenue. Many
Sialkot exporters subcontract stitching to informal home workers, so the two sectors depend on
each other.

## How does government promote industry?

- **Export processing zones (EPZs):** fenced, serviced estates where firms import raw materials and
  machinery duty-free if they export most output. Examples: Karachi EPZ (the first, set up in the
  1980s), Sialkot, Risalpur, Gujranwala, and mineral EPZs at Saindak and Duddar in Balochistan.
- **Industrial estates and special economic zones:** SITE in Karachi and Hyderabad, Sundar
  Industrial Estate near Lahore, Hattar and Gadoon Amazai in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Hub in Balochistan,
  and CPEC special economic zones such as Rashakai and Allama Iqbal Industrial City near Faisalabad.
- **Other measures:** tax holidays, cheaper credit for small firms, training institutes, power and
  gas at subsidised tariffs for exporters, and infrastructure such as motorways and dry ports.

**Why build them?** To attract foreign investment and technology, earn foreign exchange, create
jobs and spread industry beyond Karachi and Lahore.

## Can global communications create service jobs?

Call centres, software houses and online freelancers need fast broadband, reliable power and
English-speaking, computer-literate graduates. Pakistan's information technology and IT-enabled
service exports were roughly US$3 billion a year in the mid-2020s, and online freelancing has grown
fast. **Limits:** power cuts, uneven internet outside big cities, a small pool of highly skilled
staff, and strong competition from other countries. A feasibility answer weighs these.

## Is tourism a realistic route to development?

**Natural attractions:** Fairy Meadows below Nanga Parbat, the Deosai Plains, Lake Saiful Muluk in
the Kaghan valley, Hingol National Park and Kund Malir beach on the Makran coast. **Cultural
attractions:** the Makli necropolis near Thatta, Rohtas Fort, Takht-i-Bahi, the Kalash valleys,
Sufi shrines at Sehwan and Multan, and the Kartarpur Corridor opened in 2019 for Sikh pilgrims.
Tourism employs guides, drivers, hotel and restaurant staff and craft sellers, earns foreign
exchange and adds to GDP and GNP. **Constraints:** poor road access and landslides, seasonal
demand, security perceptions, limited hotels, and damage to fragile mountain environments from
litter and unplanned building.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why are sugar mills built beside the cane fields?**
Cut cane is bulky and loses its sugar content quickly, so it must be crushed within a day or two.
Mills therefore sit in cane districts such as Rahim Yar Khan and Mardan.

**Why are fertiliser plants near Daharki and Sadiqabad?**
Urea is made from natural gas, which is both raw material and fuel, so plants are built on or near
the gas fields and pipelines of upper Sindh and southern Punjab.

**What makes an EPZ different from an ordinary industrial estate?**
An EPZ gives duty-free imports and tax concessions on condition that most output is exported. An
industrial estate offers serviced plots and infrastructure without that export condition.

**Is brick-making formal or informal?**
Mostly informal or semi-formal: kilns are often unregistered, use seasonal and family labour, and
have been criticised for bonded labour and smoke pollution.
