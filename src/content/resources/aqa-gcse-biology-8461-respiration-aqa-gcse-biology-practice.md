## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.**

**(a)** Give the **two** products of aerobic respiration. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why respiration is described as an exothermic reaction. **[1]**

**2.** Name the type of respiration, and the type of cell, that produces each substance.

**(a)** Lactic acid **[1]**
**(b)** Ethanol **[1]**
**(c)** Water **[1]**

**3.** Explain why muscles become fatigued during a long period of vigorous exercise. **[2]**

**4.** A student writes: "Plants photosynthesise during the day and respire at night." Correct the statement. **[2]**

**5.** Give **two** examples of metabolic reactions that build larger molecules from smaller ones. **[2]**

### Section B: longer and structured questions

**6.** A baker mixes yeast, flour, sugar and water to make dough. She puts 50 cm³ of dough in a measuring
cylinder at 30 °C. After 30 minutes the volume is 74 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of increase in volume. Give the unit. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the dough increases in volume. **[2]**
**(c)** The same dough kept in a fridge at 4 °C increases in volume much more slowly. Explain why. **[2]**

**7.** A student's blood lactic acid concentration is measured before, during and after 10 minutes of hard
exercise. The exercise stops at 10 minutes.

| Time (minutes) | 0 | 5 | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lactic acid (arbitrary units) | 1.0 | 4.0 | 9.0 | 6.0 | 3.0 | 1.5 |

**(a)** Describe how the lactic acid concentration changes. Use the data. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the concentration increases during the exercise. **[2]**
**(c)** (Higher tier only) Explain why the concentration falls after the exercise. **[2]**
**(d)** (Higher tier only) Explain why the student's breathing rate is still above resting at 20 minutes. **[1]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) An athlete's resting oxygen uptake is 0.3 dm³ per minute. Her mean oxygen uptake is
measured in each 2-minute period after a race.

| Period after race (minutes) | 0 to 2 | 2 to 4 | 4 to 6 | 6 to 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mean oxygen uptake (dm³ per minute) | 2.0 | 1.2 | 0.6 | 0.3 |

Calculate her oxygen debt. **[3]**

**9.** The roots of plants in waterlogged soil are surrounded by water instead of air.

**(a)** Suggest why the root cells respire anaerobically. **[1]**
**(b)** Give the products of anaerobic respiration in the root cells. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest why plants in waterlogged soil grow slowly. **[2]**

**10.**

**(a)** Copy and complete the table to show the small molecules used to build each large molecule. **[4]**

| Large molecule | Small molecules used |
|---|---|
| Starch | |
| Cellulose | |
| Lipid | |
| Protein | |

**(b)** A person eats much more protein than the body needs. Explain why the amount of urea they excrete
increases. **[2]**

**11.** A walker's leg muscles respire aerobically, but a sprinter's leg muscles also respire anaerobically.
Explain why. **[3]**

**12.** (Higher tier only) Describe and explain the changes that happen in the body during and after a period of
vigorous exercise. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Carbon dioxide and water (both needed) [1]
**(b)** It transfers energy to the surroundings [1]

**2. (a)** Anaerobic respiration in muscle cells [1]
**(b)** Anaerobic respiration in yeast or plant cells [1]
**(c)** Aerobic respiration in any living cell [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each mark needs both the type of respiration and the cell.

**3.** Anaerobic respiration causes lactic acid to build up in the muscles [1]; so the muscles stop contracting efficiently [1]

**4.** Plants respire all the time, day and night [1]; they photosynthesise only when there is light, so in the day they both photosynthesise and respire [1]

**5.** Any two: glucose to starch, glycogen or cellulose; glycerol and fatty acids to lipids; glucose and nitrate ions to amino acids; amino acids to proteins [2]
*Examiner insight:* One mark for each correct reaction; respiration is a breakdown reaction and does not count here.

**6. (a)** (74 − 50) ÷ 30 = 0.8 [1]; **cm³ per minute** [1]
**(b)** Yeast respires anaerobically (fermentation), producing carbon dioxide [1]; the gas is trapped in the dough, so it rises [1]
**(c)** Respiration is controlled by enzymes [1]; at low temperature the enzymes work more slowly, so less carbon dioxide is produced per minute [1]
*Examiner insight:* "The enzymes are denatured" is wrong for (c); low temperature slows enzymes but does not denature them.

**7. (a)** It rises from 1.0 to 9.0 during the 10 minutes of exercise [1]; then falls after exercise, to 1.5 by 40 minutes [1]
**(b)** Not enough oxygen reaches the muscles to meet the demand for energy [1]; so muscles respire anaerobically, producing lactic acid [1]
**(c)** Blood carries lactic acid to the liver [1]; where it is converted back into glucose [1]
**(d)** Extra oxygen is still needed to react with the remaining lactic acid (repaying the oxygen debt) [1]

**8.** Extra oxygen per minute in each period: 1.7, 0.9, 0.3, 0 dm³ [1]; multiply by 2 minutes: 3.4 + 1.8 + 0.6 + 0 [1]; oxygen debt = **5.8 dm³** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Using total uptake (8.2 dm³) without subtracting the resting value scores the second mark at most.

**9. (a)** There is little oxygen in the water around the roots [1]
**(b)** Ethanol and carbon dioxide [1]
**(c)** Anaerobic respiration transfers much less energy [1]; so less energy is available for processes such as building larger molecules for growth [1]

**10. (a)** Starch: glucose [1]; cellulose: glucose [1]; lipid: glycerol and fatty acids [1]; protein: amino acids [1]
**(b)** Excess proteins are broken down (to amino acids) [1]; and are converted to urea for excretion, so more urea is made [1]

**11.** Sprinting needs energy much faster than walking [1]; the heart and lungs cannot supply oxygen to the muscles fast enough [1]; so anaerobic respiration takes place as well as aerobic [1]

**12.** One mark for each point:
- during exercise, heart rate, breathing rate and breath volume increase [1]
- this supplies more oxygenated blood to the muscles and removes more carbon dioxide [1]
- if oxygen supply is insufficient, the muscles respire anaerobically and lactic acid builds up [1]
- long vigorous activity makes muscles fatigued so they stop contracting efficiently [1]
- after exercise, blood carries lactic acid to the liver, where it is converted back into glucose [1]
- breathing stays fast and deep to take in the extra oxygen (oxygen debt) needed to react with the lactic acid [1]
*Examiner insight:* Top answers explain each change, not just list it, and cover both "during" and "after".

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that respiration "makes" energy.
- Saying low temperature denatures enzymes.
- Forgetting to subtract resting uptake from recovery uptake.
- Naming the type of respiration but not the type of cell.
- Writing that lactic acid is "breathed out".
