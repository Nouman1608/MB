These questions cover subsection 3.2.3, **Solving equations and inequalities** (A17 to A22), of the AQA GCSE Mathematics (8300) specification, for teaching from September 2015 with exams from May/June 2017 (version 1.0). Questions 1 to 6 and 12 use content for both tiers. Questions 7 to 11 are marked "(Higher)" because they use Higher tier only content: the quadratic formula, completing the square, linear/quadratic simultaneous equations, iteration, quadratic inequalities and regions. Each question says whether it is non-calculator (Paper 1 style) or calculator (Papers 2 and 3 style).

They are different from the questions in the [Algebra practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-mathematics-algebra-practice/), so you can use both. Learn the content first in the [study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-maths-8300-solving-equations-and-inequalities/) and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-maths-8300-solving-equations-and-inequalities-revision-notes/). The course hub is [AQA GCSE Mathematics](/boards/aqa/gcse/mathematics/) and the [printable checklist](/checklists/aqa/gcse/mathematics/) lists every statement.

## Questions

**1.** (non-calculator) Solve 7(x − 2) = 3x + 10. **[3]**

**2.** (non-calculator) Solve (3x − 1)/4 = x − 2. **[3]**

**3.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** Solve x² − 5x − 24 = 0. **[3]**
**(b)** Solve x² − 36 = 0. **[2]**

**4.** (non-calculator) Solve the simultaneous equations

4x + 3y = 5
2x − 5y = 9 **[4]**

**5.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** Solve 9 − 4x ≥ 21. **[2]**
**(b)** List all the integers n that satisfy −7 < 3n − 1 ≤ 8. **[3]**
**(c)** Describe how you would show −4 ≤ x < 1 on a number line. **[1]**

**6.** (calculator) At a café, 4 coffees and 3 muffins cost £13.10. 2 coffees and 5 muffins cost £11.10.

**(a)** Form two equations and solve them to find the cost of one coffee and the cost of one muffin. **[5]**
**(b)** Ella has £20. She buys 3 coffees and some muffins. Form and solve an inequality to find the greatest number of muffins she can buy. **[3]**

**7.** (calculator) (Higher) Solve 4x(x + 1) = 7. Give your answers to 2 decimal places. **[4]**

**8.** (non-calculator) (Higher)

**(a)** Write x² + 8x + 11 in the form (x + a)² + b. **[2]**
**(b)** Hence solve x² + 8x + 11 = 0. Give your answers in surd form. **[2]**

**9.** (non-calculator) (Higher) Solve algebraically the simultaneous equations

y = 2x − 1
y = x² − 3x + 5 **[5]**

**10.** (calculator) (Higher) f(x) = x³ + 3x − 9.

**(a)** Show that the equation f(x) = 0 has a solution between x = 1 and x = 2. **[2]**
**(b)** Show that x³ + 3x − 9 = 0 can be rearranged to give x = ∛(9 − 3x). **[1]**
**(c)** Use xₙ₊₁ = ∛(9 − 3xₙ) with x₀ = 1.5 to find x₁, x₂ and x₃. Give each to 4 decimal places. **[3]**
**(d)** Show that the solution is 1.61 correct to 2 decimal places. **[2]**

**11.** (non-calculator) (Higher)

**(a)** Solve x² − 3x − 10 > 0. Give your answer in set notation. **[4]**
**(b)** A region R on a graph is defined by y > x − 1, y ≤ 4 and x ≥ 1.
(i) Which boundary line should be drawn dashed? **[1]**
(ii) Does the point (5, 4) lie in R? Give a reason. **[1]**

**12.** (calculator) A right-angled triangle has sides of length x cm, (x + 7) cm and (x + 8) cm. The longest side is the hypotenuse.

**(a)** Show that x² − 2x − 15 = 0. **[3]**
**(b)** Solve the equation and work out the perimeter of the triangle. **[3]**
**(c)** Work out the area of the triangle. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** 7x − 14 = 3x + 10 [1]
4x = 24 [1]
**x = 6** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The first mark is for expanding the bracket correctly; 7x − 2 = 3x + 10 loses it, but correct steps after it can still earn a follow-through mark.

**2.** 3x − 1 = 4(x − 2) [1]
3x − 1 = 4x − 8, so −1 + 8 = 4x − 3x [1]
**x = 7** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Multiplying only the x by 4 (giving 3x − 1 = 4x − 2) is a method error, so only the first mark is available.

**3. (a)** (x − 8)(x + 3) = 0 [1] [1]
**x = 8 or x = −3** [1]
**(b)** (x − 6)(x + 6) = 0, or x² = 36 [1]
**x = 6 or x = −6** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), brackets that expand to give two of the three terms correctly usually earn the first mark; in (b), giving only x = 6 loses the final mark.

**4.** Multiply the second equation by 2: 4x − 10y = 18 [1]
Subtract from the first: 13y = −13 [1]
**y = −1** [1]
Substitute: 4x − 3 = 5, so **x = 2** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A method mark needs a correct attempt to eliminate one variable, with the operation (add or subtract) consistent with the signs; adding here gives 8x − 7y = 23 and earns nothing.

**5. (a)** −4x ≥ 12 [1]
**x ≤ −3** [1]
**(b)** −6 < 3n ≤ 9 [1]
−2 < n ≤ 3 [1]
**n = −1, 0, 1, 2, 3** [1]
**(c)** A **closed (filled) circle at −4**, an **open circle at 1**, and a line joining them [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), including −2 or leaving out 3 loses the final mark; the inequality signs decide which end values count.

**6. (a)** Let a coffee cost £c and a muffin £m.
4c + 3m = 13.10 [1]
2c + 5m = 11.10 [1]
Double the second: 4c + 10m = 22.20, then subtract: 7m = 9.10 [1]
m = 1.30, so **a muffin costs £1.30** [1]
4c = 13.10 − 3.90 = 9.20, so **a coffee costs £2.30** [1]
**(b)** Let k be the number of muffins: 3 × 2.30 + 1.30k ≤ 20, so 6.90 + 1.30k ≤ 20 [1]
1.30k ≤ 13.10, so k ≤ 10.07... [1]
**10 muffins** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Money answers need two decimal places (£1.30, not £1.3); in (b) the answer must be a whole number that satisfies the inequality, so 10.07 or 11 loses the final mark.

**7.** 4x² + 4x − 7 = 0 [1]
x = (−4 ± √(4² − 4 × 4 × (−7))) / (2 × 4) [1]
x = (−4 ± √128) / 8 [1]
**x = 0.91 or x = −1.91** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The first mark is for rearranging to = 0; using the formula on 4x² + 4x = 7 with c = 7 is a method error and loses all marks that follow.

**8. (a)** (x + 4)² [1] − 5, so **(x + 4)² − 5** [1]
**(b)** (x + 4)² = 5 [1]
**x = −4 + √5 or x = −4 − √5** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Hence" means you must use part (a); solving with the formula instead may not earn the marks, and leaving out the ± loses the final mark.

**9.** x² − 3x + 5 = 2x − 1 [1]
x² − 5x + 6 = 0 [1]
(x − 2)(x − 3) = 0 [1]
x = 2 or x = 3 [1]
**x = 2, y = 3 and x = 3, y = 5** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The final mark needs both y-values, each correctly paired with its x; a graphical or trial-and-improvement answer scores nothing when the question says "algebraically".

**10. (a)** f(1) = 1 + 3 − 9 = −5 [1]
f(2) = 8 + 6 − 9 = 5; the sign changes, so there is a solution between 1 and 2 [1]
**(b)** x³ = 9 − 3x, so x = ∛(9 − 3x) [1]
**(c)** **x₁ = 1.6510** [1]
**x₂ = 1.5936** [1]
**x₃ = 1.6159** [1]
**(d)** f(1.605) = −0.0505 (negative) [1]
f(1.615) = 0.0573 (positive); the sign changes, so the solution is **1.61 to 2 d.p.** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a) and (d) the conclusion must mention the change of sign; correct values with no statement lose the final mark in each part.

**11. (a)** (x − 5)(x + 2) = 0 [1]
Critical values x = 5 and x = −2 [1]
x < −2 or x > 5 [1]
**{x : x < −2} ∪ {x : x > 5}** [1]
**(b)(i)** **y = x − 1** (strict inequality) [1]
**(ii)** **No**: on y = x − 1, at x = 5, y = 4, so (5, 4) is on the dashed line and 4 > 4 is false [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), writing x < −2 or x > 5 without set notation does not follow the instruction and loses the final mark; −2 > x > 5 is wrong, because no number satisfies it.

**12. (a)** x² + (x + 7)² = (x + 8)² [1]
x² + x² + 14x + 49 = x² + 16x + 64 [1]
x² − 2x − 15 = 0 [1]
**(b)** (x − 5)(x + 3) = 0 [1]
x = 5, since x = −3 would give a negative length [1]
Sides 5, 12 and 13: **perimeter 30 cm** [1]
**(c)** ½ × 5 × 12 = **30 cm²** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Show that" in (a) needs both expansions written out; jumping from the Pythagoras line straight to the given equation loses the marks for the missing working.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Expanding only part of a bracket, such as 7(x − 2) = 7x − 2.
- Dividing a quadratic by x instead of factorising, which loses the root x = 0.
- Adding equations when the signs need subtracting, or the reverse.
- Forgetting to reverse the inequality sign after dividing by a negative.
- Including a boundary value in a list of integers when the inequality is strict.
- Using the quadratic formula before rearranging the equation to = 0.
- Giving one value of x and no y-values in simultaneous equations.
- Rounding iterates during the calculation instead of using the full calculator value.
- Writing a "show that" with steps missing.
- Not rejecting a negative length in a geometry context.
