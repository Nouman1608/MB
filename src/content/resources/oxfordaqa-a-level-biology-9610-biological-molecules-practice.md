## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define the term *hydrolysis*. **[2]**

**2.** A polysaccharide chain contains 250 glucose units joined in a single unbranched chain. State the number of water molecules released when the chain was made. **[1]**

**3.** Which statement about cellulose is correct?

A: It is made by condensation of alpha-glucose.

B: It is made by condensation of beta-glucose.

C: It contains peptide bonds.

D: It gives a blue-black colour with iodine solution. **[1]**

**4.** Name the bond that joins glycerol to a fatty acid, and the test that shows the presence of lipid. **[2]**

**5.** A pentapeptide is made from five glycine molecules. The relative molecular mass of glycine is 75 and the relative molecular mass of water is 18. Calculate the relative molecular mass of the pentapeptide. **[3]**

### Section B: longer questions

**6.** Starch and cellulose are both polymers of glucose.

**(a)** Explain why starch is suited to storing glucose in a plant cell. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain how the structure of cellulose makes it suited to its function in the plant cell wall. **[3]**

**7.** A triglyceride is made from one glycerol molecule (relative molecular mass 92) and three molecules of a fatty acid (relative molecular mass 256 each).

**(a)** Calculate the relative molecular mass of the triglyceride. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain how a phospholipid differs from this triglyceride. **[2]**

**8.** A student tested a sample of a sugar solution with Benedict's reagent and the mixture stayed blue. The student then boiled a second portion of the sample with dilute hydrochloric acid, neutralised it and heated it with Benedict's reagent. An orange-red precipitate formed.

**(a)** What conclusion can be drawn about the sugar? **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the acid was boiled with the sample before the second test. **[2]**

**(c)** Trehalose is a non-reducing disaccharide. Hydrolysis of trehalose gives two glucose molecules. Predict the result of the Benedict's test on trehalose before and after hydrolysis, and explain your prediction. **[3]**

**9.** Insulin is a protein made of two polypeptide chains linked by disulfide bridges.

**(a)** Name the bond that joins amino acids in a polypeptide. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why a change in one amino acid in the primary structure could stop a protein working. **[3]**

**10.** Compare the structures of a saturated triglyceride and an unsaturated triglyceride, and explain one difference in their properties. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Hydrolysis is the breaking of a chemical bond between two molecules [1] in a reaction that uses a molecule of water [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that water is used. Do not describe it as "adding water to the cell".

**2.** 249 [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* A chain of n units has n minus 1 bonds, so 250 minus 1 gives 249.

**3.** **B** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Cellulose is beta-glucose. Option A describes starch and C describes protein.

**4.** Ester bond [1]. Emulsion test (shake with ethanol, then add to water, giving a cloudy white emulsion) [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Four peptide bonds form, so four water molecules are lost [1]. Mass of five glycine molecules is 5 x 75 = 375 [1]. Mass of pentapeptide is 375 - (4 x 18) = 375 - 72 = 303 [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The common slip is to subtract five waters. Count the bonds, not the monomers.

**6. (a)** Starch is a large molecule that is insoluble, so it does not affect the water potential of the cell [1]. The amylose helix is compact, so a lot of glucose is stored in a small space [1]. The branched amylopectin has many ends, so enzymes can hydrolyse glycosidic bonds at many points and release glucose quickly [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** Cellulose is made of beta-glucose, so the chain is straight rather than coiled [1]. Hydrogen bonds form between parallel chains, making strong fibres [1]. These fibres give strength to the wall, so the cell resists the pressure when water enters by osmosis [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Strong" alone scores nothing. Link chain shape, hydrogen bonding and function.

**7. (a)** Three water molecules are lost in forming three ester bonds [1]. 92 + (3 x 256) - (3 x 18) = 92 + 768 - 54 = 806 [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** In a phospholipid one fatty acid is replaced by a phosphate-containing group [1]. The phosphate head is hydrophilic while the fatty acid tails are hydrophobic [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** The sugar is a non-reducing sugar [1] because it gave a negative result before hydrolysis and a positive result after hydrolysis [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** The acid hydrolyses the glycosidic bond [1], releasing monosaccharides that are reducing sugars and so react with Benedict's reagent [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** Before hydrolysis the test is negative, because trehalose is a non-reducing sugar [1]. After hydrolysis the test is positive [1], because the glucose released is a reducing sugar [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the information in the stem. Hydrolysis gives glucose, which is a reducing sugar, so the second result must be positive.

**9. (a)** Peptide bond [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** The primary structure is the sequence of amino acids, and it determines where hydrogen bonds and disulfide bridges form [1]. A different amino acid could change which bonds form, so the tertiary structure changes [1]. The shape of the protein, for example an active site, would then no longer fit, so the protein would not function [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Saturated: no double bonds in the fatty acid chains, so the chains are straight [1]. Unsaturated: one or more double bonds, which put kinks in the chains [1]. Straight chains pack more closely, so saturated triglycerides tend to be solid at room temperature [1]. Kinked chains pack less closely, so unsaturated triglycerides tend to be liquid [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Subtracting the wrong number of water molecules in a mass calculation.
- Writing "glucose" for starch or cellulose without the isomer.
- Skipping the neutralising step or the hydrolysis step in the non-reducing sugar test.
- Naming the wrong bond (glycosidic, ester and peptide bonds are easily swapped).
- Describing a property without linking it to structure.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these OxfordAQA past paper questions?**
No. They are original questions for section 3.1.1 of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Biology (9610). Use them with the official past papers.

**Do I need to remember the structure of trehalose?**
No. It is not named in the specification. The specification expects you to apply condensation and hydrolysis to structures you are given, so you may be given a structure that you have not met.
