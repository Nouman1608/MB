## Section A

**1.** Define an ore. **[1]**

**2.** State what is meant by overburden. **[1]**

**3.** Name the **two** subsurface extraction methods in the syllabus. **[2]**

**4.** Describe how bioleaching recovers metal from low-grade ore. **[2]**

**5.** State **two** advantages of surface extraction compared with subsurface extraction. **[2]**

**6.** Name **two** land restoration strategies and **two** ways of repurposing land after extraction. **[4]**

## Section B

**7.** A zinc deposit contains 400,000 tonnes of ore with an ore grade of 2.5% zinc.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of zinc in the deposit. Show your working. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of rock left over as waste once all the zinc has been extracted. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain **two** factors, other than ore grade, that could lead a company to decide not to mine this deposit. **[4]**

**8.** Describe how strip mining is carried out, and explain why it is suited to a coal seam lying close to the surface. **[3]**

**9.** A mining company is comparing two possible copper sites. The information is invented for practice.

| | Site A | Site B |
|---|---|---|
| Ore grade | 0.8% | 1.6% |
| Distance to nearest railway | 5 km | 300 km (no road) |
| Climate | Mild, rain all year | Ground frozen for most of the year |
| Land use | Farmland near a town | Uninhabited tundra |

Suggest which site the company is more likely to develop. Justify your answer using the information. **[4]**

**10.** Explain how a large open-pit mine can affect water supplies in the surrounding area. **[4]**

**11.** Describe what an environmental impact assessment is, and explain why a government may require one before allowing a mine to open. **[3]**

**12.** A quarry has closed and part of the pit has filled with water. The local council must choose between using the pit for landfill or turning it into a lake for recreation and a nature reserve. Discuss the benefits and limitations of each option. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Rock containing minerals and metals [1].

**2.** The soil and rock lying above a deposit, which must be removed to reach it [1].

**3.** Deep mining [1]; shaft mining [1].

**4.** Bacteria break down the ore [1] and release metal compounds into a solution (leachate), from which the metal is recovered [1].

**5.** Any two: lower cost per tonne [1]; safer for workers (no risk of tunnel collapse or underground gases) [1]; recovers a larger proportion of the deposit [1]; large machines can move large quantities quickly [1].

**6.** Restoration, any two: replacement of overburden [1]; soil improvement [1]; bioremediation [1]; tree planting [1]. Repurposing, any two: landfill [1]; lakes [1]; recreation [1]; nature reserves [1]. Maximum 2 for each group.

**7. (a)** 400,000 × 0.025 (or 400,000 × 2.5 ÷ 100) [1] = 10,000 tonnes [1].

**(b)** 400,000 − 10,000 = 390,000 tonnes [1].

**(c)** Two factors, each with an explanation, 2 marks each:
- **Cost and profit**: if the price of zinc is low, income may not cover extraction, processing and restoration costs [1], so the company would make a loss [1].
- **Accessibility and terrain**: a remote or mountainous site needs new roads or railways [1], raising the cost of bringing in machinery and taking out ore [1].
- **Environmental impact assessment**: the EIA may show serious harm, for example to a river or protected habitat [1], so permission is refused or conditions make the project too expensive [1].
- **Supply and demand**: if supply already meets demand [1], the extra zinc may not sell at a profitable price [1].
- **Geology or climate**: a deep or steeply angled deposit or extreme climate raises costs [1] and may make extraction unsafe or seasonal [1].

**8.** Overburden is removed in a long strip to expose the seam, and the coal is dug out [1]; the overburden from the next strip is placed in the gap left by the previous one [1]. It suits a shallow seam because only a thin layer of overburden must be moved, so the coal can be reached cheaply without tunnels [1].

**9.** Site A is more likely [1]. Justification, any three:
- It is only 5 km from a railway, so ore can be transported cheaply, whereas Site B would need a 300 km route built [1].
- The mild climate allows work all year; frozen ground at Site B would slow work and damage equipment [1].
- Although Site B's grade is double, Site A's lower grade may be offset by much lower transport and building costs [1].
- Credit an argument for Site B if fully justified: higher grade halves the ore to be processed and there are no nearby residents to be affected [1].
- A limitation of Site A: farmland and a town nearby mean noise, dust and loss of farmland, so the EIA may impose costly conditions [1].

Maximum 4.

**10.** Any four:
- Processing ore and suppressing dust use large volumes of water [1], which can lower river levels or ground water available to farms and homes [1].
- Pumping water out of a deep pit lowers the local water table [1], so nearby wells may dry up [1].
- Rain washing over exposed rock and spoil carries sediment into rivers [1], making water cloudy and harming aquatic life [1].
- Chemicals used in processing, or toxic metals in tailings, can leak into rivers and ground water [1], making water unsafe to drink [1].

**11.** An EIA is a study of the likely effects of a proposed project on the environment and people, carried out before permission is given [1]. It identifies harmful impacts so that they can be avoided or reduced, or the project refused [1], and lets the government and local people make an informed decision [1].

**12.** Levels-based. Credit up to 6 from:
- **Landfill benefits**: provides space to dispose of the area's waste [1]; earns income from waste disposal fees, which can pay for landscaping [1].
- **Landfill limitations**: leachate may pollute ground water, especially as the pit already contains water [1]; methane may be released, and lorries bring noise and smell [1].
- **Lake and nature reserve benefits**: creates wetland habitat and increases biodiversity [1]; provides recreation and tourism income [1]; cheaper because the pit is already filling with water [1].
- **Lake and nature reserve limitations**: steep, deep pit sides can be dangerous for swimmers [1]; water may be polluted by minerals from the quarry [1]; income may be smaller than from landfill [1].
- **Judgement** that weighs both, for example the lake is safer because water in the pit makes landfill pollution likely [1].

Maximum 6; an answer that covers only one option scores no more than 3.

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Question 4**: describing plants instead of bacteria. Phytomining uses plants; bioleaching uses bacteria.
- **Question 7(a)**: using 2.5 instead of 0.025, giving an answer 100 times too large. Check that the zinc is a small fraction of the ore.
- **Question 9**: choosing a site without using the figures. Each justification mark needs a piece of information from the table.
- **Question 10**: writing "it pollutes water" without the cause (sediment, chemicals or tailings) and the effect.
- **Question 12**: discussing only one option, or giving benefits without limitations.
