Cambridge IGCSE Environmental Management (0680) for the 2027-2029 series is assessed by two externally assessed papers and is not tiered. Paper 1, Principles of Environmental Management, and Paper 2, Environmental Management in Context, are each 1 hour 45 minutes, 80 marks and 50% of the qualification. First examination is in 2027 and, at the date it was verified, this syllabus had not yet had a live sitting. Cambridge also publishes a 2025-2026 edition, so confirm which one applies to you. Support pages include [Land](/resources/igcse-cambridge-environmental-management-land/), [Natural Resources](/resources/igcse-cambridge-environmental-management-natural-resources/) and [Natural hazards](/resources/cambridge-igcse-environmental-management-natural-hazards-study-guide/).

## Paper 1: Principles of Environmental Management (80 marks, 50%)

Paper 1 combines short-answer or structured questions with extended-response questions based on source material. The title points to principles, so revise the underlying ideas: how natural resources are used and managed, how land and water are affected by human activity, and how management decisions are justified. Practise explaining a concept and then applying it to a source. The topic list includes natural resources, land, water, the atmosphere and human activities, ecosystems, biodiversity and fieldwork, natural hazards and human population, so no area can be skipped.

## Paper 2: Environmental Management in Context (80 marks, 50%)

Paper 2 uses short-answer questions, data processing and analysis questions, and extended-response questions based on source material. The emphasis on context means applying what you know to unfamiliar places, data sets and scenarios. Practise reading tables and graphs, doing calculations from data, spotting patterns and writing balanced conclusions. For the fieldwork strand, see the [Ecosystems, biodiversity and fieldwork study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-environmental-management-ecosystems-biodiversity-and-fieldwork-study-guide/).

The record does not state how marks are split between the question types on either paper, so use the specification and specimen papers for that.

## Timing and marks-per-minute

- Each paper: 105 minutes / 80 marks = 1.3125 minutes per mark, or about 1 minute 19 seconds.
- Total sitting time: 2 x 105 = 210 minutes for 2 x 80 = 160 marks.
- A 6-mark extended response therefore merits about 8 minutes (6 x 1.3125 = 7.9 minutes).
- Each paper is worth 50 / 80 = 0.625% of the qualification per mark.

Because both papers have the same duration, marks and weighting, neither should be treated as the minor one.

## Command words and question styles

- **State** and **give**: short answers, one point per mark.
- **Describe**: say what a pattern, trend or feature is, quoting data.
- **Explain**: give reasons and consequences.
- **Calculate**: show working and give units.
- **Suggest**: apply knowledge to an unfamiliar situation.
- **Evaluate** or **assess**: weigh advantages and disadvantages and conclude.

## Building a revision plan across the seven topics

Seven topic areas and two equally weighted papers suggest a rotating plan. Take each topic in turn, write a one-page summary of key terms, processes and management strategies, and add one specific example or data set for each. After every topic, answer one short data question and one extended response from a source, so that you practise both papers' styles together. Pay particular attention to links between topics: land use affects water, the atmosphere and ecosystems, and population pressure affects natural resources, so the best extended answers often draw on more than one area. In the final weeks, switch to timed practice at about 1.3 minutes per mark, and review which command words you tend to misread.

## Worked practice scenario

An original data-processing question. A forest reserve had an area of 18,400 hectares of forest cover in one year and 15,640 hectares ten years later.

Question: "(a) Calculate the percentage decrease in forest cover. (b) Suggest two management strategies to slow the loss."

```
(a) Decrease = 18,400 - 15,640 = 2,760 hectares
    Percentage decrease = 2,760 / 18,400 x 100 = 15%
```

Check: 15,640 / 18,400 x 100 = 85%, and 100 - 85 = 15%, which agrees.

(b) Two strategies: (1) legal protection with enforcement and monitoring of the reserve boundary, which limits clearing; (2) working with local communities on alternative livelihoods, so there is less pressure to clear trees for fuel or farmland. A strong answer links each strategy to how it reduces the loss.

The percentage is calculated on the original value, not the new one, which is the usual slip.

A further habit is worth building for extended responses based on source material. Read the source before the question, underline the figures and claims you could use, and then write an answer that quotes or refers to that evidence in every paragraph. An answer that only repeats general knowledge ignores the source and usually leaves marks unclaimed, whereas one that links a named piece of evidence to a management idea shows both skills at once.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Confirm which edition of 0680 applies to your year.
- Revise all seven topic areas.
- Practise data processing from tables and graphs.
- Practise extended answers using source material.
- Use roughly 1.3 minutes per mark for pacing.
- Show working for calculations, with units.
- Use specific examples and data in explanations.
- Reserve time at the end to read through extended answers.

## Self-test

1. How many marks and how many minutes are on each paper?
2. What does Paper 2 include that is not named for Paper 1?
3. What percentage of the qualification is each paper?
4. In the worked example, why is the percentage based on 18,400?

**Answers:** 1. 80 marks and 105 minutes. 2. Data processing and analysis questions. 3. 50% each. 4. Because a percentage decrease is measured against the original value.
