Condensed recall for **3.1.2 Methods of language analysis**, the toolkit integrated into Paper 1 of AQA A-level
English Language (7702). The full explanation and worked examples are in the
[Paper 1 methods of language analysis study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-english-language-7702-methods-of-language-analysis-3-1-2/).

## The core demand

Identify and describe the **salient** features of language in the texts, so that you can explain how they create
meanings and representations. Salient means prominent and significant: patterns and marked choices, not every
feature you can find.

## Phonetics, phonology and prosodics

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Alliteration / sibilance | Repeated initial consonant sounds / repeated "s" and "sh" sounds |
| Assonance | Repeated vowel sounds |
| Stress | Emphasis on a syllable or word, often marked in a transcript |
| Intonation | Rise and fall of pitch, signalling questions, certainty or attitude |
| Elision | Omission of a sound in connected speech ("gonna", "fish 'n' chips") |
| Micropause / pause | A very brief pause / a timed pause, shown in the transcript key |

## Graphology

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Typography | Choice of typeface, size, weight and style |
| Layout | Arrangement of text blocks, headings, columns and white space |
| Anchorage | Words fixing the meaning of an image (for example a caption) |
| Salience (visual) | The element that draws the eye first through size, colour or position |

## Lexis and semantics

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Semantic field | A group of words from the same area of meaning |
| Connotation | Associations a word carries beyond its literal meaning |
| Collocation | Words that habitually occur together |
| Euphemism / dysphemism | Softened expression / deliberately harsh expression |
| Register | Variety of language suited to a situation (field, tenor, mode) |
| Jargon | Specialist lexis of an occupation or interest group |

## Grammar, including morphology

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Noun phrase | Head noun with determiners and modifiers ("the old red bus") |
| Pre- / post-modification | Words before the head / phrases or clauses after it ("bus with no driver") |
| Modal verb | Auxiliary expressing possibility, obligation or ability (may, must, could) |
| Aspect | Progressive ("is running") or perfect ("has run") forms of the verb |
| Active / passive voice | Subject performs the action / subject is affected by it |
| Main / subordinate clause | Clause that can stand alone / clause dependent on another |
| Sentence functions | Declarative, interrogative, imperative, exclamative |
| Sentence structures | Simple, compound, complex, compound-complex, minor |
| Morpheme | Smallest unit of meaning; free (book) or bound (-s, un-) |

## Pragmatics

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Implicature | Meaning implied rather than stated |
| Grice's maxims | Quantity, quality, relation, manner: flouting one creates implicature |
| Speech act | The action an utterance performs (request, promise, warning) |
| Face | Public self-image; positive face (approval) and negative face (freedom) |
| Deixis | Words whose reference depends on context (here, then, you, this) |
| Presupposition | An assumption taken for granted by an utterance |

## Discourse

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cohesion | How a text holds together through reference, repetition, ellipsis and conjunctions |
| Anaphoric / cataphoric reference | Pointing back / pointing forward |
| Adjacency pair | Two linked turns, such as question and answer or greeting and greeting |
| Discourse marker | Words organising talk or text (so, anyway, right, firstly) |
| Repair | Correcting a problem in talk, by oneself or another speaker |

## Precise descriptions to memorise

- Not "short sentence" but "minor sentence", "simple declarative" or "imperative".
- Not "describing words" but "pre-modifying adjectives in the noun phrase".
- Not "the word 'we'" but "first-person plural pronoun, used inclusively / exclusively".
- Not "a question" but "interrogative" (and only "rhetorical" if no answer is expected).
- Not "makes it sound informal" but "colloquial lexis and contractions create an informal tenor".

## Exam traps

- Treating length as structure: a long sentence can be simple; a short one can be complex.
- Assuming every "we" is inclusive: in "We at Brightway Bank" it excludes the reader.
- Naming a level with no evidence, such as "the phonology is interesting".
- Ignoring prosody in transcripts, where stress and intonation carry attitude.
- Ignoring how an older text's grammar or lexis reflects its period.
- Spending the answer on one level; aim for range with depth.

## Self-test

1. What does "salient" mean in the specification's wording?
2. Name the six language levels.
3. Identify the head and the post-modifier in "a house with green shutters".
4. What is the difference between tense and aspect?
5. Give the sentence function of "Mind the gap".
6. Is "When the bell rang, everyone left." simple, compound or complex? Why?
7. What is a free morpheme? Give an example.
8. Name Grice's four maxims.
9. What is deixis? Give two examples.
10. What is anaphoric reference?
11. What is anchorage in graphology?
12. "Could you possibly close the window?" Which aspect of face does the speaker protect, and how?

### Answers

1. Prominent and significant: the features that do most to create the text's meanings.
2. Phonetics, phonology and prosodics; graphology; lexis and semantics; grammar, including morphology; pragmatics;
   discourse.
3. Head: "house"; post-modifier: the prepositional phrase "with green shutters".
4. Tense locates time (present or past); aspect shows whether an action is ongoing (progressive) or completed
   relative to a point (perfect).
5. Imperative.
6. Complex: it has a subordinate clause ("When the bell rang") and a main clause ("everyone left").
7. A morpheme that can stand alone as a word, for example "chair".
8. Quantity, quality, relation, manner.
9. Words whose meaning depends on the context of utterance, for example "here", "tomorrow", "you".
10. A word, usually a pronoun, referring back to something already mentioned.
11. The use of words, such as a caption, to fix the intended meaning of an image.
12. The hearer's negative face (freedom from imposition), by using an interrogative, the modal "could" and the
    hedge "possibly" to make the request indirect.
