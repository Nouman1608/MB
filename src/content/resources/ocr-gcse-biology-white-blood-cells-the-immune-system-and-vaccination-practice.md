## Questions

**1.** Platelets are not whole cells. State what platelets are and give one way they help to defend the body. **[2]**

**2.** Explain how a phagocyte is adapted to destroy pathogens. **[3]**

**3.** The trachea is lined with mucus-producing cells and ciliated cells.
**(a)** Describe how these cells help to protect the lungs from pathogens. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why this is called a non-specific defence. **[1]**

**4.** A patient's blood count is measured per mm³. Before infection the white blood cell count was 8000. During infection it was 14 400. After a treatment, the platelet count fell from 280 000 to 140 000.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in white blood cells. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage fall in platelets. **[2]**

**5.** A cut on a finger bleeds, then stops. Explain how the bleeding stops. **[4]**

**6.** A volunteer is given an injection of an antigen on day 0 and a second injection on day 28. The antibody concentration in the blood was measured (arbitrary units).

| Day | 0 | 7 | 14 | 21 | 28 | 31 | 35 | 42 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Antibody | 0 | 3 | 15 | 8 | 4 | 60 | 120 | 90 |

**(a)** State the day on which the antibody concentration peaked after the first injection. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times higher the peak after the second injection is than after the first. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the response to the second injection is faster and larger. **[3]**

**7.** **(a)** Explain how a vaccination against a disease protects a person from later infection. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why a measles vaccine does not protect against chickenpox. **[1]**

**8.** **(a)** A boy has a bacterial ear infection. Name the type of medicine a doctor would prescribe. **[1]**
**(b)** A man has influenza, caused by a virus. Explain why the doctor does not prescribe an antibiotic. **[2]**
**(c)** A nurse cleans a wound with iodine solution before covering it. Name this type of substance and state its purpose. **[2]**

**9.** A population of 5 000 000 bacteria is treated with an antibiotic that kills 99.8 % of them.
**(a)** Calculate the number of bacteria that survive. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the surviving bacteria could produce a population that is mostly resistant. **[3]**

**10.** Before a vaccination programme there were 12 400 cases of a disease in one year. Five years after it started there were 310 cases in one year.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease in cases. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest two reasons why cases did not fall to zero. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest why a booster dose may be recommended. **[1]**

**11.** A trial gave a new vaccine to 2000 children. 1920 were protected against the disease. 300 had a sore arm for one day. One child had a serious allergic reaction. Evaluate whether it is worthwhile to offer the vaccine to all children. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Platelets are small fragments of cells (they have no nucleus) [1]. They help blood to clot at a wound, which seals it and stops pathogens entering [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the fragment idea and a named function separately; "they fight germs" is too vague to link to a clot.

**2.** It can change shape / is flexible, so it can squeeze out of capillaries and engulf pathogens [1]. Its membrane folds around the pathogen to engulf it into a vacuole (phagocytosis) [1]. It has many lysosomes with enzymes that digest the pathogen [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* For adaptation questions, state the feature and what it allows; a list of features with no function leaves the explanation incomplete.

**3.** **(a)** Mucus traps pathogens and dust [1]. Cilia move (beat) in a sweeping action [1] and carry the mucus upwards to the throat so it is swallowed, and the acid in the stomach kills many microbes [1].
**(b)** It works against any type of pathogen, not one specific pathogen [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the structure and its action, such as "cilia sweep mucus upwards"; "keeps germs out" does not show how.

**4.** **(a)** 14 400 - 8000 = 6400 [1]; 6400 / 8000 x 100 = **80 %** [1].
**(b)** 280 000 - 140 000 = 140 000, and 140 000 / 280 000 x 100 = **50 %** [1] for the method and [1] for the answer. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the starting value. Show the change and the division clearly, so that your method can be followed even if the final figure is wrong.

**5.** Platelets stick to the damaged area and release chemicals [1]. Soluble fibrinogen is converted into insoluble fibrin [1]. Fibrin forms a mesh that traps red blood cells to make a clot [1]. The clot dries to a scab, which seals the cut, stopping blood loss and pathogens getting in [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name fibrinogen and fibrin the right way round; "the blood goes solid" is not enough for a four-mark explanation.

**6.** **(a)** Day 14 [1].
**(b)** 120 / 15 [1] = **8 times** [1].
**(c)** Memory cells were made after the first injection [1]. They recognise the antigen at once and divide quickly [1]. More lymphocytes make antibodies sooner than in the first response, giving a faster, higher peak [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Memory cells" is the expected idea. Quote figures when comparing the curves, for example time to peak (14 days against 7 days).

**7.** **(a)** The vaccine contains antigens from the pathogen in a safe form (dead, inactive or weakened, or parts of it) [1]. Lymphocytes with matching receptors divide and make antibodies [1]. Memory cells remain in the blood [1]. If the real pathogen infects later, memory cells give a rapid, large response and the person does not get ill [1].
**(b)** The antigens are different, so the antibodies and memory cells do not fit chickenpox [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A common slip is to say the vaccine contains antibodies. The vaccine supplies antigens and the person makes the antibodies.

**8.** **(a)** An antibiotic [1].
**(b)** Antibiotics act on bacteria and not on viruses [1], so they would not treat the infection (and would add to the risk of resistance) [1].
**(c)** An antiseptic [1], used to kill microorganisms on the skin / wound to prevent infection [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* An answer that only says "antibiotics do not work" gains little. State that the cause is a virus and the antibiotic acts on bacteria.

**9.** **(a)** 100 - 99.8 = 0.2 % survive [1]; 0.2 / 100 x 5 000 000 = **10 000** [1].
**(b)** Some bacteria carry a mutation that makes them resistant [1]. The antibiotic kills the non-resistant bacteria but the resistant ones survive [1] and reproduce, passing on the resistance, so most of the new population is resistant [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that resistance was there by mutation before the antibiotic was used. "Bacteria adapt to the drug" suggests the individual changes, which loses the link to natural selection.

**10.** **(a)** 12 400 - 310 = 12 090 [1]; 12 090 / 12 400 x 100 = **97.5 %** [1].
**(b)** Any two of: not everyone was vaccinated; the vaccine does not protect everyone; new people arrive who are not immune, for example visitors or newborns [1] [1].
**(c)** Boosters raise the number of memory cells and antibodies again, so the protection lasts [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Two separate reasons are needed for two marks. Repeating the same idea in different words gives only one reason.

**11.** Benefits: 1920 / 2000 x 100 = 96 % protected [1]. Protected children make memory cells and do not get the disease [1].
Risks: 300 / 2000 x 100 = 15 % had a short-lived sore arm, which is minor [1]. One child in 2000 had a serious reaction, which is rare [1].
Limits: 4 % (80 children) were not protected, so some risk remains [1].
Judgement: benefits outweigh risks, so the vaccine is worthwhile for most children, though children known to have allergies need medical advice [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluate answers do best with figures from the data, both sides, and a final judgement that follows from them.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "fight" or "attack" for white blood cells without engulfing, digesting or antibodies.
- Describing platelets as cells, or leaving out fibrin when explaining clotting.
- Forgetting that mucus is swept to the throat and swallowed, not coughed out only.
- Saying a vaccine contains antibodies instead of antigens.
- Not mentioning memory cells when asked about a faster second response.
- Dividing by the new value instead of the original in percentage change questions.
- Saying antibiotics treat viral infections or that bacteria "learn" resistance.
- Giving only one side in an evaluation question.
