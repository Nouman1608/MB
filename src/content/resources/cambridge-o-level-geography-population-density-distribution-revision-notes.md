Condensed notes for sub-topic 1.4 of Cambridge O Level Geography (2217), 2026 series.

## Two different ideas

```
population density = population / area      (people per km²)
```

- **Density** is a number (an average).
- **Distribution** is a pattern (where people are).
- Averages hide variation, so describe distribution from the map.
- Describing words: uneven, clustered, linear, dispersed, dense, sparse.

**Describing a map in four steps:** (1) overall pattern, e.g. uneven; (2) where it is dense, with
named places and compass directions; (3) where it is sparse; (4) a figure from the key or scale.
Leave the reasons out unless the question says explain.

## World pattern

- **Dense:** South Asia, East Asia, Western Europe, north-eastern USA, Nile valley, Java.
- **Sparse:** hot deserts (Sahara, Arabia, central Australia), cold lands (Siberia, northern
  Canada, Greenland, Antarctica), high mountains (Himalaya, Andes), dense rainforest (Amazon).

## Factors

**Physical**
- Relief: flat lowland (+) vs steep, high land (−).
- Climate: moderate, reliable rainfall (+) vs too hot, cold, dry or wet (−).
- Water: rivers, groundwater (+) vs none (−).
- Soils: alluvial or volcanic (+) vs thin, leached, saline (−).
- Vegetation: open land (+) vs dense forest (−).
- Resources: minerals, fuels, fish (+).

**Human**
- Economic: jobs in industry and services, ports, trade.
- Transport and accessibility.
- Political: capital cities, planned schemes (Brasília, 1960), stability vs conflict.
- Historical: long-settled farming regions and old cities.
- **Technology** (irrigation, desalination, air conditioning) can overcome physical limits.

## Case studies

**Pakistan**
- Dense: Punjab plains and Indus valley; flat, fertile alluvium, irrigation, Lahore and
  Faisalabad. Punjab holds about half of the population. Karachi: coastal port and industry.
- Sparse: Balochistan (largest province by area, well under a tenth of the people): dry, rugged,
  little water, remote. Northern mountains: steep, cold, short growing season; people in valleys.

**Egypt**: most people in the Nile valley and delta, only a few per cent of the land; the desert is
almost empty.

**Brazil**: coastal south-east dense; Amazon interior sparse; Brasília built inland.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Population density | Average number of people per km² |
| Population distribution | The way people are spread across an area |
| Densely populated | Many people per km² |
| Sparsely populated | Few people per km² |
| Choropleth map | Map shading areas by density category |
| Dot map | Map in which each dot represents a fixed number of people |
| Physical factor | A natural feature of the environment, e.g. relief or climate |
| Human factor | A feature created by people, e.g. jobs, transport, government policy |

## Exam traps

- No unit for density.
- Explaining when asked to describe.
- "Good climate" with no detail.
- Only physical or only human factors.
- Forgetting dense pockets in sparse regions (oases, mines, valleys).

## Self-test

1. A district has 450,000 people on 1,500 km². Calculate its density.
2. Give two physical reasons why the Sahara is sparsely populated.
3. Name one human factor that explains Karachi's large population.
4. Why is an average density figure misleading for Egypt?
5. How can technology change population distribution?

**Answers:** 1. 450,000 ÷ 1,500 = **300 people per km²**. 2. Any two: very low rainfall, lack of
fresh water, extreme daytime heat, poor soils, little vegetation. 3. Any one: its role as the main
port; industry and jobs; trade links. 4. Almost everyone lives on a small area near the Nile, so the
average combines very dense and almost empty areas. 5. Irrigation, desalination and air
conditioning allow dense settlement in places that were too dry or hot, for example cities in the
Gulf states.
