These questions cover **3.2.3.2 Uses of chlorine and chlorate(I)** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification: the reactions of chlorine with water (forming chloride and chlorate(I), and forming chloride and oxygen), chlorine in water treatment, how society weighs chemicals added to water, and the reaction of chlorine with cold, dilute sodium hydroxide. This is International AS content, assessed in Unit 1. Mr values: Cl₂ 71.0, O₂ 32.0, NaCl 58.5, NaClO 74.5.

## Section A

**1.** State the oxidation state of chlorine in the ClO⁻ ion and name the compound NaClO. **[2]**

**2.** Define the term *disproportionation*. **[1]**

**3.** Write an ionic equation for the reaction of chlorine with water to form chloride ions and chlorate(I) ions. **[1]**

**4.** State why chlorine is added to drinking water. **[1]**

**5.** Write an equation for the reaction of chlorine with cold, dilute sodium hydroxide, and give one use of the solution formed. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why chlorine water kept in a clear bottle on a sunny windowsill gradually stops working as a bleach. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** In sunlight, chlorine reacts with water: 2Cl₂ + 2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4Cl⁻ + O₂.

**(a)** Give the oxidation state of chlorine before and after the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Give the oxidation state of oxygen before and after the reaction. **[1]**

**(c)** State, with a reason, whether this is a disproportionation reaction. **[1]**

**8.** Evaluate the use of chlorine to treat drinking water. **[4]**

**9.** A few drops of universal indicator are added to freshly made chlorine water. Describe and explain what is seen. **[3]**

**10.** A manufacturer bubbles 3.55 g of chlorine into excess cold, dilute sodium hydroxide and makes the solution up to 250 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in moles, of chlorine used. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the concentration of sodium chlorate(I) in the solution. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the mass of sodium chloride also formed. **[1]**

**11.** A sample of chlorine water containing 0.284 g of dissolved chlorine is left in sunlight until all the chlorine has reacted to form chloride ions and oxygen.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of oxygen released. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the amount, in moles, of H⁺ ions formed. **[1]**

**12.** Explain why much more chlorate(I) is formed when chlorine reacts with sodium hydroxide than when it reacts with water. **[2]**

**13.** A town council is deciding whether to add a new chemical to the public water supply. Suggest two factors, other than cost, that it should weigh up. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **+1** [1]; **sodium chlorate(I)** [1]

**2.** A reaction in which the same element is simultaneously oxidised and reduced [1]

**3.** **Cl₂ + H₂O ⇌ 2H⁺ + Cl⁻ + ClO⁻** [1]

**4.** To kill bacteria (micro-organisms) and so prevent water-borne disease [1]

**5.** **Cl₂ + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaClO + H₂O** [1]; used as bleach (or as a disinfectant) [1]

**6.** In sunlight, chlorine reacts with water to form chloride ions and oxygen, 2Cl₂ + 2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4Cl⁻ + O₂ [1]; the chlorine (and the chloric(I) acid in equilibrium with it) is used up, so there is nothing left to bleach [1]

*Examiner insight:* Name the products of the sunlight reaction. "The chlorine evaporates" does not earn the marks.

**7. (a)** **0** in Cl₂ and **−1** in Cl⁻ [1]

**(b)** **−2** in H₂O and **0** in O₂ [1]

**(c)** **No**: chlorine is only reduced; a different element (oxygen) is oxidised [1]

**8.** Benefit: kills bacteria and prevents serious water-borne diseases such as cholera [1]; benefit: some chlorine remains in the water, protecting it in the pipes [1]; risk: chlorine is toxic, and it can form potentially harmful chlorinated organic compounds [1]; conclusion: the doses are small and the health benefits outweigh the risks [1]

*Examiner insight:* "Evaluate" requires a judgement. A list of points with no conclusion cannot reach full marks.

**9.** The indicator turns red [1]; because chlorine water contains acids (HCl and HClO) [1]; then it turns colourless, because chloric(I) acid (HClO / ClO⁻) bleaches it [1]

**10. (a)** n(Cl₂) = 3.55 / 71.0 = **0.0500 mol** [1]

**(b)** n(NaClO) = 0.0500 mol; concentration = 0.0500 / 0.250 = **0.200 mol dm⁻³** [1]

**(c)** n(NaCl) = 0.0500 mol; mass = 0.0500 × 58.5 = **2.93 g** [1]

*Examiner insight:* One mole of chlorine gives one mole of each salt, because one chlorine atom goes to −1 and the other to +1.

**11. (a)** n(Cl₂) = 0.284 / 71.0 = 4.00 × 10⁻³ mol; n(O₂) = 2.00 × 10⁻³ mol (2 : 1) [1]; mass = 2.00 × 10⁻³ × 32.0 = **0.0640 g** [1]

**(b)** n(H⁺) = 2 × 4.00 × 10⁻³ = **8.00 × 10⁻³ mol** (4H⁺ per 2Cl₂) [1]

**12.** The reaction with water is a reversible equilibrium that lies to the left, so little ClO⁻ forms [1]; in NaOH, the hydroxide ions remove H⁺, so the reaction goes to completion and all the chlorine is converted to Cl⁻ and ClO⁻ [1]

*Examiner insight:* Refer to the position of equilibrium, not just "NaOH is more reactive than water".

**13.** Any two of these: the health benefit and how many people it helps [1]; the risk or toxicity at the dose used, including any by-products [1]. Also accept: that individuals cannot choose to opt out of a public supply; whether alternative methods would work as well.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling the sunlight reaction disproportionation because chlorine is involved.
- Writing NaClO₃ for sodium chlorate(I).
- Leaving "cold" and "dilute" out of the conditions for the NaOH reaction.
- Giving only the red colour for universal indicator in chlorine water.
