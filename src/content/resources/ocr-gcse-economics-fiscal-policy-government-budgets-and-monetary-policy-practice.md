## Questions

**1.** State whether each tax is direct or indirect: (i) a tax on a firm's profits, (ii) a duty charged on each litre of fuel, (iii) national insurance contributions. **[3]**

**2.** Island C's government plans these figures for one year (billions of cu).

| Spending | cu | Revenue | cu |
|---|---|---|---|
| Health | 50 | Income tax | 64 |
| Education | 36 | National insurance | 30 |
| Pensions and benefits | 52 | VAT | 41 |
| Transport | 16 | Excise duties | 14 |
| Debt interest | 9 | Corporation tax | 16 |
| Other | 15 | Other | 5 |

**(a)** Calculate the budget balance and state whether it is a surplus or a deficit. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of revenue from indirect taxes, to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**3.** Island C has an income tax of 0% on the first 10,000 cu, 25% on income from 10,000 to 30,000 cu and 45% on income above 30,000 cu.
**(a)** Calculate the tax paid and the average tax rate (to 1 decimal place) for someone earning 42,000 cu. **[3]**
**(b)** Show that the tax is progressive by comparing with someone earning 20,000 cu. **[2]**

**4.** In a fictional market for headphones, price is in cu.

| Price | Quantity demanded | Quantity supplied | Quantity supplied if a 5 cu tax is added |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 400 | 400 | 100 |
| 11 | 360 | 460 | 160 |
| 12 | 320 | 520 | 220 |
| 13 | 280 | 580 | 280 |
| 14 | 240 | 640 | 340 |

**(a)** State the equilibrium price and quantity before the tax. **[1]**
**(b)** After a 5 cu tax on each unit, state what buyers now pay and how many are bought. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the government's tax revenue and the amount of it that falls on buyers. **[2]**

**5.** Country D's government spends 120 million cu on a rail link. Households re-spend 75% of any extra income. Calculate the total rise in income. **[3]**

**6.** During a recession Country D borrows 60 million cu to build a motorway.
**(a)** Explain what is meant by the opportunity cost of this decision. **[2]**
**(b)** Give one benefit and one cost of using borrowing to fund it. **[2]**

**7.** Analyse how a cut in income tax could raise economic growth and reduce unemployment. **[6]**

**8.** Evaluate whether progressive taxes are the best way to redistribute income. **[8]**

**9.** A central bank raises its interest rate.
**(a)** Explain what is meant by monetary policy. **[2]**
**(b)** An interest-only mortgage of 200,000 cu rises from 3% to 5%. Calculate the extra interest paid in a year. **[3]**
**(c)** A saver earns 2.5% while inflation is 1.5%. Calculate the approximate real rate of return. **[2]**

**10.** Analyse how a rise in interest rates could affect price stability and economic growth. **[6]**

**11.** Evaluate the effects of a cut in interest rates on consumers and businesses. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** (i) Direct, because it is paid directly from profit [1]. (ii) Indirect, because it is added to the price of a good [1]. (iii) Direct, because it is paid from earnings [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Give a one-word classification for each part. A short reason helps to avoid confusing the type of tax with who finally pays it.

**2. (a)** Spending = 50 + 36 + 52 + 16 + 9 + 15 = 178 [1]. Revenue = 64 + 30 + 41 + 14 + 16 + 5 = 170, so the balance is 170 − 178 = −8, a **deficit of 8 billion cu** [1].
**(b)** Indirect taxes = 41 + 14 = 55 [1]. 55 ÷ 170 × 100 = **32.4%** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Say whether it is a surplus or deficit as well as giving the number, and use the correct taxes. National insurance and corporation tax are not in the indirect total.

**3. (a)** Income in the 25% band = 30,000 − 10,000 = 20,000, so tax = 5,000 [1]. Income in the 45% band = 42,000 − 30,000 = 12,000, so tax = 5,400 [1]. Total = **10,400 cu**, and 10,400 ÷ 42,000 × 100 = **24.8%** [1].
**(b)** On 20,000 cu: (20,000 − 10,000) × 0.25 = 2,500 cu, which is 12.5% [1]. The average rate rises from 12.5% to 24.8% as income rises, so the tax is progressive [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show the tax band by band. Applying 45% to the whole income is the usual error, and a progressive claim needs average rates compared.

**4. (a)** Demand equals supply at price **10 cu** and quantity **400** [1].
**(b)** With the tax, demand equals the new supply at price 13 [1] and quantity 280, so buyers pay **13 cu** and buy **280** [1].
**(c)** Revenue = 5 × 280 = **1,400 cu** [1]. Buyers pay 3 cu more per unit, so their share = 3 × 280 = **840 cu** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Use the new quantity (280), not the old one, for revenue. Sellers keep 13 − 5 = 8 cu, so their share is 560 cu.

**5.** Multiplier = 1 ÷ (1 − 0.75) = 1 ÷ 0.25 = 4 [1]. Total rise = 120 × 4 [1] = **480 million cu** [1].
*Examiner insight:* State the formula and substitute into it. Give units, and note that saving, taxes and imports would reduce the real effect.

**6. (a)** Opportunity cost is the next best alternative given up [1]. Here the 60 million cu cannot be spent on another use, such as hospitals or schools [1].
**(b)** Benefit: demand and jobs rise during the recession [1]. Cost: higher debt and interest payments in future [1].
*Examiner insight:* Link opportunity cost to the choice in the question. A generic definition on its own is thin.

**7.** Lower income tax raises disposable income [1]. Households can spend more, so consumption rises [1]. Aggregate demand rises [1]. Firms produce more, so output and growth increase [1]. They need more workers to meet demand [1], so unemployment falls [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Build a chain with each link causing the next. Mention of spare capacity or inflation is useful evaluation, but this command is "analyse".

**8.** For: higher earners pay a higher average rate, so incomes after tax are more equal [1], and the revenue pays for benefits and services that help low earners [1]. Low-income households tend to spend a large share of extra income, which supports demand [1]. Against: very high rates may reduce effort or encourage avoidance [1], and skilled workers might move abroad [1]. Other methods such as benefits, free services or a minimum wage may target need more directly [1]. Judgement: progressive taxes are an effective part of redistribution at moderate rates, but they work best alongside spending, since too steep a rate may lower revenue [2]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A balanced answer ends with a supported judgement that says when the policy works best, not just "it depends".

**9. (a)** Monetary policy is the use of interest rates (and the money supply) by the central bank [1] to influence spending, inflation and growth [1].
**(b)** 200,000 × 0.03 = 6,000 [1]. 200,000 × 0.05 = 10,000 [1]. Extra = **4,000 cu a year** [1].
**(c)** Real rate is approximately 2.5 − 1.5 [1] = **1.0%** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show both interest figures before subtracting, and use the word "approximately" for the real rate.

**10.** Higher rates make borrowing dearer [1] and saving more rewarding [1]. Consumption and investment fall [1]. Aggregate demand falls [1], so price rises slow down and the economy moves towards price stability [1]. The cost is slower growth, as lower output may reduce jobs [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Cover both parts of the question. An answer on inflation alone leaves part of the question unanswered.

**11.** Consumers: borrowing is cheaper, so households with loans have lower repayments [1] and spend more [1]. Savers earn less, so those relying on interest lose income [1]. Businesses: borrowing costs fall, so more projects look profitable and investment rises [2]. Limits: the effect depends on confidence, since firms may not invest if sales look weak [1], and banks may not pass on the cut. Higher demand may also raise inflation [1]. Judgement: a cut helps borrowers and firms most, and is stronger when confidence is good and spare capacity exists [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Separate consumers from businesses and include both winners and losers. A one-sided answer can still be well developed, but it leaves out the evaluation.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the old quantity instead of the new one to find tax revenue or subsidy cost.
- Charging the top tax rate on a whole income instead of the band only.
- Mixing up a deficit with the national debt.
- Stopping a chain at "spending rises" without reaching output, jobs or prices.
- Answering "evaluate" with a list of points and no judgement.
- Saying savers gain when rates fall.
- Forgetting units such as cu, million or per year.
- Giving a definition of opportunity cost without applying it.
