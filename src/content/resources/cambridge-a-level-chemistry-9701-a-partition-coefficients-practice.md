## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State what is meant by the term *partition coefficient*. **[2]**

**2.** Which solute would have the largest value of Kpc(hexane/water)?

A: sodium chloride

B: ethanol

C: iodine

D: ammonia

**[1]**

**3.** Kpc(ether/water) = 4.0 for solute Q. 10.0 g of Q is shaken with equal volumes of ether
and water until equilibrium is reached. What mass of Q is in the ether layer?

A: 2.0 g

B: 2.5 g

C: 8.0 g

D: 7.5 g

**[1]**

**4.** Explain why a partition coefficient has no units, and why the order of the solvents must
always be stated. **[2]**

**5.** Ethanoic acid molecules form hydrogen-bonded pairs (dimers) in some non-polar solvents but
exist as single molecules in water. Explain why a simple partition coefficient cannot be used for
ethanoic acid between such a solvent and water. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** Iodine is shaken with cyclohexane and water until equilibrium is reached. A 5.00 cm³
sample of the cyclohexane layer needs 12.00 cm³ of 0.0400 mol dm⁻³ sodium thiosulfate. A
50.0 cm³ sample of the aqueous layer needs 6.00 cm³ of 0.00500 mol dm⁻³ sodium thiosulfate.
(I₂ + 2S₂O₃²⁻ → 2I⁻ + S₄O₆²⁻)

**(a)** Calculate the concentration of iodine in each layer. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate Kpc(cyclohexane/water). **[1]**

**7.** 3.00 g of compound Z is dissolved in 150 cm³ of water. The solution is shaken with
50.0 cm³ of an organic solvent. Kpc(organic/water) = 9.0. Calculate the mass of Z extracted
into the organic solvent. **[3]**

**8.** The extraction in Question 7 is repeated with fresh solution, but the 50.0 cm³ of organic
solvent is used as two separate 25.0 cm³ portions.

**(a)** Calculate the total mass of Z extracted. **[3]**

**(b)** Compare your answer with Question 7 and state what this shows about extraction
technique. **[1]**

**9.** Kpc(organic/water) = 15 for a compound dissolved in 100 cm³ of water. Calculate the volume
of organic solvent needed to extract 95% of the compound in a single extraction. **[2]**

**10.** 2.40 g of a solute is shaken with 40.0 cm³ of hexane and 60.0 cm³ of water. At
equilibrium, the aqueous layer contains 0.30 g of the solute. Calculate Kpc(hexane/water).
**[3]**

**11.** Predict the order of Kpc(hexane/water) values for methanol, butan-1-ol and iodine, from
smallest to largest. Explain your order in terms of the polarity of each solute. **[3]**

**12.** A chemist wants to extract a polar organic compound from water. She replaces hexane with
a more polar organic solvent that still forms a separate layer with water. Predict and explain
the effect on Kpc(organic/water) for the compound. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The ratio of the concentrations of a solute in two immiscible solvents [1] when
equilibrium has been reached, at a stated temperature [1].

**2.** **C** [1]. Iodine is non-polar and forms only instantaneous dipole–induced dipole forces,
which it can form with hexane.

**3.** **C** [1]. x ÷ (10.0 − x) = 4.0, so x = 8.0 g.

**4.** It is a ratio of two concentrations measured in the same units, so the units cancel [1].
Kpc(A/B) is the reciprocal of Kpc(B/A), so the value has no meaning unless the order is given
[1].

**5.** The solute must be the same species in both solvents for the ratio to be constant [1].
Dimerisation means the species in the non-polar layer (dimers) differ from those in water
(single molecules), so the simple ratio of concentrations changes with concentration [1].

**6. (a)** Cyclohexane: mol S₂O₃²⁻ = 0.01200 × 0.0400 = 4.80 × 10⁻⁴; mol I₂ = 2.40 × 10⁻⁴ [1];
[I₂] = 2.40 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 0.00500 = **0.0480 mol dm⁻³** [1]. Water: mol S₂O₃²⁻ = 3.00 × 10⁻⁵; mol
I₂ = 1.50 × 10⁻⁵; [I₂] = 1.50 × 10⁻⁵ ÷ 0.0500 = **3.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³** [1].

**(b)** Kpc = 0.0480 ÷ 3.00 × 10⁻⁴ = **160** [1].

**7.** Let x g be extracted: 9.0 = (x ÷ 50.0) ÷ ((3.00 − x) ÷ 150) [1]. So 9.0 = 3x ÷ (3.00 − x),
giving 27.0 − 9.0x = 3x [1] and x = **2.25 g** [1].

**8. (a)** For each 25.0 cm³ portion: 9.0 = (x ÷ 25.0) ÷ ((m − x) ÷ 150), so 9.0(m − x) = 6x and
x = 0.60m [1]. First: 0.60 × 3.00 = 1.80 g, leaving 1.20 g [1]. Second: 0.60 × 1.20 = 0.72 g;
total = **2.52 g** [1].

**(b)** More is extracted (2.52 g against 2.25 g): several smaller portions are more efficient
than one large portion of the same total volume [1].

**9.** 15 = (0.95 ÷ V) ÷ (0.05 ÷ 100) [1]; V = 95 ÷ 0.75 = **127 cm³** [1].

**10.** Mass in hexane = 2.40 − 0.30 = 2.10 g [1]. [hexane] = 2.10 ÷ 40.0 = 0.0525 g cm⁻³;
[water] = 0.30 ÷ 60.0 = 0.0050 g cm⁻³ [1]. Kpc = 0.0525 ÷ 0.0050 = **10.5** [1].

**11.** Methanol < butan-1-ol < iodine [1]. Methanol is small and polar and hydrogen bonds
strongly with water, so it stays mainly in water [1]. Butan-1-ol also hydrogen bonds but has a
longer non-polar chain, so it partitions more into hexane; iodine is non-polar and forms only
instantaneous dipole–induced dipole forces, so it strongly favours hexane [1].

**12.** Kpc(organic/water) increases [1]. The compound's polarity is closer to that of the new
solvent, so it forms stronger intermolecular forces with it and more of the compound moves into
the organic layer [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving "immiscible" or "at equilibrium" out of the definition.
- Treating the two volumes as equal when they are not.
- Forgetting the 1 : 2 ratio of iodine to thiosulfate.
- Using the starting mass, not the mass remaining, for the second extraction.
- Giving Kpc with units, or without stating which solvent is on top.
- Writing "like dissolves like" without naming the intermolecular forces involved.
