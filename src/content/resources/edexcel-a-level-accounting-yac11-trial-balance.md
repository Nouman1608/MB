## Step 1: balance each ledger account

A trial balance lists **balances**, not transactions, so every account is balanced first. Total each side, put the difference on the smaller side as *balance c/d*, then bring it down on the opposite side as *balance b/d* on the first day of the next period. The side of the balance b/d decides the trial balance column.

**Example: bank account of Corrin Print, March 2026**

| Dr | $ | Cr | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Balance b/d | 2,640 | Trade payables | 4,980 |
| Trade receivables | 6,310 | Wages | 3,750 |
| Revenue (cash sales) | 1,420 | Drawings | 900 |
| Balance c/d | 670 | Rent | 1,410 |
| | **11,040** | | **11,040** |
| | | Balance b/d | 670 |

Receipts total 10,370 and payments total 11,040, so payments exceed receipts by 670. The balance b/d sits on the **credit** side: the business has an overdraft of 670 and it goes in the credit column. Reading the side wrongly is the most common reason a bank balance ends up in the wrong column.

## Step 2: put each balance in the right column

The rule comes from double entry: a debit balance is an asset, an expense or drawings; a credit balance is a liability, income or capital. The items below cause most of the errors.

| Item | Column | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Sales returns (returns inwards) | Debit | Reduces revenue, so it sits opposite revenue |
| Purchases returns (returns outwards) | Credit | Reduces purchases |
| Carriage inwards and carriage outwards | Debit | Both are expenses |
| Allowance for irrecoverable debts | Credit | Reduces the asset trade receivables |
| Provision for depreciation | Credit | Reduces the non-current asset |
| Rent received, commission received | Credit | Income |
| Bank overdraft | Credit | Money owed to the bank |
| Opening inventory | Debit | An asset brought forward from last year |
| Closing inventory | Not in the trial balance | Recorded only when the financial statements are prepared, so it is usually given as a note below the trial balance |

Closing inventory of 5,100 is shown as a note: it will be used in the cost of sales and in the statement of financial position.

**Checking the arithmetic.** The draft debit total was 185,760. Remove 5,100 (closing inventory), 900, 400, 1,750 and 280 (moved to credit), and add 1,300 (sales returns): 185,760 − 8,430 + 1,300 = 178,630. The draft credit total was 176,600. Add 3,330 (the four items moved in) and remove 1,300: 178,630. Moving a balance from one column to the other changes the **difference** between the totals by twice that balance, which is why one misplaced item of 400 creates a gap of 800.

## Finding a missing figure

When one balance is missing, usually capital, it is the amount that makes the totals agree. Suppose capital were not given above: the debit total is 178,630 and the credits other than capital total 131,930, so capital is 178,630 − 131,930 = 46,700. Show this subtraction: a figure that simply appears in the table gives no evidence of method.

## 1.2.2 What a trial balance does and does not prove

**What it does.** It checks the **arithmetic** of the double entry: every debit has been matched by an equal credit, and the accounts have been balanced and listed correctly. It brings the balances together in one list, which is the starting point for the statement of profit or loss and the statement of financial position. If it disagrees, it tells you that an error exists.

**What it cannot do.** These are the limitations to explain, each with an example:

1. **Some errors leave it agreeing.** Omission, commission, principle, original entry, complete reversal and compensating errors all put equal amounts on both sides. A cheque from a customer credited to the wrong customer's account, for example, still balances.
2. **It does not locate errors.** A difference shows that something is wrong, not where. A difference of 450 could be a one-sided entry of 450, a transposition (1,270 posted as 1,720) or several errors combined.
3. **It is not a financial statement.** It does not show profit, net assets or liquidity. Users need the statement of profit or loss and the statement of financial position for that.
4. **It is drawn up before year-end adjustments.** Accruals, prepayments, depreciation for the year and irrecoverable debts not yet written off are not in it, so the balances are not yet the figures that will be reported.
5. **It covers one date only.** It says nothing about the pattern of transactions during the period.

**Annotated model answer.** *Explain one limitation of a trial balance.* (2 marks)

> A trial balance can agree even when the books contain errors, because some errors put equal amounts on the debit and credit sides. **[point]** For example, if repairs of $900 are debited to the motor van account instead of repairs, both totals are still correct, but non-current assets are overstated and profit is overstated by $900. **[linked development]**

The second sentence earns the mark for *explain* because it shows the consequence. Naming "error of principle" alone would only identify the limitation.

## Common mistakes

- Putting a bank balance in the debit column without checking which side the balance b/d is on.
- Listing closing inventory in the trial balance alongside opening inventory.
- Treating sales returns as income or purchases returns as an expense.
- Putting the allowance for irrecoverable debts or provision for depreciation in the debit column because they "belong to" an asset.
- Inventing a balancing figure other than the one the question names, to force totals to agree.
- Writing "the trial balance proves the accounts are correct" in an evaluation.

## Exam technique for the command words

- **Prepare** (a trial balance): use the standard format, a heading with the business name and date ("Trial balance at 31 December 2025"), two columns, and totals that agree.
- **State** (a purpose): one precise point, such as "to check the arithmetical accuracy of the double entry".
- **Explain** (a limitation): the point and its consequence, as in the model answer.
- **Evaluate** (the usefulness of a trial balance): give what it achieves and what it cannot reveal, and finish with a judgement, for example that it is a useful first check but must be supported by control accounts and review of postings.
