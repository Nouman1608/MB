An overview of how AQA GCSE History (8145) is put together, for students
starting the course and for anyone deciding whether it is the right
specification for them.

## Two components

AQA organises GCSE History subject content into two components:

- **Understanding the Modern World** — study of the wider world and how
change happens over time.
- **Shaping the Nation** — study focused more specifically on Britain and
national development.

Within these components, schools choose from AQA's set of period studies,
wider-world depth studies, thematic studies and British depth studies —
so two schools both teaching AQA GCSE History may cover genuinely
different content depending on which specific studies they select, while
still being assessed on the same specification.

## The historic environment study

One required element is a **historic environment study**, based on a
specified list of historic sites tied to whichever British depth study a
school has chosen. AQA refreshes the specified sites periodically for
each two-year examination cycle, so the exact sites in scope depend on
your examination series — always confirm the current list with your
teacher or AQA directly rather than relying on a previous year's list.

## Why the subject is structured this way

AQA designed the specification so students engage with key issues such as
conflict, and understand what drives change and how the past continues to
influence the present — building on the historical skills introduced at
Key Stage 3, and preparing students who go on to study History further.

## Study approach

Because the specific period, depth and thematic studies your school
teaches are chosen by the school (not fixed nationally), the single most
important first step is confirming exactly which studies you are being
examined on — revision guides and past papers are only useful once you
know which specific studies apply to you. From there, the recurring
skills AQA GCSE History rewards are: using sources as evidence,
explaining causation and consequence, and analysing change and continuity
over time — all worth practising against whichever specific content your
course covers, since these skills transfer across every period and
depth study AQA offers.
