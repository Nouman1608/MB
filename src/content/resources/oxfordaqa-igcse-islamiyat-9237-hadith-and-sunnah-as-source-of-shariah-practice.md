## Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** What is meant by the term Sunnah? **[1]**

**2.** Which source of authority comes first in Islam, the Qur'an or the Sunnah? **[1]**

**3.** Give **two** examples of things that the Qur'an commands but the Sunnah explains in detail. **[2]**

**4.** The Prophet (pbuh) approved of Mu'adh ibn Jabal (RA) judging by the Qur'an, then the Sunnah, then his own reasoning.

**(a)** State **one** way in which this shows the position of the Sunnah. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain **one** reason why Muslims follow the Sunnah. **[3]**

**5.** Explain **two** differences between the Hadith and the Sunnah. **[4]**

**6.** Explain **two** ways in which the Sunnah helps Muslims in their daily worship. **[4]**

## Section B: longer-answer questions

**7.** Explain **two** reasons why the Qur'an tells Muslims to obey the Prophet (pbuh).
Apply **one** teaching from a source of authority in your answer. **[6]**

**8.** Explain **two** ways in which the character of the Prophet (pbuh) is a model for Muslims.
Apply **one** teaching from a source of authority in your answer. **[6]**

**9.**

> **Qur'an 53:1-6**
>

'Muslims can live by the Qur'an alone without needing the Hadith.'

Examine why this statement is significant for Muslims.

In your answer you should:

## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. In the 4-mark questions, one mark for each point identified and one for explaining it. The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks on that pattern plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general, accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The 12-mark question is marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

**1.** The way or practice of the Prophet (pbuh): his example in words, actions and approvals [1].

**2.** The Qur'an [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: how to pray (number and form of rak'ahs); the nisab and rates of Zakah; the rites of Hajj; the details of fasting.

**4. (a)** Any one [1]: it is the second source after the Qur'an; it is used when the Qur'an does not give a direct answer; reasoning comes only after the Sunnah.

**(b)** One reason identified [1] and explained [2].

- **The Qur'an commands it** [1]: "Obey Allah and obey the Messenger" (4:59) [1], so following the Sunnah is part of obeying Allah [1].
- **He is the model** [1]: the Qur'an calls him an excellent pattern (33:21) [1], so Muslims copy his behaviour [1].
- **It explains the Qur'an** [1]: the Sunnah shows how to pray, give Zakah and perform Hajj [1], which the Qur'an commands without describing in full [1].

**5.** For each of two differences: difference identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Nature** [1]: a Hadith is a report, while the Sunnah is the practice itself [1].
- **Form** [1]: a Hadith is a text with a chain of narrators, while the Sunnah is a way of life [1].
- **Grading** [1]: Hadith are graded by reliability, while the Sunnah is what the sound Hadith establish [1].
- **Content** [1]: a Hadith may describe his sayings, actions or approvals, while the Sunnah is the pattern they form [1].

**6.** For each of two ways: way identified [1] and explained [1].

- **How to pray** [1]: the Prophet (pbuh) said "Pray as you have seen me praying", so the details of Salah come from his example [1].
- **How to fast and break the fast** [1]: the Sunnah gives the timings, the pre-dawn meal and breaking the fast promptly [1].
- **How to perform Hajj** [1]: "Take your rites from me" means pilgrims follow the way he performed it [1].
- **Extra worship** [1]: it shows voluntary prayers and remembrance that bring a Muslim closer to Allah [1].

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- **He is the Messenger of Allah** [1]: obeying him is part of obeying Allah [1].
- **He explains the revelation** [1]: he was sent to make the message clear to the people [1].
- **He is a model** [1]: his behaviour shows how to apply the Qur'an in daily life [1].
- **It leads to Allah's love** [1]: following him is linked to being loved and forgiven by Allah [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "He who obeys the Messenger has obeyed Allah" (Qur'an 4:80) [2].
- "If you should love Allah, then follow me, [so] Allah will love you" (Qur'an 3:31) [2].
- "Whatever the Messenger has given you, take it" (Qur'an 59:7) [2].

**8.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Ways (AO1):

- **Honesty** [1]: he was known as the trustworthy, and Muslims copy his truthfulness [1].
- **Kindness and mercy** [1]: he was gentle with family, neighbours, children and enemies, and Muslims follow his compassion [1].
- **Patience** [1]: he bore hardship without bitterness, which teaches Muslims to be steadfast [1].
- **Justice** [1]: he applied the same rule to the powerful and the weak, which guides fair dealing [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "There has certainly been for you in the Messenger of Allah an excellent pattern" (Qur'an 33:21) [2].
- Aisha (RA) said that his character was the Qur'an [2].

**9.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** the Qur'an is complete (5:3) and protected (15:9); it is the word of Allah, while Hadith were collected later; Hadith include weak and false reports that need grading.
- **Against the statement:** the Qur'an orders obedience to the Messenger (4:59); the passage says he "does not speak from [his own] inclination", which many scholars use to support the authority of his teaching; the Qur'an does not give the detail of prayer or Zakah; the Hadith of Mu'adh shows the Sunnah as the second source.
- **Balanced view:** the Qur'an is the foundation, and the authentic Sunnah shows how to apply it; Muslims need both, with Hadith checked for reliability.

*Use of the passage*

- "Has not strayed, nor has he erred" supports trusting his guidance.
- "Nor does he speak from [his own] inclination" supports his religious teaching as guided.
- "It is not but a revelation revealed" shows the divine origin of the message; some scholars apply this to his explanation of it.
- Credit candidates who note that scholars differ on whether this passage covers the Sunnah.

Level 4 answers use the passage directly, argue both sides with named verses and examples, and reach a reasoned judgement.
