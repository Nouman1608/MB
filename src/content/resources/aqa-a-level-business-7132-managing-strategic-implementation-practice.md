These questions cover section 3.10.3 managing strategic implementation of the AQA A-level Business (7132) specification. All businesses and figures are invented. Revise first with the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-managing-strategic-implementation/) and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-managing-strategic-implementation-revision-notes/).

Questions 6 to 10 use this data. Halbury Foods is launching a new product range. The activities, durations in weeks and dependencies are:

| Activity | Duration | Must follow |
|---|---|---|
| A: Market research | 2 | none |
| B: Develop recipes | 5 | none |
| C: Design packaging | 4 | A |
| D: Source ingredients | 3 | B |
| E: Set up production line | 6 | A, B |
| F: Print packaging | 2 | C, D |
| G: Trial production | 3 | E |
| H: Launch | 2 | F, G |

## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** An activity has an earliest start time of 5 weeks, a duration of 3 weeks and a latest finish time of 12 weeks. What is its total float? **[1]**

A: 2 weeks

B: 4 weeks

C: 7 weeks

D: 9 weeks

**2.** Which **one** of the following is most likely to suit an autocratic leadership style? **[1]**

A: A team of highly skilled specialists in a research laboratory

B: An urgent crisis requiring a quick decision

C: A creative design team seeking new ideas

D: A long-term plan that depends on staff commitment

**3.** Define "critical path". **[2]**

**4.** State **two** reasons why communication is important when implementing a new strategy. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** problem a tall organisational structure can cause for strategic implementation. **[3]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**6.** Calculate the earliest start time for each of activities A to H. **[4]**

**7.** Calculate the minimum time to complete the project, and identify the critical path. **[4]**

**8.** Calculate the total float for activities A, C, D and F. **[4]**

**9.** Activity E takes 9 weeks instead of 6. State the new project duration and explain what has happened to the critical path. **[3]**

**10.** Halbury can reduce trial production (G) to 2 weeks by hiring extra staff. Explain whether this would speed up the launch, and by how much, assuming E takes 6 weeks. **[3]**

**11.** Analyse why a democratic leadership style might help Halbury implement its new strategy, and one reason why it might not. **[6]**

**12.** Evaluate the usefulness of network analysis for a business implementing a major strategy. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. Total float = LFT − duration − EST = 12 − 3 − 5 = 4 weeks. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. In a crisis speed matters and an autocratic style allows quick decisions. **[1]**

**3.** The longest path through the network [1], which determines the shortest time in which the project can be completed (activities on it have no float) [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: it explains the strategy and reasons to staff [1]; it reduces uncertainty and resistance [1]; it co-ordinates departments [1]; it gives feedback on progress and problems [1]. **[2]**

**5.** In a tall structure, messages pass through many layers [1], so they are slow and may be distorted or lost [1], which delays decisions and the implementation of the strategy, and managers at the top may lose touch with what is happening [1]. **[3]**

**6.** A: 0, B: 0 [1]. C: 2, D: 5 [1]. E: 5 (must wait for B), F: 8 (waits for D, which ends at 8, and C, which ends at 6) [1]. G: 11, H: 14 (waits for G, which ends at 14, and F, which ends at 10) [1]. **[4]**

**7.** Earliest finish times: A 2, B 5, C 6, D 8, E 11, F 10, G 14, H 16 [1]. The minimum project time is **16 weeks** [1]. The critical path is B, E, G, H [1]: 5 + 6 + 3 + 2 = 16 [1]. **[4]**

**8.** Latest finish times: H 16, G 14, F 14, E 11, D 12, C 12, B 5, A 5. A: 5 − 2 − 0 = **3** [1]. C: 12 − 4 − 2 = **6** [1]. D: 12 − 3 − 5 = **4** [1]. F: 14 − 2 − 8 = **4** [1]. **[4]**

**9.** E would finish at 5 + 9 = 14 [1], so G finishes at 17 and the project takes **19 weeks** [1]. The critical path remains B, E, G, H, but it is now longer, and all other activities have more float [1]. **[3]**

**10.** With E at 6 weeks, G could start at 11 and finish at 13 [1]. Launch H could then start at 13 and the project would take **15 weeks**, one week quicker [1]. The saving is only one week because B, E, G, H is still critical, and the other paths are shorter (the next longest, B, D, F, H, takes only 12 weeks) [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Democratic style: involving managers and staff in planning the launch gives access to their knowledge of production, packaging and customers [1], which improves the plans [1]. Staff who help to shape the plan are more committed to it and less likely to resist [1]. Against: consultation takes time [1], which could delay a launch with a fixed deadline [1], and different views may cause disagreement, so an autocratic or mixed approach may be needed if time is short [1]. **[6]**

**12.** For: network analysis shows the minimum completion time and the critical activities [1], so managers focus attention where delay would matter [1]. It shows float, so resources can move from non-critical to critical tasks [1]. It shows dependencies clearly and helps co-ordination [1]. It allows quick re-planning when something changes [1]. Against: it relies on accurate estimates of time, which are hard for new strategies [1]. It ignores costs unless extended, and speeding up work may cost more [1]. Complex strategies have too many activities for a simple diagram [1]. It does not deal with the people side of implementation, such as motivation and resistance [1]. Factors: the size and complexity of the project, the reliability of the estimates, and whether it is used with other tools such as budgets and good leadership [1]. Judgement: network analysis is very useful for the timing of well-understood projects [1], but it cannot make a strategy succeed on its own, as leadership, communication and structure also matter [1]. **[12]**

*Examiner insight:* In network questions, show the forward and backward pass, then say what the critical path and float mean for decisions.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Choosing the shortest path as the critical path.
- Forgetting to subtract the duration when calculating float.
- Saying a shorter non-critical activity speeds up the project.
- Describing communication as one-way.
- Recommending a leadership style with no link to the situation.
