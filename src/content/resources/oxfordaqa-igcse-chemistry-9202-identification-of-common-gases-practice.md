This set practises section 3.4.2 Identification of common gases (statements a to e) of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Chemistry (9202) specification, Version 6.2, for exams May/June 2018 onwards. Section A checks recall of each test; Section B applies the tests to reactions from across the course. The course is untiered, so every question suits every candidate.

## Section A

**1.** Describe the test for hydrogen gas and give the positive result. **[2]**

**2.** A student puts a lighted splint into a test tube of oxygen and says this is the test for oxygen. Correct the student's method and give the positive result. **[2]**

**3.** Limewater is used to test for carbon dioxide.

**(a)** Give the chemical name of limewater. **[1]**

**(b)** Write a balanced symbol equation, with state symbols, for the reaction of carbon dioxide with limewater. **[2]**

**4.** Which gas turns damp red litmus paper blue? **[1]**

**5.** Explain why the litmus paper must be damp when testing for chlorine. **[1]**

**6.** Give two observations that are made when damp blue litmus paper is held in chlorine, in the order in which they happen. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A teacher holds the open stopper of a bottle of concentrated hydrochloric acid near the mouth of a test tube containing a colourless gas. White smoke forms.

**(a)** Name the gas in the test tube. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the gas that escapes from concentrated hydrochloric acid. **[1]**

**(c)** Name the white solid and write a balanced equation for its formation. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why this test should be done in a fume cupboard. **[1]**

**8.** Sodium chloride solution is electrolysed using inert electrodes. A gas is collected at each electrode.

**(a)** Name the gas formed at the negative electrode and describe a test to identify it. **[3]**

**(b)** Name the gas formed at the positive electrode and describe a test to identify it, including both observations. **[4]**

**9.** Copper carbonate is heated strongly in a boiling tube. A gas is given off and a black solid remains.

**(a)** Name the gas given off. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe how the student should test the gas, and give the result. **[2]**

**(c)** A student holds a lighted splint at the mouth of the tube and it goes out. Explain why this observation does not identify the gas. **[1]**

**10.** Four unlabelled test tubes, P, Q, R and S, each contain one of these gases: ammonia, chlorine, hydrogen, oxygen. The results of three tests are shown.

| Tube | Damp red litmus | Lighted splint at mouth | Glowing splint inside |
|---|---|---|---|
| P | no change | pop | does not relight |
| Q | bleached white | no pop | does not relight |
| R | turns blue | no pop | does not relight |
| S | no change | no pop; burns more brightly | relights |

**(a)** Identify the gas in each tube. **[4]**

**(b)** The student did not test any of the gases with damp blue litmus. Explain why the results already identify tube Q. **[1]**

**11.** A student adds dilute hydrochloric acid to a white powder. The mixture fizzes. The student thinks the gas could be hydrogen or carbon dioxide.

**(a)** Describe two tests the student could do, and the result each would give if that gas were present. **[4]**

**(b)** The gas turns limewater cloudy white. Suggest what type of compound the white powder contains. **[1]**

**12.** Both ammonia and chlorine have a sharp, choking smell.

**(a)** Explain why a student should not identify these gases by smell. **[2]**

**(b)** A student has a strip of damp red litmus and a strip of damp blue litmus. Describe the results each gas gives with each strip, and explain the results. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** Hold a lighted splint at the mouth of the test tube [1]; a (squeaky) pop is heard [1].

*Examiner insight:* "It burns" or "it explodes" is not the expected observation; write "pop".

**2.** Use a **glowing** splint, not a lighted one, placed into the tube [1]; the glowing splint relights [1].

**3. (a)** Calcium hydroxide solution [1].

**(b)** Ca(OH)₂(aq) + CO₂(g) → CaCO₃(s) + H₂O(l): correct formulae, balanced [1]; correct state symbols [1].

**4.** Ammonia [1].

**5.** Chlorine must dissolve in water to form an acidic solution / to act on the litmus [1].

**6.** The litmus turns red [1]; it is then bleached white [1].

*Examiner insight:* Both observations are needed, and "white then red" is the wrong order, so it scores only one mark.

**7. (a)** Ammonia [1].

**(b)** Hydrogen chloride [1].

**(c)** Ammonium chloride [1]; NH₃ + HCl → NH₄Cl [1].

**(d)** Ammonia (and hydrogen chloride) are toxic / choking gases, so they must not be breathed in [1].

**8. (a)** Hydrogen [1]. Hold a lighted splint at the mouth of the tube [1]; a pop is heard [1].

**(b)** Chlorine [1]. Hold damp blue litmus paper in the gas [1]; it turns red [1], then is bleached white [1].

*Examiner insight:* Naming "chloride" for the gas at the anode loses the first mark; chloride is the ion, chlorine is the gas.

**9. (a)** Carbon dioxide [1].

**(b)** Bubble the gas through limewater [1]; it turns cloudy white [1].

**(c)** Other gases also put out a flame, so this does not show the gas is carbon dioxide [1].

**10. (a)** P hydrogen [1]; Q chlorine [1]; R ammonia [1]; S oxygen [1].

**(b)** Only chlorine bleaches litmus; ammonia, the only other gas that changes litmus, turns red litmus blue, not white [1].

*Examiner insight:* Tube P's glowing splint not relighting does not matter; the pop with the lighted splint is the positive result.

**11. (a)** Hold a lighted splint at the mouth of the tube [1]: a pop would show hydrogen [1]. Bubble the gas through limewater [1]: cloudy white would show carbon dioxide [1].

**(b)** A carbonate [1].

**12. (a)** Both gases have the same kind of smell, so smell cannot tell them apart [1]; both are toxic, so breathing them in is harmful [1].

**(b)**

- Ammonia turns damp red litmus blue [1] and leaves damp blue litmus blue [1].
- Chlorine turns damp blue litmus red then white [1] and bleaches damp red litmus white [1].
- Explanation: ammonia dissolves to form an alkaline solution, while chlorine dissolves to form an acidic solution and is also a bleach [1].

*Examiner insight:* Students often say chlorine turns red litmus "red". Red litmus cannot change to red; chlorine bleaches it.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a test with no result, or a result with no test.
- Confusing lighted and glowing splints.
- Omitting "damp" or "bubble through".
- Giving only one of chlorine's two observations, or giving them in the wrong order.
- Naming ions (chloride, ammonium) where the question asks for a gas.
- Treating a splint going out as proof of carbon dioxide.
