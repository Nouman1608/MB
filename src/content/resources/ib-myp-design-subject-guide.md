Design, and the resultant development of new technologies, has given
rise to profound changes in society, transforming how we access and
process information, adapt our environment, communicate, solve
problems, work and live. IB Middle Years Programme Design challenges
students to apply practical and creative-thinking skills to solve
design problems, encourages them to explore the role of design in
historical and contemporary contexts, and raises their awareness of
the responsibilities involved in making design decisions -- including
the environmental, social and ethical consequences a design choice can
carry, which the course treats as inseparable from technical
competence.

## The design cycle

Inquiry and problem-solving are at the heart of the subject. MYP
Design requires the use of the design cycle as a tool: it structures
the inquiry and analysis of problems, the development of feasible
solutions, the creation of solutions, and the testing and evaluation
of the solution. A solution can be a model, prototype, product or
system, independently created and developed by students. Unlike a
fixed set-piece project repeated identically by every student, the
design cycle is meant to be applied to a genuine problem of the
student's own framing, so two students in the same class can produce
entirely different solutions while still being assessed against the
same four criteria.

## Aims

The course encourages and enables students to:

- enjoy the design process, and develop an appreciation of its
  elegance and power
- develop knowledge, understanding and skills from different
  disciplines to design and create solutions to problems using the
  design cycle
- use and apply technology effectively to access, process and
  communicate information, model and create solutions, and solve
  problems
- develop an appreciation of the impact of design innovations for
  life, global society and environments
- appreciate past, present and emerging design within cultural,
  political, social, historical and environmental contexts
- develop respect for others' viewpoints and appreciate alternative
  solutions to problems
- act with integrity and honesty, and take responsibility for their
  own actions, including honestly reporting where a solution did not
  fully meet its own design specification.

## How it's assessed

MYP Design is assessed against four equally weighted criteria, each scored
on an eight-point scale (1-8), rather than through an SL/HL exam model:
Criterion A, Inquiring and analysing (identifying a design problem and
researching it); Criterion B, Developing ideas (writing a design
specification and generating a solution); Criterion C, Creating the
solution (planning and making a prototype); and Criterion D, Evaluating
(testing the solution and reflecting on how it could be improved).

Where schools opt into MYP eAssessment for an IB-validated result, Design
is assessed by ePortfolio rather than an on-screen exam -- students submit
a complete design folder containing their design brief, specification,
development work and evaluation, which is marked by their own teacher
against published year-5 criteria and then externally moderated by the IB
on a sampled basis. This ePortfolio route reflects that Design's four
criteria are best evidenced through a sustained body of process work,
rather than through timed, on-screen questions, which suit knowledge-based
subjects more naturally than a design process does.

## The design cycle in more depth

The four assessment criteria map directly onto the four broad stages of the design cycle, which is
what makes MYP Design's assessment structure distinctive compared with a content-recall subject: a
student is graded on the process of designing as much as on the finished artefact. Inquiring and
analysing (Criterion A) means identifying a genuine problem and researching it properly before
jumping to solutions. Developing ideas (Criterion B) means generating and justifying a design
specification -- the measurable criteria a successful solution must meet -- rather than sketching a
single idea and building it unreflectively. Creating the solution (Criterion C) is the practical
planning and making stage, including a logical, time-managed plan for producing the chosen solution.
Evaluating (Criterion D) requires testing the finished solution against the original specification
and reflecting honestly on where it succeeded or fell short, not simply describing what was made.

## The four criteria at a glance

| Criterion | Design cycle stage | What it rewards |
|---|---|---|
| A — Inquiring and analysing | Identify and research the problem | A clearly defined problem, genuine research, and a justified design brief |
| B — Developing ideas | Specify and propose solutions | Feasible, testable design specifications and well-justified chosen design |
| C — Creating the solution | Plan and make | A logical, followed production plan alongside competent making |
| D — Evaluating | Test and reflect | Honest testing against the specification and a reasoned evaluation |

## Why "creating" is graded on planning, not just craft

A common misconception is that Criterion C rewards how polished or professionally finished the
physical prototype looks. In fact, the criterion is weighted toward the quality of planning --
a logical, appropriately detailed and accurately followed plan -- alongside competent making skill,
so a student with a clear, well-justified production plan they demonstrably followed can score well
even if the final prototype has minor practical flaws, provided those flaws are honestly addressed
in the Criterion D evaluation.

## How this connects to further study

The design cycle taught in MYP Design maps closely onto DP Design Technology's own design process,
so a student who has internalised the specification-driven approach of Criterion B, and the
honest, evidence-based evaluation of Criterion D, enters DP Design Technology with a head start on
its own internally assessed coursework, which follows a very similar inquiry-to-evaluation
structure.
