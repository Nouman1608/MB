This guide covers subtopic **2.3 Recruitment, selection and training of employees** of Cambridge O Level Business Studies 7115 (2026 series). The combined [Recruitment, Selection, Training and Communication study guide](/resources/o-level-business-recruitment-selection-and-training-of-employees-and-internal-and-external/) sets out each stage with cost calculations. This page concentrates on the decisions examiners ask you to recommend and justify: what goes into a job description and person specification, how to compare candidates fairly, which training suits which situation, how to choose employees for redundancy, and how employment law shapes each step.

## What the syllabus asks for

- **2.3.1** Recruitment and selecting employees: methods; internal versus external recruitment; main stages; recommend and justify who to employ; benefits and limitations of part-time and full-time employees.
- **2.3.2** The importance of training and the methods of training: importance to the business and to employees; benefits and limitations of induction, on-the-job and off-the-job training.
- **2.3.3** Why reducing the size of the workforce might be necessary: dismissal versus redundancy with examples; situations where downsizing is necessary, for example automation or reduced demand; recommend and justify which employees to make redundant.
- **2.3.4** Legal controls over employment issues and their impact on employers and employees: employment contracts, unfair dismissal, discrimination, health and safety, legal minimum wage.

## From vacancy to job offer

A vacancy arises when someone leaves, is promoted, or the business expands. Before advertising, the business decides what the job involves and what kind of person it needs.

### Job description and person specification

| Job description (the job) | Person specification (the person) |
|---|---|
| Job title and department | Qualifications needed |
| Who the post reports to | Experience needed |
| Main duties and responsibilities | Skills, such as using a till or driving |
| Hours and place of work | Personal qualities, such as reliability or teamwork |
| Pay and conditions | Often split into **essential** and **desirable** criteria |

The job description is used to write the advert and, later, the contract. The person specification is used to **shortlist and select**: every applicant is compared against the same criteria, which makes selection fairer and helps the business avoid discrimination.

### Choosing where to advertise

| Method | Suits | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Internal notice or email | Promotions, staff who know the business | Small pool, no new ideas |
| Local newspaper or radio | Local, less specialised jobs | May not reach skilled applicants |
| Specialist magazine or website | Skilled or professional posts | Cost of advertising |
| Recruitment agency | Urgent or hard-to-fill posts | Agency fee |
| Job centre (government) | Unskilled or semi-skilled posts | Limited choice for specialist roles |

### Selection methods

- **Application forms and CVs**: let the business compare applicants on the same points.
- **Interviews**: test communication and attitude, but rely on the interviewer's judgement.
- **Skills tests or practical tasks**: show whether the applicant can actually do the work, such as a typing test or cooking a dish.
- **References**: a previous employer or teacher confirms reliability and experience.

## Recommending who to employ

**Worked example 1 (invented candidates).** Brightwater Hotel needs a front-desk supervisor quickly for its busy season. The person specification lists: experience of hotel reception (essential), supervising others (essential), a second language (desirable), able to start within four weeks (essential).

| Criterion | Candidate A (internal receptionist) | Candidate B (external, manager at another hotel) |
|---|---|---|
| Reception experience | 3 years at Brightwater | 6 years elsewhere |
| Supervising | Trains new receptionists informally | Supervised a team of 8 |
| Second language | No | Yes |
| Start date | Immediately | 8 weeks' notice |

> "Brightwater should appoint Candidate A. Although B has more supervising experience and a second language, B cannot start for 8 weeks, which fails an essential criterion during the busy season. A already knows the hotel's systems, so needs little induction, and promoting A shows other staff that promotion is possible. The risk is A's limited supervising experience, which could be reduced by a short off-the-job supervisory course, and A's receptionist post must now be filled."

The answer uses the criteria, rejects the alternative for a stated reason and deals with a weakness. That is what "recommend and justify" requires.

## Part-time or full-time?

Part-time employees suit businesses whose busy periods are short or predictable, such as lunchtimes in a café, and they give flexibility to cover absences. Full-time employees suit roles needing continuity with customers or deep knowledge, such as an accounts clerk. Many businesses mix both: a core of full-time staff plus part-timers at peak times.

## Training: matching the method to the need

| Situation | Most suitable training | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A new cashier starts on Monday | Induction, then on-the-job | Learns rules, layout and safety first, then learns the till beside a colleague |
| A factory buys a complex new machine | Off-the-job, often from the supplier | Specialist knowledge; avoids damage to the machine during learning |
| A shop assistant moves to the stockroom | On-the-job | Cheap and directly relevant |
| A supervisor is promoted to manager | Off-the-job course plus on-the-job support | Needs wider management skills not taught at the workstation |

**Why training matters to the business**: higher productivity and quality, fewer accidents, staff able to use new technology, more flexible workers, and lower labour turnover if staff feel valued. **To employees**: new skills, confidence, better promotion chances and, often, higher pay. A risk to the business is that well-trained employees may be recruited by competitors.

## Reducing the workforce

**Dismissal** is ending employment because of the employee's conduct or ability, such as theft or repeatedly missing targets after warnings. **Redundancy** is ending employment because the job is no longer needed, such as when a branch closes.

### Choosing who to make redundant

**Worked example 2 (invented).** Corran Printers must cut 2 of its 6 machine operators because a new automated press does the work of two people. It uses the criteria below.

| Operator | Skills on new press | Years of service | Absence record |
|---|---|---|---|
| P | Trained | 12 | Good |
| Q | Not trained | 2 | Poor |
| R | Trained | 4 | Good |
| S | Not trained | 9 | Good |
| T | Trained | 1 | Average |
| U | Not trained | 3 | Average |

> "Corran should keep the three trained operators, P, R and T, because their skills are needed for the new press. Of the untrained operators, Q has the shortest service and the poorest absence record, so Q should be made redundant. Between S and U, U has fewer years of service and a weaker absence record, so U should be the second redundancy. S could be retrained on the new press. Using clear criteria applied to everyone reduces the risk of a claim of unfair treatment, but morale among remaining staff may still fall, so the decision should be explained openly."

## Legal controls and their impact

The law varies between countries, so answers should describe the general effect.

- **Employment contracts**: employers must state pay, hours and duties; employees know their rights; disputes are easier to settle.
- **Unfair dismissal**: employers must have a fair reason and follow a fair procedure, such as warnings; employees are protected from being sacked without cause; employers must keep records, which takes management time.
- **Discrimination**: recruitment, pay, training and promotion must not treat people unfairly because of characteristics such as gender, race or religion; adverts and person specifications must use job-related criteria only; the business may gain from a wider pool of talent.
- **Health and safety**: employers must provide safe equipment, training and conditions; this costs money but reduces accidents, absence and compensation claims.
- **Legal minimum wage**: raises costs for businesses with many low-paid workers, which may raise prices or cut hours; employees gain a guaranteed minimum income and may be more motivated.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing up recruitment (attracting applicants) and selection (choosing between them).
- Confusing the job description with the person specification.
- Choosing a candidate with "more experience" without checking the essential criteria in the case.
- Treating redundancy as a punishment; that is dismissal.
- Naming a specific country's law; describe the general control and its impact.

## Exam technique for 7115 command words

- **Identify / State**: for example, name one selection method.
- **Explain**: give the method or law, then its effect on the business or employee in this case.
- **Recommend and justify**: choose one option, link it to two facts from the case, and explain why the alternative is weaker.
