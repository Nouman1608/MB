These questions cover **topic 13, New and emerging technologies** (section 13.1) of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Information Technology 9626 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027 (version 3). This is **A Level only** content, examined on Paper 3 (Advanced Theory), which is based on sections 12–21; in Paper 4 candidates apply knowledge and understanding of all subject content. All questions can be answered on paper. The syllabus states that calculators are not allowed in Paper 3, so work question 6 by hand.

## Questions

**1.** Identify the new or emerging technology described in each statement.

**(a)** An object is built up one layer at a time from a CAD model. **[1]**
**(b)** Directions are shown on top of a live camera view of a street. **[1]**
**(c)** Binary data is encoded as a sequence of DNA bases. **[1]**
**(d)** A device worn on the wrist records heart rate and sends it to a phone. **[1]**

**2.** Describe the difference between computer-assisted translation and machine translation. **[2]**

**3.** Describe how blockchain technology makes it difficult to change a record without the change being detected. **[4]**

**4.** An airline trains new pilots using virtual reality. Explain **two** advantages to the airline of this method. **[4]**

**5.**

**(a)** Describe holographic imaging and give **one** use of it. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe how holographic data storage differs from storage on a standard optical disc. **[2]**
**(c)** Give **one** reason why holographic data storage is not yet widely used. **[1]**

**6.** (non-calculator) A warehouse replaces its forklift trucks, which used 110,000 kWh of electricity a year, with 15 robots. Each robot is rated at 1.2 kW and runs for 16 hours a day on 300 days a year.

**(a)** Calculate the energy the robots use in a year. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage reduction in energy use, to 1 decimal place. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the real environmental benefit may be smaller than your answer to (b) suggests. **[2]**

**7.** Describe how new and emerging technologies are used in:

**(a)** tissue engineering **[2]**
**(b)** the development of artificial blood vessels **[2]**
**(c)** the design of medical tools and equipment. **[2]**

**8.** Explain how vision enhancement technology could help:

**(a)** a person with low vision **[2]**
**(b)** a mountain rescue team searching at night. **[2]**

**9.** Explain the impact of new and emerging technologies on scientific research. **[5]**

**10.** A farming co-operative plans to install IoT soil sensors in its fields and to replace its tractor drivers with autonomous tractors. Discuss the impact of this plan on the co-operative and on its workers. **[8]**

**11.** Evaluate the impact of new and emerging technologies on the environment. Refer to e-waste, recycling, power consumption, manufacturing processes and energy from wireless signals. **[10]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 3D printing. [1]
**(b)** Augmented reality. [1]
**(c)** Molecular data storage. [1]
**(d)** Wearable computing. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Identify" needs only the name, but it must be the precise one; "virtual reality" for (b) earns nothing because the real street is still seen.

**2.** In computer-assisted translation a human translator does the translating, helped by software such as a translation memory and terminology database [1]; in machine translation the software produces the translation on its own, without a human translator. [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A difference needs both sides stated; describing CAT alone, however well, leaves the comparison incomplete.

**3.** Records are grouped into blocks, and each block contains the hash of the previous block [1], so the blocks are linked in a chain. [1] Changing a record changes that block's hash, so it no longer matches the hash stored in the next block. [1] Copies of the ledger are held on many nodes, so an altered copy will not match the others and is rejected. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "It is encrypted" or "it is very secure" is too vague to credit; the answer needs the hash link and the distributed copies.

**4.** Pilots can practise dangerous situations, such as engine failure, with no risk to people or aircraft [1], so they are better prepared for real emergencies. [1] Training does not use a real aircraft or fuel [1], so it costs the airline less, and aircraft stay in service carrying paying passengers. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each advantage should be developed with its consequence for the airline; two features with no "so" usually earn only half the marks.

**5. (a)** A three-dimensional image (hologram), made from the interference pattern of laser light, that can be viewed from different angles [1]; used for security holograms on bank cards or for 3D anatomy images in teaching. [1]
**(b)** A standard optical disc stores data on its surface layer or layers, read bit by bit [1]; holographic storage records data through the whole depth of the medium and can read a whole page of data at once, giving much higher capacity and speed. [1]
**(c)** It is mostly still in development, so the drives and media are expensive and not widely available. [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep (a) and (b) apart: an answer to (b) about 3D pictures confuses holographic imaging with holographic storage.

**6. (a)** 15 × 1.2 × 16 × 300 [1] = **86,400 kWh** [1]
**(b)** (110,000 − 86,400) ÷ 110,000 × 100 [1] = **21.5%** [1]
**(c)** Making the robots uses energy and raw materials, and the robots and old forklifts become e-waste at the end of their lives [1]; the benefit also depends on how the electricity is generated, so the saving in emissions may be smaller than the saving in kWh. [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the working in (a) and (b); a correct method with an arithmetic slip can usually still earn credit, a bare wrong number cannot. In (b) divide by the **original** figure, 110,000, not by 86,400.

**7. (a)** Layers of living cells are 3D bioprinted, or grown on a scaffold [1], to make tissue that can repair or replace a patient's damaged tissue. [1]
**(b)** Bioprinting and tissue engineering are used to develop blood vessels for grafts [1], which could reduce the need for donor vessels and lower the risk of rejection if the patient's own cells are used. [1]
**(c)** CAD software and 3D printing produce quick, cheap prototypes of new tools [1], and VR or AR lets surgeons practise with new equipment before using it on patients. [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Each part needs a point about that specific area; repeating "3D printing makes custom prosthetics" in all three parts earns credit only where it fits.

**8. (a)** Smart glasses or electronic magnifiers enlarge text and increase contrast [1], so the person can read, shop or travel more independently. [1]
**(b)** Night vision devices amplify low light or detect infrared heat [1], so the team can spot a missing person in darkness and search safely without relying only on torches. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "It helps them see better" restates the question; the second mark in each part comes from what the person can now do.

**9.** Indicative points; any five, including a drawback.
- AI analyses data sets far too large for people, finding patterns quickly, so discoveries can be made faster. [1]
- Robots carry out repeated experiments continuously and precisely, freeing researchers' time. [1]
- IoT sensor networks collect field data, such as weather or pollution readings, continuously and remotely. [1]
- VR or holographic imaging lets scientists view and manipulate 3D structures such as molecules. [1]
- Drawback: equipment is expensive, so research teams with less money may fall behind. [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Points should be about research, not general business use; an answer that only says "computers are faster" earns little.

**10.** Indicative points; credit any eight valid, developed points.
- Soil sensors send moisture and nutrient readings continuously, so water and fertiliser are applied only where needed, cutting costs. [1]
- Autonomous tractors can work day and night without breaks, so planting and harvesting finish faster in good weather. [1]
- The co-operative faces high set-up costs for sensors, tractors and network coverage in the fields. [1]
- Tractor drivers may lose their jobs or need retraining as technicians or data analysts. [1]
- Remaining workers need new skills to read sensor data and maintain the systems. [1]
- IoT devices may have weak security, so a hacked system could disrupt the farm. [1]
- Safety: an autonomous tractor may not react correctly to a person or animal in a field, so supervision is still needed. [1]
- Conclusion: benefits in efficiency are likely, but the plan should include retraining and a phased introduction. [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* "Discuss" needs both the organisation and the workers, with balance; an answer only about cost savings stays in the lower part of the mark range however many points it lists.

**11.** Indicative points; credit any ten valid points covering the named areas.
- **E-waste**: cheap wearables and IoT devices are replaced often [1] and contain hazardous materials that can pollute land and water if dumped. [1]
- **Recycling**: robots with AI vision sort recyclable materials faster and more accurately than people [1]; some 3D printers use recycled plastic as their material. [1]
- **Power consumption**: data centres running AI, and blockchains that use energy-intensive methods to agree blocks, use large amounts of electricity [1], but smart meters and IoT heating controls can reduce energy use in homes and buildings. [1]
- **Manufacturing processes**: 3D printing adds material only where it is needed, so less is wasted than in cutting from a block [1], and local printing of parts reduces transport emissions. [1]
- **Energy from wireless signals**: small sensors can harvest energy from radio signals such as Wi-Fi, so they need fewer batteries, reducing battery waste. [1]
- **Judgement**: the overall impact depends on how devices are designed, used and disposed of; benefits outweigh harm only if devices last longer and are recycled properly. [1] **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* When a question lists areas, an answer that omits one limits the mark; "Evaluate" also needs a weighed judgement, not a list of facts.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the wrong technology name on "Identify" questions, such as VR for an overlay on the real world.
- Describing only one side of a "difference" question.
- Calling blockchain "secure" or "encrypted" without the hash links and distributed copies.
- Confusing holographic imaging with holographic data storage.
- Dividing by the new figure instead of the original when finding a percentage reduction.
- Ignoring the energy and materials used to make new devices when judging environmental benefit.
- Repeating the same medical example across tissue engineering, blood vessels and medical tools.
- Missing a balancing point or conclusion on "Discuss" and "Evaluate".
- Writing about the technology in general instead of the scenario's user.
