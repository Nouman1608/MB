## 1.1 Types of number

- Natural numbers: 1, 2, 3, ... Integers: whole numbers including 0 and negatives.
- Prime: exactly two factors. Square numbers: 1, 4, 9, ... Cube numbers: 1, 8, 27, ...
- Rational: can be written as a/b with integers a and b. Irrational: cannot (π, √2).
- Common factors divide both numbers; common multiples are shared by both. Six billion is 6 000 000 000.
- Reciprocal of n is 1/n. The reciprocal of a fraction is the fraction turned upside down.

**Method for HCF and LCM.** Break both numbers into prime factors, then compare the powers.

```
56  = 2³ × 7
210 = 2 × 3 × 5 × 7
HCF = 2 × 7 = 14
LCM = 2³ × 3 × 5 × 7 = 840
```

Check: HCF × LCM = 14 × 840 = 11760 = 56 × 210.

## 1.2 Sets

- Notation: ∈, ∉, ⊆, ⊈, ∪, ∩, A′, ∅, ξ and n(A).
- Describe a set by listing, {2, 4, 6, ...}, or by a rule, {x: 1 ⩽ x ⩽ 5}. Pairs work too: {(x, y): y = 2x + 1}.
- Two or three sets only on a Venn diagram. Start with A ∩ B, then fill the rest of each circle, then outside.
- A′ means everything in ξ not in A. The empty set is a subset of every set.

**Reminder.** n(ξ) = 50, n(A) = 27, n(B) = 19, n(A ∩ B) = 8. Then n(A ∪ B) = 27 + 19 − 8 = 38 and n((A ∪ B)′) = 50 − 38 = 12.

## 1.4 to 1.6 Fractions, decimals, percentages, ordering, operations

- Convert: fraction to decimal by dividing; decimal to percentage by × 100.
- Dot notation: 0.4̇ means 0.444...; 0.6̇3̇ means 0.636363...
- Mixed number to improper: 2 3/5 = 13/5.
- Symbols: = equal, ≠ not equal, > greater than, < less than, ⩾ at least, ⩽ at most.
- Order of operations: brackets, indices, divide and multiply, add and subtract.
- Add or subtract fractions with a common denominator. Multiply across. Divide by multiplying by the reciprocal.

**Recurring decimal to fraction.** x = 0.4111... gives 10x = 4.111... and 100x = 41.111..., so 90x = 37 and x = 37/90. For 0.6̇3̇, 100x − x = 63, so x = 63/99 = 7/11.

**Reminder.** 5/6 − 3/4 × 2/3 = 5/6 − 1/2 = 1/3. Do the multiplication first.

Negative numbers: subtracting a negative adds. −3 − (−8) = 5, and 4 × (−2.5) = −10. A temperature falling from 3 to −5 degrees changes by 8 degrees.

## 1.8 Standard form

1. Move the decimal point until the number is at least 1 but below 10.
2. The power of 10 is the number of places moved: positive for large numbers, negative for small.

- 45 000 000 = 4.5 × 10⁷ and 0.00306 = 3.06 × 10⁻³.
- Multiply: multiply the numbers, add the powers. (6 × 10⁻³) × (5 × 10⁷) = 30 × 10⁴ = 3 × 10⁵.
- Add: match powers first. 3.2 × 10⁻³ + 4.5 × 10⁻⁴ = 3.2 × 10⁻³ + 0.45 × 10⁻³ = 3.65 × 10⁻³.

## 1.9 Estimation

- Decimal places count after the point. Significant figures start at the first non-zero digit.
- Rounding: check the digit after the cut-off. A 5 or higher means round up.
- Estimate by rounding every value to 1 significant figure. Show the rounded values.
- Round a final answer sensibly for the context, for example money to 2 decimal places.
- Do not round part-way through a calculation.

## 1.10 Limits of accuracy

- A length of 847.6 m to 1 decimal place: 847.55 ⩽ l < 847.65.
- To the nearest 10: 850 means 845 ⩽ x < 855.
- Largest sum or product: upper with upper. Smallest: lower with lower.
- Largest difference a − b or quotient a ÷ b: upper a with lower b. Smallest: lower a with upper b.

**Reminder.** Sides 9.3 cm and 6.1 cm, each to 1 decimal place. Upper bound of area = 9.35 × 6.15 = 57.5025 cm². Lower bound of 9.3 ÷ 6.1 is 9.25 ÷ 6.15 = 1.50 to 3 significant figures.

## 1.11 and 1.12 Ratio, proportion and rates

- Simplify ratios in the same units. Divide a quantity: parts, one part, multiply.
- Share 240 in the ratio 3 : 5. One part = 240 ÷ 8 = 30, so shares are 90 and 150.
- Direct proportion: multiply or divide by the same factor. Inverse proportion: one quantity goes up as the other goes down, and the product stays fixed.
- Speed = distance ÷ time. Density = mass ÷ volume. Population density is people per unit area. Flow rate is volume per unit time.
- Pressure, density and similar formulas are given in the question, according to the syllabus. Speed, distance and time are not.
- Average speed = total distance ÷ total time.
- Unit conversion: to change km/h to m/s divide by 3.6. So 72 km/h = 20 m/s.

## 1.13 and 1.17 Percentages, interest, growth and decay

| Task | Method |
|---|---|
| Percentage of a quantity | 0.375 × 80 or 37.5/100 × 80 |
| One quantity as a percentage | (part ÷ whole) × 100 |
| Percentage change | (change ÷ original) × 100 |
| Increase p% | multiply by 1 + p/100 |
| Decrease p% | multiply by 1 − p/100 |
| Reverse percentage | divide by the multiplier |
| Simple interest | principal × rate × time |
| Compound interest | principal × multiplier^years, then subtract principal for the interest |

**Reminder.** 480 dollars at 5% for 3 years: simple interest 72 dollars; compound amount 480 × 1.05³ = 555.66 dollars, interest 75.66 dollars.

**Reverse.** A price after a 20% discount is 1920 dollars. Original = 1920 ÷ 0.8 = 2400 dollars.

**Growth and decay.** Value = start × multiplierⁿ. A machine worth 8000 dollars losing 15% a year is worth 8000 × 0.85³ = 4913 dollars after 3 years. Know the terms deposit, discount, profit, loss and earnings. Percentages over 100% are allowed, so 120% of 85 is 102.

## 1.14 Using a calculator

- Keep unrounded values in the calculator memory and round once, at the end.
- Enter 2 h 30 min as 2.5 hours, or use the degrees-minutes-seconds key.
- Interpret displays: money 4.8 is 4.80; time 3.25 hours is 3 h 15 min.
- Bracket the denominator: type a ÷ (b × c), not a ÷ b × c.

## 1.15 and 1.16 Time and money

- 24-hour clock: 03 15 is 3.15 a.m. and 15 15 is 3.15 p.m.
- A bus leaves at 09 50 and arrives at 13 15. Journey = 3 h 25 min = 3 5/12 h. Covering 205 km gives 205 ÷ 3 5/12 = 60 km/h.
- Time zones: find the journey time, add it to the departure time, then add or subtract the time difference.
- Read timetables carefully. Check the unit (minutes or hours) before dividing.
- Currency: use the rate as quoted, multiply or divide by it, then check the size of the answer is sensible. If 1 euro = 1.25 dollars, 40 euros is 50 dollars, so you multiply by 1.25.

## 1.18 Surds

- √a × √a = a and √a × √b = √(ab).
- Simplify with the largest square factor: √75 = 5√3.
- Rationalise a single surd by multiplying top and bottom by it: 10/√5 = 2√5.
- For a denominator a + √b, multiply top and bottom by a − √b: 9/(4 + √7) = 9(4 − √7)/(16 − 7) = 4 − √7.
- Brackets: (1 + √3)² = 1 + 2√3 + 3 = 4 + 2√3.

## Must-know distinctions

- HCF (largest common factor) versus LCM (smallest common multiple).
- Rational (fraction form) versus irrational (non-terminating, non-recurring).
- Simple interest (same every year) versus compound (interest on interest).
- Percentage increase versus percentage of: 120% of x is an increase of 20%.
- Decimal places versus significant figures.
- Bounds of the data versus bounds of the answer: the answer's upper bound may use a lower bound of the data.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Rounding part-way through a long calculation and then losing accuracy in the final answer.
- Writing 3.25 hours as 3 h 25 min.
- Mixing up which bound to use for a difference or a quotient.
- Forgetting to subtract the principal when the question asks for the interest.
- Dividing by 1.2 when you should multiply, or the reverse, in percentage questions.
- Leaving the answer as 30 × 10⁴, which is not in standard form.
- Giving a decimal for an exact surd or fraction question.
- Showing no rounded values in an estimation question.
- Multiplying the two numbers together and calling the result the LCM.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between rational and irrational numbers?**
A rational number can be written as a/b, where a and b are integers. An irrational number, such as π or √2, cannot, and its decimal neither terminates nor recurs.

**How do you add numbers in standard form?**
Match the powers of 10 first, then add the number parts. For example, 3.2 × 10⁻³ + 4.5 × 10⁻⁴ = 3.2 × 10⁻³ + 0.45 × 10⁻³ = 3.65 × 10⁻³.

**How do you rationalise a denominator of the form a + √b?**
Multiply the top and bottom by a − √b. For example, 9/(4 + √7) becomes 9(4 − √7)/(16 − 7), which simplifies to 4 − √7.

**What is the difference between simple and compound interest?**
Simple interest is the same amount every year. Compound interest is earned on the interest already added, so the value after n years is the start value times the multiplier to the power n.

**Which rates formulas are given in the Cambridge O Level Mathematics 4024 exam?**
According to the syllabus, formulas for pressure, density and similar rates are given in the question, but speed, distance and time are not, so you must learn those.
