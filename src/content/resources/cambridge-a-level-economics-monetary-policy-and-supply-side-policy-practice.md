## Questions

**1.** Define monetary policy. **[2]**

**2.** For each action, state whether it is a monetary policy tool. If it is, name the type of tool and say whether it is expansionary or contractionary.

**(a)** The central bank raises its policy interest rate. **[1]**
**(b)** The government lowers the maximum loan-to-value ratio for mortgages. **[1]**
**(c)** The central bank buys government bonds from banks, increasing the money banks can lend. **[1]**
**(d)** The government cuts the basic rate of income tax. **[1]**

**3.** A savings account pays a nominal interest rate of 3.8%.

**(a)** Calculate the approximate real interest rate when inflation is 1.6%. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the approximate real interest rate when inflation rises to 3.1%. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how the change in (b) affects the incentive to save. **[2]**

**4.** A household has a variable-rate mortgage of 180,000 cu. The rate rises from 4.2% to 5.7%.

**(a)** Calculate the change in annual interest. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the change per month. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how households facing this change could affect aggregate demand. **[3]**

**5.** A house costs 320,000 cu. The maximum loan-to-value ratio falls from 90% to 75%.

**(a)** Calculate the maximum loan and the minimum deposit before and after, and the number of times larger the deposit becomes. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how this credit regulation could affect real output. **[3]**

**6.** In an economy the demand for money is L = 600 - 50r, where L is in cu bn and r is the interest rate in %. The central bank cuts the money supply from 450 to 400 cu bn.

**(a)** Calculate the equilibrium interest rate before and after. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain the effect on investment and aggregate demand. **[3]**

**7.** Explain whether expansionary or contractionary monetary policy suits each situation.

**(a)** Inflation is well above target and output is above its sustainable level. **[2]**
**(b)** Unemployment is rising and growth is weak with low inflation. **[2]**

**8.** In Country P, aggregate demand is Y = 1,500 - 5P and short-run aggregate supply is Y = 600 + 5P, where Y is real output in cu bn and P is the price index.

**(a)** Calculate the equilibrium price level and real output. **[2]**
**(b)** Expansionary monetary policy raises aggregate demand by 100 at every price. Calculate the new equilibrium. **[2]**
**(c)** Work out how much, in percent, each of the price level and real output has risen. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why the price level rises as well as output. **[2]**

**9.** **(a)** Define supply-side policy. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how a government-funded rail link could shift the LRAS curve to the right. **[3]**

**10.** Country T has 12 million workers producing 360 bn cu of output.

**(a)** Calculate output per worker. **[1]**
**(b)** A training programme raises the output per worker of 3 million of them by 12%. Calculate the new total output. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage increase in output. **[1]**
**(d)** The programme costs 1,500 cu per trainee. Compare the cost with the annual gain in output. **[2]**

**11.** In Country U inflation is 6%. Most of the rise comes from higher costs of imported energy. Unemployment is also rising. Evaluate whether raising interest rates is the best response. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Changes to interest rates, the money supply or credit regulations [1] made by the government or central bank to steer aggregate demand and meet macroeconomic objectives [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition that names only one tool, such as interest rates, is incomplete; list all three and say what the policy is for.

**2.** **(a)** Interest rate tool; contractionary [1].
**(b)** Credit regulation; contractionary [1].
**(c)** Money supply tool; expansionary [1].
**(d)** Not monetary policy; it is a tax change (fiscal policy) [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (d) tests whether you can separate policy types. Naming the policy it belongs to shows that you have understood the distinction.

**3.** **(a)** 3.8 - 1.6 = **2.2%** [1].
**(b)** 3.8 - 3.1 = **0.7%** [1].
**(c)** The reward for saving, after allowing for rising prices, is lower [1], so saving is less attractive and households are likely to spend more or save less [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the real rate in your explanation. Saying that the nominal rate has not changed misses the point of the question.

**4.** **(a)** Before: 180,000 x 0.042 = 7,560 [1]. After: 180,000 x 0.057 = 10,260, a rise of **2,700 cu a year** [1].
**(b)** 2,700 / 12 = **225 cu a month** [1].
**(c)** Disposable income falls by 225 cu a month [1], so consumption falls [1], and consumption is a component of AD, so AD shifts left [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Take the link all the way to AD. Stopping at "households have less to spend" leaves the chain unfinished.

**5.** **(a)** Loans: 320,000 x 0.90 = 288,000 cu before and 320,000 x 0.75 = 240,000 cu after [1]. Deposits: 32,000 cu before and 80,000 cu after [1]. The deposit is 80,000 / 32,000 = **2.5 times** larger [1].
**(b)** Fewer buyers can raise the larger deposit [1], so borrowing and spending on housing and related goods fall, AD shifts left [1], and real output is lower than it would otherwise be [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the working for loan and deposit separately. Both figures are needed before the comparison has meaning.

**6.** **(a)** At 450: 600 - 50r = 450, so r = **3%** [1]. At 400: 600 - 50r = 400, so r = **4%** [1]. The rate rises by 1 percentage point [1].
**(b)** Higher rates raise the cost of borrowing and reduce the number of projects with a high enough return [1], so investment falls [1] and AD shifts left [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Say which direction the interest rate moves and why before you discuss investment. Without that link the answer jumps a step.

**7.** **(a)** Contractionary [1], to reduce AD and relieve pressure on prices [1].
**(b)** Expansionary [1], to raise AD, output and jobs, with little risk of higher inflation [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give a reason tied to the situation described, not just the policy name.

**8.** **(a)** 1,500 - 5P = 600 + 5P, so 10P = 900. **P = 90** [1]. Y = 1,500 - 450 = **1,050** [1].
**(b)** New AD: Y = 1,600 - 5P. 10P = 1,000, so **P = 100** [1], **Y = 1,100** [1].
**(c)** Price level: 10 / 90 = **11.1%** [1]. Output: 50 / 1,050 = **4.8%** [1].
**(d)** SRAS slopes upward [1], so a rightward shift of AD moves the economy along it: firms raise prices to cover higher costs and encourage more output [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Percentage changes are measured from the starting value, so divide by 90 and 1,050, not by the new values.

**9.** **(a)** Policy that aims to increase the economy's capacity to produce [1] by improving the quantity, quality or efficiency of factors of production [1].
**(b)** The rail link cuts transport time and costs [1], so firms and workers are more productive and can produce more with the same resources [1], raising potential output, which shifts LRAS right [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Finish with the shift in LRAS. Describing cheaper transport without the effect on capacity does not answer the question.

**10.** **(a)** 360 bn / 12 m = **30,000 cu** [1].
**(b)** Trained: 3 m x 30,000 x 1.12 = 100.8 bn. Others: 9 m x 30,000 = 270 bn [1]. Total **370.8 bn cu** [1].
**(c)** 10.8 / 360 = **3.0%** [1].
**(d)** Cost: 3 m x 1,500 = 4.5 bn cu [1]. The annual gain of 10.8 bn is 2.4 times the cost, if the gain lasts [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both figures stated in the same units. Add a condition, such as the gain lasting, to show judgement.

**11.** Cost-push inflation means SRAS has shifted left, so higher interest rates address the wrong problem [1]. A rate rise shifts AD left [1]. The price level falls or rises more slowly, but real output falls and unemployment rises further [1]. Supply-side policy such as training or infrastructure raises productivity and lowers costs [1], shifting SRAS and LRAS right, so output and jobs rise while the price level is lower [1]. However, supply-side policy takes years and is costly, and firms may not use new skills [1]. A rate rise acts faster on demand-pull pressure [1]. Judgement: supply-side measures fit better with cost-push inflation and rising unemployment, though they cannot offer a quick fix [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation needs a reason for each point and a conclusion that depends on the situation given, such as the cause of inflation.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining monetary policy with one tool only.
- Saying lower interest rates make saving more attractive.
- Working out percentage changes from the new value instead of the starting value.
- Describing a policy without linking it to a component of AD or to AS.
- Treating a supply-side measure as a shift of AD.
- Quoting a rate change in cu or a cu change as a percentage.
- Forgetting to say which direction the equilibrium price level and output move.
- Writing evaluation without a reason or a conclusion.
