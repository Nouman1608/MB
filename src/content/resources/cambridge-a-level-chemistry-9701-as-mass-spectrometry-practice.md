## Section A

**1.** State what the m/e value of the molecular ion peak tells you about a compound. **[1]**

**2.** Lithium has two isotopes: ⁶Li, abundance 7.6%, and ⁷Li, abundance 92.4%. Calculate the relative
atomic mass of lithium to one decimal place. **[1]**

**3.** Explain why fragments in a mass spectrum are always written as positive ions. **[1]**

**4.** State the ratio of the heights of the M⁺ and [M+2]⁺ peaks for a compound containing one bromine
atom. **[1]**

**5.** Write an equation for the fragmentation of the molecular ion of propanone, CH₃COCH₃, to give an ion
at m/e 43. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** The mass spectrum of neon shows peaks at m/e 20 (relative abundance 90.5), 21 (0.3) and 22 (9.2).

**(a)** Calculate the relative atomic mass of neon to one decimal place. **[2]**

**(b)** State the number of protons and neutrons in an atom of ²²Ne. **[1]**

**7.** Compound V contains carbon, hydrogen and one oxygen atom. Its mass spectrum shows M⁺ at m/e 74 with
relative abundance 30.0 and an [M+1]⁺ peak with relative abundance 1.32.

**(a)** Calculate the number of carbon atoms in V. **[1]**

**(b)** Deduce the molecular formula of V. **[1]**

**(c)** The spectrum also has strong peaks at m/e 59 and 45. Suggest the formula of each fragment ion.
**[2]**

**(d)** V is an alcohol. Suggest its structure. **[1]**

**8.** Compound W shows peaks at m/e 112 and 114 in a height ratio of 3 : 1. The M⁺ peak has relative
abundance 100 and the [M+1]⁺ peak has relative abundance 6.6.

**(a)** Identify the halogen present. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of carbon atoms in W. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest the molecular formula of W. **[1]**

**9.** The mass spectrum of 1,2-dibromoethane, CH₂BrCH₂Br, has three peaks in the molecular ion region, at
m/e 186, 188 and 190. Explain why there are three peaks and predict the ratio of their heights. **[3]**

**10.** Propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol are positional isomers.

**(a)** State the m/e value of the molecular ion peak for both compounds. **[1]**

**(b)** One isomer gives a strong peak at m/e 31; the other gives a strong peak at m/e 45. Identify which
isomer gives each peak, and give the formula of each fragment ion. **[2]**

**11.** The mass spectrum of 1-chloropropane, CH₃CH₂CH₂Cl, has peaks at m/e 78 and 80 in the ratio 3 : 1,
and a strong peak at m/e 43.

**(a)** Explain the peaks at m/e 78 and 80. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify the ion at m/e 43 and the species lost to form it. **[2]**

**12.** A hydrocarbon has its M⁺ peak at m/e 86 with relative abundance 15.0, and an [M+1]⁺ peak with
relative abundance 0.99. Deduce its molecular formula, showing your working. **[2]**

**13.** The mass spectrum of ethanoic acid, CH₃COOH, has M⁺ at m/e 60 and fragment peaks at m/e 45 and 43.
Give the formula of the ion responsible for each fragment peak. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The relative molecular mass, Mr, of the compound [1].

**2.** (6 × 7.6 + 7 × 92.4) / 100 = 6.9 [1].

**3.** Only charged particles are detected, and the ions are positive (uncharged radicals are not
detected) [1].

**4.** 1 : 1 [1].

**5.** CH₃COCH₃⁺ → CH₃CO⁺ + •CH₃ [1].

**6. (a)** (20 × 90.5 + 21 × 0.3 + 22 × 9.2) / 100 [1] = 2018.7 / 100 = 20.2 [1].

**(b)** 10 protons and 12 neutrons [1].

**7. (a)** n = (100 × 1.32) / (1.1 × 30.0) = 4 [1].

**(b)** 4 C = 48 and 1 O = 16, leaving 74 − 64 = 10 for hydrogen: C₄H₁₀O [1].

**(c)** m/e 59: C₃H₇O⁺, from loss of CH₃ (74 − 15) [1]; m/e 45: C₂H₅O⁺, such as CH₃CHOH⁺, from loss of
C₂H₅ (74 − 29) [1].

**(d)** Butan-2-ol, CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₃: breaking either C–C bond next to the C–OH carbon gives CH₃CHOH⁺ (45)
or CH₃CH₂CHOH⁺ (59) [1].

**8. (a)** Chlorine [1].

**(b)** n = (100 × 6.6) / (1.1 × 100) = 6 [1].

**(c)** 6 C = 72 and 1 Cl = 35, leaving 112 − 107 = 5 for hydrogen: C₆H₅Cl [1].

**9.** Each bromine atom can be ⁷⁹Br or ⁸¹Br, in roughly equal amounts [1]. The molecule can contain
⁷⁹Br + ⁷⁹Br (186), ⁷⁹Br + ⁸¹Br (188) or ⁸¹Br + ⁸¹Br (190) [1]. The mixed combination can arise in two
ways, so the ratio is 1 : 2 : 1 [1].

**10. (a)** m/e 60 [1].

**(b)** Propan-1-ol gives m/e 31, CH₂OH⁺; propan-2-ol gives m/e 45, CH₃CHOH⁺ [1 for each isomer correctly
matched with its ion, maximum 2].

**11. (a)** Both are molecular ions, M⁺ [1]: m/e 78 contains ³⁵Cl and m/e 80 contains ³⁷Cl, and the 3 : 1
ratio reflects the natural abundance of the two chlorine isotopes [1].

**(b)** C₃H₇⁺ (CH₃CH₂CH₂⁺) [1]; a chlorine atom (Cl•) is lost [1].

**12.** n = (100 × 0.99) / (1.1 × 15.0) = 6 carbon atoms [1]. 86 − 72 = 14 hydrogen atoms, so the formula
is C₆H₁₄ [1].

**13.** m/e 45: COOH⁺ [1]; m/e 43: CH₃CO⁺ [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing by 100 when the abundances do not add up to 100.
- Missing the + charge on fragment ions, or naming the radical as the detected species.
- Writing the [M+1]⁺ formula upside down or forgetting the factor of 100.
- Confusing the 3 : 1 chlorine pattern with the 1 : 1 bromine pattern.
- Giving a fragment formula whose mass does not match the m/e value: always add up the atoms.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I find what was lost to form a fragment?**
Subtract the fragment's m/e from the m/e of M⁺. A difference of 15 means CH₃ was lost, 29 means C₂H₅ or
CHO, 17 means OH and 45 means COOH.

**What do three peaks at M, M+2 and M+4 mean?**
They show two halogen atoms. A 9 : 6 : 1 ratio means two chlorine atoms; a 1 : 2 : 1 ratio means two
bromine atoms.
