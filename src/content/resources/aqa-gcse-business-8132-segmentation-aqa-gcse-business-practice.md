## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Define the term *market segmentation*. **[2]**

**2.** State **two** ways in which a business could segment its market. **[2]**

**3.** Distinguish between a market segment and a target market. **[2]**

**4.** Lornby Leisure has 6,500 members. 2,080 of them are aged 65 or over. Calculate the percentage of members aged 65 or over. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** way a car manufacturer could use income segmentation. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **one** drawback to a small business of targeting several market segments. **[2]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** Quarry Lane Books sells books in a town centre. Its customer data shows the following.

| Age group | Customers per month | Average spend |
|---|---|---|
| Under 18 | 900 | £8 |
| 18 to 40 | 1,100 | £14 |
| Over 40 | 700 | £22 |

**(a)** Calculate the monthly revenue from each age group. **[3]**
**(b)** Identify the age group that brings in the most revenue. **[1]**
**(c)** Analyse how Quarry Lane Books could use this data to plan its promotion. **[4]**

**8.** Explain how a garden centre chain could use location segmentation. **[3]**

**9.** Ferrow Fragrances sells perfume and aftershave.

**(a)** Explain **one** benefit to Ferrow Fragrances of segmenting its market by gender. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain **one** risk of relying on gender segmentation. **[2]**

**10.** Halwyn Holidays plans to target couples aged 55 to 70 with above-average incomes. Recommend **two** elements of a marketing mix for this segment, giving a reason for each. **[4]**

**11.** Penrowe Juice sells one fruit juice to all customers through supermarkets. Research shows a growing number of health-conscious customers aged 25 to 40 on high incomes, and many parents buying juice for young children. Analyse the benefits to Penrowe Juice of segmenting its market. **[6]**

**12.** Tolcarne Trainers is a small business selling running shoes online. It has a limited marketing budget. The owner is deciding whether to target only serious runners aged 25 to 45 with high-priced, high-performance shoes, or to aim at all ages and incomes with a wide range. Evaluate which approach the owner should take. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** Dividing a market into groups of customers [1] who share a characteristic and have similar needs [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any two of: gender, age, location, income [1] [1]. **[2]**

**3.** A market segment is a group of customers within a market who share a characteristic [1]; the target market is the segment or segments the business chooses to aim at [1]. **[2]**

**4.** 2,080 ÷ 6,500 × 100 [1] = **32%** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** It could make a basic model for lower-income customers and a premium model for higher-income customers [1], pricing each to suit what the group can afford [1], so it sells to more of the market and earns a higher profit per car from the premium buyers [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Each segment needs its own product version or promotion [1], which a small business with limited money may not afford, so costs rise and profit falls [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Under 18: 900 × £8 = **£7,200** [1]. 18 to 40: 1,100 × £14 = **£15,400** [1]. Over 40: 700 × £22 = **£15,400** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** 18 to 40 and over 40 bring in the same revenue, **£15,400** each; accept either or both [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** The over-40 group has the fewest customers but spends the most each [1], so promotion could aim to attract more over-40 customers, for example through local printed press or book events [1]. The 18 to 40 group is large and earns as much revenue [1], so targeted social media adverts could keep them buying, making promotion spending more effective [1]. **[4]**

**8.** Customers' needs differ by area, for example small city gardens and large rural gardens [1]. The chain can stock patio and balcony plants in city branches and lawnmowers and large shrubs in rural ones [1], so each branch sells more of what local customers want and holds less unsold stock [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** Ferrow can design scents and packaging for each group [1] and advertise them in places each group is likely to see [1], so more customers find a product that suits them and sales rise [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Many customers buy across genders or buy for others as gifts [1], so a narrow gender focus may miss sales or put some customers off [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Any two elements, each with a reason. For example: price, premium [1], because above-average incomes mean the segment can pay for comfort and quality [1]; promotion, adverts in printed press or targeted online [1], because this age group may read newspapers and the adverts avoid paying to reach young families [1]. Also accept product (relaxing, guided or cultural holidays) or place (telephone sales or a website with a call-back service) with reasons. **[4]**

**11.** A single product cannot fully meet the different needs of these groups [1]. Segmenting lets Penrowe make a low-sugar or added-vitamin juice for health-conscious adults [1], who have high incomes, so it can charge a premium price and raise profit per unit [1]. It could make a smaller carton for children [1], meeting parents' need for a convenient lunchbox drink, so more parents buy [1]. Promotion can be targeted to each group, so less is wasted on people unlikely to buy [1]. **[6]**

**12.** Knowledge: targeting a single segment means designing the mix for one group; a wide range aims at many groups [1].
Application: Tolcarne is small with a limited budget [1]; serious runners will pay high prices for performance [1].
Analysis: a single segment lets the owner spend the small budget on promotion that reaches serious runners, such as running clubs and online running forums, so little is wasted [1]; high prices give a high profit per pair [1]; specialist knowledge builds loyalty and recommendations among runners [1].
Evaluation: the segment is narrow, so total sales are limited and the business depends on one group [1]; a wide range would reach more people but needs much more stock and promotion, which the budget cannot support, and would compete with large retailers [1]. Judgement: target serious runners, because the limited budget is best spent where it reaches likely buyers who pay high prices; the business can widen its range once it has grown [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* The judgement gains credit when it is tied to the limited budget in the stem; a judgement for the wide range can also score well if it argues that the narrow segment is too small.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a segmentation criterion without saying how the business would use it.
- Mixing up the target market with segmentation itself.
- Assuming the biggest segment by number of customers is the most valuable.
- Forgetting × 100 when finding a percentage share.
- Recommending marketing mix elements with no link to the segment in the case.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I score well on a segment data question?**
Show working for each figure, then explain what the data means for a marketing decision, such as which group to promote to.

**Is targeting one segment always best for a small business?**
Not always. It saves money and focuses promotion, but a segment that is too small may not produce enough sales.
