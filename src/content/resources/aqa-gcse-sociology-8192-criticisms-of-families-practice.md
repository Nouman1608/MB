## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Which criticism of families refers to presenting family life as more perfect than it really is?

A. Loss of traditional functions
B. Unrealistic idealisation
C. Marital breakdown
D. Lack of contact with wider kinship networks

**[1]**

**2.** According to Delphy and Leonard, who are the prime beneficiaries of the exploitation of women's labour in families?

A. Capitalists
B. Children
C. Men
D. The state

**[1]**

**3.** Describe what is meant by an empty shell marriage. **[2]**

**4.** Identify and describe **one** way in which a lack of contact with wider kinship networks may affect families. **[3]**

**5.** Explain what Zaretsky meant by saying that the family cushions the effects of capitalism. **[3]**

**6.** Read Item A.

> **Item A (fictional).** Leanne and Craig both work full time at the same supermarket and earn the same wage. At home, Leanne does the cooking, washing and most of the cleaning. Craig says the arrangement is fair because "Leanne is better at that sort of thing".

- **(a)** Identify the sociological perspective most likely to use Item A as evidence that families are exploitative. **[1]**
- **(b)** Using Item A, explain how Delphy and Leonard would interpret Leanne and Craig's family. **[3]**

**7.** Explain **one** similarity and **one** difference between Zaretsky's views on families and those of Delphy and Leonard. **[4]**

**8.** Identify and explain **one** reason why the isolation of the privatised nuclear family may be a problem for its members. **[4]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**9.** Read Item B.

> **Item B (fictional).** A researcher wanted to measure how common domestic abuse is in the city of Harbridge. She counted the number of domestic abuse incidents recorded by the local police over one year and concluded that abuse affects very few families in the city.

- **(a)** From Item B, examine **one** weakness of the research. **[2]**
- **(b)** Identify and explain **one** ethical issue a researcher should consider when interviewing people who have experienced domestic abuse. **[4]**

**10.** Explain **two** ways in which functionalists might respond to criticisms of families. **[4]**

**11.** Discuss how far sociologists would agree that families are harmful to their members. **[12]**

**12.** Discuss how far sociologists would agree that Zaretsky's Marxist view provides the most convincing criticism of families. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. Unrealistic idealisation creates pressure on families that fall short of the ideal. **[1]**

**2.** C [1]. Delphy and Leonard stress men "rather than capitalists as such". **[1]**

**3.** A marriage in which the couple remain legally married or living together [1] but no longer share a loving or close relationship, for example staying together for the children or for financial reasons [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Identify: loss of practical support [1]. Describe: when families move away from relatives for work, there is no grandparent or aunt nearby to help with childcare or in emergencies [1], so parents must pay for childcare or manage alone, which adds stress and cost [1]. *Also credit:* older relatives may become lonely and isolated; children may have weaker ties with grandparents. **[3]**

**5.** Zaretsky argued that the family seems to offer a private refuge from the stress and alienation of work under capitalism [1]. By giving workers comfort and emotional support at home, it makes their exploitation bearable [1], so they are less likely to challenge capitalism, which means the family perpetuates the system rather than solving the problems it causes [1]. **[3]**

**6. (a)** Feminist [1].

**(b)** Delphy and Leonard see the family as an economic system in which men benefit from women's work [1]. Although Leanne earns the same as Craig, she still does most of the domestic work, which matches their example of wives with paid jobs still doing household tasks that are not equally shared [1]. Craig gains free time and services, so Leanne's labour is appropriated, and his claim that she is "better at that sort of thing" shows how patriarchy is made to seem natural [1]. **[4]**

**7.** Similarity: both reject the functionalist view that the family benefits everyone [1], and both focus on women's unpaid domestic labour as central to how the family works [1]. Difference: Zaretsky, a Marxist, argues the family mainly benefits capitalism, for example by reproducing workers and acting as a unit of consumption [1], whereas Delphy and Leonard, feminists, argue that men rather than capitalists are the prime beneficiaries [1]. **[4]**

**8.** Identify: emotional overload [1]. Explain: in a small, privatised family cut off from kin and neighbours, all emotional needs fall on a few people [1], so tensions and conflicts have nowhere to go [1]. Leach argued that the family's "narrow privacy" makes it a source of discontent rather than happiness [1]. *Also credit:* abuse can be hidden more easily; members lack outside support in a crisis. **[4]**

**9. (a)** One weakness, examined. Much domestic abuse is never reported to the police, because victims may be afraid, ashamed or financially dependent [1], so police records underestimate how common abuse is, and the conclusion that it affects very few families is not valid [1]. *Also credit:* records cover only one city and one year, so they cannot be generalised. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify: protection from harm [1]. Explain: talking about abuse may cause the interviewee distress [1], so the researcher should let them stop or withdraw at any time [1] and provide information about where they can get support [1]. *Also credit:* confidentiality and anonymity, especially if the abuser could identify them; informed consent. **[6]**

**10.** First, functionalists argue that criticisms describe a minority of families [1]; most families still perform core functions such as primary socialisation and the stabilisation of adult personalities [1]. Second, they argue that rising divorce does not show the family failing [1]; it may show that people value marriage highly enough to leave an unhappy one and try again, as remarriage suggests [1]. **[4]**

**11.** The real exam marks extended answers using levels of response, not point by point. The points below show indicative creditworthy content.

- Dysfunctional families harm members through domestic abuse, child abuse and neglect [1].
- Privacy can hide harm for long periods [1].
- Radical feminists see domestic violence as a product of male power; Dobash and Dobash linked husbands' violence to their sense of authority [1].
- Leach argued the isolated family is the source of "all our discontents" [1].
- Delphy and Leonard argue the family harms women by exploiting their work [1].
- Zaretsky argues the family cannot meet psychological needs and hides alienation [1].
- Unrealistic idealisation makes members who fall short feel they have failed [1].
- Against: functionalists such as Parsons argue families stabilise adult personalities and socialise children [1].
- Most people choose to live in families, which suggests they gain from them [1].
- Families provide care for the elderly and disabled that the state could not easily replace [1].
- Evaluation: harm varies by family; generalising from abusive families ignores the majority [1].
- Judgement: families can be harmful, especially for women and children in abusive homes, but most families also meet real needs, so "harmful" fits some families rather than families in general [1]. **[12]**

*Examiner insight:* Distinguish extreme harm (abuse) from structural harm (exploitation of women's work); both count, but they are different arguments.

**12.** The real exam marks extended answers using levels of response, not point by point. The points below show indicative creditworthy content.

- Zaretsky argues capitalism creates an illusion that family private life is separate from the economy [1].
- The family cannot meet individuals' psychological and social needs [1].
- It cushions the effects of capitalism but perpetuates the system and cannot compensate for alienation [1].
- It props up capitalism through housewives' domestic labour, which reproduces future workers [1].
- It is a vital unit of consumption, for example through spending on technology and branded goods [1].
- For: rising consumer spending by families supports the unit of consumption argument [1].
- Against: Delphy and Leonard argue it is men, not capitalists as such, who mainly benefit [1].
- Feminists argue Zaretsky says too little about men's power within the family [1].
- Functionalists argue families do meet emotional needs for most people [1].
- Zaretsky's solution, socialism, is hard to test [1].
- Other criticisms, such as dysfunctional families and marital breakdown, are not mainly about capitalism [1].
- Judgement: Zaretsky explains how families serve the economy, but feminists explain inequality inside families more directly, so his view is convincing only in part [1]. **[12]**

*Examiner insight:* "Most convincing" needs a comparison; weigh Zaretsky against at least one named alternative.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Mixing up Zaretsky and Delphy and Leonard, or calling Zaretsky a feminist.
- In 6(b), describing the item without using the idea of appropriation or patriarchy.
- In 9(a), saying "police records are unreliable" without explaining why abuse is under-recorded.
- Giving one side only in 12-mark answers.
