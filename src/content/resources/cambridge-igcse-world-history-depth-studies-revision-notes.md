The depth studies are the part of Cambridge IGCSE History 0470 where you study one country or conflict in detail. Every candidate studies at least one of five -- A: The First World War, 1914-18; B: Germany, 1918-45; C: Russia, 1905-41; D: The United States, 1919-41; E: The Second World War in Europe and the Asia-Pacific, 1939-c.1945 -- and answers one three-part question on it in Section B of Paper 1, as well as using it for Component 3 coursework or Paper 4.

Related: [Depth Study A study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-world-history-depth-study-first-world-war/),
[depth studies practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-world-history-depth-studies-practice/) and the
[0470 exam preparation guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-world-history-exam-preparation/).

## How the depth studies are examined (0470, 2024-2026)

| Component | Depth-study task |
|---|---|
| Paper 1 Section B (2 hours for the whole paper, 60 marks) | Two questions per depth study; answer one. Each has parts (a) 4 marks, (b) 6 marks, (c) 10 marks |
| Component 3 Coursework (40 marks) | One extended piece of up to 2000 words on the significance of an individual, group, development or event |
| Paper 4 Alternative to Coursework (1 hour, 40 marks) | Two questions per depth study; answer one, in parts (a) (AO1 account) and (b) (AO2 significance) |

## Depth Study A: The First World War, 1914-18

| Key question | Must-know facts |
|---|---|
| 1 Why was there stalemate on the Western Front? | Schlieffen Plan slowed by Belgian resistance, the BEF at Mons (August 1914) and faster-than-expected Russian mobilisation; Battle of the Marne (September 1914); race to the sea; First Ypres. Trench warfare and attrition; machine guns, gas (first major German use at Ypres, April 1915), tanks (Somme, September 1916), aircraft. Verdun (February-December 1916); Somme (July-November 1916), Haig's tactics, about 57,000 British casualties on 1 July |
| 2 To what extent was it a world war? | Indian and Canadian troops on the Western Front; Africa: German East Africa (Lettow-Vorbeck unbeaten until November 1918) and South West Africa (taken by South African forces, 1915); Japan's siege of Tsingtao (1914) and naval escort work; the Arab Revolt (from 1916) |
| 3 How important were other fronts? | Jutland (1916): British losses heavier, but the German fleet stayed in port; U-boats, unrestricted submarine warfare (1917) and convoys; Gallipoli (1915-16) failure; the Eastern Front and Russia's defeat (Brest-Litovsk, 1918); civilians -- blockade, rationing, air raids |
| 4 Why did Germany ask for an armistice? | US entry (April 1917); the failed Ludendorff Spring Offensive (March 1918); Allied advance from Amiens (August 1918); shortages and unrest at home; Kiel Mutiny and German Revolution; Kaiser abdicates (9 November); Armistice (11 November 1918) |

## Depth Study B: Germany, 1918-45

| Key question | Must-know facts |
|---|---|
| 1 Was Weimar doomed from the start? | Revolution of 1918; Weimar Constitution (proportional representation, Article 48); Versailles and the "stab in the back" myth; Spartacists (1919) and Kapp Putsch (1920); Ruhr occupation and hyperinflation (1923); Stresemann: Rentenmark, Dawes Plan (1924), Locarno (1925), League membership (1926); cultural achievements |
| 2 Why could Hitler dominate by 1934? | 25-Point Programme; Munich Putsch (1923); little success before 1930 (2.6% of the vote in 1928); Depression, unemployment and Nazi propaganda; 230 seats in July 1932; appointed Chancellor (January 1933); Reichstag Fire and March 1933 election; Enabling Act; Night of the Long Knives; Hindenburg's death (August 1934) |
| 3(a) How effectively did the Nazis control Germany? | SS, Gestapo, camps; Goebbels' propaganda, radio, rallies, censorship; persecution of Jews and minorities; opposition from churches, youth groups and the 1944 bomb plot; debate on whether Nazi Germany was totalitarian |
| 3(b) What was life like? | Hitler Youth and League of German Maidens; women and the family (marriage loans, Mother's Cross); economic recovery, DAF and KdF; anti-Semitism (Nuremberg Laws 1935, Kristallnacht 1938); war economy, rationing, bombing and the Final Solution |

## Depth Study C: Russia, 1905-41

| Key question | Must-know facts |
|---|---|
| 1 Why did Tsarism collapse in 1917? | Autocracy, a peasant majority, small industrial working class; 1905 Revolution (Bloody Sunday), October Manifesto and Dumas; Stolypin's reforms and repression; First World War defeats, the Tsar as commander-in-chief (1915), Rasputin's influence; March 1917 strikes, mutiny and abdication |
| 2 How did the Bolsheviks gain and consolidate power? | Dual power of the Provisional Government and Petrograd Soviet; continuing the war; Lenin's April Theses; July Days; Kornilov affair; Bolshevik seizure (November 1917); Constituent Assembly closed (1918); Civil War, Trotsky and the Red Army, War Communism; Kronstadt (1921) and the NEP |
| 3 How did Stalin gain and hold power? | General Secretary; Lenin's Testament suppressed; Trotsky's errors; alliances against Left and Right; Purges and show trials; NKVD and gulags; cult of personality and official culture |
| 4 What was the impact of Stalin's economic policies? | Five-Year Plans from 1928, heavy industry, new cities; collectivisation, kulaks, famine (1932-33); differing experiences of workers, peasants, women and ethnic minorities |

## Depth Study D: The United States, 1919-41

| Key question | Must-know facts |
|---|---|
| 1 How far did the economy boom? | Republican policies (low taxes, tariffs such as Fordney-McCumber, 1922); mass production -- Ford's assembly line; hire purchase and credit; decline of coal and textiles; farm overproduction and falling prices; unequal distribution of wealth |
| 2 How far did society change? | Jazz, cinema, flappers; Prohibition (1920-33), gangsters such as Al Capone; immigration quotas (1921, 1924); Red Scare and Sacco and Vanzetti; Scopes trial (1925); Ku Klux Klan; discrimination against Black Americans; women's vote (1920) and limits of change |
| 3 Causes and consequences of the Crash | Speculation and buying on the margin; overproduction and weak banks; October 1929 crash; bank failures, unemployment (about a quarter of the workforce by 1933), Hoovervilles; Hoover's limited response; Roosevelt's 1932 victory |
| 4 How successful was the New Deal? | Hundred Days: Emergency Banking Act, CCC, AAA, TVA, NIRA; Second New Deal: WPA, Social Security Act, Wagner Act (1935); opposition from Republicans, the rich, business, the Supreme Court and radicals such as Huey Long; unemployment persisted until rearmament |

## Depth Study E: The Second World War in Europe and the Asia-Pacific, 1939-c.1945

| Key question | Must-know facts |
|---|---|
| 1 How did the war in Europe develop? | Fall of France and Vichy (1940); Dunkirk; Operation Sea Lion and the Battle of Britain; Operation Barbarossa (June 1941); Stalingrad (1942-43) as turning point |
| 2 How did the Asia-Pacific war develop? | US embargoes; Pearl Harbor (7 December 1941); Malaya and the fall of Singapore (February 1942); Midway (June 1942) as turning point |
| 3 Impact on civilians | Blitz (1940-41); Allied bombing of German cities (1943-45); Japanese control of Malaya and Singapore; Nazi control of Poland, ghettos and the circumstances leading to the Holocaust; resistance in Malaya and France |
| 4 How did the Allies win? | Sicily and Italy (1943-45); D-Day (June 1944) and the Soviet advance; German surrender (May 1945); Guadalcanal, island-hopping, Okinawa; bombing of Japan and the atomic bombs (August 1945); occupations; Nuremberg and Tokyo trials |

## Worked example: a Section B (c) answer plan

**Question:** "The failure of the Schlieffen Plan was the main reason for stalemate on the Western Front by the end of 1914." How far do you agree? [10]

- **Agree (explain):** the plan's failure at the Marne meant neither side could win quickly; both sides dug in to hold ground.
- **Disagree (explain):** defensive technology -- machine guns, barbed wire, artillery -- made attacks costly; the race to the sea ended with no open flank to turn.
- **Judgement:** the plan's failure began the stalemate, but the power of defence made it last.

The Cambridge mark scheme puts this at Level 5 (10 marks) only if both sides are *explained* and a supported judgement on "how far" is reached.

## Common mistakes / exam traps

- **(a) questions answered as essays.** Part (a) rewards four relevant points; save explanation for (b) and (c).
- **Identifying without explaining.** In (b) and (c), a reason with no "because" stays at Level 2.
- **One-sided (c) answers.** Explaining only one side caps the mark at 6.
- **Wrong date ranges.** Depth Study C stops in 1941; the Great Patriotic War is not included.
- **Mixing Weimar and Nazi periods.** Read whether the question is about 1919-29 or 1933-45.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many depth studies must I study?**
At least one. Section B of Paper 1 has two questions on each of the five, and you answer one question.

**How long should I spend on the depth-study question?**
Paper 1 is 2 hours for three 20-mark questions, so about 40 minutes, weighted towards the 10-mark part (c).

**Can my coursework be on a depth study?**
Yes. Component 3 can be based on a syllabus depth study or on a centre-devised one.

## Self-test

1. Why did the Schlieffen Plan fail? *Belgian resistance, the BEF, rapid Russian mobilisation and the Battle of the Marne.*
2. What was Article 48? *The emergency power of the Weimar president to rule by decree.*
3. What was the NEP? *Lenin's 1921 New Economic Policy allowing small private trade.*
4. What was buying "on the margin"? *Buying shares with borrowed money, paying only part of the price.*
5. Why was Midway a turning point? *Japan lost four aircraft carriers and the initiative in the Pacific.*
