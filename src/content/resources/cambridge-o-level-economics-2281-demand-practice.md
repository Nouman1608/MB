These questions cover section 2.3, *Demand*, of the Cambridge O Level Economics 2281 syllabus for examination in 2026: the definition of demand and the demand curve, extensions and contractions, individual and market demand, and the conditions of demand. All markets and figures are invented. Revise first with the [study guide on demand](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-2281-demand/) and the [revision notes on demand](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-2281-demand-revision-notes/).

## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** What would cause an extension in the demand for rice? **[1]**

A: A rise in consumers' incomes

B: A fall in the price of rice

C: A rise in the price of noodles

D: A successful advertising campaign for rice

**2.** Which **one** of the following would shift the demand curve for buses to the right? **[1]**

A: A fall in bus fares

B: A fall in the price of petrol

C: A rise in the cost of parking cars in the city centre

D: A rise in bus fares

**3.** Printers and ink cartridges are complements. The price of printers falls. What happens to the demand for ink cartridges? **[1]**

A: It increases

B: It decreases

C: It extends

D: It contracts

**4.** Define *demand*. **[2]**

**5.** Explain the difference between a contraction in demand and a decrease in demand. **[2]**

**6.** Explain **one** reason why a demand curve usually slopes downwards. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** The table shows the daily demand for sandwiches at a market stall by three groups of customers.

| Price (cu) | Workers | Students | Shoppers | Market demand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 30 | 25 | 15 | |
| 4 | 45 | 35 | 20 | |
| 3 | 60 | 45 | 25 | |
| 2 | 75 | 55 | 30 | |

**(a)** Calculate market demand at each price. **[2]**

**(b)** The price falls from 4 cu to 3 cu. Name the change in market demand and calculate the percentage change in quantity demanded. **[3]**

**(c)** A nearby college closes, so the students stop buying. Explain the effect on the market demand curve. **[2]**

**8.** Incomes in a city rise. Describe a diagram showing the effect on the demand for restaurant meals, and explain the change. **[4]**

**9.** The price of smartphones falls sharply. Explain the effect on the demand for apps sold for smartphones. **[3]**

**10.** The table shows the weekly demand for umbrellas in a town before the rainy season (D1).

| Price (cu) | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quantity demanded (D1) | 250 | 200 | 150 | 100 |

The rainy season increases demand by 50 umbrellas at every price (D2). At the same time, shops raise the price from 6 cu to 8 cu.

**(a)** State the quantity demanded on D2 at 8 cu. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the net change in quantity demanded, comparing 6 cu on D1 with 8 cu on D2. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain the result in part (b), naming the two changes involved. **[3]**

**11.** Explain **two** conditions of demand that could cause a decrease in the demand for petrol-engine cars. **[4]**

**12.** A sports drink firm launches an advertising campaign. At the same time, a rival lowers the price of its own sports drink. Analyse the possible effect on demand for the first firm's drink. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** C [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A [1]. **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* "extends" and "contracts" describe movements caused by the good's own price. A change in the price of a related good shifts the curve, so the answer is "increases" or "decreases".

**4.** The quantity of a good or service that consumers are willing and able to buy [1] at each possible price over a given period of time [1]. **[2]**

**5.** A contraction is a movement along the demand curve caused by a rise in the good's own price [1]; a decrease is a shift of the whole curve to the left caused by a change in a condition of demand, such as a fall in income [1]. **[2]**

**6.** One from, two marks: at a lower price the good is more affordable [1], so consumers with limited incomes can buy more of it [1]. Or: at a lower price the good is cheaper than its substitutes, so some consumers switch to it. **[2]**

**7. (a)** 70, 100, 130, 160 [2] (award one mark if two or three are correct).

**(b)** An extension in demand [1]. Quantity rises from 100 to 130 [1]; 30 ÷ 100 × 100 = **30%** [1].

**(c)** Market demand falls at every price by the students' quantities (for example from 130 to 85 at 3 cu) [1], so the market demand curve shifts to the left: a decrease in demand [1]. **[7]**

**8.** Diagram: price on the vertical axis and quantity of meals (per week) on the horizontal axis, with D1 sloping down and D2 to the right of D1, and an arrow showing the shift [1]. At the same price, a larger quantity is demanded on D2 [1]. Higher incomes make consumers more able to afford meals out [1], so more is demanded at every price: an increase in demand [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Smartphones and apps are complements, used together [1]. Cheaper smartphones mean more people buy them [1], so more people are willing to buy apps at every price, and demand for apps increases (shifts right) [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** 150 + 50 = **200** umbrellas [1].

**(b)** 200 − 200 = **0**: no net change [1].

**(c)** The rainy season is an increase in demand, shifting the curve from D1 to D2 and raising quantity at 6 cu from 200 to 250 [1]. The price rise is a contraction along D2, from 250 to 200 [1]. The two effects are equal in size and opposite in direction, so they cancel out [1]. **[5]**

**11.** Two from, two marks each: a rise in the price of petrol, a complement [1], makes running a petrol car more expensive, so fewer people are willing to buy one at each price [1]; a fall in the price of electric cars, a substitute [1], so consumers switch away from petrol cars [1]; a change in tastes towards cleaner vehicles; a fall in incomes, making cars less affordable; a tax on petrol-engine cars. **[4]**

**12.** The advertising campaign makes more consumers willing to buy the firm's drink [1], which tends to increase demand and shift the curve to the right [1]. The rival's drink is a substitute, and its lower price makes some consumers switch to it [1], which tends to decrease demand for the first firm's drink and shift the curve to the left [1]. The overall effect depends on which influence is stronger: if the advertising is very effective, demand may still increase; if the rival's price cut is large, demand may decrease [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* with two opposing influences, analyse each separately, then say that the net effect depends on their relative strength.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using "extension" or "contraction" for a shift, or "increase" or "decrease" for a movement.
- Forgetting the time period or "willing and able" in the definition.
- Adding prices instead of quantities in aggregation questions.
- Unlabelled diagrams, or shifts with no arrow and no D1 and D2 labels.
- Getting the direction of a complement or substitute effect wrong.
