## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define the term product trial. **[2]**

**2.** A short video made by a business is shared by thousands of people with their friends online. Which **one** method of promotion is this? **[1]**

A: Sponsorship

B: Viral advertising via social media

C: Branding

D: E-newsletter

**3.** Which **two** of the following are uses of technology in promotion named in the specification? **[2]**

A: E-newsletters

B: Paper leaflets

C: Targeted advertising online

D: Billboard posters

E: Sponsorship of a local team

**4.** Rookery Driving School spent £1,800 on adverts and gained 45 new learners. Calculate the cost per new learner. **[2]**

**5.** Bramble Boutique sends an e-newsletter to 6,000 subscribers. 20% open it and 5% of those who open it buy something. The average order is £30.

**(a)** Calculate the number of subscribers who open the newsletter. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the sales revenue from the newsletter. **[2]**

**6.** Copperfield Cider sponsors the town's summer music festival and sells its cider in cans with a distinctive fox logo. State **one** example of branding used by Copperfield Cider. **[1]**

**7.** Explain **one** disadvantage to a business of using special offers. **[3]**

### Section B: longer questions

**8.** Summit Climbing Wall sponsors the climbing club at a local secondary school. Outline **one** reason why this sponsorship may be an appropriate promotion strategy for Summit. **[2]**

**9.** Explain **one** benefit to a business of using targeted advertising online. **[3]**

**10.** Pinecroft Pizza sells 900 pizzas a week at £10 each. Each pizza has a variable cost of £4. For one month it offers 25% off every pizza, and sales rise to 1,300 a week.

**(a)** Calculate the change in weekly contribution during the offer. **[3]**

**(b)** Analyse the possible effects of the special offer on Pinecroft Pizza. **[6]**

**11.** Nimbus Yoga wants to attract young professionals who live and work in the city centre. It has £2,000 to spend over three months and two options.

Option 1: adverts in the local newspaper costing £2,000, expected to bring 25 new members.

Option 2: targeted social media adverts costing £900, offering a free first class. Each free class costs Nimbus £5 and 120 people are expected to take one; 30% of them are expected to join.

Justify which option Nimbus Yoga should choose. **[9]**

**12.** Hartwell Gin is a small distillery that sells a premium gin at £38 a bottle, more than twice the price of many supermarket gins. Its bottle has a hand-drawn label, and it promotes the gin mainly through its brand story, tastings at food fairs and an e-newsletter to past buyers.

Evaluate whether branding is the most important promotion strategy for Hartwell Gin. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** Letting customers try a product free or at a low cost [1] before they decide whether to buy it, for example a free sample or a free trial period [1]. **[2]**

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A [1]. C [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The third named use is viral advertising via social media. Leaflets, billboards and sponsorship are promotion, but not the technology uses listed.

**4.** £1,800 ÷ 45 [1] = **£40** per new learner [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** 6,000 × 20% = **1,200** [1]

**(b)** Buyers = 1,200 × 5% = 60 [1]. Revenue = 60 × £30 = **£1,800** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The 5% applies to those who open the newsletter, not to all 6,000 subscribers. Read each percentage carefully.

**6.** The distinctive fox logo on its cans [1]. **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* Sponsoring the festival is sponsorship, a different strategy.

**7.** A special offer lowers the price of each item [1], so profit per unit falls and the extra sales may not make up for it [1]. If offers are used often, customers may wait for the next one rather than pay full price, which reduces profit in the long run [1]. **[3]**

**8.** The students in the club are young climbers, the segment Summit wants to reach [1], so seeing Summit's name at their club builds awareness and goodwill among them and their parents, who may book sessions at the wall [1]. **[2]**

**9.** Adverts can be shown only to people chosen by age, location or interests [1], so less money is wasted on people who would never buy [1]. This means even a small business with a limited budget can reach likely customers and measure how many clicks and sales each advert brings [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** Before: (£10 − £4) × 900 = £5,400 [1]. During: price £7.50, so (£7.50 − £4) × 1,300 = £4,550 [1]. Change = **−£850** a week [1]

**(b)** Sales rise by 400 pizzas a week, about 44%, so more people try Pinecroft's pizzas [1]. However, each pizza now earns £3.50 instead of £6, so contribution falls by £850 a week while the offer runs [1]. To keep £5,400 at the offer price Pinecroft would need about 1,543 sales a week, far more than it achieves [1]. The offer may still be worthwhile if new customers enjoy the pizzas and keep ordering at £10 once it ends [1]. On the other hand, regular customers who would have paid full price also get the discount, and some may wait for the next offer [1]. Extra orders may also need extra staff or delivery drivers, which would reduce contribution further [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* Analyse answers gain from using the calculation: the fall in contribution shows that more sales do not automatically mean more profit.

**11.** Option 1 cost per new member = £2,000 ÷ 25 = **£80** [1]. Option 2: free classes cost 120 × £5 = £600, total cost £900 + £600 = £1,500 [1]; new members = 120 × 30% = 36 [1], so cost per new member = £1,500 ÷ 36 = about **£41.67** [1]. Option 2 brings 11 more members for £500 less [1]. Young professionals in the city centre are likely to use social media, so targeted adverts can reach them more precisely than a local newspaper [1]. The free class removes the risk of trying yoga for the first time, which suits newcomers [1]. However, the 30% conversion is only a forecast; some people may take the free class with no intention of joining [1]. **Judgement:** choose Option 2, because it costs about half as much per member and fits the target segment; even if only 20% joined (24 members), it would still beat Option 1's 25 at a lower total cost [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* Strong Justify answers use both the figures and the segment. Testing a lower conversion rate shows that the recommendation is robust.

**12.** Branding supports Hartwell's £38 price: customers buying premium gin are paying partly for image and story, so a strong brand lets it charge more than twice the supermarket price [1]. The hand-drawn label makes the bottle recognisable on a shelf or as a gift, which helps it stand out from many rivals [1]. Loyal customers who identify with the brand buy again and recommend it [1]. However, tastings at food fairs are a form of product trial and may matter as much, because customers are unlikely to pay £38 for a gin they have never tasted [1]. The e-newsletter keeps past buyers in touch and can prompt repeat orders cheaply [1]. These methods work together: the tastings and newsletter are more effective because the brand gives people something memorable to return to [1]. Special offers would be less appropriate, as discounts could weaken the premium image [1]. A small distillery may not be able to afford wide advertising, which also makes branding and low-cost methods more important [1]. **Conclusion:** branding is the most important strategy because it justifies the premium price on which Hartwell's profit depends, but it relies on tastings to win first-time buyers, so the answer depends on whether Hartwell is mainly trying to keep existing customers or attract new ones [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* An Evaluate answer is stronger when it compares the strategy with the alternatives in the case and states what the conclusion depends on.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using a percentage of all subscribers when it applies only to those who opened.
- Claiming a special offer is a success because sales rose, without checking contribution.
- Describing a strategy without linking it to the segment in the case.
- Confusing sponsorship with branding when selecting from a case.
- Ignoring the cost of free product in a trial.
