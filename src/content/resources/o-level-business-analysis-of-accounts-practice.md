## Questions

**1.** **(a)** Define profitability. **[2]**
**(b)** Define liquidity. **[2]**

**2.** Marren Stationers had revenue of 310 000, cost of sales of 217 000 and profit of 46 500 last year.
**(a)** Calculate its gross profit margin. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate its profit margin. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the profit margin is lower than the gross profit margin. **[2]**

**3.** Verity Pastas made a profit of 39 000 and had capital employed of 260 000. The owner could instead put the same money in a savings account paying 4% a year.
**(a)** Calculate the return on capital employed (ROCE). **[2]**
**(b)** Justify whether the owner should regard this ROCE as satisfactory. **[3]**

**4.** At the year end Verity Pastas had inventories of 48 000, receivables of 21 000 and cash of 11 000. Its current liabilities were 64 000.
**(a)** Calculate the current ratio. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the acid test ratio. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why the acid test ratio can give a better picture of liquidity than the current ratio for a business that holds large inventories. **[3]**

**6.** Identify **two** users of a business's accounts and outline one decision each might make using ratio results. **[4]**

**7.** A supplier and an investor are each looking at two businesses.

| Ratio | Elmstead Joinery | Dovecote Textiles |
|---|---|---|
| Current ratio | 1.5 | 1.65 |
| Acid test ratio | 0.5 | 1.1 |
| ROCE | 12% | 8% |

**(a)** The supplier must choose which business to offer 60 days' credit. Justify the choice. **[4]**
**(b)** The investor wants the best return on money invested. Justify the choice. **[3]**

**8.** Ravenscar Outfitters wants a bank loan of 100 000 to open a second shop. Figures are in thousands (000). The bank has asked for a comparison of the last two years.

| Item (000) | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 800 | 880 |
| Cost of sales | 520 | 598.4 |
| Profit | 80 | 61.6 |
| Capital employed | 500 | 616 |
| Inventories | 120 | 160 |
| Receivables | 30 | 34 |
| Cash | 50 | 10 |
| Current liabilities | 125 | 170 |

Year 1 ratios were: gross profit margin 35%, profit margin 10%, ROCE 16%, current ratio 1.6 and acid test ratio 0.64.

**(a)** Calculate the gross profit margin and ROCE for Year 2. **[4]**
**(b)** Calculate the current ratio and acid test ratio for Year 2. Give the acid test ratio to two decimal places. **[4]**
**(c)** Justify whether the bank should lend the 100 000. **[6]**

**9.** Explain why a profitable business may still have liquidity problems. **[4]**

**10.** Sorrel Textiles had a gross profit margin of 40% last year. This year its revenue was 560 000 and its cost of sales was 364 000.
**(a)** Calculate this year's gross profit margin. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain **two** possible reasons why the gross profit margin has fallen. **[4]**

**11.** Explain **two** ways in which a manager might use ratio analysis of the business's own accounts. **[4]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Profitability is how well a business earns profit [1] compared with its revenue or the capital invested in it [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Liquidity is the ability of a business to pay its short-term debts [1] when they fall due [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A define answer gains credit for a precise meaning, so say what is being compared (profit with revenue or capital; cash with debts due), not just that profit is good.

**2. (a)** Gross profit = 310 000 − 217 000 = 93 000 [1]. Gross profit margin = 93 000 ÷ 310 000 × 100 [1] = **30%** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Profit margin = 46 500 ÷ 310 000 × 100 [1] = **15%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Profit margin allows for all costs, including overheads such as rent and wages [1], while gross profit margin allows only for cost of sales, so less is left after all costs [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide by revenue, not cost of sales, and show the gross profit step; in part (c) name the extra costs rather than just saying "more costs".

**3. (a)** ROCE = 39 000 ÷ 260 000 × 100 [1] = **15%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 15% is well above the 4% the savings account would pay [1], which suggests the business rewards the owner for the extra risk of running a business [1]. A fuller judgement would compare with last year and similar businesses, but on these figures the return is satisfactory [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* For a justify question, answers do best when they reach a clear judgement supported by the numbers, and say what extra comparison would strengthen it.

**4. (a)** Current assets = 48 000 + 21 000 + 11 000 = 80 000 [1]. Current ratio = 80 000 ÷ 64 000 = **1.25** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Acid test = (80 000 − 48 000) ÷ 64 000 = 32 000 ÷ 64 000 [1] = **0.5** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the subtraction of inventories as a separate line; showing the method lets a reader see where a slip happened, and these two ratios are not percentages.

**5.** Inventories are the current asset that is slowest to turn into cash [1], and they may not sell at full value [1]. The acid test ratio leaves them out, so it shows whether the business could pay its short-term debts from more liquid assets [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Explain answers need the reason (inventories are slow to convert) and the effect (the acid test is stricter), not only "it excludes inventories".

**6.** Any two users with a decision each. A bank [1] may use liquidity and profit ratios to decide whether to lend [1]. A supplier [1] may use the current ratio and acid test ratio to decide whether to give credit [1]. (Shareholders, managers, employees or government also gain credit if the decision fits.) **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming a user earns the identify mark only; the decision must link to a result from the accounts, such as ROCE or the acid test ratio.

**7. (a)** Dovecote has the higher current ratio, 1.65 against 1.5 [1]. Its acid test ratio of 1.1 means liquid assets exceed current liabilities without selling inventories [1], whereas Elmstead's 0.5 means only half the debts are covered [1]. The supplier should offer credit to **Dovecote Textiles**, because payment is more likely [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** Elmstead's ROCE of 12% is higher than Dovecote's 8% [1], so each dollar invested earns more [1]. The investor should choose **Elmstead Joinery** on return, though its weaker liquidity is a risk to check [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Using different ratios for different users shows application; one answer that treats both users the same usually misses the point.

**8. (a)** Gross profit = 880 000 − 598 400 = 281 600 [1]. Gross profit margin = 281.6 ÷ 880 × 100 = **32%** [1]. ROCE = 61.6 ÷ 616 × 100 [1] = **10%** [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** Current assets = 160 + 34 + 10 = 204 [1]. Current ratio = 204 ÷ 170 = **1.2** [1]. Acid test = (204 − 160) ÷ 170 = 44 ÷ 170 [1] = **0.26** [1]. **[4]**
**(c)** Profitability fell: gross profit margin from 35% to 32% and ROCE from 16% to 10% [1]. Capital employed rose by 116 (000) while profit fell [1]. Liquidity weakened: current ratio from 1.6 to 1.2 and acid test from 0.64 to 0.26 [1]. Cash fell from 50 to 10 while inventories rose [1]. For the bank, this suggests that the business may find it harder to meet loan repayments [1]. On balance the bank should not lend now, or should lend only with security or after a plan to reduce inventories; revenue growth of 10% is a positive but does not outweigh falling profit and weak liquidity [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Strong answers connect two or three ratios to the lender's concern, which is repayment, and then give a decision; listing every ratio without a conclusion usually gains less.

**9.** Profit is earned over a period, but liquidity depends on cash available when bills are due [1]. Sales may be made on credit, so profit is recorded while customers have not yet paid [1]. Money may be tied up in inventories or new equipment [1]. The business may then be unable to pay wages or suppliers on time, even though it is profitable [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Distinguishing profit from cash is the central idea; answers that only repeat that liquidity is "important" do not explain the link.

**10. (a)** Gross profit = 560 000 − 364 000 = 196 000 [1]. Margin = 196 000 ÷ 560 000 × 100 [1] = **35%** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Any two. Suppliers raised the cost of materials [1], so the cost of sales rose compared with revenue [1]. Prices were cut or discounts given to attract customers [1], so less was earned on each sale [1]. (Wastage or more costly production methods are also acceptable if explained.) **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Giving ways to increase sales does not explain a fall in margin; the cause must change either selling prices or cost of sales.

**11.** Any two. Compare ratios with earlier years [1] to spot problems such as falling profit margin before they become serious [1]. Compare with targets or competitors [1] to decide whether prices, costs or inventories need changing [1]. (Using liquidity ratios to plan borrowing or collect money from customers is also acceptable.) **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A manager's use of ratios should be internal and practical (control, targets, planning); answers about lending decisions belong to external users.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using cost of sales instead of revenue as the divisor for gross profit margin.
- Using revenue instead of profit in ROCE.
- Leaving inventories in the acid test calculation.
- Writing a % sign after the current ratio, or omitting it on margins and ROCE.
- Describing a result without saying what it means for the business or user.
- Giving ways to increase sales when the question is about a fall in margin.
- Naming a user without linking a ratio to a decision.
- Reaching no judgement in a justify question.
