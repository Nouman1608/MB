These questions cover sub-topic 2(c) Gases in the atmosphere of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) specification, Issue 3 (September 2024): points 2.9 to 2.14, including the practical 2.14. None of these points has a C reference. One part uses the molar volume of a gas from point 1.35C and is marked **(Paper 2 only)**. Revise first with the [gases in the atmosphere study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-gases-in-the-atmosphere/) and the [gases in the atmosphere revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-gases-in-the-atmosphere-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** Give the approximate percentages by volume of nitrogen and oxygen in dry air. **[2]**

**2.** Name the third most abundant gas in dry air. **[1]**

**3.** Hydrogen burns in oxygen.

**(a)** Name the product. **[1]**
**(b)** Write a balanced equation, with state symbols, for the reaction. **[2]**

**4.** The gas formed when sulfur burns in oxygen is dissolved in water containing universal indicator.

**(a)** State the colour of the indicator. **[1]**
**(b)** State what this shows about the oxide of sulfur. **[1]**

**5.** State what is meant by thermal decomposition. **[1]**

**6.** Give **one** human activity that increases the amount of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** A student pushes damp iron wool to the closed end of a graduated tube, inverts the tube in a trough of water and records the volume of trapped air. She leaves the apparatus for a week.

| Reading | Volume of gas / cm³ |
|---|---|
| At the start | 75.0 |
| After 3 days | 63.1 |
| After 6 days | 59.4 |
| After 7 days | 59.4 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage by volume of oxygen in the air. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the iron wool must be damp. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the student took readings until the volume stopped changing. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest why iron wool is used rather than an iron nail. **[1]**

**8.** 100 cm³ of air is passed back and forth between two gas syringes over heated copper. The volume is measured after each pass, once the apparatus has cooled.

| Number of passes | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Volume of gas / cm³ | 100 | 88 | 83 | 80 | 79 | 79 |

**(a)** Describe the colour change of the copper. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the volume of gas decreases. **[1]**
**(c)** Use the results to calculate the percentage of oxygen in the air. **[2]**
**(d)** A second student stops after two passes. State and explain how his percentage would compare with the true value. **[2]**

**9.** A teacher demonstrates the burning of phosphorus in a tube of air standing in water.

**(a)** Describe what is seen as the phosphorus burns. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the water level rises in the tube. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the volume must be read only after the apparatus has returned to room temperature. **[1]**

**10.** A piece of magnesium ribbon is heated and lowered into a gas jar of oxygen.

**(a)** Describe **two** observations. **[2]**
**(b)** Write a balanced equation, with state symbols, for the reaction. **[2]**
**(c)** The product is shaken with water and universal indicator is added. The indicator turns blue. State what this shows about magnesium oxide and about magnesium. **[2]**

**11.** Green copper(II) carbonate is heated in a boiling tube. The gas produced is bubbled through limewater.

**(a)** Describe the colour change of the solid. **[1]**
**(b)** Write a balanced equation, with state symbols, for the decomposition. **[2]**
**(c)** State what is seen in the limewater. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why the delivery tube must be removed from the limewater before heating stops. **[1]**
**(e)** 6.175 g of copper(II) carbonate is heated until its mass stops changing. Calculate the mass of copper(II) oxide left and the mass of carbon dioxide given off. (Ar: Cu = 63.5, C = 12, O = 16) **[3]**
**(f)** **(Paper 2 only)** Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide produced at rtp, in dm³. (Molar volume = 24 dm³ at rtp.) **[1]**

**12.** Explain how carbon dioxide acts as a greenhouse gas, and why increasing amounts of it may contribute to climate change. **[4]**

**13.** Zinc carbonate is heated strongly.

**(a)** Name the two products. **[1]**
**(b)** The solid left is yellow while hot. State its colour when cold. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest why sodium carbonate gives no carbon dioxide when heated with a Bunsen burner. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Nitrogen about 78% [1]; oxygen about 21% [1].

**2.** Argon [1].

**3. (a)** Water [1].
**(b)** 2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(l): formulae and balancing [1]; state symbols [1].

**4. (a)** Red / orange (acidic colour) [1].
**(b)** It is an acidic oxide (so sulfur is a non-metal) [1].

**5.** The breaking down of a compound by heat alone [1].

**6.** Any one [1]: burning fossil fuels (for example in power stations or vehicles); deforestation / burning forests.

**7. (a)** Volume of oxygen = 75.0 − 59.4 = 15.6 cm³ [1]. % oxygen = 15.6 ÷ 75.0 × 100 = 20.8% [1].
**(b)** Iron rusts only when both water and oxygen are present [1].
**(c)** To make sure all the oxygen had reacted [1].
**(d)** Iron wool has a larger surface area, so it reacts faster [1].
*Marking note:* In (a), dividing by the final volume (59.4) instead of the starting volume is the most common error and loses the second mark.

**8. (a)** Pink-brown (orange-brown) to black [1].
**(b)** The oxygen reacts with the copper to form solid copper(II) oxide, so it is removed from the gas [1].
**(c)** Volume of oxygen = 100 − 79 = 21 cm³ [1]. % oxygen = 21 ÷ 100 × 100 = 21% [1].
**(d)** Lower than the true value (17%) [1], because not all the oxygen has reacted after two passes [1].

**9. (a)** It burns with a bright (yellow-white) flame and gives white smoke [1].
**(b)** The phosphorus reacts with the oxygen in the air, forming an oxide that dissolves in the water [1]. So the volume of gas decreases and water rises to take its place [1].
**(c)** Hot gas has expanded, so reading too early gives a volume that is too large (and an oxygen percentage that is too low) [1].

**10. (a)** Very bright white flame [1]; a white powder is formed [1].
**(b)** 2Mg(s) + O₂(g) → 2MgO(s): formulae and balancing [1]; state symbols [1].
**(c)** Magnesium oxide is a basic oxide / gives an alkaline solution [1], so magnesium is a metal [1].

**11. (a)** Green to black [1].
**(b)** CuCO₃(s) → CuO(s) + CO₂(g): correct formulae [1]; state symbols [1].
**(c)** It turns milky / cloudy [1].
**(d)** Otherwise cold limewater can be sucked back into the hot tube and crack it [1].
**(e)** Mr CuCO₃ = 123.5, so moles = 6.175 ÷ 123.5 = 0.0500 mol [1]. Mass of CuO = 0.0500 × 79.5 = 3.975 g = 3.98 g [1]. Mass of CO₂ = 0.0500 × 44 = 2.20 g (or 6.175 − 3.975) [1].
**(f)** 0.0500 × 24 = 1.20 dm³ [1].

**12.** One mark per point, up to 4:
- The Earth's surface is warmed by the Sun and gives out infrared (heat) radiation [1].
- Carbon dioxide absorbs some of this infrared radiation and re-emits some back towards the Earth [1].
- This keeps the lower atmosphere warmer than it would otherwise be [1].
- More carbon dioxide (for example from burning fossil fuels) traps more heat, which may contribute to climate change, such as rising average temperatures [1].
*Marking note:* Keep to the specification's wording "may contribute to climate change"; stating that carbon dioxide destroys the ozone layer is a different issue and scores nothing.

**13. (a)** Zinc oxide and carbon dioxide [1].
**(b)** White [1].
**(c)** Sodium carbonate does not decompose at Bunsen burner temperatures (it needs a much higher temperature) [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing the oxygen volume by the final gas volume instead of the starting volume.
- Reading the volume before the apparatus has cooled.
- Giving "it burns" as an observation without a flame colour or product.
- Writing H₂O(g) and then describing water collected as a liquid at room temperature; choose a state symbol that fits the conditions.
- Leaving the delivery tube in the limewater when heating stops.
- Confusing the greenhouse effect with ozone depletion.
