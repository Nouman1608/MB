AQA A-Level English Literature B (7717) studies literature through genre: how tragedy and comedy work, and how
writers use the conventions of crime writing or political and social protest writing. It suits students who enjoy
reading widely, discussing interpretations and writing extended, well-argued essays, and it is a natural route
towards English, history, law and other humanities courses. This updated specification was first taught in
September 2025, with first A-level exams in 2027. It is linear and not tiered, and it is assessed by two written
papers worth 75 marks and 40% each, and a non-exam assessment worth 50 marks and 20%.

## How the course is organised

The course has three parts, one for each component.

**Literary genres.** Students take one of two options: Option 1A, Aspects of tragedy, or Option 1B, Aspects of
comedy. Within the chosen option they study one Shakespeare play, a second drama text and one further text, and
at least one of these is from before 1900.

**Texts and genres.** Students again choose one option: Option 2A, Elements of crime writing, or Option 2B,
Elements of political and social protest writing. They study one post-2000 prose text, one poetry text and one
further text, with at least one text from before 1900, and they also prepare for writing about an unseen passage.

**Theory and independence.** This is the non-exam assessment. Students select two texts and write about them
informed by AQA's Critical Anthology, which introduces different critical ways of reading. Text selection is part
of the work, so this component gives students more independence than the exam papers.

Your school decides which options and set texts you study, so check those details with your teacher.

## How it's assessed

| Component | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 7717/1 Paper 1: Literary genres | 2 hours 30 minutes (closed book) | 75 | 40% |
| 7717/2 Paper 2: Texts and genres | 3 hours (open book) | 75 | 40% |
| 7717/NEA Non-exam assessment: Theory and independence | No exam duration | 50 | 20% |

**Paper 1** has three sections, each worth 25 marks: Section A is a passage-based question on the Shakespeare
play, Section B is an essay on the Shakespeare play, and Section C is an essay linking texts.

**Paper 2** also has three 25-mark sections: Section A is a question on an unseen passage, Section B is an essay on
a set text, and Section C is an essay connecting texts.

**The NEA** consists of two essays of 1,250 to 1,500 words each, on two texts, informed by the Critical Anthology.
One of the two pieces may be re-creative, accompanied by a commentary. The NEA is teacher-assessed and moderated by
AQA.

The qualification is out of 200 marks. The AS version of this specification has the code 7716; this page covers
the A-level (7717) only.

## How the parts fit together

Genre is the idea that holds the course together. On Paper 1, students ask how tragedy or comedy shapes a play or
novel: its structure, characters, endings and effect on an audience. On Paper 2, they ask the same kind of
question about crime or protest writing across prose, poetry and a further text. The habit of reading any text
as an example of its genre, and comparing how different writers use the same conventions, is useful in every
section of both papers.

The difference between the papers matters for preparation. Paper 1 is closed book, so students need secure
knowledge of their texts, including key quotations. Paper 2 is open book, which shifts the challenge towards
selecting and using evidence quickly and responding to the unseen passage.

The NEA connects to both papers through critical reading. The Critical Anthology introduces ways of reading that
can sharpen interpretations of the set texts, and the experience of building an independent argument for the NEA
strengthens the comparative essays in Section C of each paper.

## Planning your study

A sensible plan for the two years is:

1. **Begin with the Literary genres option and its Shakespeare play**, building close reading of passages and an
   understanding of the genre's conventions.
2. **Add the second drama text and the further text**, practising essays that link texts.
3. **Start the Texts and genres option**, reading the set texts alongside short unseen passages from the same
   genre.
4. **Introduce the Critical Anthology and plan the NEA** once you are confident with your set texts, leaving
   enough time to draft and redraft both essays. Your teacher will set the internal deadlines.

Throughout the course, read each set text more than once, keep a quotation bank for the closed-book paper, and
practise writing about short unseen extracts regularly. Timed essays can start once a text has been studied;
full papers are most useful in the second year. Paper-by-paper strategy is covered on the exam preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is AQA A-Level English Literature B assessed?**
By Paper 1 (2 hours 30 minutes, closed book, 75 marks, 40%), Paper 2 (3 hours, open book, 75 marks, 40%) and a non-exam assessment worth 50 marks and 20%.

**What options are there in English Literature B?**
Paper 1 offers Option 1A, Aspects of tragedy, or Option 1B, Aspects of comedy. Paper 2 offers Option 2A, Elements of crime writing, or Option 2B, Elements of political and social protest writing.

**Is there an unseen text?**
Yes. Section A of Paper 2 is a question on an unseen passage, worth 25 marks.

**What does the NEA involve?**
Two essays of 1,250 to 1,500 words on two texts, informed by the Critical Anthology. One may be re-creative with a commentary.

**When are the first exams for this specification?**
It was first taught in September 2025, and the first A-level exams are in 2027.
