## Equations

```
                 light
carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen
6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂   (light over the arrow)
```

| Item | Source or fate |
|---|---|
| Carbon dioxide | From the air, enters leaves |
| Water | From the soil, absorbed by roots |
| Light | Energy source, absorbed by chlorophyll |
| Glucose | Product: used or stored |
| Oxygen | By-product, released |

- **Chlorophyll** is a green substance in **chloroplasts**, found in **some plant cells and in algae**.

## Limiting factors

The rate may be limited by:

1. **low temperature**;
2. **shortage of carbon dioxide**;
3. **shortage of light**.

- The factors **interact**: whichever is in shortest supply limits the rate.
- Raising a non-limiting factor does **not** raise the rate.
- Light and CO₂ graphs: rise, then level off (another factor now limits).
- Temperature graph: rises to an optimum, then falls (enzymes damaged).

**Spotting the limiting factor on a graph**

```
line still rising → the factor on the x-axis is limiting
line flat         → something else is limiting
two lines, one higher → the factor that differs between them was limiting the lower line
```

## Over a day in a closed greenhouse

| Time | Most likely limiting factor | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Dawn | Light | Low light; CO₂ high after night-time respiration |
| Midday | Carbon dioxide | Bright and warm, but CO₂ used up by photosynthesis |
| Dusk | Light | Light falls again |

## Greenhouse economics

| Factor | How growers enhance it | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | Heaters | Costs fuel |
| Carbon dioxide | Burning fuel such as paraffin, or releasing the gas | A paraffin heater raises temperature and CO₂ together |
| Light intensity | Lamps | Most useful when daylight is short |

**Decision rule**: only enhance a factor that is limiting, and only if the value of the extra crop is more than
the extra cost.

Worked reminder (invented): heating costs £40 a week and raises the crop by 30 kg worth £1.50 per kg.
Extra income = 30 × 1.50 = £45; net = 45 − 40 = **+£5 per week**, so it is just worthwhile.

## Uses of glucose

- Respiration.
- Converted to **insoluble starch** for storage.
- Used to produce **fat or oil** for storage.
- Used to produce **cellulose**, which strengthens the cell wall.
- Used to produce **proteins**, with **nitrate ions** absorbed from the soil.

## Required practical: rate of photosynthesis

- Change: light (lamp distance), CO₂ (sodium hydrogencarbonate concentration) or temperature (water bath).
- Measure: oxygen given off by a water plant in a fixed time (gas syringe, measuring cylinder or bubble count).
- Control: the other two factors, same plant, same time for each reading.
- Repeat and calculate a mean; identify and leave out anomalies.
- Rate = volume of oxygen ÷ time (cm³ per minute).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Photosynthesis | Using light energy to make glucose from carbon dioxide and water, releasing oxygen |
| Chlorophyll | Green substance that absorbs light |
| Chloroplast | Organelle containing chlorophyll |
| By-product | A product that is not the main aim; here, oxygen |
| Limiting factor | The factor in shortest supply that restricts the rate |
| Optimum temperature | Temperature at which the rate is highest |
| Starch | Insoluble storage form of glucose |
| Cellulose | Material made from glucose that strengthens cell walls |
| Nitrate ion | Ion from the soil providing nitrogen for proteins |
| Sodium hydrogencarbonate | Solution used to supply carbon dioxide in the practical |

## Exam traps

- Light is **not** a reactant; it goes over the arrow.
- "Some plant cells", not all: roots have no chloroplasts.
- Temperature graphs fall after the optimum; light and CO₂ graphs level off.
- Name the limiting factor **for the conditions given**, not all three.
- Proteins need **nitrate ions** as well as glucose.
- Greenhouse answers need costs **and** whether the factor is limiting.

## Self-test

1. Write the balanced symbol equation for photosynthesis.
2. Where does the plant get carbon dioxide and water from?
3. What absorbs light, and where is it found?
4. Name the three factors that may limit the rate of photosynthesis.
5. On a graph of rate against light intensity, what does a flat section show?
6. Why does the rate fall at very high temperatures?
7. Why might carbon dioxide be limiting at midday in a closed greenhouse?
8. Give one advantage of a paraffin heater for a grower.
9. Give four uses of glucose in a plant.
10. Why is glucose stored as starch rather than glucose?
11. What else, besides glucose, does a plant need to make proteins?
12. A gas syringe collects 3.6 cm³ of oxygen in 12 minutes. Calculate the rate.

### Answers

1. 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂, with light over the arrow.
2. Carbon dioxide from the air; water from the soil.
3. Chlorophyll, in chloroplasts in some plant cells and in algae.
4. Low temperature, shortage of carbon dioxide, shortage of light.
5. Light is no longer the limiting factor; another factor is limiting.
6. The enzymes that control photosynthesis are damaged.
7. Photosynthesis has used up the carbon dioxide in the closed air, while light and temperature are high.
8. It raises both the temperature and the carbon dioxide concentration.
9. Any four: respiration; starch; fat or oil; cellulose; proteins.
10. Starch is insoluble, so it can be stored in large amounts.
11. Nitrate ions absorbed from the soil.
12. 3.6 ÷ 12 = **0.30 cm³ per minute**.
