Condensed notes for subtopic **3.2 Drawing linear graphs** of Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024, 2025 to 2027. The [Drawing linear graphs study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-drawing-linear-graphs/) explains each method with worked examples; the [Drawing linear graphs practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-drawing-linear-graphs-practice/) give exam-style questions with mark schemes.

## Syllabus outcome

Draw straight-line graphs for linear equations. Equations may be written as y = mx + c, with the terms in either order, or as ax + by = c.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Linear equation | x and y only to the power 1 | y = 3x - 2 |
| Table of values | list of (x, y) pairs | x = 0, y = -2 |
| x-intercept | where the line crosses the x-axis (y = 0) | (6, 0) |
| y-intercept | where the line crosses the y-axis (x = 0) | (0, 4) |
| Substitute | replace a letter by a number | x = 2 in 3x - 2 gives 4 |
| Coordinates | the (x, y) pair for a point | (2, 4) |

## Method choice

| Equation form | Best method |
|---|---|
| y = mx + c or y = c + mx | table of values (at least 3 points) |
| ax + by = c | intercepts (x = 0 and y = 0) plus a check point |
| y = k | horizontal line through (0, k) |
| x = k | vertical line through (k, 0) |
| through the origin, y = mx | table; the point (0, 0) is on the line |

## Table of values

| Equation | x | -2 | -1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y = 3x - 2 | y | -8 | -5 | -2 | 1 | 4 | 7 |
| y = 4 - 3x | y | 10 | 7 | 4 | 1 | -2 | -5 |
| y = x/2 - 3 | y | -4 | -3.5 | -3 | -2.5 | -2 | -1.5 |
| y = 2x + 1 | y | -3 | -1 | 1 | 3 | 5 | 7 |

Check: y goes up or down by a constant step each time x goes up by 1. The step is the gradient.

## Intercept method

| Equation | x = 0 | y = 0 | Check point |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2x + 3y = 12 | (0, 4) | (6, 0) | (3, 2) |
| x - 2y = 6 | (0, -3) | (6, 0) | (2, -2) |
| 3x + y = 9 | (0, 9) | (3, 0) | (2, 3) |
| 4x - 5y = 20 | (0, -4) | (5, 0) | (10, 4) |

Steps: put x = 0, solve for y; put y = 0, solve for x; plot both; check with a third point.

If the intercepts are awkward fractions, use two other whole-number points instead.

## Rearranging to y = mx + c

| Equation | Steps | Result |
|---|---|---|
| x - 2y = 6 | -2y = -x + 6; divide by -2 | y = x/2 - 3 |
| 2x + 3y = 12 | 3y = -2x + 12; divide by 3 | y = -2x/3 + 4 |
| 3x + y = 9 | y = -3x + 9 | y = 9 - 3x |
| 4x - 5y = 20 | -5y = -4x + 20 | y = 4x/5 - 4 |

Dividing by a negative changes every sign.

## Special lines

| Line | Type | Example point |
|---|---|---|
| y = 3 | horizontal | (0, 3), (5, 3) |
| x = -2 | vertical | (-2, 0), (-2, 4) |
| y = 0 | x-axis | (7, 0) |
| x = 0 | y-axis | (0, 7) |
| y = x | diagonal through origin | (2, 2) |
| y = -x | diagonal through origin | (2, -2) |

## Is a point on the line?

Substitute the coordinates. Both sides must match.

| Point | Line | Check | On the line? |
|---|---|---|---|
| (4, 10) | y = 3x - 2 | 3(4) - 2 = 10 | yes |
| (-1, -4) | y = 3x - 2 | 3(-1) - 2 = -5 | no |
| (4, -1) | y = x/2 - 3 | 2 - 3 = -1 | yes |
| (2, 5) | 2x + 3y = 12 | 4 + 15 = 19 | no |

## Reading from a graph

| Task | Method |
|---|---|
| y for a given x | go up/down to the line, then across |
| x for a given y | go across to the line, then down/up |
| Where the line crosses x-axis | set y = 0 |
| Where two lines cross | read the point; both equations are true there |

Example: y = x + 1 and y = 5 - x cross at (2, 3).

## Drawing checklist

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| Axes | label x and y; equal steps; number the scale |
| Points | at least 3, marked as small crosses |
| Line | ruler, sharp pencil, extend past the points |
| Label | write the equation on the line |
| Working | show the table of values or intercepts |

## Exam traps

- Negative x-values: 3(-2) = -6, and -3(-2) = +6.
- The order of terms does not matter: y = 4 - 3x is y = -3x + 4.
- A negative coefficient means the line slopes down.
- Horizontal lines are y = k; vertical lines are x = k.
- A zigzag or curved line scores no marks for a straight-line graph.
- Use three points. A third point catches mistakes.
- Do not stop at the plotted points; extend the line.
- Read both scales carefully.

## Self-test

1. Complete the table for y = 2x - 3 when x = -1, 0, 3.
2. Find the intercepts of 3x + 4y = 24.
3. Does (5, 7) lie on y = 2x - 3?
4. Write y = 6 - 2x in the form y = mx + c and state m and c.
5. Write the equation of the horizontal line through (4, -2).
6. Write the equation of the vertical line through (-3, 5).
7. Where do y = 2x and y = 6 - x cross?
8. Rearrange x + 2y = 8 to make y the subject.

**Answers**

1. **-5, -3, 3**.
2. **(0, 6)** and **(8, 0)**.
3. 2(5) - 3 = 7, so **yes**.
4. **y = -2x + 6**, m = **-2** and c = **6**.
5. **y = -2**.
6. **x = -3**.
7. 2x = 6 - x gives x = 2, y = 4: **(2, 4)**.
8. 2y = 8 - x, so **y = 4 - x/2**.
