Use these data throughout: NA = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹. Ar values: H 1.01, He 4.00, B 10.81, C 12.01, N 14.01, O 16.00, Na 22.99, Mg 24.31, P 30.97, S 32.07, Cl 35.45, K 39.10, Ca 40.08, Mn 54.94, Fe 55.85. Give answers to 3 significant figures unless told otherwise.

## Questions

**1.** A sample contains 0.0350 mol of phosphorus pentachloride, PCl₅.

**(a)** Calculate the number of PCl₅ molecules in the sample. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of chlorine atoms in the sample. **[1]**

**2.** Calculate the relative formula mass, Mr, of calcium phosphate, Ca₃(PO₄)₂. **[2]**

**3.** Calculate the number of oxygen atoms in 7.50 g of potassium nitrate, KNO₃. **[3]**

**4.** Iron(II) sulfate crystals have the formula FeSO₄·7H₂O. Calculate the percentage by mass of water in the crystals. Give your answer to 2 decimal places. **[3]**

**5.** An oxide of manganese contains 72.03% manganese by mass. Determine its empirical formula. Show your working. **[4]**

**6.** A liquid used in antifreeze contains 38.69% carbon, 9.76% hydrogen and 51.55% oxygen by mass. Its molar mass is 62.08 g mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Determine the empirical formula of the compound. **[3]**
**(b)** Deduce its molecular formula. **[1]**

**7.** A technician prepares 500.0 cm³ of sodium thiosulfate solution, Na₂S₂O₃(aq), with [Na₂S₂O₃] = 0.0800 mol dm⁻³.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of Na₂S₂O₃ needed. **[3]**
**(b)** Express the concentration in g dm⁻³. **[1]**

**8.** 20.0 cm³ of magnesium chloride solution, [MgCl₂] = 0.250 mol dm⁻³, is diluted with water to a total volume of 250.0 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate [MgCl₂] in the diluted solution. **[2]**
**(b)** State [Cl⁻] in the diluted solution. **[1]**

**9.** Ammonia burns in oxygen at high temperature. All substances are gases under the conditions used.

4NH₃(g) + 5O₂(g) → 4NO(g) + 6H₂O(g)

**(a)** State Avogadro's law. **[1]**
**(b)** 60 cm³ of ammonia is mixed with 100 cm³ of oxygen and reacts completely. Calculate the total volume of gas after the reaction, measured under the same conditions. **[4]**

**10.** Deduce which of these samples contains the greatest total number of atoms: 1.00 g of hydrogen gas, H₂; 1.00 g of helium, He; 1.00 g of methane, CH₄. Show your working. **[3]**

**11.** A gaseous hydride of boron, X, contains 78.11% boron and 21.89% hydrogen by mass. Its molar mass is 27.68 g mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Determine the empirical formula of X. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the molecular formula of X, showing your reasoning. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the number of hydrogen atoms in 0.554 g of X. **[2]**
**(d)** X burns in oxygen: X(g) + 3O₂(g) → B₂O₃(s) + 3H₂O(l). State the volume of oxygen that reacts with 15 cm³ of X, both measured under the same conditions. **[1]**

**12.** A solution of potassium sulfate, K₂SO₄, has a concentration of 8.71 g dm⁻³.

**(a)** Calculate [K₂SO₄] in mol dm⁻³. **[2]**
**(b)** State [K⁺] in the solution. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the number of K⁺ ions in 30.0 cm³ of the solution. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the volume, in cm³, of the solution that contains 0.0125 mol of K₂SO₄. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 0.0350 × 6.02 × 10²³ = **2.11 × 10²² molecules** [1]
**(b)** 5 Cl per molecule: 5 × 0.0350 × 6.02 × 10²³ = **1.05 × 10²³ chlorine atoms** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In part (b) the factor of 5 must appear in your working; an answer equal to part (a) shows you counted molecules, not atoms.

**2.** Ca: 3 × 40.08 = 120.24; PO₄: 2 × (30.97 + 4 × 16.00) = 189.94 [1]
Mr = 120.24 + 189.94 = **310.18** (no units) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Adding "g mol⁻¹" to an Mr is wrong, because Mr is a ratio; keep that unit for molar mass.

**3.** M(KNO₃) = 39.10 + 14.01 + 3 × 16.00 = 101.11 g mol⁻¹ and n = 7.50 / 101.11 = 0.07418 mol [1]
n(O) = 3 × 0.07418 = 0.2225 mol [1]
number of O atoms = 0.2225 × 6.02 × 10²³ = **1.34 × 10²³** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the moles at each stage; if the final number is wrong, visible working is the only way a marker can see which steps you got right.

**4.** M(FeSO₄·7H₂O) = 55.85 + 32.07 + 4 × 16.00 + 7 × 18.02 = 278.06 g mol⁻¹ [1]
mass of water = 7 × (2 × 1.01 + 16.00) = 126.14 [1]
% water = 126.14 / 278.06 × 100 = **45.36%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The question asks for 2 decimal places, so 45.4% does not follow the instruction; always obey a stated precision over the usual 3 s.f.

**5.** % O = 100 − 72.03 = 27.97% [1]
Mn: 72.03 / 54.94 = 1.311 mol; O: 27.97 / 16.00 = 1.748 mol [1]
ratio Mn : O = 1 : 1.333 [1]
× 3 gives 3 : 4, so empirical formula **Mn₃O₄** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Rounding 1 : 1.333 to 1 : 1 gives MnO, which is a real oxide but the wrong answer; recognise .33 as a signal to multiply by 3.

**6. (a)** C: 38.69 / 12.01 = 3.221; H: 9.76 / 1.01 = 9.663; O: 51.55 / 16.00 = 3.222 [1]
divide by smallest: C 1.00, H 3.00, O 1.00 [1]
empirical formula **CH₃O** [1]
**(b)** empirical formula mass = 12.01 + 3 × 1.01 + 16.00 = 31.04; 62.08 / 31.04 = 2, so **C₂H₆O₂** [1]
*Examiner insight:* For the molecular formula, show the division of molar mass by empirical formula mass; stating C₂H₆O₂ with no reason risks losing credit if the multiple is not evident.

**7. (a)** n = C × V = 0.0800 × 0.5000 = 0.0400 mol [1]
M(Na₂S₂O₃) = 2 × 22.99 + 2 × 32.07 + 3 × 16.00 = 158.12 g mol⁻¹ [1]
m = 0.0400 × 158.12 = **6.32 g** [1]
**(b)** 0.0800 × 158.12 = **12.6 g dm⁻³** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Using V = 500.0 instead of 0.5000 dm³ makes the mass a thousand times too large; check that a lab mass looks sensible before moving on.

**8. (a)** n(MgCl₂) = 0.250 × 0.0200 = 5.00 × 10⁻³ mol [1]
[MgCl₂] = 5.00 × 10⁻³ / 0.2500 = **0.0200 mol dm⁻³** [1]
**(b)** two Cl⁻ per formula unit: [Cl⁻] = **0.0400 mol dm⁻³** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the total final volume (250.0 cm³), not by the volume of water added (230.0 cm³).

**9. (a)** At the same temperature and pressure, equal volumes of all gases contain the same number of molecules. [1]
**(b)** 60 cm³ NH₃ needs 60 × 5/4 = 75 cm³ O₂, so O₂ is in excess by 100 − 75 = 25 cm³ [1]
NO formed = 60 cm³ (ratio 4 : 4) [1]
H₂O formed = 60 × 6/4 = 90 cm³ (all gases here) [1]
total = 25 + 60 + 90 = **175 cm³** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Read the conditions: here water is a gas, so it counts; in many combustion questions it is cooled to a liquid and does not.

**10.** H₂: n = 1.00 / 2.02 = 0.495 mol, atoms = 2 × 0.495 = 0.990 mol [1]
He: n = 1.00 / 4.00 = 0.250 mol of atoms; CH₄: n = 1.00 / 16.05 = 0.0623 mol, atoms = 5 × 0.0623 = 0.312 mol [1]
**Hydrogen gas** has the most atoms (5.96 × 10²³) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Comparing moles of atoms is enough; you need not multiply each by NA, but you must multiply moles of molecules by the atoms per molecule.

**11. (a)** B: 78.11 / 10.81 = 7.226 mol; H: 21.89 / 1.01 = 21.67 mol [1]
ratio B : H = 1 : 3.00 [1]
empirical formula **BH₃** [1]
**(b)** empirical formula mass = 10.81 + 3 × 1.01 = 13.84; 27.68 / 13.84 = 2 [1]
molecular formula **B₂H₆** [1]
**(c)** n(B₂H₆) = 0.554 / 27.68 = 0.02001 mol, so n(H) = 6 × 0.02001 = 0.1201 mol [1]
number of H atoms = 0.1201 × 6.02 × 10²³ = **7.23 × 10²²** [1]
**(d)** ratio X : O₂ = 1 : 3, so **45 cm³** of O₂ [1]
*Examiner insight:* Part (c) needs the molecular formula from (b); if (b) is wrong, set out (c) clearly so your method can still be followed from your own value.

**12. (a)** M(K₂SO₄) = 2 × 39.10 + 32.07 + 4 × 16.00 = 174.27 g mol⁻¹ [1]
[K₂SO₄] = 8.71 / 174.27 = **0.0500 mol dm⁻³** [1]
**(b)** [K⁺] = 2 × 0.0500 = **0.100 mol dm⁻³** [1]
**(c)** n(K⁺) = 0.100 × 0.0300 = 3.00 × 10⁻³ mol [1]
number = 3.00 × 10⁻³ × 6.02 × 10²³ = **1.81 × 10²¹ K⁺ ions** [1]
**(d)** V = n / C = 0.0125 / 0.0500 = 0.250 dm³ [1]
= **250 cm³** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Part (d) asks for cm³; giving 0.250 with no unit, or with dm³, does not answer the question set.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out the atoms-per-formula-unit factor when the question names atoms or ions.
- Giving Mr a unit, or giving molar mass without one.
- Rounding the mole ratio in an empirical formula too early, so 1.333 becomes 1.3 and then 1.
- Forgetting the oxygen percentage when only the metal's percentage is given.
- Using cm³ directly in n = CV.
- Dividing by the volume of water added instead of the final volume after dilution.
- Writing the concentration of an ion equal to the concentration of the salt.
- Forgetting the excess reactant gas, or counting a liquid product, in a final gas volume.
- Not following a stated precision, such as "2 decimal places".
