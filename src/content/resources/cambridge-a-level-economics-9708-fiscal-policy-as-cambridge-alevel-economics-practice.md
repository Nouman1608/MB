## Section A

**1.** Which is an example of contractionary fiscal policy?

A: A cut in the rate of sales tax

B: An increase in spending on new roads

C: A rise in income tax rates

D: A cut in interest rates

**[1]**

**2.** Which is an item of government capital spending?

A: Salaries of police officers

B: Construction of a new hospital

C: Interest paid on the national debt

D: Unemployment benefits

**[1]**

**3.** Distinguish between a budget deficit and a budget surplus. **[2]**

**4.** Define a regressive tax and give one example. **[2]**

**5.** A fixed annual licence fee of 150 cu is paid by a household earning 10,000 cu and by one earning 50,000 cu. Calculate the fee as a percentage of each income and state whether it is progressive, proportional or regressive. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why a government's budget deficit may widen in a recession even if it does not change its policies. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Country G has an income tax with three bands: 0% on the first 12,000 cu of income, 10% on income from 12,000 to 40,000 cu, and 30% on income above 40,000 cu.

**(a)** Calculate the tax paid and the average rate of tax on an income of 24,000 cu. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the tax paid and the average rate of tax on an income of 60,000 cu. **[2]**

**(c)** State the marginal rate of tax at 60,000 cu and explain whether the tax is progressive. **[2]**

**8.** Country H's government revenue is 250 billion cu. Its current spending is 230 billion cu and its capital spending is 45 billion cu. GDP is 1,250 billion cu and the national debt at the start of the year is 500 billion cu.

**(a)** Calculate the budget balance and express it as a percentage of GDP. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the national debt at the end of the year and the debt-to-GDP ratio if nominal GDP grows by 3%. **[2]**

**(c)** The average interest rate on the debt is 4%. Calculate next year's interest bill as a percentage of this year's revenue. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why it might matter whether the deficit financed capital or current spending. **[2]**

**9.** Explain three reasons for taxation. **[3]**

**10.** An economy has a large amount of spare capacity. The government raises spending on schools and hospitals.

**(a)** Explain the effect on aggregate demand. **[1]**

**(b)** Using AD/AS analysis, explain the likely effect on real output, the price level and employment. **[3]**

**11.** Explain why expansionary fiscal policy has a different effect on the price level when the economy is close to full capacity than when it has much spare capacity. **[4]**

**12.** Explain two reasons why a government spends money on public services. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. Higher taxes reduce disposable income and AD. Option D is monetary policy.

**2.** **B** [1]. A hospital is a long-lasting asset; the others are current spending.

**3.** A **deficit** occurs when government **spending exceeds revenue** in a period [1]; a **surplus** occurs when **revenue exceeds spending** [1].

**4.** A tax whose **average rate falls as income rises** [1]. Example: a sales tax or excise duty that takes a larger share of a low income, or a fixed licence fee [1].

**5.** 150 / 10,000 x 100 = **1.5%**; 150 / 50,000 x 100 = **0.3%** [1]. The share falls as income rises, so the fee is **regressive** [1].

**6.** In a recession incomes, spending and profits fall, so **tax revenue falls** [1], while more people become unemployed and **benefit spending rises**, so the gap between spending and revenue widens automatically [1].

**7. (a)** Tax = 0.10 x (24,000 - 12,000) = **1,200 cu** [1]. art = 1,200 / 24,000 x 100 = **5.0%** [1].

**(b)** Tax = 0.10 x 28,000 + 0.30 x 20,000 = 2,800 + 6,000 = **8,800 cu** [1]. art = 8,800 / 60,000 x 100 = **14.7%** [1].

**(c)** mrt = **30%** [1]. The average rate rises from 5.0% to 14.7% as income rises, so the tax is **progressive** [1].

**8. (a)** Spending = 230 + 45 = 275. Balance = 250 - 275 = **-25, a deficit** [1]. 25 / 1,250 x 100 = **2.0% of GDP** [1].

**(b)** Debt = 500 + 25 = **525 billion cu** [1]. GDP = 1,250 x 1.03 = 1,287.5; ratio = 525 / 1,287.5 x 100 = **40.8%** (up from 40.0%) [1].

**(c)** Interest = 0.04 x 525 = **21 billion cu** [1]. 21 / 250 x 100 = **8.4%** of revenue [1].

**(d)** Capital spending creates **long-lasting assets** such as infrastructure that can raise productive capacity, future output and so future tax revenue [1]. Current spending is used up within the year, so borrowing for it adds to the debt **without increasing future capacity** to pay it back [1].

**9.** To **raise revenue** to finance government spending [1]; to **redistribute income**, using progressive taxes [1]; to **discourage consumption of demerit goods**, by raising their price (also creditworthy: to influence aggregate demand) [1].

**10. (a)** Government spending is a component of AD, so **AD shifts to the right** [1].

**(b)** With much spare capacity the economy is on the **highly elastic section** of AS [1]. Firms can use idle resources, so **real output rises** with **little or no rise in the price level** [1]. Firms need more workers to produce the extra output, so **employment rises** [1].

**11.** With much spare capacity, firms can expand output using **idle labour and capital** without bidding up costs [1], so an AD shift raises output with **little effect on the price level** [1]. Close to full capacity, resources are **scarce**, so firms compete for them and costs rise [1]; output can rise little or not at all, so most of the AD shift shows up as a **higher price level** [1].

**12.** **To provide public goods** [1]: goods such as street lighting and defence are non-excludable, so free riding means the market would not provide them [1]. **To raise productive capacity** [1]: spending on education, health and infrastructure improves the quality and quantity of resources, supporting long-run growth [1]. (Also creditworthy: providing merit goods, redistribution, influencing AD.)

## Where marks are usually lost

- Applying the 30% rate to the whole of the 60,000 cu income in Question 7(b).
- Confusing the deficit with the debt in Question 8.
- Calling the fee in Question 5 progressive because both households pay the same amount.
- Leaving out employment in Question 10(b).
