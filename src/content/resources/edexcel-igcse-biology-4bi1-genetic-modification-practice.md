These questions cover sub-topic 5(c), **Genetic modification (genetic engineering)** (specification points 5.12 to 5.16), of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Biology (4BI1) specification, Issue 3. The qualification is untiered and none of these points is bold-referenced, so they can be examined on either paper. The set applies to the June and November series examined under Issue 3. A calculator may be used.

## Questions

**1.** Name the enzyme that:

**(a)** cuts DNA at a specific sequence of bases **[1]**
**(b)** joins two pieces of DNA together. **[1]**

**2.** State what is meant by each term.

**(a)** Vector **[1]**
**(b)** Recombinant DNA **[1]**
**(c)** Transgenic **[1]**

**3.** Bacteria can be genetically modified to make human insulin.

**(a)** Name **two** vectors that can be used to carry a gene into a cell. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the same restriction enzyme is used to cut the human gene and the plasmid. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why bacteria can make a human protein. **[2]**

**4.** The statements describe how human insulin is made using bacteria. They are not in the correct order.

A. The recombinant plasmids are taken up by bacteria.
B. The bacteria are grown in a fermenter.
C. Ligase joins the insulin gene into the plasmid.
D. The insulin is extracted and purified.
E. A restriction enzyme cuts the insulin gene from human DNA.
F. The same restriction enzyme cuts open a plasmid.

**(a)** Write the letters in the correct order. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the bacteria are grown in a fermenter and not in a small flask. **[2]**

**5.** One genetically modified bacterium is placed in a fermenter. It divides every 20 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the number of bacteria after 4 hours. **[3]**
**(b)** Each bacterium makes the same amount of insulin. Explain why the amount of insulin produced rises as the number of bacteria rises. **[1]**

**6.** In the past, insulin for diabetics was taken from the pancreas of pigs. It is now made by genetically modified bacteria.

Suggest **three** advantages of the new method. **[3]**

**7.** A company develops maize with a gene from a bacterium. The gene makes a toxin that kills a caterpillar pest.

**(a)** Explain why the maize is described as transgenic. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest **two** benefits to the farmer of growing this maize. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest **one** concern that people might have about growing this maize. **[1]**

**8.** Rice that makes a vitamin A precursor has been developed using genetic engineering. Some people think that it should be grown widely. Others disagree.

Evaluate the use of this rice. **[4]**

**9.** A student says: "Selective breeding and genetic modification are the same because both produce crops with useful features." Evaluate this statement. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** Restriction enzyme [1]
**(b)** Ligase (DNA ligase) [1]
*Examiner insight:* A single memorable rule: restriction cuts, ligase joins. Swapping the two loses both marks.

**2.** **(a)** A structure (such as a plasmid or virus) that carries DNA into another cell [1]
**(b)** DNA that contains pieces from different sources joined together [1]
**(c)** An organism that contains genetic material transferred from a different species [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Transgenic" needs the words "different species". Genes moved within one species are not transgenic.

**3.** **(a)** Plasmid [1]; virus [1]
**(b)** The same enzyme cuts both at the same sequence [1]; so the ends match and can join together [1]
**(c)** All living things use the same genetic code [1]; so the bacterium reads the inserted human gene and makes the protein [1]
*Examiner insight:* A good (c) mentions both the code and the reading of the gene. "Bacteria are simple" does not explain anything.

**4.** **(a)** E, F, C, A, B, D (accept F, E, C, A, B, D) [3] (1 mark for E and F first, 1 mark for C then A, 1 mark for B then D)
**(b)** A fermenter holds a large volume, so a very large number of bacteria can be grown [1]; so a large amount of insulin is made, and the conditions (temperature, oxygen, nutrients) can be controlled [1]
*Examiner insight:* Cutting the gene and cutting the plasmid can be in either order, but both must come before ligase.

**5.** **(a)** 4 hours = 240 minutes ÷ 20 = 12 divisions [1]; 2¹² = 4096 [1]; so **4096 bacteria** [1]
**(b)** Each bacterium makes insulin, so more bacteria make more insulin [1]
*Examiner insight:* The number of divisions is the power of 2, not a multiplier. 12 × 2 = 24 is the usual wrong answer.

**6.** Any three of: the insulin is identical to human insulin, so it is less likely to cause an immune reaction; bacteria reproduce quickly, so a large quantity is made; production is cheaper or easier to scale up; no animals have to be killed; supply does not depend on animal numbers [3]
*Examiner insight:* Give separate points. "Cheaper" and "less expensive" are the same point.

**7.** **(a)** The maize contains a gene from a different species [1]; the gene was transferred from the bacterium to the maize [1]
**(b)** Any two of: less damage from caterpillars, so higher yield; less pesticide to buy and spray; lower costs or more profit [2]
**(c)** Any one of: the toxin may harm other insects, genes could spread to wild plants, effects on people are not fully known [1]
*Examiner insight:* A concern should be specific. "It is unnatural" is an opinion and does not score.

**8.** Benefit: it can reduce vitamin A deficiency in people who eat rice as a main food [1]; concern: possible effects on other species or the environment, or genes could spread to wild plants [1]; concern: long-term effects on health are not fully known, or the seeds may be costly for poor farmers [1]; a conclusion that weighs the points, such as "benefits could be large but testing and monitoring are needed" [1]
*Examiner insight:* One benefit and one concern are not enough on their own. The last mark is for a judgement.

**9.** Both produce crops with useful features, so the student is partly correct [1]; in selective breeding, parents are chosen within one species and the genes are passed on by breeding over many generations [1]; in genetic modification a gene is moved directly, sometimes from another species, in a single step [1]
*Examiner insight:* For "evaluate", say what is right in the statement as well as what is wrong.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Swapping the roles of restriction enzymes and ligase.
- Using two different restriction enzymes for the gene and the plasmid.
- Saying "plasmids are chromosomes".
- Calculating 2 × number of divisions rather than 2 to the power.
- Failing to use the words "different species" for transgenic.
- Giving only benefits when asked to evaluate.
