## Format at a glance

- **2 hours, 50 marks, worth 50%** of the final grade.
- Weighted **80% Reading (AO1), 20% Writing (AO2)** — the mirror of Paper 2.
- Two source texts printed in a **separate insert**, not on the question paper.
- **No dictionaries** permitted.

## The five Reading objectives

| Code | Tests |
|---|---|
| R1 | Understanding of explicit meanings |
| R2 | Understanding of implicit meanings and attitudes |
| R3 | Analyse, evaluate and develop facts/ideas/opinions, with textual support |
| R4 | How writers achieve effects and influence readers |
| R5 | Select and use information for specific purposes |

## Section A: Comprehension and Use of Language (25 marks) — Text A

Narrative, ~900 words, 20th/21st century.

- **Q1 Comprehension (16 marks):** R1 + R2. Explicit sub-questions — locate/restate. Implicit sub-questions — a clear inference **grounded in specific text detail**, not a disconnected guess.
- **Q2 Use of Language (9 marks):** R4 only. Marked on **effect on the reader**, not just meaning — a correct paraphrase of a word/phrase with no discussion of its effect is marked against the wrong objective.

## Section B: Summary and Short Response (25 marks) — Text B

Non-fiction/discursive, ~550–600 words.

- **Q3a Summary (20 marks: 10 Reading + 10 Writing), max 150 words:** R1 + R5 (Reading) and W2 + W3 (Writing), scored **independently** — missing a relevant point costs Reading marks even in fluent prose; disorganised/repetitive prose costs Writing marks even with every point present. Points past the 150-word limit are **not credited**.
- **Q3b Short response (5 marks):** R2 only — a clear inference from Text B, grounded in specific wording.

## Command words on this paper

**Identify** = name/select/recognise. **Explain** = set out reasons, make relationships clear, say why/how with evidence. **Summarise** = main points only, no detail. **Give** = answer from source or recall. Q3a is effectively a full-length "Summarise" — main points only, condensed, no incidental detail.

## Timing (2 hours, 25+25 marks)

~10 min reading both texts, ~50–55 min on Section A, ~50–55 min on Section B (extra planning time for the 150-word limit in 3a). Leave a short buffer to check Q1/3b answers are grounded in specific detail, not general impressions.

## Worked example: relevance-checking a summary point

The summary instruction asks candidates to summarise "the disadvantages of city living" described in Text B. A candidate has drafted a sentence about a character in Text B enjoying a countryside holiday. Before including it, check it against the instruction directly, not just "is this true of the text": the sentence describes an *advantage of countryside living*, not a disadvantage of city living, so despite being accurate to the text, it is irrelevant to the specific instruction and would not earn Reading marks — it would also cost valuable word-count space that could carry a genuinely relevant point instead. This relevance check, run point by point against the exact wording of the instruction rather than against the text as a whole, is the single most direct way to improve a Section B mark, since most lost marks come from including irrelevant or off-target material rather than from getting individual points factually wrong.

## Why the two source texts are printed in a separate insert

Unlike Paper 2, where source material sits directly on the question paper, Paper 1's two texts are printed in a separate insert. This is a practical, not just cosmetic, difference: it means the insert can be kept open alongside the question paper throughout, allowing a candidate to check textual detail while answering without flipping between a single combined document — a difference worth being aware of before sitting the exam so the physical layout on the day does not cost time.

## Exam traps

- Correctly paraphrasing a Q2 quotation's meaning without addressing its *effect* on the reader — marked against R4, not R1/R2.
- Copying phrases verbatim from Text B in the summary instead of re-expressing them in your own words as connected prose.
- Writing past the 150-word limit — points made after the limit are not credited at all.
- Treating Reading and Writing marks in Q3a as one combined score — they are awarded independently, so both accuracy of selection AND quality of expression matter.
- Giving a general impression in Q1/3b implicit-meaning answers instead of an inference tied to specific wording.

## Self-test

1. What is Paper 1's Reading/Writing weighting, and how does it compare with Paper 2?
2. Which assessment objective does Question 2 (Use of Language) test, and what specifically must an answer address to earn those marks?
3. What two Reading objectives and two Writing objectives does the summary task (3a) test?
4. Why can a summary lose marks even if every relevant point is included?
5. What is the word limit for Question 3a, and what happens to points made beyond it?
6. What is the difference between an explicit-meaning and an implicit-meaning sub-question in Question 1?

**Answers:** 1. 80% Reading, 20% Writing — the mirror image of Paper 2, which is 80% Writing, 20% Reading. 2. R4; the answer must address the *effect* the writer's language choice has on the reader, not just what the word or phrase means. 3. R1 and R5 (Reading); W2 and W3 (Writing). 4. Because Reading marks (R1, R5) and Writing marks (W2, W3) are awarded independently — disorganised or repetitive prose costs Writing marks even where every relevant point is present. 5. No more than 150 words; points made after the limit is reached are not credited. 6. An explicit-meaning question is answerable by locating and restating information stated directly in the text; an implicit-meaning question requires inferring what is not spelled out, supported by specific textual detail.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the word limit for the Cambridge O Level English Language 1123 summary?**
The Question 3a summary must be no more than 150 words. Points made after the limit is reached are not credited.

**How are the marks split in the 1123 summary question?**
Question 3a is worth 20 marks: 10 for Reading and 10 for Writing, scored independently. Missing a relevant point costs Reading marks even in fluent prose, while disorganised or repetitive prose costs Writing marks even when every point is present.

**What is the Use of Language question marked on?**
Question 2, Use of Language, is marked against R4 only, on the effect of the writer's language on the reader. A correct paraphrase of a word or phrase with no discussion of its effect does not earn full marks.

**Can I use a dictionary in 1123 Paper 1?**
No. Dictionaries are not permitted.
