## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which description of genetic modification is correct?

A: choosing parents with useful features and breeding them over many generations

B: changing the genetic material of an organism by removing, changing or inserting individual genes

C: producing offspring that are genetically identical to the parent

D: growing microorganisms in a fermenter to make a product

**[1]**

**2.** A variety of potato has been modified so that its leaves are not eaten by a particular beetle. State which of the four types of genetic modification this is. **[1]**

**3.** A rice plant has been modified to contain a substance the body uses to make vitamin A. State which type of genetic modification this is, and give **one** benefit. **[2]**

**4.** **(Extended)** Name the enzyme used to:

**(a)** cut the human gene out of DNA **[1]**

**(b)** join the human gene into the plasmid. **[1]**

**5.** **(Extended)** Which term describes a plasmid that contains a human gene?

A: complementary plasmid

B: recombinant plasmid

C: restriction plasmid

D: sticky plasmid

**[1]**

**6.** Explain the difference between a herbicide-resistant crop and an insect-resistant crop. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Outline **three** different examples of genetic modification that involve inserting genes into crop plants. **[3]**

**8.** **(Extended)** Outline how bacteria are genetically modified to produce a human protein such as insulin. **[6]**

**9.** **(Extended)** A student wrote: "The plasmid can be cut with any restriction enzyme, because ligase will join any two pieces of DNA." Explain why the student is wrong. **[3]**

**10.** **(Extended)** A farmer grew conventional soya and herbicide-resistant GM soya on separate fields of equal size.

| | Conventional soya | GM soya |
|---|---|---|
| Yield / tonnes per hectare | 2.5 | 3.0 |
| Number of herbicide sprays per season | 3 (before planting only) | 2 (after the crop has grown) |
| Cost of seed per hectare / units | 60 | 90 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in yield of the GM soya. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in the cost of seed. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why herbicide-resistant soya can be sprayed after the crop has grown. **[2]**

**(d)** Suggest **two** disadvantages for the farmer or the environment of growing the GM soya. **[2]**

**11.** **(Extended)** Over several years, a population of insect pests feeding on insect-resistant maize became less affected by the toxin the maize produces. Explain how this could happen. **[3]**

**12.** **(Extended)** Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of growing insect-resistant GM maize. **[6]**

**13.** Explain **two** ways in which genetic modification differs from selective breeding. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* A describes selective breeding, C asexual reproduction and D fermenter use; only B is the syllabus definition.

**2.** Insertion of genes to confer resistance to insect pests [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Insertion of genes to improve nutritional qualities [1]; reduces vitamin A deficiency in people who rely on rice (accept: improves the diet of people who eat mainly rice) [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** Restriction enzyme(s) [1].

**(b)** DNA ligase [1]. **[2]**

**5.** B [1]. **[1]**

**6.** A herbicide-resistant crop survives being sprayed with a chemical that kills weeds [1]; an insect-resistant crop makes a substance that kills or harms insect pests that feed on it [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Mixing up herbicides and insecticides is one of the most common errors in this section.

**7.** Genes inserted to give resistance to herbicides [1]; genes inserted to give resistance to insect pests [1]; genes inserted to improve nutritional qualities [1]. **[3]**

**8.** The human gene is cut out using restriction enzymes [1]; leaving sticky ends [1]; the plasmid is cut with the same restriction enzymes, giving complementary sticky ends [1]; the gene is inserted and joined with DNA ligase, forming a recombinant plasmid [1]; the recombinant plasmid is inserted into bacteria, which multiply [1]; the bacteria express the gene and make the human protein [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Each named term earns credit. "Put the gene into the bacteria" without plasmid, enzymes or sticky ends scores very little.

**9.** The plasmid must be cut with the same restriction enzyme as the gene [1]; so that it has sticky ends complementary to those on the gene [1]; the complementary sticky ends pair up before ligase joins them, so non-matching ends would not pair (and the gene would not be inserted) [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** (3.0 − 2.5) ÷ 2.5 × 100 [1] = **20%** [1].

**(b)** (90 − 60) ÷ 60 × 100 [1] = **50%** [1].

**(c)** The soya contains a gene that makes it resistant to the herbicide [1]; so spraying kills the weeds but not the crop [1].

**(d)** Any two: seed is more expensive (50% more) [1]; seed may have to be bought each season [1]; (also accept: resistance gene may spread to wild relatives, producing herbicide-resistant weeds; fewer weeds reduces food and habitat for insects, lowering biodiversity). **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* In (d) "suggest" allows any reasonable disadvantage, but each must be a different idea.

**11.** There was variation in the insect population, and some insects were less affected by the toxin [1]; these insects survived and reproduced more [1]; passing on the genes for resistance, so over generations more of the population became resistant [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* This links section 21.3 to natural selection in topic 18; "the insects got used to the toxin" earns nothing.

**12.** Advantages (up to three): less of the crop is eaten by pests, so yields rise [1]; less insecticide needs to be sprayed, saving money [1]; fewer chemicals, so less harm to other insects and to farm workers [1]. Disadvantages (up to three): pests may become resistant to the toxin over time [1]; GM seed is expensive or must be bought each season [1]; the toxin may harm insects that are not pests, reducing biodiversity (or: gene may spread to wild plants; less genetic variation in the crop) [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A one-sided answer is capped well below full marks; give a balance of both and link each point to maize.

**13.** GM changes or inserts a specific gene directly, while selective breeding chooses parents with whole sets of features [1]; GM can use a gene from a different species and takes one generation, while selective breeding takes many generations within a species [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- An incomplete definition with no mention of individual genes.
- Restriction enzyme and DNA ligase swapped.
- "Same" restriction enzyme and "complementary" sticky ends missing.
- Herbicide and insecticide confused.
- Percentages calculated with the new value as the base.
- Pest resistance explained as insects "getting used to" the toxin.
- GM crop discussions that give only advantages.
