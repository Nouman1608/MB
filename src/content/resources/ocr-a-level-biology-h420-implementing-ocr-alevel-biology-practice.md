## Section A

**1.** Describe how to read the volume of a liquid in a measuring cylinder accurately. **[2]**

**2.** Convert

**(a)** 0.35 mm to μm, **[1]**

**(b)** 4.2 cm³ to mm³, **[1]**

**(c)** 250 mg to g. **[1]**

**3.** A gas syringe collects 36 cm³ of oxygen in 3.0 minutes. Calculate the mean rate of oxygen production and give the unit. **[2]**

**4.** A balance reads to 0.01 g. Explain why a student should zero the balance with the beaker on it before adding potato cylinders, and why the cylinders should be blotted dry before they are weighed. **[2]**

**5.** A student records the results of a catalase investigation as follows: "Temp = 20 degrees, volume = 8 cm, 8.50, 9". State **three** ways in which the way the data have been recorded could be improved. **[3]**

## Section B

**6.** A student uses a potometer to measure the rate of water uptake of a leafy shoot. In 5.0 minutes an air bubble moves 42 mm along a capillary tube with a radius of 0.40 mm.

**(a)** Calculate the volume of water taken up in 5.0 minutes, in mm³. The volume of a cylinder is πr²l. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the rate of water uptake in mm³ min⁻¹. **[1]**

**7.** Explain why a colorimeter is set to zero using a blank before measuring the absorbance of coloured solutions. State what the blank should contain, and give one other way of ensuring the readings are comparable. **[3]**

**8.** The diameter of a cell is 12 μm.

**(a)** Give the diameter in metres, in standard form. **[2]**

**(b)** A concentration is recorded as 0.0025 mol dm⁻³. Write this in standard form. **[1]**

**9.** In a reaction, one set of conditions gives a reaction time of 12 s and a second set gives a time of 30 s. Calculate the rate for each, in s⁻¹ to 2 significant figures, and state how many times faster the first reaction is than the second. **[3]**

**10.** A student adds Benedict's reagent to a sample of food extract and heats it in a water bath. Describe the observations that would show **no reducing sugar** and the observations that would show a **high concentration** of reducing sugar. **[3]**

**11.** Identify **two** hazards in a dissection using a scalpel and a hot water bath, and for each suggest how the risk can be reduced. **[4]**

**12.** A student measures the effect of four pH values on enzyme activity. The volume of gas is collected in 2 minutes using a gas syringe that is read to the nearest 0.5 cm³, with three repeats at each pH. Draw a table with suitable column headings in which the student can record the results and the mean. You do not need to enter any data. **[4]**

## Answers

**How these answers work.** Marking points are shown in brackets. The mark total for each question is the sum of the points. Alternative wording that carries the same meaning is credited.

**1.** Place the cylinder on a flat surface and bring the eye level with the scale [1]. Read the volume at the bottom of the meniscus [1]. **[2]**

**2. (a)** 0.35 × 1000 = **350 μm** [1].

**(b)** 4.2 × 1000 = **4200 mm³** [1].

**(c)** 250 ÷ 1000 = **0.25 g** [1]. **[3]**

**3.** Rate = 36 ÷ 3.0 [1] = **12 cm³ min⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The unit earns a mark of its own. Write it as cm³ min⁻¹, with the minute below the line.

**4.** Zeroing with the beaker on removes the mass of the container, so only the mass of the potato is shown [1]. Blotting removes water on the surface, which would otherwise add to the mass of the potato and give a false value [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Any three [1 each]: put the quantity and unit in the column headings (Temperature / °C, Volume / cm³) and not in the cells; use the correct unit, cm³, not cm; record all values to the same number of decimal places, matching the syringe resolution (8.0, 8.5, 9.0); use a table with separate columns for repeats; put the independent variable (temperature) in the first column; add a column for the mean. **[3]**

**6. (a)** V = π × 0.40² × 42 [1] = **21 mm³** (21.1 mm³) [1].

**(b)** Rate = 21.1 ÷ 5.0 = **4.2 mm³ min⁻¹** [1]. **[3]**

**7.** The blank contains everything in the sample except the coloured substance, for example distilled water or the solvent [1]. Setting it to zero removes the absorbance caused by the solvent and the cuvette, so readings show only the coloured substance [1]. Other acceptable: use the same cuvette (or matched cuvettes) each time; use the same filter; wipe the outside of the cuvette; use the same volume [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** 12 μm = 12 × 10⁻⁶ m [1] = **1.2 × 10⁻⁵ m** [1].

**(b)** **2.5 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³** [1]. **[3]**

**9.** First: 1 ÷ 12 = **0.083 s⁻¹** [1]. Second: 1 ÷ 30 = **0.033 s⁻¹** [1]. 30 ÷ 12 = 2.5, so the first reaction is **2.5 times faster** [1]. **[3]**

**10.** No reducing sugar: the solution stays blue [1]. High concentration: an orange to brick-red precipitate forms [1]. The colour change goes through green, then yellow, then orange as the concentration increases, and the reagent must be heated [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Scalpel is sharp [1]: cut on a dissection board, cutting away from the body, and keep fingers behind the blade [1]. Hot water can scald [1]: carry only small volumes, stand the water bath on a stable surface away from the edge, and wear eye protection [1]. Other acceptable: biological material may carry microorganisms, so wash hands and disinfect surfaces. **[4]**

**12.** Marks [1 each]: first column headed "pH" (independent variable first) [1]; separate columns for Repeat 1, 2 and 3 headed "Volume of gas in 2 min / cm³" or similar with the unit in the heading [1]; a column headed "Mean volume / cm³" [1]; four rows for the four pH values, with the rows ruled [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 1: reading the top of the meniscus, or not saying that the eye should be level with the scale.
- Question 3: omitting the unit, or writing it as "cm³/min" in a way that is unclear.
- Question 6: using the diameter instead of the radius in πr².
- Question 8: moving the decimal point the wrong way.
- Question 9: giving the reaction times as the rates.
- Question 10: writing "it changes colour" without naming the colours.
- Question 12: putting the unit in each cell instead of in the heading.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why do I get marks for the unit?**
Many calculation questions give one mark for the correct unit. A rate is a quantity per unit of time, so the unit shows that you understand what the number means.

**Should I always round to the same number of decimal places?**
Raw data should have the number of decimal places that the instrument supports. Calculated values should not claim more precision than the data.
