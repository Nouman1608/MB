> candidates only.

## The Core statement

A hydrogen–oxygen fuel cell uses **hydrogen and oxygen** to produce **electricity**, with
**water as the only chemical product**.

```
2H2 + O2 → 2H2O        (no flame; energy released as electricity)
```

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fuel cell | Device in which a fuel reacts with oxygen to produce electricity directly |
| Fuel | Hydrogen, supplied continuously |
| Oxidant | Oxygen, usually from the air |
| Combustion | Burning: a fuel reacting with oxygen with a flame, releasing heat |
| Efficiency | Proportion of energy supplied that ends up as useful energy |
| Point of use | Where the energy is used — here, the vehicle itself |

## Fuel cell versus battery versus burning

| | Fuel cell | Battery | Burning hydrogen |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reactants | Supplied continuously | Fixed amount inside | Supplied continuously |
| Energy out mainly as | Electricity | Electricity | Heat (flame) |
| Stops when | Fuel supply stops | Reactants used up | Fuel supply stops |

## Petrol engine exhaust (for comparison)

- CO₂ — complete combustion; greenhouse gas.
- CO — incomplete combustion; toxic.
- Oxides of nitrogen — N₂ + O₂ at high engine temperature; acid rain, photochemical smog.
- Unburnt hydrocarbons and particulates.

## Advantages over petrol engines (Supplement)

1. **Only water produced** — no CO₂, CO or oxides of nitrogen from the vehicle.
2. **More efficient** — chemical → electrical energy directly; petrol engines lose much
   energy as heat.
3. **Hydrogen can be made from water** by electrolysis — not dependent on crude oil.
4. **Fewer moving parts** — quieter.

## Disadvantages compared with petrol engines (Supplement)

1. **Storage** — hydrogen is a low-density gas, needing high-pressure tanks; petrol is a
   liquid.
2. **Flammable/explosive** — leaks are dangerous.
3. **Few refuelling stations.**
4. **Hydrogen production** — often from methane (releases CO₂) or by electrolysis using
   electricity that may come from fossil fuels.
5. **Cost** — fuel-cell vehicles are expensive to make.

## Building a two-part comparison point

Every Supplement point needs **feature + reason + contrast with petrol**:

| Weak (no credit likely) | Strong |
|---|---|
| "It's cleaner." | "Only water is produced, so unlike a petrol engine it releases no carbon dioxide or carbon monoxide." |
| "It's more efficient." | "Chemical energy goes straight to electrical energy, whereas a petrol engine loses much energy as heat." |
| "Hydrogen is dangerous." | "Hydrogen is highly flammable and must be stored as a gas under high pressure, whereas petrol is a liquid that is easy to store." |
| "It still pollutes." | "Hydrogen is often made from methane, which releases carbon dioxide, so overall emissions are not zero." |

## Responding to claims

| Claim | Balanced response |
|---|---|
| "Zero emissions" | True at the vehicle (water only); not necessarily true for hydrogen production |
| "Hydrogen is renewable" | Only if made by electrolysis powered by renewable electricity |
| "Fuel cells replace petrol easily" | Storage, refuelling network and cost are still obstacles |

## Numbers to know (Supplement, links to 3.3)

- Hydrogen : oxygen react in a **2 : 1** mole ratio, so 2 : 1 by volume at r.t.p.
- 2 mol H₂ (4 g) → 2 mol H₂O (36 g).
- So 48 dm³ of hydrogen at r.t.p. needs 24 dm³ of oxygen, and 1 g of hydrogen gives 9 g of water.

## Exam traps

- Fuel cells produce **water**, not "nothing".
- There is **no combustion** in a fuel cell.
- Water vapour is **not** a pollutant here.
- H₂ + O₂ → H₂O is **unbalanced**; the equation needs 2H₂ and 2H₂O.
- Hydrogen is the **fuel**; oxygen is the other reactant, not a second fuel.
- "No CO₂ at all" is too strong — the vehicle emits none, but making hydrogen may.
- Compare with **petrol engines**, not batteries, unless the question says otherwise.
- One-word answers ("cleaner", "dangerous") need the reason.
- Give the number of points asked for: two advantages **and** two disadvantages, not four of
  one kind.

## Self-test

1. Name the two reactants in a hydrogen–oxygen fuel cell.
2. Name the only chemical product.
3. Write the overall equation.
4. What form of energy is the main output?
5. How does a fuel cell differ from burning hydrogen?
6. Why does a fuel cell keep working while a battery runs flat?
7. *(Supplement)* Give two pollutants from a petrol engine that a fuel cell does not produce.
8. *(Supplement)* Explain why a fuel cell is more efficient than a petrol engine.
9. *(Supplement)* Give one reason hydrogen is harder to store than petrol.
10. *(Supplement)* Why might a fuel-cell car still be responsible for some CO₂ emissions?
11. *(Supplement)* Give one practical disadvantage for drivers today.
12. In the fuel cell, which reactant is oxidised?

**Answers**

1. Hydrogen and oxygen.
2. Water.
3. 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O.
4. Electrical energy.
5. There is no flame; energy is released as electricity rather than mainly as heat.
6. Its reactants are supplied continuously; a battery contains a fixed amount.
7. Any two: carbon dioxide, carbon monoxide, oxides of nitrogen, unburnt hydrocarbons,
   particulates.
8. It converts chemical energy directly into electrical energy; a petrol engine converts
   chemical energy to heat and then movement, losing much energy as heat.
9. It is a gas with very low density, so it needs high-pressure tanks (and it is highly
   flammable).
10. The hydrogen may be made from methane, or by electrolysis using electricity from fossil
    fuels.
11. Few hydrogen refuelling stations (or high cost of the vehicle).
12. Hydrogen.
