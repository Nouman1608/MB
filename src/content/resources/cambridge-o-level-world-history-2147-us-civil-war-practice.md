These questions cover key question 1.4 of Cambridge O Level History 2147, the Paper 2 prescribed topic for November
2025 and June 2026.

## Section A

**1.** Describe the Missouri Compromise of 1820. **[4]**

**2.** Identify **two** ways in which abolitionists spread opposition to slavery. **[2]**

**3.** Describe the Dred Scott decision of 1857 and its significance. **[4]**

**4.** Describe the secession of the Southern states in 1860–61. **[4]**

**5.** Identify **two** Union victories of 1863. **[2]**

**6.** Describe the Black Codes passed by Southern states in 1865–66. **[4]**

## Section B

**7.** Why did the breakdown of the national political parties make war more likely by 1860? Explain your answer.
**[6]**

**8.** Why did the Confederacy find it difficult to fight a long war? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**9.** "Lincoln's leadership was the main reason why the North won the Civil War." How far do you agree? Explain your
answer. **[10]**

**10.** "Reconstruction failed because the North lost interest." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

**11.** Study Source A, which is invented for practice.

*Source A (invented)*: from an imagined editorial in a Georgia newspaper, November 1860. "The North has chosen a
president without asking a single Southern vote. They say he will not touch our property where it stands. But a
fence around a field is the first step to ploughing it under. We must decide now whether to wait for the plough."

What can you learn from Source A about Southern reactions to Lincoln's election? Use the source and your knowledge to
explain your answer. **[6]**

**12.** Study Source B, which is invented for practice.

*Source B (invented)*: from an imagined letter by a Union soldier from Ohio to his brother, January 1863. "We are told
the war is now to free the slaves. Half the company grumbles that they enlisted for the Union, not for this. But I have
seen the people who come into our camp at night with nothing, and I think if the war is for them then it is a better
war than the one we started."

How useful is Source B as evidence about Northern attitudes to the Emancipation Proclamation? Use the source and your
knowledge to explain your answer. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Any four, 1 mark each: Missouri was admitted as a slave state (1); Maine was admitted as a free state (1); this
kept the balance of slave and free states in the Senate (1); slavery was banned in the rest of the Louisiana Purchase
(1) north of latitude 36°30′ (1); it settled the question of slavery in the West for about thirty years (1). **[4]**

**2.** Any two, 1 mark each: newspapers such as Garrison's *The Liberator* (1); speeches and writing by formerly
enslaved people such as Frederick Douglass (1); novels such as *Uncle Tom's Cabin* (1); helping people escape through
the Underground Railroad (1); petitions to Congress (1). **[2]**

**3.** Any four, 1 mark each: Dred Scott was an enslaved man who sued for freedom after living in free territory (1);
the Supreme Court ruled against him in 1857 (1); it said Black Americans could not be citizens (1); it said Congress
could not ban slavery in the territories (1); this made the Missouri Compromise unconstitutional (1); it angered the
North and strengthened the Republicans (1). **[4]**

**4.** Any four, 1 mark each: South Carolina seceded first, on 20 December 1860 (1); six more states of the Deep South
followed by February 1861 (1); they formed the Confederate States of America (1) with Jefferson Davis as president (1);
after Fort Sumter in April 1861 and Lincoln's call for troops, four more states, including Virginia, seceded (1); the
four border slave states stayed in the Union (1). **[4]**

**5.** Any two, 1 mark each: Gettysburg (1); Vicksburg (1); Chattanooga (1). **[2]**

**6.** Any four, 1 mark each: they were laws restricting the freedom of former slaves (1); freed people had to sign
yearly labour contracts (1); unemployed Black people could be arrested for "vagrancy" (1) and hired out to work (1);
they limited the right to own or rent land (1); they banned Black people from serving on juries or carrying weapons
(1); they angered Northerners and helped the Radical Republicans take control of Reconstruction (1). **[4]**

**7.** Levels. Level 4 (6): explains two reasons. Level 3 (4–5): explains one reason; 5 for a developed explanation.
Level 2 (2–3): identifies or describes reasons. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative content: **the collapse of the
Whigs** removed a national party that had needed Northern and Southern votes and so had supported compromise; **the
Republican Party was purely Northern**, so its victory in 1860 meant government by one section; **the Democratic split
of 1860** over a federal slave code in the territories divided the last national party and let Lincoln win with about
40% of the vote; **no broker was left** to pass a deal like the Crittenden Compromise. **[6]**

**8.** Levels as Question 7. Indicative content: **resources**: a smaller population and few factories meant it could
not replace losses of men and weapons; **the blockade** cut cotton exports and imports; **finance**: printing money
caused severe inflation; **states' rights**: governors resisted central control of troops and supplies; **morale and
desertion**, worsened by conscription exemptions for large slaveholders; **enslaved people escaping** to Union lines.
**[6]**

**9.** Levels. Level 5 (9–10): explained arguments on both sides and a supported judgement. Level 4 (7–8): explains
both sides. Level 3 (4–6): explains one side. Level 2 (2–3): describes. Level 1 (1): general points. **Agree:** Lincoln
kept the border states in the Union; the Emancipation Proclamation weakened the Southern economy, allowed Black
recruitment and kept Britain and France out; he persisted through defeats until he appointed Grant; his re-election in
1864 ended hopes of a negotiated peace. **Disagree:** Northern population, industry and railways; the naval blockade;
Grant's and Sherman's campaigns; Confederate weaknesses such as inflation, states' rights disputes and desertion; the
failure of "King Cotton" diplomacy. **Judgement:** for example, resources made victory possible but not certain;
Lincoln's political leadership ensured the North kept fighting long enough to use them. **[10]**

**10.** Levels as Question 9. **Agree:** by the 1870s Northern voters cared more about the economy, especially after
the depression of 1873; corruption scandals in Grant's government reduced support; Republicans lost control of the
House in 1874; in 1877 troops were withdrawn as part of a political bargain. **Disagree:** white Southern resistance
through the Black Codes and Ku Klux Klan terror; Andrew Johnson's lenient policy in 1865 let former Confederates
regain power; freed people never received land, so they stayed economically dependent; racist attitudes existed in the
North as well. **Judgement:** loss of Northern interest explains why Reconstruction ended in 1877, but Southern
resistance and the failure to give land explain why it achieved so little before then. **[10]**

**11.** Levels. Level 3 (5–6): developed inferences supported from the source and context. Level 2 (2–4): inferences
or details explained. Level 1 (1): copies or paraphrases. Indicative content: Southerners resented that Lincoln won
without any Southern votes, showing they had lost power in the federal government; they did not trust promises to
leave slavery alone where it existed; they believed stopping its spread ("a fence around a field") would end it in
time; the writer is preparing readers for secession ("decide now"). Context: Georgia seceded in January 1861. **[6]**

**12.** Levels. Level 4 (7–8): evaluates usefulness through content and provenance, with knowledge, and reaches a
judgement. Level 3 (5–6): evaluates content or provenance. Level 2 (2–4): uses content at face value. Level 1 (1):
general comment. **Useful:** written in January 1863, the month the Proclamation took effect; a private letter, so the
soldier has little reason to hide his views; shows both resentment ("enlisted for the Union, not for this") and
growing support; matches knowledge that Northern opinion was divided and that Democrats opposed emancipation.
**Limits:** one soldier from one company; his own change of mind may not be typical; it says nothing about civilian
or political opinion, such as the Democratic gains in the 1862 elections. **Judgement:** useful for showing divided
army opinion, but needs other sources for Northern attitudes as a whole. **[8]**
