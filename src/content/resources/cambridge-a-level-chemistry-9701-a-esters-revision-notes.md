**Syllabus:** Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, **A Level** subtopic
33.2: "reaction of alcohols with acyl chlorides using the formation of ethyl ethanoate and phenyl
benzoate as examples".

## The reaction in one line

**Acyl chloride + alcohol (or phenol) → ester + HCl**, at **room temperature**, no catalyst, goes to
completion.

    CH₃COCl + CH₃CH₂OH → CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ + HCl        (ethyl ethanoate)
    C₆H₅COCl + C₆H₅OH → C₆H₅COOC₆H₅ + HCl          (phenyl benzoate)

What you would see: a fast, often vigorous reaction, with **steamy fumes of HCl** in moist air.

## Two routes to an ester compared

| | Acyl chloride route (A Level, 33.2) | Carboxylic acid route (AS, 18.2) |
|---|---|---|
| Reagents | acyl chloride + alcohol or phenol | carboxylic acid + alcohol |
| Conditions | room temperature, no catalyst | heat, concentrated H₂SO₄ catalyst |
| Reversible? | no | yes, an equilibrium |
| By-product | HCl | H₂O |
| Rate | fast | slow |
| Works with phenol? | yes | barely; not a practical route |
| Main drawback | acyl chlorides are costly, react with moisture in air and give corrosive HCl | yield limited by equilibrium |

## Why phenol needs the acyl chloride

- A lone pair on phenol's oxygen is partly **delocalised into the ring**.
- That makes the oxygen a **weaker nucleophile** than the oxygen in ethanol.
- A carboxylic acid's carbonyl carbon is not electrophilic enough to react usefully with it.
- An acyl chloride's carbonyl carbon is much **more δ+** (oxygen and chlorine both withdraw electron
  density), so phenol reacts with it readily at room temperature.

## Naming and disconnection

- Name: **alcohol part first** (ethyl, phenyl, propyl), then the **acid part** ending in **-oate**
  (ethanoate, benzoate).
- Formula: written acid part first. CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ is ethyl ethanoate; CH₃CH₂COOCH₃ is methyl
  propanoate. These are isomers, C₄H₈O₂.
- To plan a synthesis, break the **C(=O)–O** single bond:
  - carbonyl side + Cl → the acyl chloride
  - oxygen side + H → the alcohol or phenol

| Target ester | Acyl chloride | Alcohol or phenol |
|---|---|---|
| methyl ethanoate | ethanoyl chloride | methanol |
| ethyl propanoate | propanoyl chloride | ethanol |
| phenyl ethanoate | ethanoyl chloride | phenol |
| methyl benzoate | benzoyl chloride | methanol |

## Linked AS reactions you may need in the same question

- **Acid hydrolysis** (dilute acid, heat): ester + H₂O ⇌ carboxylic acid + alcohol.
- **Alkaline hydrolysis** (dilute NaOH, heat): ester + OH⁻ → carboxylate + alcohol, not reversible.
- A phenyl ester in excess NaOH(aq) gives the carboxylate salt and **sodium phenoxide**, because
  phenol is acidic enough to react with NaOH.
- Acyl chlorides are made from carboxylic acids with PCl₃ and heat, PCl₅, or SOCl₂ (33.3), so a
  two-step route from a carboxylic acid to an ester of a phenol is: acid → acyl chloride → ester.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ester | RCOOR′: an acyl group RCO– bonded through oxygen to an alkyl or aryl group R′ |
| acyl chloride | compound containing –COCl; very reactive towards nucleophiles |
| acyl group | R–C=O part of the molecule |
| aryl ester | ester whose oxygen is bonded to a benzene ring, such as phenyl benzoate |
| nucleophile | species that donates a lone pair to an electron-deficient atom |
| addition–elimination | mechanism of acyl chloride reactions: nucleophile adds to C=O, then Cl⁻ is eliminated |
| percentage yield | (actual mass ÷ theoretical mass) × 100 |

## Exam traps

- Conditions are **room temperature** only. Adding H₂SO₄ or "heat under reflux" loses the mark.
- By-product is **HCl**, not water.
- The acyl chloride route is **not reversible**; do not draw ⇌.
- Phenyl benzoate has **two** benzene rings: C₁₃H₁₀O₂.
- In the name, the alkyl group goes **first**: CH₃COOC₆H₅ is phenyl ethanoate, not "ethyl
  benzoate" or "methyl benzoate".
- "Acyl chlorides react with alcohols" includes phenol in this subtopic, even though phenol is not
  an alcohol.
- For yield questions, find the **limiting reagent** before using the 1 : 1 ratio.

## Self-test

1. State the reagents and conditions to make ethyl ethanoate by the 33.2 method.
2. Write the equation for making phenyl benzoate.
3. Give two advantages of the acyl chloride route over the carboxylic acid route.
4. Explain why phenol reacts with benzoyl chloride but hardly at all with benzoic acid.
5. Name CH₃CH₂COOCH₂CH₃.
6. Name the acyl chloride and alcohol needed to make methyl benzoate.
7. What would you observe when ethanoyl chloride is added to ethanol?
8. Give the formula of the ester formed from propanoyl chloride and phenol, and name it.
9. Phenyl ethanoate is warmed with excess NaOH(aq). Name the two organic products.
10. Starting from benzoic acid, give a two-step route to phenyl benzoate, with reagents.
11. 0.920 g of ethanol reacts with excess ethanoyl chloride to give 1.32 g of ethyl ethanoate.
    Calculate the percentage yield.

**Answers:**

1. Ethanoyl chloride and ethanol; room temperature, no catalyst.
2. C₆H₅COCl + C₆H₅OH → C₆H₅COOC₆H₅ + HCl.
3. Any two: room temperature; no catalyst; not reversible, so higher yield; faster; works with
   phenol.
4. Phenol's oxygen lone pair is delocalised into the ring, so it is a weak nucleophile. The acyl
   chloride's carbonyl carbon is far more δ+ than that of benzoic acid, so it is attacked readily.
5. Ethyl propanoate.
6. Benzoyl chloride and methanol.
7. A fast (vigorous) reaction with steamy fumes of HCl.
8. CH₃CH₂COOC₆H₅, phenyl propanoate.
9. Sodium ethanoate and sodium phenoxide.
10. PCl₅ (or SOCl₂, or PCl₃ with heat) to give benzoyl chloride; then phenol at room temperature.
11. n(ethanol) = 0.920 / 46.0 = 0.0200 mol; theoretical mass = 0.0200 × 88.0 = 1.76 g;
    yield = 1.32 / 1.76 × 100 = 75.0%.
