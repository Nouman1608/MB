## The specification points

1. Explain what is meant by the product life cycle, its stages and its purpose.
2. Complete, understand and interpret a product life cycle diagram.
3. Evaluate the usefulness of the product life cycle to a business and its stakeholders.
4. Explain what is meant by an extension strategy and its purpose.
5. Analyse extension strategies.
6. Evaluate the usefulness of an extension strategy to a business and its stakeholders.

## Core recall

### Stages

| Stage | Sales | Profit and cash | Marketing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Development | None | Negative | Research, testing |
| Introduction | Low | Often losses | Awareness; limited distribution; skimming or penetration pricing |
| Growth | Rising fast | Profit rises, unit costs fall | Wider distribution; brand preference; rivals enter |
| Maturity | Peak then flat | Highest profit, cash generated | Defend share; differentiate; promotions |
| Decline | Falling | Falling; may become a loss | Cut costs; withdraw or extend |

### Purpose

- Plan the marketing mix for each stage.
- Forecast sales and cash flow.
- Time new product launches.
- Decide when to withdraw or support a product.
- Balance the product portfolio.

### Interpreting a diagram or table

1. Look at the trend in sales (up, flat, down).
2. Look at the rate of change: percentage change = (this year ÷ last year − 1) × 100.
3. Name the stage and give figures.
4. State the implication for the mix, cash and the next product.

Growth in percentage terms usually slows before sales peak, which gives early warning.

### Extension strategies

| Strategy | Example |
|---|---|
| Product modification | New flavour, size or packaging |
| New market | New country or customer group |
| New use | Promote a different use |
| Price change | Discount, or higher price for a refreshed image |
| Promotion or repositioning | New advertising or brand message |
| New distribution | Online, new retailer |

Purpose: keep earning from a product whose development cost has been paid, usually cheaper than a new launch. Match the strategy to the cause of the decline. Judge by cost, extra contribution and payback = cost ÷ extra contribution per year.

### Evaluation

| Useful | Limits |
|---|---|
| Guides mix and cash planning | Not all products follow the curve |
| Shows when new products are needed | Stages hard to spot as they happen |
| Helps portfolio balance | Ignores external events |
| Informs investors and lenders | Business action changes the curve |

Stakeholders: shareholders (portfolio spread), staff (workload), suppliers (orders) and lenders (cash flow).

## Quick worked reminder

Zest Fizz (invented) sales £000: 120, 480, 1,150, 2,100, 2,900, 3,350, 3,420, 3,380, 3,100, 2,550. Percentage changes: +300.0, +139.6, +82.6, +38.1, +15.5, +2.1, −1.2, −8.3, −17.7. Introduction year 1; growth years 2 to 5; maturity years 6 to 8; decline years 9 to 10.

Extension: new flavour costs £300,000 and adds £500,000 sales at 35%, so £175,000 contribution and payback 1.71 years. New country costs £600,000 and adds £900,000 at 30%, so £270,000 and payback 2.22 years. At half the forecast, paybacks are 3.43 and 4.44 years.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Product life cycle | The pattern of a product's sales from launch to withdrawal |
| Introduction | The stage when the product is launched and sales are low |
| Growth | The stage when sales rise rapidly |
| Maturity | The stage when sales peak and level off |
| Decline | The stage when sales fall |
| Extension strategy | An action to lengthen a product's life |
| Product portfolio | The range of products a business sells, at different stages |
| Payback | Time taken for extra contribution to cover the cost |

## Exam traps

- Judge the stage by the trend in growth, not the size of sales.
- Name the stage and quote figures.
- Extension strategies cost money; compare cost and payback.
- Not every product follows the curve.
- Link the extension strategy to the reason for decline.

## Evaluation prompts

- Does the product's real sales pattern fit the curve, or is it a fashion item with a short, sharp cycle?
- Is a fall in sales a true decline or a temporary dip? Check the trend over several periods.
- What caused the decline: tastes, a rival, price or quality? The extension strategy must fit the cause.
- Is the payback shorter than the time the product is likely to keep selling?
- Is a new product already in development, so that the portfolio stays balanced?
- Finish with a judgement for the product and business in the case.

## Self-test

1. Name the five stages of the product life cycle.
2. In which stage are profits usually highest?
3. Why is cash flow often negative in development?
4. Sales rose from £800,000 to £1,000,000. Find the percentage change.
5. Give two extension strategies.
6. Why can an extension strategy be cheaper than a new product?
7. A strategy costs £120,000 and adds £40,000 contribution a year. Find the payback.
8. Give one limit of the product life cycle.
9. How might a business use the model to plan new products?
10. Sales: 500, 540, 546, 530. Which stage is suggested, and why?
11. Give one stakeholder who benefits from the model and explain how.
12. Why should the cause of a decline be found before choosing an extension strategy?

### Answers

1. Development, introduction, growth, maturity, decline.
2. Maturity.
3. Money is spent on research, design and testing, and there are no sales yet.
4. (1,000,000 ÷ 800,000 − 1) × 100 = **25%**.
5. Any two: new flavour or feature; new market; new use; price change; repackaging; new distribution.
6. The product has already been developed and has a known brand and customers.
7. 120,000 ÷ 40,000 = **3 years**.
8. Any one: not all products follow it; stages hard to identify; ignores external events.
9. Launch the next product while the current one is in maturity or growth, so sales do not fall.
10. Maturity: growth has slowed to about 1% and then sales have begun to fall.
11. For example shareholders: it shows whether the business has products at several stages.
12. A strategy must address the cause: if tastes have changed, a discount alone will not restore sales.
