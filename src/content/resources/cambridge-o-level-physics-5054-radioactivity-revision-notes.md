## Detection (5.2.1)

| Radiation | Detector |
|---|---|
| Alpha | Cloud chamber (short, thick, straight tracks) or spark counter |
| Beta, gamma | Geiger–Müller tube and counter |

- Count rate in **counts/s** or **counts/minute**.
- **Background radiation** sources: radon gas in the air; rocks and
  buildings; food and drink; cosmic rays.
- **Corrected count rate = measured − background** (same units).

## The three emissions (5.2.2)

Emission is **spontaneous** and **random in direction**.

| Property | α | β | γ |
|---|---|---|---|
| What it is | Helium nucleus: 2p + 2n | Fast electron from the nucleus | High-frequency EM wave |
| Charge | + | − | 0 |
| Ionising | Strong | Medium | Weak |
| Stopped by | Paper, few cm of air | Few mm aluminium | Thick lead/concrete (reduced) |
| Electric field | To − plate | To + plate, more deflected | Undeflected |
| Magnetic field | Deflected | Opposite way, more curved | Undeflected |

Identifying a source: measure the corrected count with no absorber, paper,
aluminium and lead. A big drop with paper → α; a big drop with aluminium →
β; counts left after aluminium but removed by lead → γ.

## Decay (5.2.3)

- A change in an **unstable nucleus**; spontaneous and random.

| Emission | Change in A | Change in Z |
|---|---|---|
| α (⁴₂He) | −4 | −2 |
| β (⁰₋₁e) | 0 | +1 |
| γ | 0 | 0 |

Balance **both** the top and bottom numbers.

Examples:

```
²²²₈₆Rn → ²¹⁸₈₄Po + ⁴₂He
¹³¹₅₃I  → ¹³¹₅₄Xe + ⁰₋₁e
```

## Fission and fusion (5.2.4)

| Feature | Fusion | Fission |
|---|---|---|
| Process | Two small nuclei join to form a larger one | A large nucleus (U-235) absorbs a neutron and splits |
| Products | Larger nucleus + energy | Two daughter nuclei + 2 or more neutrons + energy |
| Where | Energy source of stars | Nuclear reactors |

**Chain reaction:** neutrons from one fission cause further fissions.

| Reactor part | Job |
|---|---|
| Moderator | Slows neutrons so they cause fission more readily |
| Control rods | Absorb neutrons to control the rate |
| Coolant | Carries thermal energy away from the core |

## Half-life (5.2.5)

- **Definition:** the time taken for half the nuclei of that isotope in any
  sample to decay.
- Fraction left after n half-lives = (1/2)ⁿ: ½, ¼, ⅛, 1/16, 1/32 …
- Always subtract background before halving.
- From a graph: time for any value to halve; repeat and average.

**Carbon-14 dating:** living things keep a steady proportion of carbon-14;
after death it decays and is not replaced; compare activity per gram with
living material; half-life about 5700 years.

## Choosing an isotope

| Use | Radiation | Half-life |
|---|---|---|
| Smoke alarm | α (ionises air, short range) | Long |
| Irradiating food | γ (penetrates packaging) | Long |
| Sterilising equipment | γ | Long |
| Thickness of paper/thin sheet | β (partly absorbed) | Long |
| Thickness of thick metal | γ | Long |
| Diagnosing cancer (tracer) | γ (escapes the body) | Short (hours) |
| Treating cancer | γ beams | Long (external source) |

## Safety (5.2.6)

- Effects on living things: **cell death, mutations, cancer**.
- **Time:** reduce exposure time.
- **Distance:** increase it — tongs, never hands.
- **Shielding:** lead-lined storage boxes and screens.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Background radiation | Ionising radiation always present from natural and other sources |
| Count rate | Number of counts recorded per second or per minute |
| Spontaneous | Not affected by external conditions; cannot be triggered |
| Random | Cannot predict which nucleus decays or when |
| Ionising | Able to remove electrons from atoms |
| Half-life | Time for half the nuclei of an isotope in a sample to decay |
| Chain reaction | Self-sustaining series of fissions caused by released neutrons |
| Moderator | Material that slows neutrons in a reactor |

## Exam traps

- Background first, then halve.
- Beta decay: Z goes **up** by 1.
- Gamma is never deflected by fields.
- Beta is deflected **more** than alpha (much smaller mass).
- Alpha cannot be used in thickness gauges.
- Tracers in the body: gamma and **short** half-life.
- Moderator slows; control rods absorb.
- "Random" and "spontaneous" are both needed when asked to describe decay.

## Self-test

1. Name a detector for alpha particles.
2. List four sources of background radiation.
3. Measured 96 counts/min, background 18 counts/min. Find the corrected
   count rate.
4. Which radiation is most ionising?
5. Which radiation is not deflected by a magnetic field?
6. Complete: ²²⁶₈₈Ra → ? + ⁴₂He.
7. State the change in Z in beta decay.
8. What is the job of the moderator?
9. Define half-life.
10. 80 g of an isotope with a half-life of 3.0 years. How much remains
    after 9.0 years?
11. Why is a gamma emitter with a short half-life used as a medical
    tracer?
12. State three ways of reducing exposure when handling a source.

**Answers**

1. Cloud chamber or spark counter.
2. Radon gas; rocks and buildings; food and drink; cosmic rays.
3. 96 − 18 = **78 counts/min**.
4. Alpha.
5. Gamma.
6. ²²²₈₆Rn.
7. Increases by 1.
8. Slows the neutrons so they are more likely to cause fission.
9. The time taken for half the nuclei of that isotope in any sample to
   decay.
10. 3 half-lives: 80 → 40 → 20 → **10 g**.
11. Gamma passes out of the body to the detector; the short half-life means
    the patient's exposure is brief.
12. Reduce exposure time; increase distance (tongs); use shielding (lead).
