Condensed notes for AQA AS and A-level Biology (7401/7402) section **3.4.2 DNA and protein synthesis**. Worked examples on splicing, working back from tRNA and inhibitor experiments are in the [DNA and protein synthesis study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-7402-dna-and-protein-synthesis/). Test yourself with the [DNA and protein synthesis practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-7402-dna-and-protein-synthesis-practice/).

## Definitions first

- **Genome:** the complete set of genes in a cell.
- **Proteome:** the full range of proteins that a cell is able to produce.
- **Transcription:** production of mRNA from DNA.
- **Translation:** production of polypeptides from the sequence of codons carried by mRNA.

## mRNA and tRNA

| | mRNA | tRNA |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | Single, linear strand | Single strand folded into clover-leaf |
| Base pairing within molecule | None | Some, holding the shape |
| Three-base unit | Codon | Anticodon |
| Special site | — | Amino acid binding site |
| Job | Carries copy of gene to ribosome | Carries a specific amino acid to its codon |

Both: ribose; bases A, U, C, G; no thymine.

## Base-pairing chain

| DNA template | mRNA codon | tRNA anticodon |
|---|---|---|
| A | U | A |
| T | A | U |
| C | G | C |
| G | C | G |

- mRNA = DNA coding strand, with U for T.
- Anticodon = DNA template strand, with U for T.

## Transcription

1. DNA strands separate at the gene (hydrogen bonds break); template strand exposed.
2. Free RNA nucleotides pair with complementary template bases.
3. **RNA polymerase** joins adjacent RNA nucleotides (phosphodiester bonds, condensation).
4. DNA re-forms behind it; RNA released at end of gene.

| Prokaryotes | Eukaryotes |
|---|---|
| Transcription gives mRNA directly | Transcription gives pre-mRNA |
| No introns | Pre-mRNA spliced: introns removed, exons joined |
| | mRNA leaves nucleus via nuclear pore |

## Translation

1. mRNA attaches to ribosome.
2. tRNA anticodon pairs with complementary codon, bringing its amino acid.
3. Second tRNA pairs with next codon (ribosome holds two).
4. Peptide bond forms between amino acids (condensation).
5. Ribosome moves one codon; first tRNA leaves.
6. Repeats until a stop codon; polypeptide released.

**ATP:** energy for attaching amino acids to tRNA and for forming peptide bonds.
**Ribosome:** holds mRNA and tRNAs in place; site of peptide bond formation.

## Conversion checklist

1. Identify which strand or molecule you are given.
2. Write the complementary sequence, swapping T and U where needed.
3. Split into threes from the start codon.
4. Use only the code given in the question.
5. Stop codons give no amino acid.
6. Check: number of codons − 1 stop = number of amino acids.

**Mini example (codes given as data):** template TAC GGT ATT → mRNA AUG CCA UAA → if AUG = Met and CCA = Pro, the product is Met–Pro, with UAA as the stop codon. Anticodons used: UAC, GGU.

## Where each stage happens

| Stage | Eukaryotic cell | Prokaryotic cell |
|---|---|---|
| Transcription | Nucleus | Cytoplasm |
| Splicing | Nucleus | Not needed |
| Translation | Ribosomes in cytoplasm or on rough ER | Ribosomes in cytoplasm |

## Inhibitor logic

| Blocked | Consequence |
|---|---|
| RNA polymerase | No new mRNA; protein synthesis falls as old mRNA is broken down |
| Ribosomes | No translation; rapid stop |
| ATP supply | No amino acid–tRNA attachment, no peptide bonds |
| Splicing | Only pre-mRNA; no functional mRNA |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Template strand | DNA strand used to make mRNA |
| Codon | Three bases on mRNA coding for an amino acid or stop |
| Anticodon | Three bases on tRNA complementary to a codon |
| RNA polymerase | Enzyme that joins RNA nucleotides in transcription |
| Pre-mRNA | Eukaryotic transcript containing introns |
| Splicing | Removing introns from pre-mRNA and joining exons |
| Ribosome | rRNA and protein structure where translation occurs |
| Peptide bond | Bond between amino acids, formed by condensation |

## Exam traps

- No T in RNA.
- mRNA is complementary to the template strand, not identical to it.
- RNA polymerase, not DNA polymerase, in transcription.
- Introns are removed from pre-mRNA, not from DNA; only in eukaryotes.
- A tRNA carries one specific amino acid; it does not make it.
- Stop codons are not amino acids.
- You do not need to recall codons; use the data given.

## Quick self-test

1. Define proteome.
2. Why can two cells with the same genome have different proteomes?
3. Give two structural differences between mRNA and tRNA.
4. What does RNA polymerase do?
5. What is pre-mRNA, and in which organisms is it produced?
6. What happens during splicing?
7. Give the anticodon for the codon GCA.
8. A DNA template strand reads TTC. What is the mRNA codon?
9. State two uses of ATP in translation.
10. Why does a stop codon end translation?
11. An mRNA has 64 codons including one stop codon. How many amino acids are in the polypeptide?
12. An inhibitor of RNA polymerase is added. Why does protein synthesis not stop immediately?

### Answers

1. The full range of proteins a cell is able to produce.
2. Different genes are transcribed and translated in different cells.
3. Any two: mRNA is linear, tRNA is folded (clover-leaf); tRNA has hydrogen bonds between some bases, mRNA has none; tRNA has an amino acid binding site; tRNA has an anticodon, mRNA has codons; mRNA is usually longer.
4. Joins adjacent RNA nucleotides to form mRNA (or pre-mRNA).
5. The first product of transcription, containing introns; in eukaryotes.
6. Introns are removed and exons joined to form mRNA.
7. CGU.
8. AAG.
9. Attaching amino acids to tRNA; forming peptide bonds.
10. No tRNA has an anticodon complementary to it, so no amino acid is added and the polypeptide is released.
11. **63**.
12. mRNA already present is still translated until it is broken down.
