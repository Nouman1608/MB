These notes condense sub-topic **18.3 Selection** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Outcomes 1 to 3 (natural selection and selective breeding) are Core. Outcomes 4 to 6 (adaptation, antibiotic resistance, natural versus artificial selection) are **Extended only**.

For full explanations and worked examples, read the [selection study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-selection-cambridge-igcse-biology/) first, then test yourself with the [selection practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-selection-cambridge-igcse-biology-practice/). The [variation and selection revision notes](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-variation-and-selection-revision-notes/) cover the rest of topic 18.

## Core recall: natural selection in five links

1. **Genetic variation** within the population.
2. **Many offspring** produced, more than can survive.
3. **Struggle for survival**, including **competition for resources** (food, water, space, mates); predation and disease.
4. **Better adapted** individuals have a **greater chance of reproducing**.
5. They **pass on their alleles** to the next generation; over many generations the allele becomes more common.

**Memory hook:** Vary, Many, Struggle, Survive-and-breed, Pass on.

Apply each link to the organism in the question: name the feature, the selecting factor (predator, climate, food) and the allele that becomes more common.

## Core recall: selective breeding

1. Humans **select** individuals with **desirable features**.
2. **Cross** them to produce the next generation.
3. **Select offspring** showing the desirable features.
4. **Repeat over many generations.**

Used to improve **crop plants** and **domesticated animals**.

| Organism | Example features selected |
|---|---|
| Cereal crops | higher yield, disease resistance, short strong stems |
| Fruit and vegetables | larger size, flavour, longer shelf life |
| Dairy cattle | high milk yield |
| Beef cattle, chickens | fast growth, more meat or eggs |
| Sheep | more or finer wool |
| Dogs and horses | calm temperament |

**Why many generations?** Not all offspring inherit the desired alleles; many features are controlled by several genes; breeders want offspring that show the feature every time.

## Extended recall

- **Adaptation:** the **process**, resulting from natural selection, by which **populations** become more suited to their environment **over many generations**.
- **Adaptive feature** = a feature of an individual. **Adaptation** = a process in a population.

### Antibiotic-resistant bacteria

1. Random **mutation** → some bacteria have a resistance allele.
2. Antibiotic used → **non-resistant bacteria killed**.
3. Resistant bacteria survive → **less competition**.
4. They **reproduce asexually**, passing on the resistance allele.
5. Over many generations → a **resistant strain**.

Rapid reproduction means this happens quickly. Using antibiotics only when essential limits the development of resistant strains such as MRSA.

### Natural vs artificial selection

| | Natural | Artificial |
|---|---|---|
| Selecting agent | environment | humans |
| Features favoured | help survival and reproduction | useful to humans |
| Speed | usually slower | usually faster |
| Outcome | populations suited to environment | breeds and varieties suited to human needs |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Natural selection | Better-adapted individuals survive, reproduce and pass on their alleles |
| Selective breeding (artificial selection) | Humans choose which individuals breed, for desirable features |
| Struggle for survival | Competition for resources, plus predation and disease, that kills many offspring |
| Selecting factor | Something in the environment that kills less suited individuals, such as a predator or antibiotic |
| Adaptation (Extended) | The process by which populations become more suited to their environment over many generations |
| Strain (Extended) | A population of bacteria with the same genetic features, such as resistance |
| Resistance (Extended) | The ability of bacteria to survive an antibiotic that would normally kill them |

## Exam traps

- **Individuals changing.** Individuals do not change their alleles; populations change over generations.
- **Environment causes mutation.** The useful allele existed before the selecting factor appeared.
- **Stopping at "survive".** Marks continue: reproduce, pass on alleles, allele more common.
- **"Survival of the fittest".** Explain which feature helps and why.
- **Missing the struggle.** Over-production and competition are separate marking points.
- **Breeding without repetition.** Add "repeat over many generations".
- **Bacteria "immune".** Bacteria become **resistant**; immunity refers to the body's defences.
- **Antibiotic causes the mutation.** It selects resistant bacteria that already exist.

## Command-word reminders

- **Describe** / **explain** natural selection: all five links, applied to the context.
- **Outline** selective breeding: select, cross, select offspring, repeat; name the feature.
- **Outline the differences**: matched pairs with "whereas".

## Self-test

1. List the five ideas you must include when describing natural selection.
2. Why do organisms compete with each other?
3. A population of beetles lives on brown soil. Birds eat them. Which colour of beetle is most likely to survive, and why?
4. What happens to the allele for a useful feature over many generations of natural selection?
5. Give the three steps of selective breeding named in the syllabus.
6. Why must selective breeding be repeated over many generations?
7. Suggest one feature a breeder might select in rice plants.
8. (Extended) Describe adaptation.
9. (Extended) Where does the allele for antibiotic resistance come from?
10. (Extended) Why do resistant bacteria increase in number once an antibiotic is used?
11. (Extended) Why does antibiotic resistance develop faster than resistance in a population of mammals?
12. (Extended) State two differences between natural and artificial selection.

### Answers

1. Genetic variation; many offspring; struggle for survival including competition for resources; better adapted individuals more likely to reproduce; they pass on their alleles.
2. Resources such as food, water, space and mates are limited, and more offspring are produced than can be supported.
3. Brown beetles; they are camouflaged on brown soil, so birds are less likely to see and eat them.
4. It becomes more common in the population.
5. Select individuals with desirable features; cross them; select offspring showing the desirable features.
6. Not all offspring inherit the desired alleles, and many features are controlled by several genes, so it takes time for all offspring to show the feature.
7. Any one: higher yield, disease resistance, tolerance of drought or flooding, short stems.
8. The process, resulting from natural selection, by which populations become more suited to their environment over many generations.
9. A random mutation.
10. Non-resistant bacteria are killed, so resistant ones have less competition; they reproduce and pass on the resistance allele.
11. Bacteria reproduce very rapidly, so many generations pass in a short time.
12. Natural: environment selects, features help survival, slower. Artificial: humans select, features useful to humans, faster.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a general account of natural selection without the organism in the question.
- Saying an individual "adapted" or "evolved".
- Omitting "pass on alleles" at the end of a natural selection answer.
- Describing genetic modification instead of selective breeding.
- Claiming the antibiotic caused the resistance mutation.
