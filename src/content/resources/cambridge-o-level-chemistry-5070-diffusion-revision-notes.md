These notes condense section 1.2, Diffusion, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). The two outcomes are: describe and explain diffusion in terms of kinetic particle theory, and describe and explain the effect of relative molecular mass on the rate of diffusion of gases. The [diffusion study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-diffusion/) has the full explanations and worked examples; the [diffusion practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-diffusion-practice/) test them.

## Core recall

- **Diffusion**: the net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration, caused by the random motion of the particles.
- Happens in **gases** (fast) and **liquids** (slower). Not in solids at this level: particles only vibrate about fixed positions.
- No stirring or pushing is needed: the particles' own random motion causes it.
- When the mixture is uniform, particles still move, but there is **no net movement**.
- Diffusing particles **collide** with other particles and change direction, so overall spreading is much slower than the particles' speed.

## Rate of diffusion

| Factor | Change | Effect on rate | Particle reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relative molecular mass | lower Mr | faster | at the same temperature, lighter particles move faster |
| Temperature | higher | faster | particles have more kinetic energy and move faster |
| State | gas instead of liquid | faster | gas particles are far apart, move faster and collide less often |

Chain to learn: **lower Mr → faster particles (same temperature) → faster diffusion → travels further in the same time.**

## Mr values worth knowing

Using H = 1, C = 12, N = 14, O = 16, S = 32, Cl = 35.5, Br = 80:

| Gas | Mr | | Gas | Mr |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H₂ | 2 | | O₂ | 32 |
| He | 4 | | H₂S | 34 |
| CH₄ | 16 | | HCl | 36.5 |
| NH₃ | 17 | | CO₂ | 44 |
| N₂ | 28 | | SO₂ | 64 |
| CO | 28 | | Cl₂ | 71 |
| | | | Br₂ | 160 |

Gases with equal Mr (N₂ and CO, both 28) diffuse at the same rate at the same temperature.

## Experiments

**Ammonia and hydrogen chloride tube.** Cotton wool with ammonia solution at one end, hydrochloric acid at the other, ends sealed.
- White ring of ammonium chloride: NH₃(g) + HCl(g) → NH₄Cl(s).
- Ring forms **nearer the HCl end**: NH₃ (Mr 17) diffuses faster than HCl (Mr 36.5).
- Ring takes minutes: molecules collide with air molecules in the tube.
- Ends sealed: no gas escapes, no draughts, and the gases are harmful.

**Bromine in gas jars.** Brown bromine vapour spreads into an upper jar of air until the colour is even throughout both jars.

**Potassium manganate(VII) in water.** Purple colour spreads slowly from the crystal; faster in warm water.

**Porous pot.** Air-filled porous pot with hydrogen outside: hydrogen (Mr 2) diffuses in faster than air (N₂ 28, O₂ 32) diffuses out, so pressure in the pot rises. Hydrogen-filled pot in air: hydrogen leaves faster than air enters, so pressure falls.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Diffusion | net movement of particles from higher to lower concentration due to random motion |
| Net movement | the overall movement in one direction once all random movements are counted |
| Concentration gradient | a difference in concentration between two regions |
| Random motion | movement in all directions with frequent collisions, changing direction unpredictably |
| Relative molecular mass, Mr | sum of the relative atomic masses of the atoms in a molecule |
| Kinetic energy | energy of movement; average value rises with temperature |
| Uniform mixture | particles evenly spread, so no net movement remains |

## Exam traps

- "From high to low" earns nothing without **concentration**.
- Leaving out **random motion** of particles as the cause.
- Using Ar instead of Mr for diatomic gases: chlorine is Cl₂ (71), oxygen is O₂ (32).
- Saying the particles stop when diffusion is complete.
- Writing "ammonia is lighter, so the ring is near the ammonia end": the lighter gas travels **further**, so the ring is **further from** its starting end.
- Saying heavy particles have less energy. At the same temperature their average kinetic energy is the same; they are slower because they are heavier.

## Self-test

1. Define diffusion.
2. Why does diffusion not happen in a solid at this level?
3. State the effect of increasing relative molecular mass on the rate of diffusion of a gas.
4. Explain why lighter gas particles move faster than heavier ones at the same temperature.
5. Calculate the Mr of sulfur dioxide and of methane, and state which diffuses faster.
6. Name the white solid formed in the ammonia and hydrogen chloride experiment.
7. Explain why the white ring forms nearer the hydrogen chloride end.
8. Why does a smell take much longer to cross a room than the speed of its molecules suggests?
9. Why does potassium manganate(VII) spread faster in warm water?
10. Nitrogen and carbon monoxide are released at the same time and temperature. Which diffuses faster?
11. Hydrogen is passed around an air-filled porous pot. State and explain what happens to the pressure inside the pot.
12. A bromine and air mixture has become uniform. Is there still any movement of particles?

## Answers

1. The net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration, caused by the random motion of the particles.
2. Solid particles only vibrate about fixed positions; they cannot move from place to place.
3. Higher Mr gives a slower rate of diffusion.
4. At the same temperature all the gas particles have the same average kinetic energy, so lighter particles must move faster.
5. SO₂: 32 + 32 = 64. CH₄: 12 + 4 = 16. Methane diffuses faster.
6. Ammonium chloride, NH₄Cl.
7. Ammonia (Mr 17) is lighter than hydrogen chloride (Mr 36.5), so it diffuses faster and travels further along the tube in the same time.
8. Its molecules collide repeatedly with air molecules and change direction, so their overall progress is slow.
9. The particles have more kinetic energy and move faster at the higher temperature.
10. Neither: both have Mr 28, so they diffuse at the same rate.
11. It rises: hydrogen (Mr 2) diffuses in faster than the heavier air molecules diffuse out, so there are more particles in the pot.
12. Yes: the particles still move randomly, but there is no net movement in any direction.
