These notes condense section 3.6, Firms and production, of the Cambridge IGCSE Economics 0455 syllabus for examination in 2026: demand for factors of production, labour-intensive and capital-intensive production, and production and productivity. All figures are invented. For explanations and worked examples read the [firms' production study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-firms-production/), then practise with the [firms' production practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-firms-production-practice/). The combined [demand for factors, production and productivity revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-demand-for-factors-production-and-productivity-and-revision-notes/) also cover market structure.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Factors of production | Land, labour, capital and enterprise |
| Derived demand | Demand for a factor arises from demand for the product it helps make |
| Labour-intensive production | Uses a high proportion of labour relative to capital |
| Capital-intensive production | Uses a high proportion of capital relative to labour |
| Production | Total output of goods and services in a period |
| Productivity | Output per unit of input in a period |
| Labour productivity | Output per worker (or per hour worked) |

## 3.6.1 Four influences on demand for a factor

1. **Demand for the product:** more sales → more factors needed.
2. **Price of different factors:** dearer factor → firm switches to the relatively cheaper one.
3. **Availability:** scarce factor → higher price or cannot hire → use less or a substitute.
4. **Productivity:** more productive factor → more output per unit of cost → firm prefers it.

Worked reminder: output must rise from 2,000 to 2,400 units, and each worker makes 40 units. Workers needed rise from 50 to 60. If productivity also rises to 48 units, only 2,400 / 48 = 50 are needed.

## 3.6.2 Choosing a method

```
Cost of each method = fixed cost + (number of workers x wage) + other costs
Switch point: set the two costs equal and solve
```

Worked reminder: by hand needs 12 workers; by machine needs a 2,400-a-week machine plus 4 workers.

```
12W = 2,400 + 4W  →  8W = 2,400  →  W = 300
Wage below 300: by hand cheaper; above 300: machine cheaper
```

| Points towards labour-intensive | Points towards capital-intensive |
|---|---|
| Low wages, plentiful labour | High wages, scarce labour |
| Small or varied output | Large, steady output |
| Personal or craft product | Identical goods in bulk |
| Little finance available | Finance and technology available |
| Need for flexibility | Need for consistent quality |
| High unemployment (government preference) | Shortage of workers |

| | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Labour-intensive | Flexible; low set-up cost; suits individual orders; personal service | Higher cost per unit at high output; quality varies; slower |
| Capital-intensive | Low cost per unit at high output; fast; consistent | High fixed cost; risky if sales fall; breakdowns; needs skilled operators; may cut jobs |

## 3.6.3 Production versus productivity

```
Labour productivity  = output / workers
Capital productivity = output / machines
Land productivity    = output / hectares
% change = (new - old) / old x 100
```

Worked reminder: a call centre handles 9,000 calls a week with 30 staff, then 10,800 calls with 40 staff.

```
Production: up (10,800 - 9,000) / 9,000 = 20%
Productivity: 9,000 / 30 = 300 falls to 10,800 / 40 = 270, down 10%
```

| Production | Productivity | Example cause |
|---|---|---|
| Up | Up | New technology, training |
| Up | Down | Extra workers each less productive |
| Down | Up | Machines replace some workers |
| Down | Down | Falling demand, workers kept idle |

**Influences on productivity:** education and training; health and motivation; amount and quality of capital per worker; technology; organisation and management; quality of land and raw materials; working conditions.

**Why productivity matters to firms:** lower cost per unit → lower prices or higher profit → greater competitiveness; can fund higher wages.

## Exam traps

- **Total versus ratio.** Production is a total; productivity is per unit of input.
- **Right denominator.** Per worker, per machine or per hectare: read the question.
- **Capital-intensive is not always cheaper.** It depends on output level and wages.
- **Two influences at once.** Higher demand and higher productivity can cancel out in factor demand.
- **Product type matters.** Services needing personal contact stay labour-intensive.
- **Percentages use the old value as the base.**

## Self-test

1. Name the four factors of production.
2. Why is demand for labour described as derived?
3. State two influences on a firm's demand for capital.
4. Define capital-intensive production.
5. Give two reasons a hairdresser uses labour-intensive production.
6. Method A uses 15 workers; method B uses a machine costing 1,800 a week and 6 workers. At what weekly wage do they cost the same?
7. Distinguish production from productivity.
8. A factory makes 7,200 units with 24 workers. Calculate labour productivity.
9. A field of 20 hectares yields 90 tonnes. Calculate land productivity.
10. Give two influences on labour productivity.
11. Production rises but productivity falls. Suggest one reason.
12. Give one disadvantage of capital-intensive production for a firm.

### Answers

1. Land, labour, capital and enterprise.
2. Firms want workers only because consumers want the goods and services workers produce.
3. Any two: demand for the product; price of capital relative to labour; availability; productivity of capital.
4. Production that uses a high proportion of capital relative to labour.
5. Any two: each customer wants an individual service; personal contact is needed; machines cannot do the task; low set-up cost.
6. 15W = 1,800 + 6W, so 9W = 1,800 and W = 200.
7. Production is total output; productivity is output per unit of input.
8. 7,200 / 24 = 300 units per worker.
9. 90 / 20 = 4.5 tonnes per hectare.
10. Any two: education and training; capital and technology; motivation; organisation; health; working conditions.
11. The firm hired more workers, but each produced less; for example machinery was not increased.
12. High fixed costs if sales fall; breakdowns stop production; needs skilled operators; large initial cost.
