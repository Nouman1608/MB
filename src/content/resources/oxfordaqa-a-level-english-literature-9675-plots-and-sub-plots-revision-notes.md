These notes condense one aspect named in the 9675 Unit 1 content: "the use of plots and sub-plots".
They support the passage-based Section A question described in the
[Unit 1 Section A study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-english-literature-9675-unit-1-section-a/).
For the full explanation and a worked paragraph, read the
[plots and sub-plots study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-english-literature-9675-plots-and-sub-plots/).

## The idea in one box

- A **sub-plot** is a second line of action with its own small arc, running beside the hero's
  journey towards death.
- The specification pairs it with the **structural pattern** of tragedy: complication, catastrophe,
  order to disorder.
- In a passage answer, a sub-plot is the quickest route to the **links beyond the passage** that
  show significance.
- Unit 1 is **closed book**: sub-plot evidence must be learned as short quotations and precise
  references.

## Five jobs a sub-plot does

| Job | What it does | Ask yourself |
|---|---|---|
| Mirror | Repeats the tragic error in another case | Does the repetition make the error seem universal? |
| Contrast or parody | Shows a nobler, lower or comic version | Does the contrast raise or expose the hero? |
| Complicate | Sub-plot characters become causes | When do the two lines converge, and why then? |
| Widen the damage | Shows suffering beyond the hero | Who else pays for the hero's flaw? |
| Pace and feeling | Delays, builds suspense, releases tension | What does the shift of focus do to the audience? |

## Play by play

**King Lear**
- Gloucester and Edmund mirror Lear and his daughters: a father deceived by a child's words.
- Blinding on stage makes the metaphor of blindness literal.
- Edmund joins Goneril and Regan: the plots **converge**, and his order leads to Cordelia's death.
- Difference matters: Gloucester tends to despair, Lear to rage.

**Hamlet**
- Three avenging sons: Hamlet, Laertes, Fortinbras.
- Laertes and Fortinbras act decisively, so they are **foils** for Hamlet's delay.
- Ophelia's real madness against Hamlet's performed madness.
- Fortinbras inherits Denmark: the sub-plot supplies the **restored order**.

**Othello**
- Close to a single plot; the **absence** of a separate story keeps the audience inside Iago's
  scheme.
- Roderigo strand: Iago's manipulation in miniature.
- Cassio and Bianca strand: the handkerchief, and the overheard laughter Othello misreads.

**Doctor Faustus (B text)**
- Comic scenes **parody** conjuring: servants raise devils for trivial ends.
- The horse that turns to hay shows magic as trickery.
- The B text expands episodes such as Benvolio's humiliation and revenge.
- Parody shrinks Faustus's bargain and sharpens the pathos of the last hour.

**The Duchess of Malfi**
- Julia's mercenary affair against the Duchess's loving secret marriage.
- Both women die through the brothers' corruption; Julia by the poisoned Bible.
- The contrast tests the moral basis of the brothers' rage at female desire.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning | Use it to say |
|---|---|---|
| Main plot | The protagonist's line of action | Where the passage sits in the hero's journey |
| Sub-plot | A secondary line with its own arc | What a second case adds to the tragedy |
| Parallel | A repetition of a pattern in another case | That the error is not unique to the hero |
| Foil | A character who sets off another by contrast | What the hero lacks or has in excess |
| Parody | A comic or debased imitation | How the main action is exposed or deflated |
| Convergence | The point where plot lines join | How the sub-plot becomes a cause of catastrophe |
| Double plot | Two lines of near-equal weight | Why structure doubles the suffering |
| Complication | The stage where problems multiply | Why a sub-plot event worsens the hero's position |

## How to use it in a passage answer

1. **Passage from the main plot:** find one sub-plot moment that mirrors or contrasts with it.
2. **Passage from the sub-plot:** argue what it adds to the main tragedy; show convergence.
3. **Passage where plots meet:** comment on the timing of the convergence.
4. Keep the sub-plot to one or two paragraphs: the **printed passage stays central**.

## Exam traps

- Summarising the sub-plot instead of arguing what it shows.
- Calling two plots "the same" when the difference carries the meaning.
- Writing off comic scenes as "comic relief" with no comment on what they parody.
- Using sub-plot evidence from memory inaccurately; keep quotations short.
- Losing the passage under a long digression about another strand.

## Self-test

1. Which two phrases from the 9675 Unit 1 content are most relevant to sub-plots?
2. Define a foil.
3. In *King Lear*, which character forges a letter, and against whom?
4. Name the three sons in *Hamlet* who seek to avenge a father.
5. How does Fortinbras's fate affect the ending of *Hamlet*?
6. Why might the near-absence of a sub-plot in *Othello* be significant?
7. Which object links the Cassio and Bianca strand to the main plot of *Othello*?
8. Give one way the comic scenes in *Doctor Faustus* comment on the main plot.
9. How does Julia die in *The Duchess of Malfi*?
10. What does convergence mean, and why does its timing matter?
11. Give one difference between Gloucester's response to suffering and Lear's.
12. How long should a sub-plot discussion be in a 25-mark passage answer?

## Answers

1. "The use of plots and sub-plots" and "the structural pattern of the text as it moves through
   complication to catastrophe".
2. A character whose situation or temperament sets off another character's by contrast.
3. Edmund forges a letter against his brother Edgar.
4. Hamlet, Laertes and Fortinbras.
5. He inherits Denmark, so the sub-plot provides the restored order after the catastrophe.
6. It concentrates the audience's attention on Iago's scheme with no escape into another story, and
   the specification notes that absence can be as significant as presence.
7. The handkerchief.
8. Any one: servants conjuring for trivial ends parody Faustus's ambition; the trick horse shows
   magic as cheap deception, which shrinks Faustus's bargain.
9. The Cardinal makes her kiss a poisoned Bible.
10. The point where the plot lines join; late convergence drives the play towards catastrophe and
    shows the sub-plot becoming a cause.
11. Gloucester tends towards despair (he wishes to die at Dover); Lear tends towards rage and then
    madness.
12. One or two paragraphs; the printed passage must remain the centre of the answer.
