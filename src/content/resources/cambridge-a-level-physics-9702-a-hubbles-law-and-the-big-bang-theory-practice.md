## Section A

**1.** State Hubble's law. **[1]**

**2.** State what is meant by the redshift of light from a galaxy. **[1]**

**3.** A spectral line with a laboratory wavelength of 589.0 nm is observed at 595.5 nm in the light from a galaxy. Calculate the speed of the galaxy relative to the Earth. **[2]**

**4.** State the SI unit of the Hubble constant. **[1]**

**5.** Calculate the recession speed of a galaxy at a distance of 5.0 × 10²⁴ m. **[1]**

**6.** Radiation of frequency 6.17 × 10¹⁴ Hz is emitted by a galaxy receding at 4.5 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹. Calculate the frequency received at the Earth. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** In the spectrum of a galaxy, a hydrogen line with a laboratory wavelength of 410.2 nm is observed at 430.7 nm.

**(a)** Calculate the recession speed of the galaxy. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the distance to the galaxy. **[1]**

**(c)** Predict the observed wavelength of the hydrogen line whose laboratory wavelength is 656.3 nm. **[2]**

**8.** The table shows data for four galaxies.

| Distance d / 10²⁴ m | 1.0 | 2.0 | 3.0 | 4.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Recession speed v / 10⁶ m s⁻¹ | 2.4 | 4.5 | 7.0 | 9.2 |

**(a)** Explain how the data support Hubble's law. **[1]**

**(b)** Use the data to determine a value for the Hubble constant. **[2]**

**(c)** State how the distances to these galaxies could have been measured. **[1]**

**9.** Explain why the redshift of light from distant galaxies leads to the idea that the Universe is expanding. **[3]**

**10.** Explain how Hubble's law leads to the Big Bang theory. **[3]**

**11.** A student says: "All galaxies are moving away from the Milky Way, so the Milky Way must be at the centre of the Universe."

Explain why this conclusion is not justified. **[2]**

**12.** A few nearby galaxies show blueshift. Suggest why this does not contradict the idea of an expanding Universe. **[2]**

**13.** Light from a very distant object has Δλ/λ = 2.0. A student uses Δλ/λ ≈ v/c and concludes that the object is moving at 6.0 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹. Explain why this method gives an invalid result here. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The recession speed of a galaxy is (approximately) proportional to its distance from the observer [1].

**2.** The spectral lines are observed at longer wavelengths (lower frequencies) than their laboratory values, because the galaxy is moving away [1].

**3.** Δλ = 595.5 − 589.0 = 6.5 nm [1]; v = cΔλ/λ = 3.00 × 10⁸ × 6.5 ÷ 589.0 = 3.3 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹, moving away [1].

**4.** s⁻¹ [1].

**5.** v = H₀d = 2.3 × 10⁻¹⁸ × 5.0 × 10²⁴ = 1.2 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹ [1].

**6.** Δf = fv/c = 6.17 × 10¹⁴ × 4.5 × 10⁶ ÷ 3.00 × 10⁸ = 9.3 × 10¹² Hz [1]; received frequency = 6.17 × 10¹⁴ − 0.093 × 10¹⁴ = 6.08 × 10¹⁴ Hz [1].

**7. (a)** Δλ/λ = (430.7 − 410.2) ÷ 410.2 = 0.050 [1]; v = 0.050 × 3.00 × 10⁸ = 1.5 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** d = v/H₀ = 1.5 × 10⁷ ÷ 2.3 × 10⁻¹⁸ = 6.5 × 10²⁴ m [1].

**(c)** Every line shifts by the same fraction, Δλ = 0.050 × 656.3 = 33 nm [1]; observed wavelength = 689 nm [1].

**8. (a)** v increases in proportion to d: v/d is roughly constant (a graph of v against d is a straight line through the origin) [1].

**(b)** Gradient of the best-fit line through the origin (or mean of v/d) [1] = 2.3 × 10⁻¹⁸ s⁻¹ (accept 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁸ to 2.4 × 10⁻¹⁸ s⁻¹) [1].

**(c)** Using standard candles (objects of known luminosity) in each galaxy, by measuring their radiant flux intensity [1].

**9.** Light from (almost) all distant galaxies is redshifted, so they are all moving away from us [1]. The more distant the galaxy, the greater the redshift, so the faster it is receding [1]. Galaxies moving apart with speed proportional to distance means that the distances between galaxies are increasing: the Universe is expanding [1].

**10.** Hubble's law shows that galaxies are moving apart, with speed proportional to distance [1]. Tracing the motion back in time, all galaxies would have been together at approximately the same time in the past [1]. This suggests the Universe began from an extremely small, hot, dense state and has been expanding ever since: the Big Bang theory [1].

**11.** In a uniformly expanding Universe every galaxy moves away from every other [1]; an observer in any galaxy would see all distant galaxies receding with speed proportional to distance, so the observation does not single out the Milky Way as a centre [1].

**12.** For nearby galaxies, the recession speed from expansion (H₀d) is small because d is small [1]; their own motion, caused by gravitational attraction within the local group of galaxies, can be larger and directed towards us, giving a blueshift [1].

**13.** The result is greater than the speed of light, which is impossible for the object's motion [1]; the equation Δλ/λ ≈ v/c is only valid when v is much less than c, so it cannot be used for such a large shift [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing by the observed wavelength instead of the laboratory value.
- Forgetting that a lower received frequency means the galaxy is receding.
- Explaining expansion without stating that more distant galaxies recede faster.
- Omitting the idea of tracing the expansion back in time when linking Hubble's law to the Big Bang.
- Claiming the Milky Way is at the centre of the expansion.
- Using a non-SI value of H₀ when the question works in metres and seconds.
