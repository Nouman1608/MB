## Questions

**1.** (a) State three differences between a sensory neurone and a motor neurone. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe how Schwann cells produce a myelin sheath. **[2]**

**2.** A person touches a sharp object and pulls their hand away before feeling pain.
**(a)** Name, in order, the structures that the impulse passes through from receptor to effector in the spinal reflex arc. **[3]**
**(b)** State where in the spinal cord the cell bodies of relay neurones are found, and what the white matter contains. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why a reflex is protective. **[2]**

**3.** An impulse travels 0.35 m along a myelinated axon in 7.0 ms. An unmyelinated axon of the same length takes 0.28 s.
**(a)** Calculate the speed in the myelinated axon, in m/s. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times faster the myelinated axon conducts. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the myelinated axon conducts faster. **[2]**

**4.** The table shows the membrane potential at one point on an axon after a stimulus.

| Time (ms) | 0.0 | 0.5 | 1.0 | 1.5 | 2.0 | 2.5 | 3.0 | 3.5 | 4.0 | 5.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Potential (mV) | −70 | −70 | −55 | +20 | +40 | +10 | −40 | −75 | −78 | −70 |

**(a)** State the resting potential and the peak potential, and calculate the change between them. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the change in potential between 1.0 ms and 2.0 ms. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain the change in potential between 2.5 ms and 3.5 ms. **[2]**
**(d)** The axon cannot fire for 2.5 ms after an action potential begins. Calculate the maximum number of action potentials per second. **[2]**

**5.** (a) Explain how myelination increases the speed of conduction along an axon. **[4]**
**(b)** A disease destroys the myelin on part of a sensory neurone. Suggest the effect on the person. **[2]**

**6.** (a) Describe how an action potential in a presynaptic neurone leads to an action potential in the postsynaptic neurone at a cholinergic synapse. **[6]**
**(b)** Explain why transmission across a synapse is in one direction only. **[2]**

**7.** The table shows the mean depolarisation of the postsynaptic membrane at a synapse bathed in different calcium ion concentrations.

| Calcium ions (mmol dm⁻³) | 0.5 | 1.0 | 2.0 | 4.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Depolarisation (mV) | 0.8 | 2.4 | 5.6 | 6.0 |

**(a)** Describe the relationship shown. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in depolarisation when the concentration rises from 1.0 to 2.0 mmol dm⁻³. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how calcium ions affect the depolarisation. **[2]**

**8.** In dim light a pupil has a diameter of 8.0 mm. In bright light the diameter is 3.0 mm.
**(a)** Describe the changes in the iris muscles in bright light. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage decrease in diameter. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate how many times larger the area of the dilated pupil is than the area of the constricted pupil. **[2]**

**9.** Explain the effect on a cholinergic synapse of:
**(a)** nicotine, **[2]**
**(b)** cobra venom alpha toxin, **[2]**
**(c)** lidocaine applied to an axon. **[2]**

**10.** (a) Explain why people with Parkinson's disease are given L-DOPA rather than dopamine. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe how MDMA increases the effect of serotonin at synapses. **[2]**

**11.** An isolated axon has a stimulating electrode and three recording electrodes. R1 is nearest the stimulus. Lidocaine is applied to the membrane at R2. R3 is beyond R2. After a stimulus, R1 records a peak of +40 mV. R2 records a rise from −70 mV to −62 mV only. R3 records no change from −70 mV.
**(a)** Calculate the amplitude of the response at R1 from the resting potential. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the rise at R2 as a percentage of the amplitude at R1. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the result at R2. **[3]**
**(d)** Explain the result at R3. **[2]**
**(e)** Explain why R1 shows a full response. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) Any three: sensory carries impulses towards the CNS, motor away from it [1]; sensory cell body lies off to the side of the fibre, motor cell body is at the end with dendrites [1]; sensory cell body is outside the cord, motor cell body is in the CNS [1]. (b) A Schwann cell wraps its membrane round the axon many times [1], forming layers of lipid-rich membrane (myelin), with gaps between cells being nodes [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Differences need both sides stated; "sensory is long" alone is weak because both are long.

**2.** (a) Sensory neurone [1], relay neurone [1], motor neurone, ending at the effector muscle [1]. (b) Cell bodies of relay neurones are in the grey matter [1]; white matter contains myelinated axons running up and down the cord [1]. (c) Short pathway with few synapses [1], so the response is fast and avoids damage before the brain is involved [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers tend to gain more when they name the sequence in the right order and use "neurone" with its type.

**3.** (a) Speed = distance / time = 0.35 / 0.0070 [1] = **50 m/s** [1]. (b) Unmyelinated speed = 0.35 / 0.28 = 1.25 m/s [1]; 50 / 1.25 = **40 times** [1]. (c) Action potentials occur only at the nodes of Ranvier [1] because myelin insulates the axon, and local currents carry depolarisation between nodes [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert milliseconds to seconds before dividing, and write the unit on the final line.

**4.** (a) Resting −70 mV, peak +40 mV [1]; change = 40 − (−70) = **110 mV** [1]. (b) Voltage-gated sodium channels open [1], and sodium ions diffuse into the axon [1]; once threshold (about −55 mV) is reached, more channels open, so depolarisation is rapid (positive feedback) [1]. (c) Sodium channels close and voltage-gated potassium channels open [1]; potassium ions diffuse out, repolarising the membrane [1]. (d) Maximum frequency = 1 / 0.0025 [1] = **400 per second** [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Explanations tend to gain credit when the ion, the direction and the type of channel are all named.

**5.** (a) Myelin is an insulator, so ions cannot cross the membrane where it is present [1]; action potentials occur only at nodes of Ranvier [1]; local currents carry the depolarisation between nodes, so the impulse jumps (saltatory conduction) [1]; fewer places need to be depolarised, so conduction is faster [1]. (b) Impulses slow or fail to reach the CNS [1], so sensation is reduced or lost [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that the action potential occurs at nodes only; "insulation speeds it up" alone is too vague.

**6.** (a) Action potential arrives at presynaptic knob [1]; voltage-gated calcium channels open and Ca²⁺ diffuses in [1]; vesicles fuse with the presynaptic membrane and release acetylcholine by exocytosis [1]; acetylcholine diffuses across the cleft [1] and binds to receptors on the postsynaptic membrane, opening sodium channels [1]; Na⁺ enters, depolarising the membrane to threshold and starting an action potential [1]. (b) Only the presynaptic knob contains vesicles of transmitter [1]; only the postsynaptic membrane has receptors [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Sequence questions tend to reward every link in the chain, so do not skip the calcium step.

**7.** (a) Depolarisation increases as calcium concentration rises [1]; the increase slows at higher concentrations, from 5.6 to 6.0 mV between 2.0 and 4.0 mmol dm⁻³ [1]. (b) (5.6 − 2.4) / 2.4 × 100 [1] = **133%** [1]. (c) More Ca²⁺ entering means more vesicles fuse and release acetylcholine [1], so more sodium channels open and the membrane depolarises more [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote figures from the table when describing a trend, and show the subtraction in a percentage calculation.

**8.** (a) Circular muscle contracts [1]; radial muscle relaxes [1]; pupil constricts, reducing light entering the eye [1]. (b) (8.0 − 3.0) / 8.0 × 100 [1] = **62.5%** [1]. (c) Areas: π × 4.0² = 50.3 mm² and π × 1.5² = 7.07 mm² [1]; 50.3 / 7.07 = **7.1 times** [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Use radius, not diameter, in the area formula; the ratio (8.0 / 3.0)² is a quicker check.

**9.** (a) Nicotine binds to acetylcholine receptors, mimicking acetylcholine and opening sodium channels [1]; it is not broken down by acetylcholinesterase, so stimulation is prolonged [1]. (b) Alpha toxin binds to the acetylcholine receptors and blocks them [1], so acetylcholine cannot depolarise the postsynaptic membrane and the muscle is paralysed [1]. (c) Lidocaine blocks voltage-gated sodium channels [1], so action potentials are not generated or conducted and sensation is lost [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the target (receptor or channel) and the outcome in each case; "it blocks nerves" is too vague.

**10.** (a) Parkinson's disease involves a shortage of dopamine in the brain [1]; dopamine does not cross from the blood into the brain easily, but L-DOPA does [1]; in the brain L-DOPA is converted to dopamine, which relieves symptoms [1]. (b) MDMA causes serotonin to be released from presynaptic neurones [1] and blocks its reuptake, so more serotonin remains in the cleft [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Explain why L-DOPA works as a precursor, not that it "is dopamine".

**11.** (a) 40 − (−70) = **110 mV** [1]. (b) Rise = −62 − (−70) = 8 mV [1]; 8 / 110 × 100 = **7.3%** [1]. (c) Lidocaine blocks voltage-gated sodium channels [1], so no positive feedback occurs and threshold (−55 mV) is not reached [1]; the small rise comes from local current spreading from R1 [1]. (d) The action potential is not regenerated at R2, so no depolarisation reaches threshold at R3 [1]; no sodium ions enter at R3 [1]. (e) R1 is not under lidocaine, so its sodium channels work normally [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* In data questions, link each recording to a mechanism: blocked channels, no threshold, no regeneration.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "sodium goes in" without saying it diffuses through voltage-gated channels.
- Leaving out potassium ions in repolarisation.
- Forgetting calcium ions in the synapse sequence.
- Naming acetylcholine as the cause of the next action potential without describing the sodium channels.
- Dividing by milliseconds without converting to seconds.
- Using diameter in place of radius in the area formula.
- Confusing nicotine (mimics) with alpha toxin (blocks).
- Giving lidocaine as a synapse drug when it acts on axons.
