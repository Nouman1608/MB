## Questions

**1.** The scores on a reasoning test are modelled by X ~ N(72, 5²).
**(a)** Find P(X < 65). **[2]**
**(b)** Find P(68 < X < 79). **[3]**

**2.** A normal curve has points of inflection at x = 14 and x = 26.
**(a)** Find the mean and the standard deviation. **[2]**
**(b)** State the interval that contains about 95% of the values. **[1]**
**(c)** State the approximate proportion of values between 14 and 26. **[1]**

**3.** X ~ N(55, 8²).
**(a)** Find k such that P(X < k) = 0.92. **[3]**
**(b)** Find m such that P(X > m) = 0.2. **[2]**

**4.** X ~ N(40, σ²) and P(X > 46) = 0.1. Find σ. **[4]**

**5.** X ~ N(μ, σ²), P(X < 12) = 0.15 and P(X > 20) = 0.25. Find μ and σ. **[6]**

**6.** The mass of a parcel handled by a depot is modelled by X ~ N(2.8, 0.35²) kg.
**(a)** Find the probability that a parcel has mass over 3.5 kg. **[2]**
**(b)** Of 600 parcels, how many would you expect to exceed 3.5 kg? **[1]**
**(c)** The heaviest 5% of parcels are sent by a different route. Find the minimum mass of these parcels. **[2]**

**7.** Eight parcels from question 6 are chosen at random. Use the unrounded probability from question 6(a).
**(a)** Find the probability that exactly 2 exceed 3.5 kg. **[3]**
**(b)** State one assumption needed for your method in part (a). **[1]**

**8.** For each situation, name a suitable distribution and give a reason from the context.
**(a)** The number out of 40 sown seeds that germinate, when each germinates with probability 0.9, independently. **[2]**
**(b)** The heights of 800 adults, shown by a symmetrical bell-shaped histogram. **[2]**
**(c)** The time customers wait at a counter, with mean 3 minutes and standard deviation 3 minutes, shown by a histogram with a long right tail. **[2]**

**9.** The waiting time T minutes at a clinic is modelled by T ~ N(18, 11²).
**(a)** Find P(T < 0). **[2]**
**(b)** What does your answer say about the model? **[2]**

**10.** X ~ B(150, 0.4).
**(a)** Write down a normal distribution that could approximate X. **[2]**
**(b)** Using this model, state the interval within which about 95% of values lie. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why a normal model suits this binomial distribution, and why P(X = 60) needs more care. **[2]**

**11.** The volume X ml of juice in a carton is modelled by X ~ N(μ, σ²). Of the cartons, 3% hold less than 245 ml and 8% hold more than 262 ml.
**(a)** Find μ and σ. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the proportion of cartons with less than 250 ml. **[2]**
**(c)** A pack contains 12 cartons chosen at random. Find the probability that at least 2 hold less than 250 ml. **[2]**
**(d)** The firm changes only the mean so that 1% of cartons hold less than 245 ml. Find the new mean. **[2]**
Use unrounded values from part (a) in the later parts.

## Answers

**1. (a)** z = (65 − 72)/5 = −1.4 [1]. P(X < 65) = **0.0808** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** z = −0.8 and z = 1.4 [1]. P(68 < X < 79) = P(X < 79) − P(X < 68) [1]. = **0.707** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the distribution and the probability statement before the number, so your method is visible even if the calculator entry slips.

**2. (a)** μ is halfway between the inflection points: μ = (14 + 26)/2 = **20** [1]. Inflection points are μ ± σ, so 2σ = 12 and σ = **6** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** μ ± 2σ gives **8 to 32** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** The interval 14 to 26 is μ ± σ, so about **two-thirds** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Explain which fact you are using. A bare "20 and 6" gives the reader no way to follow your reasoning if one value is wrong.

**3. (a)** P(X < k) = 0.92, so z = 1.4051 [1]. k = 55 + 1.4051 × 8 [1]. k = **66.2** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** P(X < m) = 0.8, so z = 0.8416 [1]. m = 55 + 0.8416 × 8 = **61.7** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Sketch the shaded area. Part (b) is an upper tail, and the most common slip is entering 0.2 where 0.8 belongs.

**4.** P(X < 46) = 0.9 [1]. z = 1.2816 [1]. (46 − 40)/σ = 1.2816 [1]. σ = 6/1.2816 = **4.68** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the equation with the z-value in it. Rounding z to 1.28 before dividing can change the third significant figure.

**5.** P(X < 12) = 0.15 gives z = −1.0364 [1]. 12 = μ − 1.0364σ [1]. P(X < 20) = 0.75 gives z = 0.6745 [1]. 20 = μ + 0.6745σ [1]. Subtract: 8 = 1.7109σ, so σ = **4.68** [1]. μ = 12 + 1.0364 × 4.6758 = **16.8** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Subtract the equations to find σ first, then substitute for μ. Keep unrounded z-values until the final line.

**6. (a)** z = (3.5 − 2.8)/0.35 = 2 [1]. P(X > 3.5) = **0.0228** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** 600 × 0.02275 = 13.65, so about **14 parcels** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** P(X < w) = 0.95, so z = 1.6449 [1]. w = 2.8 + 1.6449 × 0.35 = **3.38 kg** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that the answer in part (b) is an expected value, and round it in context. Part (c) is a boundary, so include the unit.

**7. (a)** Let Y be the number exceeding 3.5 kg, so Y ~ B(8, 0.02275) [1]. P(Y = 2) = 8C2 × (0.02275)² × (0.97725)⁶ [1]. = **0.0126** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** The parcels are independent, with the same probability of exceeding 3.5 kg [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* An assumption must be about the model, in context. "The numbers are random" is too vague.

**8. (a)** **Binomial** B(40, 0.9) [1]. There is a fixed number of seeds, two outcomes and a constant, independent probability of success [1] **[2]**
**(b)** **Normal** [1]. The data are continuous, symmetrical and bell-shaped, with one central peak [1] **[2]**
**(c)** **Normal is not suitable** [1]. The histogram is skewed to the right, and a mean equal to the standard deviation would give a lot of probability below zero, which is impossible for a waiting time [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the model and tie the reason to the wording of the context. A reason that fits any situation earns little.

**9. (a)** z = (0 − 18)/11 = −1.636 [1]. P(T < 0) = **0.0509** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** The model gives about a 5% chance of a negative waiting time [1]. That is impossible, so the normal model is not a good fit for this variable [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* State the numerical result, then draw the conclusion about the model in context. A calculation with no comment misses the point of the question.

**10. (a)** μ = np = 60 [1]. σ² = npq = 150 × 0.4 × 0.6 = 36, so the model is **N(60, 36)** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** σ = 6, so μ ± 2σ gives **48 to 72** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** n is large and p is not close to 0 or 1, so the binomial distribution is roughly symmetrical and bell-shaped [1]. The normal variable is continuous, so P(X = 60) = 0, whereas the binomial has positive probability at 60. A continuity correction treats "X = 60" as the interval 59.5 to 60.5 [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Give two linked ideas, one about why the shape suits a normal curve and one about discrete against continuous. Do not just restate the formulae.

**11. (a)** P(X < 245) = 0.03 gives z = −1.8808, so 245 = μ − 1.8808σ [1]. P(X < 262) = 0.92 gives z = 1.4051, so 262 = μ + 1.4051σ [1]. Subtract: 17 = 3.2859σ, so σ = **5.17** [1]. μ = 245 + 1.8808 × 5.1737 = **255** (254.7) [1] **[4]**
**(b)** z = (250 − 254.73)/5.1737 = −0.914 [1]. P(X < 250) = **0.180** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Let Y be the number below 250 ml, so Y ~ B(12, 0.18026) [1]. P(Y ≥ 2) = 1 − P(Y ≤ 1) = **0.665** [1] **[2]**
**(d)** P(X < 245) = 0.01 gives z = −2.3263 [1]. New mean = 245 + 2.3263 × 5.1737 = **257 ml** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In a multi-part question, use the unrounded values from part (a) in later parts. Starting later parts from 255 and 5.17 can move the third significant figure.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Entering the variance in the calculator where the standard deviation is needed.
- Using the area to the right as the input to the inverse normal function.
- Rounding z-values to two decimal places in a two-unknown problem.
- Skipping the sketch, then shading the wrong tail.
- Naming a model with no reason, or giving a reason that does not mention the context.
- Calling a distribution normal because the data are "random", rather than continuous, symmetrical and bell-shaped.
- Stating a probability for a negative time and not commenting on the model.
- Confusing the two-thirds and 95% intervals, or reading σ as the distance from the mean to the edge of the curve.
- Treating an expected frequency as exact when the answer should be rounded in context.
