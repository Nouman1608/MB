**Syllabus:** Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, **A Level**
subtopic 24.1: predict products from state, E⦵ and concentration; F = Le; Q = It; mass and
volume of products; describe the electrolytic determination of the Avogadro constant.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Electrolysis | decomposition of an electrolyte by a direct electric current |
| Electrolyte | molten or aqueous ionic substance that conducts by movement of ions |
| Cathode | negative electrode; reduction |
| Anode | positive electrode; oxidation |
| Inert electrode | does not react (platinum, graphite) |
| Active electrode | takes part, e.g. a copper anode dissolves |
| Faraday constant, F | charge on one mole of electrons, 9.65 × 10⁴ C mol⁻¹ |
| Avogadro constant, L | particles per mole, 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹ |
| Charge on electron, e | 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C (magnitude) |

## Product rules

**Molten binary compound:** metal at cathode, non-metal at anode.

**Aqueous solution, cathode** (more positive E⦵ is reduced):

- Ag⁺ (+0.80 V) and Cu²⁺ (+0.34 V) → the metal is deposited.
- Ions of reactive metals (Na⁺, K⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺, all very negative E⦵) → **hydrogen** from water.

**Aqueous solution, inert anode** (less positive E⦵ is oxidised; compare with
O₂/H₂O at +1.23 V):

- I⁻ (+0.54 V) and Br⁻ (+1.07 V) → halogen, even when dilute.
- Cl⁻ (+1.36 V) → **chlorine if concentrated**, **oxygen if dilute**.
- SO₄²⁻, NO₃⁻ → **oxygen** from water.

**Active copper anode:** Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻; no gas.

## Half-equations to know

    cathode:  Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu          Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag
              2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂          2H₂O + 2e⁻ → H₂ + 2OH⁻
    anode:    2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻         2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻      2I⁻ → I₂ + 2e⁻
              2H₂O → O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻    4OH⁻ → O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻
              Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻

## Side effects in solution

- CuSO₄(aq), inert electrodes: blue colour fades as Cu²⁺ is removed; solution becomes acidic
  (H⁺ from the anode).
- CuSO₄(aq), copper electrodes: colour stays the same; Cu²⁺ formed at the anode replaces Cu²⁺
  removed at the cathode.
- Concentrated NaCl(aq): solution becomes alkaline (OH⁻ from the cathode).

## Calculation chain

    Q = It                      (I in A, t in s)
    mol e⁻ = Q ÷ F
    mol product = mol e⁻ ÷ (electrons in half-equation per product)
    mass = mol × M;    gas volume = mol × 24.0 dm³ (room conditions)

| Product | Electrons per mole of product | Faradays per mole |
|---|---|---|
| Ag | 1 | 1 F |
| Cu, H₂, Cl₂ | 2 | 2 F |
| Al | 3 | 3 F |
| O₂ | 4 | 4 F |

**Example:** 0.400 A through AgNO₃(aq) for 15.0 min.

    Q = 0.400 × 900 = 360 C;  mol e⁻ = 360 ÷ 96 500 = 3.73 × 10⁻³
    mass Ag = 3.73 × 10⁻³ × 107.9 = 0.403 g

**Example:** 2.00 A through concentrated NaCl(aq) for 10.0 min; volume of chlorine.

    Q = 1200 C;  mol e⁻ = 0.012 44;  mol Cl₂ = 0.006 22
    volume = 0.006 22 × 24.0 = 0.149 dm³ = 149 cm³

**Cells in series:** the same charge passes through each cell, so the moles of electrons are
equal; compare products through their electron ratios.

## F = Le

    F = L × e = 6.022 × 10²³ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ ≈ 9.64 × 10⁴ C mol⁻¹

Rearranged: L = F ÷ e; this is the basis of the Avogadro experiment.

## The Avogadro constant experiment

1. Weigh clean, dry copper electrodes.
2. Series circuit: d.c. supply, variable resistor, ammeter, electrodes in CuSO₄(aq).
3. Constant current I for time t (timer; adjust resistor).
4. Rinse, dry (propanone), reweigh; use mass change of an electrode.
5. Q = It; electrons = Q ÷ e; mol Cu = Δm ÷ 63.5; mol e⁻ = 2 × mol Cu.
6. L = electrons ÷ mol e⁻.

Sources of error: current not constant; copper flaking from the cathode; electrodes not dry;
inaccurate timing.

## Exam traps

- Convert minutes or hours to seconds.
- Hydrogen, not sodium or potassium, forms at the cathode from aqueous solutions of their salts.
- Bromide and iodide are oxidised in preference to water even when dilute; chloride only when
  concentrated.
- Oxygen needs **4** electrons per molecule, so it forms half the moles of hydrogen.
- A copper anode dissolves; no oxygen forms.
- Use 24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹ at room conditions, 22.4 dm³ mol⁻¹ only at s.t.p.
- In the Avogadro calculation, divide charge by e (not F) to get the number of electrons.

## Self-test

1. State the products of electrolysing molten zinc chloride with inert electrodes.
2. Name the cathode product when aqueous potassium nitrate is electrolysed, and explain.
3. Name the anode product when dilute NaCl(aq) is electrolysed with inert electrodes.
4. Write the anode half-equation for electrolysis of CuSO₄(aq) with copper electrodes.
5. State the relationship between F, L and e.
6. Calculate the charge passed by 0.150 A in 2.00 hours.
7. How many faradays are needed to produce one mole of oxygen gas?
8. 0.0500 mol of electrons passes through CuSO₄(aq) with inert electrodes. Calculate the mass
   of copper deposited. (A<sub>r</sub>: Cu = 63.5)
9. For the same charge, calculate the volume of oxygen at room conditions.
10. Explain why the colour of CuSO₄(aq) fades with inert electrodes but not with copper
    electrodes.
11. In an Avogadro experiment, which measurements are needed?
12. Why is a variable resistor included in the Avogadro circuit?

**Answers**

1. Zinc at the cathode; chlorine at the anode.
2. Hydrogen: K⁺/K has a very negative E⦵, so water is reduced in preference.
3. Oxygen (mainly), because chloride is too dilute to be discharged in preference to water.
4. Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻.
5. F = Le.
6. Q = 0.150 × 7200 = **1080 C**.
7. **4 F**.
8. mol Cu = 0.0250; mass = 0.0250 × 63.5 = **1.59 g**.
9. mol O₂ = 0.0500 ÷ 4 = 0.0125; volume = 0.0125 × 24.0 = **0.300 dm³**.
10. With inert electrodes Cu²⁺ is removed at the cathode and not replaced; with copper
    electrodes the anode dissolves, forming Cu²⁺ at the same rate.
11. Current, time, and the mass change of an electrode (plus the known charge on the electron).
12. To keep the current constant throughout the experiment.
