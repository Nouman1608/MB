These notes cover section 6 of Unit M1 in Pearson Edexcel International A Level Mathematics (YMA01): the moment of a force, and simple problems with coplanar parallel forces acting on a body, with the conditions for equilibrium. For worked examples, use the [M1 moments study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-mathematics-yma01-m1-moments-edexcel-alevel-maths/).

## Definitions

```
moment about P = F × d     (d = perpendicular distance from P to the line of action)
units: N m
```

- A moment has a sense: clockwise or anticlockwise.
- A force through P has no moment about P.
- For vertical forces on a horizontal rod, d is the distance along the rod.

## Conditions for equilibrium (parallel forces)

| Condition | Equation |
|---|---|
| No resultant force | Total upward force = total downward force |
| No resultant moment about any point | Clockwise moments = anticlockwise moments |

Both are needed. A rod with zero resultant force can still turn.

## Choosing where to take moments

| Situation | Take moments about |
|---|---|
| Two unknown reactions | One of the supports (removes that reaction) |
| On the point of tilting about D | D (and set the other reaction to zero) |
| Unknown position of the centre of mass | A support, so the equation is linear in x |
| Checking an answer | A second point; the moments must still balance |

## Where weights act

| Body | Weight acts at |
|---|---|
| Uniform rod or plank | Its midpoint |
| Non-uniform rod | Its centre of mass G (often an unknown distance x) |
| Light rod | Nowhere; no weight |
| Particle (person, load) | The point where it is placed |

## Tilting

- "On the point of tilting about D": the rod is about to turn about D and lift off the other support, so **that other reaction is zero**.
- "Greatest load" or "furthest distance" questions are tilting questions.
- After setting the reaction to zero, take moments about the pivot. Only the weights are left.

**Example:** uniform plank, mass 30 kg, length 8 m, supports 2 m from each end. A man of mass 75 kg walks beyond the support D. The plank's weight is 2 m from D on the other side. Tilting: 30g(2) = 75g(y), so y = 0.8 m beyond D.

## Rods on strings

- Vertical strings act like supports, but pull upwards.
- "The tensions are equal": each tension is half the total weight.
- "The tension at A is k times the tension at C": use T_A + T_C = total weight to find both, then take moments.
- A string can break: compare each tension with its stated limit.

## Ranges

If a load's position x varies, write each reaction in terms of x by taking moments about the other support. Then apply each condition, such as R ≥ 0 or T ≤ limit, to get a range for x. Give the final range as two inequalities combined.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Moment | The turning effect of a force about a point |
| Line of action | The line along which a force acts |
| Pivot | The point a body turns about |
| Reaction | The upward force from a support |
| Centre of mass | The point where the weight of a body can be taken to act |
| Tilting | Turning about one support, with the reaction at the other support zero |

## Exam traps

- Distances measured from the end of the rod, not from the pivot.
- The rod's weight forgotten.
- A non-uniform rod's weight placed at the midpoint.
- The wrong reaction set to zero when tilting.
- An answer for a position that lies beyond the end of the rod, not checked.
- g left out of some terms but not others.

## Self-test

1. Define the moment of a force about a point.
2. A 12 N force acts at right angles to a spanner, 0.25 m from the bolt. Find its moment about the bolt.
3. Why is it helpful to take moments about a support?
4. A uniform rod is 3 m long. Where does its weight act?
5. State the two conditions for a rod to be in equilibrium under parallel forces.
6. A light rod AB of length 2 m rests on supports at A and B, with a 30 N load 0.5 m from A. Find the reaction at B.
7. Find the reaction at A in question 6.
8. A plank is on the point of tilting about support D. What do you know about the other support?
9. A uniform rod of mass 4 kg, length 2 m, is pivoted 0.5 m from one end. What mass at that end balances it?
10. A non-uniform rod AB of length 2 m and weight 50 N hangs from strings at A and B. The tension at B is 20 N. Where is its centre of mass?
11. Two equal and opposite parallel forces act on a rod at different points. Is the rod in equilibrium? Why?
12. How would you check the reactions you have found for a beam?

**Answers:**

1. The force multiplied by the perpendicular distance from the point to its line of action.
2. 12 × 0.25 = 3 N m.
3. The reaction at that support has no moment about it, so it does not appear in the equation.
4. At its midpoint, 1.5 m from each end.
5. The resultant force is zero, and the resultant moment about any point is zero.
6. Moments about A: 2R_B = 30 × 0.5, so R_B = 7.5 N.
7. R_A = 30 − 7.5 = 22.5 N.
8. The reaction there is zero.
9. The weight acts 0.5 m beyond the pivot on the other side: 4g(0.5) = mg(0.5), so m = 4 kg.
10. Moments about A: 20 × 2 = 50x, so x = 0.8 m from A.
11. No: the resultant force is zero but the forces have a turning effect, so the rod rotates.
12. Take moments about a different point and check that they balance, or check that the reactions add to the total weight.
