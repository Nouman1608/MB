## Food security

**Definition**: having enough food to feed a population.

**The six biological threats in the specification**

1. Increasing birth rate in some countries.
2. Changing diets in developed countries, so scarce food resources are transported around the world.
3. New pests and pathogens that affect farming.
4. Environmental changes that affect food production, such as famine when rains fail.
5. The cost of agricultural inputs.
6. Conflicts affecting the availability of water or food.

**Principle**: sustainable methods must be found to feed all people on Earth.

**Food statistics method**

```
food per person = total food ÷ population
if population grows faster than food production → food per person falls → food security worsens
```

Worked reminder (invented): 12 million tonnes for 30 million people = 0.40 t per person; later 13.5 million
tonnes for 36 million people = 0.375 t per person. Food per person fell.

## Farming techniques

```
animal uses food in respiration for movement and warmth → energy transferred to the environment
limit movement + control temperature → less energy transferred → more biomass (meat) from the same food
```

- Some animals are fed **high-protein foods** to increase growth.
- Some people have **ethical objections** to intensive methods.

| For intensive farming | Against intensive farming |
|---|---|
| More efficient conversion of feed into meat | Animals cannot move freely or behave naturally |
| Cheaper food | Disease spreads easily in crowded conditions |
| Less land per animal | Heating buildings costs money and energy |

**Efficiency**: mass gained ÷ mass of feed eaten × 100.

## Sustainable fisheries

- Fish stocks in the oceans are **declining**.
- Stocks must stay at a level where **breeding continues**.

| Measure | How it works |
|---|---|
| Control of net size (larger mesh) | Young fish escape, grow and breed |
| Fishing quotas | Limit the amount caught, so enough adults remain to breed |

## Biotechnology

| Product | Organism | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| Mycoprotein | Fungus *Fusarium* | Grown on glucose syrup in aerobic conditions; biomass harvested and purified; protein-rich and suitable for vegetarians |
| Human insulin | Genetically modified bacterium | Harvested and purified; used to treat diabetes |
| Golden rice | GM crop | Improved nutritional value: contains a substance the body uses to make vitamin A |

**Mycoprotein in four steps**

```
Fusarium + glucose syrup → grown with oxygen (aerobic) → biomass harvested → purified into food
```

Model answer to learn (2 marks): "Oxygen is supplied so the fungus can respire aerobically, transferring
enough energy for rapid growth of biomass."

- Large quantities of microorganisms can be **cultured for food**.
- **GM crops** could provide more food or food with improved nutritional value.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Food security | Having enough food to feed a population |
| Sustainable | Meeting present needs without harming the ability of future generations to meet theirs |
| Agricultural inputs | Things farmers must buy, such as seed, fertiliser, feed and fuel |
| Intensive farming | Raising animals with limited movement and controlled temperature to increase efficiency |
| Fishing quota | Limit on the amount of a fish species that may be caught |
| Mesh size | Size of the holes in a fishing net |
| Mycoprotein | Protein-rich food made from the fungus *Fusarium* |
| Genetically modified (GM) | Having a gene from another organism inserted |
| Biotechnology | Using living organisms to make useful products |
| Golden rice | GM rice with improved nutritional value |

## Exam traps

- Food security is about having **enough** food, not just healthy food.
- Intensive farming reduces **energy transfer from the animal to the environment**; say "respiration".
- Larger **mesh**, not larger nets.
- Mycoprotein comes from a **fungus** grown **aerobically** on **glucose syrup**.
- Insulin is made by **bacteria**, not by fungi.
- Evaluations need ethics or disadvantages as well as benefits, and a judgement.

## Self-test

1. Define food security.
2. Give three biological factors that threaten food security.
3. Why do changing diets in developed countries threaten food security elsewhere?
4. Name two ways farmers restrict energy transfer from food animals to the environment.
5. Explain why restricting movement increases the efficiency of meat production.
6. Give one objection to intensive farming.
7. A pig eats 300 kg of feed and gains 60 kg. Calculate the efficiency.
8. How does a larger mesh size help conserve fish stocks?
9. What is a fishing quota?
10. Name the organism used to make mycoprotein and what it is grown on.
11. What type of organism is genetically modified to make human insulin?
12. Give one way GM crops could help feed a growing population.

### Answers

1. Having enough food to feed a population.
2. Any three: increasing birth rate; changing diets in developed countries; new pests and pathogens;
   environmental change such as failed rains; cost of agricultural inputs; conflicts.
3. Scarce food resources are transported around the world to meet demand, away from the people who need them.
4. Limiting the animals' movement; controlling the temperature of their surroundings.
5. Less energy is transferred in respiration for muscle contraction, so more of the food becomes biomass.
6. Any one: ethical objections to restricting the animals; disease spreads easily; cost of heating.
7. 60 ÷ 300 × 100 = **20%**.
8. Young fish escape, survive and breed, so the population is maintained.
9. A limit on the amount of a fish species that can be caught.
10. *Fusarium* (a fungus); glucose syrup, in aerobic conditions.
11. A bacterium.
12. Any one: higher yields (more food); improved nutritional value, such as golden rice.
