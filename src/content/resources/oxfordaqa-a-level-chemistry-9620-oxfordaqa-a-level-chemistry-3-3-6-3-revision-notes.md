## Specification in brief

- Bonds absorb infrared at **characteristic wavenumbers**.
- **Fingerprinting** identifies a molecule by comparison of spectra.
- Use spectra and the **data booklet** to identify **bonds**, **functional groups** and **impurities**.
- Link IR absorption by bonds in **CO₂, methane and water vapour** to **global warming**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Wavenumber | Number of waves per centimetre (cm⁻¹); proportional to the energy of the radiation |
| Transmittance | Percentage of radiation passing through the sample; dips show absorption |
| Absorption band | A dip in the spectrum at the wavenumber a bond absorbs |
| Fingerprint region | Below about 1500 cm⁻¹; complex pattern unique to each compound |
| Fingerprinting | Identifying a compound by matching its fingerprint region to reference spectra |
| Impurity | Substance other than the product; shown by a band the product cannot give |
| Greenhouse gas | Gas whose bonds absorb infrared emitted by the Earth |

## Recall: ranges (from the data booklet)

| Bond | cm⁻¹ | Appearance |
|---|---|---|
| O–H alcohol | 3230–3550 | broad |
| N–H | 3300–3500 | medium |
| C–H | 2850–3300 | almost always present |
| O–H acid | 2500–3000 | very broad |
| C≡N | 2220–2260 | sharp |
| C=O | 1680–1750 | strong, sharp |
| C=C | 1620–1680 | medium/weak |
| C–O | 1000–1300 | fingerprint region |

## Recall: reading order

1. Above 3000: alcohol O–H? N–H?
2. 2500–3000 very broad: acid O–H?
3. 2220–2260: C≡N?
4. 1680–1750: C=O?
5. 1620–1680: C=C?
6. Combine presence **and absence** of bands.

| Evidence | Group |
|---|---|
| O–H (broad, 3230–3550), no C=O | alcohol |
| C=O, no O–H | aldehyde or ketone |
| very broad O–H (2500–3000) + C=O | carboxylic acid |
| C=C only | alkene |
| C≡N | nitrile |

## Recall: impurities and reaction monitoring

| Reaction | Band that appears | Band that should disappear |
|---|---|---|
| alcohol → aldehyde/ketone | C=O (1680–1750) | O–H (3230–3550) |
| aldehyde over-oxidised → acid | very broad O–H (2500–3000) | — |
| alcohol → alkene (elimination) | C=C (1620–1680) | O–H (3230–3550) |
| halogenoalkane → nitrile | C≡N (2220–2260) | — |
| halogenoalkane → alcohol | O–H (3230–3550) | — |

A broad O–H band in a product that should have no O–H shows **water** or **unreacted alcohol**.

### Worked reminder

Product of heating 1-chloropropane with aqueous NaOH: broad band at 3340 cm⁻¹, C–O band near 1050 cm⁻¹, nothing at 1680–1750. The O–H (alcohol) and C–O bands show **propan-1-ol** has formed; the absence of C=O rules out oxidation to propanal or propanoic acid. Only the fingerprint region, compared with a reference spectrum, would confirm propan-1-ol rather than propan-2-ol.

## Recall: global warming chain

1. The Earth's surface, warmed by the Sun, **emits infrared**.
2. **C=O** in CO₂, **C–H** in CH₄ and **O–H** in H₂O **absorb** some of it.
3. The energy is **re-emitted in all directions**; some returns to the surface.
4. **Higher concentrations** → more absorption → **global warming**.

## Exam traps

- Absorptions are **dips**, not peaks.
- Wavenumber **decreases** left to right.
- Do not use **C–H** as evidence of a functional group.
- Alcohol O–H (**3230–3550**) and acid O–H (**2500–3000**) are different ranges.
- C=O alone cannot separate **aldehydes from ketones**.
- Fingerprinting needs a **reference spectrum** to compare with.
- Name the **bonds** in the greenhouse gases, not just the gases.
- An absent band means **no significant amount detected**, not proof of total purity.
- Quote the **measured wavenumber and the range** it falls in, every time.

## Self-test

1. Why does a molecule absorb infrared radiation?
2. Give the range for the C=O absorption.
3. How do the O–H bands of an alcohol and a carboxylic acid differ?
4. Which region is called the fingerprint region?
5. Explain how fingerprinting identifies a compound.
6. A spectrum has a strong dip at 1715 cm⁻¹ and nothing between 3230 and 3550 cm⁻¹. Which two functional groups are possible?
7. How would IR show that ethanol has been oxidised to ethanoic acid?
8. A sample of hex-1-ene shows a broad band at 3400 cm⁻¹. Suggest why.
9. Which band shows that butanenitrile has formed from 1-bromopropane?
10. Name the bond in each greenhouse gas that absorbs infrared: CO₂, CH₄, H₂O.
11. Explain how infrared absorption by these gases contributes to global warming.
12. Why can IR not distinguish pentan-2-one from pentan-3-one using the C=O band alone?

### Answers

1. Its **bonds vibrate**; radiation at a matching frequency is **absorbed**, increasing the vibration.
2. **1680–1750 cm⁻¹.**
3. Alcohol O–H: **broad, 3230–3550**; acid O–H: **very broad, 2500–3000**.
4. **Below about 1500 cm⁻¹.**
5. The pattern there is **unique to each compound**; matching it to a **reference spectrum** identifies the compound.
6. **Aldehyde or ketone.**
7. A **very broad O–H band at 2500–3000** appears with a **C=O band at 1680–1750**; the alcohol O–H band at 3230–3550 disappears.
8. It contains **water or an alcohol impurity** (O–H); hex-1-ene has no O–H.
9. A sharp **C≡N** band at **2220–2260 cm⁻¹**.
10. CO₂: **C=O**; CH₄: **C–H**; H₂O: **O–H**.
11. The bonds **absorb infrared emitted by the Earth** and **re-emit** it in all directions, so some returns to the surface; more gas means more warming.
12. Both are ketones with the **same bonds**, so both show C=O in the same range; only the **fingerprint region** differs.
