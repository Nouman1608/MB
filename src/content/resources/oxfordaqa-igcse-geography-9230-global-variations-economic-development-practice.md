Related: [global variations study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-geography-9230-global-variations-economic-development/) and [global variations revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-geography-9230-global-variations-economic-development-revision-notes/). Question 11 asks for an example: use the LIC or NEE you have studied.

## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by an NEE. **[1]**

**2.** Name **two** measures of development that are social, not economic. **[2]**

**3.** Define infant mortality. **[1]**

**4.** Name the three things combined in the Human Development Index. **[3]**

**5.** Give **one** way in which Fairtrade helps farmers. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** The table shows invented data for three countries.

| | Country X | Country Y | Country Z |
|---|---|---|---|
| GNI per head (US$) | 650 | 6 200 | 52 000 |
| Life expectancy (years) | 56 | 72 | 83 |
| People per doctor | 15 000 | 1 500 | 250 |
| Birth rate (per 1 000) | 41 | 20 | 10 |

**(a)** Calculate how many times greater the GNI per head of Country Z is than Country X. Give your answer to the nearest whole number. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the difference in life expectancy between Country Z and Country X. **[1]**

**(c)** Using the data, suggest which country is most likely to be an LIC and which is most likely to be an NEE. Give reasons. **[3]**

**7.** Explain **two** limitations of using GNI per head as the only measure of development. **[4]**

**8.** Describe the link between the Demographic Transition Model and the level of development. **[4]**

**9.** Explain how physical factors and historical factors can each cause uneven development. **[6]**

**10.** Explain **two** consequences of uneven development. **[4]**

**11.** For an LIC or NEE that you have studied, explain how the growth of tourism helps to reduce the development gap. **[6]**

**12.** Explain why aid does not always reduce the development gap. **[4]**

**13.** "Debt relief is the most effective way to reduce the development gap." To what extent do you agree? **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** A country with rapid economic growth, mainly through industrial development, that is between an LIC and an HIC (accept: newly emerging economy) [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Any two from: life expectancy; infant mortality; literacy rate; people per doctor; access to safe water; birth rate; death rate [1 each]. **[2]**

**3.** The number of babies who die before their first birthday per 1 000 live births [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Life expectancy [1]; education (or literacy or years of schooling) [1]; income (GNI per head) [1]. **[3]**

**5.** Any one from: farmers receive a guaranteed minimum price; they receive a premium for community projects; it gives stable income [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** 52 000 ÷ 650 = 80 [1] (80.0, so 80 times greater) [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** 83 - 56 = 27 years [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** Country X is most likely an LIC, because it has the lowest income, life expectancy and doctor access, and the highest birth rate [1]. Country Y is most likely an NEE, because its indicators are in the middle [1], with a GNI of 6 200 and life expectancy of 72, which are higher than the LIC but well below the HIC [1]. **[3]**

**7.** First limitation, such as the average hides inequality, as a few rich people raise the average while most remain poor [2]. Second limitation, such as it ignores the informal economy and subsistence farming, so real income is not measured [2]; or it does not show quality of life such as health and education [2]; or prices differ between countries [2]. **[4]**

**8.** In Stage 1 and Stage 2, birth rates are high, which is typical of LICs, and population growth is rapid in Stage 2 as death rates fall [1]. In Stage 3, birth rates fall as countries develop, for example NEEs [1]. In Stage 4 and Stage 5, birth and death rates are low, as in many HICs [1]. As countries develop, death rates fall first because of better health, food and sanitation, and then birth rates fall as incomes and education rise [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Physical factor with development, for example a landlocked country with no port has higher trade costs, or droughts and hazards destroy crops and infrastructure, or tropical diseases reduce workers' health [up to 3 marks]. Historical factor with development, for example colonial rule built an economy based on exporting raw materials, so little industry developed [up to 3 marks]; or borders drawn by colonial powers contributed to conflict that discourages investment. Maximum 6. **[6]**

**10.** Two consequences, each with development (2 marks each). For example: a gap in health, where people in poorer countries have lower life expectancy and higher infant mortality because of poor access to doctors and clean water [2]; a gap in wealth, where low incomes mean poor housing and little access to education [2]; international migration, where people move to richer countries to find work, which brings remittances but may cause brain drain [2]. **[4]**

**11.** Named country [1]. How tourism creates jobs, for example in hotels and as guides [1]. How it earns foreign currency to pay for imports and services [1]. Multiplier effect, where tourist spending supports local farmers, shops and crafts [1]. Improved infrastructure or conservation funding [1]. A balancing point, such as leakage of profits or seasonal work, or the idea that benefits are best when local people own businesses [1]. Maximum 6. **[6]**

**12.** Aid may create dependency, so a country relies on outside help [1]. It may be tied to conditions that benefit the donor, such as buying goods from the donor country [1]. Money may be lost to corruption or not reach the poorest people [1]. Short-term emergency aid may not tackle the long-term causes of poverty [1]. **[4]**

**13.** Indicative content:
- Agree: debt relief frees government money for health, education and infrastructure, rather than interest payments [1].
- Agree: it can be done on a large scale and does not depend on charity [1].
- Disagree: conditions may be attached, and countries may borrow again [1].
- Disagree: strategies such as investment and industrial development create jobs and economic growth [1].
- Disagree: intermediate technology, Fairtrade and microfinance help at local level and directly reach poor people [1].
- Judgement: debt relief can be powerful where debt payments are very high, but no single strategy works alone; a combination suited to the country is best; a justified conclusion is needed [1]. Maximum 6. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not showing working in calculation questions.
- Treating the death rate as proof of low development.
- Giving one-word limitations of a measure ("averages") without an explanation.
- Writing about the DTM stages without linking them to development.
- Describing an example in general terms, with no named country.
- Judgements without a reasoned conclusion.
