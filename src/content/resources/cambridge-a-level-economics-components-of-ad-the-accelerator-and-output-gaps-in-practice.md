## Questions

**1.** Define autonomous consumption. **[2]**

**2.** Country J has the consumption function C = 80 + 0.6Y.

**(a)** Calculate consumption when income is 500 and when it is 800. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate saving when income is 500. **[2]**
**(c)** State whether the rise in income from 500 to 800 is a movement along or a shift of the function, and give the change in consumption. **[2]**

**3.** Distinguish between a movement along the consumption function and a shift of the function, giving two causes of a shift. **[4]**

**4.** In Country K the accelerator coefficient is 4. Output over five years is 500, 520, 550, 560 and 560.

**(a)** Calculate net investment in each of years 2 to 5. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why net investment falls in year 4 although output rises. **[2]**

**5.** A firm has a capital stock of 900 and needs 3 units of capital per unit of output. Output is 300. Depreciation is 27 a year.

**(a)** Output rises to 320. Calculate gross investment. **[3]**
**(b)** Output then falls to 310. Calculate gross investment and the capital stock after the year. **[3]**

**6.** A government spends 55 cu on teachers' pay, 25 cu on a new bridge, 40 cu on pensions and 15 cu on unemployment benefit.

**(a)** Calculate government spending as it appears in aggregate demand. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why transfer payments are excluded from G but still affect AD. **[2]**

**7.** Country L has exports of 150 and imports M = 40 + 0.2Y.

**(a)** Calculate net exports when Y = 500 and when Y = 800. **[2]**
**(b)** Foreign incomes rise and exports increase by 25. Calculate net exports at Y = 500. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why net exports fall as domestic income rises. **[2]**

**8.** In Country M, AD = 160 + 0.8Y.

**(a)** Calculate AD when Y is 700, 800 and 900. **[2]**
**(b)** State the equilibrium level of national income. **[1]**
**(c)** Full employment income is 900. State the type and size of the gap. **[2]**
**(d)** State how far equilibrium income is below full employment income. **[1]**

**9.** In Country N, AD = 230 + 0.75Y and full employment income is 800.

**(a)** Calculate AD at full employment, and state the type and size of the gap. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate equilibrium income. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain what is likely to happen to prices, and why the equilibrium found in (b) is unlikely to last. **[3]**

**10.** Explain why a small slowdown in the growth of output can cause a large fall in investment. **[6]**

**11.** Evaluate the view that a deflationary gap will close without government action. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Consumption that does not depend on current income [1], for example spending financed by savings or borrowing, or changed by wealth or confidence [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition that only says "spending on goods" is too vague. State the link to income, and add a correct example.

**2. (a)** C at 500 = 80 + 0.6 × 500 = **380** [1]. C at 800 = 80 + 480 = **560** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** S = Y - C [1] = 500 - 380 = **120** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** It is a **movement along** the function, caused by a change in income [1]. Consumption rises by 560 - 380 = **180** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show S = Y - C. A correct saving figure with the working visible is easier to credit than a bare number.

**3.** A movement along is caused by a change in income and keeps the same function [1]. A shift changes autonomous consumption, so consumption changes at every income [1]. Causes of a shift: a fall in household wealth [1] and a rise in interest rates or a fall in confidence [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the cause for each. Income appears on the axis, so it can never be given as a cause of a shift.

**4. (a)** Changes in output are 20, 30, 10, 0 [1]. Net investment = 4 × change: year 2 = **80**, year 3 = **120** [1], year 4 = **40**, year 5 = **0** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Net investment depends on the change in output, which falls from 30 to 10 [1]. Firms need less extra capital even though output is still rising [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Refer to the change in output, not its level, and use the figures from the table.

**5. (a)** Capital needed = 3 × 320 = 960 [1]. Net investment = 960 - 900 = 60 [1]. Gross = 60 + 27 = **87** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Capital needed = 3 × 310 = 930, so desired net investment from 960 is -30 [1]. Depreciation removes only 27, so gross investment cannot go below zero and is **0** [1]. Capital stock = 960 - 27 = **933**, 3 units above the 930 needed [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Stating that gross investment is zero and not negative is the point this question tests. State the leftover capital to show you know why.

**6. (a)** Pay of teachers and the bridge count as spending on goods and services [1]. G = 55 + 25 = **80 cu** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Transfers are not payment for output produced [1]. They raise household income, and any part spent appears in consumption [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Adding all four items to give 135 is the common slip. Say why the two transfer items are excluded.

**7. (a)** At Y = 500, M = 40 + 100 = 140, so X - M = 150 - 140 = **10** [1]. At Y = 800, M = 40 + 160 = 200, so X - M = **-50** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** X = 175, so net exports = 175 - 140 = **35** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Higher income raises spending on imports [1], and imports are subtracted from AD, so net exports fall [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the result as a negative number where imports exceed exports. Explain that imports are induced by income.

**8. (a)** At 700, AD = 160 + 560 = **720**; at 800, AD = **800** [1]; at 900, AD = 160 + 720 = **880** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Equilibrium is where AD = Y, so **800** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** At 900, AD is 880, which is 20 below 900 [1], so it is a **deflationary gap of 20** [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** 900 - 800 = **100** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* The gap in AD (20) and the gap in income (100) are different numbers. Label each one and say which the question asked for.

**9. (a)** AD at 800 = 230 + 600 = 830 [1]. AD exceeds output by 30, so it is an **inflationary gap** [1] of **30** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Y = 230 + 0.75Y [1], so 0.25Y = 230 and Y = **920** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Output cannot rise beyond full capacity, so extra spending raises prices [1]. Firms and workers raise prices and wages when resources are fully used [1]. The 920 would be in money terms. In real terms output stays near 800 and the rise in price level reduces real spending [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Separate real output from the price level. Do not claim real output reaches 920.

**10.** Net investment equals v × change in output [1]. If growth slows, the change in output falls [1]. For example with v = 4, a rise of 30 gives investment of 120 but a rise of 10 gives 40 [1]. Investment falls by two thirds although output is still rising [1]. Firms then order fewer capital goods, so AD falls, which can slow output growth further [1]. A cause of volatility is that investment depends on the change in output, which moves more than the level [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Use figures to show the size of the fall. Say that output still rises, since this is the feature the accelerator explains.

**11.** For: if there is unemployment, wages may fall, which lowers costs and shifts SRAS right [1], and lower prices may raise real wealth and exports [1]. Interest rates may fall as saving exceeds borrowing [1]. Against: wages are often slow to fall [1], falling prices can cause households to delay spending [1], and weak demand lowers investment through the accelerator, which deepens the gap [1]. The time taken matters, since a long wait means lost output and long-term unemployment [1]. Judgement: self-correction is more likely when wages and prices are flexible and the gap is small; a large gap or low confidence favours action by government [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A one-sided list of causes is weaker than a conclusion that depends on a condition, such as wage flexibility or the size of the gap.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Including transfer payments in G.
- Describing a change in income as a shift.
- Basing the accelerator on the level of output.
- Giving negative gross investment.
- Mixing up the gap in AD and the gap in income.
- Subtracting in the wrong order, so a deflationary gap comes out as an inflationary one.
- Forgetting that imports are subtracted in AD.
- Evaluation with no judgement or no condition.
