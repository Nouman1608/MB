**HL only.** These notes cover Regional study 6, Europe and the First World War (1871 to 1923), in the Europe option examined on HL Paper 3. The country case for the war's impact is Britain. See the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-europe-first-world-war/) for fuller explanation.

## What Paper 3 wants

Two 15-mark essays from two different regional studies in two hours. The top band needs an in-depth understanding of the argument, a thorough and balanced evaluation of perspectives and a substantiated judgement.

## Peace and the balance of power (1871 to 1890)

- **Imperialism:** Berlin Conference (1884 to 1885); colonial rivalry; Fashoda (1898).
- **Berlin 1878:** Bismarck honest broker; San Stefano revised; Austria occupies Bosnia; Cyprus to Britain; Russia disappointed.
- **Ottoman decline:** nationalist revolts; financial weakness; Austria and Russia competing in the Balkans.
- **Alliances:** isolating France; Dual Alliance, Triple Alliance, Reinsurance Treaty.

## German foreign policy

| Factor | Points |
|---|---|
| Domestic | Powerful Kaiser, army and Junkers; SPD largest party in 1912; fear of change |
| Wilhelm II | Dismissed Bismarck; impulsive (Kruger Telegram, *Daily Telegraph*) |
| Weltpolitik | Colonies, trade, navy; Tirpitz's laws; naval race with Britain |
| Responses | Britain joins the Entente; France allied with Russia; Russia backs Slavs; Austria depends on Germany |

## Causes of the war: how to rank

- **Long-term:** alliance blocs, naval and army rivalry, Balkan nationalism, imperial competition.
- **Medium-term:** crises of 1905 to 1913.
- **Short-term:** Sarajevo, blank cheque, ultimatum, mobilisations, Belgium.
- **Debate:** Fischer (German aims); Clark (shared responsibility); primacy of domestic policy.

## Why the Central Powers lost

- **Strategy:** Schlieffen Plan failed; two fronts; attrition.
- **Economy:** blockade, shortages, smaller resources; tanks and artillery improvements in 1918.
- **USA:** submarine campaign and Zimmermann Telegram; April 1917; American troops and supplies.
- **Home fronts:** strikes, hunger and mutiny in Germany; national revolts in Austria-Hungary; collapse of allies in autumn 1918.

## Britain as the case

| Theme | Points |
|---|---|
| Social | About three-quarters of a million dead; DORA (1914); conscription (1916) |
| Economic | Heavy borrowing; debt; lost export markets; stronger state control |
| Women | War work; vote for some women over thirty (1918); many returned to the home |
| Marginalized | Conscientious objectors; German-origin residents; Irish nationalists after 1916 |

## The treaties

| Treaty | With | Key terms |
|---|---|---|
| Versailles (1919) | Germany | War guilt, reparations, territory, 100,000 army, Rhineland demilitarised |
| St Germain (1919) | Austria | Union with Germany forbidden |
| Neuilly (1919) | Bulgaria | Territorial and military losses |
| Trianon (1920) | Hungary | Heavy territorial loss |
| Sèvres (1920) | Ottoman Empire | Replaced by Lausanne (1923) |

- **Big Three:** Wilson (self-determination, League), Clemenceau (security), Lloyd George (balance).
- **Reactions:** German *Diktat*; Italian "mutilated victory"; US rejection.
- **Impact:** Ruhr occupation (1923) and hyperinflation; minorities in new states.

## Worked example: planning an essay

**Question:** "To what extent was the entry of the USA the main reason for the defeat of the Central Powers?"

1. **Thesis:** American entry was important, above all for money, supplies and fresh troops in 1918, but the blockade, the two-front war and the exhaustion of Germany's reserves had already weakened the Central Powers.
2. **Paragraph 1, the USA:** unrestricted submarine warfare and the Zimmermann Telegram led to war in April 1917; loans and supplies; large numbers of troops by mid-1918.
3. **Paragraph 2, economic strain:** blockade, shortages, strikes; the Allies' wider resources.
4. **Paragraph 3, military:** failed Schlieffen Plan; failure of the Spring Offensive; Allied tactics and tanks in the Hundred Days.
5. **Paragraph 4, collapse of allies and home front:** Bulgaria, the Ottomans and Austria-Hungary sought armistices; mutiny and revolution in Germany.
6. **Judgement:** the USA tipped a balance already moving against Germany, so it was a major but not the sole cause.

## Common mistakes

- Telling the July Crisis as a story without ranking causes.
- Blaming Germany alone or excusing it completely.
- Forgetting the Austrian and Balkan dimension.
- Writing about the whole war when the question asks for the impact on one country.
- Treating the treaties as one document.

## Self-test

1. Name the members of the Triple Alliance.
2. What was the Schlieffen Plan?
3. Give two reasons the USA entered the war.
4. What was Article 231?
5. Which treaty replaced Sèvres?

**Answers:** 1. Germany, Austria-Hungary and Italy. 2. A German plan to defeat France quickly through Belgium before turning on Russia. 3. For example unrestricted submarine warfare and the Zimmermann Telegram. 4. The war guilt clause in the Treaty of Versailles. 5. The Treaty of Lausanne (1923).

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I compare Fischer and Clark?**
Say what each emphasises, what evidence supports it, and what it leaves out.

**Should I mention the Ottoman Empire?**
Yes, in the Balkans and in the post-war treaties.
