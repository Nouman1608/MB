## Section A - short questions

**1.** Which is the correct order of a complex supply chain in the syllabus? **[1]**
A supplier, wholesaler, agent, retailer, consumer   B supplier, agent, wholesaler, retailer, consumer   C manufacturer, retailer, wholesaler, consumer   D supplier, manufacturer, consumer

**2.** A car maker's suppliers of steel and tyres form which part of its supply chain? **[1]**
A downstream   B upstream   C the enterprise   D the consumer

**3.** A typhoon closes the port used by a supplier for two weeks. This is which type of risk? **[1]**
A ethical   B financial   C natural disaster   D technological

**4.** Define global supply chain management. **[2]**

**5.** State **two** benefits of global supply chain management. **[2]**

**6.** Identify **two** examples of environmental risk in a global supply chain. **[2]**

## Section B - structured questions

**7.** Explain the difference between a simple supply chain and a complex supply chain, giving an example of each. **[4]**

**8.** *BrightBike designs bicycles in Country A. Frames are made by a supplier in Country B, gears by a supplier in Country C, and BrightBike assembles the bicycles in its own factory before shipping them to retailers in 20 countries.*
(a) Identify **one** activity in each of the upstream, enterprise and downstream parts of BrightBike's supply chain. (3 marks)
(b) Explain **two** reasons why BrightBike's suppliers are important to its success. (4 marks) **[7]**

**9.** Explain how advancement in logistics and communication has made global supply chain management possible. **[4]**

**10.** *FashionNow sources clothes from factories in several countries to keep prices low. A news report has shown that one factory has unsafe buildings and pays very low wages.*
(a) Identify the category of risk shown. (1 mark)
(b) Explain **two** possible effects of this risk on FashionNow. (4 marks)
(c) Explain **one** way FashionNow could reduce this risk in future. (2 marks) **[7]**

**11.** Explain **two** political risks and **one** financial risk to a business that imports electronic components from another continent. **[6]**

**12.** *A coffee roaster buys all its beans from one region that has suffered droughts in recent years.*
Discuss the importance of risk assessment for the coffee roaster. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** B. Supplier, agent, wholesaler, retailer, consumer. **[1]**

**2.** B. Suppliers of inputs are upstream. **[1]**

**3.** C. A typhoon is a natural disaster. **[1]**

**4.** The planning and control of all stages of a supply chain that crosses national borders [1], so that goods flow from suppliers to consumers at the right cost, quality and time [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Any two: lower cost of production [1]; lower labour costs [1]; lower cost of purchasing [1]; advancement in logistics and communication [1]. **[max 2]**

**6.** Any two: pollution from a supplier's factory or from transport [1]; deforestation to grow crops or obtain timber [1]; waste or water contamination at a supplier [1]. **[max 2]**

**7.** A simple supply chain goes from supplier to manufacturer to consumer [1], for example a brand that buys cotton abroad, makes T-shirts and sells them online directly to consumers [1]. A complex supply chain has more intermediaries: supplier, agent, wholesaler, retailer, consumer [1], for example frozen prawns sold through an agent to a wholesaler abroad, who supplies many supermarkets [1]. **[4]**

**8.** (a) Upstream: frame supplier in Country B, gear supplier in Country C, or inbound transport [1]. Enterprise: design or assembly of the bicycles in BrightBike's factory [1]. Downstream: shipping to, or selling through, retailers in 20 countries [1]. (3 marks)
(b) Any two, 2 marks each: quality, because faulty frames or gears would make the bicycles unsafe and damage the brand [2]; reliability, because late frames would stop assembly and delay deliveries to 20 countries [2]; cost, because frames and gears are a large part of the cost of each bicycle, so supplier prices affect BrightBike's profit [2]; innovation, because suppliers may develop lighter frames or better gears [2]. (max 4 marks) **[7]**

**9.** Any two developments, 2 marks each: containerisation and larger ships have made moving goods between continents cheaper and more reliable [1], so components can be sourced from many countries economically [1]; tracking systems let businesses see where goods are at all times [1], so they can plan production and warn customers of delays [1]; email, videoconferencing and electronic data interchange let orders and designs be shared instantly [1], so suppliers in different time zones can be coordinated [1]. **[max 4]**

**10.** (a) Ethical risk [1]. (1 mark)
(b) Any two, 2 marks each: damage to FashionNow's reputation, so customers boycott the brand and sales fall [2]; campaign groups or the media may continue to criticise the brand, and other retailers may stop stocking it [2]; legal action or fines in some countries [2]; if the building is closed for safety reasons, supply of clothes stops [2]. (max 4 marks)
(c) Any one: audit suppliers' factories regularly [1], checking building safety and pay before and during contracts [1]; or require a supplier code of conduct [1] and stop using suppliers that break it [1]. (2 marks) **[7]**

**11.** Political, any two, 2 marks each: a new tariff on imported components raises costs [2]; an embargo or war stops supplies from the country completely [2]; political unrest closes ports or factories [2]; a change in export rules in the supplier's country delays shipments [2].
Financial, any one, 2 marks: an exchange rate change makes components more expensive in the importer's currency [2]; the supplier goes out of business, cutting off supplies [2]; transport costs rise sharply [2]. **[6]**

**12.** Up to 2 for identifying the risks, up to 2 for how assessment helps, up to 2 for a justified judgement.
Risks: drought is a natural disaster risk that could cut the bean harvest [1]; relying on one region means one event could stop all supplies [1]; poor harvests raise bean prices, a financial risk [1].
How assessment helps: it scores drought as high likelihood and high impact, so the roaster acts first on it [1]; it leads to actions such as buying from a second region or holding safety stock [1]; it allows the roaster to agree prices in advance with suppliers [1].
Judgement: for example, risk assessment is very important here because a single-region supply with repeated droughts could halt production completely, so planning ahead protects sales and reputation [2]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reversing upstream and downstream.
- Leaving the agent out of the complex supply chain.
- Naming a risk category without an example linked to the scenario.
- Treating risk assessment as a definition only, with no reasons why it matters.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between upstream and downstream?** Upstream covers the suppliers and inputs flowing into the business; downstream covers distribution of the finished product to consumers.

**Is a new tariff a political or financial risk?** It is a political risk, because it results from a government decision, although its effect is to raise costs.
