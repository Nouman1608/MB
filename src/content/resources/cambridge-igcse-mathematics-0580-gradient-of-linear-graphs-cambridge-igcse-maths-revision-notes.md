These notes condense subtopic **3.3 Gradient of linear graphs** from Topic 3 of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Core (C3.3) finds the gradient of a straight line **from a grid only**. **Extended** (E3.3) also calculates the gradient from the coordinates of two points; those items are marked *(Extended)*. Full explanations are in the [gradient of linear graphs study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-gradient-of-linear-graphs-cambridge-igcse-maths/), and exam-style questions in the [gradient practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-gradient-of-linear-graphs-cambridge-igcse-maths-practice/).

## Core facts

```
gradient = change in y / change in x      ("up over across")
```

- Measure left to right.
- Up: positive. Down: negative. Flat: 0. Vertical: not defined.
- Same straight line, same gradient everywhere.
- Steeper line, larger size of gradient: –4 is steeper than 2.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Gradient | Steepness of a line: change in y divided by change in x |
| Rise | Vertical change between two points on the line |
| Run | Horizontal change between the same two points |
| Positive gradient | Line slopes upwards from left to right |
| Negative gradient | Line slopes downwards from left to right |
| Collinear | Lying on the same straight line |
| Scale | What one small square represents on an axis |

## Reading a gradient from a grid

```
1. Pick two points exactly on grid intersections, far apart.
2. Draw the right-angled triangle under (or over) the line.
3. Convert the triangle's sides into AXIS UNITS.
4. Gradient = vertical side / horizontal side, with the sign
   from the direction of the line.
5. Simplify.
```

**Reminder example.** x-axis: 5 small squares = 1 unit. y-axis: 1 small square = 10 units. A line falls 3 squares while moving 10 squares right.

```
run  = 10 / 5   = 2 units
rise = 3 x 10   = 30 units, downwards
gradient = -30 / 2 = -15
```

## *(Extended)* Gradient from coordinates

```
m = (y2 - y1) / (x2 - x1)
```

This formula is not on the printed list in the paper.

```
(-4, 3) and (2, -9):   m = (-9 - 3) / (2 - (-4)) = -12 / 6 = -2
(1.5, 2) and (3.5, 7): m = (7 - 2) / (3.5 - 1.5) = 5 / 2  = 2.5
(-6, -1) and (-2, 1):  m = (1 - (-1)) / (-2 - (-6)) = 2 / 4 = 1/2
```

**Finding an unknown.** Set the gradient expression equal to the given value and solve.

```
(k, 4) and (3, 10) with gradient 2:
(10 - 4) / (3 - k) = 2  ->  6 = 2(3 - k)  ->  k = 0
```

**Collinear points.** Show the gradients of AB and BC are equal, and state that B is a common point.

## Quick sign check

| Picture | Expect |
|---|---|
| Line rises to the right | positive answer |
| Line falls to the right | negative answer |
| Steep line | size bigger than 1 |
| Shallow line | size less than 1 |

If your calculation disagrees with the picture, recheck it before moving on.

## Gradient with real units

When the axes measure real quantities, the gradient is a rate: y-units **per** x-unit.

| Graph | Gradient means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Cost ($) against items | price per item | gradient 4: $4 per item |
| Distance (km) against time (h) | speed | gradient 60: 60 km/h |
| Volume (litres) against time (min) | rate of flow | gradient –3: 3 litres per minute leaving |

A negative gradient means the y-quantity is decreasing. Always state the units when asked what a gradient represents.

## Exam traps

- Dividing across by up: the reciprocal of the right answer.
- Counting squares when the axes have different scales.
- Losing the minus sign for a falling line.
- *(Extended)* Subtracting in different orders on the top and bottom.
- *(Extended)* 2 – (–4) written as –2 instead of 6.
- Giving the gradient of a vertical line as 0, or of a horizontal line as "undefined".
- Leaving 6/8 instead of 3/4.

## Self-test

1. What is the gradient of a horizontal line?
2. A line falls 8 units for every 2 units to the right. State its gradient.
3. On a grid where each small square is 1 unit on both axes, a line rises 3 squares over 12 squares. Find its gradient.
4. On a grid, 1 square on the x-axis is 2 units and 1 square on the y-axis is 0.5 units. A line rises 8 squares over 4 squares. Find its gradient.
5. Which is steeper: a line with gradient –3 or a line with gradient 2?
6. *(Extended)* Find the gradient of the line through (2, 3) and (7, 13).
7. *(Extended)* Find the gradient of the line through (–5, 4) and (3, –2).
8. *(Extended)* Find the gradient of the line through (–1, 6) and (4, 6).
9. *(Extended)* The line through (1, 2) and (5, p) has gradient –1. Find p.
10. *(Extended)* Do (0, 1), (2, 7) and (5, 15) lie on one straight line?
11. Explain why a vertical line has no gradient.
12. *(Extended)* Find the gradient of the line through (0.4, 1.2) and (1.6, –0.6).

### Answers

1. **0**
2. **–4**
3. 3/12 = **1/4**
4. Rise 8 × 0.5 = 4 units, run 4 × 2 = 8 units: **1/2**
5. **–3** (size 3 is bigger than 2)
6. 10/5 = **2**
7. –6/8 = **–3/4**
8. 0/5 = **0** (same y-coordinates: horizontal)
9. (p – 2)/4 = –1, so p – 2 = –4: **p = –2**
10. Gradients 6/2 = 3 and 8/3; they differ, so **no**
11. The change in x is 0, and **you cannot divide by 0**
12. –1.8/1.2 = **–1.5**
