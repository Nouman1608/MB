These notes condense **section 12.4 The impact of IT** (Topic 12 IT in society) of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Information Technology 9626 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. A Level content, examined in Paper 3 Advanced Theory. Full explanations and worked examples are in the [impact of IT study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-ict-9626-impact-of-it/); test yourself with the [impact of IT practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-ict-9626-impact-of-it-practice/).

## The method for any area

**Technology, change, benefit, cost.** Name the IT, say what is done differently, say who gains, and say who loses or what new risk appears. Always include both sides in a "discuss" answer.

## The twelve areas: recall

| Area | Technology examples | Benefit | Drawback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sport | Wearable sensors, video review, ball tracking, streaming | Fair decisions; better training; wider audience | Slows play; cost; athlete data privacy |
| Manufacturing | Robots, CAD/CAM, automated stock control | Fast, consistent, 24-hour work; safer | Job losses; high cost; failure stops production |
| Healthcare | Health records, scanners, telemedicine, robotic surgery | Faster diagnosis; remote advice | Data theft; over-reliance; cost |
| Education | Virtual learning, interactive boards, online tests | Personalised work; access at any time | Digital divide; distraction; plagiarism |
| Banking | Online banking, ATMs, contactless, fraud detection | 24-hour service; lower cost | Branch closures; phishing; outages |
| E-business and finance | Online shops, electronic payment, trading | Global market; low overheads | Fraud; small shops lose trade |
| News | Online news, live blogs, citizen reporting | Speed and reach | False stories; loss of local papers |
| Entertainment and media | Streaming, online games, e-books | Choice on demand; low distribution cost | Piracy; game addiction; shops close |
| Family and home | Smart devices, video calls, home working | Contact with relatives; flexibility | Less face-to-face time; privacy; screen time |
| Government | Online services, open data, consultation | Faster and cheaper services | Exclusion; data security |
| Politics | Targeted adverts, social media campaigns | Reach voters; engage young people | False information; manipulation |
| Individuals and organisations | Communication, information, automation | Convenience; efficiency; new jobs | Lost jobs; information overload; security cost |

## Monitoring and surveillance

| Group | Benefits | Concerns |
|---|---|---|
| Individuals | Safer public places; quicker help | Loss of privacy; being watched; data misuse |
| Organisations | Protect property; check rules are followed; measure productivity | Staff stress and mistrust; legal duties for data |
| Security and policing | Prevent and solve crime; track suspects | Wrong identification; bias; misuse of powers; loss of trust |

Tools: CCTV, number plate recognition, facial recognition, location tracking, online tracking, staff computer monitoring.

**Fair surveillance** is proportionate, legal, open about what is collected and securely stored.

## Quick examples

**Production.** A machine makes one item every 30 seconds. In an 8-hour shift (28,800 seconds), it makes 28,800 ÷ 30 = **960 items**. A person making one every 2 minutes (120 seconds) makes 28,800 ÷ 120 = **240 items**.

**Banking.** If an online payment costs £0.20 and a branch payment costs £2.80, moving 5,000 payments online saves (2.80 − 0.20) × 5,000 = **£13,000**.

## Key words

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Automation | Using machines or software to do tasks with little human help |
| Telemedicine | Giving medical advice or treatment at a distance using communications |
| E-business | Business carried out electronically, such as online sales |
| Surveillance | Close observation of people or places |
| Digital divide | The gap between people with and without access to IT |
| Privacy | A person's right to control information about themselves |

## Exam traps

- A list of technologies with no explanation of the change they cause.
- All benefits and no drawbacks.
- "It is faster" without saying what is faster or for whom.
- General statements about "society" instead of the group named.
- Treating surveillance as always good or always bad.
- Repeating the same example across several areas.

## Self-test

1. What are the four steps for analysing an impact?
2. Give one benefit and one drawback of IT in manufacturing.
3. Give one benefit and one drawback of IT in healthcare.
4. State two effects of IT on banking.
5. State one way in which IT has changed news.
6. Give one negative effect of IT on entertainment and media.
7. Give one effect of IT on family life.
8. State one use of IT by governments and one risk.
9. How may IT be used in political campaigns?
10. Give two examples of monitoring and surveillance technology.
11. Give one benefit and one concern of surveillance for organisations.
12. A machine makes one part every 40 seconds. How many parts does it make in 8 hours?

### Answers

1. Technology, change, benefit, cost or risk.
2. Benefit: fast, consistent, 24-hour production (or safer work). Drawback: job losses (or high set-up cost).
3. Benefit: faster, more accurate diagnosis. Drawback: sensitive data at risk of theft.
4. Any two: 24-hour online service; lower costs; branch closures; phishing and fraud.
5. News is published online instantly and anyone can report events, or false stories spread quickly.
6. Piracy, which reduces income for creators (or addiction to games, or shops for physical media closing).
7. Easier contact with distant relatives, or less face-to-face time.
8. Use: online services such as tax returns. Risk: people without access are excluded, or data is hacked.
9. Targeted online adverts and social media messages aimed at groups of voters.
10. Any two: CCTV, number plate recognition, facial recognition, location tracking, online tracking.
11. Benefit: protects property or checks that rules are followed. Concern: staff stress and mistrust.
12. 8 × 3,600 = 28,800 seconds; 28,800 ÷ 40 = 720 parts.
