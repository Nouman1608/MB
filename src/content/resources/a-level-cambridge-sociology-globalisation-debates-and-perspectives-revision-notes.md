These notes condense syllabus heading 7 of Cambridge International A Level Sociology (9699),
*Key debates, concepts and perspectives* on globalisation. In short: globalisation is increasing
worldwide interconnection; sociologists disagree about whether it is real, new and beneficial;
it may make identities more alike or more diverse; and it raises questions about whether
democracy is spreading and whether nation states can still solve global problems. Paper 4 tests
this through 35-mark essays that need evaluation.

Full explanations are in the [globalisation debates study guide](/resources/a-level-cambridge-sociology-globalisation-debates-and-perspectives/).

## Syllabus coverage

9699 (2024–2026) Paper 4 Section A, heading 7: **7.1** Perspectives on globalisation; **7.2**
Globalisation and identity; **7.3** Globalisation, power and politics.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Globalisation** | Intensification of worldwide social relations linking distant places (Giddens, 1999) |
| **Time-space compression** | The world "shrinking" as communication and travel speed up (Harvey, 1989) |
| **Glocalisation** | Adapting global products or ideas to local cultures (Robertson, 1992) |
| **Global culture** | Shared media, brands, consumer goods and values circulating worldwide |
| **Transnational corporation (TNC)** | A company producing and selling in many countries |
| **Cultural convergence / homogenisation** | Cultures becoming more alike |
| **Cultural divergence** | Cultures becoming more distinct, or differences strengthened |
| **Westernisation** | Spread of Western values, consumerism and institutions |
| **Hybrid identity** | Identity blending more than one culture |
| **Cultural defence** | Protecting a culture from perceived external threat, often through religion |
| **Ethnic revitalisation** | Revival of ethnic language, tradition or pride |
| **Global social movement** | Networked campaign across borders, e.g. climate or alter-globalisation movements |

## 7.1 Who benefits? Perspectives at a glance

| Perspective | Is it real? | Who benefits? | Name to use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Globalist | Yes, sweeping | Everyone, through growth and choice | Ohmae (1990, 1995) |
| Sceptic | Exaggerated | States and regional blocs still decide | Hirst and Thompson (1996) |
| Transformationalist | Real but uneven | Mixed; states adapt | Held et al. (1999) |
| Marxist | Real — global capitalism | Transnational capitalist class | Sklair (1991); Wallerstein |
| Feminist | Real — gendered | Men and capital, via women's cheap and care labour | Enloe (1989); Hochschild's global care chains |
| Postmodernist | Real — cultural | Individuals gain choice; identities fragment | Baudrillard; Bauman |

**Problems defining globalisation:** not new (empires, colonial trade); several processes not one;
uneven access, so not truly "global".

**Dimensions:** economic (TNCs, finance), political (UN, WTO, treaties), cultural (media, brands,
migration).

## 7.2 Identity: convergence or divergence?

**Convergence arguments**
- Ritzer's McDonaldization (1993): efficiency, calculability, predictability, control spread
  worldwide.
- Global media spread Western consumerism and individualism (Westernisation).

**Divergence arguments**
- Hybridity: Bhabha (1994), Hall on diaspora.
- Cultural defence: Bruce (1996) — religion protecting national or ethnic identity.
- Ethnic revitalisation of languages and traditions.
- Huntington (1996): clash of civilisations; Barber (1995): "Jihad vs. McWorld".

**Evaluation:** glocalisation suggests a middle position — global forms with local content.
Convergence may be stronger in consumer goods than in values.

## 7.3 Power and politics

| Issue | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| Spread of liberal democracy | Fukuyama, "The End of History?" (1989); more elections and rights treaties since 1948 | Democratic reversals; democracy promotion can serve Western interests |
| Global social movements | Seattle WTO protests (1999), World Social Forum (2001), Occupy (2011), Fridays for Future (2018); Castells on networks (1996) | Fragmented aims; limited lasting policy change |
| Nation state | Sceptics: states still control borders, tax, military | Globalists: markets and TNCs constrain states; Beck: risks cross borders |
| Global problems | Transformationalists: cooperation, e.g. Kyoto (1997), Paris (2015) | Agreements weakly enforced; national interest dominates |

## Worked essay plan

*Practice question:* "Globalisation benefits everyone." Evaluate this view. [35]

1. **Introduction:** define globalisation; note debate about its extent.
2. **For:** globalist claims — growth, cheaper goods, spread of rights and technology.
3. **Against (Marxist):** transnational capitalist class gains; TNCs move production to low-wage
   economies.
4. **Against (feminist):** women's labour in export factories and global care chains.
5. **Sceptic angle:** benefits are regional and depend on state policy.
6. **Transformationalist conclusion:** benefits are real but very unequal; who benefits depends on
   class, gender and country.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- Treating "globalist" and "globalisation" as the same thing — globalist is a *perspective*.
- Assuming globalisation only means economics; 7.1 names cultural and political dimensions too.
- Writing only about homogenisation in identity questions; 7.2 expects divergence as well.
- Listing all six perspectives with no evaluation — depth beats breadth in a 35-mark essay.
- Forgetting examples from more than one society.

## Quick self-test

1. Who coined "glocalisation"?
2. State the three positions in Held et al.'s typology.
3. Give one argument that globalisation strengthens local identities.
4. Name one global social movement and its date.
5. Give one reason sceptics say the nation state remains powerful.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the safest overall conclusion?** A transformationalist judgement — globalisation is real
but uneven, and its benefits depend on position — lets you weigh every side.

**How does 7.2 differ from Paper 1 section 1.3?** Paper 1 introduces hybrid identities; 7.2 asks
you to evaluate convergence against divergence on a global scale.

**Which examples work best?** Use examples you can explain in detail — a TNC, a protest movement or
a climate agreement — rather than many vague references.
