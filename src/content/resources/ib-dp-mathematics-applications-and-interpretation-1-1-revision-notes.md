## Definitions

- A number is in the form **a × 10ᵏ** when **1 ≤ a < 10** and **k ∈ ℤ**.
- **k** is the order of magnitude: each increase of 1 in k makes the number 10 times larger.
- **Calculator or computer notation** (4.6E7, 4.6e7) is not acceptable in a final answer, as the IB guide states. Write 4.6 × 10⁷.

## Key results

| Operation | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Multiply | (a × 10ᵐ)(b × 10ⁿ) = ab × 10ᵐ⁺ⁿ | (3 × 10⁵)(5 × 10²) = 15 × 10⁷ = 1.5 × 10⁸ |
| Divide | (a × 10ᵐ) ÷ (b × 10ⁿ) = (a ÷ b) × 10ᵐ⁻ⁿ | (2 × 10⁻³) ÷ (5 × 10⁴) = 0.4 × 10⁻⁷ = 4 × 10⁻⁸ |
| Add or subtract | Match the powers first | 6.1 × 10⁵ + 3 × 10⁴ = 6.1 × 10⁵ + 0.3 × 10⁵ = 6.4 × 10⁵ |
| Power | (a × 10ᵐ)ⁿ = aⁿ × 10ᵐⁿ | (4 × 10⁻²)³ = 64 × 10⁻⁶ = 6.4 × 10⁻⁵ |
| Square root | Make the power of 10 even first | √(9 × 10⁷) = √(90 × 10⁶) = 9.49 × 10³ (3 s.f.) |

## Method in steps

### Ordinary number to a × 10ᵏ

```
1. Place the point after the first non-zero digit: that is a.
2. Count how many places the point moved: that is |k|.
3. Moved left (big number) -> k positive.
   Moved right (small number) -> k negative.
```

### Tidying a result such as 0.36 × 10⁹ or 52 × 10⁻⁴

```
a too big   (e.g. 52):   divide a by 10, add 1 to k   -> 5.2 × 10^-3
a too small (e.g. 0.36): multiply a by 10, subtract 1 from k -> 3.6 × 10^8
```

### a × 10ᵏ to an ordinary number

Move the point k places: right if k > 0, left if k < 0, filling with zeros. 3.07 × 10⁻³ = 0.003 07; 3.07 × 10⁵ = 307 000.

## Small worked reminders

```
Order these, smallest first: 4.2 × 10^-3, 9.9 × 10^-4, 1.1 × 10^-3
Compare k first: -4 < -3, so 9.9 × 10^-4 is smallest.
Then compare a for equal k: 1.1 < 4.2.
Order: 9.9 × 10^-4, 1.1 × 10^-3, 4.2 × 10^-3
```

```
"How many times larger is 6 × 10^9 than 1.5 × 10^6?"
6 × 10^9 ÷ (1.5 × 10^6) = 4 × 10^3 = 4000 times
```

## Using your GDC

- Use the exponent key (EE or ×10ˣ) so each number is entered as a single value.
- If typing powers of 10 by hand, bracket each number in a division: (6 × 10⁵) ÷ (3 × 10²).
- Scientific display mode shows results as a value and an exponent, but you must still write the answer with "× 10".
- Store unrounded answers for later parts.
- Estimate the power of 10 mentally to catch keying errors.

## Must-know distinctions

| Distinction | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Correct value, wrong form** | 23.5 × 10⁶ equals 2.35 × 10⁷, but only the second is in the required form |
| **Negative k, not negative number** | 3 × 10⁻² = 0.03 is positive; −3 × 10² = −300 is negative |
| **Ratio vs difference** | "How many times" means divide; "how much more" means subtract |
| **Exact vs 3 s.f.** | 5 × 10² is exact; 4.23 × 10⁴ is rounded and is the default when the answer is not exact |
| **Three significant figures** | In this form, a with two decimal places: 6.07 × 10⁻⁵ |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| a × 10ᵏ form | The required notation, also called standard form or scientific notation |
| Order of magnitude | The power of 10, k, describing the scale of a number |
| Exponent | The integer k |
| Significant figures | Digits from the first non-zero digit onwards; three by default in IB answers that are not exact |

## Quick self-test

1. Write 52 600 000 in the form a × 10ᵏ.
2. Write 0.000 038 1 in the form a × 10ᵏ.
3. Write 650 × 10⁻⁴ in the form a × 10ᵏ.
4. Calculate (5 × 10⁶) × (4 × 10⁻²).
5. Calculate (9 × 10⁵) ÷ (1.5 × 10⁹).
6. Calculate 7.1 × 10⁶ − 4 × 10⁵.
7. Calculate (2 × 10⁻³)³.
8. Calculate √(4.9 × 10⁷).
9. A GDC shows 3.0458E-4. Write this correct to 3 significant figures in the required form.
10. Which is larger: 8.9 × 10⁻⁵ or 1.2 × 10⁻⁴?
11. A storage device holds 2 × 10¹² bytes. Each photo uses 4 × 10⁶ bytes. How many photos fit?
12. Write 6.02 × 10²³ ÷ 1000 in the form a × 10ᵏ.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving an answer as 15 × 10⁷ or 0.4 × 10⁻⁷ when the form a × 10ᵏ was asked for.
- Copying "E" notation from the GDC.
- Adding numbers by adding their powers of 10.
- Moving k the wrong way when tidying a.
- Not converting units first, so the answer is out by a power of 10.
- Giving 2 significant figures (4.2 × 10⁴) where 3 are expected.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is 23.5 × 10⁶ marked wrong when it has the right value?**
Because a must satisfy 1 ≤ a < 10. When a question asks for the form a × 10ᵏ, the form itself is being assessed.

**How do I compare two numbers written as a × 10ᵏ?**
Compare the powers of 10 first; the larger k is the larger positive number. Only when k is equal do you compare the values of a.
