These notes condense **3.3.12.1 Condensation polymers** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International A2 content, examined in Unit 4 and able to reappear in Unit 5. For explanations and worked examples read the [condensation polymers study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-12-1/); then try the [condensation polymers practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-12-1-practice/).

## The subtopic in five lines

1. Condensation polymers form when monomers with two reactive groups join and lose water at every link.
2. Diacid + diol → polyester (–COO– links); diacid + diamine → polyamide (–CONH– links); amino acids → polyamide.
3. Named examples: Terylene (polyester), nylon 6,6 and Kevlar (polyamides).
4. You must go monomers → repeating unit, chain → repeating unit, and chain → monomers.
5. Polyamide chains are held by hydrogen bonds; polyester chains by permanent dipole–dipole forces.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Condensation polymerisation | Monomers join with loss of a small molecule (water) at each link |
| Repeating unit | Smallest section of chain that repeats; drawn in brackets with bonds through them |
| Residue | The part of a monomer left in the chain after water is lost |
| Ester link | –CO–O– |
| Amide (peptide) link | –CO–NH– |
| Polyester | Polymer with ester links, for example Terylene |
| Polyamide | Polymer with amide links, for example nylons and Kevlar |
| Diol / diamine / dicarboxylic acid | Molecule with two –OH / two –NH₂ / two –COOH groups |

## The named polymers

| Polymer | Monomers | Repeating unit | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Terylene | Benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid + ethane-1,2-diol | –[CO–C₆H₄–CO–O–CH₂CH₂–O]– | Clothing fibres, drinks bottles |
| Nylon 6,6 | Hexanedioic acid + hexane-1,6-diamine | –[CO–(CH₂)₄–CO–NH–(CH₂)₆–NH]– | Clothing, ropes, carpets |
| Kevlar | Benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid + benzene-1,4-diamine | –[CO–C₆H₄–CO–NH–C₆H₄–NH]– | Body armour, bulletproof vests |

C₆H₄ is a benzene ring bonded at positions 1 and 4.

## Nylon numbers

- Two-monomer nylon x,y: x = carbons in the diamine, y = carbons in the diacid (count the C=O carbons).
- Nylon 6,10: hexane-1,6-diamine + decanedioic acid.
- One-monomer nylon: one number, the carbons in the amino acid. Nylon 6 from 6-aminohexanoic acid gives –[NH–(CH₂)₅–CO]–.

## Drawing methods

**Monomers → repeating unit**

1. Remove OH from each –COOH, and H from each –OH or –NH₂.
2. Join C=O carbon to O or N.
3. One residue of each monomer in the brackets; bonds through both brackets.
4. Water lost: 2n H₂O for two monomers; n H₂O for one amino acid.

**Chain → repeating unit**

1. Mark every –CO–O– or –CO–NH–.
2. Find the shortest stretch that repeats.
3. Cut through linkages at both ends.

**Chain → monomers**

1. Cut every C–O (ester) or C–N (amide) bond in the linkages.
2. Add OH to each carbonyl carbon (→ –COOH).
3. Add H to each O or N (→ –OH or –NH₂).

## Mr of a repeating unit

- Two monomers: Mr(A) + Mr(B) − 36.0.
- One amino acid: Mr(amino acid) − 18.0.
- Number of units in a chain = chain Mr ÷ repeating unit Mr.
- Examples: polyester from hexanedioic acid (146.0) and ethane-1,2-diol (62.0): 172.0. Nylon 6,10: 116.0 + 202.0 − 36.0 = 282.0. Nylon 6: 131.0 − 18.0 = 113.0.

## Forces between chains

| Polymer type | Force | Groups involved |
|---|---|---|
| Polyamide | Hydrogen bonds | δ+ H of N–H on one chain and lone pair on δ− O of C=O on a neighbouring chain |
| Polyester | Permanent dipole–dipole | Polar C=O groups on neighbouring chains |
| All | Van der Waals | Whole chains |

Kevlar: flat, rigid chains (benzene rings) line up in parallel, giving many hydrogen bonds and very high strength.

Consequences to quote:

- Stronger forces between chains → more energy to separate them → higher melting point and greater strength.
- Polyamide > polyester of similar chain length, because hydrogen bonds are stronger than dipole–dipole forces.
- Melting overcomes forces between chains; covalent links within chains stay intact.

## Addition or condensation? Quick test

| Look at the repeating unit | Type |
|---|---|
| Backbone of carbon atoms only, no O or N in the chain | Addition |
| –CO–O– or –CO–NH– in the backbone | Condensation |

## Exam traps

- Two residues of one monomer inside a two-monomer repeating unit.
- –OH or –H left on the ends of the repeating unit.
- n H₂O instead of 2n H₂O for two monomers.
- Hydrogen bonds claimed for polyesters.
- C linked to C or N to O at a junction.
- "Hydrogen bonds" without naming the N–H and C=O groups on different chains.
- Confusing addition polymers (no other product) with condensation polymers.

## Self-test

Answer without looking, then check below.

1. Name the small molecule lost in condensation polymerisation of a diacid and a diol.
2. Give the linkage in a polyamide.
3. Name the two monomers of Terylene.
4. Give one typical use of Kevlar.
5. Draw the repeating unit formed from butanedioic acid, HOOC–(CH₂)₂–COOH, and ethane-1,2-diamine.
6. How many water molecules are lost per repeating unit in question 5?
7. Give the repeating unit of the polymer made from 4-aminobutanoic acid, H₂N–(CH₂)₃–COOH.
8. Name the monomers in nylon 6,10.
9. Calculate the Mr of the nylon 6,6 repeating unit (monomers 146.0 and 116.0).
10. Why can polyester chains not hydrogen bond to each other?
11. Name the strongest force between nylon 6,6 chains.
12. Why are Kevlar fibres stronger than nylon 6,6 fibres?

### Answers

1. Water.
2. Amide link, –CO–NH–.
3. Benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid and ethane-1,2-diol.
4. Bulletproof vests or body armour.
5. –[CO–(CH₂)₂–CO–NH–CH₂CH₂–NH]–.
6. Two.
7. –[NH–(CH₂)₃–CO]–.
8. Hexane-1,6-diamine and decanedioic acid.
9. 146.0 + 116.0 − 36.0 = 226.0.
10. They have no hydrogen atom bonded to oxygen or nitrogen.
11. Hydrogen bonds.
12. Kevlar's rigid, flat chains line up in parallel, so many more N–H···O=C hydrogen bonds form between neighbouring chains.
