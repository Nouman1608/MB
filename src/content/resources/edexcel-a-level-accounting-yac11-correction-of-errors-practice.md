## Section A

**1.** For each error, state whether the trial balance will still agree.

**(a)** A purchase invoice for 760 was recorded as 670 in both the purchases account and the supplier's account. **[1]**

**(b)** Bank charges of 45 were credited in the bank account only. **[1]**

**(c)** A computer costing 1,320 was debited to the computer repairs account. **[1]**

**(d)** Rent received of 900 was debited to bank and also debited to rent received. **[1]**

**2.** Prepare the journal entries, without narratives, to correct:

**(a)** error 1(a) **[2]**

**(b)** error 1(d) **[2]**

**3.** A trial balance shows debits of 214,380 and credits of 214,830. State the amount of the suspense entry and the side on which it appears. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why a complete reversal of entries does not require a suspense account entry. **[2]**

**5.** Profit for the year was first calculated as 31,600. It is then found that the owner's private expenses of 700 were debited to the wages account. Calculate the revised profit. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Pellow Garden Supplies' trial balance at 30 September 2026 did not agree, and the difference was entered in a suspense account. Profit for the year was first calculated as 27,340. The following errors were then found:

- (i) Carriage inwards of 380 was debited to carriage outwards.
- (ii) A credit sale of 1,250 was entered in the sales day book as 1,520.
- (iii) Purchases returns of 410 were debited to the trade payables control account but not credited to purchases returns.
- (iv) Insurance of 1,180 paid by cheque was credited to bank and also credited to insurance.
- (v) The discount allowed total of 560 was credited to the trade receivables control account but not debited to discount allowed.
- (vi) Repairs to a machine costing 950 were debited to the machinery account.

**(a)** Prepare journal entries to correct errors (i) to (vi). Narratives are not required. **[6]**

**(b)** Prepare the suspense account, showing the original difference. **[4]**

**(c)** Prepare a statement of revised profit for the year. **[5]**

**(d)** Calculate the overall change in gross profit caused by the corrections. **[2]**

**7.** Brannock Wholesale's trade payables control account has a credit balance of 26,480. The list of supplier balances totals 25,745. These errors are found:

- (i) The purchases day book was overcast by 600.
- (ii) A contra entry of 350 with the receivables ledger was recorded in the supplier's account but not in the control accounts.
- (iii) A supplier's balance of 1,240 was included in the list as 1,420.
- (iv) Discount received of 85 was debited twice in a supplier's account.
- (v) A debit balance of 60 on a supplier's account was included in the list as a credit balance.

**(a)** Prepare the corrected trade payables control account. **[4]**

**(b)** Prepare a statement correcting the total of the list of balances. **[3]**

**(c)** State the effect of error (i) on profit for the year. **[1]**

**8.** Explain why a suspense account should have no balance once all corrections have been made, and state what should be done if a balance remains. **[3]**

**9.** The owner of a business suggests writing off a remaining suspense balance of 140 to the statement of profit or loss rather than investigating it. Explain **two** reasons why this is not acceptable. **[4]**

**10.** A cheque payment of 2,150 to the supplier Darnley Ltd was debited to bank and credited to Darnley Ltd. Prepare the correcting journal, with a narrative, and state its effect on the trial balance and on profit. **[3]**

**11.** A trial balance has debits exceeding credits by 360. The only error is that rent of 2,480 was correctly credited to bank but debited to the rent account as 2,840.

**(a)** Show that the difference is consistent with a transposition error. **[1]**

**(b)** Prepare the correcting journal. **[2]**

**(c)** State the effect of the correction on profit. **[1]**

**(c)** Agrees (error of principle) [1]

**(d)** Disagrees: debits exceed credits by 1,800 [1]

**2. (a)** Dr Purchases 90 [1]; Cr supplier's account (trade payables) 90 [1]

**3.** Credits exceed debits by 214,830 − 214,380 = **450** [1]; the suspense entry is a **debit** of 450, on the short side [1].

**8.** Every correction involving suspense should cancel part of the original difference, so when all such errors are corrected the entries total the opening balance and the account closes [1]. A remaining balance means at least one error is still undiscovered, or a correcting journal is wrong [1]. The accountant should recheck the journals and continue investigating the books until the balance is cleared [1].

**9.** Any two reasons, two marks each:

- The balance hides one or more real errors [1]; these may affect income, expenses, assets or liabilities, so profit and the statement of financial position would still be misstated [1].
- A small net balance can conceal larger errors that partly cancel [1]; for example, errors of 1,140 and 1,000 in opposite directions leave only 140 [1].
- Writing it off breaks the double entry discipline the trial balance is meant to check [1]; future errors would be harder to find, and errors arising from fraud could go undetected [1].

**10.** Dr Darnley Ltd 4,300; Cr Bank 4,300 [1]. Narrative: correction of payment to Darnley Ltd posted on the wrong sides [1]. The trial balance was not affected because equal debit and credit entries were made, and profit is unaffected because only liability and asset accounts are involved [1].

**11. (a)** 2,840 − 2,480 = 360, and 360 ÷ 9 = 40, a whole number, as a transposition gives [1].

**(b)** Dr Suspense 360 [1]; Cr Rent 360 [1]

**(c)** Rent expense was overstated by 360, so profit **increases by 360** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the original amount instead of twice it for a double posting (Questions 1(d), 6(iv)) or a reversal (Question 10).
- Placing the opening suspense balance on the long side.
- Treating carriage inwards and carriage outwards as a profit change: both are expenses, so profit for the year is unchanged even though gross profit moves.
- Adjusting the list of balances for a day book casting error, which only affects the control account and the general ledger.
- Reversing the direction of a profit adjustment; show increases and decreases in separate columns.

## Approaching correction of errors questions

Work through the list one error at a time. Write what was done, what should have been done and the correction before you draft the journal, then decide whether suspense is involved by checking whether equal debits and credits were originally posted. Build the suspense account as you go, and check that it closes before you start the revised profit statement. For control account questions, sort every error into "control account", "list" or "both" first.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these YAC11 correction of errors questions from past papers?**
No. They are original questions on outcomes 1.2.5 to 1.2.9, written for revision. Use them alongside the official past papers.

**Should journal narratives always be included?**
Include them whenever a question asks for journal entries, unless it says narratives are not required. A short narrative stating what was corrected is enough.
