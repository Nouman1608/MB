These questions cover key question 1.3 of Cambridge IGCSE History 0470: "How was Germany unified?"

## Section A

**1.** Describe the Zollverein. **[4]**

**2.** State **two** reasons why the Frankfurt Parliament was weak. **[2]**

**3.** What happened at Olmütz in 1850? **[2]**

**4.** Describe the war over Schleswig-Holstein and its outcome, 1864-65. **[4]**

**5.** Identify **three** terms of the settlement that followed the Austro-Prussian War. **[3]**

**6.** Describe the events that led France to declare war on Prussia in July 1870. **[4]**

## Section B

**7.** Why was Germany not unified in 1848-50? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**8.** Why was Prussia able to defeat Austria so quickly in 1866? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**9.** Why did Bismarck make a lenient peace with Austria in 1866? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**10.** "Bismarck was responsible for the unification of Germany." How far do you agree? Explain your
answer. **[10]**

**11.** "The war of 1870 was caused more by French mistakes than by Bismarck's plans." How far do you agree? Explain
your answer. **[10]**

**12.** Study the source below, which is invented for practice.

*Source A (invented)*: from a speech by a Prussian liberal deputy, September 1866. "Four years ago I voted against
every thaler Herr von Bismarck asked for. I said he broke the constitution. Today I vote to forgive him, because he has
given us what our speeches never could. I have not stopped loving liberty, but I have learned that Germany must first
exist before she can be free."

Why was this speech made? Use the source and your knowledge to explain your answer. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any four, 1 mark each: a customs union of German states (1); led by Prussia (1); came into force in 1834 (1);
tariffs between member states were removed (1); a common tariff was charged on goods from outside (1); Austria was
not a member (1); most German states joined (1); it encouraged trade, railways and industry (1). **[4]**

**2.** Any two, 1 mark each: it had no army (1); it had no power to raise taxes (1); it depended on Prussia, for
example over Schleswig-Holstein (1); it was divided over whether to include Austria (1); it spent months debating (1);
the princes regained control of their own armies (1). **[2]**

**3.** 1 mark each: Prussia agreed under Austrian and Russian pressure to abandon its plan for a union of German states
(the Erfurt Union) (1); the old German Confederation was restored under Austrian leadership (1). **[2]**

**4.** Any four, 1 mark each: in 1863 the Danish king tried to bind Schleswig more closely to Denmark (1); German
nationalists were outraged (1); Prussia and Austria went to war with Denmark together in 1864 (1); Denmark was
defeated (1); Denmark gave up the duchies (1); the Convention of Gastein (1865) gave Schleswig to Prussian and
Holstein to Austrian administration (1); the arrangement left a source of dispute between Austria and Prussia (1).
**[4]**

**5.** Any three, 1 mark each: Austria was excluded from German affairs (1); the German Confederation was dissolved (1);
Venetia went to Italy (1); Austria lost no land to Prussia (1); Prussia annexed Schleswig-Holstein, Hanover,
Hesse-Kassel, Nassau and Frankfurt (1); the North German Confederation was formed (1); the southern states remained
independent (1). **[3]**

**6.** Any four, 1 mark each: the Spanish throne was offered to Prince Leopold of Hohenzollern-Sigmaringen (1); France
feared being surrounded by Hohenzollern rulers (1); France protested and the candidacy was withdrawn (1); the French
ambassador met William I at Ems and demanded a promise that it would never be renewed (1); William politely refused
(1); Bismarck edited the report of the meeting, the Ems Telegram, to make it seem insulting (1); it was published and
French opinion demanded war (1); France declared war on 19 July 1870 (1). **[4]**

**7.** Levels. Level 4 (6): explains two reasons. Level 3 (4-5): explains one reason; 5 for a developed explanation
with specific support. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes reasons. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative
content: **the Frankfurt Parliament lacked power**, because it had no army and relied on Prussia, which ignored it at
Malmö; **disagreement over Austria**, because a grossdeutsch Germany needed Austria to divide its empire, which it
refused; **Frederick William IV refused the crown** in April 1849, because he would not accept it from an elected
assembly and feared Austria; **Austria recovered** its strength and, backed by Russia, forced Prussia to give up the
Erfurt Union at Olmütz in 1850. **[6]**

**8.** Levels as for Question 7. Indicative content: **diplomatic isolation of Austria**, because Bismarck had Russian
friendship, French neutrality and an Italian alliance that forced Austria to fight on two fronts; **the Prussian
army**, reformed by Roon, was well trained and armed with the breech-loading needle gun; **railways**, because Moltke
used them to concentrate armies quickly for Königgrätz; **economic strength** from the Zollverein, coal and iron paid
for it all; Austria's forces were divided between Germany and Italy. **[6]**

**9.** Levels as for Question 7. Indicative content: **to avoid a war of revenge**, because a humiliated Austria might
ally with France; **to keep Austria as a possible future ally**; **to stop other powers intervening**, especially
France, by ending the war quickly; **his aim was limited**: to exclude Austria from Germany, not destroy it; **to
avoid annexing non-German peoples**. **[6]**

**10.** Levels. Level 5 (10): explains both sides and reaches a supported judgement on "how far". Level 4 (7-9):
explains both sides (7 for one explanation on each side, 1 more for each additional explanation). Level 3 (4-6):
explains one side. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative content. **Agree:**
he isolated Austria in 1866 and France in 1870; he chose when to fight; the lenient Treaty of Prague kept Austria
neutral in 1870; the Ems Telegram made France the aggressor and brought in the southern states. **Disagree:** the
Zollverein had already made Prussia the economic leader; Roon and Moltke built the army; William I backed him;
Austria was weakened by its defeat in 1859; Napoleon III blundered; German nationalism provided support once war came.
**Judgement:** for example, Prussia's strength made unification possible, but Bismarck's diplomacy decided when and how
it happened. **[10]**

**11.** Levels as for Question 10. Indicative content. **Agree:** France demanded a guarantee after the candidacy was
withdrawn; French press and public opinion demanded war; Napoleon III needed a success after failures such as
Luxembourg; France declared war without allies. **Disagree:** Bismarck supported the Hohenzollern candidacy; he edited
the Ems Telegram to provoke France; he wanted a war to bring the southern states into a united Germany; Prussia was
ready and France was not. **Judgement:** for example, Bismarck created the provocation, but France chose to respond
with war. **[10]**

**12.** Levels. Level 3 (5-6): explains the purpose in context, using the source and knowledge. Level 2 (3-4):
explains the purpose using the source only, or explains the context without the purpose. Level 1 (1-2): describes the
source. Indicative content: **purpose**: to justify voting for the Indemnity Bill, which approved Bismarck's spending
since 1862 after the event ("Today I vote to forgive him"); to persuade other liberals to support Bismarck now that he
had delivered unity in the north ("given us what our speeches never could"); to explain why he put national unity
before liberty. **Context:** in 1866 Prussia defeated Austria at Königgrätz and created the North German
Confederation; the Prussian parliament passed the Indemnity Bill in September 1866; the liberals split, and many
formed the National Liberal Party to support Bismarck. **[6]**
