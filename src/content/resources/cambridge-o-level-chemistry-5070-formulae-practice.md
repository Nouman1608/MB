## Section B

**7.** Zinc reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid to produce aqueous zinc chloride and hydrogen gas.

**(a)** Write the word equation. **[1]**

**(b)** Write the balanced symbol equation with state symbols. **[3]**

**(c)** Write the ionic equation for the reaction. **[2]**

**8.** Aqueous silver nitrate is added to aqueous magnesium chloride. A white precipitate of silver chloride forms.

**(a)** Write the balanced symbol equation with state symbols. **[3]**

**(b)** Identify the spectator ions. **[1]**

**(c)** Write the ionic equation. **[1]**

**9.** Aqueous sodium hydroxide is added to aqueous iron(III) chloride. A red-brown precipitate of iron(III) hydroxide forms. Write the ionic equation, with state symbols, for this reaction. **[2]**

**10.** When solid copper(II) nitrate is heated, it forms solid copper(II) oxide, nitrogen dioxide gas and oxygen gas. Deduce the balanced equation with state symbols. **[3]**

**11.** A student writes the equation for burning magnesium as:

Mg + O → MgO₂

Identify **two** errors and write the correct balanced equation. **[3]**

**12.** Ethane, C₂H₆, burns in excess oxygen to form carbon dioxide gas and water.

**(a)** State the empirical formula of ethane. **[1]**

**(b)** Write the balanced symbol equation, with state symbols, for the complete combustion of ethane. Assume the water is produced as a gas. **[3]**

**13.** Element X is a metal that forms the ion X³⁺. Write the formula of each compound of X.

**(a)** Its oxide **[1]**

**(b)** Its chloride **[1]**

**(c)** Its sulfate **[1]**

**(d)** Its hydroxide **[1]**

**14.** Aqueous calcium chloride is mixed with aqueous sodium carbonate. A white precipitate of calcium carbonate forms, and sodium chloride remains in solution.

**(a)** Write the balanced symbol equation with state symbols. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why sodium ions and chloride ions are not included in the ionic equation. **[1]**

**(c)** Write the ionic equation, with state symbols. **[2]**

**1.** The number and type of different atoms in one molecule [1]. **[1]**

**2. (a)** K₂CO₃ [1]

**(b)** Fe(OH)₂ [1]

**(c)** NH₄Cl [1]

**(d)** Al₂(SO₄)₃ [1]

**[4]**

**3. (a)** CH₂ [1]

**(b)** NO₂ [1]

**[2]**

**4.** C₂H₆O (also accept C₂H₅OH) [1]. **[1]**

**5.** SiO₂ is a giant covalent structure with no separate molecules, so the formula shows only the simplest ratio of Si to O atoms (1 : 2) [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂ [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** zinc + hydrochloric acid → zinc chloride + hydrogen [1]

**(b)** Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂: correct formulae [1]; balanced [1]; state symbols Zn(s), HCl(aq), ZnCl₂(aq), H₂(g) [1]

**(c)** Zn(s) + 2H⁺(aq) → Zn²⁺(aq) + H₂(g): correct species [1]; balanced with state symbols [1]

**[6]**

*Examiner insight:* chloride ions are spectators in (c); only zinc and the hydrogen ions change. Check that the charges balance: 2+ on each side.

**8. (a)** 2AgNO₃(aq) + MgCl₂(aq) → 2AgCl(s) + Mg(NO₃)₂(aq): correct formulae [1]; balanced [1]; state symbols [1]

**(b)** Mg²⁺ and NO₃⁻ [1]

**(c)** Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) → AgCl(s) [1]

**[5]**

*Examiner insight:* magnesium chloride supplies two chloride ions, so two silver nitrate units are needed. The ionic equation is the same whichever soluble chloride and silver salt are used.

**9.** Fe³⁺(aq) + 3OH⁻(aq) → Fe(OH)₃(s): correct species and balancing [1]; state symbols [1]. **[2]**

**10.** 2Cu(NO₃)₂(s) → 2CuO(s) + 4NO₂(g) + O₂(g): correct formulae [1]; balanced [1]; state symbols [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* balance copper and nitrogen first (2 Cu(NO₃)₂ gives 4 N, so 4NO₂), then count oxygen: 12 on the left; 2 + 8 = 10 in CuO and NO₂, so one O₂ is needed.

**11.** Oxygen should be **O₂** (diatomic) [1]; magnesium oxide is **MgO** (Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ in a 1 : 1 ratio), not MgO₂ [1]. Correct equation: **2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO** [1]. **[3]**

**12. (a)** CH₃ [1]

**(b)** 2C₂H₆(g) + 7O₂(g) → 4CO₂(g) + 6H₂O(g): correct formulae [1]; balanced [1]; state symbols [1]

**[4]**

*Examiner insight:* balancing with C₂H₆ + 3½O₂ gives a fraction; doubling every coefficient gives whole numbers. Check: 4 C, 12 H and 14 O on each side.

**13. (a)** X₂O₃ [1]

**(b)** XCl₃ [1]

**(c)** X₂(SO₄)₃ [1]

**(d)** X(OH)₃ [1]

**[4]**

*Examiner insight:* the charges are balanced in the same way as for aluminium (Al₂O₃, AlCl₃, Al₂(SO₄)₃, Al(OH)₃); the brackets in (c) and (d) are essential.

**14. (a)** CaCl₂(aq) + Na₂CO₃(aq) → CaCO₃(s) + 2NaCl(aq): correct formulae [1]; balanced [1]; state symbols [1]

**(b)** They are **spectator ions**: present in solution before and after the reaction, unchanged [1]

**(c)** Ca²⁺(aq) + CO₃²⁻(aq) → CaCO₃(s): correct species [1]; state symbols [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Missing brackets around compound ions, such as SO₄²⁻ in Al₂(SO₄)₃.
- Writing O or Cl instead of O₂ or Cl₂.
- Changing a subscript (MgO₂) to balance.
- Splitting a precipitate or a metal into ions in an ionic equation.
- Leaving out state symbols when the question asks for them.
