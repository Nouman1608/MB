Prescribed subject 1 pairs Genghis Khan (c.1200-1227) with Richard I of England (1173-1199). Paper 1 sources are set on one case study, so revise both under the guide's three headings: leadership, campaigns and impact.

Related: [Military leaders study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-world-history-dp-history-ib-dp-world-history-prescribed-subjects-1/) and [Military leaders practice questions](/resources/ib-ib-dp-world-history-dp-history-ib-dp-world-history-prescribed-subjects-1-practice/).

## Genghis Khan: timeline

| Date | Event | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| c.1160s | Temüjin born; father Yesügei later poisoned by Tatars | Early isolation; career built on alliances, not inheritance |
| 1180s-1190s | Alliances with Toghrul (Ong Khan) and Jamukha; break with Jamukha | Shows political skill and willingness to discard allies |
| 1202-1204 | Defeats Tatars, Kereit and Naiman | Steppe unified by conquest |
| 1206 | *Kurultai* proclaims him Genghis Khan | Legitimacy through assembly; new decimal army created |
| 1209-1210 | Western Xia submits | First sedentary state brought under tribute |
| 1211-1215 | War on the Jin; Jin court moves to Kaifeng; Zhongdu falls (1215) | Siege engineers recruited; huge plunder |
| 1218 | Otrar incident: caravan killed; envoy executed by the Shah | The trigger for the western campaign |
| 1219-1221 | Conquest of Khwarazm: Bukhara, Samarkand, Urgench, Merv, Nishapur | Terror as policy; destruction of cities |
| 1227 | Dies during the final campaign against Western Xia | Empire passes to his sons; Ögedei elected in 1229 |

## Richard I: timeline

| Date | Event | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1157 | Born, third son of Henry II and Eleanor of Aquitaine | Not expected to inherit England |
| 1173-1174 | Joins the revolt against Henry II | Family rivalry shaped his career |
| 1179 | Takes Taillebourg in Aquitaine | Reputation as a siege commander |
| 1189 | Allies with Philip II against Henry II; crowned in September | Comes to power through rebellion |
| 1190-1191 | Seizes Messina; conquers Cyprus | Secures bases and money for the crusade |
| July 1191 | Acre surrenders | Main military gain of the crusade |
| August 1191 | Execution of about 2,700 Muslim prisoners | Debated: military necessity or atrocity |
| September 1191 | Victory over Saladin at Arsuf | Discipline on the march; coast secured |
| 1192 | Turns back from Jerusalem; relieves Jaffa; Treaty of Jaffa | Truce, coastal strip, pilgrim access |
| 1192-1194 | Captured by Leopold V; held by Henry VI; ransom of 150,000 marks | Heavy taxation in his lands |
| 1194-1199 | Wars with Philip II; Château Gaillard built | Defence of Normandy |
| 1199 | Dies from a crossbow wound at Châlus | Angevin lands pass to John |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Kurultai* | Mongol assembly of leaders that chose or confirmed a khan |
| *Tumen* | Mongol unit of 10,000; with *mingghan* (1,000), *jaghun* (100) and *arban* (10) |
| *Keshig* | Genghis Khan's personal guard, also a training ground for commanders |
| *Yassa* | Laws and orders associated with Genghis Khan |
| *Anda* | Sworn brother; Jamukha was Temüjin's |
| Angevin Empire | The lands of Henry II and his sons: England, Normandy, Anjou, Aquitaine and more |
| Saladin tithe | Tax levied in 1188 to pay for the crusade |
| Treaty of Jaffa | 1192 truce between Richard and Saladin |
| Justiciar | Chief minister governing England in the king's absence |

## Comparison grid

| Theme | Genghis Khan | Richard I |
|---|---|---|
| Route to power | Built a following from almost nothing | Inherited, after rebelling against his father |
| Army | Mass mounted archers, decimal units | Knights, infantry and crossbowmen, often hired |
| Strength | Mobility, scale and adaptation | Tactical discipline, siege skill, logistics by sea |
| Limits | Struggled at first against walled cities | Could not hold Jerusalem without a permanent army |
| Legacy | A new empire and new trade links | A secured coast, but a costly absence from his lands |

## Source checklist

- **Who and when?** *The Secret History of the Mongols* (thirteenth century, Mongol insider); Juvaini and Rashid al-Din (Persian historians working for Mongol rulers); Ibn al-Athir (hostile Arab contemporary). For Richard: crusader narratives (Ambroise, the *Itinerarium*), Baha al-Din ibn Shaddad (Saladin's side).
- **Numbers.** Chronicle figures for armies and massacres are often inflated. Treat them as evidence of how events were perceived.
- **Purpose.** Praise poems and court histories glorify; enemy chronicles condemn. Both can still be valuable for attitudes.

## Exam traps

- **Ignoring the date limits.** Genghis Khan's case study starts around 1200; do not spend paragraphs on Kublai Khan.
- **Personal genius only.** A top-band Question 4 answer weighs the leader against wider factors: his army, resources and opponents.
- **"Richard neglected England."** This is one historians' view; present it as a debate, not a fact.
- **Confusing Acre and Arsuf.** Acre was a siege (surrender in July 1191); Arsuf a battle on the march (September 1191).
- **Using own knowledge in Question 1.** Comprehension marks come from the source only.

## Self-test

1. At which assembly, and in which year, was Temüjin proclaimed Genghis Khan?
   *The kurultai of 1206.*
2. Name the four Mongol decimal units from smallest to largest.
   *Arban, jaghun, mingghan, tumen.*
3. What event in 1218 triggered the invasion of the Khwarazmian Empire?
   *The killing of a Mongol-sponsored caravan at Otrar and the execution of an envoy.*
4. Why did the Mongols recruit engineers from conquered peoples?
   *Their cavalry could not take walled cities without siege engines.*
5. In which year did Zhongdu fall?
   *1215.*
6. During which campaign did Genghis Khan die?
   *The final campaign against Western Xia, in 1227.*
7. What were Richard I's parents' names?
   *Henry II and Eleanor of Aquitaine.*
8. Which island did Richard conquer on his way to the Holy Land?
   *Cyprus.*
9. What were the main terms of the Treaty of Jaffa?
   *A three-year truce, a Christian-held coastal strip and access for Christian pilgrims to Jerusalem.*
10. Why did Richard turn back from Jerusalem?
    *He judged that it could not be held once the crusade army left.*
11. Who captured Richard on his return, and who held him for ransom?
    *Leopold V of Austria captured him; the Emperor Henry VI held him.*
12. Give one value and one limitation of Baha al-Din ibn Shaddad as a source on Richard.
    *Value: a close adviser of Saladin with direct knowledge of Muslim decisions. Limitation: written to honour Saladin, so it presents his choices favourably.*
