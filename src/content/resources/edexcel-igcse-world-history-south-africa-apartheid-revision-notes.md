## The apartheid laws

| Law | Year | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Prohibition of Mixed Marriages Act | 1949 | Banned marriage across colour lines |
| Population Registration Act | 1950 | Classified everyone by race |
| Group Areas Act | 1950 | Separate residential areas; forced removals |
| Suppression of Communism Act | 1950 | Used to ban opponents of apartheid |
| Natives (Abolition of Passes…) Act | 1952 | Single reference book for black men |
| Separate Amenities Act | 1953 | Separate — and unequal — public facilities |
| Bantu Education Act | 1953 | State-controlled, inferior black schooling |
| Promotion of Bantu Self-Government Act | 1959 | Homelands (Bantustans) |

| Movement | Leaders | Methods | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| ANC (1950s) | Mandela, Sisulu, Tambo, Luthuli | Defiance Campaign, Freedom Charter | Treason Trial; banned 1960 |
| PAC | Sobukwe | Anti-pass protest | Sharpeville; banned 1960 |

## Worked example: two effects

*Explain two effects of the Sharpeville Massacre (1960).*

1. **The banning of the ANC and PAC:** the government declared a state of emergency and outlawed both
   organisations, forcing them underground and into exile.
2. **A turn to armed struggle and world condemnation:** ANC leaders formed Umkhonto we Sizwe (1961),
   and Sharpeville turned international opinion against South Africa, which left the Commonwealth in
   1961.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- **Saying segregation began in 1948.** It existed before; apartheid systematised it.
- **Treating Botha as a pure hardliner.** He reformed some laws, but kept white political power and
  used force.
- **Overlooking internal resistance.** Sanctions mattered, but township unrest and the UDF made the
  country ungovernable.
- **Mixing up Sharpeville (1960) and Soweto (1976).** Different causes: passes v Afrikaans teaching.
- **Forgetting the violence of 1990–94.** Negotiations were not smooth; mention Hani and Inkatha.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why did Botha introduce reforms?**
Economic pressure, the need for skilled black labour,
international isolation and the hope of winning Coloured and Indian support.

**Why did de Klerk free Mandela?**
Sanctions, economic decline, the failure of repression and the end
of the Cold War made negotiation the only realistic option.

**What did the 1994 election achieve?**
It gave all adults the vote and brought the ANC to power with
Mandela as President.
