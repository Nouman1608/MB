These notes condense section 21.2, Biotechnology, of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Statements 21.2.1 to 21.2.4 are Core. Statements 21.2.5 to 21.2.7 are Supplement ("Extended only") and appear only on Papers 2 and 4. For explanations and worked data, read the [section 21.2 study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-biotechnology-cambridge-igcse-biology/); to practise, use the [section 21.2 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-biotechnology-cambridge-igcse-biology-practice/).

## Tier map

- **Core:** yeast in biofuels (21.2.1), yeast in bread (21.2.2), pectinase (21.2.3), biological washing powders, including investigating them (21.2.4).
- **Extended only:** lactase (21.2.5), fermenters and their products (21.2.6), fermenter conditions (21.2.7).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Anaerobic respiration (yeast) | Release of energy from glucose without oxygen, producing alcohol and carbon dioxide |
| Biofuel | Fuel made from living material; here, ethanol from yeast |
| Pectin | Substance in plant cell walls that helps hold cells together |
| Pectinase | Enzyme that breaks down pectin |
| Protease | Enzyme that breaks down proteins into amino acids |
| Lipase | Enzyme that breaks down fats and oils into fatty acids and glycerol |
| Amylase | Enzyme that breaks down starch into simpler sugars |
| Lactase | Enzyme that breaks down lactose into glucose and galactose |
| Fermenter | Vessel for growing microorganisms in controlled conditions on a large scale |
| Denatured | Enzyme whose active site has changed shape so it no longer works |

## Yeast: which product matters?

Word equation: **glucose → alcohol + carbon dioxide**.

| Use | Conditions | Useful product | Fate of the other product |
|---|---|---|---|
| Biofuel (21.2.1) | Sugar from plant material; tanks kept without oxygen | Ethanol, separated and used as fuel | Carbon dioxide released |
| Bread (21.2.2) | Dough with sugar, kept warm | Carbon dioxide bubbles, trapped so the dough rises | Alcohol evaporates during baking |

- Oxygen present → aerobic respiration → carbon dioxide and water, little or no ethanol.
- Warm dough → enzymes nearer optimum → faster gas production.
- Too hot → enzymes denatured → no more gas. Baking kills the yeast.

## Pectinase (21.2.3)

- Breaks down pectin in cell walls → more juice released (higher yield) and clearer juice.
- Investigation variables: enzyme concentration or temperature (independent); volume of juice after a fixed time (dependent); mass and type of fruit, volume of enzyme, temperature, time (controlled).
- Control: water or boiled pectinase instead of active enzyme.

## Biological washing powders (21.2.4)

| Enzyme | Stain type | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Protease | Protein | Blood, egg, grass |
| Lipase | Fat and oil | Butter, oil, gravy |
| Amylase | Starch | Pasta, potato, custard |

- Products are small and soluble, so they dissolve and rinse away.
- Benefit: effective at lower temperatures → less energy used; less damage to delicate fabrics.
- High temperature → enzymes denatured → no better than non-biological powder.
- Investigation: same size of cloth, same stain, same volume and concentration of solution; measure time for stain to disappear; rate = 1 ÷ time; control with boiled or non-biological powder; repeat and average.

## Lactase (Extended only, 21.2.5)

- Lactase breaks lactose → glucose + galactose.
- Some people do not produce enough lactase, so they cannot digest lactose.
- Lactose-free milk lets these people drink milk.

## Fermenters (Extended only, 21.2.6 and 21.2.7)

| Product | Organism |
|---|---|
| Insulin | GM bacteria with the human insulin gene |
| Penicillin | The fungus *Penicillium* |
| Mycoprotein | The fungus *Fusarium* |

| Condition | How controlled | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | Cold-water jacket; probe | Respiration releases heat; avoid denaturing; keep growth fast |
| pH | Probe; add acid or alkali | Enzymes' optimum pH; carbon dioxide can lower pH |
| Oxygen | Sterile air bubbled in; paddles mix | Aerobic respiration releases energy for growth |
| Nutrient supply | Add glucose and a nitrogen source | Energy from glucose; nitrogen for amino acids and proteins |
| Waste products | Gas outlet; wastes removed | Wastes can change pH or be toxic |

Sterilise the vessel and nutrients first, so no other microorganism competes, contaminates the product or causes harm.

## Calculations to rehearse

- **Mean rate** = change ÷ time. Dough rising 30 cm³ in 50 minutes = 0.6 cm³ per minute.
- **Percentage increase** = (new − original) ÷ original × 100. Juice rising from 16 cm³ to 20 cm³ = 4 ÷ 16 × 100 = 25%.
- **Rate from time** = 1 ÷ time. A stain removed in 8 minutes gives 0.125 per minute.
- **Mean of repeats:** leave out anomalous results first.

## Exam traps

- Bread: the useful product is **carbon dioxide**, not alcohol.
- Biofuel: the tanks are kept **without oxygen**.
- Pectinase acts on **pectin in cell walls**, not on membranes.
- Lactase gives **glucose and galactose**: name both.
- Penicillin and mycoprotein are **fungal**; insulin is from **GM bacteria**.
- Fermenters are mainly **cooled**, not heated.
- A powder with only protease will not speed removal of a fat stain.
- When describing a trend, **quote numbers** from the table, including the peak.
- "The enzyme is killed" earns nothing: write **denatured**, active site changes shape.

## Quick self-test

1. Write the word equation for anaerobic respiration in yeast.
2. Which product of yeast respiration makes bread rise?
3. Why are biofuel tanks kept free of oxygen?
4. What happens to the alcohol produced in bread dough?
5. State two effects of adding pectinase to crushed fruit.
6. Suggest a control for a pectinase investigation.
7. Which enzyme in a biological powder removes an egg stain?
8. Give one environmental advantage of biological washing powders.
9. Name the two products of lactose breakdown. (Extended)
10. Name the organism used to produce mycoprotein. (Extended)
11. Why is sterile air bubbled into a fermenter? (Extended)
12. Juice volume rose from 15 cm³ (no enzyme) to 24 cm³ (pectinase). Calculate the percentage increase.

### Answers

1. glucose → alcohol + carbon dioxide.
2. Carbon dioxide.
3. So that yeast respires anaerobically, producing ethanol (with oxygen it would respire aerobically and make little ethanol).
4. It evaporates during baking.
5. More juice is released; the juice is clearer.
6. The same mass of fruit with water, or boiled pectinase, instead of active enzyme.
7. Protease.
8. Washing at lower temperatures uses less energy.
9. Glucose and galactose.
10. *Fusarium* (a fungus).
11. To supply oxygen for aerobic respiration, which releases energy for growth; sterile so no contaminating microorganisms enter.
12. (24 − 15) ÷ 15 × 100 = **60%**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Swapping the useful products of bread-making and biofuel production.
- Describing a graph trend without data or without the optimum.
- Leaving out a control, or a reason for repeating.
- Naming only one product of lactase.
- Giving the wrong organism for a fermenter product.
- Writing "heat the fermenter".
