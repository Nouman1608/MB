These questions practise Criterion D (applying mathematics in real-life contexts) from the IB *Mathematics guide for the MYP* (first assessment 2022). Section A has short everyday problems. Section B has longer situations where you must also justify the accuracy of your answer and whether it makes sense. All prices, rates and measurements are invented for practice. The marks are Marlbridge practice tariffs. Read the [Criterion D study guide](/resources/ib-ib-myp-mathematics-myp-mathematics-ib-myp-mathematics-assessment-criteria-d-applying-mathematics-in-real-life-contexts/) and the [Criterion D revision notes](/resources/ib-ib-myp-mathematics-myp-mathematics-ib-myp-mathematics-assessment-criteria-d-applying-mathematics-in-real-life-contexts-revision-notes/) first.

## Questions

### Section A: everyday problems

**1.** A recipe for 4 people uses 300 g of pasta. How much pasta is needed for 10 people? **[2]**

**2.** A school trip has 175 students. Each bus holds 52 students. How many buses are needed? **[2]**

**3.** A train travels 84 km in 48 minutes. Find its average speed in km/h. **[2]**

**4.** A phone costs 480. A payment plan asks for a 20% deposit and then 12 monthly payments of 34. How much more does the plan cost than paying 480 at once? **[3]**

**5.** A 750 ml bottle of juice costs 2.40. A 1.2 litre bottle costs 3.60. Which is better value? Show your reasoning. **[3]**

### Section B: longer situations

**6.** A household compares two electricity tariffs for a 30-day month. Tariff X has a standing charge of 0.45 per day and costs 0.28 per kWh. Tariff Y has a standing charge of 0.25 per day and costs 0.31 per kWh. The household expects to use 350 kWh.
**(a)** Find the monthly cost on each tariff. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the monthly usage for which the two tariffs cost the same. **[2]**
**(c)** Recommend a tariff and explain when your recommendation would change. **[2]**

**7.** A rectangular patio measures 3.4 m by 2.6 m. It is to be covered with square tiles of side 0.4 m, sold in packs of 10. Tiles can be cut, but a cut piece cannot be reused elsewhere.
**(a)** Find the number of tiles needed by working out how many fit along each side. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the number of packs to buy. **[1]**
**(c)** A friend divides the patio area by the tile area and gets 56 tiles. Explain why this underestimates. **[2]**

**8.** 2500 is saved in an account paying 3.2% compound interest per year.
**(a)** Find the amount after 5 years, to the nearest cent. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the number of complete years until the amount first exceeds 3000. **[2]**
**(c)** State one assumption the calculation makes. **[1]**

**9.** To find the height of a tree, a student stands 15 m from its base on level ground. The angle of elevation to the top is 38°, measured to the nearest degree, from an eye height of 1.6 m.
**(a)** Calculate the height of the tree. **[3]**
**(b)** By considering the accuracy of the angle, justify a suitable degree of accuracy for your answer. **[3]**

**10.** A car uses 30 litres of fuel to travel 420 km. Fuel costs 1.65 per litre.
**(a)** Estimate the fuel cost of a 650 km journey. **[3]**
**(b)** Give one reason why the actual cost might differ from your estimate. **[1]**

**11.** A seedling is 8 cm tall and grows 2.5 cm a week. A student models its height as h = 8 + 2.5w, where w is the number of weeks.
**(a)** Use the model to predict the height after 52 weeks. **[1]**
**(b)** Discuss whether this prediction makes sense, and suggest how the model's use should be limited. **[3]**

**12.** A runner completes 5 km in 26 minutes 30 seconds.
**(a)** Assuming the same pace, predict the time for 10 km. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain whether the real 10 km time is likely to be shorter or longer than this prediction. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Pasta per person = 300 ÷ 4 = 75 g [1]. For 10 people: **750 g** [1] **[2]**

**2.** 175 ÷ 52 = 3.37 [1]. Three buses would leave students behind, so **4 buses** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The rounding direction comes from the context. Writing "3.37 buses" or "3 buses" does not make sense for the situation.

**3.** 48 minutes = 0.8 hours [1]. Speed = 84 ÷ 0.8 = **105 km/h** [1] **[2]**

**4.** Deposit = 0.2 × 480 = 96 [1]. Monthly total = 12 × 34 = 408 [1]. Plan total = 504, so it costs **24 more** [1] **[3]**

**5.** 750 ml bottle: 2.40 ÷ 7.5 = 0.32 per 100 ml [1]. 1.2 litre bottle: 3.60 ÷ 12 = 0.30 per 100 ml [1]. The **1.2 litre bottle** is better value, as long as all of it will be used [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Comparing on the same basis (per 100 ml) is the strategy. The final condition shows the answer was checked against the situation.

**6.** **(a)** X: 30 × 0.45 + 350 × 0.28 = 13.50 + 98.00 = **111.50** [1]. Y: 30 × 0.25 + 350 × 0.31 = 7.50 + 108.50 = **116.00** [1]
**(b)** 13.50 + 0.28k = 7.50 + 0.31k [1], so 6 = 0.03k and **k = 200 kWh** [1]
**(c)** Tariff X, which is 4.50 cheaper at 350 kWh [1]; Tariff Y would be cheaper only if usage fell below 200 kWh a month [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The break-even value turns a single comparison into a justified recommendation that holds even if the usage estimate is a little wrong.

**7.** **(a)** 3.4 ÷ 0.4 = 8.5, so 9 tiles along the length [1]. 2.6 ÷ 0.4 = 6.5, so 7 tiles along the width [1]. 9 × 7 = **63 tiles** [1]
**(b)** 63 ÷ 10 = 6.3, so **7 packs** [1]
**(c)** 8.84 ÷ 0.16 = 55.25 assumes the cut-off half tiles can fill gaps elsewhere [1], but cut pieces cannot be reused, so each part tile along the edges needs a whole tile [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Two strategies give different answers here; explaining why one fits the situation better is exactly what strand v asks for.

**8.** **(a)** 2500 × 1.032⁵ [1] = **2926.43** [1]
**(b)** After 5 years the amount is 2926.43, below 3000 [1]; after 6 years it is 3020.08, so **6 years** [1]
**(c)** For example: the interest rate stays at 3.2% for every year, or no money is added or withdrawn [1] **[5]**

**9.** **(a)** Height above eye level = 15 tan 38° [1] = 11.72 m [1]. Tree height = 11.72 + 1.6 = **13.3 m** [1]
**(b)** The angle could be between 37.5° and 38.5° [1]. 15 tan 37.5° + 1.6 = 13.1 m and 15 tan 38.5° + 1.6 = 13.5 m [1]. The height lies between about 13.1 m and 13.5 m, so it is reasonable to give it to 2 significant figures, **13 m**; 3 significant figures would claim more accuracy than the angle allows [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "To the nearest degree" means up to half a degree either way. Showing the range is the evidence that justifies the chosen accuracy.

**10.** **(a)** Fuel use = 420 ÷ 30 = 14 km per litre [1]. Fuel for 650 km = 650 ÷ 14 = 46.43 litres [1]. Cost = 46.43 × 1.65 = **76.61** [1]
**(b)** For example: fuel use changes with speed, traffic or load, so 14 km per litre may not hold for the whole journey [1] **[4]**

**11.** **(a)** h = 8 + 2.5 × 52 = **138 cm** [1]
**(b)** The model assumes the plant grows at the same rate for a whole year [1]. Real growth usually slows as a plant matures and depends on season, light and water, so 138 cm may be far too large for many plants [1]. The model should only be used over the range of weeks for which growth was measured to be steady [1] **[4]**

**12.** **(a)** 2 × 26 min 30 s = **53 minutes** [1]
**(b)** Likely **longer** [1], because a runner usually cannot hold the same pace over double the distance as fatigue builds [1] **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Rounding a count of buses, packs or tins down.
- Comparing prices on different bases.
- Using the area method for tiles when cut pieces cannot be reused.
- Giving an answer to more significant figures than the measurements allow.
- Writing "the answer makes sense" without referring to the situation.
- Extending a linear model far beyond the data it was built on.
- Forgetting to convert minutes to hours before finding a speed.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why do these questions ask for assumptions?**
Real situations need simplifying before mathematics can be applied. Stating assumptions, such as a fixed interest rate, shows you identified the relevant elements of the situation and lets a reader judge your answer.

**How many significant figures should I give in a real-life answer?**
No more than the data supports. Work out the range of possible answers from the accuracy of the inputs, as in the tree question, and round to a value that the whole range agrees on.
