This guide covers sub-topic **6.2 Leaf structure** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. All three statements are Core, so the content can be tested on every paper, and "observe, record and measure images of biological specimens" is one of the practical-paper skills, so leaf sections also appear on Paper 5 and Paper 6.

The [plant nutrition study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-plant-nutrition/) gives a table of leaf tissues and one worked answer on the palisade layer. This page goes further: how to recognise each structure in a real image, how to tell which way up a section is, the routes taken by light, gases and water, and how to build a full-mark adaptation answer. Condense it with the [leaf structure revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-leaf-structure-revision-notes/) and practise with the [leaf structure practice questions](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-leaf-structure-subtopic-practice/).

## What 6.2 asks you to do

| Statement | Command word | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 6.2.1 | State, explain | Most leaves have a large surface area and are thin; explain how these features are adaptations for photosynthesis |
| 6.2.2 | Identify | In diagrams and images of a dicotyledonous leaf: chloroplasts, cuticle, guard cells and stomata, upper and lower epidermis, palisade mesophyll, spongy mesophyll, air spaces, vascular bundles, xylem and phloem |
| 6.2.3 | Explain | How each structure in 6.2.2 adapts leaves for photosynthesis |

"Identify" means name, select or recognise. "Explain" means give the reason, so every feature must be linked to light, carbon dioxide, water or the products.

## Leaf shape (6.2.1)

A leaf is the plant's photosynthesis organ, and its shape solves two problems at once.

- **Large surface area.** More light falls on the leaf, so more is absorbed by chlorophyll. There is also more surface through which carbon dioxide can diffuse in.
- **Thin.** Carbon dioxide entering through the stomata has only a short distance to diffuse to the mesophyll cells, so it arrives quickly. Light can also reach the cells in the lower layers.

A full answer pairs each feature with its effect: "thin, so there is a short diffusion distance for carbon dioxide" scores, while "thin, so it is efficient" does not.

## Recognising structures in an image (6.2.2)

Photographs of leaf sections rarely look like textbook diagrams. Use these recognition clues.

| Structure | What it looks like in a section |
|---|---|
| Cuticle | A thin, clear or shiny line on the outer surface with no cells in it |
| Upper epidermis | One layer of flat, closely fitted cells with no chloroplasts |
| Palisade mesophyll | Tall, column-shaped cells standing upright in a tight row, crowded with green chloroplasts |
| Spongy mesophyll | Rounded, irregular cells with fewer chloroplasts and many gaps between them |
| Air spaces | The empty-looking gaps between spongy cells, linked to each other and to the stomata |
| Vascular bundle | A distinct group of cells in the midrib or a vein, often in the middle of the leaf |
| Xylem | Wide, thick-walled, empty-looking cells on the upper side of the bundle |
| Phloem | Smaller, thinner-walled cells on the lower side of the bundle |
| Lower epidermis | One layer of flat cells, broken at intervals by pores |
| Guard cells and stoma | A pair of curved cells, each containing chloroplasts, with a pore (the stoma) between them |
| Chloroplasts | Small green oval bodies inside mesophyll cells and guard cells |

### Which way up is the section?

Look for the palisade layer. It lies just under the **upper** epidermis, so the side with tightly packed upright cells is the top. Confirm with two more clues: most stomata are in the **lower** epidermis, and in the vascular bundle the xylem faces the upper surface. If a question shows the section upside down, these clues still work.

### Seen from above

A peel of the lower epidermis viewed from above shows wavy-edged epidermal cells without chloroplasts and many pairs of guard cells. Each pair surrounds one stoma. The guard cells are the only cells of the epidermis with chloroplasts, which helps you pick them out. Remember the difference in terms: the **stoma** is the hole; the **guard cells** are the cells that open and close it.

## Following the routes through a leaf (6.2.3)

The adaptations make sense if you follow each thing the leaf needs, or makes, along its path.

**Light:** cuticle (transparent) → upper epidermis (transparent, no chloroplasts) → palisade mesophyll, where most of it is absorbed by the many chloroplasts → spongy mesophyll, which absorbs some of what is left.

**Carbon dioxide:** air outside → stoma in the lower epidermis → air spaces in the spongy mesophyll → dissolves in the water on the surface of mesophyll cells → diffuses into the cells and into the chloroplasts. It moves down a concentration gradient because photosynthesis uses it up inside the leaf.

**Water:** xylem in the vascular bundle → mesophyll cells, where it is a raw material for photosynthesis.

**Oxygen:** made in the chloroplasts → diffuses out of the cells into the air spaces → out through the stomata.

**Sucrose:** made from glucose in mesophyll cells → loaded into the phloem → carried to other parts of the plant.

### Worked example: tracing light

A question asks: "Name, in order, the structures light passes through before it reaches a chloroplast in a spongy mesophyll cell." **[3]**

- Cuticle, then upper epidermis [1]
- then palisade mesophyll [1]
- then into the spongy mesophyll cell [1]

Leaving out the cuticle is the most common slip: it is not made of cells, but light still passes through it.

## Building a full adaptation answer

Every mark needs three things in one sentence: the **structure**, its **feature**, and **why the feature helps photosynthesis**.

| Structure | Feature | Why it helps photosynthesis |
|---|---|---|
| Cuticle | waxy and transparent | lets light through; reduces water loss so the leaf keeps water for photosynthesis |
| Upper epidermis | transparent, no chloroplasts | light passes straight to the palisade cells |
| Palisade mesophyll | many chloroplasts; near upper surface; tall and closely packed | absorbs the most light |
| Spongy mesophyll | loosely arranged with air spaces | gases diffuse easily to and from many cells |
| Air spaces | linked to each other and to stomata | large internal surface for carbon dioxide to diffuse into cells |
| Stomata and guard cells | pores that can open and close | let carbon dioxide in and oxygen out |
| Xylem | in the veins, close to the mesophyll | supplies water, a raw material |
| Phloem | in the veins | carries sucrose away to the rest of the plant |
| Chloroplasts | contain chlorophyll | absorb light and transfer energy for making carbohydrate |

### Annotated model answer

**Explain how the spongy mesophyll and stomata are adapted for photosynthesis.** **[4]**

> The spongy mesophyll cells are loosely packed, so there are many air spaces between them [1]. The air spaces give a large surface area of moist cell surface for carbon dioxide to diffuse into the cells [1]. Stomata in the lower epidermis allow carbon dioxide to diffuse into the leaf from the air [1], and oxygen produced in photosynthesis to diffuse out [1].

Why it scores: each sentence names a structure, states the feature and links it to a gas used or made in photosynthesis. A sentence such as "the spongy layer has air spaces for gas exchange" would earn at most one mark, because it never says which gas or why.

## Common mistakes

- Calling the cuticle a layer of cells. It is a non-cellular waxy layer made by the epidermis.
- Writing that guard cells are the pores. The pore is the stoma.
- Placing the air spaces in the palisade layer. They are mainly in the spongy mesophyll.
- Swapping xylem and phloem inside the vascular bundle: xylem faces the upper surface.
- Saying stomata "absorb carbon dioxide". Carbon dioxide diffuses through them; it is absorbed by the mesophyll cells.
- Saying the epidermis "protects the leaf" when the question asks about photosynthesis. Link it to light passing through.
- Writing "thin for efficiency" or "large surface area for more photosynthesis" with no light or diffusion link.

## Exam technique for 6.2

- **Identify** on a diagram: use the exact syllabus names, such as "palisade mesophyll", not "palisade layer cells" or "green cells".
- **Explain** an adaptation: structure + feature + link to photosynthesis, one mark per complete link.
- **Describe** a route: put the structures in order and use the words "diffuses" and "concentration gradient" for gases.
- Images on the practical papers may ask for a drawing: use clear single lines, no shading, and label with ruled lines.
