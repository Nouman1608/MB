**Syllabus:** Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620, 2026–2028, subtopic 9.3 Alloys and their properties.

## Definition

**Alloy:** a **mixture** of a **metal** with **other elements**.

- Mixture, not compound: no fixed ratio, no chemical formula, composition can vary.
- The other elements can be metals **or non-metals** (carbon in stainless steel).

## The two named alloys

| Alloy | Made of | Key properties | Named use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brass | copper + zinc | harder than copper | (none named in the syllabus) |
| Stainless steel | iron + chromium, nickel, carbon | hard; strong; resists rusting | cutlery |

**Memory hook:** **B**rass has **no iron**; **S**tainless **S**teel starts with iron.

## Properties compared with the pure metal

- Alloys can be **harder** and **stronger** than the pure metal.
- This makes them **more useful** for many jobs.
- Stainless steel also **resists rusting**, unlike pure iron.

**Cutlery answer (2 marks):** stainless steel is **hard** (keeps its shape and edge) and **resists rusting** (in contact with water and food).

## Pure metal or alloy: side by side

| Feature | Pure metal | Alloy |
|---|---|---|
| Substance type | element | mixture |
| Sizes of atoms | all the same | two or more different sizes |
| Arrangement | regular layers | layers disrupted |
| Composition | fixed (one element) | can vary |
| Hardness and strength | lower | higher |

## Suggesting a use for an unfamiliar alloy

Questions may give data for an alloy you have not met. Use this frame:

1. Pick the property in the data that matters for the job (hardness, strength, resistance to corrosion).
2. Compare it with the pure metal's value in the data.
3. Write "[alloy] is suitable for [job] because it is [property], so [consequence]".

Example: an alloy that is harder than its pure metal and does not corrode in damp air suits outdoor fittings, because it will not dent easily or weaken by corroding.

## Identifying alloys in diagrams

| Diagram shows | It represents |
|---|---|
| All circles the same size, regular rows | pure metal |
| Mostly one size of circle, with a few circles of a **different size** scattered among them, rows distorted | **alloy** |
| Two kinds of particle in a strict alternating, repeating pattern | usually a compound (for example an ionic lattice) |
| Two kinds of particle in separate groups or separate regions | mixture of separate substances |

**Checklist for "identify the alloy":** different-sized atoms? mixed within the metal? no fixed pattern? Then it is the alloy.

## Why alloys are harder *(Extended)*

Three marking points, in order:

1. Pure metal: atoms are all the **same size**, in **regular layers**, which can **slide over each other**.
2. Alloy: contains atoms of a **different size**.
3. These **disrupt the layers**, so the layers can **no longer slide over each other**; more force is needed to change the shape.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Alloy | a mixture of a metal with other elements |
| Mixture | two or more substances not chemically combined |
| Compound | two or more elements chemically combined in a fixed ratio |
| Brass | alloy of copper and zinc |
| Stainless steel | alloy of iron with chromium, nickel and carbon |
| Hard | resists scratching and denting |
| Strong | can withstand a large force without breaking |
| Layers *(Extended)* | regular rows of atoms in a metal that can slide over each other |

## Exam traps

- **Alloy ≠ compound.** Never give an alloy a formula.
- **"Mixture of metals" loses marks** when the alloy contains carbon. Say "a metal with other elements".
- **Brass contains no iron;** stainless steel contains no copper or zinc.
- **Cutlery needs two properties:** hardness **and** resistance to rusting.
- *(Extended)* **Layers, not atoms.** It is the layers that can no longer slide.
- *(Extended)* **Different size is the key word.** "Different atoms" alone may not earn the mark.
- *(Extended)* **Not stronger bonds.** The explanation is about disrupted layers, not stronger bonding.

## Self-test

1. Define an alloy.
2. Name the elements in brass.
3. Name the main metal in stainless steel and **three** other elements it contains.
4. State **two** ways in which an alloy's properties can differ from those of the pure metal.
5. Why is stainless steel used for cutlery?
6. Why is an alloy classed as a mixture rather than a compound?
7. Which non-metal is named in the syllabus composition of stainless steel?
8. A diagram shows circles of one size in regular rows, with a few larger circles scattered among them. What does it represent?
9. A diagram shows two kinds of circle alternating strictly throughout. Is this likely to be an alloy? Explain.
10. *(Extended)* Explain why pure metals are malleable, in terms of structure.
11. *(Extended)* Explain why stainless steel is harder than pure iron.
12. A 200 g brass sample is 70 % copper by mass. Calculate the mass of zinc.

**Answers:**

1. A mixture of a metal with other elements.
2. Copper and zinc.
3. Iron; chromium, nickel and carbon.
4. Harder; stronger (also more useful; stainless steel resists rusting).
5. It is hard and resists rusting.
6. The elements are not chemically combined in a fixed ratio; the composition can vary.
7. Carbon.
8. An alloy.
9. No: a strict alternating pattern shows a fixed ratio, which usually represents a compound.
10. The atoms are the same size and arranged in layers, which can slide over each other when a force is applied.
11. The atoms of chromium, nickel and carbon are a different size from iron atoms; they disrupt the layers so the layers can no longer slide over each other.
12. Copper = 0.70 × 200 = 140 g, so zinc = 200 − 140 = 60 g.
