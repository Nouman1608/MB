These notes condense sub-topic **3.16.3 Benefits and risks of artificial intelligence** of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Computer Science (9645), Version 1.1 (International A-level content in topic 3.16, examined in Unit 4). For worked examples, see the [benefits and risks study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-computer-science-9645-benefits-risks-of-artificial-intelligence/), then practise with the [benefits and risks practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-computer-science-9645-benefits-risks-of-artificial-intelligence-practice/).

## What the specification requires

- Understand the **benefits** associated with the use of artificial intelligence.
- Understand the **risks** associated with the use of artificial intelligence.

## Benefits

| Benefit | Why it applies | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Improved and more consistent decision making | The same learned rules apply to every case; no tiredness or mood | More accurate analysis of medical data |
| Ability to analyse very large data sets quickly | Processing speed far beyond human reviewers | 1,000,000 records in about 33 minutes at 500 per second, against 25,000 hours (12.5 years) at 40 per hour |
| Continuous availability | No breaks or sleep | 168 hours a week against 40 for one person, 4.2 times as long |
| Lower cost of operation | Cheaper per case | £4.00 against £0.05 per case saves £395,000 over 100,000 cases |
| Available to more people and in more areas | Expertise can be copied and delivered by device | Screening or tutoring where specialists are scarce |

## Risks

| Risk | Cause or mechanism | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Elimination of jobs; social impact | AI does tasks people did | 60% of calls handled by AI removes the work of 120 of 200 call-centre staff |
| Bias in decision making | Biased or unrepresentative training data (gender, race and so on) | Hiring data: 50% of men hired, 20% of women; a copy of the decisions repeats the gap |
| False information | Incorrect training data; output follows patterns, not checked facts | 2% wrong on 50,000 answers a day is 1,000 wrong answers a day |
| Plagiarism | Generative AI output presented as a person's own work, or copying others' material | Submitted essay written by a system |
| Use in surveillance | AI can watch and identify people on a huge scale | 2,000 cameras give 48,000 hours of footage a day, which only automation can review |
| Risk to human existence | Possible future superintelligent systems pursuing goals that conflict with human interests | A long-term concern; general intelligence is still research |

## Worked reminders

- **Consistency:** specialists right on 92% of 500 scans is 460; an AI at 96% is 480, 20 more.
- **Bias ratio:** 20% / 50% = 0.4, so the rate for women is 40% of the rate for men.
- **Job loss:** 200 × 0.6 = 120 jobs' work no longer needed; 80 remain.

## Writing a balanced answer

1. Name the scenario.
2. At least two benefits, each with a reason.
3. At least two risks, each with a reason.
4. A short conclusion that weighs them.

Useful structure: "A benefit is ... because ... . However, a risk is ... because ... . Overall ... ."

## Making points stronger

| Weak | Stronger |
|---|---|
| AI is faster | It analyses very large data sets quickly, so patterns can be found that a team of people could not |
| AI is biased | If training data under-represents a group, the system works less well for that group |
| AI takes jobs | Fewer staff are needed for tasks AI can do, so there may be unemployment and a need to retrain |
| AI is cheaper | The running cost per case is lower, though the system costs money to build and maintain |

## Benefits and risks are linked

| Feature | Benefit | Linked risk |
|---|---|---|
| Learns from large data sets | Accurate and consistent decisions | Biased or wrong data is repeated consistently |
| Fast and cheap | Large tasks affordable; wide access | Fewer jobs needed |
| Creates content | Saves time | False information; plagiarism |
| Never tires | Constant monitoring | Loss of privacy |

## Exam traps

- **Reason needed.** A bare list earns few marks.
- **Cause of bias** is the data, not opinions held by the computer.
- **Consistent is not the same as correct.** A system can be consistently wrong.
- **Both sides** in a "discuss" question.
- **Superintelligence** is a possible future risk, not a present one.
- **Plagiarism** concerns presenting generated work as one's own.
- **Surveillance** has benefits as well as privacy risks.

## Quick self-test

1. Name three benefits of AI from the specification.
2. Why can AI decision making be more consistent than human decision making?
3. Calculate: 1,000,000 records at 500 per second. How many seconds?
4. Name three risks of AI from the specification.
5. How can an AI system develop bias?
6. Why might an AI system output false information?
7. What is plagiarism in the context of generative AI?
8. Why is surveillance by AI a privacy risk?
9. What is a superintelligent system?
10. A call centre of 200 staff has 60% of its calls handled by AI. How many jobs' work is no longer needed?
11. Why is cost saving not the whole story?
12. What should the end of a discuss answer contain?

### Answers

1. Any three of: improved and more consistent decisions; analysing very large data sets quickly; continuous availability; lower cost; available to more people and areas.
2. It applies the same learned rules to every case and is not affected by tiredness or mood.
3. 2,000 seconds (about 33 minutes).
4. Any three of: job losses; bias; false information; plagiarism; surveillance; risk from superintelligent systems.
5. By learning from training data that is biased or unrepresentative.
6. The training data was incorrect, or it follows patterns rather than checked facts.
7. Presenting work produced by a system as one's own (or copying others' work without credit).
8. It can track and identify people on a huge scale, possibly without their knowledge.
9. A system with intelligence far beyond human ability, a possible future development.
10. 120
11. The system costs money to build, maintain and supervise, and there are social costs such as job losses.
12. A conclusion that weighs the benefits against the risks.
