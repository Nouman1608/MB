## Section A

**1.** Define the term *tactic*. **[2]**

**2.** Identify **two** objectives that a public limited company is likely to pursue. **[2]**

**3.** Explain the difference between an aim and an objective. **[3]**

**4.** Corlenna Tours states: "We will increase bookings." Identify **two** elements of a SMART objective that this statement lacks and rewrite it as a SMART objective. **[4]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why a social enterprise might set a financial objective. **[2]**

**6.** Pellow Hardware has sales revenue of $1 500 000 and a profit margin of 6%. Its objective is a profit margin of 8% next year at the same level of revenue.

**(a)** Calculate the profit Pellow Hardware made this year.

**(b)** Calculate the reduction in total costs needed to meet the objective. **[4]**

## Section B

**7.** Grantham Ridge Foods is a family-owned private limited company. Explain **two** reasons why its objectives may differ from those of a public limited company in the same industry. **[4]**

**8.** Brisco Leisure has the objective of raising annual profit by $40 000. It is considering two options:

- Option X: extra revenue $95 000, extra costs $62 000.
- Option Y: extra revenue $70 000, extra costs $28 000.

**(a)** Calculate the extra profit from each option.

**(b)** Explain which option better meets the objective and why revenue alone should not decide. **[5]**

**9.** Analyse how a recession might change the objectives of a car dealership. **[6]**

**10.** Weylin Cosmetics plans to set environmental objectives as part of a triple bottom line approach. Analyse **one** benefit and **one** cost of this decision. **[4]**

**11.** Explain how the way objectives are communicated might affect the workforce of a large retailer. **[4]**

**12.** Kestwick Clothing, a plc, has been offered a contract to buy garments from a supplier whose factory has poor safety standards. The supplier's price is 15% lower than the current supplier's. Evaluate whether ethics should influence Kestwick's decision. **[10]**

---

## Answers

**1.** A short-term action [1] that carries out part of a strategy to help reach an objective [1].

**2.** Any two: profit growth [1]; a rising share price [1]; higher dividends; growth in sales or market share. Allow other relevant objectives such as cost reduction.

**3.** An aim is a broad, long-term intention [1]. An objective is a specific, measurable goal [1] with a time limit that helps the business achieve the aim [1].

**4.** Missing elements, any two: specific (bookings of which kind or from which market) [1]; measurable (no figure) [1]; time-limited (no deadline). Rewrite must include a figure [1] and a deadline [1], for example: "Increase coach-holiday bookings from 2 400 to 2 760 (15%) by 31 December."

**5.** A social enterprise must cover its costs to survive [1], and a surplus allows it to keep funding or expand its social purpose [1].

**6.** **(a)** Profit = 6% × 1 500 000 [1] = $90 000 [1].

**(b)** Target profit = 8% × 1 500 000 = $120 000, so costs must fall from $1 410 000 to $1 380 000 [1]: a reduction of $30 000 [1].

**7.** Any two, each explained. The family can keep control because shares are not sold publicly [1], so it can pursue long-term growth or family values rather than short-term returns [1]. A plc faces pressure from shareholders who can sell their shares [1], so it is more likely to prioritise profit growth and dividends to protect its share price [1]. Allow: a family firm may value independence and passing the business on, which a plc does not.

**8.** **(a)** Option X: 95 000 − 62 000 = $33 000 [1]. Option Y: 70 000 − 28 000 = $42 000 [1].

**(b)** Option Y better meets the objective because $42 000 exceeds the $40 000 target, while Option X falls short [1]. Option X brings more revenue, but its costs are much higher [1], and the objective is about profit, so the option must be ranked on profit, not revenue [1].

**9.** In a recession, consumer incomes and confidence fall, so fewer people buy new cars [1]. Sales and cash inflows drop, which makes survival and cash flow more important than growth [1]. The dealership may change its objective from expanding to a new site to protecting cash, for example by cutting inventory [1]. It may shift towards objectives in areas where demand holds up, such as servicing and repairs [1], because these bring steady revenue when car buying is postponed [1]. Market share may also become a priority if rivals are struggling, so the dealership could accept lower margins to win customers [1].

**10.** Benefit: customers who value environmental performance may prefer Weylin's products [1], allowing higher sales or a price premium that helps profit [1]. Cost: measuring and meeting environmental objectives, such as recyclable packaging, raises costs [1], which cuts profit margin unless prices rise [1].

**11.** If objectives are explained clearly, staff understand what they are working towards [1], which can raise motivation and make targets more likely to be met [1]. If staff are involved in setting store targets, commitment may rise further [1]. If targets are imposed without explanation or seem unrealistic, staff may feel stressed or resentful, which can increase labour turnover [1].

**12.** For ignoring ethics: a 15% lower price cuts costs and could raise profit significantly [1], which a plc's shareholders expect [1]; lower costs might allow lower prices and higher market share [1].
For letting ethics decide: unsafe factories risk injury to workers, which is morally wrong regardless of profit [1]; if the link were discovered, media coverage could damage Kestwick's brand and sales [1]; pressure groups and customers could boycott the brand [1]; the cost saving could be wiped out by lost sales [1].
Judgement: Kestwick should let ethics shape the decision [1] because, as a well-known plc brand, its reputation is worth more than a 15% saving [1]; the judgement depends on how much its customers care about supply-chain standards and whether the supplier could be required to improve safety first [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining a tactic or strategy without linking it to the objective it serves.
- Rewriting an objective as SMART but leaving out a deadline or a figure.
- Ranking options by revenue rather than by the objective given.
- Listing changes in objectives without explaining the cause.
- One-sided answers to an "evaluate" question on ethics, or no judgement.
