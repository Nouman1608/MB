## Records, reasons and trade-offs

**Different types of records.** Instead of full double entry (every transaction in ledger accounts, checked by a trial balance), a small trader may keep only bank statements or a cash book, filed invoices and a list of receivables and payables.

**Reasons:** no bookkeeping knowledge; cost of a bookkeeper or software; small scale, so the owner sees most transactions; the owner mainly wants to know the bank balance.

| Advantage | Disadvantage |
|---|---|
| Cheaper | No trial balance, so errors go unnoticed |
| Quicker and simpler | Theft and fraud harder to detect |
| Needs little skill | Profit has to be rebuilt, less reliable |
| | Poor control of trade receivables and trade payables |
| | Lenders and tax authorities may not accept the figures |

## Formulas to know

| Item | Formula |
|---|---|
| Capital | Assets - liabilities (statement of affairs) |
| Profit or loss | Closing capital - opening capital + drawings - capital introduced |
| Credit sales | Receipts from credit customers + closing trade receivables + irrecoverable debts written off - opening trade receivables |
| Credit purchases | Payments to credit suppliers + closing trade payables - opening trade payables |
| Revenue | Credit sales + cash sales |
| Cost of sales | Opening inventory + purchases - closing inventory |
| Gross profit | Revenue - cost of sales |
| Mark-up (%) | Gross profit / Cost of sales x 100 |
| Gross profit margin (%) | Gross profit / Revenue x 100 |
| Rate of inventory turnover (times) | Cost of sales / Average inventory |
| Inventory turnover (days) | Average inventory / Cost of sales x 365 days |
| Average inventory | (Opening inventory + closing inventory) / 2 |

The syllabus says only its listed ratio formulas are accepted in candidate responses, so write them in this form.

## Method in steps

1. **Opening statement of affairs.** List every asset and liability at the start. Net the allowance against trade receivables. Capital is the balancing figure.
2. **Closing statement of affairs.** Same again at the end. If the question gives no closing balance for depreciation or the allowance, work them out first.
3. **Quick profit.** Use the capital formula. Drawings include cash, goods and private expenses.
4. **Rebuild** credit sales, purchases and each expense, one working at a time.
5. **Statement of profit or loss** from those figures, with every 5.1 adjustment.
6. **Statement of financial position.** It is the closing statement of affairs reorganised, with the capital section in full.
7. **Check.** Profit from step 5 should equal step 3. If not, an adjustment is missing.

## Adjustments from 5.1 in one place

| Item | Expense or income for the year |
|---|---|
| Expense paid | Paid - opening accrual + opening prepayment + closing accrual - closing prepayment |
| Income received | Received - opening accrued income + opening income in advance + closing accrued income - closing income in advance |
| Straight line | Cost x rate |
| Reducing balance | Rate x carrying amount at the start of the year |
| Revaluation | Opening value + additions - closing valuation |
| Irrecoverable debt | Expense; reduces trade receivables |
| Allowance | Expense is the increase; a decrease is added to income |
| Goods taken by owner | Deduct from purchases (or cost of sales); add to drawings |
| Private expense paid by business | Remove from expenses; add to drawings |

## Worked reminders (Tolliver Tiles)

**Loss from capital.** Opening capital 14,200; closing capital 9,500; drawings 5,400; equipment worth 3,000 introduced.

```
9,500 - 14,200 + 5,400 - 3,000 = -2,300   a loss of 2,300
```

**Missing receipts.** Opening trade receivables 4,100; credit sales 52,300; irrecoverable debt written off 250; closing trade receivables 5,050. Cash from customers is the balancing figure.

```
4,100 + 52,300 - 250 - 5,050 = 51,100
```

**Missing purchases.** Paid to suppliers 36,400; trade payables 2,800 opening and 3,150 closing.

```
Purchases = 36,400 + 3,150 - 2,800 = 36,750
```

**Ratios.** Cost of sales 36,000; inventory 3,800 opening, 5,200 closing. Average inventory is 4,500, so rate of inventory turnover = 36,000 / 4,500 = 8 times, and inventory turnover is 4,500 / 36,000 x 365 = 45.6 days. If the margin is 30%, mark-up = 30/70 = 42.9%.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Mark-up vs margin.** Mark-up uses cost of sales as the base; margin uses revenue. For the same sale, the mark-up percentage is always the larger.
- **Converting.** Margin m% gives mark-up m/(100 - m). Mark-up k% gives margin k/(100 + k).
- **Statement of affairs vs statement of financial position.** Same layout, but a statement of affairs is built from listed balances because there are no full ledgers.
- **Drawings vs expenses.** Anything the owner takes for private use is drawings, whether cash, goods or a private bill paid by the business.
- **Irrecoverable debts vs the allowance.** A debt written off is certain; the allowance is an estimate of the closing receivables that may not be paid.
- **Capital introduced vs profit.** Both increase capital, but only profit appears in the statement of profit or loss.
- **Opening vs closing statement.** Opening capital is the start of the year; profit cannot be found without both.

## Quick self-test

1. A trader has assets of 18,400 and liabilities of 6,100. What is capital?
2. Closing capital 15,800; opening capital 12,300; drawings 7,200; capital introduced 2,000. Find the profit.
3. Receipts from credit customers 40,000; trade receivables 4,700 opening and 5,200 closing; no irrecoverable debts. Find credit sales.
4. Payments to suppliers 28,900; trade payables 3,100 opening and 3,600 closing. Find purchases.
5. A business earns a margin of 20%. What is its mark-up?
6. Cost of sales is 24,000 and the mark-up is 50%. Find revenue and gross profit.
7. Average inventory is 3,500 and the rate of inventory turnover is 6 times. Find cost of sales.
8. Rent paid 5,400; prepaid 450 at the start and 500 at the end. Find the rent expense.
9. Equipment cost 10,000, accumulated depreciation 3,000, depreciated at 20% on the reducing balance. Find the charge.
10. Loose tools valued at 2,200 at the start; 600 bought; valued at 2,000 at the end. Find the depreciation by revaluation.
11. Trade receivables at the year end are 6,250 and the allowance is to be 4%. The allowance at the start was 190. Find the expense.

### Answers

1. 18,400 - 6,100 = **12,300**.
2. 15,800 - 12,300 + 7,200 - 2,000 = **8,700** profit.
3. 40,000 + 5,200 - 4,700 = **40,500**.
4. 28,900 + 3,600 - 3,100 = **29,400**.
5. 20 / 80 x 100 = **25%**.
6. Gross profit 50% x 24,000 = **12,000**; revenue = **36,000**.
7. 6 x 3,500 = **21,000**.
8. 450 + 5,400 - 500 = **5,350**.
9. 20% x (10,000 - 3,000) = **1,400**.
10. 2,200 + 600 - 2,000 = **800**.
11. Allowance = 4% x 6,250 = 250; increase 250 - 190 = **60** expense.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out drawings of goods or private expenses when finding profit from capital.
- Forgetting to add back a debt written off when rebuilding credit sales.
- Using the wrong base: gross profit over revenue for mark-up, or over cost of sales for margin.
- Using closing inventory instead of average inventory for turnover.
- Not netting the allowance against trade receivables in a statement of affairs.
- Treating a decrease in the allowance as an expense.
- Showing capital introduced as income in the statement of profit or loss.
- Not checking that the two profit figures agree.
- Unlabelled workings, so one slip hides every correct method step.
