**These notes condense key question 2.4 of Cambridge O Level History 2147: "Who was to blame for the Cold War?"** Learn
the events of 1945–49 as a chain of action and reaction, so you can argue about blame with evidence. The full
explanation is in the [Cold War origins study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-world-history-2147-blame-cold-war/);
exam practice is in the [Cold War origins practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-world-history-2147-blame-cold-war-practice/).

## Condensed recall by focus point

**Why did the alliance break down in 1945?**
- Ideological opposites: capitalism and democracy against communism and one-party rule.
- Yalta (February 1945): agreement on zones, the UN and free elections; tension over Poland.
- Potsdam (July–August 1945): Truman and Attlee replace Roosevelt and Churchill; disputes over reparations and
  Poland's frontier; Truman hints at the bomb.
- Atomic bomb (August 1945) increased Soviet suspicion.
- 1946: Kennan's Long Telegram; Churchill's Iron Curtain speech; Novikov's telegram.

**How did the USSR gain control of Eastern Europe?**
- Red Army already present at the end of the war.
- Coalition governments, then communists took key ministries such as the interior and police.
- Salami tactics removed opponents one by one (Hungary).
- Rigged elections (Poland, January 1947); coup (Czechoslovakia, February 1948).
- Cominform (1947); Comecon (1949). Yugoslavia under Tito the exception.

**How did the USA react?**
- Containment: Truman Doctrine (March 1947), aid to Greece and Turkey.
- Marshall Plan (June 1947): economic recovery to weaken communism's appeal.
- Merged Western zones of Germany and introduced the Deutschmark (June 1948).

**Consequences of the Berlin Blockade**
- Airlift June 1948 to May 1949; Stalin backed down.
- Germany divided: FRG (May 1949), GDR (October 1949).
- NATO (April 1949); USA committed to Western Europe.
- Berlin a lasting symbol and flashpoint.

**Who was more to blame?**
- Orthodox: USSR. Revisionist: USA. Post-revisionist: both, through mutual suspicion.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cold War | Hostility between the superpowers without direct full-scale war between them |
| Iron Curtain | Churchill's phrase for the division between Eastern and Western Europe |
| Satellite state | A country formally independent but controlled by the USSR |
| Salami tactics | Removing opponents slice by slice until communists held full power |
| Containment | US policy of preventing the further spread of communism |
| Truman Doctrine | 1947 pledge to support free peoples against subjugation |
| Marshall Plan | US economic aid programme for European recovery |
| Dollar imperialism | Soviet description of Marshall Aid as a tool of US domination |
| Cominform | Communist Information Bureau, co-ordinating communist parties, 1947 |
| Comecon | Council for Mutual Economic Assistance, linking Eastern European economies to the USSR, 1949 |
| Berlin Airlift | Western supply of West Berlin by air, 1948–49 |
| NATO | North Atlantic Treaty Organisation, Western military alliance, 1949 |
| Warsaw Pact | Soviet-led military alliance, 1955 |

## Evidence grid for the blame question

| Event | Case against the USSR | Case against the USA |
|---|---|---|
| Eastern Europe 1945–48 | Free elections promised at Yalta were not held | The USSR saw a buffer as defence after invasion |
| Atomic bomb | | Kept secret from Stalin until Potsdam; seen as a threat |
| Truman Doctrine | Response to communist pressure in Greece and Turkey | Divided the world into two camps |
| Marshall Plan | Stalin forced satellites to refuse aid | Seen by the USSR as economic domination |
| Berlin Blockade | Starved civilians to force the West out | Followed Western currency reform without Soviet agreement |
| Alliances | | NATO (1949) came six years before the Warsaw Pact |

## Exam traps

- **Mixing up Yalta and Potsdam.** Yalta: Roosevelt and Churchill, before victory in Europe. Potsdam: Truman and
  Attlee, after it.
- **Saying the Truman Doctrine gave money to all of Europe.** It concerned Greece and Turkey; Marshall Aid was for
  Europe.
- **Dating NATO and the Warsaw Pact together.** 1949 and 1955.
- **Forgetting Yugoslavia** as an exception to Soviet control.
- **Choosing a side without evidence** for the other.

## Self-test

1. What was agreed at Yalta about Germany and Eastern Europe?
2. Why was Potsdam more tense than Yalta?
3. What did Kennan's Long Telegram recommend?
4. What were "salami tactics"?
5. Which country had a communist takeover in February 1948?
6. Why did Truman announce his Doctrine in March 1947?
7. Give **two** aims of the Marshall Plan.
8. What did Stalin call Marshall Aid?
9. What Western action triggered the Berlin Blockade?
10. How did the West respond to the Blockade?
11. Give **two** consequences of the Blockade.
12. Why was the Warsaw Pact formed in 1955?

**Answers:** 1. Germany would be divided into four zones; Eastern European states would hold free elections. 2.
Roosevelt had died and Truman was more suspicious; disputes over reparations and Poland; the atomic bomb. 3. Firm
containment of Soviet expansion. 4. Communists removing opponents step by step until they held full power. 5.
Czechoslovakia. 6. Britain could no longer support Greece and Turkey, and he wanted to stop communism spreading there.
7. Any two: rebuild Europe's economy; reduce poverty that helped communism; create markets for US goods; strengthen
Western Europe. 8. "Dollar imperialism". 9. The introduction of the Deutschmark in the Western zones (and plans for a
West German state). 10. The Berlin Airlift, supplying the city by air for about eleven months. 11. Any two: Germany
divided into FRG and GDR; NATO formed; Berlin became a symbol of the Cold War; Western commitment to Europe confirmed.
12. West Germany joined NATO.
