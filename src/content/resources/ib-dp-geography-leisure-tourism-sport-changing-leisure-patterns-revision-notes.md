Condensed recall for **5.1 Changing leisure patterns** (IB DP Geography, Option E: Leisure, tourism and sport). Fuller explanation is in the [Leisure, tourism and sport study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-geography-leisure-tourism-sport-study-guide/).

## Key terms

- **Leisure:** time free from work and obligations.
- **Recreation:** what people do with that time.

## Influences on changing patterns

| Influence | Effect |
|---|---|
| Time and income | Shorter working weeks, paid holidays and rising disposable income expand options |
| Mobility | Cheaper air travel, car ownership and better transport extend how far people travel |
| Technology | Internet, online booking, streaming and gaming change what people do and how they plan trips |
| Demographics | Ageing populations, changing households and a growing middle class in emerging economies change demand |
| Culture and values | Health awareness, interest in nature, heritage and experiences |

## Unequal participation

People with low income, disability, caring responsibilities or little access to green space may have fewer opportunities. A geographer looks for patterns by **age, gender, income and location** and asks why they exist.

## Commercialisation

Leisure has become commercialised: theme parks, shopping as leisure, fitness industries, sporting events.

## Describing and explaining a pattern

1. Describe the trend or difference using data if given (quote values and units).
2. Link it to one or two influences from the table.
3. Say which groups are included or excluded.
4. Note a limit of the data, such as who was surveyed.

## Exam traps

- Listing influences without explaining how they change behaviour.
- Treating leisure opportunity as equal for everyone.
- Describing holidays rather than geographical patterns and causes.
- Using invented participation statistics.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish leisure from recreation.
2. Give three influences on changing leisure patterns.
3. How has technology changed leisure?
4. Name two groups who may have fewer leisure opportunities.
5. What does commercialisation of leisure mean?

**Answers:** 1. Leisure is free time; recreation is what is done in it. 2. For example income, mobility, technology. 3. It changes activities (streaming, gaming) and how trips are planned (online booking). 4. For example people on low incomes or with disabilities. 5. Leisure becoming organised as paid products and industries.
