## Questions

**1.** Find the magnitude and the direction, to 1 decimal place, of the vector −5i − 2j. **[3]**

**2.** A force of 18 N acts in the direction 125°. Write it in the form xi + yj, giving x and y to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**3.** (A Level only) Find the vector of magnitude 45 that has the same direction as 2i − 10j + 11k. **[4]**

**4.** (A Level only) The vectors a = 2i + pj − 3k and b = −6i + 9j + qk are parallel.

**(a)** Find p and q. **[3]**
**(b)** Find |b|, giving your answer to 3 significant figures. **[1]**

**5.** (A Level only) The points A, B and C have coordinates (1, −1, 2), (3, 1, 3) and (2, 0, 7).

**(a)** Show that angle ABC is a right angle. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the area of triangle ABC. **[1]**

**6.** A, B, C and D are the vertices of a quadrilateral with position vectors a, b, c and d. The midpoints of AB, BC, CD and DA are P, Q, R and S. Show that PQRS is a parallelogram. **[5]**

**7.** Three forces act on a particle: F₁ = (4i + 3j) N, F₂ = (−10i + 5j) N and F₃. The resultant of the three forces is (−3i + 4j) N.

**(a)** Find F₃. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the magnitude and direction of F₃, giving the direction to 1 decimal place. **[3]**
**(c)** The particle has mass 2.5 kg. Find its acceleration as a vector and the magnitude of that acceleration. **[2]**

**8.** (A Level only) A particle P has position vector (5i − 4j) m at time t = 0 s. It has velocity (−6i + 2j) m s⁻¹ and constant acceleration (2i − j) m s⁻².

**(a)** Find the velocity of P at t = 3 and show that P is then moving parallel to j. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the position vector of P at t = 3. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the distance of P from the origin at t = 3, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**9.** Two ships move at constant velocity. At time t hours, ship P has position (2i + 10j) + t(3i − 4j) km and ship Q has position (−4i − 2j) + t(6i + 2j) km.

**(a)** Write down the position vector of each ship when t = 1. **[2]**
**(b)** Show that the ships collide, and find when and where. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the distance between the ships when t = 0. **[2]**
**(d)** Find the speed of each ship. **[2]**

**10.** (A Level only) ABCD is a parallelogram. Relative to an origin O, the vertices A, B and C are at a = i + 2k, b = 4i + 4j + 2k and c = 4i + 7j + 6k.

**(a)** Find →AB and |→AB|. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the position vector of D. **[2]**
**(c)** Show that ABCD is a rhombus. **[2]**
**(d)** Find the position vector of the midpoint of AC and show that it is also the midpoint of BD. **[2]**

**11.** Two forces act on a particle: one of magnitude 12 N in the direction 30°, the other of magnitude 8 N in the direction 150°. Find the exact resultant in the form xi + yj, then its magnitude and direction to 1 decimal place. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** |v| = √(25 + 4) = √29 ≈ 5.4 [1]. The vector is in the third quadrant, with acute angle tan⁻¹(2/5) = 21.8° [1]. Direction = 180° + 21.8° = **201.8°** [1]. Magnitude **√29 ≈ 5.4** **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A sketch settles the quadrant. An answer that stops at the calculator's 21.8° shows the vector has not been placed, and is likely to cost you credit.

**2.** x = 18 cos 125° [1] = **−10.3** [1]. y = 18 sin 125° = **14.7** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the full angle in cos and sin and the signs look after themselves. Writing the answer as 10.3i + 14.7j loses the sign of x.

**3.** |2i − 10j + 11k| = √(4 + 100 + 121) = √225 = 15 [1]. Unit vector = (2i − 10j + 11k)/15 [1]. Multiply by 45 [1]: **6i − 30j + 33k** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Dividing by the magnitude is the step that matters. Show the magnitude line before using it, so that one arithmetic slip still leaves the method visible.

**4. (a)** b = ka, so −6 = 2k and k = −3 [1]. Then 9 = k × p gives **p = −3** [1], and q = k × (−3) = **9** [1].
**(b)** |b| = √(36 + 81 + 81) = √198 = **14.1** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Find the scale factor from the component with no unknown first. Checking that all three components fit b = −3a is a quick safeguard.

**5. (a)** →AB = 2i + 2j + k, so AB² = 9 [1]. →BC = −i − j + 4k, so BC² = 18 [1]. →AC = i + j + 5k, so AC² = 27 [1]. AB² + BC² = AC², so angle ABC is **90°** by the converse of Pythagoras [1].
**(b)** Area = ½ × 3 × 3√2 = **9√2/2 ≈ 6.36** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* On a "show that" question, give all three squared lengths and the sum. The conclusion in words, naming the right-angled vertex, completes the argument.

**6.** p = (a + b)/2, q = (b + c)/2, r = (c + d)/2 and s = (d + a)/2 [1]. →PQ = q − p = (c − a)/2 [1]. →SR = r − s = (c − a)/2 [1]. So →PQ = →SR [1]. Opposite sides PQ and SR are equal and parallel, so PQRS is a **parallelogram** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Use position vectors for the midpoints rather than a diagram. State that equal vectors mean equal and parallel sides, because that sentence is what turns the algebra into a proof.

**7. (a)** F₃ = (−3i + 4j) − (4i + 3j) − (−10i + 5j) [1] = **3i − 4j** N [1].
**(b)** |F₃| = √(9 + 16) = **5 N** [1]. Fourth quadrant, acute angle tan⁻¹(4/3) = 53.1° [1]. Direction = 360° − 53.1° = **306.9°** [1].
**(c)** a = (−3i + 4j)/2.5 [1] = **−1.2i + 1.6j** m s⁻², with magnitude √(1.44 + 2.56) = **2 m s⁻²** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Subtract all the known forces from the resultant. Reversing the sign (adding them instead) is a common slip, and a check that the three forces sum to the resultant catches it.

**8. (a)** v = (−6i + 2j) + 3(2i − j) [1] = −j, which has no i-component, so P is moving parallel to j [1].
**(b)** r = (5i − 4j) + 3(−6i + 2j) + 4.5(2i − j) [1] = **−4i − 2.5j** m [1].
**(c)** Distance = √(16 + 6.25) = √22.25 [1] = **4.72 m** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The position formula needs the starting position added on. Leaving it out gives the displacement instead, which is a different quantity.

**9. (a)** P: (2 + 3)i + (10 − 4)j = **5i + 6j** km [1]. Q: (−4 + 6)i + (−2 + 2)j = **2i** km [1].
**(b)** i-components: 2 + 3t = −4 + 6t, so t = 2 [1]. j-components at t = 2: 10 − 8 = 2 and −2 + 4 = 2, so both agree [1]. They collide **2 hours** after t = 0 at **8i + 2j** km [1].
**(c)** →PQ at t = 0 = (−4i − 2j) − (2i + 10j) = −6i − 12j [1]. Distance = √(36 + 144) = √180 = **6√5 ≈ 13.4 km** [1].
**(d)** P: √(9 + 16) = **5 km h⁻¹** [1]. Q: √(36 + 4) = **2√10 ≈ 6.32 km h⁻¹** [1] **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* A collision needs both components to match at the same time. Solving one equation and stopping leaves the argument unfinished, so always substitute into the other.

**10. (a)** →AB = b − a = **3i + 4j** [1]. |→AB| = √(9 + 16) = **5** [1].
**(b)** d = a + c − b [1] = **i + 3j + 6k** [1].
**(c)** →BC = c − b = 3j + 4k, so |→BC| = 5 [1]. Adjacent sides are equal and ABCD is a parallelogram, so it is a **rhombus** [1].
**(d)** Midpoint of AC = (a + c)/2 = **(5/2)i + (7/2)j + 4k** [1]. Midpoint of BD = (b + d)/2 = (5i + 7j + 8k)/2, which is the same point [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* For D, think of a closed route round the parallelogram: →AD = →BC. Writing that statement first makes the working easy to follow and easy to credit.

**11.** F₁ = 12 cos 30° i + 12 sin 30° j = 6√3 i + 6j [1]. F₂ = 8 cos 150° i + 8 sin 150° j = −4√3 i + 4j [1]. Resultant = **2√3 i + 10j** N [1]. Magnitude = √(12 + 100) = √112 = 4√7 ≈ **10.6 N** [1]. Both components are positive, so the angle is tan⁻¹(10/(2√3)) [1] = **70.9°** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Work in components. Adding the two magnitudes (20 N) is a typical error. If the question asks for an exact resultant, keep the surds.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving the acute angle from tan⁻¹ and not the direction in [0°, 360°).
- Getting →AB the wrong way round, so every sign in the answer is reversed.
- Squaring a negative component and writing a negative result.
- Rounding in the middle of a multi-part answer and carrying an inaccurate value forward.
- Ending a proof (collinear, parallelogram, right angle) without a conclusion in words.
- Adding magnitudes of forces or velocities instead of adding the vectors.
- Mixing up velocity and speed, or position and displacement.
- Using s = ut + ½at² for the position and omitting the starting position.
- Writing a direction for a 3D vector, which the specification excludes.
