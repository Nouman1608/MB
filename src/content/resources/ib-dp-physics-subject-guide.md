IB Diploma Programme Physics is one of the DP sciences and covers the physical world
from motion and energy through waves and fields to the structure of the atom and the
life of stars. It is offered at Standard Level and Higher Level, and the current guide
was first assessed in 2025. At both levels the final grade comes from two written papers
and an internally assessed scientific investigation, weighted 36%, 44% and 20%
respectively. SL and HL share that split; what changes is the length of the papers, the
number of marks and the content they cover. This page explains how the syllabus is
arranged and how the assessment draws on it.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus is organised into five themes, each labelled with a letter.

**A. Space, time and motion.** Kinematics, forces and momentum, and work, energy and
power. HL adds rigid body mechanics and Galilean and special relativity.

**B. The particulate nature of matter.** Thermal energy transfers, the greenhouse effect,
gas laws, and current and circuits. HL adds thermodynamics.

**C. Wave behaviour.** Simple harmonic motion, the wave model, wave phenomena, standing
waves and resonance, and the Doppler effect. This theme has no HL-only sub-topic in the
list, though HL students still study the shared content at the greater depth the HL
papers demand.

**D. Fields.** Gravitational fields, electric and magnetic fields, and motion in
electromagnetic fields. HL adds induction.

**E. Nuclear and quantum physics.** Structure of the atom, radioactive decay, fission,
and fusion and stars. HL adds quantum physics.

The themes are not a strict teaching order. Current and circuits, for example, sits in
Theme B, and many schools teach it alongside the fields work in Theme D.

## How it's assessed

| Component | SL | HL | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paper 1 (Paper 1A multiple choice and Paper 1B data-based) | 90 minutes, 45 marks | 120 minutes, 60 marks | 36% |
| Paper 2 (short and extended response) | 90 minutes, 55 marks | 150 minutes, 90 marks | 44% |
| Internal assessment (individual scientific investigation) | 24 marks | 24 marks | 20% |

Paper 1 is taken as two booklets completed together without a break. At SL, Paper 1A
has 25 multiple-choice questions for 25 marks on SL material only, and Paper 1B has
data-based questions for 20 marks. At HL, Paper 1A has 40 multiple-choice questions for
40 marks on SL and additional HL material, and Paper 1B again carries 20 marks of
data-based questions. Paper 2 is made up of short-answer and extended-response
questions. Calculators are permitted on both papers, and a clean copy of the Physics
data booklet is required for both.

The internal assessment is an individual scientific investigation worth 24 marks at
both levels. It is assessed by the teacher and externally moderated.

## How the parts fit together

Because both papers draw on the whole syllabus, the themes need to be learned as a
connected whole. Energy is the clearest thread: it appears in work, energy and power in
Theme A, in thermal transfers and the gas laws in Theme B, in oscillations in Theme C,
in potential energy in fields in Theme D, and in binding energy, fission and fusion in
Theme E. Field ideas also link Theme D with Theme A's forces and with the motion of
charged particles.

Some skills run through every theme and every component: handling units and
uncertainties, using and rearranging equations from the data booklet, reading and
plotting graphs, and explaining physical reasoning in words. Paper 1B and the
investigation both test how you handle data, so practical work in lessons supports the
written papers as well as the internal assessment.

## Planning your study

A common route through the course starts with Theme A, because kinematics, forces and
energy supply the tools used everywhere else. Themes B and C often follow, then Theme D,
with Theme E towards the end because it uses ideas from energy, fields and waves.

Across the course, a workable plan looks like this:

- **First year:** Themes A, B and C, with regular practical work and short data
  exercises. HL students add rigid body mechanics, relativity and thermodynamics as the
  relevant themes are taught.
- **Investigation:** choose a research question that can genuinely be tested with the
  equipment available, then plan, collect and evaluate. Your school will set the timing.
- **Second year:** Themes D and E, including induction and quantum physics at HL,
  followed by revision across all five themes and full timed papers.

Keep a list of the HL-only sub-topics if you are taking HL, and check that each one has
its own notes and practice. Begin multiple-choice and data-based practice early, since
both reward familiarity, and use the exam preparation page for paper-by-paper advice.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is IB DP Physics assessed?**
At both SL and HL, Paper 1 is worth 36%, Paper 2 is worth 44% and the internal assessment, an individual scientific investigation, is worth 20%.

**How long are the IB Physics papers at SL and HL?**
At SL, Paper 1 and Paper 2 each last 90 minutes. At HL, Paper 1 lasts 120 minutes and Paper 2 lasts 150 minutes.

**What is the difference between Paper 1A and Paper 1B?**
Paper 1A is multiple choice (25 questions at SL, 40 at HL) and Paper 1B contains data-based questions worth 20 marks. The two booklets are completed together.

**Which topics are HL only in DP Physics?**
Rigid body mechanics and Galilean and special relativity in Theme A, thermodynamics in Theme B, induction in Theme D and quantum physics in Theme E.

**Can you use a calculator in IB Physics exams?**
Yes. Calculators are permitted on both papers, and a clean copy of the Physics data booklet is required.
