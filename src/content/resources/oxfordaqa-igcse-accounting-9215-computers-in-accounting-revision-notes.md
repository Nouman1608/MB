These notes condense the section 3.5 statement "Use of computers in accounting": the "advantages and disadvantages of using computerised software for recording accounting data compared to manual methods". The fuller explanation and an applied answer are in the [computers in accounting study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-9215-computers-in-accounting/); test yourself with the [computers in accounting practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-9215-computers-in-accounting-practice/).

## The core idea

In a manual system a person writes each transaction in a book of prime entry, copies it to two ledger accounts, balances the accounts and totals the trial balance. In a computerised system a person enters the transaction **once** and the software does the rest. Every advantage and most disadvantages follow from that.

## Stage by stage

| Stage | Manual | Computerised |
|---|---|---|
| Book of prime entry | Written by hand | Created when the transaction is entered |
| Ledger posting | Copied twice by hand | Automatic, both sides |
| Balancing | Added by hand | Instant |
| Trial balance | Listed and totalled; may disagree | On demand; always agrees |
| Financial statements | Drafted by hand | Printed as a report |

The person still decides **which account** and **what amount**. That is where errors of commission, principle and original entry come from in both systems.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Accounting software | A program that records transactions and produces ledgers, a trial balance and financial statements |
| Manual method | Books of prime entry and ledgers kept by hand on paper |
| Automatic posting | One entry updates both the debit and credit accounts |
| Validation check | A test applied by the software to data as it is entered, such as rejecting an invalid account code |
| Audit trail | A record of who entered or changed each item, and when |
| Back-up | A copy of the data kept in another place in case the original is lost |
| Access levels | Permissions that limit what each user can see or change |
| Garbage in, garbage out | Wrong input produces wrong output, however good the software |

## Advantages over manual methods

- Faster: one entry instead of several copying steps.
- Arithmetic always correct: balances and totals are automatic.
- Information is up to date at any moment, helping decisions.
- Reports on demand: aged receivables, customer statements, financial statements, ratios.
- Easy storage, search and back-up.
- Several staff can work on the records at the same time.

## Disadvantages compared with manual methods

- Costs: hardware, software, updates, training.
- Input errors still posted; staff may trust the output too much.
- Security risks: hacking, viruses, unauthorised changes, theft of data.
- System failure or power cut stops work; data lost without back-ups.
- Training needs and staff resistance; reliance on a few skilled users.

## Errors and software

| Still possible | Prevented |
|---|---|
| Omission | Addition and balancing errors |
| Commission | One-sided entries |
| Principle | Unequal amounts on the two sides |
| Original entry | Transposition between the two sides |
| Reversal through a wrong journal | |

Because every entry is posted to both sides, a **computerised trial balance always agrees**, so it reveals even less than a manual one.

## Controls

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Fraud or unauthorised change | Passwords, access levels, audit trail |
| Data loss | Regular back-ups kept elsewhere |
| Viruses and hacking | Security software, updates |
| Wrong input | Validation checks; checking totals against source documents |

## When software is worth it

| Points towards software | Points towards manual |
|---|---|
| Many transactions | Few transactions |
| Many credit customers and suppliers | Mainly cash sales |
| Owner needs current information to manage | Owner reviews figures only at the year end |
| Budget for set-up and training | Tight budget |
| Several staff recording at once | One person keeps the records |

## Exam traps

- "Computers remove all errors." They do not remove errors of omission, commission, principle or original entry.
- Advantages without the comparison: say faster or more accurate **than manual methods**.
- Generic points: apply to the size and transactions of the business in the question.
- Forgetting cost is ongoing (updates, subscriptions, training), not just a one-off purchase.
- Saying a balanced computerised trial balance proves the records are correct.

## Self-test

1. Why is computerised recording faster than manual recording?
2. Give two errors software cannot prevent.
3. Give two errors software does prevent.
4. What is an audit trail?
5. Why should back-ups be stored in a different place?
6. What is a validation check? Give an example.
7. Explain "garbage in, garbage out".
8. Give two costs of introducing accounting software.
9. Why is up-to-date information an advantage?
10. Give one control against fraud by staff.
11. Why might a small business selling only for cash keep manual records?
12. Can a computerised trial balance disagree in normal use?

**Answers**

1. One entry is posted automatically to the ledger accounts and totals are calculated, removing copying and adding steps.
2. Any two: omission, commission, principle, original entry.
3. Any two: addition errors, one-sided entries, unequal amounts on each side, transposition between sides.
4. A record of who entered or changed each transaction and when.
5. So that a fire, theft or system failure at the business does not destroy both the data and its copy.
6. A check made by the software on input, such as rejecting an account code that does not exist or a date outside the period.
7. If wrong data is entered, the software processes it correctly and produces wrong results.
8. Any two: hardware, software licence or subscription, updates, staff training.
9. Owners and managers can make decisions, such as chasing overdue customers, using current figures.
10. Passwords with access levels, or an audit trail.
11. It has few transactions and no credit customers, so the time saved would not justify the cost.
12. No, because every entry is posted to both sides automatically.
