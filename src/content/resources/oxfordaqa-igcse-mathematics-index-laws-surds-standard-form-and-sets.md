## N6: Index laws

In aⁿ, a is the base and n is the index (power). Two laws cover multiplication and division of the same base:

```
aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ
aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ
```

Integer powers include zero and negative numbers:

```
a⁰ = 1 (for a ≠ 0)
a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ
```

A negative power means "reciprocal", not "negative number". So 5⁻² = 1/25, which is positive.

When terms have coefficients, deal with the numbers and each letter separately.

**Worked example 1.** Simplify (4p⁵q²) × (3p⁻⁶q) ÷ (6pq⁴).

```
Numbers:  4 × 3 ÷ 6 = 2
p:        5 + (−6) − 1 = −2   →  p⁻²
q:        2 + 1 − 4 = −1      →  q⁻¹
Answer:   2p⁻²q⁻¹ = 2/(p²q)
```

**Worked example 2.** Simplify 12y⁷ ÷ 8y³. The numbers give 12/8 = 3/2 and the powers give y⁷⁻³ = y⁴. The answer is 3y⁴/2.

**Worked example 3.** Solve 3²ˣ⁻¹ = 1/27. Write 1/27 = 3⁻³. Equal bases mean equal indices, so 2x − 1 = −3, giving x = −1.

### Fractional powers (Extension only)

A power of 1/n means the nth root, and a power of m/n means "root, then power":

```
a^(1/n) = ⁿ√a        a^(m/n) = (ⁿ√a)ᵐ
```

To use this, you also need (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ, which is why a^(m/n) can be built from the root and the power. Take the root first, because it keeps the numbers small.

**Worked example 4 (Extension only).** Evaluate 625^(3/4).

```
625^(1/4) = 5   (since 5⁴ = 625)
5³ = 125
```

**Worked example 5 (Extension only).** Evaluate 8^(−2/3). The negative sign gives a reciprocal: 8^(−2/3) = 1 ÷ 8^(2/3). Then 8^(1/3) = 2 and 2² = 4, so the answer is 1/4.

**Worked example 6 (Extension only).** Simplify (16x⁸)^(3/4). Apply the power to each factor: 16^(3/4) = 2³ = 8, and (x⁸)^(3/4) = x⁶. The answer is 8x⁶.

**Worked example 7 (Extension only).** Solve x^(3/2) = 125. Raise both sides to the power 2/3, because (x^(3/2))^(2/3) = x. So x = 125^(2/3) = 5² = 25.

## N7: Exact calculation

"Exact" means you do not round part-way. Keep fractions as fractions (and surds as surds) until the end. A decimal such as 0.333 stands in for 1/3 but is not equal to it, so repeated rounding drifts away from the true answer.

### Fractions (Core)

- Add or subtract: use a common denominator, then add or subtract the numerators.
- Multiply: numerators together and denominators together. Cancel common factors first to keep numbers small.
- Divide: turn the second fraction upside down and multiply.
- Mixed numbers: convert to improper fractions before you start.

**Worked example 8.** Calculate 3²⁄₅ − 1³⁄₄.

```
17/5 − 7/4 = 68/20 − 35/20 = 33/20 = 1 13/20
```

**Worked example 9.** Calculate 2²⁄₃ × 1⁴⁄₅. Convert: 8/3 × 9/5. Cancel the 3 with the 9: 8/1 × 3/5 = 24/5 = 4⁴⁄₅.

**Worked example 10.** Calculate 5/6 ÷ 10/21. Multiply by the reciprocal: 5/6 × 21/10. Cancel 5 with 10, and 21 with 6 (a factor of 3): (1 × 7)/(2 × 2) = 7/4.

**Worked example 11.** Calculate (3/5 + 1/2) ÷ (7/10 − 1/4). Work out each bracket first.

```
3/5 + 1/2 = 6/10 + 5/10 = 11/10
7/10 − 1/4 = 14/20 − 5/20 = 9/20
11/10 ÷ 9/20 = 11/10 × 20/9 = 22/9 = 2 4/9
```

### Surds (Extension only)

Roots like √2 and √7 are irrational: no whole number or fraction equals them. Leaving them as √2 or √7 keeps them exact, and these are called surds. These rules let you simplify:

```
√(ab) = √a × √b        √(a/b) = √a / √b
```

To simplify, pull out the largest square factor.

**Worked example 12 (Extension only).** Simplify √200 and 3√12 + √75.

```
√200 = √(100 × 2) = 10√2
3√12 = 3 × 2√3 = 6√3
√75 = 5√3
Sum = 6√3 + 5√3 = 11√3
```

You can only add or subtract surds that have the same root after simplifying, like terms in algebra.

**Expanding brackets** works as in algebra, with √a × √a = a.

**Worked example 13 (Extension only).** Expand (2 + √5)(3 − √5).

```
= 6 − 2√5 + 3√5 − (√5 × √5)
= 6 + √5 − 5
= 1 + √5
```

**Worked example 14 (Extension only).** Expand (4 + √3)² = 16 + 8√3 + 3 = 19 + 8√3. Note that (√7 + 2)(√7 − 2) = 7 − 4 = 3 because the middle terms cancel (difference of two squares).

### Rationalising a denominator (Extension only)

Rationalising means rewriting a fraction so the denominator is a whole number.

- For a single surd, multiply top and bottom by that surd.
- For a denominator a + √b, use the conjugate a − √b on both top and bottom. The two denominators multiply to a² − b, which is rational.

**Worked example 15 (Extension only).** Rationalise 6/√3 and 5/(2√5).

```
6/√3 = 6√3/3 = 2√3
5/(2√5) = 5√5/(2 × 5) = √5/2
```

**Worked example 16 (Extension only).** Write 10/(3 + √2) with a rational denominator.

```
10(3 − √2) / [(3 + √2)(3 − √2)] = (30 − 10√2)/(9 − 2) = (30 − 10√2)/7
```

**Worked example 17 (Extension only).** Write (1 + √3)/(2 − √3) in the form a + b√3. Multiply by (2 + √3)/(2 + √3). The denominator is 4 − 3 = 1. The numerator is (1 + √3)(2 + √3) = 2 + √3 + 2√3 + 3 = 5 + 3√3. So the answer is 5 + 3√3.

## N8: Standard form

Standard form writes a number as A × 10ⁿ. Here A is at least 1 but less than 10, and n is a whole number (positive or negative). Large numbers have positive n and numbers between 0 and 1 have negative n.

**Worked example 18.** Write 45 000 000 and 0.000 062 in standard form. The decimal point moves 7 places left for the first and 5 places right for the second.

```
45 000 000 = 4.5 × 10⁷
0.000 062 = 6.2 × 10⁻⁵
```

### Calculating

- **Multiply or divide:** do the A values first, then the powers of 10, then tidy up so that 1 ≤ A < 10.
- **Add or subtract:** first rewrite both numbers so they share one power of 10.

**Worked example 19.** Calculate (3.2 × 10⁵) × (4 × 10⁻²).

```
3.2 × 4 = 12.8        10⁵ × 10⁻² = 10³
12.8 × 10³ = 1.28 × 10⁴
```

**Worked example 20.** Calculate (7.2 × 10⁸) ÷ (1.8 × 10³). The A parts give 7.2 ÷ 1.8 = 4 and the powers give 10⁸⁻³ = 10⁵, so the answer is 4 × 10⁵.

**Worked example 21.** Calculate (6.5 × 10⁴) + (8 × 10³). Write 8 × 10³ as 0.8 × 10⁴. Then 6.5 × 10⁴ + 0.8 × 10⁴ = 7.3 × 10⁴.

**Worked example 22.** Light takes how long to travel 1.5 × 10¹¹ m at 3 × 10⁸ m/s? Time = distance ÷ speed = (1.5 × 10¹¹) ÷ (3 × 10⁸) = 0.5 × 10³ = 5 × 10² seconds.

### Calculator displays

Your calculator may show standard form as 3.6E9 or as 3.6 × 10⁹, and 4.5E-3 means 4.5 × 10⁻³. "E" stands for "× 10 to the power". Do not copy the display as written: in your answer, write the full form with the × and the power of 10. Check that the answer you write has the right size, for example that a negative power matches a small number.

## N9: Sets

A set is a collection of objects, called elements, written in curly brackets. These are the symbols:

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ξ | the universal set (everything under consideration) |
| n(A) | the number of elements in A |
| A′ | the complement of A: elements of ξ not in A |
| A ∪ B | union: in A, or in B, or in both |
| A ∩ B | intersection: in both A and B |

In a Venn diagram, a rectangle represents ξ and each set is a circle. Overlapping circles show A ∩ B.

**Worked example 23.** ξ = {integers from 1 to 18}, A = {multiples of 4}, B = {factors of 24}.

```
A = {4, 8, 12, 16}      B = {1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12}
A ∩ B = {4, 8, 12}
A ∪ B = {1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16}, so n(A ∪ B) = 8
A′ = {1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11, 13, 14, 15, 17, 18}
(A ∪ B)′ = {5, 7, 9, 10, 11, 13, 14, 15, 17, 18}, so n((A ∪ B)′) = 10
```

For two sets, add the sizes of A and B, then take away the size of the overlap, so that the overlap is counted once. Check: n(A ∪ B) = 4 + 7 − 3 = 8.

Start with the most-overlapped region and work outwards. Fill the middle first, then subtract from each set total.

**Worked example 24.** Of 40 students, 24 play football, 18 play tennis and 7 play neither. How many play both?

```
Play at least one sport: 40 − 7 = 33
n(F ∩ T) = 24 + 18 − 33 = 9
Football only = 24 − 9 = 15     Tennis only = 18 − 9 = 9
Check: 15 + 9 + 9 + 7 = 40
```

**Worked example 25.** A club of 60 members: 25 play chess, 20 act, 22 play music. 8 do chess and acting, 9 do chess and music, 7 do acting and music, and 4 do all three.

```
Centre (all three) = 4
Chess and acting only = 8 − 4 = 4
Chess and music only = 9 − 4 = 5
Acting and music only = 7 − 4 = 3
Chess only = 25 − 4 − 4 − 5 = 12
Acting only = 20 − 4 − 4 − 3 = 9
Music only = 22 − 5 − 4 − 3 = 10
Total in circles = 12 + 9 + 10 + 4 + 5 + 3 + 4 = 47
Outside the circles = 60 − 47 = 13
```

## Common errors

- Writing 5⁻² as −25 or −10. It is 1/25.
- Adding indices when multiplying different bases: 2³ × 3² is not 6⁵.
- Applying a fractional power to the numbers but forgetting the letters in (16x⁸)^(3/4).
- Adding surds with different roots: √2 + √3 is not √5.
- Forgetting the middle terms when squaring (4 + √3)²; the answer is not 16 + 3.
- Leaving 12.8 × 10³ as it is. A must satisfy 1 ≤ A < 10.
- Adding standard-form numbers by adding the powers of 10.
- Counting A ∩ B twice in n(A ∪ B), or filling the outer regions of a Venn diagram before the centre.
