## Section A

**1.** State the main idea of Lamarck's theory of evolution. **[1]**

**2.** State what is now known about the type of inheritance that Lamarck suggested. **[1]**

**3.** Give the **three** statements that describe how evolution occurs through natural selection. **[3]**

**4.** Name the **four** stages in the formation of a new species, in order. **[4]**

## Section B

**5.** A population of 1 000 moths lives in a wood. At the start, 200 are dark and 800 are light. Birds eat the moths they can see. The wood has recently become covered in dark soot. In each generation, 60 % of the dark moths and 30 % of the light moths survive to breed. The next generation again has 1 000 moths in the same proportions as the survivors.

**(a)** Calculate the number of dark and light survivors in the first generation. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of dark moths in the next generation. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** After a second generation the percentage of dark moths is 50.0 %. Explain why the proportion of dark moths is increasing. **[3]**
**(d)** State one way in which this model is simpler than a real population. **[1]**

**6.** Seed-eating birds were measured before and after a drought. The mean beak length of the adults was 5.8 mm before and 6.4 mm after.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in mean beak length. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(b)** The drought killed most of the soft seeds. Explain how this could have caused the change. **[3]**
**(c)** A student says: "The birds stretched their beaks to reach the seeds, and so their chicks had longer beaks." Explain why this is not the accepted explanation. **[2]**

**7.** A species of beetle takes 2 years to produce each new generation. Calculate how many years are needed for 600 generations. **[1]**

**8.** Explain why variation in a population is needed for natural selection to occur. **[2]**

**9.** Explain what is meant by the phrase "survival of the fittest" in natural selection. **[2]**

**10.** Explain why a species may become extinct when its environment changes quickly. **[2]**

**11.** Compare Lamarck's theory with the theory of evolution by natural selection, using the evolution of a long neck in giraffes as an example. **[4]**

**12.** Two populations of a species of fish are separated when a lake splits into two lakes. The lakes have different food supplies. Explain how, over a very long time, the two populations may become two different species. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Changes that occur in an organism during its lifetime can be inherited [1]

**2.** In the vast majority of cases this type of inheritance cannot occur [1]

**3.** Individuals within a species show a wide range of variation because of differences in their genes [1]; individuals with characteristics most suited to the environment are more likely to survive to breed successfully [1]; the genes that enabled these individuals to survive are passed on to the next generation [1]

**4.** Isolation [1]; genetic variation [1]; natural selection [1]; speciation [1]

**5. (a)** dark: 200 × 0.6 = **120** [1]; light: 800 × 0.3 = **240** [1]
**(b)** 120 ÷ (120 + 240) × 100 [1] = **33.3%** [1]
**(c)** Dark moths are better camouflaged on the dark soot, so they are less likely to be eaten [1]; they are more likely to survive to breed [1]; they pass on the genes for dark colour to the next generation, so the proportion of dark moths increases [1]
**(d)** Any one of the following [1]: survival rates are not exactly the same every year; other factors affect survival; the population size does not stay at exactly 1 000; there may be mutations or movement of moths in and out

**6. (a)** (6.4 − 5.8) ÷ 5.8 × 100 [1] = **10.3%** [1]
**(b)** Birds with longer beaks (because of their genes) could eat the harder seeds [1]; they were more likely to survive and breed [1]; they passed on the genes for longer beaks, so the mean beak length of the next generation increased [1]
**(c)** A change in an individual's body during its lifetime (such as stretching) is not usually inherited [1]; the offspring inherit genes, and the change is explained by natural selection of genes for longer beaks [1]

**7.** 600 × 2 = **1 200 years** [1]

**8.** If all individuals were identical, none would be better suited to the environment than others [1]; variation (in genes) means that some individuals are more likely to survive and breed, and so to pass on their genes [1]

**9.** The individuals with characteristics most suited to the environment [1]; are more likely to survive and breed successfully (and pass on their genes) [1]

**10.** If no individuals in the population have characteristics suited to the new conditions, natural selection cannot produce a population that can survive [1]; so the species may not respond sufficiently to the change and die out (become extinct) [1]

**11.** Lamarck: a giraffe stretches its neck to reach leaves, and this change is passed to its offspring [1]; natural selection: giraffes show variation in neck length because of their genes [1]; longer-necked giraffes are more likely to reach food, survive and breed, passing on their genes [1]; we now know that stretching a neck in a lifetime is not usually inherited, so natural selection is the accepted theory [1]

**12.** Marking points, 1 mark each [6]:
- isolation: the two populations are separated by the splitting of the lake and cannot breed together
- genetic variation: each population contains a wide range of alleles
- the different food supplies create different conditions in each lake
- natural selection: in each lake, the alleles that help individuals survive and breed are selected and become more common
- over many generations, the populations become more and more different
- speciation: they become so different that they can no longer interbreed to produce fertile offspring, so they are different species

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that individuals change or adapt during their lives to become better suited.
- Leaving out genes, or forgetting that survivors must breed.
- Writing "survival of the fittest" without saying "best suited to the environment".
- Saying speciation occurs without isolation.
- Omitting "fertile offspring" from the end of speciation.
- Getting the percentage by dividing by the wrong total.
