## What 5.5 covers

The syllabus lists:

1. receipts and payments accounts and income and expenditure accounts
2. how to prepare receipts and payments accounts
3. how to prepare accounts for revenue-generating activities, e.g. refreshments,
   subscriptions
4. how to prepare income and expenditure accounts and statements of financial position
5. how to make adjustments to financial statements as detailed in 5.1 (sole traders)
6. how to define and calculate the accumulated fund

## Two accounts compared

| Receipts and payments account | Income and expenditure account |
|---|---|
| A summary of the cash book | The club's equivalent of a statement of profit or loss |
| Records all cash and bank receipts and payments, capital or revenue | Records only revenue income and expenditure for the year |
| No adjustments for accruals, prepayments or depreciation | Applies matching: accruals, prepayments, depreciation |
| Ends with the closing cash or bank balance | Ends with a surplus (income exceeds expenditure) or deficit |

A club exists to serve its members, not to make a profit, so "surplus" and
"deficit" replace "profit" and "loss", and the members' interest is called the
**accumulated fund** instead of capital.

## Worked example: Riverside Rowing Club

**Step 1: the accumulated fund at the start.** The accumulated fund is the club's
assets less its liabilities. On 1 January 2028 the club had: boats 42,000; café
inventory 850; subscriptions in arrears 360; bank 3,900; subscriptions in advance
240; owing to café suppliers 410; electricity accrued 180.

```
Assets      42,000 + 850 + 360 + 3,900          = 47,110
Liabilities 240 + 410 + 180                     =    830
Accumulated fund at 1 January 2028              = 46,280
```

**Step 2: the receipts and payments account.**

```
Riverside Rowing Club: Receipts and payments account for the year ended 31 December 2028
Receipts                                 Payments
Balance b/d                    3,900     Café supplies                  5,920
Subscriptions                 18,600     Café assistant's wages         2,600
Café takings                   9,750     Electricity                    1,960
Competition entry fees         2,300     Rent of boathouse              7,200
Donations                        500     Competition prizes and costs   1,450
                                         New boat                       6,500
                                         Insurance                      1,800
                                         Balance c/d                    7,620
                              ------                                   ------
                              35,050                                   35,050
                              ======                                   ======
Balance b/d                    7,620
```

The new boat is capital expenditure. It appears here because cash was paid, but it
will **not** appear in the income and expenditure account.

**Step 3: subscriptions.** At 31 December 2028: subscriptions in arrears for 2028
were 420, and 330 had been received in advance for 2029. Of the 360 arrears at the
start of the year, 300 was received and 60 is to be written off because the
members have left.

```
Subscriptions account ($)
Balance b/d (arrears)       360    Balance b/d (in advance)        240
Income and expenditure   18,630    Bank                         18,600
Balance c/d (in advance)    330    Subscriptions written off        60
                                   Balance c/d (arrears)           420
                         ------                                 ------
                         19,320                                 19,320
                         ======                                 ======
Balance b/d (arrears)       420    Balance b/d (in advance)        330
```

Income for the year is the balancing figure, **18,630**. The 60 written off is
treated like an irrecoverable debt: an item of expenditure.

**Step 4: the café account.** The café is a revenue-generating activity, so its
result is calculated separately and the profit is transferred to income. Café
inventory at 31 December 2028 was 920 and café suppliers were owed 530.

```
Purchases = 5,920 paid − 410 owing at start + 530 owing at end = 6,040

Café account for the year ended 31 December 2028
Sales                                                    9,750
Opening inventory                             850
Purchases                                   6,040
                                            6,890
Closing inventory                            (920)       5,970
Gross profit                                             3,780
Café assistant's wages                                   2,600
Profit on café                                           1,180
```

**Step 5: the income and expenditure account.** Electricity of 210 was accrued at
31 December 2028; insurance of 300 was prepaid. The boats were valued at 44,000 at
the year end (revaluation method).

```
Riverside Rowing Club: Income and expenditure account for the year ended 31 December 2028
Income
   Subscriptions                                         18,630
   Profit on café                                         1,180
   Competition: entry fees 2,300 less costs 1,450           850
   Donations                                                500
                                                         21,160
Expenditure
   Rent of boathouse                          7,200
   Electricity (1,960 − 180 + 210)            1,990
   Insurance (1,800 − 300)                    1,500
   Depreciation of boats (42,000 + 6,500 − 44,000) 4,500
   Subscriptions written off                     60      15,250
Surplus for the year                                      5,910
```

**Step 6: the statement of financial position.**

```

Current assets
   Café inventory                               920
   Subscriptions in arrears                     420
   Other receivables (insurance prepaid)        300
   Bank                                       7,620
                                              9,260

   Café suppliers                               530

   Other payables (electricity)                 210

   Surplus for the year                                   5,910
                                                         52,190
```

## Reading the results

The club made a surplus of 5,910, but its bank balance rose by only 3,720
(from 3,900 to 7,620). The two figures differ because they measure different
things:

- the new boat cost 6,500 in cash but is not expenditure; only its depreciation is
- depreciation of 4,500 is expenditure but no cash was paid
- subscription balances changed: more arrears are owed to the club and more
  subscriptions were received in advance
- the club owes more to café suppliers and for electricity than a year ago

A committee should look at both. The surplus shows whether income from members
and activities covers the cost of running the club; the receipts and payments
account shows whether there is enough cash to pay bills and buy equipment. Here
the café adds 1,180 and competitions 850, so the club is not relying on
subscriptions alone.

## Common mistakes

- Including the new boat, or the opening bank balance, in income and expenditure.
- Using subscriptions received (18,600) instead of subscriptions for the year.
- Forgetting that opening subscriptions in advance are this year's income.
- Showing café takings as income instead of the café profit.
- Using café payments instead of purchases.
- Calculating the opening accumulated fund without the liabilities.
- Calling the result "profit" or the fund "capital".

## Exam technique

- **Explain** the difference between the two main accounts in terms of cash
  versus matching, and capital versus revenue items.
- **Prepare** the subscriptions account first; the income figure is its balancing
  figure.
- **Define** accumulated fund as total assets less total liabilities of the club.
- **Advise** a committee on, for example, raising subscriptions by referring to the
  surplus or deficit and the club's bank balance.
