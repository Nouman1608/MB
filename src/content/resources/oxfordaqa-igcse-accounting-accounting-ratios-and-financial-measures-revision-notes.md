The specification says the formulae are in an OxfordAQA guide, "Formulae for accounting ratios". The forms below are the standard forms. Learn the board's own versions from that guide and use them if they differ.

## The nine measures: formulae and units

| Measure | Standard form | Answer given as |
|---|---|---|
| Gross profit margin % | gross profit / revenue x 100 | percentage |
| Markup % | gross profit / cost of sales x 100 | percentage |
| Rate of inventory turnover | cost of sales / average inventory | times per year |
| Profit in relation to revenue % | profit for the year / revenue x 100 | percentage |
| Return on capital employed % | profit from operations / capital employed x 100 | percentage |
| Current ratio | current assets : current liabilities | ratio, for example 2 : 1 |
| Liquid capital ratio | (current assets - inventory) : current liabilities | ratio |
| Trade receivable days | trade receivables / credit revenue x 365 | days |
| Trade payable days | trade payables / credit purchases x 365 | days |

## Building blocks

- Cost of sales = opening inventory + purchases - closing inventory.
- Gross profit = revenue - cost of sales.
- Average inventory = (opening + closing) / 2.
- Profit from operations = gross profit - operating expenses, before loan interest.
- Profit for the year = profit from operations - loan interest.
- Capital employed = total assets - current liabilities = equity + non-current liabilities.

## Method in steps

1. Write the formula first.
2. Pick the right figures. Mark which are from the income statement and which are from the statement of financial position.
3. Work out any building block you need, such as cost of sales or capital employed.
4. Substitute and calculate. Show the working.
5. Give the unit: percentage, times, days or ratio.
6. If asked to comment, compare with something and give a reason.

## Worked reminder: Thornsby Stationers

Revenue 96,000 (73,000 on credit). Cost of sales 72,000, from opening inventory 11,000, purchases 74,000 (43,800 on credit) and closing inventory 13,000. Profit from operations 18,500, loan interest 500. Non-current assets 58,000. Current assets: inventory 13,000, trade receivables 15,000, bank 4,000. Current liabilities: trade payables 6,000 and other payables 10,000.

| Measure | Working | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Gross profit | 96,000 - 72,000 | 24,000 |
| Gross profit margin | 24,000 / 96,000 x 100 | 25.0% |
| Markup | 24,000 / 72,000 x 100 | 33.3% |
| Rate of inventory turnover | 72,000 / 12,000 | 6.0 times |
| Profit in relation to revenue | 18,000 / 96,000 x 100 | 18.75% |
| Capital employed | 58,000 + 32,000 - 16,000 | 74,000 |
| Return on capital employed | 18,500 / 74,000 x 100 | 25.0% |
| Current ratio | 32,000 : 16,000 | 2.0 : 1 |
| Liquid capital ratio | 19,000 : 16,000 | 1.19 : 1 |
| Receivable days | 15,000 / 73,000 x 365 | 75 days |
| Payable days | 6,000 / 43,800 x 365 | 50 days |

Customers take 75 days to pay but suppliers are paid in 50. That squeezes cash, even though the current ratio of 2.0 : 1 looks healthy.

## Interpretation cues

| Measure | Higher | Lower |
|---|---|---|
| Gross profit margin and markup | Better pricing or cheaper buying | Discounts, dearer suppliers, sales mix |
| Profit in relation to revenue | Tight expense control | Expenses growing faster than revenue |
| Return on capital employed | Capital used productively | Falling profit or new capital not yet earning |
| Inventory turnover | Faster selling; less cash tied up | Slow-moving or excess inventory |
| Current ratio | Safer cover, but may mean idle assets | Possible trouble paying short-term debts |
| Liquid capital ratio | Cover without relying on inventory | Below 1 : 1 means reliance on selling inventory |
| Receivable days | Slow collection or generous terms | Quicker cash, tighter credit control |
| Payable days | Cash kept longer, supplier goodwill at risk | Suppliers paid quickly, cash used sooner |

## Must-know distinctions

- Margin divides by revenue. Markup divides by cost of sales. Markup is the larger figure for the same trading.
- Margin to markup: margin / (100 - margin) x 100. Markup to margin: markup / (100 + markup) x 100. For example, 40% margin is 66.7% markup, and 80% markup is 44.4% margin.
- Current ratio includes inventory. The liquid capital ratio does not.
- Profit in relation to revenue uses profit for the year. ROCE uses profit from operations and capital employed.
- Profit is not cash. Credit sales raise profit but not cash. Depreciation lowers profit but not cash. Buying equipment, repaying loans and drawings lower cash without lowering profit when they happen.
- Paying a trade payable from the bank lowers current assets and current liabilities by the same amount. A current ratio above 1 : 1 then rises. Buying a non-current asset from the bank lowers current assets only, so the ratio falls.

## Cash and profit: effect of common transactions

| Transaction | Profit | Cash at bank |
|---|---|---|
| Sale on credit | Rises now | No change until the customer pays |
| Customer pays a trade receivable | No change | Rises |
| Purchase of inventory on credit | No change until sold | No change until paid |
| Depreciation charge | Falls | No change |
| Purchase of equipment from the bank | No change now | Falls |
| Drawings in cash | No change | Falls |
| Payment of an expense | Falls | Falls |

When asked why profit and cash differ, name the transactions in the question and say which of the two each one changes.

## Appraisal and limitations in brief

- Profitability appraisal: use the margin, markup, profit in relation to revenue and ROCE. Compare years, state what moved, give a reason, reach a judgement.
- Liquidity appraisal: use the current ratio, liquid capital ratio and the days measures. State whether short-term debts can be met.
- Financial limitations: historic figures, year-end snapshots, differing accounting policies, inflation, ratios do not explain why.
- Non-financial limitations: staff, reputation, customer loyalty, competitors, market conditions, management quality.

## Quick self-test

1. A trader has revenue of 54,000 and cost of sales of 36,000. Calculate the gross profit margin and the markup.
2. Cost of sales is 63,000 and the gross profit margin is 30%. Find revenue.
3. An item costing 50 sells for 80. Find the margin and the markup.
4. Cost of sales is 84,000 and average inventory is 12,000. Find the rate of inventory turnover.
5. Profit from operations is 12,000, equity is 50,000 and a loan is 10,000. Find the return on capital employed.
6. Current assets are 45,000, including inventory of 18,000. Current liabilities are 30,000. Find the current ratio and the liquid capital ratio.
7. Trade receivables are 9,000 and credit revenue is 54,750. Find trade receivable days.
8. Trade payables are 10,950 and credit purchases are 54,750. Find trade payable days.
9. Current assets are 20,000 and current liabilities are 10,000. The business pays 5,000 to trade payables from the bank. Find the new current ratio. Does profit change?
10. Give two non-financial limitations of ratio analysis.

### Answers

1. Gross profit 18,000. Margin = 18,000 / 54,000 x 100 = 33.3%. Markup = 18,000 / 36,000 x 100 = 50%.
2. Cost of sales is 70% of revenue, so revenue = 63,000 / 0.70 = 90,000.
3. Gross profit 30. Margin = 30 / 80 x 100 = 37.5%. Markup = 30 / 50 x 100 = 60%.
4. 84,000 / 12,000 = 7 times.
5. Capital employed = 50,000 + 10,000 = 60,000. ROCE = 12,000 / 60,000 x 100 = 20%.
6. Current ratio = 45,000 : 30,000 = 1.5 : 1. Liquid capital ratio = 27,000 : 30,000 = 0.9 : 1.
7. 9,000 / 54,750 x 365 = 60 days.
8. 10,950 / 54,750 x 365 = 73 days.
9. Current assets become 15,000 and current liabilities 5,000, so the ratio rises from 2 : 1 to 3 : 1. Profit does not change.
10. Any two of: staff morale or skills, reputation, customer loyalty, competitor actions, market conditions, quality of management.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the wrong base for markup, dividing by revenue instead of cost of sales.
- Using closing inventory instead of average inventory for turnover when both are available.
- Leaving out the unit, or writing days as a percentage.
- Using total revenue for receivable days when credit revenue is given.
- Forgetting to remove inventory in the liquid capital ratio.
- Deducting loan interest before ROCE when the formula uses profit from operations.
- Calling a figure good or bad with no comparison and no reason.
- Treating profit and cash as the same thing when explaining a falling bank balance.
- Giving limitations that are generic and not tied to the business in the question.
