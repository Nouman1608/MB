## Questions

**1.** State the three profit figures that an internal income statement of a limited company must distinguish below gross profit. **[3]**

**2.** Pellow Robotics Ltd issues 150,000 ordinary shares of £0.20 each at £0.65 per share. All money is received.

**(a)** Calculate the amounts debited to bank and credited to share capital and to share premium. **[3]**
**(b)** State the sub-heading under which share premium appears in the statement of financial position. **[1]**

**3.** Explain why dividends paid are not shown as an expense in a company's income statement. **[2]**

**4.** State the statement of financial position sub-heading for each item: share premium; 7% debentures repayable 2035; tax payable; insurance prepaid; delivery vehicles. **[5]**

**5.** Morven Tiles Ltd's year ends 31 December 2025. Revenue £420,000; cost of sales £238,000; operating expenses £109,500. It issued 4% debentures of £120,000 on 1 April 2025. Tax for the year is £15,900. Calculate profit from operations, profit for the year before tax and profit for the year after tax. **[5]**

**6.** Fenmore Ltd has had 7.5% debentures of £80,000 in issue all year. Debenture interest paid during the year was £3,000.

**(a)** Calculate the debenture interest charge for the year and the amount accrued. **[2]**
**(b)** State where each amount appears in the financial statements. **[2]**

**7.** Tarrow Brewing Ltd's year ends 30 June 2026. At 1 July 2025: ordinary shares of £1 each £300,000; share premium £45,000; retained earnings £118,600.

- August 2025: final dividend of 5p per share paid for the previous year.
- 1 October 2025: 100,000 ordinary shares issued at £1.40 each.
- December 2025: interim dividend of 6p per share paid on all shares then in issue.
- Profit for the year after tax: £61,250.

Prepare the statement of changes in equity for the year ended 30 June 2026. **[8]**

**8.** Ridley Glass Ltd's retained earnings were £52,400 at the start of the year and £47,900 at the end. Dividends paid in the year were £18,000 and tax for the year was £4,500. There were no other movements. Calculate profit for the year after tax and profit for the year before tax. **[4]**

Prepare the income statement for the year ended 31 March 2026. **[12]**

**10.** Using Question 9, prepare the statement of financial position of Calder Print Ltd at 31 March 2026. **[10]**

**11.** A shareholder in Calder Print Ltd says: "Profit for the year after tax is the only profit figure that matters to me. The other two lines are clutter." Evaluate this view. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Profit from operations [1]; profit for the year before tax [1]; profit for the year after tax [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the specification's labels; "net profit" alone does not identify which level you mean.

**2. (a)** Bank 150,000 x £0.65 = **£97,500** [1]; share capital 150,000 x £0.20 = **£30,000** [1]; share premium 150,000 x £0.45 = **£67,500** [1].
**(b)** **Equity** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Crediting all £97,500 to share capital ignores the premium; work the split per share first.

**3.** Dividends are a distribution of profit to the owners (shareholders), not a cost of earning revenue [1]. They are deducted from retained earnings in the statement of changes in equity [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Say where dividends do go, as well as why they are not an expense.

**4.** Share premium: equity [1]. Debentures repayable 2035: non-current liabilities [1]. Tax payable: current liabilities [1]. Insurance prepaid: current assets [1]. Delivery vehicles: non-current assets [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Check repayment dates: debentures repayable years after the year end are non-current, not current.

**5.** Gross profit 420,000 - 238,000 = £182,000 [1]. Profit from operations 182,000 - 109,500 = **£72,500** [1]. Interest for 9 months 4% x 120,000 x 9/12 = £3,600 [1]. Profit before tax 72,500 - 3,600 = **£68,900** [1]. Profit after tax 68,900 - 15,900 = **£53,000** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Check the issue date: debentures issued on 1 April carry only 9 months' interest to a December year end, not £4,800.

**6. (a)** Charge 7.5% x 80,000 = **£6,000** [1]; accrued 6,000 - 3,000 = **£3,000** [1].
**(b)** £6,000: income statement, finance costs, after profit from operations [1]. £3,000: statement of financial position, current liabilities (other payables) [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The charge goes below profit from operations; listing it among operating expenses misplaces it.

**7.**

```
Tarrow Brewing Ltd: Statement of changes in equity
for the year ended 30 June 2026
                      Share      Share   Retained
                    capital    premium   earnings      Total
                          £          £          £          £
At 1 July 2025      300,000     45,000    118,600    463,600
Share issue         100,000     40,000               140,000
Profit for the year                        61,250     61,250
Dividends paid                            (39,000)   (39,000)
At 30 June 2026     400,000     85,000    140,850    625,850
```

Opening balances [1]; share capital 100,000 x £1 = £100,000 [1]; premium 100,000 x £0.40 = £40,000 [1]; profit £61,250 [1]; final dividend 300,000 x 5p = £15,000 [1]; interim 400,000 x 6p = £24,000, total £39,000 [1]; closing share capital and premium [1]; closing retained earnings **£140,850** and total **£625,850** [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* The interim dividend is paid on 400,000 shares because the issue came first; check dates before multiplying.

**8.** Closing = opening + profit after tax - dividends, so profit after tax = 47,900 - 52,400 + 18,000 [1] = **£13,500** [1]. Profit before tax = profit after tax + tax [1] = 13,500 + 4,500 = **£18,000** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Retained earnings fell, but that does not mean a loss; write the relationship before rearranging it.

Cost of sales £250,100 [1]; gross profit £235,900 [1]; rent received £5,000 [1]; wages £75,000 [1]; insurance £4,500 [1]; provision 3% x 42,000 = £1,260, increase £160 [1]; premises 2% x 300,000 = £6,000 [1]; equipment 20% x 50,000 = £10,000 [1]; profit from operations **£94,840** [1]; interest 5% x 70,000 = £3,500 [1]; profit before tax **£91,340** [1]; profit after tax **£76,940** [1]. **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* Dividends paid of £16,000 do not belong here; they reduce retained earnings in Question 10.

Premises £258,000 [1]; equipment £40,000 [1]; inventory and prepayment [1]; receivables net £40,740 [1]; bank and total assets £387,720 [1]; retained earnings 37,830 + 76,940 - 16,000 = **£98,770** [1]; share capital and premium [1]; debentures as non-current [1]; other payables £4,950 [1]; tax payable and total **£387,720** [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the retained earnings working, so a profit carried from Question 9 can be followed even if it is wrong.

**11.** For: profit after tax is the figure added to retained earnings, and dividends can only come out of it [1]; it is the return that belongs to shareholders [1]. Against: profit from operations (£94,840) shows how well the trading activity performed before financing decisions [1]; the gap to profit before tax (£3,500 interest) shows the cost of debenture finance, which ranks before shareholders [1]; the tax line shows how much profit is lost to tax, which managers cannot easily control [1]. Judgement: profit after tax matters most for dividends, but the other lines explain why it changed, so they are useful, not clutter [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluate needs arguments on both sides and a supported conclusion; use the company's own figures as evidence.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividends paid shown in the income statement.
- Debenture interest charged for the amount paid, or for twelve months when the debentures were issued part-way through the year.
- Finance costs deducted before profit from operations.
- Share issue proceeds credited wholly to share capital.
- Interim dividends calculated on the share total before a mid-year issue when the issue came first.
- Profit before tax, not after tax, added to retained earnings.
- Income received in advance and accrued interest left out of other payables.
- Totals in the statement of financial position that do not agree, with no working shown.
