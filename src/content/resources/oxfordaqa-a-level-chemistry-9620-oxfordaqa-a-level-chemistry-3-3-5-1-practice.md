This set practises **3.3.5.1 Oxidation of alcohols** from section 3.3.5 Alcohols of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 2 and in the synoptic Unit 5. Revise first with the [oxidation of alcohols study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-5-1/) or the [oxidation of alcohols revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-5-1-revision-notes/). Take Ar values as H = 1.0, C = 12.0 and O = 16.0.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** Which alcohol is **not** easily oxidised by acidified potassium dichromate(VI)?

A: 2-methylbutan-1-ol

B: 2-methylbutan-2-ol

C: 3-methylbutan-2-ol

D: pentan-3-ol **[1]**

**2.** Classify each alcohol as primary, secondary or tertiary.

**(a)** cyclohexanol **[1]**

**(b)** 2-methylpropan-1-ol **[1]**

**(c)** 3-methylpentan-3-ol **[1]**

**3.** Write an equation, using [O], for the oxidation of pentan-2-ol and name the organic product. **[2]**

**4.** Name the oxidising agent used to oxidise alcohols in this subtopic and state the colour change seen when an alcohol is oxidised. **[2]**

**5.** State what is seen when butanal and butanone are separately warmed with Tollens' reagent. **[2]**

**6.** Give the name and structure of the aldehyde formed when 2,2-dimethylpropan-1-ol is oxidised. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** A student needs a sample of propanal, CH₃CH₂CHO, made from propan-1-ol.

**(a)** Describe the method, including the relative amounts of reactants and the apparatus. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why the method gives propanal rather than propanoic acid. **[2]**

**8.** The boiling points of three compounds are: propanal about 48 °C; propan-1-ol about 97 °C; propanoic acid about 141 °C. Explain why propanal has the lowest boiling point. **[3]**

**9.** Propan-1-ol is oxidised to propanoic acid by acidified dichromate(VI).

**(a)** Write the half-equation for the oxidation of propan-1-ol to propanoic acid. **[1]**

**(b)** The half-equation for dichromate(VI) is Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6e⁻ → 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O. Write the overall equation. **[2]**

**10.** A sample containing 0.150 g of propan-2-ol, CH₃CH(OH)CH₃, is oxidised to propanone by 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ acidified potassium dichromate(VI).

Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 8H⁺ + 3CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ → 2Cr³⁺ + 3CH₃COCH₃ + 7H₂O

Calculate the volume of dichromate(VI) solution, in cm³, that reacts exactly. **[3]**

**11.** Three alcohols, A, B and C, all have the formula C₄H₁₀O.

- A turns acidified dichromate(VI) green. When the product is distilled off as it forms, it gives a silver mirror with Tollens' reagent. A has an unbranched chain.
- B turns acidified dichromate(VI) green, but its product does not react with Tollens' reagent.
- C leaves acidified dichromate(VI) orange.

Identify A, B and C, and give the name of the organic product formed from A and from B. **[5]**

**12.** Explain why acidified potassium dichromate(VI) can distinguish propanal from propanone, but cannot distinguish propanal from propan-1-ol. **[3]**

**13.** 4.40 g of 3-methylbutan-1-ol, (CH₃)₂CHCH₂CH₂OH, is heated under reflux with excess acidified potassium dichromate(VI).

**(a)** Name the organic product and write an equation for its formation using [O]. **[2]**

**(b)** 3.57 g of product is collected. Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]

**2. (a)** Secondary [1]

**(b)** Primary [1]

**(c)** Tertiary [1]

**3.** **CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₂CH₃ + [O] → CH₃COCH₂CH₂CH₃ + H₂O** [1]; **pentan-2-one** [1]

**4.** Acidified potassium dichromate(VI) [1]; orange to green [1]

**5.** Butanal: silver mirror forms [1]; butanone: no silver mirror (no change) [1]

**6.** **2,2-Dimethylpropanal, (CH₃)₃CCHO** [1]

**7. (a)** Use propan-1-ol in excess (limited acidified dichromate) [1]; warm gently in apparatus set up for distillation, with a sloping condenser leading to a collecting vessel [1]; collect the propanal as it distils off, as it forms [1]

**(b)** Propanal has the lowest boiling point in the mixture, so it leaves the flask as soon as it forms [1]; it is no longer in contact with the oxidising agent, so it is not oxidised further to propanoic acid [1]

**8.** Propan-1-ol and propanoic acid both have O–H groups and form hydrogen bonds between their molecules [1]; propanal has no O–H group, so its molecules cannot hydrogen bond to each other; the strongest forces are permanent dipole–dipole forces [1]; these weaker intermolecular forces need less energy to overcome, so propanal boils at a lower temperature [1] **[3]**

**9. (a)** **CH₃CH₂CH₂OH + H₂O → CH₃CH₂COOH + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻** [1]

**(b)** Multiply to balance 12 electrons: 2 × dichromate, 3 × alcohol [1]; **2Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 16H⁺ + 3CH₃CH₂CH₂OH → 4Cr³⁺ + 3CH₃CH₂COOH + 11H₂O** [1]

**10.** n(propan-2-ol) = 0.150 ÷ 60.0 = 2.50 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; n(Cr₂O₇²⁻) = 2.50 × 10⁻³ ÷ 3 = 8.33 × 10⁻⁴ mol [1]; volume = 8.33 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 0.0200 = 0.0417 dm³ = **41.7 cm³** [1] **[3]**

**11.** A is **butan-1-ol** [1]; its product is **butanal** [1]; B is **butan-2-ol** [1]; its product is **butanone** [1]; C is **2-methylpropan-2-ol** (tertiary) [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The silver mirror proves an aldehyde, so A must be primary; "unbranched" rules out 2-methylpropan-1-ol.

**12.** Propanal is oxidised to propanoic acid, so dichromate turns from orange to green; propanone (a ketone) is not oxidised, so it stays orange [1]; propan-1-ol is a primary alcohol and is also oxidised [1]; so propanal and propan-1-ol both turn the dichromate green and give the same observation [1] **[3]**

**13. (a)** **3-Methylbutanoic acid** [1]; **(CH₃)₂CHCH₂CH₂OH + 2[O] → (CH₃)₂CHCH₂COOH + H₂O** [1]

**(b)** n(alcohol) = 4.40 ÷ 88.0 = 0.0500 mol [1]; Mr of acid = 102.0, so n(acid) = 3.57 ÷ 102.0 = 0.0350 mol [1]; yield = 0.0350 ÷ 0.0500 × 100 = **70.0 %** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Classifying by branching instead of by the C–OH carbon.
- One [O] in the direct primary-to-acid equation.
- Distillation described without "as it forms", or without the alcohol in excess.
- Boiling points explained by "weaker bonds".
- Tollens' and Fehling's observations swapped.
