The course is untiered and a calculator may be used on both papers. Questions 5, 6, 7 and 9 test bold **B** statements and are labelled "Paper 2 only".

## Questions

**1.** A student models organisms as cubes. Cube A has sides of 1 mm. Cube B has sides of 5 mm.

**(a)** Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of each cube. **[2]**
**(b)** Use your answers to explain why a large multicellular organism needs a transport system but a unicellular organism does not. **[3]**

**2.** Give **two** differences between transport in xylem and transport in phloem. **[2]**

**3.** Name the substance carried in the plasma from:

**(a)** the liver to the kidneys **[1]**
**(b)** the small intestine to muscle cells **[1]**
**(c)** respiring muscle cells to the lungs. **[1]**

**4.** Explain how the structure of a red blood cell makes it suited to transporting oxygen. **[4]**

**5.** (Paper 2 only) A student uses a potometer with a capillary tube of radius 0.4 mm. In still air the bubble moves 18 mm in 3 minutes. With a fan blowing on the shoot, the bubble moves 45 mm in 3 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the volume of water taken up per minute in still air. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in the rate of water uptake when the fan is switched on. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the fan increases the rate of transpiration. **[2]**
**(d)** State **two** variables the student should keep constant. **[2]**

**6.** (Paper 2 only) A leafy shoot stands in a flask of water with a layer of oil on the surface. The flask and shoot have a mass of 152.60 g at the start and 149.72 g after 8 hours.

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of mass loss in g per hour. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why there is a layer of oil on the water. **[1]**
**(c)** The experiment is repeated in more humid air, with all other conditions the same. Predict and explain the effect on the rate of mass loss. **[2]**

**7.** (Paper 2 only) Explain how water from the soil enters a root hair cell. **[3]**

**8.** Describe how phagocytes and lymphocytes protect the body against a pathogen. **[4]**

**9.** (Paper 2 only) A person receives a vaccine against a virus. Months later, the same virus enters their body. The peak antibody concentration after the vaccine was 16 arbitrary units; after the later infection it was 240 arbitrary units.

**(a)** Calculate how many times greater the second peak is than the first. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the person does not become ill after the later infection. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain how platelets reduce the chance of infection when the skin is cut. **[3]**

**10.** A student's resting heart rate is 76 beats per minute. During a run it rises to 140 beats per minute.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in heart rate. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why heart rate increases during exercise. **[4]**
**(c)** Name the hormone, and the gland that secretes it, that increases heart rate when a person is frightened. **[2]**

**11.** This question is about the heart.

**(a)** Describe the path taken by blood from the vena cava to the aorta. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why the wall of the left ventricle is thicker than the wall of the right ventricle. **[2]**
**(c)** State the function of the valves in the heart. **[1]**
**(d)** A coronary artery becomes narrowed. Explain how this could lead to a heart attack, and give **two** lifestyle factors that increase the risk. **[4]**

**12.** This question is about blood vessels and the circulation.

**(a)** Compare the structure of an artery with the structure of a vein. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how the structure of a capillary suits its function. **[2]**
**(c)** Name the blood vessel that carries blood to the kidneys, and the vessel that carries blood from the gut to the liver. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** A: SA = 6 mm², V = 1 mm³, ratio **6 : 1** [1]; B: SA = 150 mm², V = 125 mm³, ratio **1.2 : 1** [1]
**(b)** The larger organism has a smaller surface area to volume ratio [1]; its inner cells are a long way from the surface, so diffusion would be too slow to supply them [1]; a unicellular organism has a large ratio and short diffusion distance, so diffusion across its membrane is enough [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each ratio needs both the area and the volume to be right; an answer that only says "B is bigger" without linking size to diffusion distance or rate does not get the explanation marks.

**2.** Xylem carries water and mineral ions; phloem carries sucrose and amino acids [1]; xylem flow is upwards only, while phloem moves substances up and down the plant [1]
*Examiner insight:* A difference must state both sides; "phloem carries sugar" alone does not score unless you also say what xylem carries.

**3. (a)** **Urea** [1]
**(b)** **Glucose** (or amino acids / digested food) [1]
**(c)** **Carbon dioxide** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c), "oxygen" is a common wrong answer: oxygen travels in red blood cells and moves towards muscles, not away from them.

**4.** Biconcave shape [1]; gives a large surface area to volume ratio for fast diffusion of oxygen [1]; no nucleus, so more space for haemoglobin [1]; haemoglobin combines with oxygen to form oxyhaemoglobin [1]
*Examiner insight:* Features and explanations are marked separately, so a list of three features with no reasons scores half the marks at most.

**5. (a)** π × 0.4² × 18 = 9.048 mm³ in 3 minutes [1]; 9.048 ÷ 3 = **3.02 mm³ per minute** [1]
**(b)** (45 − 18) ÷ 18 × 100 [1] = **150%** [1]
**(c)** Moving air carries water vapour away from the leaf surface, keeping the water vapour concentration gradient steep [1]; so water vapour diffuses out of the stomata faster [1]
**(d)** Any two: temperature [1]; light intensity [1]; humidity; the same shoot
*Examiner insight:* In (b) the distances can be used directly because the tube radius is the same; dividing by the new value (45) instead of the original (18) loses the answer mark.

**6. (a)** 152.60 − 149.72 = 2.88 g lost [1]; 2.88 ÷ 8 = **0.36 g per hour** [1]
**(b)** To stop water evaporating from the water surface, so all the loss is from the shoot [1]
**(c)** The rate decreases [1]; the more humid air means the water vapour concentration gradient between leaf and air is smaller and less water vapour diffuses out [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Predict and explain" carries a mark for the direction and a separate one for the reason; a reason without a stated prediction loses the first mark.

**7.** Cell sap in the root hair cell is more concentrated than the soil water [1]; water moves into the cell by osmosis [1]; across the partially permeable cell membrane, from a dilute to a more concentrated solution [1]
*Examiner insight:* Saying water enters by "diffusion" or "active transport" is not credited; the mark needs the word osmosis.

**8.** Phagocytes engulf (ingest) pathogens [1]; and digest them [1]; lymphocytes release antibodies [1]; which are specific to the pathogen's antigens [1]
*Examiner insight:* Two separate points are needed for each cell type; "white blood cells kill pathogens" without naming what each type does scores nothing.

**9. (a)** 240 ÷ 16 = **15 times** [1]
**(b)** The vaccine caused lymphocytes to produce memory cells [1]; on infection, memory cells make antibodies sooner and faster [1]; and in greater quantity, so the virus is destroyed before symptoms develop [1]
**(c)** Platelets trigger conversion of soluble fibrinogen to insoluble fibrin [1]; fibrin threads form a mesh that traps red blood cells to form a clot [1]; the clot seals the wound so micro-organisms cannot enter [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), saying the vaccine "contains antibodies" or "kills the virus" contradicts the mechanism and loses the first mark.

**10. (a)** (140 − 76) ÷ 76 × 100 [1] = **84.2%** [1]
**(b)** Muscles respire faster and need more oxygen and glucose [1]; more carbon dioxide is produced and is detected by the brain [1]; nerve impulses make the heart beat faster [1]; so more blood reaches the muscles, delivering oxygen and glucose and removing carbon dioxide faster [1]
**(c)** Adrenaline [1]; adrenal gland [1]
*Examiner insight:* "The heart beats faster to get more oxygen" is too vague for full marks; say that oxygen is needed for aerobic respiration in the muscles.

**11. (a)** Vena cava → right atrium → right ventricle [1]; → pulmonary artery → lungs [1]; → pulmonary vein → left atrium [1]; → left ventricle → aorta [1]
**(b)** The left ventricle pumps blood all round the body [1]; so it must generate a higher pressure than the right ventricle, which pumps only to the lungs [1]
**(c)** To prevent the backflow of blood [1]
**(d)** Fatty deposits narrow the coronary artery, so less blood and oxygen reach the heart muscle [1]; heart muscle cannot respire aerobically and cells die, causing a heart attack [1]; any two of: smoking, diet high in saturated fat or salt, lack of exercise [1] [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), one wrong or missing structure loses the mark for that stage of the route, so write the full chain in order with no gaps.

**12. (a)** Artery has a thicker wall than a vein [1]; artery has more muscle and elastic tissue [1]; vein has a wider lumen and valves [1]
**(b)** Wall is one cell thick, giving a short diffusion distance [1]; so substances such as oxygen and glucose diffuse quickly between blood and tissue cells [1]
**(c)** Renal artery [1]; hepatic portal vein [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Compare" needs a point about both vessels in each comparison; writing "arteries are thick" with no reference to veins earns limited credit.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculating surface area to volume ratio with the area of only one face.
- Defining transpiration without the word **evaporation**, or saying water (not water vapour) leaves through the stomata.
- Explaining a potometer result without referring to the concentration gradient of water vapour.
- Saying a potometer measures transpiration exactly: it measures water uptake.
- Giving "food" or "nutrients" for what xylem carries.
- Mixing up phagocytes and lymphocytes, or saying antibodies "eat" pathogens.
- Saying vaccines contain antibodies.
- Writing "the heart pumps faster to get more energy" with no link to oxygen or respiration.
- Naming the hepatic vein instead of the hepatic portal vein for blood from the gut.
- Dividing by the new value instead of the original value in percentage change calculations.
