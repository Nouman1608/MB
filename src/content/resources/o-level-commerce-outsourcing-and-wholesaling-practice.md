## Section A

**1.** Define contract manufacturing. **[1]**

**2.** State **two** reasons why outsourcing has grown. **[2]**

**3.** State **three** functions a wholesaler performs for a manufacturer. **[3]**

**4.** What is a cash and carry wholesaler? **[2]**

## Section B

**5.** A sportswear company in Lahore has always made and delivered its own products. It is considering outsourcing production to a contract manufacturer and deliveries to a contract logistics firm.

**(a)** Explain **two** benefits to the company of using contract manufacturing. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain **two** problems it might face. **[4]**
**(c)** Explain why the growth of ecommerce has increased the use of contract logistics. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **three** services a wholesaler provides to a small retailer, and why each is valuable. **[6]**

**7.** Explain how a voluntary chain operates and how it benefits both the wholesaler and the independent retailer. **[6]**

**8.** "The wholesaler is no longer needed in modern commerce." Discuss this statement. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** When a business pays another firm to make its goods, or parts of them, to its own specification [1].

**2.** Any two: lower costs; access to specialist expertise or equipment; focus on core activities; flexibility to change output; growth of ecommerce; easier coordination through digital communication [1] [1].

**3.** Any three: buys in bulk; stores goods; bears risk; pays promptly; distributes to many retailers; provides market information [1] [1] [1].

**4.** A wholesale warehouse where retailers select goods, pay for them immediately [1] and transport them themselves, with no credit or delivery provided [1].

**5. (a)** Any two, 2 marks each: **lower costs** — the contractor's scale or lower wages make each garment cheaper, increasing profit [1] [1]; **expertise** — the contractor has specialist machines and skilled workers, which may improve quality [1] [1]; **focus** — the company can spend its time and capital on design and marketing its brand [1] [1]; **flexibility** — it can order more for busy seasons without building a bigger factory [1] [1].
**(b)** Any two, 2 marks each: **less control over quality** — faulty products would damage the brand [1] [1]; **dependence** — if the contractor is late or closes, the company cannot supply customers [1] [1]; **confidentiality** — new designs could be copied [1] [1]; **reputation** — poor working conditions at the contractor could cause bad publicity [1] [1].
**(c)** Online sales mean many small orders sent to individual homes [1]; this needs warehouses, picking and fast delivery that small firms cannot provide cheaply [1], so they pay specialist logistics firms that have the systems and vehicles already [1].

**6.** Any three, 2 marks each: **breaking bulk** — the retailer can buy small quantities, so it does not tie up capital or need large storage space [1] [1]; **credit** — the retailer can pay after selling the goods, helping its cash flow [1] [1]; **wide range** — many manufacturers' goods in one place save time and give customers choice [1] [1]; **delivery** — saves the retailer transport costs [1] [1]; **advice** — information on new products and displays helps sales [1] [1].

**7.** A group of independent retailers agree to buy most of their stock from one wholesaler [1] and trade under a shared name with joint advertising and promotions [1]. **Wholesaler:** gains regular, guaranteed orders from loyal customers [1], so it can compete with direct buying by supermarkets [1]. **Retailer:** pays lower prices because of the group's bulk buying [1] and benefits from shared branding while remaining independently owned [1].

**8.** Levels-based. **For the statement:** large supermarket chains buy direct from manufacturers and have their own warehouses [1]; ecommerce lets manufacturers sell direct to consumers [1]; cutting out the wholesaler removes its profit margin, lowering prices [1]. **Against:** small retailers cannot buy in bulk or deal with many manufacturers, so they rely on breaking bulk, credit and delivery [1]; manufacturers still benefit from storage, risk-bearing and distribution to thousands of small shops [1]; independent wholesalers have adapted through cash and carry and voluntary chains [1]. **Judgement:** the wholesaler is less needed by large retailers but remains important for small shops and manufacturers selling widely [1] [1].

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between contract manufacturing and contract logistics?** Contract manufacturing outsources making the goods; contract logistics outsources storing, transporting and delivering them.

**Why might a manufacturer bypass the wholesaler?** If it sells to large retailers that buy in bulk, or sells direct to consumers online, it may save the wholesaler's profit margin.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating a benefit of outsourcing without explaining the effect on the business.
- Writing wholesaler functions without saying whether they help the manufacturer or the retailer.
- Confusing a voluntary chain with a franchise.
- Giving a one-sided answer to a "discuss" question.
