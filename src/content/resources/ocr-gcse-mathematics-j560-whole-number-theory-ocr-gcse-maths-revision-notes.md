## Key terms

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Factor (divisor) | Divides the number exactly | 7 is a factor of 91 |
| Multiple | Result of multiplying the number by a whole number | 91 is a multiple of 7 |
| Prime | Exactly two factors, 1 and itself | 2, 23, 409 |
| Composite | More than two factors | 391 = 17 × 23 |
| Common factor | A factor of each number | 5 is a common factor of 35 and 50 |
| Common multiple | A multiple of each number | 70 is a common multiple of 35 and 10 |
| HCF | Highest common factor | HCF(35, 50) = 5 |
| LCM | Lowest common multiple | LCM(35, 10) = 70 |
| Square, cube | n × n, n × n × n | 49 = 7², 343 = 7³ |
| Root | Undoes a power | √49 = 7, ∛343 = 7 |
| Index (power) | How many times the base is a factor | In 2⁴, the index is 4 |

**Must know:** primes under 20 are 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19. The squares 1 to 225 (up to 15²) and the cubes 1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 1 000.

## Method boxes

**All factors of n.**

```
Pair from 1 upwards: 1 × n, 2 × ?, 3 × ?, ...
Stop when the pairs meet or repeat.
A square number has an odd number of factors.
```

**Is n prime?**

```
Find the primes up to √n.
Use divisibility tests (2, 3, 5), then divide by 7, 11, 13, ...
No prime divides n  →  n is prime.
One does            →  give the factor pair as evidence.
```

**Prime factorisation.**

```
Divide by the smallest prime that fits, or split into any factor pair.
Repeat until every number is prime.
Collect repeats into powers; write primes in ascending order.
Multiply back to check.
```

**HCF and LCM from factorisations.**

```
HCF: shared primes, LOWEST power of each
LCM: every prime that appears, HIGHEST power of each
Venn: overlap = HCF; everything in both circles = LCM
Check: HCF × LCM = product of the two numbers
```

## Divisibility tests

| By | Test |
|---|---|
| 2 | Even last digit |
| 3 | Digit sum divisible by 3 |
| 4 | Last two digits divisible by 4 |
| 5 | Ends in 0 or 5 |
| 6 | Passes the tests for 2 and 3 |
| 9 | Digit sum divisible by 9 |
| 10 | Ends in 0 |

## Using a prime factorisation

| Question | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Is it a square? | Every power even |
| Is it a cube? | Every power a multiple of 3 |
| Square root | Halve every power |
| Cube root | Divide every power by 3 |
| Smallest multiplier to make a square | Multiply by one more of each prime with an odd power |
| Is it a multiple of k? | k's prime factors all appear, with at least the same powers |

Reminder: 2⁴ × 3² = 144 and √144 = 2² × 3 = 12.

## HCF or LCM?

| Wording | Use |
|---|---|
| Largest possible equal groups; longest equal pieces; greatest number of identical packs | HCF |
| When will they next happen together; smallest square from tiles; equal numbers of two items sold in different pack sizes | LCM |

**Reverse problem:** for two numbers, product = HCF × LCM. So other number = HCF × LCM ÷ known number.

## Place value reminder

From one fact you can write related facts. If 36 × 54 = 1 944, then 360 × 54 = 19 440 and 1 944 ÷ 54 = 36. Each × 10 on a factor moves the digits of the product one place left.

## Exam traps

- **1 is not prime**, and 2 is the only even prime.
- **Digit sums do not test for 7.** Divide instead.
- **Composite numbers left in a "prime factorisation"**, such as 2³ × 9.
- **Power notation mixed up:** 2³ means 2 × 2 × 2, not 2 × 3.
- **Different bases combined:** 2³ × 3² is not 6⁵.
- **HCF and LCM swapped:** the HCF is never bigger than the smaller number; the LCM is never smaller than the larger number.
- **Tile questions:** the LCM gives the side of the square; the number of tiles needs one more step.
- **Listing too few multiples** when finding an LCM by listing: keep going until a common one appears.

## Quick self-test

1. List all the factors of 150.
2. Show that 323 is not prime.
3. Decide whether 307 is prime. Show your method.
4. Write 1 400 as a product of prime factors in power notation.
5. 2 025 = 3⁴ × 5². Explain why 2 025 is a square number and find √2 025.
6. Find the smallest whole number that 1 400 can be multiplied by to give a square number.
7. Find the HCF and LCM of 2³ × 3² × 5 and 2 × 3³ × 7.
8. Find the HCF and LCM of 72 and 120.
9. Two lights flash together. One flashes every 16 seconds, the other every 24 seconds. After how many seconds do they next flash together?
10. Ribbons of 96 cm and 156 cm are cut into pieces of equal length, as long as possible, with none left over. Find the length of each piece and the total number of pieces.
11. Two numbers have HCF 4 and LCM 120. One of them is 24. Find the other.
12. Given that 36 × 54 = 1 944, write down 360 × 540.

### Answers

1. **1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15, 25, 30, 50, 75, 150**.
2. 323 = **17 × 19**, so it has factors other than 1 and itself.
3. 17² = 289 and 18² = 324, so test primes up to 17. 307 is odd, its digit sum is 10, it does not end in 0 or 5; 7 × 43 = 301, 11 × 27 = 297, 13 × 23 = 299, 17 × 18 = 306, each leaving a remainder. **307 is prime.**
4. **2³ × 5² × 7**.
5. Both powers (4 and 2) are even. √2 025 = 3² × 5 = **45**.
6. 2³ × 5² × 7 has odd powers of 2 and 7, so multiply by 2 × 7 = **14**. (1 400 × 14 = 19 600 = 140².)
7. HCF = 2 × 3² = **18**; LCM = 2³ × 3³ × 5 × 7 = **7 560**.
8. 72 = 2³ × 3², 120 = 2³ × 3 × 5. HCF = 2³ × 3 = **24**; LCM = 2³ × 3² × 5 = **360**.
9. LCM(16, 24) = **48 seconds**.
10. HCF(96, 156) = **12 cm**; 96 ÷ 12 + 156 ÷ 12 = 8 + 13 = **21 pieces**.
11. 4 × 120 ÷ 24 = **20**. Check: HCF(20, 24) = 4 and LCM(20, 24) = 120.
12. Each factor is 10 times larger, so the product is 100 times larger: **194 400**.
