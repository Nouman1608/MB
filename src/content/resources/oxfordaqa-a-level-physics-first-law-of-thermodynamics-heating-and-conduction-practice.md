## Questions

**1.**
**(a)** Define the internal energy of a body. **[2]**
**(b)** A gas is compressed very quickly in a well-insulated cylinder. State what happens to its internal energy. **[1]**

**2.**
**(a)** A gas receives 130 J of energy by heating and does 45 J of work on its surroundings. Calculate the change in its internal energy. **[2]**
**(b)** A different gas loses 60 J by heating while the surroundings do 25 J of work on it. Calculate the change in its internal energy. **[2]**

**3.** A copper block of mass 0.45 kg at 92 °C is lowered into 0.30 kg of water at 18 °C in an insulated container. The specific heat capacity of copper is 385 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹ and of water is 4180 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹.
**(a)** Calculate the energy the copper would lose in cooling to 25 °C. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the final common temperature, assuming no energy is lost to the surroundings. **[4]**

**4.** A heater of power 150 W melts 0.120 kg of ice at 0 °C. The specific latent heat of fusion of ice is 3.34 × 10⁵ J kg⁻¹. Assume all the heater's energy goes into the ice.
**(a)** Calculate the energy needed to melt the ice. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the time taken. **[2]**
**(c)** A thermometer in the ice and water stays at 0 °C throughout. Explain this in terms of the energies of the molecules. **[2]**

**5.** A radiator receives 0.065 kg of steam at 100 °C. The steam condenses and the water then cools to 45 °C. The specific latent heat of vaporisation of water is 2.26 × 10⁶ J kg⁻¹. Calculate the total energy released. **[4]**

**6.** A water heater has a power of 7.5 kW. Of this, 92% reaches the water. Water flows through it at a steady 0.0900 kg s⁻¹. The specific heat capacity of water is 4180 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹.
**(a)** Calculate the temperature rise of the water. **[3]**
**(b)** The density of water is 1000 kg m⁻³. Calculate the volume of water heated in one minute, in litres. **[1]**

**7.** A student determines the specific heat capacity of a metal block of mass 0.500 kg by an electrical method. The heater runs at 11.6 V and 3.40 A for 300 s. The block warms from 21.5 °C to 48.0 °C.
**(a)** Calculate the electrical energy supplied. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the specific heat capacity of the metal. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the student's value is likely to be larger than the true value, and suggest one improvement to the procedure. **[2]**
**(d)** Each temperature reading has an uncertainty of ±0.5 K. Calculate the percentage uncertainty in the temperature rise. **[2]**

**8.** A uniform aluminium rod has length 0.60 m and diameter 12 mm. Its ends are held at 100 °C and 20 °C. The thermal conductivity of aluminium is 205 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹.
**(a)** Calculate the rate of energy transfer along the rod in the steady state. **[3]**
**(b)** An identical rod is made of a steel with thermal conductivity 50 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹. Calculate the rate of energy transfer for the same end temperatures. **[1]**
**(c)** State one condition needed for the rate in part (a) to be correct for a real rod. **[1]**

**9.** A glass pane has area 1.4 m² and thickness 6.0 mm. The thermal conductivity of the glass is 1.0 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹. The inner face is at 21 °C and the outer face is at 3 °C.
**(a)** Calculate the rate of energy transfer through the pane. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the U-value of the pane. **[1]**

**10.** A small house has these parallel surfaces. The inside is at 20 °C and the outside at 2 °C.

| Surface | Area / m² | U-value / W m⁻² K⁻¹ |
|---|---|---|
| Walls | 38 | 0.30 |
| Roof | 52 | 0.18 |
| Windows | 6.5 | 2.8 |
| Door | 2.0 | 1.6 |

**(a)** Calculate the total rate of energy loss through the surfaces. **[4]**
**(b)** Calculate the energy lost in one day, in kilowatt-hours. **[2]**
**(c)** The windows are replaced with ones of U-value 1.4 W m⁻² K⁻¹. Calculate the reduction in the total rate of energy loss. **[2]**

**11.** A cylindrical hot-water tank holds 120 kg of water at 60 °C in a room at 18 °C. Its surface area is 1.8 m² and the U-value of its lagging is 0.90 W m⁻² K⁻¹. The specific heat capacity of water is 4180 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹.
**(a)** Calculate the initial rate of energy loss from the tank. **[3]**
**(b)** Estimate the time for the water temperature to fall by 1.0 K at this rate. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the next 1.0 K fall takes longer than the first. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The sum of the kinetic energies [1] and potential energies of the particles, which are randomly distributed [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** It **increases**, because work is done on the gas and almost no energy leaves by heating (ΔU = W) [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Name both energies and the random distribution. A definition with only kinetic energy is incomplete.

**2. (a)** Q = +130 J and W = −45 J, because the gas does the work [1]. ΔU = 130 − 45 = **+85 J** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Q = −60 J and W = +25 J [1]. ΔU = −60 + 25 = **−35 J** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the sign of Q and of W before adding. A bare answer with a wrong sign leaves little to credit.

**3. (a)** Δθ = 92 − 25 = 67 K [1]. Q = 0.45 × 385 × 67 = **1.2 × 10⁴ J** (11 608 J) [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Energy lost by copper = energy gained by water [1]. 0.45 × 385 × (92 − θ) = 0.30 × 4180 × (θ − 18), that is 173.25 (92 − θ) = 1254 (θ − 18) [1]. 15 939 + 22 572 = (173.25 + 1254) θ, so θ = 38 511 / 1427.25 [1]. θ = **27 °C** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* State the energy balance in words first. The final temperature must lie between 18 °C and 92 °C.

**4. (a)** Q = ml = 0.120 × 3.34 × 10⁵ [1] = **4.0 × 10⁴ J** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** t = Q / P = 40 080 / 150 [1] = **2.7 × 10² s** (267 s) [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The energy supplied increases the potential energy of the molecules as they move apart [1]. Their average kinetic energy does not change, so the temperature is constant [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Say which energy changes and which does not. Mentioning only "breaking bonds" leaves out the kinetic energy.

**5.** Condensation stage: Q = ml = 0.065 × 2.26 × 10⁶ [1] = 146 900 J [1]. Cooling stage: Q = mcΔθ = 0.065 × 4180 × 55 [1] = 14 944 J. Total = 146 900 + 14 944 = 161 844 J = **1.6 × 10⁵ J** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Split a mixed question into stages and add them. Using mcΔθ throughout gives a far smaller value.

**6. (a)** Useful power = 0.92 × 7500 = 6900 W [1]. Δθ = P / ((Δm/Δt) c) = 6900 / (0.0900 × 4180) [1] = **18 K** (18.3 K) [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Mass in 60 s = 0.0900 × 60 = 5.4 kg, so volume = 5.4 / 1000 = 5.4 × 10⁻³ m³ = **5.4 litres** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Apply the efficiency to the power before dividing by the flow rate and specific heat capacity.

**7. (a)** P = VI = 11.6 × 3.40 = 39.44 W [1]. E = Pt = 39.44 × 300 = **1.18 × 10⁴ J** (11 832 J) [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Δθ = 48.0 − 21.5 = 26.5 K [1]. c = E / (mΔθ) = 11 832 / (0.500 × 26.5) = **893 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Some of the electrical energy is lost to the surroundings, so the block gains less energy than VIt and Δθ is smaller than it should be [1]. Improvement: lag the block or fit a lid [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Uncertainty in Δθ = 0.5 + 0.5 = 1.0 K [1]. Percentage = 1.0 / 26.5 × 100 = **3.8%** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the direction of the error to the reason: less energy reaches the block, so Δθ is too small and c too large.

**8. (a)** A = π (0.0060)² = 1.13 × 10⁻⁴ m² [1]. Rate = kAΔθ/L = 205 × 1.13 × 10⁻⁴ × 80 / 0.60 [1] = **3.1 W** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Rate scales with k: 3.09 × 50 / 205 = **0.75 W** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** The rod must be in a steady state, with its sides lagged so that energy only passes along the length [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* The diameter is given, so halve it before finding the area, and convert millimetres to metres.

**9. (a)** Δθ = 18 K and L = 0.0060 m [1]. Rate = 1.0 × 1.4 × 18 / 0.0060 = **4.2 × 10³ W** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** U = k/L = 1.0 / 0.0060 = **1.7 × 10² W m⁻² K⁻¹** (167) [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert the thickness to metres first. Units of W m⁻² K⁻¹ are needed with the value.

**10. (a)** Rate = (ΣUA) Δθ with Δθ = 18 K [1]. UA values: walls 11.4, roof 9.36, windows 18.2, door 3.2 W K⁻¹ [1]. Sum = 42.16 W K⁻¹ [1]. Rate = 42.16 × 18 = **7.6 × 10² W** (759 W) [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** Energy = 0.759 kW × 24 h [1] = **18 kWh** (18.2 kWh) [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Reduction = 6.5 × (2.8 − 1.4) × 18 [1] = **1.6 × 10² W** (164 W) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each surface's UA separately before adding. The windows give the largest share of the total from the smallest area.

**11. (a)** Δθ = 60 − 18 = 42 K [1]. UA = 0.90 × 1.8 = 1.62 W K⁻¹ [1]. Rate = 1.62 × 42 = **68 W** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Energy for a 1.0 K fall = 120 × 4180 × 1.0 = 501 600 J [1]. t = 501 600 / 68 = **7.4 × 10³ s** (about 2.0 hours) [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** As the water cools the temperature difference with the room falls [1], so the rate of energy loss falls and each 1.0 K step takes longer [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (c), refer to the temperature difference, not just "less heat".

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the wrong sign for W when a gas expands.
- Saying kinetic energy rises during a change of state.
- Leaving thicknesses in millimetres or masses in grams.
- Using mcΔθ for a stage where the temperature is constant, or ml where it changes.
- Using the diameter as the radius when finding the area of a rod.
- Adding the U-values themselves instead of adding the products UA.
