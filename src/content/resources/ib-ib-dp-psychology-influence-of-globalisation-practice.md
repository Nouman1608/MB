> **These are original questions written for Marlbridge** on the HL extension "the influence of
> globalization on individual attitudes, identities and behaviour" (IB DP Psychology, first assessment
> 2019). They are not past-paper questions. Question 13 uses the 22-mark essay tariff; the others use
> practice tariffs. The survey in Question 9 is invented for practice.

## Section A

**1.** Define "globalisation" as used in psychology. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between acculturation after migration and globalisation-based acculturation. **[2]**

**3.** Outline what Arnett (2002) meant by "bicultural identity". **[2]**

**4.** Outline what Arnett (2002) meant by "identity confusion". **[2]**

**5.** Explain why Becker et al. (2002) is described as a natural experiment. **[2]**

**6.** State one reason why a questionnaire developed in one country may not be valid in another. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Explain how social cognitive theory could account for the findings of Becker et al. (2002). **[4]**

**8.** Explain one strength and one limitation of Buchan et al. (2009) as evidence about the influence of globalisation on behaviour. **[4]**

**9.** Invented survey: 300 teenagers in one city complete a questionnaire. Those who report more hours per week of international online media also report a stronger "global citizen" identity (r = +0.42).
(a) Describe the relationship shown.
(b) Explain why the researcher cannot conclude that online media causes a global identity.
(c) Suggest how the study could be redesigned to give stronger evidence about change over time. **[6]**

**10.** Outline how the findings of Chen, Benet-Martínez and Bond (2008) relate to Berry's acculturation model. **[3]**

**11.** A student writes: "Globalisation is making everyone the same." Using research, explain why this claim is an oversimplification. **[4]**

**12.** Explain why a researcher studying the effects of globalisation in a community should consider an emic approach. **[3]**

**13.** Discuss the influence of globalisation on individual attitudes, identities and behaviour. **[22]**

## Answers

**1.** The process by which societies become more interconnected through trade, migration, media and digital communication **[1]**, studied in psychology for its effects on individuals' attitudes, identities and behaviour **[1]**.

**2.** Acculturation after migration follows moving to live in a different culture **[1]**; globalisation-based acculturation happens through contact with global culture at home, without moving **[1]**.

**3.** Holding two identities at once **[1]**: one rooted in the local culture and family, the other linked to a global culture spread through media, technology and English **[1]**.

**4.** A state in which a young person feels secure in neither the local nor the global identity **[1]**, more likely where the two cultures are very different **[1]**.

**5.** The independent variable, the arrival of television in a Fijian province in 1995, occurred naturally **[1]**; the researchers did not manipulate it or randomly allocate participants **[1]**.

**6.** Items may carry different meanings in a different culture, so the tool may not measure the same construct (an etic measure) **[1]**.

**7.** Television characters acted as models with high status and apparent success **[1]**. Girls paid attention to their appearance and retained it **[1]**. Interviews showed girls linked slimness to success, a form of vicarious reinforcement **[1]**, which motivated them to imitate dieting and other weight-control behaviour **[1]**.

**8.** Strength: it measured real behaviour, decisions about money, using the same procedure in six countries, so the findings are not limited to one culture **[2]**. Limitation: a public goods game is artificial, and exposure to globalisation may be confounded with education or income, so it is unclear which variable explains the contributions **[2]**.

**9.** (a) A moderate positive correlation **[1]**: teenagers who use more international online media tend to report a stronger global identity **[1]**.
(b) Correlation does not show causation **[1]**: teenagers who already see themselves as global citizens may seek out international media, or a third variable such as family income or English proficiency may explain both **[1]**.
(c) A longitudinal design, measuring the same teenagers' media use and identity on several occasions **[1]**, so that changes in identity can be related to earlier media use **[1]**.

**10.** Berry's model links integration, keeping one's own culture while taking part in another, with better well-being **[1]**. Chen and colleagues found that bicultural identity integration was associated with better adjustment **[1]**, extending the same idea to people who experience a second culture through globalisation rather than migration **[1]**.

**11.** Arnett (2002) argued that many young people develop a bicultural identity, keeping a local identity alongside a global one, rather than replacing it **[1]**. He also described self-selected cultures, including a return to traditional or religious groups **[1]**. Becker et al. (2002) showed change in one behaviour among some girls, not uniform change across a society **[1]**, and Buchan et al. (2009) found that the effect of globalisation varied with each individual's exposure **[1]**.

**12.** An emic approach studies a culture from within, using concepts that are meaningful to its members **[1]**. Changes that an outside researcher labels as "Westernisation" or as harmful may have different meanings locally, for example a new style of dress or eating **[1]**. Interviews or measures developed with local people would make the findings more credible and reduce researcher bias **[1]**.

**13.** Marked with the essay criteria:
- **A Focus:** defines globalisation and states that its influence varies across attitudes, identities and behaviour **[2]**.
- **B Knowledge and understanding:** bicultural identity, identity confusion and globalisation-based acculturation, with links to social identity and social cognitive theory **[6]**.
- **C Use of research:** two or three of Arnett (2002), Chen, Benet-Martínez and Bond (2008), Becker et al. (2002) and Buchan et al. (2009), explained and linked **[6]**.
- **D Critical thinking:** confounding variables in natural experiments; correlational data; etic measures; problems defining globalisation; a balance of harmful and positive effects; a conclusion about how far the influence is shown **[6]**.
- **E Clarity and organisation:** a planned argument that returns to the question **[2]**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Question 2:** defining acculturation without saying where the contact comes from.
- **Question 7:** naming social cognitive theory without its processes.
- **Question 9(b):** writing "correlation is not causation" without an alternative explanation.
- **Question 12:** defining emic without applying it to globalisation research.
- **Question 13:** spending most of the essay on Berry's core model rather than globalisation research.
