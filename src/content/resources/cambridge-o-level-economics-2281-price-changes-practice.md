This set tests section 2.6 **Price changes** of the Cambridge O Level Economics 2281 syllabus for examination in 2026: the causes of price changes and their consequences for equilibrium price and sales. All markets and figures are invented. Teaching is in the [price changes study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-2281-price-changes/) and the [price changes revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-2281-price-changes-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which event would raise the equilibrium price of wheat and reduce the quantity sold?

A: A rise in the price of rice, a substitute for wheat

B: A drought in wheat-growing regions

C: A subsidy to wheat farmers

D: A fall in the price of bread-making machines

**[1]**

**2.** Butter and margarine are substitutes. The price of margarine falls. What happens in the market for butter?

A: Demand falls; price and sales fall

B: Demand rises; price and sales rise

C: Supply falls; price rises and sales fall

D: Supply rises; price falls and sales rise

**[1]**

**3.** For each event, state which curve shifts in the market for bicycles and in which direction.

**(a)** A government campaign encourages cycling to work. **[1]**

**(b)** The price of steel used to make bicycle frames rises. **[1]**

**(c)** Bus fares are cut sharply. **[1]**

**4.** The daily market for cinema tickets in Town C is shown by Qd = 1,000 − 50P and Qs = 100P − 500. A new streaming service becomes cheaper, and demand for cinema tickets falls by 150 at every price.

**(a)** Calculate the original equilibrium price and quantity. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the new equilibrium price and quantity. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the percentage change in the ticket price. **[1]**

**(d)** Calculate the change in cinemas' total revenue. **[2]**

**5.** A market starts with Qd = 260 − 2P and Qs = 3P − 40.

**(a)** In case X, demand rises by 50 and supply by 100 at every price. In case Y, demand rises by 100 and supply by 25. Calculate the new equilibrium price and quantity in each case. **[4]**

**(b)** Use your answers to explain why the effect on price is uncertain when both curves shift to the right. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** Explain, with the help of a demand and supply diagram described in words, the effect on the market for fish of a storm that damages many fishing boats. **[5]**

**7.** A government places a tax on each litre of petrol sold. Explain how this could affect the equilibrium price and sales of large cars. **[4]**

**8.** Explain why a general rise in wages might shift the demand curve in one market and the supply curve in another. **[4]**

**9.** Explain how a fall in demand for printed newspapers could affect workers in the paper industry. **[3]**

**10.** In one year, incomes rise and new farming technology spreads in Country F. Analyse the likely effect on the price and sales of vegetables. **[4]**

**11.** A new factory opens in a town and employs many people. Discuss whether the price of rented housing in the town will rise. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. A drought decreases supply, so price rises and sales fall. A raises demand (price and sales up), C and D increase supply (price down). **[1]**

**2.** A [1]. Cheaper margarine draws buyers away, so demand for butter shifts left, lowering price and sales. **[1]**

**3. (a)** Demand shifts right (increases). [1]

**(b)** Supply shifts left (decreases), because costs rise. [1]

**(c)** Demand shifts left (decreases), because buses are a substitute that is now cheaper. [1]

**4. (a)** 1,000 − 50P = 100P − 500, so 1,500 = 150P and P = $10 [1]. Q = 1,000 − 500 = 500 tickets [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** New demand: Qd = 850 − 50P. 850 − 50P = 100P − 500, so 1,350 = 150P and P = $9 [1]. Q = 850 − 450 = 400 tickets [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** (9 − 10) ÷ 10 × 100 = −10% [1]. **[1]**

**(d)** Before: 10 × 500 = $5,000 [1]. After: 9 × 400 = $3,600, a fall of $1,400 [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Check each equilibrium by putting the price into both equations. If the two quantities differ, there is a slip.

**5. (a)** Original: 300 = 5P, P = $60, Q = 140. Case X: 310 − 2P = 3P + 60, so P = $50 [1] and Q = 210 [1]. Case Y: 360 − 2P = 3P − 15, so P = $75 [1] and Q = 210 [1]. **[4]**

**(b)** In both cases sales rose, from 140 to 210, because both curves moved right [1]. Price fell in case X, where the supply shift was larger, and rose in case Y, where the demand shift was larger, so the price result depends on the relative size of the shifts [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Fewer boats can fish, so less fish is offered at every price: supply decreases [1]. On a diagram with price on the vertical axis and quantity on the horizontal axis, S1 shifts left to S2 while D is unchanged [1]. At the original price P1 there is now a shortage [1]. The price rises, causing demand to contract along D [1], until a new equilibrium at a higher price P2 and lower sales Q2 [1]. **[5]**

**7.** The tax raises the cost of selling petrol, so the supply of petrol decreases and its price rises [1]. Petrol is a complement to cars, especially large cars that use a lot of it [1], so running such a car becomes dearer and demand for large cars shifts left [1]. At the old price there is a surplus, so the price of large cars falls and sales fall [1]. **[4]**

**8.** Wages are income to workers, so a general rise gives buyers more to spend [1], shifting demand for normal goods such as meals out to the right [1]. Wages are also a cost to firms, so in a labour-heavy industry such as hairdressing the cost of each service rises [1], which shifts supply of that service to the left [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Demand for paper is derived from demand for newspapers, so falling newspaper sales reduce demand for paper [1]. The price and sales of paper fall [1], so paper mills need fewer workers, and some may lose their jobs or see lower pay [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Rising incomes increase demand for vegetables, shifting demand right, which on its own raises price and sales [1]. New technology lowers costs, shifting supply right, which on its own lowers price and raises sales [1]. Sales are certain to rise, since both shifts increase them [1]. The price could rise, fall or stay the same, depending on whether the demand shift or the supply shift is larger [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Rise: new workers move to the town and need housing, so demand for rented housing increases [1]; housing supply cannot rise quickly because building takes time, so there is a shortage at the old rent [1], and rents rise [1]. Not necessarily: if the factory hires people who already live in the town, demand may hardly change [1]; if there are many empty flats, or landlords build quickly, supply may rise enough to hold rents down [1]. Judgement: rents are likely to rise, at least in the short run, because housing supply is slow to change; the rise will be larger the more workers move in from elsewhere [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Analysing the wrong market, for example petrol instead of cars.
- Moving the wrong curve for a wage change without asking who earns the wage.
- Giving a definite price result when both curves shift the same way with no sizes given.
- Stating the new price but not the new sales.
- Leaving out the shortage or surplus that explains why the price moves.
- Ending a discussion without saying what the outcome depends on.
