For the full sequences, worked data and a model evaluation, use the [heart disease risk factors study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-biology-9610-heart-disease-may-be-associated-with-specific-risk-factors/); then try the [heart disease risk factors practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-biology-9610-heart-disease-may-be-associated-with-specific-risk-factors-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Atheroma | Lipid-containing material within the walls of the arteries |
| Endothelium | The inner lining of a blood vessel |
| Aneurysm | A balloon-like bulge in a weakened artery wall, which may burst |
| Thrombosis | Formation of a blood clot (thrombus) inside a blood vessel |
| Myocardial infarction | Death of part of the heart muscle when blood flow to it is interrupted; a heart attack |
| Coronary heart disease (CHD) | Disease in which the coronary arteries are affected, for example by atheroma |
| Risk factor | A factor associated with an increased likelihood of a disease |
| Incidence | Number of new cases in a population in a given time |
| Confounding variable | A variable other than the one studied that could explain a pattern |

## Atheroma in four steps

1. Endothelium damaged (for example by high blood pressure or smoke chemicals).
2. Lipids, including cholesterol, and white blood cells build up in the wall.
3. Fibrous plaque forms and bulges into the lumen.
4. Lumen narrows; wall loses elasticity; blood pressure may rise further.

## Two consequences

| Event | Link to atheroma |
|---|---|
| Aneurysm | Wall beneath the atheroma is weakened; high pressure makes it bulge; may burst and bleed |
| Thrombosis | Atheroma damages or breaks the endothelium; clotting is triggered; the thrombus may block the artery or break away and block a smaller one |

## Myocardial infarction chain

Thrombus on atheroma in a coronary artery → blood flow interrupted → cardiac muscle beyond gets no oxygen or glucose → no aerobic respiration, not enough ATP → muscle cells die. A blockage near the start of a main coronary artery affects more muscle.

## Risk factors and mechanisms

| Factor | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Diet | Saturated fat associated with raised blood cholesterol; salt associated with raised blood pressure |
| Blood cholesterol | High levels make deposition in artery walls, and so atheroma, more likely |
| Smoking | Carbon monoxide binds haemoglobin (less oxygen carried, heart works harder); nicotine raises heart rate and blood pressure and makes platelets stickier (thrombosis); smoke chemicals damage endothelium |
| High blood pressure | Damages endothelium; strains weakened walls (aneurysm); heart works harder |

## Data toolkit

- **Rate** = cases ÷ number of people × 1000 (or 100 000). Compare rates, not raw counts, when groups differ in size.
- **Relative risk** = rate in exposed group ÷ rate in unexposed group.
- **Absolute difference** = rate in exposed group − rate in unexposed group.
- **Percentage difference** = (higher − lower) ÷ lower × 100.

**Worked reminder.** 45 cases in 5000 people with high blood pressure; 30 cases in 10 000 without. Rates: 9 per 1000 and 3 per 1000. Relative risk = 3. Absolute difference = 6 per 1000.

## Evaluation checklist

- Correlation, not proof of cause.
- Confounding variables: age, sex, other risk factors, activity.
- Were groups matched or results adjusted?
- Sample size and duration of the study.
- Overlapping error bars or ranges: difference may be due to chance.
- Self-reported data may be inaccurate.
- Plausible mechanism and repeated findings strengthen a causal claim.

## Exam traps

- "Atheroma is a blood clot." It is lipid material in the wall; a clot is a thrombus.
- "Atheroma forms in the lumen." It forms in the wall.
- "The heart runs out of blood." The heart muscle is deprived of oxygen via a coronary artery.
- "Smoking blocks arteries with tar." Use the named mechanisms: carbon monoxide, nicotine, endothelial damage.
- Comparing raw numbers from groups of different sizes.
- Writing "proves" or "causes" from correlation data.
- Naming a risk factor without a mechanism when "explain" is asked.

## Quick self-test

1. Define atheroma.
2. Why does atheroma tend to get worse over time?
3. Explain how atheroma increases the risk of an aneurysm.
4. Explain how atheroma increases the risk of thrombosis.
5. What is a myocardial infarction?
6. Why does heart muscle die when a coronary artery is blocked?
7. How is carbon monoxide from cigarette smoke linked to CHD?
8. How is nicotine linked to thrombosis?
9. Name the four risk factors for CHD named in the specification.
10. Group A: 60 cases in 4000 people. Group B: 20 cases in 5000 people. Calculate the relative risk for group A.
11. Give two reasons a study might wrongly suggest a factor causes CHD.
12. What two features make a causal link more convincing?

### Answers

1. Lipid-containing material within the walls of the arteries.
2. Narrowing raises resistance and blood pressure, which damages the endothelium further, so more atheroma can form.
3. The wall beneath the atheroma is weakened; blood at high pressure makes it bulge outwards.
4. It damages or breaks the endothelium, which triggers clotting and the formation of a thrombus.
5. Death of part of the heart muscle when its blood supply is interrupted.
6. It receives no oxygen, so it cannot respire aerobically and make enough ATP.
7. It combines with haemoglobin, reducing oxygen carried, so the heart must work harder.
8. Nicotine makes platelets more likely to stick together, so clots form more easily.
9. Diet, blood cholesterol, cigarette smoking and high blood pressure.
10. A: 15 per 1000. B: 4 per 1000. Relative risk = 15 ÷ 4 = **3.75**.
11. Any two: confounding variables not controlled; small sample; groups not matched; inaccurate self-reported data; difference due to chance.
12. A plausible biological mechanism, and consistent results from repeated, well-controlled studies.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between a risk factor and a cause?**
A risk factor is associated with a higher incidence of a disease. Showing that it causes the disease needs more evidence than a correlation, such as controlled comparisons and a mechanism.

**What causes a myocardial infarction?**
An interruption of blood flow to cardiac muscle, usually when a thrombus forms on an atheroma in a coronary artery.
