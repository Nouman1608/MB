## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Explain what is meant by a human resources strategy. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between vertical fit and horizontal fit in a human resources strategy. **[3]**

**3.** Pembury Hotels has 60 staff and 18 of them leave in a year. Each leaver costs £4,500. Calculate its labour turnover and the annual cost of turnover. **[3]**

**4.** Which **one** of the following is a measure of labour productivity?

A: Leavers divided by average staff.

B: Revenue divided by the number of employees.

C: Days lost divided by days possible.

D: Recruitment cost divided by the number hired. **[1]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why a human resources strategy might be less important to a business with three employees. **[2]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**6.** Pembury Hotels (60 staff, revenue £2,400,000, labour cost £1,080,000) plans a strategy to cut turnover from 30% to 15%. It will spend £500 per employee on training and £6,000 on a rota system that gives staff more choice over shifts.

**(a)** Calculate the labour cost as a percentage of revenue. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the cost of the strategy in the first year. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the saving in leaver costs if the target is met, and the net effect in the first year. **[4]**

**(d)** Evaluate whether Pembury Hotels should go ahead with the strategy. **[8]**

**7.** Explain **two** components that a human resources strategy for a growing business should include. **[4]**

**8.** Evaluate the importance of a human resources strategy to a supermarket chain and its stakeholders. **[9]**

**9.** Dunsford Software has 50 staff and plans to double in size in three years. Developers are hard to recruit, a rival is offering higher pay and 20% of Dunsford's developers left last year. The company's culture is informal and it has never had a written plan for its workforce. Recommend and justify a human resources strategy for Dunsford Software. **[10]**

**10.** A manufacturer introduces a strategy of paying above the market rate, but cuts its training budget to pay for it. Analyse why this may show a lack of horizontal fit. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** A human resources strategy is a long-term plan [1] for obtaining, developing, rewarding and keeping the workforce needed to achieve the business's objectives [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Vertical fit means the strategy supports the business's overall objectives [1]. Horizontal fit means the different parts of the strategy, such as recruitment, training and pay, work together [1]. Example: a growth objective needs recruitment and training (vertical), and training is wasted without a route to promotion (horizontal) [1]. **[3]**

**3.** Turnover = 18 ÷ 60 × 100 = **30%** [1]. Cost = 18 × £4,500 [1] = **£81,000** [1]. **[3]**

**4.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**5.** With three employees, tasks and relationships are simple and the owner knows each person [1], so an informal approach to recruiting and rewarding is often enough, and the cost of a written strategy may exceed its benefit [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** £1,080,000 ÷ £2,400,000 × 100 [1] = **45%** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Training = 60 × £500 = £30,000 [1]. Total = £30,000 + £6,000 = **£36,000** [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** Leavers at 15% = 9, which is 9 fewer than 18 [1]. New cost = 9 × £4,500 = £40,500 [1]. Saving = £81,000 − £40,500 = **£40,500** [1]. Net effect = £40,500 − £36,000 = **+£4,500** [1]. **[4]**

**(d)** For: the strategy covers its cost in year 1, with a small gain of £4,500 [1], and part of the cost (the rota system) is a one-off, so net benefits should rise [1]. Staff with more choice over shifts may be more motivated, and stable teams give guests better service [1].
Against: the gain is small and rests on turnover falling by half, which is uncertain [1]; training takes staff away from work [1]; labour is already 45% of revenue, so extra cost reduces profit if the target is missed [1].
Other factors: the effect on guest satisfaction and the local labour market [1].
Judgement: Pembury should go ahead, because the break-even risk is low and the non-financial gains (reputation, service) are likely, but it should review turnover every quarter and stop spending if progress is slow [1]. **[8]**

**7.** Workforce plan [1]: forecasting how many staff and what skills are needed as the business grows, so that recruitment starts early [1]. Training and development [1]: building skills in the new staff and in existing staff for new roles, which supports growth and retention [1]. (Also accept recruitment and selection, reward, or structure, each with a developed reason.) **[4]**

**8.** Importance to the business: labour is usually a large share of a supermarket's costs, so planning it affects profit [1]; the chain needs enough staff at peak times and a stream of new managers [1]; a poor strategy means high turnover and recruitment costs [1].
Stakeholders: customers depend on staffing for service and availability [1]; employees benefit from training, rotas and careers [1]; shareholders gain from lower costs and a stable workforce [1]; the community gains from jobs [1].
Evaluation: the strategy matters less where jobs are simple and quickly learned, as on the tills, but more for managers and skilled roles [1]. Judgement: it is very important, because a large chain with thousands of staff cannot manage its labour costs or quality without a long-term plan [1]. **[9]**

**9.** Recommendation: a retention and skills strategy [1], with a written workforce plan for growth from 50 to 100 staff [1], a pay review for developers [1], career paths and training [1], and a graduate and apprenticeship scheme to widen the supply [1].
Justification: the objective is rapid growth, but developers are scarce, so Dunsford cannot rely on hiring at the market rate (vertical fit) [1]. A 20% leaver rate means it loses skills as fast as it recruits them, so retention is as important as recruitment [1]. Pay, career paths and training should work together (horizontal fit) [1].
Risks and alternatives: higher pay raises cost and may not be matched by rivals; an alternative of using outsourced developers is quicker but costs more and weakens control [1]. The informal culture may be lost as the company grows, so communication must be planned [1].
Judgement: the retention and skills strategy is better, because it is cheaper over time than constant recruiting, and it should be reviewed against turnover and hiring targets each year [1]. (Maximum 10 marks.) **[10]**

**10.** Horizontal fit means the parts of the strategy support each other [1]. Paying above the market rate attracts and keeps skilled staff [1], but cutting training means those staff cannot develop, and the business may not gain new skills [1]. The extra pay may be wasted because staff become frustrated and leave for jobs offering progression [1]. The two parts work against each other, because one raises cost to keep staff and the other removes a reason to stay [1]. **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing human resource tasks without linking them to business objectives.
- Giving a recommendation without costs or an alternative.
- Missing the idea of fit.
- Using the formula for turnover but forgetting to multiply by 100.
- Offering no judgement.
