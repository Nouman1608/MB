This guide covers subtopic **11.7 Carboxylic acids** of Topic 11, Organic chemistry, in the Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620 syllabus for 2026–2028. Outcome 1 is **Core**; outcomes 2 and 3 are **Supplement** and are labelled *(Extended)*. For quick recall use the [carboxylic acids revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-carboxylic-acids-revision-notes/); for exam-style practice use the [carboxylic acids practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-carboxylic-acids-practice/). How ethanol itself is made is in the [alcohols study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-alcohols/).

## What 11.7 asks you to do

- **Core 1:** describe the reaction of **ethanoic acid** with **(a) metals, (b) bases, (c) carbonates**, including the **names and formulae of the salts** produced.
- **Supplement 2:** describe the formation of ethanoic acid by the **oxidation of ethanol** (a) with **acidified aqueous potassium manganate(VII)** and (b) by **bacterial oxidation during vinegar production**.
- **Supplement 3:** describe the reaction of a carboxylic acid with an alcohol using an **acid catalyst** to form an **ester**.

Linked outcomes elsewhere: the general formula **CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH** (11.1); naming and drawing ethanoic acid (11.2, Core); naming unbranched carboxylic acids up to four carbons and the esters made from them (11.2, *Extended*); and ethanoic acid as a **weak acid** (7.1, *Extended*).

## The –COOH group

The functional group is the **carboxyl group**, –COOH: a carbon atom double-bonded to one oxygen and single-bonded to an –OH group. Displayed formula of ethanoic acid, CH₃COOH:

```
    H   O
    |   ||
H — C — C — O — H
    |
    H
```

| Acid *(names beyond ethanoic acid: Extended)* | Structural formula | Salts are called |
|---|---|---|
| methanoic acid | HCOOH | methanoates |
| ethanoic acid | CH₃COOH | ethanoates |
| propanoic acid | CH₃CH₂COOH | propanoates |
| butanoic acid | CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH | butanoates |

The carbon in –COOH counts in the name: propanoic acid has **three** carbons, not three plus one.

## Reactions of ethanoic acid (Core)

Ethanoic acid reacts like other acids, but because the **ethanoate ion, CH₃COO⁻**, carries a single negative charge, salt formulae follow the same rules as for nitrates or chlorides.

### Writing ethanoate formulae

Treat CH₃COO⁻ as a single unit with charge 1−, and balance the charges:

| Metal ion | Salt | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Na⁺ | sodium ethanoate | CH₃COONa |
| K⁺ | potassium ethanoate | CH₃COOK |
| Mg²⁺ | magnesium ethanoate | (CH₃COO)₂Mg |
| Ca²⁺ | calcium ethanoate | (CH₃COO)₂Ca |
| Zn²⁺ | zinc ethanoate | (CH₃COO)₂Zn |
| Cu²⁺ | copper(II) ethanoate | (CH₃COO)₂Cu |

A 2+ metal ion needs **two** ethanoate ions, so the equation needs **two** CH₃COOH molecules.

### With metals: salt + hydrogen

```
2CH₃COOH + Zn → (CH₃COO)₂Zn + H₂
```

Observations: bubbles (effervescence), the metal gets smaller. The gas pops with a lighted splint.

### With bases: salt + water

```
CH₃COOH + KOH → CH₃COOK + H₂O
2CH₃COOH + CuO → (CH₃COO)₂Cu + H₂O
```

With copper(II) oxide, the black solid dissolves to give a blue solution.

### With carbonates: salt + water + carbon dioxide

```
2CH₃COOH + CaCO₃ → (CH₃COO)₂Ca + H₂O + CO₂
```

Observations: effervescence; the gas turns limewater milky.

### Worked example 1: building an equation from scratch

*Write the balanced equation for ethanoic acid reacting with magnesium carbonate, and name the salt.*

1. Pattern: acid + carbonate → salt + water + carbon dioxide.
2. Salt: magnesium is Mg²⁺, so the salt is **magnesium ethanoate, (CH₃COO)₂Mg**.
3. Two ethanoate ions are needed, so use **2CH₃COOH**.
4. Write and check: 2CH₃COOH + MgCO₃ → (CH₃COO)₂Mg + H₂O + CO₂. Carbon: 4 + 1 = 4 + 1; hydrogen: 8 = 6 + 2; oxygen: 4 + 3 = 4 + 1 + 2.

### Weak acid behaviour *(Extended)*

Ethanoic acid is a **weak acid**: it is only **partially dissociated** in water. Compared with hydrochloric acid of the same concentration, it has a lower concentration of H⁺ ions, so it fizzes **more slowly** with metals and carbonates. The products are the same type; only the rate differs.

## Making ethanoic acid from ethanol *(Extended)*

Ethanol is **oxidised** to ethanoic acid. In equations, the oxidising agent is often written as [O]:

```
CH₃CH₂OH + 2[O] → CH₃COOH + H₂O
```

**Method (a): acidified aqueous potassium manganate(VII).** Warm ethanol with the acidified solution. The manganate(VII) is reduced and changes from **purple to colourless**.

**Method (b): bacterial oxidation during vinegar production.** Bacteria oxidise ethanol to ethanoic acid when wine or another ethanol solution is exposed to **air**. Vinegar is a dilute solution of ethanoic acid.

Notice the contrast with 11.6: fermentation must **exclude** oxygen to produce ethanol, while vinegar production **needs** oxygen from the air to turn ethanol into ethanoic acid.

## Esters *(Extended)*

A carboxylic acid reacts with an alcohol, with an **acid catalyst**, to form an **ester** and **water**:

```
carboxylic acid + alcohol → ester + water
CH₃COOH + CH₃CH₂OH → CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ + H₂O
ethanoic acid + ethanol → ethyl ethanoate + water
```

The –OH from the acid and the H from the alcohol's –OH leave as water, and the two parts join through an **ester linkage**, –COO–.

**Naming:** alcohol part first, ending **-yl**; acid part second, ending **-oate**.

| Acid | Alcohol | Ester | Structural formula |
|---|---|---|---|
| ethanoic | methanol | methyl ethanoate | CH₃COOCH₃ |
| propanoic | ethanol | ethyl propanoate | CH₃CH₂COOCH₂CH₃ |
| methanoic | propan-1-ol | propyl methanoate | HCOOCH₂CH₂CH₃ |
| butanoic | methanol | methyl butanoate | CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₃ |

### Worked example 2: naming an ester from its formula

*Name CH₃CH₂COOCH₃ and identify the acid and alcohol used to make it.*

1. Find the –COO– linkage and split there: **CH₃CH₂CO** | **OCH₃**.
2. The side containing **C=O** is the acid part: CH₃CH₂CO– has three carbons, from **propanoic acid** → "propanoate".
3. The side attached to the single-bonded O is the alcohol part: –CH₃ has one carbon, from **methanol** → "methyl".
4. Name: **methyl propanoate**.

Because the acid part is written first in the formula but second in the name, CH₃CH₂COOCH₃ and CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ are different esters (methyl propanoate and ethyl ethanoate), even though both are C₄H₈O₂.

## Annotated model answer *(Extended)*

**Question:** Describe how ethanoic acid can be made from ethanol in the laboratory, and state what you would observe. [2]

**Answer:** Warm ethanol with acidified aqueous potassium manganate(VII) [1]. The solution changes from purple to colourless [1].

*Why it scores:* the reagent is named in full, including *acidified* and *aqueous*, and both colours are given.

## Common mistakes

- **Writing CH₃COOMg or CH₃COOCa**, ignoring the 2+ charge.
- **Forgetting to double the acid** for magnesium, zinc and carbonate reactions.
- **Calling the salt "ethanate" or "acetate"**: use ethanoate.
- **Counting carbons wrongly** in acid names: CH₃CH₂COOH is propanoic acid.
- **Reversing ester names**: the alcohol supplies the first word.
- **Giving "potassium manganate" without "acidified"**, or giving the colour change the wrong way round.
- **Saying vinegar forms without air.** Bacterial oxidation needs oxygen.

## Exam technique

- **Describe** (reactions): products, an observation, and a balanced equation where marks allow.
- **Name and give the formula** of a salt: use ion charges, then check the bracket.
- **Describe** (oxidation) *(Extended)*: reagent in full and colour change; or bacteria plus air for vinegar.
- **Describe** (esterification) *(Extended)*: acid + alcohol, acid catalyst, ester + water, and name the ester.
