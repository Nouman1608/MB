## Questions

**1.** A car of mass 1250 kg accelerates at 2.4 m/s². Calculate the resultant force on the car. **[2]**

**2.** A sledge of mass 22 kg is pulled forward by a force of 85 N. Friction of 30 N acts against the motion. Calculate the acceleration of the sledge. **[3]**

**3.** A trolley is pulled by different forces and its acceleration is measured with light gates. The mass of the system is kept constant.

| Force (N) | 0.5 | 1.0 | 1.5 | 2.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Acceleration (m/s²) | 0.62 | 1.25 | 1.88 | 2.50 |

**(a)** Describe the relationship between force and acceleration shown by the data. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of the system, using the 2.0 N result. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest how the mass of the system can be kept constant as the force is increased. **[1]**

**4.** **(a)** Define inertial mass. **[1]**
**(b)** The same resultant force of 12 N is applied separately to two objects. Object P accelerates at 3.0 m/s² and object R at 0.50 m/s². Calculate the inertial mass of each. **[2]**
**(c)** State which object has more inertia and explain how you know. **[1]**

**5.** **(a)** A lorry of mass 7500 kg travels at 8.0 m/s. Calculate its momentum. **[2]**
**(b)** A cyclist and bicycle have a combined mass of 90 kg and a momentum of 540 kg m/s. Calculate their velocity. **[2]**

**6.** A trolley of mass 0.60 kg moves at 2.0 m/s towards a stationary trolley of mass 0.40 kg. They stick together. Calculate the velocity of the joined trolleys, stating its direction. **[4]**

**7.** An astronaut of mass 80 kg floats at rest in space. She throws a 4.0 kg tool away from her at 5.0 m/s. Calculate the velocity of the astronaut, including its direction relative to the tool. **[4]**

**8.** (Higher Tier only) A cricket ball of mass 0.16 kg moving at 30 m/s is brought to rest by a fielder's hands in 0.040 s. Calculate the average force on the ball. **[3]**

**9.** (Higher Tier only) A driver of mass 65 kg travelling at 15 m/s is stopped in a crash.

**(a)** Calculate the average force on the driver if the stopping time is 0.020 s with no air bag. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the average force if an air bag makes the stopping time 0.20 s. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the air bag reduces the risk of injury. **[2]**

**10.** A car travels round a roundabout at a constant speed of 9 m/s.
**(a)** Explain why the velocity of the car is changing. **[2]**
**(b)** A rubber bung is whirled in a horizontal circle on a string. The string breaks. Describe the motion of the bung afterwards. **[2]**

**11.** A bumper car A of mass 200 kg moves at 3.0 m/s and hits a stationary bumper car B of mass 150 kg. The cars lock together.
**(a)** Calculate the momentum of A before the collision. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the speed of the locked cars immediately after the collision. **[2]**
**(c)** State what happens to the momentum of A during the collision. **[1]**
**(d)** The locked cars then slow down because of a constant resultant force of 140 N. Calculate their deceleration. **[2]**
**(e)** Calculate the time taken for the locked cars to stop. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** F = ma [1]. F = 1250 × 2.4 = **3000 N** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Writing the equation before substituting shows your method, and the unit is part of the answer.

**2.** Resultant force = 85 - 30 = 55 N [1]. a = F / m = 55 / 22 [1] = **2.5 m/s²** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Using 85 N alone in F = ma is the usual error. State the resultant first.

**3. (a)** Acceleration is directly proportional to force (doubling the force doubles the acceleration) [1].
**(b)** m = F / a = 2.0 / 2.50 [1] = **0.80 kg** [1]
**(c)** Move weights from the trolley to the hanger (so the total mass stays the same) [1].
*Examiner insight:* Answers on a controlled variable gain credit when they say how the control is achieved, not just "keep the mass the same".

**4. (a)** Inertial mass is the ratio of force to acceleration [1].
**(b)** P: 12 / 3.0 = **4.0 kg** [1]. R: 12 / 0.50 = **24 kg** [1]
**(c)** R has more inertia: for the same force it has the smaller acceleration, so it is harder to change its velocity [1].
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both objects and the reason, in terms of acceleration for the same force.

**5. (a)** p = mv [1] = 7500 × 8.0 = **60 000 kg m/s** [1]
**(b)** v = p / m = 540 / 90 [1] = **6.0 m/s** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Momentum has the unit kg m/s. Give it in full, not "N" or "J".

**6.** Momentum before = 0.60 × 2.0 + 0.40 × 0 = 1.2 kg m/s [1]. Mass after = 0.60 + 0.40 = 1.0 kg [1]. 1.2 = 1.0 × v [1]. **v = 1.2 m/s in the original direction of the 0.60 kg trolley** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Use the combined mass after the trolleys stick, and give the direction for a vector answer.

**7.** Momentum of tool = 4.0 × 5.0 = 20 kg m/s [1]. Total momentum before = 0, so the astronaut has 20 kg m/s the opposite way [1]. v = 20 / 80 [1] = **0.25 m/s, in the opposite direction to the tool** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Starting from "total momentum before = 0" makes the equal and opposite momenta clear.

**8.** Change in momentum = 0.16 × 30 - 0 = 4.8 kg m/s [1]. F = change in momentum / time [1] = 4.8 / 0.040 = **120 N** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Find the change in momentum first, then divide by time.

**9. (a)** Change in momentum = 65 × 15 = 975 kg m/s [1]. F = 975 / 0.020 = **48 750 N** [1]
**(b)** F = 975 / 0.20 = **4875 N** [1]
**(c)** The change in momentum is the same, but the air bag increases the stopping time [1], so the force on the driver is smaller (ten times smaller here) [1].
*Examiner insight:* An explanation that mentions only "cushioning" loses the key idea: same change in momentum, longer time, smaller force.

**10. (a)** The direction of travel keeps changing [1]. Velocity is a vector, so a change in direction is a change in velocity even at constant speed [1].
**(b)** There is no longer a resultant force towards the centre [1], so the bung moves in a straight line at constant velocity along the direction it was travelling when the string broke [1].
*Examiner insight:* Say "velocity" for the quantity that changes and "speed" for the one that stays constant.

**11. (a)** p = 200 × 3.0 = **600 kg m/s** [1]
**(b)** Mass after = 350 kg, so v = 600 / 350 [1] = **1.7 m/s** [1]
**(c)** The momentum of A decreases (some is transferred to B) [1].
**(d)** a = F / m = 140 / 350 [1] = **0.40 m/s²** [1]
**(e)** t = (v - u) / a = 1.714 / 0.40 [1] = **4.3 s** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Carrying the unrounded speed of 1.714 m/s into later steps avoids small rounding drift in the final answer.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using one force, not the resultant, in F = ma.
- Leaving out the unit, or using the wrong one for momentum.
- Not converting grams to kilograms before substituting.
- Forgetting the stationary object's zero momentum, or using one mass when objects join.
- Missing the direction for velocity or momentum.
- Describing circular motion as "changing speed".
- Describing a safety feature without the time and change-in-momentum link (Higher Tier only).
