## Key definitions

| Term | Definition in exam wording |
|---|---|
| Diffusion | The net movement of molecules or ions from a region of their higher concentration to a region of their lower concentration, down a concentration gradient, as a result of their random movement |
| Osmosis | The net movement of water molecules from a region of higher water potential to a region of lower water potential, through a partially permeable membrane |
| Water potential | How freely water can move; highest in pure water and lower in more concentrated solutions |
| Turgid | A plant cell full of water, firm, with the contents pressing on the wall |
| Turgor pressure | The pressure of the cell contents pushing outwards on the cell wall |
| Flaccid | A plant cell that has lost water, so it is soft and limp |
| Plasmolysis | The cell membrane pulling away from the cell wall after a cell loses a lot of water |

## Water as a solvent

- **Digestion**: enzymes and food are dissolved in water.
- **Excretion**: urea is dissolved in water and removed in urine.
- **Transport**: blood plasma carries dissolved glucose, amino acids, ions, hormones and urea.

## Energy source

Diffusion and osmosis are **passive**. The energy is the **kinetic energy** of the random movement of molecules and ions. They do not need energy from respiration.

## Factors affecting diffusion

| Factor | Higher or larger means | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Surface area | Faster | More particles cross at once |
| Temperature | Faster | More kinetic energy |
| Concentration gradient | Faster | More particles move down than back |
| Distance | Slower | Particles travel further |

Cube surface area to volume ratio: 1 cm cube is 6 : 1, 2 cm cube is 3 : 1, 3 cm cube is 2 : 1. Small objects have a larger ratio and exchange faster.

## Osmosis in cells

| Surroundings | Water moves | Plant cell becomes | Animal cell (no wall) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Higher water potential than the cell (pure water, dilute solution) | In | Turgid | Swells and may burst |
| Same water potential | No net movement | No change | No change |
| Lower water potential than the cell (concentrated solution) | Out | Flaccid, then plasmolysed | Shrinks |

- The cell membrane is **partially permeable**. The cell wall is **fully permeable**.
- Plants are supported by turgor pressure. If cells lose water, the plant wilts.
- The cellulose wall stops a plant cell from bursting.

## Potato cylinder practical

1. Cut cylinders of equal size and measure the start mass.
2. Place them in different sucrose concentrations, one per beaker, for a set time.
3. Remove, blot dry, and measure the end mass.
4. Calculate **percentage change = (end mass - start mass) divided by start mass x 100**.
5. Plot percentage change against concentration. Where the line crosses zero, the solution has the same water potential as the potato.

| Start mass (g) | End mass (g) | Percentage change |
|---|---|---|
| 2.50 | 2.85 | +14.0 |
| 2.60 | 2.34 | -10.0 |

**Control variables:** size of cylinder, volume of solution, time, temperature, drying method.

## Dialysis tubing practical

- Dialysis tubing is partially permeable: water and glucose pass, starch does not.
- Starch and glucose inside the bag, water outside: after a time, iodine outside stays orange-brown (no starch), Benedict's outside goes orange-red on heating (glucose present).
- A bag of concentrated sugar solution in water gains mass as water enters by osmosis.

## Short model answers

**Define osmosis.** The net movement of water molecules from a region of higher water potential to a region of lower water potential through a partially permeable membrane.

**Explain why a lettuce leaf in pure water becomes crisp.** Water enters the cells by osmosis, because the water potential outside is higher. The cells become turgid and the turgor pressure supports the leaf.

**Explain why a red blood cell bursts in pure water but a plant cell does not.** Both take in water by osmosis. The plant cell has a strong cellulose wall that resists expansion, but the animal cell has no wall.

**State why diffusion is faster at a higher temperature.** The particles have more kinetic energy and move faster.

## Exam traps

- Osmosis needs **water potential** and a **partially permeable membrane** in the definition.
- Diffusion does not use energy from respiration.
- Percentage change uses the **start** mass as the divisor.
- A plasmolysed cell is not burst; the membrane has pulled away from the wall.
- Do not say that the particles "stop moving" at equilibrium.
- Do not write "water concentration" where the syllabus uses water potential.

## Self-test

1. Name the two other roles of water as a solvent besides digestion.
2. Define diffusion.
3. State the four factors listed for diffusion.
4. What is the source of energy for diffusion?
5. Define osmosis.
6. Which solution has the highest water potential?
7. What does turgid mean?
8. What happens to a plant cell in a very concentrated solution?
9. Why does a red blood cell burst in pure water?
10. Calculate the percentage change when a cylinder goes from 4.00 g to 3.72 g.
11. Why are percentage changes used rather than mass changes?
12. What shows that dialysis tubing is partially permeable?

### Answers

1. Excretion and transport.
2. The net movement of molecules or ions from a higher concentration to a lower concentration, down a concentration gradient, as a result of their random movement.
3. Surface area, temperature, concentration gradient, distance.
4. The kinetic energy of the random movement of the particles.
5. The net movement of water molecules from a higher water potential to a lower water potential through a partially permeable membrane.
6. Pure water.
7. Full of water and firm, with the contents pressing on the cell wall.
8. It loses water by osmosis, becomes flaccid and may become plasmolysed.
9. Water enters by osmosis and there is no cell wall to resist the swelling.
10. (3.72 - 4.00) = -0.28; -0.28 divided by 4.00 x 100 = -7.0 per cent.
11. The start masses differ, so percentage change allows a fair comparison.
12. Glucose passes through but starch does not.
