Section 2.2 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 covers **direct data entry (DDE)**: ways of getting data into a computer straight from its source, without a person typing it. The syllabus names seven devices: **magnetic stripe reader, chip and PIN reader, Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) reader, Optical Mark Recognition/Reader (OMR), Optical Character Recognition/Reader (OCR), bar code reader** and **QR scanner**. As in 2.1, you need their characteristics, uses, advantages and disadvantages, and questions usually ask you to choose between them for a scenario.

For condensed recall use the [direct data entry revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-direct-data-entry-and-associated-devices-revision-notes/), and test yourself with the [direct data entry practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-direct-data-entry-and-associated-devices-practice/).

## Why direct data entry matters

Typing data in by hand is slow and introduces **transcription errors**, such as a mistyped digit. DDE devices read data that has already been recorded on a card, a tag, a form or a label. The common benefits are therefore:

- **Speed**: reading takes a fraction of a second.
- **Accuracy**: no retyping, so fewer errors.
- **Lower staffing costs**: fewer people needed for data entry.

The common drawback is that the data must first be put into a machine-readable form, such as printing a bar code or encoding a card, and the equipment must be bought and maintained.

## Magnetic stripe reader

- **Characteristics**: reads data stored on a magnetic stripe on the back of a card when the card is swiped through the reader. The stripe holds a small amount of data, such as a card or account number and an expiry date.
- **Uses**: older bank cards; hotel room key cards; library and membership cards; some travel tickets.
- **Advantages**: fast, simple to use; cards are cheap to produce; stripe is not affected by being wet; data cannot be read by eye.
- **Disadvantages**: the stripe can be damaged by strong magnets or wear; stores only a small amount of data; data can be copied (cloned) relatively easily, so it is less secure; the card must be in contact with the reader.

## Chip and PIN reader

- **Characteristics**: the card contains a microchip. The card is inserted into the reader, which reads the chip, and the customer types a **Personal Identification Number (PIN)** on a numeric keypad. The PIN is checked to confirm the cardholder.
- **Uses**: card payments in shops, restaurants and petrol stations; cash machines.
- **Advantages**: more secure than a magnetic stripe because the chip is harder to copy and the PIN must be known; the chip can hold more data; the customer keeps the card in their own hands while paying.
- **Disadvantages**: a PIN can be seen by someone watching (shoulder surfing); people can forget their PIN; slower than contactless payment; readers cost more than magnetic stripe readers.

## RFID reader

- **Characteristics**: reads data from an **RFID tag** using radio waves. A tag contains a microchip and an antenna. **Passive tags** have no battery and are powered by the reader's signal, so they work only at short range. **Active tags** have their own battery and can be read from much further away.
- **Uses**: stock tracking in warehouses and shops; tracking parcels and luggage; contactless payment cards; livestock and pet identification; access cards; library books; toll collection on roads.
- **Advantages**: **no line of sight** needed, so tags can be read through packaging or clothing; several tags can be read at almost the same time; tags can be read while moving; data on some tags can be updated; tags are hard-wearing.
- **Disadvantages**: tags and readers cost more than bar codes; signals can be blocked or disrupted by metal or liquids; data can sometimes be read by an unauthorised reader nearby, which raises privacy and security concerns; tags can be read when the owner does not intend it.

## Optical Mark Recognition/Reader (OMR)

- **Characteristics**: detects **marks** made in set positions on a specially designed form, such as shaded boxes or lines. It recognises whether a position is marked or not; it does not read writing.
- **Uses**: multiple-choice answer sheets; lottery tickets; questionnaires and surveys; school registers; voting papers in some countries.
- **Advantages**: very fast processing of large numbers of forms; accurate, with no typing errors; forms are simple to fill in.
- **Disadvantages**: forms must be designed and printed precisely, which costs time and money; marks must be made correctly (wrong pen or faint marks may not be read); folded or damaged forms can be rejected; only choices can be recorded, not free answers.

## Optical Character Recognition/Reader (OCR)

- **Characteristics**: **software** that analyses a scanned image or photograph of text and converts the shapes of the characters into editable text. A scanner or camera captures the image first.
- **Uses**: converting printed documents into word-processed files; reading car number plates in car parks and traffic systems; scanning passports at border control; processing forms filled in by hand in block capitals.
- **Advantages**: much faster than retyping; the text produced can be edited, searched and stored in less space than an image; reduces staffing costs.
- **Disadvantages**: errors with poor-quality originals, unusual fonts or untidy handwriting, so the output needs checking; less accurate than OMR for forms, because it must recognise every character.

The key difference: **OMR detects whether a mark is present in a fixed position; OCR recognises what each character is.**

## Bar code reader

- **Characteristics**: a bar code is a series of dark and light lines of different widths representing a number. The reader shines light (often a laser) on the code and detects the reflected pattern. The number usually identifies a product, which the computer looks up in a database.
- **Uses**: shop checkouts (finding price and description, updating stock); libraries (books and borrower cards); tracking parcels; patient wristbands; ticket checking.
- **Advantages**: fast and accurate; bar codes are very cheap to print; automatic stock control is possible because each sale updates the stock level; if the code cannot be read, the number printed below it can be typed in.
- **Disadvantages**: needs a line of sight, so the code must face the reader; damaged, wet or creased codes may not scan; stores only a short number, not detailed data; codes can be swapped to commit fraud.

## QR scanner

- **Characteristics**: a **Quick Response (QR) code** is a two-dimensional pattern of black and white squares. It holds much more data than a bar code, such as a website address, contact details or ticket information. Most smartphones can scan QR codes with their camera.
- **Uses**: opening a website from a poster or menu; tickets and boarding passes on a phone; making payments; adding contact details; product information.
- **Advantages**: stores far more data than a bar code; can be read from any angle; still readable if partly damaged; no special scanner needed for many uses, since phones can read them; saves typing long web addresses.
- **Disadvantages**: the user needs a device with a camera and suitable software; a fake QR code can be placed over a real one and lead to a harmful website (a security risk); people cannot tell where a code leads before scanning it.

## Comparing the devices

| Device | Line of sight? | Contact needed? | Typical data held |
|---|---|---|---|
| Magnetic stripe | No (swiped) | Yes | Small: account number, expiry date |
| Chip and PIN | No (inserted) | Yes | More than a stripe; verified by PIN |
| RFID | No | No | Identifier, sometimes updateable data |
| OMR | Yes (optical) | Form fed through reader | Positions of marks |
| OCR | Yes (optical) | No | Any printed or neatly written text |
| Bar code | Yes | No | Short number |
| QR code | Yes | No | Much more: web address, text, ticket data |

## Worked example: choosing between DDE devices

*A school wants a fast way to record which of its 1,200 students are present each morning. Compare using OMR registers with RFID student cards. [4]*

> With OMR, teachers shade a box beside each student's name, and the forms are read quickly and accurately by the reader (1), but the forms must be printed and collected each day and marks made incorrectly may be rejected (1). With RFID cards, students tap or walk past a reader as they enter, so attendance is recorded automatically with no paperwork (1), but cards cost more, can be lost or forgotten, and a student could carry a friend's card (1).

## Common mistakes

- Saying OCR "reads marks". OMR reads marks; OCR recognises characters.
- Saying RFID "needs to be scanned like a bar code". It needs no line of sight.
- Describing a bar code as storing the price. It usually stores a product number; the price is looked up in the database.
- Calling chip and PIN "contactless". Chip and PIN requires the card to be inserted; contactless cards use RFID.
- Using "it is faster" without saying faster than what.

## Exam technique

- **Identify** the device: choose the one the scenario fits best and use its full name or the syllabus abbreviation.
- **Describe** how a device is used: say what the user does and what the device reads.
- **Compare** or **justify**: refer to line of sight, contact, data capacity, security and cost.
- **Explain** an advantage: state the feature and its consequence, such as "no line of sight, so luggage tags can be read while bags move along a conveyor".
