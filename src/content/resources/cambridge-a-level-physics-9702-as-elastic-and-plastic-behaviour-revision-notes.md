Condensed notes on subtopic 6.2 Elastic and plastic behaviour (Cambridge 9702,
AS Topic 6). For full explanations and the extended worked example, use the
[elastic and plastic behaviour study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-as-elastic-and-plastic-behaviour/);
for the whole topic, see the [Deformation of Solids revision notes](/resources/as-physics-deformation-of-solids-revision-notes/).

## What 6.2 asks you to do

- Use the terms elastic deformation, plastic deformation and elastic limit
- Understand that the area under a force–extension graph is the work done
- Determine elastic potential energy from that area, within the limit of proportionality
- Recall and use Eₚ = ½Fx = ½kx², within the limit of proportionality

## Three definitions

> **Elastic deformation**: the object returns to its original shape and size when the load is removed.

> **Plastic deformation**: the object is permanently deformed; it does not return to its original shape when the load is removed.

> **Elastic limit**: the point beyond which deformation is no longer elastic.

## Two limits, two different questions

| | Limit of proportionality | Elastic limit |
|---|---|---|
| Question it answers | Is F still proportional to x? | Will the object return to its original length? |
| Seen on graph as | End of the straight-line section | Cannot be seen on loading alone; shown by unloading |
| Beyond it | Graph curves | Permanent extension remains |

The elastic limit is at or beyond the limit of proportionality. Between them the material is elastic but does not obey Hooke's law.

## Area under the force–extension graph

- Area = **work done** on the object, for any graph shape (sum of F∆x strips).
- Elastic region: work done = elastic potential energy stored, all recoverable.
- Plastic region: some of the work done is dissipated as internal energy and is not recovered.
- Loading and unloading lines that differ: the enclosed area is the energy not recovered.

**Finding the area:**

- Straight line from the origin: triangle, ½Fx.
- Straight line between two points: trapezium, ½(F₁ + F₂)∆x.
- Curve: count squares, then multiply by the energy of one square (force per square × extension per square).

## Elastic potential energy equations

```
Ep = (1/2) F x = (1/2) k x^2       within the limit of proportionality
added energy from x1 to x2 = (1/2) k (x2^2 - x1^2)     NOT (1/2) k (x2 - x1)^2
```

Ep ∝ x²: double the extension → four times the energy.

**Example — spring launcher.** A spring with k = 800 N m⁻¹ is compressed by 0.050 m and launches a 0.020 kg ball horizontally. If all the stored energy becomes kinetic energy:

```
Ep = 0.5 x 800 x 0.050^2 = 1.0 J
(1/2) m v^2 = 1.0   so   v = sqrt(2 x 1.0 / 0.020) = 10 m/s
```

## Loading beyond the elastic limit (metal wire)

- Loading curve bends after the limit of proportionality.
- Unloading line is approximately **parallel to the original straight section**.
- It meets the extension axis at the **permanent extension**.
- Energy recovered = area under the unloading line; energy dissipated = total work done − energy recovered.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to recall |
|---|---|
| Elastic deformation | Returns to original shape when load removed |
| Plastic deformation | Permanent change of shape when load removed |
| Elastic limit | Point beyond which deformation becomes plastic |
| Limit of proportionality | End of the region where F ∝ x |
| Elastic potential energy | Energy stored in a deformed object, recoverable when it returns to its original shape |
| Permanent extension | Extension remaining after the load is removed from a plastically deformed object |
| Work done in deformation | Area under the force–extension graph |

## Exam traps

- Using ½kx² beyond the limit of proportionality.
- ½k(x₂ − x₁)² instead of ½k(x₂² − x₁²).
- Defining elastic and plastic without saying "when the load is removed".
- Claiming the elastic limit is where the graph stops being straight.
- Forgetting ½ in ½Fx.
- N × mm left uncorrected: gives millijoules, not joules.
- Saying all of the area under a plastic loading curve is "stored" energy.

## Self-test

1. Define elastic deformation.
2. Define plastic deformation.
3. What is the elastic limit?
4. Distinguish between the limit of proportionality and the elastic limit.
5. What does the area under a force–extension graph represent?
6. A spring with k = 250 N m⁻¹ is extended by 0.040 m. Find the energy stored.
7. A wire extends by 1.5 mm under 60 N, within its limit of proportionality. Find the energy stored.
8. The extension of a spring is doubled within its limit of proportionality. By what factor does the stored energy change?
9. A spring with k = 50 N m⁻¹ is stretched from 0.10 m to 0.20 m extension. Find the extra energy stored.
10. Why can ½kx² not be used beyond the limit of proportionality?
11. After unloading, a wire is 1.2 mm longer than it was originally. What type of deformation occurred?
12. On a force–extension graph, one square represents 0.5 N by 1.0 mm. The area under a curve is 36 squares. Find the work done.

**Answers:**
1. The object returns to its original shape and size when the load is removed.
2. The object is permanently deformed and does not return to its original shape when the load is removed.
3. The point beyond which deformation is no longer elastic (becomes plastic).
4. The limit of proportionality is where F stops being proportional to x; the elastic limit is where the object stops returning to its original length. They can be different points.
5. The work done in deforming the object.
6. ½ × 250 × 0.040² = **0.20 J**.
7. ½ × 60 × 1.5 × 10⁻³ = **0.045 J**.
8. **×4**.
9. ½ × 50 × (0.20² − 0.10²) = **0.75 J**.
10. The graph is no longer a straight line, so the area is not a triangle and k is not constant.
11. **Plastic** deformation (the elastic limit was exceeded).
12. One square = 0.5 × 1.0 × 10⁻³ = 5 × 10⁻⁴ J; 36 squares = **0.018 J**.
