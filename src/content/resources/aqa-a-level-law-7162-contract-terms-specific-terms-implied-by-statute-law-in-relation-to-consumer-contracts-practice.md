## Section A

**1.** Which section of the Consumer Rights Act 2015 implies a term that goods will match their description? A: s9. B: s10. C: s11. D: s49. **[1]**

**2.** Which of the following is a consumer under the Consumer Rights Act 2015? A: a limited company buying office chairs. B: a sole trader buying a van for her deliveries. C: a teacher buying a sofa for her flat. D: a charity buying a minibus. **[1]**

**3.** Identify two aspects of the quality of goods listed in section 9(3) of the Consumer Rights Act 2015. **[2]**

**4.** State the normal time limit for the short-term right to reject and the section that sets it. **[2]**

**5.** Identify the two remedies available under section 24 of the Consumer Rights Act 2015. **[2]**

**6.** Identify the two terms implied into a contract to supply services that are named in the specification. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Explain when goods will fail to be fit for a particular purpose under section 10 of the Consumer Rights Act 2015, referring to two cases. **[4]**

**8.** Explain the order in which a consumer can use the remedies for goods that breach the implied terms. **[5]**

**9.** Explain the remedies available to a consumer when a service is not performed with reasonable care and skill, and when it is not performed within a reasonable time. **[5]**

**10.** *Joel buys a "brand new, boxed" games console from an online trader for £400. When it arrives the box has been opened and the console has scratches and a faulty controller. He tells the trader within a week.* Suggest which implied terms have been broken and what remedy Joel should seek. **[5]**

**11.** *Amira pays a decorator, Finch Interiors, £1,500 to paint her living room, with no completion date agreed. The job should take a week but takes five weeks, and the paint is applied so thinly that the old colour shows through. Separately, Amira bought a £60 waterproof jacket from an outdoor shop, telling the assistant she wanted it for hill walking in heavy rain. Two months later the seams leak in the first rainstorm. The shop replaced it once and the replacement also leaks.* Advise Amira on her rights under the Consumer Rights Act 2015. **[8]**

**12.** Discuss whether the Consumer Rights Act 2015 strikes an appropriate balance between freedom of contract and the need to protect the consumer in its implied terms and remedies. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** C: s11. **[1]**

**2.** C: a teacher buying a sofa for her flat. **[1]**

**3.** Any two of: **fitness for all the purposes for which goods of that kind are usually supplied** [1]; **appearance and finish**; **freedom from minor defects**; **safety**; **durability** [1].

**4.** **30 days** [1], beginning with the day after the latest of ownership or possession passing, delivery and any required installation, set by **section 22** [1].

**5.** A **price reduction** of an appropriate amount, which may be the full price [1]; or the **final right to reject** [1].

**6.** **Section 49**: the service must be performed with reasonable care and skill [1]. **Section 52**: where no time is fixed, the service must be performed within a reasonable time [1].

**7.** Section 10 applies where the consumer **makes known a particular purpose**, expressly or by implication [1], and the goods are **not reasonably fit** for it, unless the consumer did not rely, or it was unreasonable to rely, on the trader's skill or judgment [1]. In **Priest v Last (1903)** a hot-water bottle had only one obvious purpose, so buying it made that purpose known and the buyer recovered when it burst [1]. In **Griffiths v Peter Conway (1939)** a buyer who did not reveal her unusually sensitive skin could not claim that a coat was unfit, because the special purpose was never made known [1].

**8.** First, the **short-term right to reject** under **s20**, normally exercisable within **30 days** under s22, giving a full refund [1]. Secondly, the consumer may require **repair or replacement** under **s23**, which the trader must carry out within a reasonable time, without significant inconvenience and at its own cost [1]; the consumer cannot insist on one if it is impossible or disproportionate compared with the other [1]. Thirdly, under **s24**, a **price reduction or the final right to reject** [1], available only after one failed repair or replacement, or where those remedies are impossible or the trader has not provided them properly [1].

**9.** For breach of **s49** (reasonable care and skill), the consumer may require **repeat performance under s55** [1], which the trader must carry out within a reasonable time, without significant inconvenience and at its own cost [1]. If repeat performance is **impossible or not done properly**, the consumer may claim a **price reduction under s56** [1], which may be up to the full price [1]. For breach of **s52** (reasonable time), repeating the service would not cure the delay, so the remedy is a **price reduction under s56** [1].

**10.** This is a **trader–consumer** contract, so the Act applies [1]. The console was described as "brand new, boxed" but arrived opened, so it does not match its description under **s11** [1]. Scratches and a faulty controller affect **appearance and finish and freedom from minor defects**, so it is not of **satisfactory quality under s9** [1]. Joel complained within a week, which is **inside the 30-day period** [1], so he should use the **short-term right to reject under s20** and claim a full refund with no deduction for use [1].

**11.** Both contracts are between a **trader and a consumer**, so the Act applies [1].
Decorating: applying paint so thinly that the old colour shows is likely below the standard of a reasonably competent decorator, a breach of **s49** [1]. With no completion date, five weeks for a one-week job is likely a breach of **s52** [1]. For the poor work, Amira can require **repeat performance under s55** at Finch's cost [1]; if this is not done properly, she can claim a **price reduction under s56**, which is also her remedy for the delay [1].
Jacket: she made known the particular purpose of hill walking in heavy rain and relied on the assistant, so leaking seams breach **s10**, and probably **s9** on durability [1]. The fault appeared within six months, so it is **presumed present at delivery** [1]. The short-term right has expired, but one replacement has failed, so she may use **s24**: a price reduction or the **final right to reject**, with no deduction for use because she is within six months [1].

**12.** Award one mark per developed point, to a maximum of 8:
- Classical **freedom of contract** would leave quality to the parties' bargain; the Act overrides this by implying terms that **cannot be excluded** against consumers [1].
- This responds to **inequality of bargaining power** and information: consumers rarely negotiate terms or inspect goods expertly [1].
- The **s9 test is flexible**, taking account of price and description, so traders selling cheap or second-hand goods are not held to unrealistic standards [1].
- The **six-month presumption** places the burden of proof on the trader, who is better placed to know about defects [1].
- The **tiered remedies** give traders a chance to repair or replace, which respects the bargain and avoids waste [1].
- But the tiers are **complex**, and many consumers will not know when the 30-day clock pauses or when they can move to s24 [1].
- **Services terms are weaker**: s49 only requires reasonable care, not a result, and s52 depends on what is "reasonable" [1].
- A **reasoned conclusion**, for example that the Act protects consumers well on paper and still allows traders some freedom, but its effectiveness depends on consumers knowing and enforcing their rights [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not checking that the contract is between a trader and a consumer before applying the Act.
- Giving a full refund after 30 days without explaining why the short-term right is still open.
- Missing that a failed replacement unlocks the s24 remedies.
- Using repeat performance as the remedy for a late service.
- Listing sections without applying them to the dates and facts given.
