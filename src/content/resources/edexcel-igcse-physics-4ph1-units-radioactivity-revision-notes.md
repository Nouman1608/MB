Condensed notes for sub-topic **7(a) Units** of Pearson Edexcel
International GCSE Physics (4PH1). Statement 7.1: use the becquerel (Bq),
centimetre (cm), hour (h), minute (min) and second (s). Core content,
examinable on both papers. Worked examples are in the
[Units in radioactivity study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-physics-4ph1-units-radioactivity/).

## The five units at a glance

| Unit | Symbol | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| becquerel | Bq | Activity: decays per second |
| second | s | Time; count rate in counts per second |
| minute | min | Time; count rate in counts per minute |
| hour | h | Time, often for half-lives |
| centimetre | cm | Range in air, source-to-detector distance, absorber thickness |

## Activity and the becquerel

- **Activity** = number of nuclei decaying per second.
- **1 Bq = 1 decay per second.**
- 1 kBq = 1000 Bq; 1 MBq = 1 000 000 Bq.
- Activity decreases with time (statement 7.11).

## Count rate is a different quantity

- A detector measures **count rate**: detected counts per second or per minute.
- Unit: counts/s or counts/min. **Not Bq.**
- Count rate < activity, because:
  - radiation is emitted in all directions and most misses the detector
  - some radiation is absorbed on the way (by air, or by the detector window)
  - (the reading also includes background, which must be subtracted)
- Count rate and activity both halve in one half-life.

## Time conversions

```
1 min = 60 s
1 h   = 60 min = 3600 s
```

- Hours → minutes: × 60. Minutes → seconds: × 60. Hours → seconds: × 3600.
- Reverse direction: divide.
- Rate per minute = rate per second × 60 (the per-minute number is bigger).
- Total counts ÷ counting time = count rate, in counts per that time unit (for example, 600 counts in 2.0 min = 300 counts/min = 5.0 counts/s).
- Longer counting times give more counts and a more reliable average, because decay is random.

**Half-life calculations**

```
number of half-lives = elapsed time ÷ half-life   (same units!)
```

**Background correction**

```
corrected count rate = measured count rate − background count rate   (same units!)
```

## Centimetres

- Alpha range in air: a few cm.
- In the penetration practical, keep the source-to-detector distance fixed (in cm).
- 1 m = 100 cm; 1 cm = 10 mm.
- Greater distance from a source = lower exposure.

## Mini worked example

*Illustrative figures.* Half-life 40 min; activity 12 000 Bq; find the activity after 2.0 h.

- 2.0 h = 120 min → 120 ÷ 40 = 3 half-lives.
- 12 000 → 6000 → 3000 → **1500 Bq**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Activity | Rate of decay of a source; number of decays per second |
| Becquerel (Bq) | Unit of activity; one decay per second |
| Count rate | Number of counts recorded by a detector per unit time |
| Background count rate | Count rate recorded with no source present |
| Corrected count rate | Measured count rate minus background count rate |
| Half-life | Time for the activity (or count rate) to fall to half |
| Range | Distance radiation travels through a material before being absorbed |

## Exam traps

- **Bq for count rate.** Wrong unit: use counts/s or counts/min.
- **Mismatched units** when dividing elapsed time by half-life.
- **Subtracting backgrounds in different units.**
- **×60 versus ÷60 confusion.** Ask: should the number get bigger or smaller?
- **Decimal hours.** 1.5 h is 90 min, not 150 min; 2.5 h is 150 min.
- **No unit on the answer.**
- **Using the observed count after background** as if it were the activity of the source.

## Self-test

1. What is the unit of activity, and what does one of these units mean?
2. Why is the count rate from a detector usually much less than the activity of the source?
3. Convert 2.5 h into seconds.
4. Convert 7200 s into minutes.
5. A count rate is 0.75 counts per second. What is it in counts per minute?
6. Convert 2880 counts per minute into counts per second.
7. A half-life is 30 min. How many half-lives are there in 1.5 h?
8. A reading is 330 counts/min and background is 0.50 counts/s. Find the corrected count rate in counts/min.
9. Why should the unit "Bq" not be used for the reading on a Geiger-Müller counter?
10. Give one context in Topic 7 where centimetres are used.
11. A sample's activity falls from 4000 Bq to 1000 Bq in 90 min. Find the half-life in seconds.
12. Express 1.5 MBq in becquerels.

## Answers

1. The becquerel (Bq); one decay per second.
2. Radiation goes in all directions and only a fraction reaches the detector; some is absorbed on the way.
3. 2.5 × 3600 = 9000 s.
4. 7200 ÷ 60 = 120 min.
5. 0.75 × 60 = 45 counts/min.
6. 2880 ÷ 60 = 48 counts/s.
7. 1.5 h = 90 min; 90 ÷ 30 = 3 half-lives.
8. Background = 0.50 × 60 = 30 counts/min; corrected = 330 − 30 = 300 counts/min.
9. The counter measures count rate (detected counts), not the number of decays per second in the source.
10. Any one: range of alpha particles in air; distance between source and detector in the penetration practical; thickness of an absorber.
11. 4000 → 2000 → 1000 is 2 half-lives; half-life = 90 ÷ 2 = 45 min = 45 × 60 = 2700 s.
12. 1.5 × 1 000 000 = 1 500 000 Bq (1.5 × 10⁶ Bq).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is "counts per minute" a unit listed in 4PH1?**
Not as a single unit, but it is built from units the specification lists (minute and second), and count rates are routinely given that way.

**Do I need to convert to seconds every time?**
No. Any time unit works, as long as elapsed time and half-life (or two count rates) are in the same unit.
