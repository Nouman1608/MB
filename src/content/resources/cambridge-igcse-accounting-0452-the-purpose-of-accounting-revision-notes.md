For full explanations and worked examples, use the [1.1 study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-the-purpose-of-accounting/); to test yourself under exam conditions, use the [1.1 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-the-purpose-of-accounting-practice/).

## What 1.1 asks for

- The difference between book-keeping and accounting.
- The purposes of measuring business profit and loss.
- The role of accounting in providing information for monitoring progress and decision-making.

This content underpins the whole course: a question on almost any later topic can end with "explain how this information helps the owner".

## Book-keeping and accounting

| | Book-keeping | Accounting |
|---|---|---|
| Core job | Recording transactions | Using the records |
| Activities | Source documents, books of prime entry, ledger posting, balancing, trial balance | Adjustments, financial statements, analysis, interpretation, advice |
| Nature of work | Routine, rule-based, detailed | Judgement, summary, communication |
| Question it answers | What happened? | What does it mean and what should be done? |
| Output | Up-to-date ledger accounts | Statements, comparisons and recommendations |

**Link:** accounting is only as reliable as the book-keeping beneath it. An unrecorded expense overstates profit in the statement and misleads every decision made from it.

**One owner, both jobs:** in a small business the owner may do both. The distinction is between two kinds of work.

## Measuring profit and loss

**Profit** = revenue earned in a period - expenses incurred in earning it. If expenses exceed revenue, the result is a **loss**. The period must always be stated.

Six purposes, each with its user:

| Purpose | Who needs it and why |
|---|---|
| Judge performance | Owner: is the return on capital and effort worth it? |
| Guide drawings | Owner: drawing more than profit reduces capital |
| Support borrowing | Lender: can interest and repayments be met? |
| Calculate tax | Tax authority: tax is based on profit |
| Compare | Owner and managers: better or worse than last year or a rival? |
| Share out the result | Partners share profit; companies pay dividends from it |

**Profit is not cash.** The bank balance can rise because of a loan, capital introduced or the sale of a non-current asset, none of which is profit. It can fall because of a purchase of equipment or drawings, neither of which is an expense.

## Monitoring progress

- Compare the same headings for equal periods (month with month, year with year).
- Express each cost as a percentage of revenue so periods of different size can be compared.
- Look for costs rising faster than revenue: they explain a falling profit even when sales grow.
- Act early: regular figures warn the owner before cash runs out.

**Quick method:** change = new - old; percentage change = change / old x 100. For example, rent of 4,000 rising to 4,600 is a change of 600, which is 600 / 4,000 x 100 = 15%.

## Supporting decisions

Accounting information lets alternatives be compared in money terms before a choice is made. Common decisions: pricing, cutting or controlling costs, replacing assets, allowing credit, borrowing, expanding, employing staff.

A strong decision answer does four things:

1. Makes a clear choice.
2. Supports it with a figure from the question.
3. Gives one factor against, often cash or a non-financial point.
4. Concludes.

Outside users decide from the same records: lenders on loans, suppliers on credit, the tax authority on tax due.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Book-keeping | The detailed recording of financial transactions |
| Accounting | Summarising, analysing, interpreting and communicating the recorded information |
| Revenue | Income earned from the business's main activity, such as sales |
| Expenses | Costs of running the business in the period |
| Profit | Revenue greater than expenses for a period |
| Loss | Expenses greater than revenue for a period |
| Drawings | Money or goods taken by the owner for private use |
| Monitoring progress | Comparing results over time to see whether the business is improving |
| Decision-making | Choosing between alternatives using the available information |
| Financial statements | Summaries such as the statement of profit or loss and the statement of financial position |

## Exam traps

- "Book-keeping is done by a junior and accounting by a senior" scores nothing. Describe the work.
- A higher bank balance is not evidence of a profit.
- Drawings are not an expense, so they never reduce profit.
- An **explain** question needs a reason or consequence for each point; a list of purposes earns only the identification marks.
- In **advise** questions, a recommendation with no figure is weak, and a calculation with no recommendation does not answer the question.
- Percentage change is worked on the **old** figure, not the new one.

## Self-test

**1.** Give one activity that is book-keeping and one that is accounting.

**2.** Why does accounting depend on accurate book-keeping?

**3.** Define profit.

**4.** State two purposes of measuring profit, apart from judging performance.

**5.** A bank balance rose by 6,000 in a year, during which a loan of 10,000 was received. What would the balance have done without the loan?

**6.** Why should an owner not take drawings above profit year after year?

**7.** Revenue rose from 50,000 to 55,000. Calculate the percentage increase.

**8.** Wages were 15,000 on revenue of 50,000 and 18,700 on revenue of 55,000. Express each as a percentage of revenue and comment.

**9.** Name two decisions an owner can make with accounting information.

**10.** Why does a lender ask for past profit figures?

**11.** State the meaning of the command word "advise".

**12.** Give one reason a cash purchase of new equipment lowers the bank balance but does not reduce profit by the full amount in the year of purchase.

## Self-test answers

**1.** Book-keeping: entering invoices in the sales journal, posting to the ledger, balancing accounts. Accounting: preparing the statement of profit or loss, analysing the results, advising the owner.

**2.** The statements are built from the records, so any missing or misposted entry makes the statements, and decisions based on them, wrong.

**3.** The amount by which revenue earned in a period is greater than the expenses incurred in earning it.

**4.** Any two: to set drawings; to support a loan application; to calculate tax; to compare with other periods or businesses; to share profit between partners or pay dividends.

**5.** It would have fallen by 4,000 (6,000 - 10,000).

**6.** Each year the excess reduces the owner's capital, so the business is gradually run down.

**7.** 5,000 / 50,000 x 100 = 10%.

**8.** 15,000 / 50,000 = 30%; 18,700 / 55,000 = 34%. Wages are growing faster than revenue, which reduces profit, so wage costs should be investigated.

**9.** Any two: setting prices, controlling costs, replacing an asset, offering credit, borrowing, expanding, employing staff.

**10.** Past profits are evidence of whether the business can afford interest and repayments.

**11.** Write down a suggested course of action in a given situation.

**12.** The equipment is capital expenditure; only part of its cost (depreciation) is charged as an expense each year of its life.
