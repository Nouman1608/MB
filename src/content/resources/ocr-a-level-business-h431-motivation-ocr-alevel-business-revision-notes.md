## What the specification lists

- Meaning of motivation.
- Theory: content (McClelland, Herzberg, Maslow), expectancy (Vroom), goal-setting (Locke, Drucker's management by objectives) and excellence (Peters).
- Evaluate the usefulness of theories.
- Monetary and non-monetary methods; usefulness of motivation practices.
- Impact and importance of motivation; recommend and justify methods.

## Core recall

### Meaning

- **Motivation:** the desire to work towards a goal; the reason a person puts in effort.
- It differs from satisfaction: a satisfied worker may still make little effort.

### Content theories

| Theory | Main idea | Business use |
|---|---|---|
| Maslow | Five levels: physiological, safety, social, esteem, self-actualisation; a level motivates only until it is met | Find the worker's level; match the reward |
| Herzberg | Hygiene factors (pay, conditions, supervision) prevent dissatisfaction; motivators (achievement, recognition, interesting work) raise motivation | Fix hygiene first, then enrich jobs |
| McClelland | Needs for achievement, power and affiliation | Match tasks: stretch targets, leadership roles, team projects |

### Expectancy theory (Vroom)

- Force = **E × I × V**.
- E: expectancy, effort leads to performance (0 to 1).
- I: instrumentality, performance leads to reward (0 to 1).
- V: valence, how much the reward is valued.
- Any factor at zero means zero motivation.
- Use: diagnose which link is weak.

### Goal-setting and excellence

- **Locke:** specific, challenging, accepted goals plus feedback improve performance.
- **Drucker (MBO):** manager and employee agree objectives, review progress and results.
- **Peters:** excellence comes from valuing people, autonomy, initiative and closeness to customers.

### Methods

| | Monetary | Non-monetary |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | Pay, bonus, commission, profit share, share options | Recognition, enrichment, training, flexible hours, team working |
| Pros | Fast, clear | Cheaper, longer lasting |
| Cons | Costly, fades, can push short-term focus | Slower, depends on managers |

- **Enrichment:** more depth (planning, deciding); fits Herzberg.
- **Enlargement:** more tasks at the same level.
- **Rotation:** moving between tasks to reduce boredom.

### Evaluating a theory: three questions

1. Does it fit this business and its workers?
2. Can the business measure or use it?
3. What does it ignore?

### Impact and importance

- Output, quality, absence and turnover.
- Customer service in service businesses.
- Greatest where labour is a large share of cost, skills are scarce and staff have discretion.
- Stakeholders: owners (profit), managers (easier supervision), customers (service), staff (satisfaction).

### Recommend and justify

Diagnose with a theory, choose a method, count its cost, name a risk and give a judgement.

## Quick worked reminder

Maple Contact Centre (invented): E 0.8 for every option, valence 6. Pooled team bonus I 0.5 gives 0.8 × 0.5 × 6 = 2.4. Individual bonus I 0.9 gives 4.32, which is 80% higher. A £150 monthly bonus for 40 agents costs £6,000; a rise from 60 to 66 calls a day adds 5,280 calls a month, worth £13,200 at £2.50 each; net gain £7,200. Watch quality and stress.

## Exam prompts and how to approach them

- **"Explain Herzberg's two-factor theory":** separate hygiene factors from motivators, say pay is a hygiene factor, and give an example of each from the case.
- **"Evaluate a bonus scheme":** give the benefit (clear target, fast effect), the cost (money, effect fades), a risk (rushed work) and a judgement.
- **"Recommend a method":** diagnose with a theory, choose monetary or non-monetary methods, count the cost and name a risk.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Motivation | The desire to work towards a goal |
| Hygiene factor | A factor that prevents dissatisfaction but does not motivate |
| Motivator | A factor that raises motivation, such as recognition |
| Self-actualisation | Reaching one's full potential |
| Expectancy | Belief that effort will lead to performance |
| Instrumentality | Belief that performance will lead to reward |
| Valence | The value a person places on the reward |
| Management by objectives | Agreeing objectives between manager and employee |
| Job enrichment | Giving a job more depth and responsibility |
| Profit share | A share of profit paid to employees |

## Exam traps

- Pay is a hygiene factor in Herzberg's theory, not a motivator.
- Maslow's levels are needs, not rewards; link each level to a case example.
- A zero in any Vroom factor makes the force zero.
- Do not recommend a pay rise alone without a cost and a risk.
- Name the theory used and apply it to the case.

## Self-test

1. Define motivation.
2. Name Maslow's five levels in order.
3. Give two Herzberg hygiene factors and two motivators.
4. State McClelland's three needs.
5. Calculate Vroom's force when E = 0.6, I = 0.5 and V = 8.
6. What does an instrumentality of 0.2 suggest?
7. What did Locke find about goals?
8. Explain management by objectives in one sentence.
9. Give one monetary and one non-monetary method.
10. Distinguish between job enrichment and job enlargement.
11. Give one weakness of a bonus scheme.
12. A bonus costs £5,000 a month and adds £9,200 of revenue. Calculate the net gain.

### Answers

1. The desire to work towards a goal; the reason a person puts in effort.
2. Physiological, safety, social, esteem, self-actualisation.
3. Hygiene: pay, working conditions, supervision, job security (any two). Motivators: achievement, recognition, interesting work, responsibility, promotion (any two).
4. Achievement, power and affiliation.
5. 0.6 × 0.5 × 8 = **2.4**.
6. The worker thinks good performance is unlikely to lead to a reward, so the force is low.
7. Specific, challenging goals that the person accepts, with feedback, lead to higher performance than vague goals.
8. Managers and employees agree objectives together, then review progress and results.
9. Monetary: a bonus. Non-monetary: recognition (or training, flexible hours).
10. Enrichment adds depth, with more planning and decision-making; enlargement adds more tasks at the same level.
11. It can be costly, its effect may fade, or it may encourage rushed work and stress.
12. £9,200 − £5,000 = **£4,200**.
