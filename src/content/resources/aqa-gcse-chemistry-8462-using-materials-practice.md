These questions cover section **4.10.3 Using materials (chemistry only)** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462)
specification, for teaching from September 2016. The content is assessed on Paper 2 in the separate chemistry
qualification. The specification marks nothing in this section (HT only), so no question is labelled Higher tier

## Questions

**1.** A student sets up three test tubes, each with an iron nail.
Tube A: nail in tap water, open to the air.
Tube B: nail in boiled water with a layer of oil on the surface.
Tube C: nail with anhydrous calcium chloride, sealed with a bung.

**(a)** Predict which tubes show rusting after a week. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain the results for tubes B and C. **[3]**
**(c)** State the conclusion about the conditions needed for rusting. **[1]**

**2.** Methods are used to prevent iron objects from rusting.

**(a)** Name **three** methods that act as a barrier. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why barrier methods stop working if the coating is scratched. **[1]**

**3.** A steel water pipe is connected to a block of magnesium buried in the ground.

**(a)** Name this type of protection. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain, using relative reactivity, how the magnesium protects the pipe. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why the magnesium block has to be replaced from time to time. **[1]**

**4.** A tin can is made of iron coated with tin. Explain why the iron rusts quickly once the tin coating is scratched, given that tin is less reactive than iron. **[2]**

**5.** Alloys have different properties from the pure metals they contain.

**(a)** Name the metals in bronze and in brass. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why most metals in everyday use are alloys. **[2]**
**(c)** Give a use for aluminium alloys and the property that makes them suitable. **[2]**

**6.** Gold jewellery is made from an alloy.

**(a)** State what the term 24 carat means. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of gold in 14 carat gold. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** A pendant has a mass of 12.0 g and is 18 carat gold. Calculate the mass of gold. **[2]**

**7.** The table shows data on three steels.

| Steel | Percentage of carbon | Other metals | Properties |
|---|---|---|---|
| X | 0.1 | none | Soft, easily shaped |
| Y | 1.0 | none | Very hard, brittle |
| Z | 0.1 | 18% chromium, 8% nickel | Hard, does not rust |

**(a)** Identify which steel is best for car body panels and give a reason. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify which steel is best for kitchen sinks and give a reason. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest a use for steel Y. **[1]**

**8.** Soda-lime glass and borosilicate glass are both used in laboratories and homes.

**(a)** Name the raw materials for soda-lime glass. **[3]**
**(b)** State how borosilicate glass differs in its raw materials and in its properties. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe how clay ceramics such as bricks are made. **[2]**

**9.** Polymers can be thermosoftening or thermosetting.

**(a)** Explain the difference in behaviour when each is heated. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the difference in terms of structure. **[2]**
**(c)** Low density and high density poly(ethene) are both made from ethene. State why they have different properties. **[1]**

**10.** A bicycle manufacturer compares materials for a frame.

| Material | Density / g/cm³ | Relative strength | Rusts? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steel | 7.8 | 1.0 | Yes |
| Aluminium alloy | 2.7 | 0.8 | No |
| Carbon fibre composite | 1.6 | 1.2 | No |

**(a)** Calculate how many times denser steel is than the carbon fibre composite. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe what a composite is and name the matrix and reinforcement in a carbon fibre composite. **[3]**
**(c)** Evaluate the use of carbon fibre composite for the frame. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Only **tube A** rusts [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Tube B: the boiled water has **no dissolved air** and the oil stops air entering [1], so there is no oxygen and no rusting [1]. Tube C: calcium chloride absorbs moisture, so there is **no water** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** Both **air (oxygen) and water** are necessary for iron to rust [1]. **[1]**

**2. (a)** Any three [1] each: greasing; painting; electroplating; coating with plastic. **[3]**
**(b)** Air and water can reach the iron through the break [1]. **[1]**

**3. (a)** **Sacrificial** protection [1]
**(b)** Magnesium is **more reactive** than iron [1], so it **loses electrons** (corrodes) in preference to iron [1], which protects the pipe [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** The magnesium is **used up** as it corrodes [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Tin is **less reactive** than iron, so the iron is the metal that corrodes once exposed [1]; the iron has no sacrificial protection, and the tin may even make it rust faster [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** Bronze: **copper and tin** [1]; brass: **copper and zinc** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Pure metals are often **too soft** [1]; alloys are **harder / stronger** because the different-sized atoms distort the layers and stop them sliding [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Aircraft [1]; **low density** [1] (accept other correct pairings). **[2]**

**6. (a)** **100% pure gold** [1]
**(b)** 14 ÷ 24 × 100 [1] = **58.3%** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** 18 ÷ 24 = 0.75 [1]; 0.75 × 12.0 = **9.00 g** [1] **[2]**

**7. (a)** **X** [1], because it is **soft and easily shaped** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** **Z** [1], because it is **hard and does not rust** (contains chromium and nickel) [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Any one: cutting tools; knives; chisels [1]. **[1]**

**8. (a)** **Sand**, **sodium carbonate** and **limestone** [1] [1] [1] **[3]**
**(b)** It is made from **sand and boron trioxide** [1]; it **melts at a higher temperature** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Wet clay is **shaped** [1] and then **heated in a furnace** [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** A thermosoftening polymer **melts / softens** on heating [1]; a thermosetting polymer **does not melt** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Thermosoftening: **separate chains** held by weak forces that break on heating [1]. Thermosetting: chains joined by **strong cross-links** (covalent bonds) [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** They are made under **different conditions**, giving different chain structures (branched or straight) [1]. **[1]**

**10. (a)** 7.8 ÷ 1.6 [1] = **4.9** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** A composite has a **matrix (binder)** surrounding and binding the **reinforcement** [1]; in carbon fibre, the matrix is a **polymer resin** [1] and the reinforcement is **carbon fibres** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** Advantages: **strongest** (1.2), **lowest density** (1.6), does not rust [1]; disadvantage: more expensive / harder to repair or recycle; a conclusion [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying rusting needs only water, or giving air without water.
- Confusing sacrificial protection with a barrier.
- Calling an alloy a compound.
- Mixing up thermosoftening and thermosetting polymers.
- Calculating carats as grams instead of fractions of 24.
