## Questions

**1.** The first term of a sequence is 3. The rule is "multiply by 4, then subtract 6". Write down the next three terms. **[2]**

**2.** The nth term of a sequence is 6n − 5.

**(a)** Write down the first three terms. **[1]**
**(b)** Work out the 40th term. **[1]**
**(c)** Show that 100 is not a term of this sequence. **[2]**

**3.** Here is a sequence: 23, 19, 15, 11, ...

**(a)** Work out a formula for the nth term. **[2]**
**(b)** Which is the first term below −50? Give its value. **[3]**

**4.** A gardener lays paving stones in a path. Path 1 uses 5 stones, path 2 uses 8 stones and path 3 uses 11 stones. Each path is made by adding a column of 3 stones to the one before.

**(a)** Write a formula for the number of stones in path n. **[2]**
**(b)** One path uses 128 stones. Which path is it? **[2]**

**5.** **(a)** Write down the 8th triangular number. **[1]**
**(b)** From 45, 64, 100, 125, 216, write down all the cube numbers. **[2]**
**(c)** Show that the sum of the 3rd and 4th triangular numbers is a square number. **[1]**

**6.** A Fibonacci-type sequence has 3rd term 13 and 6th term 57. Find the first two terms and write down the first six terms. **[4]**

**7.** **(a)** Find the common ratio of the geometric progression 625, 375, 225, ... and write down the next two terms. **[3]**
**(b)** Decide whether 3, 6, 11, 18, ... is arithmetic, geometric or quadratic. Give a reason and write down the next term. **[3]**

**8.** **(Higher tier only)** A sequence has x₁ = 5 and xₙ₊₁ = 2xₙ − 7.

**(a)** Find x₂, x₃ and x₄. **[3]**
**(b)** A different sequence has the same rule xₙ₊₁ = 2xₙ − 7 and x₃ = 11. Find its first term x₁. **[3]**

**9.** **(Higher tier only)** Here is a sequence: 6, 13, 24, 39, 58, ...

**(a)** Work out the nth term. **[4]**
**(b)** Work out the 20th term. **[1]**
**(c)** Show that 500 is not a term of the sequence. **[2]**

**10.** **(Higher tier only)** Find the nth term of 3, 7, 12, 18, 25, ... Give your answer in the form an² + bn. **[4]**

**11.** **(Higher tier only)**

**(a)** Find the nth term of 1/4, 2/7, 3/10, 4/13, ... **[2]**
**(b)** Find the nth term of √5, √10, √15, √20, ... Write the 4th term in the form a√5, and find the 20th term. **[3]**
**(c)** The geometric sequence 3, 3√2, 6, 6√2, ... has common ratio √2. Write down its nth term and find the 6th term in the form a√2. **[3]**

**12.** Sequence A has nth term 5n + 2. Sequence B has nth term 40 − 3n.

**(a)** Write down the first three terms of each sequence. **[2]**
**(b)** Show that no term of A is equal to the term of B in the same position. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the first position in which the term of A is greater than the term of B. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** 2nd term: 4 × 3 − 6 = 6 [1]. 3rd term: 4 × 6 − 6 = 18, 4th term: 4 × 18 − 6 = 66 [1]. The terms are **6, 18, 66**.
*Examiner insight:* Show each term on its own line. A slip early on carries through the rest, and written working lets you see where it happened.

**2. (a)** n = 1, 2, 3 gives 6 − 5, 12 − 5, 18 − 5, so **1, 7, 13**. [1]
**(b)** 6 × 40 − 5 = 240 − 5 = **235**. [1]
**(c)** Set 6n − 5 = 100, so 6n = 105 and n = 17.5 [1]. The position must be a whole number, so 100 is not a term. [1]
*Examiner insight:* A "show that" answer needs the equation solved and a sentence linking the non-whole n to the conclusion. A bare "no" does not show anything.

**3. (a)** d = −4 [1]. The terms of −4n are −4, −8, ... and 23 is 27 more than −4, so the nth term is **27 − 4n**. [1]
**(b)** Solve 27 − 4n < −50, so 4n > 77 and n > 19.25 [1]. The first whole n is 20 [1]. The 20th term is 27 − 80 = **−53**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Give the term, not only the position. Check your answer: the 19th term is −49, which is not below −50, so the 20th is the first.

**4. (a)** The common difference is 3, so start with 3n [1]. 3n gives 3, 6, 9 and each path has 2 more, so **3n + 2**. [1]
**(b)** 3n + 2 = 128 gives 3n = 126 [1], so n = 42, which is **path 42**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* In context questions, use the structure of the diagram to explain your formula. Here 3n is the columns of 3 and the 2 is the part that does not change.

**5. (a)** 8 × 9/2 = **36**. [1]
**(b)** 64 = 4³ [1] and 125 = 5³ and 216 = 6³ [1]. The cube numbers are **64, 125 and 216**.
**(c)** 3rd triangular number is 6 and 4th is 10, and 6 + 10 = 16 = 4², **a square number**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Write down the numbers you are adding, not only "16". The line 6 + 10 = 16 is what shows the claim holds.

**6.** Call the first two terms a and b. The 3rd term gives a + b = 13 [1]. The 6th term is 3a + 5b = 57 [1]. Substituting a = 13 − b: 39 − 3b + 5b = 57, so 2b = 18, b = 9 [1]. Then a = 4. The sequence is **4, 9, 13, 22, 35, 57**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Check by adding forwards: 13 + 22 = 35 and 22 + 35 = 57. If the check fails, the algebra has gone wrong.

**7. (a)** r = 375 ÷ 625 = 3/5 [1]. Check: 225 ÷ 375 = 3/5. Next terms: 225 × 3/5 = 135 [1] and 135 × 3/5 = 81. [1]
**(b)** Differences 3, 5, 7. Not constant, so not arithmetic [1]. Ratios 6 ÷ 3 = 2 and 11 ÷ 6 = 11/6 differ, so not geometric. Second differences are 2, 2, so it is **quadratic** [1]. Next first difference 9, next term 18 + 9 = **27**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Give a reason for the type: name the differences or ratios you tested. A label with no evidence leaves nothing to judge.

**8. (a)** x₂ = 2 × 5 − 7 = 3 [1]. x₃ = 2 × 3 − 7 = −1 [1]. x₄ = 2 × (−1) − 7 = **−9**. [1]
**(b)** x₃ = 2x₂ − 7 = 11, so 2x₂ = 18 and x₂ = 9 [1]. x₂ = 2x₁ − 7 = 9, so 2x₁ = 16 [1]. **x₁ = 8**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Be careful with the negative in x₄. Write 2 × (−1) in brackets so that −2 − 7 gives −9.

**9. (a)** First differences 7, 11, 15, 19 and second differences 4, 4, 4 [1]. So a = 4 ÷ 2 = 2 [1]. Terms of 2n²: 2, 8, 18, 32, 50. Subtracting from the sequence gives 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 [1], which is n + 3. The nth term is **2n² + n + 3**. [1]
**(b)** 2 × 400 + 20 + 3 = **823**. [1]
**(c)** 2n² + n + 3 = 500 gives 2n² + n − 497 = 0 [1]. The discriminant is 1 + 8 × 497 = 3977, which lies between 63² = 3969 and 64² = 4096, so it is not a perfect square and n is not a whole number. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Check your formula on the first two terms before using it in (b) and (c). A wrong formula spoils all three parts.

**10.** First differences 4, 5, 6, 7 and second differences 1, 1, 1 [1]. So a = 1/2 [1]. Terms of n²/2: 0.5, 2, 4.5, 8, 12.5. Subtracting: 2.5, 5, 7.5, 10, 12.5 [1], which is 2.5n. The nth term is **n²/2 + 5n/2**, which equals n(n + 5)/2. [1]
*Examiner insight:* When the second difference is odd, a is a fraction. Keep it as a fraction rather than rounding, and the remainder will then be linear.

**11. (a)** Numerators 1, 2, 3, 4 give n [1]. Denominators 4, 7, 10, 13 increase by 3, so 3n + 1. The nth term is **n/(3n + 1)**. [1]
**(b)** The numbers under the root are 5, 10, 15, 20, so the nth term is **√(5n)** [1]. The 4th term is √20 = 2√5 [1]. The 20th term is √100 = **10**. [1]
**(c)** The first term is 3 and each term multiplies by √2, so the nth term is **3 × (√2)ⁿ⁻¹** [1]. For n = 6: 3 × (√2)⁵ [1]. (√2)⁵ = 4√2, so the 6th term is **12√2**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* For a fraction sequence, treat the numerator and the denominator as two separate sequences. For surds, simplify the final answer fully.

**12. (a)** A: 7, 12, 17. B: 37, 34, 31. [2]
**(b)** Set 5n + 2 = 40 − 3n, so 8n = 38 [1]. n = 4.75, which is not a whole number, so there is no position where they are equal. [1]
**(c)** A is greater when 5n + 2 > 40 − 3n, so n > 4.75 [1]. The first whole position is **5**. Check: A = 27 and B = 25. [1]
*Examiner insight:* An inequality gives a range of n, so round to a whole position and check it. Testing positions 4 and 5 directly confirms the answer.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the common difference as the constant, so 6n − 5 becomes n − 5.
- Dropping the sign on a decreasing sequence, writing 27 + 4n for 27 − 4n.
- Giving a decimal n as a position instead of concluding that the number is not a term.
- Mixing up arithmetic and geometric: adding the ratio or multiplying by the difference.
- Calling a sequence quadratic without computing the second differences.
- Not halving the second difference before finding the n² coefficient.
- Skipping the check on the first two terms of a quadratic nth term.
- Leaving a surd answer unsimplified, such as √20 instead of 2√5.
- Treating xₙ₊₁ as xₙ + 1.
- Answering with the position when the question asks for the term.
