This set covers **section 1.8, Degree of accuracy**, of the Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) specification, Issue 2. **Tiers:** questions marked **(Higher tier only)** solve problems using upper and lower bounds, which the specification lists for the Higher Tier only. The Foundation Tier includes identifying bounds, so questions 4, 6, 8(a) and 8(b) are for both tiers. Part 8(c) uses a bound in a calculation, so it is marked **(Higher tier only)**.

Related: [section 1.8 study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-degree-of-accuracy-edexcel-igcse-maths/) and [section 1.8 revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-degree-of-accuracy-edexcel-igcse-maths-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Round 7 295 618

**(a)** to the nearest 1000 **[1]**

**(b)** to the nearest million. **[1]**

**2.** Write 0.006 395 1

**(a)** correct to 3 significant figures **[1]**

**(b)** correct to 2 decimal places. **[1]**

**3.** Write 485 296 correct to 2 significant figures. **[1]**

**4.**

**(a)** A rope is 6.3 m long, correct to 1 decimal place. Write down its lower and upper bounds. **[2]**

**(b)** A bag of rice has a mass of 900 g, correct to the nearest 50 g. Write down its lower and upper bounds. **[2]**

**5.** Work out an estimate for (21.8 × 4.97) ÷ 0.489. You must show your working. **[3]**

**6.** A number n is 0.47, correct to 2 significant figures. Write down the error interval for n. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.**

**(a)** Work out an estimate for √99.6 ÷ 2.03². Show your working. **[2]**

**(b)** Is your estimate bigger or smaller than the exact value? Give a reason. **[1]**

**8.** The attendance at a football match was 12 500, correct to the nearest 100. Each ticket cost £24.

**(a)** Write down the smallest possible attendance. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the largest possible attendance. **[1]**

**(c)** **(Higher tier only)** Work out the least possible amount of money taken from ticket sales. **[1]**

**9.** **(Higher tier only)** A tank fills with 450 litres of water, correct to the nearest 10 litres, in 32 minutes, correct to the nearest minute.

**(a)** Work out the upper bound of the rate of flow, in litres per minute. **[2]**

**(b)** Work out the lower bound of the rate of flow. **[2]**

**(c)** Write down the rate of flow to a suitable degree of accuracy. Give a reason for your answer. **[1]**

**10.** **(Higher tier only)** A rectangle has length 9.4 cm and width 3.7 cm, both correct to 1 decimal place.

**(a)** Work out the upper bound of the difference between the length and the width. **[2]**

**(b)** Work out the lower bound of the area of the rectangle. **[2]**

**11.** **(Higher tier only)** A lorry can carry a maximum load of 2.5 tonnes, correct to the nearest 0.1 tonne. Crates each have a mass of 64 kg, correct to the nearest kg. Work out the greatest number of crates the lorry can definitely carry. Show your working. **[3]**

**12.** **(Higher tier only)** A circle has radius 7.3 cm, correct to 1 decimal place.

**(a)** Work out the lower and upper bounds of the area of the circle. **[3]**

**(b)** Write down the area to a suitable degree of accuracy. Give a reason. **[1]**

**13.** Ali says, "4.6, correct to 1 decimal place, has an upper bound of 4.649." Explain why the upper bound is 4.65. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **7 296 000** [1]

**(b)** **7 000 000** [1]

**2. (a)** **0.006 40** [1]

**(b)** **0.01** [1]

**3.** **490 000** [1]

**4. (a)** **6.25 m** and **6.35 m** [2] (1 mark for each)

**(b)** Half of 50 g is 25 g [1]: **875 g** and **925 g** [1]

**5.** 21.8 → 20, 4.97 → 5, 0.489 → 0.5 [1]; (20 × 5) ÷ 0.5 [1] = **200** [1]

**6.** Unit 0.01, half 0.005 [1]: **0.465 ≤ n < 0.475** [1]

**7. (a)** √100 ÷ 2² [1] = 10 ÷ 4 = **2.5** [1]

**(b)** **Bigger**: 99.6 was rounded up and 2.03 was rounded down, and both make the result larger [1]

**8. (a)** **12 450** [1]

**(b)** **12 549** [1]

**(c)** 12 450 × 24 = **£298 800** [1]

**9. (a)** 455 ÷ 31.5 [1] = **14.44 litres per minute** (2 d.p.) [1]

*Examiner insight:* The largest rate comes from the most water in the least time, so the upper bound of the volume is divided by the lower bound of the time.

**(b)** 445 ÷ 32.5 [1] = **13.69 litres per minute** (2 d.p.) [1]

**(c)** **14 litres per minute**, because both bounds round to 14 to 2 significant figures [1]

**10. (a)** 9.45 − 3.65 [1] = **5.8 cm** [1]

**(b)** 9.35 × 3.65 [1] = **34.1275 cm²** [1]

**11.** Lower bound of load = 2450 kg and upper bound of crate = 64.5 kg [1]; 2450 ÷ 64.5 = 37.98… [1]; **37 crates** [1]

**12. (a)** Bounds of radius 7.25 and 7.35 [1]; π × 7.25² = **165.1 cm²** [1]; π × 7.35² = **169.7 cm²** [1]

**(b)** **170 cm²** (2 s.f.), because both bounds round to 170 to 2 s.f. [1]

**13.** Every value up to (but not including) 4.65 rounds to 4.6, including 4.6499…, which is greater than 4.649; the bound is the boundary value 4.65 [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing 0.0064 for question 2(a), which has only 2 significant figures.
- Writing 49 instead of 490 000 in question 3.
- Giving 6.349 as the upper bound in question 4(a).
- Rounding 0.489 to 0.49 in question 5 instead of to 1 significant figure.
- Using 12 550 as the largest attendance in question 8(b); people are counted in whole numbers.
- Using the upper bound of the load in question 11, which does not guarantee the crates fit.
