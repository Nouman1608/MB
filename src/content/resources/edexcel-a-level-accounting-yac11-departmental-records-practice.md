## Section A

**1.** State **two** reasons why a business with several departments prepares departmental statements. **[2]**

**2.** Suggest a suitable basis for apportioning each of these costs between departments.

**(a)** Rent of the building **[1]**

**(b)** Advertising **[1]**

**(c)** Canteen costs for staff **[1]**

**3.** Distinguish between allocation and apportionment of costs. **[2]**

**4.** Rent of 18,000 is shared between three departments by floor area. The areas are 450, 300 and 150 square metres. Calculate the rent charged to each department. **[3]**

**5.** A department had revenue of 45,000, opening inventory of 3,000, purchases of 25,000 and closing inventory of 4,000. Calculate its cost of sales, its gross profit and its gross profit as a percentage of revenue (to one decimal place). **[4]**

## Section B

Cobalt Garden Centre has three departments: Plants, Equipment and Cafe. The figures for the year ended 31 December 2026 are below.

| | Plants | Equipment | Cafe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | 84,000 | 52,000 | 24,000 |
| Opening inventory | 6,000 | 9,000 | 1,000 |
| Purchases | 41,000 | 31,000 | 11,000 |
| Closing inventory | 7,000 | 10,000 | 1,500 |
| Direct wages | 12,000 | 7,500 | 8,000 |
| Floor area (square metres) | 500 | 300 | 200 |

Shared expenses: rent 20,000 and heating and lighting 5,000 (both apportioned by floor area); advertising 4,800 (apportioned by revenue); office costs 7,500 (shared equally).

**6.** Prepare a columnar statement of profit or loss for Cobalt Garden Centre for the year ended 31 December 2026, showing the profit or loss of each department and in total. **[14]**

**7.** Using your answer to Question 6, calculate for each department:

**(a)** gross profit as a percentage of revenue (one decimal place) **[2]**

**(b)** profit or loss as a percentage of revenue (one decimal place) **[2]**

**8.** Explain why the loss shown for a department may be misleading when deciding whether to close it. **[3]**

**9.** Calculate the contribution of each department, defined as gross profit less direct wages, and the total shared expenses. **[3]**

**10.** The owner is considering closing the Cafe. The shared expenses would not change. Calculate the effect on total profit and recommend whether the Cafe should close. **[6]**

**11.** Explain **one** limitation of departmental statements. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: to identify which departments are profitable and which are not [1]; to compare the performance of departments [1]; to help decisions on resources, such as closing or expanding a department [1]; to help control costs by showing where they arise [1].

**2. (a)** Floor area [1]

**(b)** Revenue [1]

**(c)** Number of employees [1]

**3.** Allocation charges a cost that belongs entirely to one department to that department in full [1]; apportionment divides a cost shared by departments between them on a fair basis [1].

**4.** Total area 450 + 300 + 150 = 900 [1]; 18,000 × 450 ÷ 900 = **9,000** [1]; 18,000 × 300 ÷ 900 = **6,000**, and 18,000 × 150 ÷ 900 = **3,000** [1].

**5.** Cost of sales 3,000 + 25,000 − 4,000 = **24,000** [1]; gross profit 45,000 − 24,000 = **21,000** [1]; 21,000 ÷ 45,000 × 100 [1] = **46.7%** [1].

**6.** Cobalt Garden Centre: Statement of profit or loss for the year ended 31 December 2026

| | Plants | Equipment | Cafe | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | 84,000 | 52,000 | 24,000 | 160,000 |
| Opening inventory | 6,000 | 9,000 | 1,000 | 16,000 |
| Purchases | 41,000 | 31,000 | 11,000 | 83,000 |
| | 47,000 | 40,000 | 12,000 | 99,000 |
| Closing inventory | (7,000) | (10,000) | (1,500) | (18,500) |
| **Cost of sales** | (40,000) | (30,000) | (10,500) | (80,500) |
| **Gross profit** | 44,000 | 22,000 | 13,500 | 79,500 |
| Direct wages | 12,000 | 7,500 | 8,000 | 27,500 |
| Rent | 10,000 | 6,000 | 4,000 | 20,000 |
| Heating and lighting | 2,500 | 1,500 | 1,000 | 5,000 |
| Advertising | 2,520 | 1,560 | 720 | 4,800 |
| Office costs | 2,500 | 2,500 | 2,500 | 7,500 |
| **Total expenses** | (29,520) | (19,060) | (16,220) | (64,800) |
| **Profit/(loss) for the year** | **14,480** | **2,940** | **(2,720)** | **14,700** |

Revenue row [1]; cost of sales for each department [3]; total cost of sales [1]; gross profit for each department [2]; rent apportioned 10,000 / 6,000 / 4,000 [1]; heating and lighting 2,500 / 1,500 / 1,000 [1]; advertising by revenue (84 : 52 : 24 of 160) giving 2,520 / 1,560 / 720 [1]; office costs 2,500 each [1]; direct wages [1]; total expenses [1]; profit or loss for each department and total **14,700** [1].

**7. (a)** Plants 44,000 ÷ 84,000 × 100 = **52.4%**; Equipment 22,000 ÷ 52,000 × 100 = **42.3%**; Cafe 13,500 ÷ 24,000 × 100 = **56.3%** [2 for three correct, 1 for two correct]. Note that 13,500 ÷ 24,000 = 56.25, which rounds to 56.3%.

**(b)** Plants 14,480 ÷ 84,000 × 100 = **17.2%**; Equipment 2,940 ÷ 52,000 × 100 = **5.7%**; Cafe −2,720 ÷ 24,000 × 100 = **−11.3%** [2 for three correct, 1 for two correct].

**8.** Part of the loss comes from shared costs that are apportioned to the department [1]; those costs may continue even if the department closes [1], so closing it could lower total profit rather than raise it [1].

**9.** Plants 44,000 − 12,000 = **32,000**; Equipment 22,000 − 7,500 = **14,500**; Cafe 13,500 − 8,000 = **5,500** [2]. Shared expenses 20,000 + 5,000 + 4,800 + 7,500 = **37,300** [1]. (Check: 32,000 + 14,500 + 5,500 − 37,300 = 14,700.)

**10.** The Cafe contributes 5,500 towards shared expenses [1]. If it closes and shared expenses stay the same, total profit falls by 5,500 from 14,700 to **9,200** [1]. The loss of 2,720 is not saved: removing it would wrongly suggest profit of 17,420 [1].
Recommendation: do not close the Cafe on financial grounds alone, because it adds 5,500 to profit [1], and it may attract customers for the other departments [1]. The owner should instead look at ways of improving it, such as reducing its direct wages of 8,000 relative to revenue, or using the space differently [1].

**11.** Any one limitation with a development: apportionment depends on a chosen basis [1], so a different basis gives different departmental profits, although the total is unchanged [1]; or departments depend on each other [1], so the results ignore the effect of one department on the sales of another [1]; or the statement is historical [1] and does not show what will happen if a department closes [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the total inventory figures instead of the department's own (Question 6).
- Apportioning advertising on floor area rather than revenue.
- Rounding the Cafe's gross profit percentage down (Question 7).
- Concluding that a department with a loss must close, without using contribution (Question 10).
- Totals that are not the sum of the departmental columns.
- Dividing by total revenue instead of department revenue.

## Approaching these questions

Build the table in stages: cost of sales and gross profit first, then direct expenses, then each apportioned expense on its own line with the working beside it. Check each apportioned row adds up to its original figure. For recommendations, separate the costs that stop if the department closes from the costs that do not, and then give a decision with a reason.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these Edexcel YAC11 questions from past papers?**
No. They are original questions on outcome 1.3.7, written for revision. Use them alongside the official past papers.

**Do departmental profits add up to the profit of the business?**
Yes, if all expenses have been shared out. Any cost that is not apportioned must be shown in the total column, and the totals must still equal the profit for the year.
