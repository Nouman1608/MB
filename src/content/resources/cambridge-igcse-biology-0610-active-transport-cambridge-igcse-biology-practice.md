These questions cover sub-topic 3.3, Active transport, of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. The definition is Core. Questions on its importance (including ion uptake by root hairs) and on protein carriers are labelled **(Extended)**, and a Core candidate can skip them. All data are invented.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which statement describes active transport? **[1]**

A: Movement of water from a dilute to a concentrated solution

B: Movement of particles from a higher to a lower concentration, using kinetic energy only

C: Movement of particles through a cell membrane from a lower to a higher concentration, using energy from respiration

D: Movement of particles through a cell wall from a higher to a lower concentration

**2.** State **one** similarity and **one** difference between active transport and diffusion. **[2]**

**3.** Name the cell structures where most of the energy for active transport is released. **[1]**

**4.** The concentration of an ion is 84 units inside a cell and 6 units in the surrounding solution. The ion continues to enter the cell.

**(a)** Calculate how many times more concentrated the ion is inside the cell. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the process moving the ion into the cell. **[1]**

**5.** *(Extended)* State what moves molecules or ions across a membrane during active transport. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** Roots were kept in an aerated solution of potassium ions at two temperatures. The uptake of potassium ions was measured.

| Time / hours | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Uptake at 25 °C (arbitrary units) | 0 | 10 | 19 | 27 |
| Uptake at 5 °C (arbitrary units) | 0 | 3 | 6 | 8 |

**(a)** Describe the effect of temperature on uptake. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain this effect. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the mean rate of uptake at 25 °C over the 3 hours. **[1]**

**(d)** Suggest why air was bubbled through the solution. **[1]**

**7.** In another experiment, a respiratory poison was added to the solution after 2 hours. Uptake of ions stopped almost at once. Explain this result. **[3]**

**8.** Roots killed by boiling stop absorbing ions from a dilute solution, but ions still enter the dead tissue slowly from a concentrated solution. Explain both observations. **[3]**

**9.** *(Extended)* Explain the importance of active transport for the uptake of mineral ions by root hair cells. **[4]**

**10.** *(Extended)* Plants growing in waterlogged soil often grow poorly. The air spaces in the soil are filled with water. Suggest and explain why the roots take up fewer mineral ions. **[4]**

**11.** *(Extended)* Describe how a protein carrier moves an ion across a cell membrane during active transport. **[3]**

**12.** A student writes: "Osmosis and active transport both need energy from respiration because both happen in living cells." Explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. A is osmosis, B is diffusion, and D wrongly names the cell wall and the direction. **[1]**

**2.** Similarity: both move particles, and both can move substances across cell membranes [1]. Difference: active transport moves particles against a concentration gradient using energy from respiration; diffusion moves them down a gradient using kinetic energy only [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Mitochondria [1]. **[1]**

**4. (a)** 84 ÷ 6 = **14 times** [1].

**(b)** Active transport [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Protein carriers [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** Uptake is greater at 25 °C than at 5 °C [1]: after 3 hours it is 27 units at 25 °C but only 8 units at 5 °C [1].

**(b)** At the lower temperature, the enzymes that control respiration work more slowly [1], so less energy is released for active transport of the ions [1].

**(c)** 27 ÷ 3 = **9 units per hour** [1].

**(d)** To supply oxygen for aerobic respiration in the root cells [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* In (b), temperature affects active transport through respiration. An answer that only says "particles move faster when warm" describes diffusion and misses the point.

**7.** The poison stops respiration in the root cells [1]. No energy is released [1], so the ions can no longer be moved against the concentration gradient by active transport [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Dead cells do not respire, so no energy is available for active transport [1]; ions cannot be taken up against the gradient from the dilute solution [1]. In a concentrated solution the ions are at a higher concentration outside, so some still enter by diffusion, which needs no energy from respiration [1]. **[3]**

**9.** Mineral ions such as nitrate and magnesium are usually at a lower concentration in the soil water than inside root hair cells [1]. Diffusion would move them out of the cell, not in [1]. Active transport uses energy from respiration to move the ions in against the concentration gradient [1]. The plant needs these ions, for example nitrate to make amino acids and proteins, or magnesium to make chlorophyll [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Waterlogged soil contains little oxygen [1], so root cells cannot carry out much aerobic respiration [1]. Less energy is released [1], so less active transport of mineral ions takes place, and the plant lacks the ions it needs for growth [1]. **[4]**

**11.** The ion binds to a specific protein carrier in the membrane, on the side of lower concentration [1]. Energy from respiration changes the shape of the carrier [1], which releases the ion on the other side of the membrane, where its concentration is higher [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Osmosis is a passive process: water moves down a water potential gradient using only the kinetic energy of the molecules [1]. Only active transport uses energy from respiration, because it moves particles against a concentration gradient [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "down" or "along" the concentration gradient for active transport.
- Giving the energy source as "food" or "the sun" rather than respiration.
- Explaining temperature effects with particle speed instead of respiration.
- (Extended) Calling ion uptake by root hairs diffusion.
- (Extended) Leaving out the shape change of the carrier, or the energy step.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I recognise an active transport question?**
Look for a substance moving to where its concentration is already higher, or uptake that falls when oxygen is removed or a respiratory poison is added.

**Do Core candidates need protein carriers?**
No. Protein carriers and the importance of active transport are Extended content; Core candidates need the definition.
