This set works through topic 16, Inheritance, of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027, one subtopic at a time: 16.1 Passage of information from parents to offspring (questions 1–2), 16.2 The roles of genes in determining the phenotype (questions 3–9) and 16.3 Gene control (question 10). It is A Level content, examined on Paper 4; chi-squared can also appear on Paper 5.

These differ from the [topic-level inheritance practice questions](/resources/a-level-biology-inheritance-practice/). For explanations, use the [inheritance study guide](/resources/a-level-biology-9700-inheritance/) and [revision notes](/resources/a-level-biology-9700-inheritance-revision-notes/).

Critical value of χ² at p = 0.05 with 3 degrees of freedom: 7.82.

## Questions

**1.** A species of insect has a diploid number of 8.

**(a)** State the number of chromosomes in a gamete, and explain why gametes are made by a reduction division. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain what is meant by a homologous pair of chromosomes. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the number of chromosome combinations possible in one insect's gametes by independent assortment alone. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the number of chromosome combinations possible in a zygote, and name the process this depends on. **[2]**
**(e)** Explain why the real number of genetically different gametes is far larger than your answer to (c). **[2]**

**2.** Four animal cells in meiosis, seen in photomicrographs, are described.

- P: paired homologous chromosomes lined up across the equator.
- Q: two cells, each with single chromatids moving to opposite poles.
- R: one cell, with a group of chromosomes of two chromatids at each pole, nuclear envelopes forming and the cell surface membrane pinching in.
- S: two cells, each with single chromosomes of two chromatids on the equator.

**(a)** Name the stage shown by each cell. **[4]**
**(b)** Describe what happens to the nuclear envelope and spindle between stages R and S. **[2]**
**(c)** State how stage R would differ in a plant cell. **[1]**

**3.** In peas, the allele Le (tall) is dominant to le (dwarf).

**(a)** Explain the terms locus and test cross. **[2]**
**(b)** A tall pea plant of unknown genotype is test crossed. Use genetic diagrams to show the two possible outcomes and what each shows. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why a Lele plant is as tall as a LeLe plant. **[2]**

**4.** The HBB gene has two codominant alleles, Hbᴬ and Hbˢ.

**(a)** Explain how the Hbˢ allele leads to sickle cell anaemia. **[4]**
**(b)** The ABO gene has alleles Iᴬ and Iᴮ (codominant) and Iᴼ (recessive). A group AB man and a group O woman plan children. Draw a genetic diagram and calculate the probability that their first two children are both group A. **[3]**

**5.** Haemophilia A is caused by a recessive allele of the F8 gene, on the X chromosome. A woman without haemophilia, whose father had haemophilia, has children with a man who has haemophilia.

**(a)** Explain why the woman must be a carrier. **[1]**
**(b)** Draw a genetic diagram and state the probability that a daughter of this couple has haemophilia. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain how the recessive F8 allele causes haemophilia. **[2]**

**6.** Explain how each allele affects the phenotype.

**(a)** The recessive TYR allele, which causes albinism. **[2]**
**(b)** The dominant HTT allele, which causes Huntington's disease; explain why one copy is enough. **[4]**

**7.** In fruit flies, normal wings (N) are dominant to vestigial wings (n), on an autosome. Red eyes (Xᴿ) are dominant to white eyes (Xʳ). A true-breeding red-eyed, normal-winged female was crossed with a white-eyed, vestigial-winged male.

**(a)** Give the genotypes of the F1 females and males. **[2]**
**(b)** The F1 were interbred. Draw a genetic diagram to show the F2 phenotypes and their ratio in each sex. **[6]**

**8.** In a plant, tall stem (T) is dominant to dwarf (t), and purple flowers (P) are dominant to white (p). A plant from a cross of TTPP × ttpp was test crossed with ttpp. The offspring were 143 tall purple, 137 dwarf white, 42 tall white and 38 dwarf purple.

**(a)** Calculate the expected number in each class if the genes are not linked. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate χ². Show your working. **[3]**
**(c)** State the degrees of freedom and what the χ² value shows. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain these results, using a genetic diagram of the test cross. **[4]**

**9.** In a plant, the dominant allele C codes for an enzyme that makes purple pigment. Gene I, on a different chromosome, has a dominant allele I coding for a protein that inhibits this enzyme. Alleles c and i code for non-functional proteins.

**(a)** A white IICC plant is crossed with a white iicc plant. State and explain the F1 phenotype. **[2]**
**(b)** An F1 plant is test crossed. Draw a genetic diagram and give the phenotype ratio. **[4]**
**(c)** Name this type of gene interaction. **[1]**

**10.** In *E. coli*, the lac operon codes for enzymes that take up and break down lactose.

**(a)** Distinguish between a structural gene and a regulatory gene, using the lac operon as an example. **[2]**
**(b)** β-galactosidase is an inducible enzyme. Explain how this differs from a repressible enzyme. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how adding lactose leads to the production of β-galactosidase. **[3]**
**(d)** State what is meant by a transcription factor. **[2]**
**(e)** Give one difference between how lactose switches on the lac operon and how gibberellin switches on genes for stem growth. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **4** [1]; gametes must be **haploid so fertilisation restores the diploid number** rather than doubling it [1].
**(b)** One maternal and one paternal chromosome [1] with the **same genes at the same loci**, though alleles may differ [1].
**(c)** 4 pairs: 2⁴ = **16** [1]
**(d)** 16 × 16 = **256** [1]; **random fertilisation** [1].
**(e)** **Crossing over** in prophase I exchanges sections between **non-sister chromatids** [1], making **new allele combinations** on chromatids [1].
*Examiner insight:* For (b), "two identical chromosomes" scores nothing: the mark depends on "same genes, same loci".

**2. (a)** P **metaphase I** [1]; Q **anaphase II** [1]; R **telophase I** [1]; S **metaphase II** [1].
**(b)** In prophase II the **nuclear envelopes break down** [1]; **new spindles form** and fibres attach to the centromeres [1].
**(c)** A **cell plate** forms instead of the membrane pinching in [1].
*Examiner insight:* Paired chromosomes on the equator always means metaphase I; single chromatids moving means anaphase II.

**3. (a)** Locus: the **position of a gene on a chromosome** [1]. Test cross: crossing an individual of dominant phenotype with a **homozygous recessive** one to find its genotype [1].
**(b)** LeLe × lele: all Lele, **all tall** [1]. Lele × lele: Lele and lele, **1 tall : 1 dwarf** [1]. Any dwarf offspring means **Lele**; all tall means **LeLe** [1].
**(c)** One Le allele codes for a **functional enzyme** that makes active gibberellin (GA₁) [1], **enough for normal stem elongation** [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (b), a diagram without the conclusion linking each ratio to a genotype loses the final mark.

**4. (a)** HBB codes for the **β-globin polypeptide** of haemoglobin [1]. In Hbˢ a **base substitution** changes **glutamic acid to valine** [1]. At low oxygen concentration haemoglobin S **forms fibres**, so red cells become **sickle-shaped** [1], carry less oxygen and **block capillaries** [1].
**(b)** IᴬIᴮ × IᴼIᴼ gives **1 IᴬIᴼ (group A) : 1 IᴮIᴼ (group B)** [1]. P(one child group A) = ½ [1]; P(both) = ½ × ½ = **¼** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Write codominant and multiple alleles as a gene letter with superscripts; plain A, B and O can cost the genotype mark.

**5. (a)** Her father gave her his only X, **Xʰ**, and she has no haemophilia, so she is **XᴴXʰ** [1].
**(b)** XᴴXʰ × XʰY; gametes Xᴴ, Xʰ and Xʰ, Y [1]; offspring XᴴXʰ carrier female, XʰXʰ female with haemophilia, XᴴY unaffected male, XʰY male with haemophilia [1]; probability a daughter has haemophilia = **½** [1].
**(c)** The allele codes for **non-functional factor VIII** [1], a **blood-clotting** protein, so blood clots slowly [1].
*Examiner insight:* "A daughter has haemophilia" (½) differs from "a child is a daughter with haemophilia" (¼).

**6. (a)** The allele codes for **non-functional tyrosinase** [1], so tyrosine is not converted to **melanin**, giving pale skin, hair and eyes [1].
**(b)** HTT codes for the protein **huntingtin** [1]. The disease allele has an **expanded CAG repeat**, giving the protein an **extra-long glutamine chain** [1]. This **damages neurones in the brain** [1]. One copy is enough because the abnormal protein causes damage **even when normal huntingtin is also made** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Name the gene, the protein and its effect separately; never call the gene "huntingtin".

**7. (a)** Females **NnXᴿXʳ** [1]; males **NnXᴿY** [1].
**(b)** Female gametes NXᴿ, NXʳ, nXᴿ, nXʳ [1]; male gametes NXᴿ, NY, nXᴿ, nY [1].

| | NXᴿ | NY | nXᴿ | nY |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NXᴿ | NNXᴿXᴿ | NNXᴿY | NnXᴿXᴿ | NnXᴿY |
| NXʳ | NNXᴿXʳ | NNXʳY | NnXᴿXʳ | NnXʳY |
| nXᴿ | NnXᴿXᴿ | NnXᴿY | nnXᴿXᴿ | nnXᴿY |
| nXʳ | NnXᴿXʳ | NnXʳY | nnXᴿXʳ | nnXʳY |

Correct genotypes [1]. Females **3 normal red : 1 vestigial red** [1]. Males **3 normal red : 3 normal white : 1 vestigial red : 1 vestigial white** [1]. White eyes occur **only in males**, as every female gets Xᴿ from her father [1].
*Examiner insight:* The Y chromosome carries no eye-colour allele; writing Yʳ loses the gamete mark.

**8. (a)** 360 ÷ 4 = **90** per class [1].
**(b)** (O − E)² = 2809, 2209, 2304, 2704 [1]; (O − E)²/E = 31.21 + 24.54 + 25.60 + 30.04 [1]; χ² = **111.4** [1].
**(c)** 4 − 1 = **3** degrees of freedom [1]; 111.4 > 7.82, so the difference is **significant** [1].
**(d)** The genes are **linked**; the parent is **TP/tp** [1]. Tall purple and dwarf white are **parental classes** from gametes TP and tp [1]. Tall white and dwarf purple are **recombinants** from gametes Tp and tP [1], made by **crossing over between the two loci** in prophase I, so these classes are small [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Linked" alone is not enough; explain the small classes by crossing over.

**9. (a)** **IiCc, white** [1]: the I allele makes the inhibitor, so no purple pigment forms [1].
**(b)** IiCc × iicc; gametes IC, Ic, iC, ic and ic [1]; offspring IiCc, Iicc, iiCc, iicc [1]; only iiCc is purple, the rest white [1]; **3 white : 1 purple** [1].
**(c)** **Epistasis** (gene I is epistatic to gene C) [1].
*Examiner insight:* A memorised ratio with no diagram scores little; work it out from the pathway.

**10. (a)** A structural gene codes for a functional protein, e.g. **lacZ (β-galactosidase)** [1]; a regulatory gene codes for a protein controlling other genes, e.g. **lacI (repressor)** [1].
**(b)** An inducible enzyme is **made only when its substrate is present** [1]; a repressible enzyme is **made until its product builds up and stops its synthesis** [1].
**(c)** Without lactose the **repressor binds to the operator** [1]. Lactose **binds to the repressor, changing its shape** so it leaves the operator [1]; **RNA polymerase** binds to the promoter and transcribes lacZ, lacY and lacA [1].
**(d)** A **protein that binds to DNA** [1] and **increases or decreases the rate of transcription** in eukaryotes [1].
**(e)** Lactose changes the repressor's shape, whereas gibberellin causes **DELLA proteins to be broken down**, releasing transcription factors [1].
*Examiner insight:* The repressor binds to the operator, not the promoter; swapping them loses the mark, and cAMP is not required.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling homologous chromosomes "identical".
- Mixing up anaphase I (homologues separate) and anaphase II (chromatids separate).
- Using plain dominant/recessive letters for codominant alleles.
- Giving the Y chromosome an allele, or not splitting a sex-linked ratio by sex.
- Stopping at "significant" without explaining the cause.
- Swapping the operator and the promoter.
