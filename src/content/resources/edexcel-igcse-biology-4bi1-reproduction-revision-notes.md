## Key definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Sexual reproduction | Fusion of male and female gametes; offspring genetically different |
| Asexual reproduction | One parent, no gametes, mitosis; offspring genetically identical (clones) |
| Gamete | Sex cell: sperm and egg in humans; male nucleus in pollen and female nucleus in ovule in plants |
| Fertilisation | Fusion of a male and a female gamete (their nuclei) |
| Zygote | The single cell formed at fertilisation; divides to form an embryo |
| Pollination | Transfer of pollen from an anther to a stigma |
| Germination | Growth of a seed into a seedling |
| Ovulation | Release of an egg from an ovary |
| Menstruation | Breakdown and loss of the uterus lining |

## 3.1–3.2 Sexual vs asexual

| | Sexual | Asexual |
|---|---|---|
| Number of parents | Usually two | One |
| Gametes and fertilisation | Yes | No |
| Variation | Yes | No (clones) |
| Speed | Slower | Faster |
| Survives a change in conditions | Some offspring may | All offspring equally at risk |

Zygote → divides by mitosis → ball of cells → embryo.

## 3.3 Flowers

**Male part:** stamen = anther + filament. **Female part:** carpel = stigma + style + ovary (with ovules).

| | Insect-pollinated | Wind-pollinated |
|---|---|---|
| Petals | Large, bright, scented | Small, green or absent |
| Nectar | Yes | No |
| Anthers | Inside flower | Outside, on long filaments |
| Stigma | Inside, sticky | Outside, large, feathery |
| Pollen | Larger, sticky or spiky, less | Small, light, smooth, lots |

### Method in steps: an "adaptation" answer

1. Name the feature ("anthers hang outside the flower").
2. Say what it does ("pollen is shaken out by the wind").
3. Link to pollination ("so pollen is carried to other flowers").

## 3.4 Seed and fruit formation

Pollen lands on stigma → pollen tube grows down style → enters ovule → male nucleus fuses with female nucleus → zygote → embryo.

| Before | After fertilisation |
|---|---|
| Ovule | Seed |
| Ovary | Fruit |
| Zygote | Embryo plant |

## 3.5 Germination practical

Needed: **water**, **oxygen**, **suitable temperature**. Light is not usually needed.

| Condition | Why it is needed |
|---|---|
| Water | Activates enzymes; seed swells and the coat splits |
| Oxygen | Aerobic respiration releases energy for growth |
| Warmth | Enzymes work at a useful rate (too cold: slow; too hot: denatured) |

### Method in steps: a fair germination test

1. Change one condition only (the independent variable).
2. Keep the same species, number of seeds, volume of water, time and other conditions.
3. Count seeds with a visible root after a set time.
4. Calculate **percentage germination** = (number germinated ÷ number set up) × 100.
5. Repeat and find a mean.

Small reminder: 26 of 40 seeds germinate → (26 ÷ 40) × 100 = **65%**.

## 3.6 Food reserves in germination

Stores in cotyledons or endosperm → enzymes digest them:

| Store | Enzyme | Product | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starch | Amylase | Maltose → glucose | Respiration; cellulose for cell walls |
| Protein | Protease | Amino acids | New proteins for growth |
| Lipid | Lipase | Fatty acids and glycerol | Respiration; membranes |

**Dry mass:** falls at first (stored food respired, no photosynthesis yet) → rises once leaves photosynthesise faster than the seedling respires.

### Method in steps: explaining a dry mass graph

1. Describe the shape with numbers from the graph (falls from day 0 to the lowest point, then rises).
2. Explain the fall: stored starch is broken down to glucose and respired; carbon dioxide is lost.
3. State why it is not replaced: no leaves yet, so no photosynthesis.
4. Explain the rise: leaves photosynthesise; glucose is made faster than it is respired, so new tissue is built.

Small reminder: dry mass 1.50 g → 1.26 g. Change = (1.26 − 1.50) ÷ 1.50 × 100 = **−16%** (a 16% decrease).

## 3.7 Asexual reproduction in plants

- **Runners** (natural): side stems along the soil (strawberry); new plants form where roots grow; genetically identical to the parent.
- **Cuttings** (artificial): cut shoot below a node → remove lower leaves (less transpiration) → rooting powder (plant hormones) → damp compost, covered to keep humid.

Small reminder: 18 of 24 cuttings root → (18 ÷ 24) × 100 = **75%**.

## 3.8 Reproductive systems

| Male | Function |
|---|---|
| Testes (in scrotum) | Sperm and testosterone; cooler outside the body |
| Sperm duct | Carries sperm to the urethra |
| Glands | Add fluid with nutrients → semen |
| Urethra / penis | Carry semen out; penis places it in the vagina |

| Female | Function |
|---|---|
| Ovary | Eggs; oestrogen and progesterone |
| Oviduct | Cilia move egg; site of fertilisation |
| Uterus | Lining for implantation; muscular wall for birth |
| Cervix | Ring of muscle; keeps fetus in; widens at birth |
| Vagina | Receives sperm; birth canal |

## 3.9 and 3.10B The menstrual cycle

| Hormone | Made in | Role | Paper |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oestrogen | Ovary | Repairs and thickens the uterus lining (days 5–14) | 1 and 2 |
| Progesterone | Ovary (corpus luteum) | Maintains the lining (days 14–28) | 1 and 2 |
| FSH | Pituitary gland | Egg matures in follicle; stimulates oestrogen production | Paper 2 only |
| LH | Pituitary gland | Surge triggers ovulation (about day 14); forms corpus luteum | Paper 2 only |

Timeline (about 28 days): days 1–5 menstruation → repair → ovulation about day 14 → maintenance → progesterone falls → menstruation.

**Paper 2 only feedback:** oestrogen inhibits FSH and stimulates LH; progesterone inhibits FSH and LH.

### Method in steps: reading a hormone graph

1. Find the day of the oestrogen peak: ovulation follows soon after.
2. Match each rise to its effect: oestrogen rising → lining thickening; progesterone high → lining kept.
3. Find where progesterone falls: menstruation follows.
4. (Paper 2 only) Look for the sharp LH peak just before ovulation, and the FSH rise at the start of the cycle.

If an embryo implants, progesterone stays high, the lining is kept, and there is no menstruation.

## 3.11–3.12 Placenta and amniotic fluid

**Placenta:** blood of mother and embryo does not mix. Diffusion across a thin barrier.

- Mother → embryo: glucose, amino acids, fatty acids, minerals, vitamins, water, oxygen.
- Embryo → mother: carbon dioxide, urea.
- Adaptations: large surface area (villi), thin barrier, rich blood supply on both sides.

Small reminder, a two-mark explanation: "The placenta has many villi, which give a large surface area [1], so more glucose and oxygen diffuse into the embryo's blood per second [1]."

**Amniotic fluid:** shock absorber; supports and cushions the embryo against knocks; lets it move.

## 3.13 Secondary sexual characteristics

| Testosterone (testes) | Oestrogen (ovaries) |
|---|---|
| Sperm production begins | Menstrual cycle begins |
| Facial and body hair | Breasts develop |
| Voice deepens | Hips widen |
| Muscles develop | |

Pubic and underarm hair grow in both sexes at puberty.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Pollination vs fertilisation:** pollen moved vs nuclei fused.
- **Ovule vs ovary:** ovule → seed; ovary → fruit.
- **Oestrogen vs progesterone:** repairs and thickens vs maintains.
- **FSH vs LH (Paper 2 only):** egg matures vs egg released.
- **Runners vs cuttings:** natural vs artificial; both give clones.
- **Dry mass vs fresh mass:** dry mass shows food use; fresh mass is distorted by water uptake.

## Quick self-test

1. Define pollination.
2. Give two features of a wind-pollinated stigma.
3. What does the ovary become after fertilisation?
4. Name the three conditions a seed needs to germinate.
5. Which enzyme breaks down starch in a germinating seed, and what is the glucose used for?
6. Why does the dry mass of a seedling fall during the first few days?
7. Why are the lower leaves removed from a cutting?
8. Give the function of the cilia in the oviduct.
9. Which hormone maintains the uterus lining?
10. (Paper 2 only) Which hormone triggers ovulation, and where is it made?
11. Name two substances that pass from embryo to mother across the placenta.
12. 30 out of 48 seeds germinate. Calculate the percentage germination.

### Answers

1. Transfer of pollen from an anther to a stigma.
2. Large/feathery; hangs outside the flower.
3. The fruit.
4. Water, oxygen, a suitable temperature.
5. Amylase; respiration (and making cellulose).
6. Stored food is respired and the seedling cannot yet photosynthesise.
7. To reduce water loss by transpiration.
8. Sweep the egg towards the uterus.
9. Progesterone.
10. LH; the pituitary gland.
11. Carbon dioxide and urea.
12. (30 ÷ 48) × 100 = **62.5%**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "pollen joins the egg" for fertilisation instead of "male nucleus fuses with female nucleus".
- Giving a flower feature without explaining how it helps pollination.
- Stating that the ovary becomes the seed.
- Leaving out oxygen, or adding light, in the list of germination conditions.
- Saying germinating seeds lose mass because "they use energy", without naming respiration of stored food.
- Not keeping all but one variable the same when designing a germination test.
- Mixing up oestrogen (repair) and progesterone (maintain).
- Saying FSH or LH comes from the ovary (Paper 2 only content).
- Saying mother's and embryo's blood mix in the placenta.
- Calling cuttings a natural method.
