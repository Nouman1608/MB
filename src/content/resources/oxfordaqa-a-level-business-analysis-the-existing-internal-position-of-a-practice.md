## Questions

**1.** Define a "cash cow" in the Boston Matrix, and give **one** way a business might use the cash it generates. **[2]**

**2.** Ottercliffe Pet Foods Ltd has current assets of £1,260,000, including inventory of £540,000, and current liabilities of £720,000. Calculate its current ratio and acid test ratio. **[4]**

**3.** Ferrowyn Paints plc has operating profit of £2.34 million, non-current liabilities of £5.2 million and total equity of £7.8 million. Calculate its return on capital employed and its gearing ratio. **[4]**

**4.** Draycott Wholesale Ltd has cost of sales of £2,920,000 and trade payables of £416,000. Its inventory was £340,000 at the start of the year and £390,000 at the end. Calculate its inventory turnover and its payables days. **[4]**

**5.** Skellmere Energy plc paid total dividends of £3.15 million on 42 million shares. Its share price is £1.25. Calculate the dividend per share and the dividend yield. **[4]**

**6.** Fenwray Glass Ltd (£000): operating profit 480; non-current liabilities 800; total equity 2,400; inventory 300; trade receivables 420; cash 180; current liabilities 500. A customer who owes Fenwray £60,000 has gone out of business and will not pay.

**(a)** Explain how this event changes Fenwray's statement of financial position and income statement. Ignore tax. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate Fenwray's ROCE and acid test ratio before and after the amendment. **[4]**

**7.** Explain **two** benefits to a hotel chain of treating each hotel as a profit centre. **[4]**

**8.** Explain **two** limitations of using a company's published financial statements to judge how well it will perform in future. **[4]**

**9.** Vennick Beverages sells four brands:

| Brand | Market growth (% a year) | Relative market share |
|---|---|---|
| Glimmerspring sparkling water | 15 | 1.4 |
| Old Mill cordial | 1 | 2.0 |
| Zest Shot energy drink | 18 | 0.3 |
| Tonic Royale mixer | −2 | 0.5 |

**(a)** Place each brand in the correct Boston Matrix category. **[4]**
**(b)** Analyse how this portfolio analysis could help Vennick plan its marketing for next year. **[6]**

**10.** Kestrine Hotels plc runs city-centre hotels. The founder, who led with a democratic style and rewarded teams for guest satisfaction scores, has retired. Analyse **two** factors that are likely to have shaped Kestrine's culture. **[6]**

**11.** Kestrine Hotels plc's data (£m). Industry averages: ROCE 11%, operating profit margin 12%, gearing 45%.

| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 24.0 | 27.0 |
| Operating profit | 3.6 | 3.51 |
| Non-current liabilities | 9.12 | 14.04 |
| Total equity | 14.88 | 12.96 |
| Current ratio | 0.9 | 0.75 |

In Year 2 Kestrine borrowed to open two hotels, both opening in the final quarter. Evaluate whether the data show that Kestrine's performance has worsened. **[12]**

**12.** Kestrine's new chief executive wants to replace its team-based, guest-focused culture with one built on individual sales targets and cost control. Evaluate whether she should change Kestrine's culture. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** A product with a high share of a low-growth market [1]. Its surplus cash can fund promotion of question marks or continued investment in stars [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition naming only one axis (for example "a best-selling product") gains less credit than one that names both market growth and market share.

**2.** Current ratio = 1,260,000 ÷ 720,000 [1] = **1.75** [1]. Acid test = (1,260,000 − 540,000) ÷ 720,000 [1] = **1.0** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Ratios are written as a number (1.75 or 1.75:1), not a percentage; adding a % sign shows the formula has been misunderstood.

**3.** Capital employed = 5.2 + 7.8 = £13 million [1]; ROCE = 2.34 ÷ 13 × 100 = **18%** [1]. Gearing = 5.2 ÷ 13 × 100 [1] = **40%** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show capital employed as a separate line: if the final figure is wrong, a correct method is still visible to the marker.

**4.** Average inventory = (340,000 + 390,000) ÷ 2 = £365,000 [1]; inventory turnover = 2,920,000 ÷ 365,000 = **8 times** [1]. Payables days = 416,000 ÷ 2,920,000 × 365 [1] = **52 days** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* When opening and closing inventory are both given, using only the closing figure gives about 7.49 times, which will not match the expected answer.

**5.** Dividend per share = 3,150,000 ÷ 42,000,000 [1] = **£0.075 (7.5p)** [1]. Dividend yield = 0.075 ÷ 1.25 × 100 [1] = **6%** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep units consistent: dividing 7.5 (pence) by 1.25 (pounds) gives 600%, an answer that should prompt you to check.

**6. (a)** Trade receivables fall by £60,000, and retained earnings (total equity) fall by £60,000 [1]. The bad debt is an expense, so operating profit falls by £60,000 to £420,000 [1].
**(b)** Before: ROCE = 480 ÷ (800 + 2,400) × 100 = **15%**; acid test = (900 − 300) ÷ 500 = **1.2** [1]. After: capital employed = 800 + 2,340 = 3,140 [1]; ROCE = 420 ÷ 3,140 × 100 = **13.38%** [1]; acid test = (840 − 300) ÷ 500 = **1.08** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Both sides of the statement must change; amending only the asset line leaves net assets and total equity out of balance.

**7.** Each hotel's revenue and costs are measured, so head office can see which hotels earn most and where to invest [1], for example extending a hotel with high occupancy and profit rather than a struggling one [1]. Hotel managers are judged on their own results [1], which can motivate them to control costs and raise guest spending [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each benefit needs a reason and a consequence in the hotel context; two bare benefits with no development earn little credit.

**8.** The statements are historic [1], so a past profit says little about a market that has since changed, such as a new rival entering [1]. They omit morale, brand reputation and other non-financial factors [1]; falling morale may not show in profit until later years [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Build on the limits the specification names, historic data and missing non-financial factors, and develop each in context; a vague "accounts can be wrong" earns little without that support.

**9. (a)** Glimmerspring: **star** [1]. Old Mill: **cash cow** [1]. Zest Shot: **question mark** [1]. Tonic Royale: **dog** [1].
**(b)** Old Mill generates surplus cash with little promotion needed [1], so Vennick can use it to fund marketing elsewhere [1]. Glimmerspring needs continued spending to hold share in a fast-growing market [1], so it should keep a large promotion budget [1]. Zest Shot needs a decision: spend heavily to build share or withdraw [1], while Tonic Royale's falling market suggests cutting its budget unless bars buy it with other Vennick lines [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Analysis credit comes from linking each category to a specific spending decision; classifying products again in part (b) earns nothing extra.

**10.** Leadership style: the founder's democratic approach involved staff in decisions [1], which is likely to have created a culture of trust where staff use their own judgement with guests [1], so service is personal and staff stay [1]. Reward systems: team rewards for satisfaction scores [1] encourage staff to cooperate and put guests first [1], so the culture is customer-focused rather than competitive between individuals [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the factors in the case; a general list of culture influences with no link to Kestrine gains little credit.

**11.** Revenue rose by 12.5%, but the operating profit margin fell from 15% to 13% [1]. ROCE fell from 3.6 ÷ (9.12 + 14.88) × 100 = 15% to 3.51 ÷ (14.04 + 12.96) × 100 = 13% [1], and gearing rose from 38% to 52% [1]. Liquidity fell, with the current ratio down from 0.9 to 0.75 [1]. Taken alone, this suggests worse performance and higher risk [1]. However, both measures of profitability are still above the industry averages of 11% and 12% [1], so Kestrine remains more profitable than its rivals [1]. Context explains much of the fall: the two new hotels are fully counted in Year 2 capital employed but traded for only one quarter [1], so their profit is not yet in the figures [1]. Hotels are paid quickly by guests, so a current ratio below 1 may be normal [1]. The real risk is gearing above the 45% average, which raises interest costs if rates rise [1]. Judgement: performance has worsened only slightly, mostly because of timing, and the next full year's ROCE and gearing will show whether the expansion is working [1]. **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* The top marks need a justified conclusion that uses the trend, the benchmark and the context together; a list of rises and falls is unlikely to gain the highest credit.

**12.** For: team rewards and a democratic culture may have let costs rise [1]; individual targets could raise revenue per guest and productivity [1]. A new leader can use the change to set a clear direction [1]. Against: the existing culture is positive and strong, built on guest satisfaction [1], which is part of Kestrine's brand reputation [1]. Individual sales targets may make staff compete rather than help each other [1], and pushing sales on guests may cut satisfaction scores [1]. Changing a strong culture takes years and experienced staff may leave [1], raising recruitment costs [1]. Judgement: she should keep the guest-focused culture and add cost control through team targets, since a full change risks the reputation that keeps profitability above the industry average [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation should weigh the reasons for changing culture against the problems of changing it in this business, then reach a conclusion that follows from that weighing.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using total assets instead of non-current liabilities plus total equity for capital employed.
- Leaving inventory in the acid test ratio.
- Mixing pence and pounds in dividend yield.
- Using closing rather than average inventory for inventory turnover.
- Amending one line of a statement without changing the matching line.
- Describing ratio changes with no trend, benchmark or context.
- Classifying Boston Matrix products without linking them to marketing decisions.
- Writing about culture in general terms instead of using the case.
- Ending an evaluation without a clear, supported judgement.
