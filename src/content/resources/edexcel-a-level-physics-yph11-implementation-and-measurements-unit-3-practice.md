These questions cover section 3.4 Implementation and measurements of Unit 3: Practical Skills in Physics I, Pearson Edexcel IAL Physics (YPH11), Issue 3. In each question you are checking the work of an inexperienced student.

## Section A

**1.** A micrometer measuring a wire shows 1 mm on the sleeve with the half-millimetre mark visible, and the thimble reads 09. With the jaws closed it read +0.01 mm. State the diameter of the wire. **[2]**

**2.** On Vernier calipers measuring a rod, the main-scale reading just before the Vernier zero is 12 mm and the seventh Vernier mark lines up with a main-scale mark. State the reading to the correct number of significant figures and its uncertainty. **[2]**

**3.** A student times three repeats with a stopwatch reading to 0.01 s and records 3.4 s, 3.42 s and 3.5 s. Comment on the way the times are recorded. **[2]**

**4.** A student measures the resistance of a wire at lengths of 0.20 m, 0.25 m, 0.30 m and 0.35 m, using a wire 1.00 m long. Comment on the number and the range of readings. **[3]**

**5.** Explain how a set square improves the measurement of the extension of a spring. **[2]**

**6.** State what a student should do on finding a reading that is inconsistent with the others. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A student hangs loads on a copper wire 1.5 m long and measures its extension by reading a pointer against a metre rule.

| Load / N | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Extension / mm | 0.5 | 1.0 | 1.6 | 2.0 | 2.5 |

**(a)** Comment on the precision with which the extensions are recorded. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage uncertainty in the 2.5 mm reading. **[2]**
**(c)** Identify the inconsistent reading, with a reason. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest **two** improvements, using additional apparatus or a change of method, and explain each. **[4]**

**8.** A student releases a trolley from rest at distances s from a marker at the bottom of a ramp, and times each run once by hand with a stopwatch.

| s / m | 0.2 | 0.40 | 0.60 | 0.80 | 1.00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| t / s | 1.13 | 1.6 | 1.96 | 2.46 | 2.53 |

**(a)** Comment on the significant figures in the table. **[2]**
**(b)** For uniform acceleration from rest, s = ½at². Use s/t² to show which reading is inconsistent, and state the time expected for that distance. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest **two** improvements to the method, with reasons. **[4]**

**9.** A student measures a wire's diameter once, near one end, with a micrometer. The sleeve shows 0 mm with the half-millimetre mark visible, and the thimble reads 32. The student records "0.32 mm".

**(a)** Give the correct reading and explain the student's error. **[2]**
**(b)** Comment on taking a single reading and suggest how the measurement should be made. **[3]**

**10.** To find the wavelength of laser light, a student shines the laser through a diffraction grating onto a wall. She measures, once, the distance from the central spot to the first-order spot on the left with a 30 cm ruler, and the grating-to-wall distance by laying the same ruler end to end several times. Comment on her measurements and suggest improvements. **[6]**

**11.** A student investigating the I–V characteristic of a 12 V filament lamp takes readings at 0.5 V, 1.0 V, 1.5 V, 2.0 V and 3.0 V.

**(a)** Comment on the range and number of readings. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the readings at low p.d. alone could mislead the student about the lamp's behaviour. **[2]**

**12.** To find the speed of sound, a student uses a signal generator at 2.00 kHz, a loudspeaker, a microphone and an oscilloscope. She moves the microphone until the traces are in phase, moves it on to the next in-phase position, and measures the distance between the two positions as 0.17 m with a metre rule. She calculates v = fλ = 340 m s⁻¹. Comment on her method and suggest **two** improvements. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** 1 + 0.5 + 0.09 = 1.59 mm [1]; corrected for zero error 1.59 − 0.01 = **1.58 mm** [1]. **[2]**

**2.** **12.7 mm** [1]; uncertainty half the resolution, **0.05 mm** [1]. **[2]**

**3.** The times are not recorded to a consistent number of decimal places [1]; all should be to 0.01 s (3.40 s, 3.42 s, 3.50 s), although reaction time means the real uncertainty is larger than 0.01 s [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Four values are too few to draw a reliable line or spot an anomaly; take at least six [1]. The range 0.20–0.35 m uses only a small part of the 1.00 m wire [1], so extend it to about 0.10–0.90 m to reduce the uncertainty in the gradient [1]. **[3]**

**5.** The set square, placed on the bench against the rule, checks that the rule is vertical [1], so each extension is measured along the line of the spring and not along a tilted scale [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Check the calculation or recording [1], then repeat the measurement; exclude it only if a reason is found [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** A metre rule resolves only 1 mm [1], so recording extensions to 0.1 mm claims a precision the instrument cannot give [1].
**(b)** Uncertainty 0.5 mm [1]; 0.5 ÷ 2.5 × 100% = **20%** [1].
**(c)** **1.6 mm at 30 N** [1]: the other readings give 0.050 mm per newton, so 1.5 mm is expected [1].
**(d)** Use a Vernier scale attached to a reference wire, or a travelling microscope, to read the extension [1], because it resolves 0.1 mm or better, cutting the percentage uncertainty [1]. Use a longer wire, for example 3 m along the bench over a pulley [1], because the extension is proportional to length, so it becomes larger and its percentage uncertainty smaller [1]. **[10]**

**8. (a)** The distance 0.2 m should be 0.200 m to match the others [1]; the time 1.6 s should be recorded to two decimal places like the others [1].
**(b)** s/t² is 0.156 m s⁻² for 0.2, 0.40, 0.60 and 1.00 m [1] but 0.80 ÷ 2.46² = 0.132 m s⁻² at 0.80 m, so this reading is inconsistent [1]. Expected t = √(0.80 ÷ 0.156) = **2.26 s** [1].
**(c)** Use light gates at the release point and at the marker, connected to an electronic timer, instead of hand timing [1], removing reaction-time error, which is large for times of 1–3 s [1]. Repeat each run at least three times and use the mean [1], so random variation in release and timing is reduced [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Showing the ratio for every row is what turns "looks wrong" into a creditworthy identification.

**9. (a)** Reading 0 + 0.5 + 0.32 = **0.82 mm** [1]; the student missed the half-millimetre mark on the sleeve [1].
**(b)** A single reading cannot show whether the wire is uniform or circular [1]. Measure at several places along the wire [1], rotating it through 90° at each place, and take the mean, quoting half the range as the uncertainty [1]. **[5]**

**10.** One reading on one side only gives no check on random error [1]; measure between the first-order spots on both sides and halve the distance [1]. A 30 cm ruler laid end to end adds an uncertainty at each step [1]; use a metre rule or tape measure for the grating-to-wall distance [1]. Use the second and higher orders as well [1], since larger distances have smaller percentage uncertainties and give a mean wavelength [1]. **[6]**

**11. (a)** The range is too small: readings reach only 3.0 V of the lamp's 12 V rating [1]. Extend to about 12 V [1] with at least ten readings, spaced roughly every 1 V [1].
**(b)** At low p.d. the filament stays cool, so the I–V graph is nearly straight [1]; the curve, caused by resistance rising as the filament heats, only appears at higher p.d. [1]. **[5]**

**12.** Measuring one wavelength gives a large percentage uncertainty: 0.5 mm in 170 mm is small, but judging the in-phase positions adds several millimetres [1], so the uncertainty in λ is large [1]. Improvement: move through several wavelengths, for example five, and divide the total distance by five [1], which reduces the percentage uncertainty [1]. Improvement: repeat at several frequencies and plot λ against 1/f [1]; the gradient gives v and averages out random error [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Identifying an anomaly by eye with no calculation.
- Forgetting the half-millimetre mark on a micrometer.
- Suggesting "more readings" without saying how many or over what range.
- Giving an improvement with no reason.
- Treating the stopwatch's display resolution as the timing uncertainty.
