## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Tropism | Directional growth response to a directional stimulus; positive is towards, negative is away |
| Nastic response | Response whose direction does not depend on the stimulus direction |
| Abiotic stress | Stress from non-living factors such as drought or cold |
| Herbivory | Feeding on plants by animals |
| Alkaloid | Nitrogen-containing chemical defence, toxic or bitter to herbivores |
| Pheromone | Airborne chemical signal released by a damaged plant, detected by neighbours |
| Apical dominance | Apical bud suppresses lateral bud growth |
| Abscission zone | Region at the base of a leaf stalk where the leaf separates |
| Serial dilution | Repeated dilution by a constant factor to give a concentration series |

## 5.1.5(a) Types of response

- **Tropisms:** phototropism (light), geotropism or gravitropism (gravity), hydrotropism (water), thigmotropism (touch), chemotropism (chemicals).
- **Abiotic stress:** water shortage leads to stomatal closure; winter leads to leaf loss in deciduous plants.
- **Herbivory:** alkaloids (such as caffeine and nicotine), pheromones and folding on touch (*Mimosa*).
- **Folding on touch:** turgor is lost from cells at the base of each leaflet because ions leave and water follows by osmosis. It is nastic.

| Organ | Light | Gravity |
|---|---|---|
| Shoot | Positive | Negative |
| Root | Often negative | Positive |

### Auxin in tropisms: steps to quote

1. Auxin (IAA) is made at the tip.
2. The stimulus makes it move sideways: to the shaded side, or to the lower side.
3. In a shoot, more auxin means more cell elongation on that side, so it bends towards light or away from gravity.
4. In a root, the higher auxin level inhibits elongation on the lower side, so the root bends down.
5. Mechanism of elongation: auxin makes cells pump hydrogen ions into the wall, the wall acidifies, cellulose bonds loosen and turgor stretches the cell.

### Practical: tropisms (PAG11)

- Seedlings such as cress or mustard; one-sided light or horizontal position.
- Control: darkness, light from all sides, or a clinostat that rotates seedlings.
- Measure the angle of curvature with a protractor or photograph; use at least ten seedlings and calculate a mean.
- Control species, age, temperature, light intensity, humidity.

## 5.1.5(b) Roles of hormones

| Hormone | Role |
|---|---|
| Auxin | Elongation, tropisms, apical dominance, inhibits leaf fall |
| Gibberellin | Stem elongation, germination (amylase) |
| ABA | Stomatal closure, dormancy |
| Ethene | Leaf fall, ripening |

### Stomatal closure steps

1. Water stress raises ABA in the leaf.
2. ABA binds to guard-cell receptors; calcium ions rise in the cytoplasm.
3. Ion channels open and potassium ions leave.
4. Guard-cell water potential rises, water leaves by osmosis, turgor falls.
5. Stomata close; water loss falls but carbon dioxide uptake falls too.

### Leaf loss steps

1. Shorter days and colder weather lower auxin from the leaf.
2. Ethene stimulates the abscission zone.
3. Separation-layer cells make enzymes that digest cell walls.
4. The leaf falls; a protective layer seals the scar.

### Germination steps

1. The seed takes up water; ABA level falls relative to gibberellin.
2. The embryo makes gibberellin.
3. Gibberellin acts on the aleurone layer and switches on transcription of the amylase gene.
4. Amylase hydrolyses starch to maltose, then glucose.
5. Glucose is respired and used for growth until photosynthesis starts.

## 5.1.5(c) Apical dominance evidence

| Treatment | Result | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Remove apical bud | Laterals grow | Apex normally suppresses them |
| Remove apex, add auxin to stump | Laterals stay short | Auxin replaces the apex |
| Remove apex, add plain carrier | Laterals grow | Carrier has no effect (control) |
| Higher auxin concentration | Greater inhibition | Effect depends on concentration |


## 5.1.5(d) Gibberellin evidence

- **Stem elongation:** dwarf plants that grow taller when given gibberellin make too little of it. Dwarf plants that do not respond cannot respond to it (for example, their receptors or response pathway is faulty).
- **Concentration:** growth typically rises with concentration, then levels off when something else is limiting.
- **Germination:** embryo-less half-seeds do not digest starch unless gibberellin is added; iodine shows a clear zone where starch has gone.

## 5.1.5(e) Investigations and serial dilution

- **Method for 1 in 10:** 1 cm³ of solution plus 9 cm³ of distilled water, mixed, then repeat from the new tube.
- **Check:** each step divides concentration by 10. From 0.5 g dm⁻³ to 0.0005 g dm⁻³ takes three steps.
- Zero-hormone control uses distilled water.
- Plot concentration on a log scale.
- **Analysis:** mean and standard deviation for each group; percentage change = (new − control) ÷ control × 100.
- **Statistics:** Student's t-test for a difference between two means; degrees of freedom = n₁ + n₂ − 2. When the calculated t is larger than the critical value for p = 0.05, the difference is significant, so reject the null hypothesis. Use a correlation test for a relationship between two variables.
- **Variables:** species, seed age, volume of solution, temperature, light, time.

## 5.1.5(f) Commercial uses

| Use | Hormone | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| Control ripening | Ethene (also removal of it) | Stimulates softening and starch to sugar; positive feedback |
| Rooting powder | Auxin | Cuttings form roots, giving clones |
| Weedkiller | Synthetic auxin (such as 2,4-D) | Not broken down, so uncontrolled growth in broad-leaved weeds |

Evaluate: higher yield, cheaper labour and uniform fruit supply against run-off, non-target damage and resistance.

## Interpreting an unfamiliar hormone experiment

1. Identify the independent variable (treatment or concentration), the dependent variable and the control.
2. Compare every treatment with the control, not with each other, and quote values from the data with units.
3. Calculate percentage change or a rate (change ÷ time) if the data allow it.
4. Describe the trend, then any plateau or reversal, and state at what concentration it happens.
5. Link the result to the hormone's role. Say what the result shows, and what it cannot show.
6. Suggest a limitation: small sample, no repeats, variable not controlled, only one species.

## Must-know distinctions

| Compare | Key difference |
|---|---|
| Tropism and nastic response | Directional against non-directional |
| Natural and synthetic auxin | Synthetic auxin is not broken down by the plant, so it persists |
| Dwarf lacking gibberellin and dwarf that cannot respond | Only the first grows when gibberellin is applied |
| Shoot and root response to auxin | High auxin stimulates shoot elongation but inhibits root elongation |
| Gibberellin and abscisic acid in seeds | Gibberellin promotes germination; ABA maintains dormancy |

## Quick self-test

1. Define a tropism.
2. Why does a horizontal root curve downwards?
3. Name the hormone that closes stomata and the ion that leaves guard cells.
4. Which enzyme does gibberellin stimulate in germinating barley, and where?
5. A shoot grows from 18 mm to 27 mm. What is the percentage increase?
6. How many 1 in 10 dilution steps turn 0.5 g dm⁻³ into 0.0005 g dm⁻³?
7. Why is a plain lanolin control used in an apical dominance experiment?
8. Why can a synthetic auxin kill weeds but not the cereal crop?
9. What hormone causes leaf fall, and which hormone's decline helps trigger it?
10. Why does the leaf of *Mimosa* fold when touched?

### Answers

1. A directional growth response to a directional stimulus.
2. Auxin collects on the lower side, where it inhibits elongation; the upper side grows faster.
3. Abscisic acid; potassium ions.
4. Amylase, in the aleurone layer.
5. (27 − 18) ÷ 18 × 100 = 50%.
6. Three steps (0.5, 0.05, 0.005, 0.0005).
7. It shows the carrier does not affect growth, so any difference is due to the hormone.
8. It causes uncontrolled growth in broad-leaved weeds, while cereals are far less affected.
9. Ethene; the decline of auxin.
10. Cells at the base of each leaflet lose turgor as ions leave and water follows by osmosis.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a hormone name without its role in the specific process the question asks about.
- Saying auxin "bends the shoot" without stating that it moves to the shaded side.
- Forgetting that high auxin concentrations inhibit root growth.
- Missing the control in an experiment, or comparing treatments with the wrong control.
- Quoting a percentage change from the wrong baseline.
- Stating "it is a fair test" without naming the variables kept constant.
- Describing a t-test result without stating the degrees of freedom and the null hypothesis.
- Saying stomata "close because guard cells shrink" without osmosis and turgor.
- Describing weedkillers as "poisons" rather than synthetic auxins causing uncontrolled growth.
