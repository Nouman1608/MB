These questions cover **3.2.1 Scalars and vectors** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630) specification. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 1. Section A has short questions; Section B has longer, structured questions. If a point is unfamiliar, read the [scalars and vectors study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-scalars-and-vectors-oxfordaqa/) or the [scalars and vectors revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-scalars-and-vectors-oxfordaqa-revision-notes/) first. Take g = 9.81 N kg⁻¹.

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Which list contains only vector quantities?

A: force, mass, velocity

B: acceleration, displacement, weight

C: speed, distance, energy

D: velocity, power, momentum

**[1]**

**2.** State one difference between distance and displacement. **[1]**

**3.** Forces of 9.0 N and 12 N act at right angles at a point. Calculate the magnitude of the resultant and its angle to the 12 N force. **[2]**

**4.** A rope pulls a sledge with a force of 140 N at 35° above the horizontal. Calculate the horizontal and vertical components of the force. **[2]**

**5.** A car travels round a roundabout at a constant speed. Explain why the car is not in equilibrium. **[2]**

**6.** A ball travelling horizontally at 12 m s⁻¹ is hit straight back along the same line at 18 m s⁻¹. State the magnitude of the change in velocity. **[1]**

**7.** Two forces of 7 N and 3 N act at a point. Which of these cannot be their resultant?

A: 4 N

B: 6 N

C: 10 N

D: 11 N

**[1]**

### Section B

**8.** A swimmer swims at 1.2 m s⁻¹ relative to the water, heading straight across a river 45 m wide. The river flows at 0.50 m s⁻¹ parallel to the banks.

**(a)** Calculate the magnitude and direction of the swimmer's resultant velocity. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the time taken to cross the river. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate how far downstream the swimmer lands. **[1]**

**(d)** Calculate the angle to the straight-across line at which the swimmer must head, upstream, to land directly opposite the starting point. **[2]**

**9.** Two forces act at a point. Force P is 25 N along the positive x-axis. Force Q is 15 N at 120° to the positive x-axis, measured anticlockwise.

**(a)** Calculate the total x-component and the total y-component of the two forces. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the magnitude and direction of the resultant. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe how a scale drawing could be used to check your answer. **[1]**

**10.** A picture of weight 18 N hangs from a wire attached to a single hook. Each side of the wire makes an angle of 40° with the horizontal.

**(a)** Calculate the tension in the wire. **[2]**

**(b)** The wire is replaced by a shorter one, so each side makes 15° with the horizontal. Calculate the new tension and explain why the shorter wire is more likely to break. **[3]**

**11.** A 5.0 kg mass hangs at rest from a knot. The knot is held by a horizontal string with a tension of 20 N and by a second string at an angle to the vertical.

**(a)** Calculate the weight of the mass. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the tension in the second string and its angle to the vertical. **[3]**

**(c)** Describe how the three forces on the knot would appear when drawn tip to tail. **[1]**

**12.** A crate of weight 600 N is pulled across a level floor at constant velocity by a rope at 30° above the horizontal. The tension in the rope is 150 N.

**(a)** Explain why the crate is in equilibrium. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the frictional force on the crate. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the normal contact force from the floor on the crate. **[2]**

**(d)** The tension is increased to 180 N at the same angle. Assuming the frictional force stays at 130 N, calculate the resultant horizontal force and describe the crate's motion. **[2]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** **B** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Each other option hides one scalar: mass in A, every item in C, and power in D.

**2.** Displacement has a direction (distance does not); or displacement is measured in a straight line from start to finish [1] **[1]**

**3.** √(9.0² + 12²) = **15 N** [1]; tan⁻¹(9.0/12) = **37°** to the 12 N force [1] **[2]**

**4.** Horizontal = 140 cos 35° = **115 N** [1]; vertical = 140 sin 35° = **80 N** [1] **[2]**

**5.** Its direction of motion is changing, so its velocity is changing [1]; a changing velocity means an acceleration, so there must be a resultant force [1] **[2]**

**6.** **30 m s⁻¹** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* 18 − 12 = 6 m s⁻¹ ignores the reversal. With the original direction positive, Δv = −18 − 12 = −30 m s⁻¹.

**7.** **D** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* The resultant of 7 N and 3 N must lie between 4 N and 10 N.

### Section B

**8.**

**(a)** √(1.2² + 0.50²) = **1.3 m s⁻¹** [1]; tan⁻¹(0.50/1.2) = **23° downstream** of the straight-across direction [1]

**(b)** t = 45 ÷ 1.2 = **38 s** (37.5 s) [1]

**(c)** 0.50 × 37.5 = **19 m** [1]

**(d)** The upstream component of the swimmer's velocity must equal the current: 1.2 sin θ = 0.50 [1]; θ = **25°** upstream [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b), only the across-river component moves the swimmer across, so the current does not change the crossing time.

**9.**

**(a)** x: 25 + 15 cos 120° = 25 − 7.5 = **17.5 N** [1]; y: 15 sin 120° = **13.0 N** [1]

**(b)** R = √(17.5² + 13.0²) = **21.8 N** [1]; tan⁻¹(13.0/17.5) = **36.6°** above the positive x-axis [1]

**(c)** Draw P and Q tip to tail to a stated scale with the correct angle between them, then measure the length and angle of the line from the start to the finish [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* cos 120° is negative. Forgetting the sign gives 32.5 N for the x-component and a resultant that is far too large.

**10.**

**(a)** 2T sin 40° = 18 [1]; T = 18 ÷ (2 × 0.643) = **14.0 N** [1]

**(b)** T = 18 ÷ (2 sin 15°) = **34.8 N** [1]; at a smaller angle each side has a smaller vertical component per newton of tension [1], so a larger tension is needed to support the same weight, which may exceed the wire's breaking force [1] **[5]**

**11.**

**(a)** W = 5.0 × 9.81 = **49 N** [1]

**(b)** The second string supplies 20 N horizontally and 49 N vertically [1]; T = √(49.05² + 20²) = **53 N** [1]; angle to vertical = tan⁻¹(20/49.05) = **22°** [1]

**(c)** They form a closed triangle, because the resultant force is zero [1] **[5]**

**12.**

**(a)** It moves at constant velocity, so its acceleration is zero and the resultant force on it is zero [1]

**(b)** Friction = horizontal component of tension = 150 cos 30° = **130 N** [1]

**(c)** Vertical: N + 150 sin 30° = 600 [1]; N = 600 − 75 = **525 N** [1]

**(d)** 180 cos 30° − 130 = 156 − 130 = **26 N** forward [1]; the resultant force is not zero, so the crate accelerates in the direction of motion [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c), the rope's upward component reduces the normal force. Writing N = 600 N treats the rope as horizontal.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Resultants given without a direction.
- Sin and cos swapped when the angle is measured from the vertical.
- Ignoring a sign when a vector reverses or an angle exceeds 90°.
- Calling constant speed in a curve "equilibrium".
