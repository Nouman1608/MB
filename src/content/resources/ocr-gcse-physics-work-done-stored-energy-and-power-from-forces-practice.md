## Questions

**1.** State the equation that links work done, force and distance, and state what one newton-metre is equal to. **[2]**

**2.** A student drags a suitcase with a steady horizontal force of 32 N for 14 m along a corridor. Calculate the work done. **[2]**

**3.** A tractor pulls a log along the ground. The tractor does 21,000 J of work and the log moves 35 m along the line of the pulling force. Calculate the force. **[3]**

**4.** A skier is pulled up a gentle slope by a tow rope. The rope pulls with a force of 120 N along the slope and does 9.0 kJ of work. Calculate the distance the skier moves. **[3]**

**5.** A person pushes a loaded trolley across a car park at constant speed. The push is 30 N and the trolley moves 20 m.
**(a)** Calculate the work done by the person. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the energy transfer. **[2]**

**6.** A builder lifts a bag of cement of mass 8.5 kg vertically through 2.4 m at a steady speed.
**(a)** Calculate the force needed. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the work done. **[1]**
**(c)** State the gain in gravitational potential energy, and explain why it has this value. **[2]**

**7.** A cyclist and bicycle have a total mass of 80 kg and travel at 6.0 m/s. The cyclist brakes and stops. The braking force is a constant 240 N.
**(a)** Calculate the kinetic energy at the start. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the distance travelled while braking. **[2]**

**8.** A waiter carries a tray at constant height and constant speed across a restaurant. Explain why the upward force from the waiter's hand does no work on the tray. **[3]**

**9.** Power is the rate at which energy is transferred.
**(a)** Write down the equation for power in terms of work done and time. **[1]**
**(b)** A hoist lifts a crate of mass 120 kg through 5.0 m in 8.0 s. Calculate the power of the hoist. **[4]**

**10.** Two students, A and B, run up the same staircase, which has a vertical height of 4.0 m. Student A has mass 60 kg and takes 12 s. Student B has mass 75 kg and takes 18 s.
**(a)** Calculate the power of each student, to 3 significant figures. **[4]**
**(b)** Student B says: "I did more work, so I have more power." Evaluate this statement. **[2]**

**11.** A winch pulls a boat of mass 400 kg up a slipway. The pulling force is 1,500 N, along the slope, and the boat moves 9.0 m along the slope in 15 s. The boat rises by a vertical height of 3.0 m.
**(a)** Calculate the work done by the winch. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the gain in gravitational potential energy of the boat. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate how much of the work is not stored as gravitational potential energy, and state where it goes. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the power of the winch. **[2]**

**12.** A car of mass 900 kg travels at 12 m/s and brakes to a stop in 4.0 s. The brakes provide a constant resistive force of 2,700 N.
**(a)** Calculate the kinetic energy of the car before braking. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the braking distance. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the average power at which energy is transferred while braking. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Work done = force × distance moved along the line of action of the force [1]. 1 N m = **1 J** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* An answer that says "force × distance" without the phrase about the line of action can still show the idea, but linking the equation to the correct direction of movement makes the answer more secure.

**2.** W = F × s = 32 × 14 [1]. **448 J** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A correct number with no unit, or with N m written where J is expected, is the commonest way to drop a mark on a short calculation.

**3.** F = W ÷ s [1]. = 21,000 ÷ 35 [1]. **600 N** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Rearranging first, then substituting, shows your method clearly even if the arithmetic slips.

**4.** 9.0 kJ = 9,000 J [1]. s = W ÷ F = 9,000 ÷ 120 [1]. **75 m** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Converting kJ to J in your working is the step most often missed; writing it on its own line helps you notice.

**5. (a)** W = F × s = 30 × 20 [1]. **600 J** [1]
**(b)** The trolley moves at constant speed, so its kinetic energy does not change [1]. The 600 J is transferred to thermal energy stores of the wheels, the ground and the surroundings (they warm up) [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* For "describe the energy transfer", name the store that gains energy; saying only that energy is "used up" or "lost" does not describe a transfer.

**6. (a)** At steady speed, force = weight = 8.5 × 10 = **85 N** [1]
**(b)** W = 85 × 2.4 = **204 J** [1]
**(c)** GPE gain = **204 J** [1], because the work done by the lifting force at steady speed is all stored in the gravitational potential energy store (m × g × h = 8.5 × 10 × 2.4 = 204 J) [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A statement that the energy is "equal" needs a reason; link the lifting force to the weight rather than just repeating the number.

**7. (a)** KE = ½ × m × v² = 0.5 × 80 × 6.0² [1]. **1,440 J** [1]
**(b)** The braking work removes all the kinetic energy, so s = W ÷ F = 1,440 ÷ 240 [1]. **6.0 m** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Squaring only the speed, not the whole product, is the usual slip; write v² as 36 before multiplying.

**8.** The force from the hand is upwards [1]. The tray moves horizontally, which is at right angles to the force [1]. Nothing moves along the direction of the force, so work done = force × 0 = 0 [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Say the movement is at right angles to the force; "nothing is lifted" or "the waiter is not tired" does not explain the physics.

**9. (a)** Power = work done ÷ time [1]
**(b)** Weight = 120 × 10 = 1,200 N [1]. Work = 1,200 × 5.0 = 6,000 J [1]. P = 6,000 ÷ 8.0 [1]. **750 W** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A mass in kg is not a force; turning it into a weight in newtons is the step that links the question to the equation.

**10. (a)** A: work = 60 × 10 × 4.0 = 2,400 J [1]; P = 2,400 ÷ 12 = **200 W** [1]. B: work = 75 × 10 × 4.0 = 3,000 J [1]; P = 3,000 ÷ 18 = **167 W** [1]
**(b)** The statement is not correct [1]. Power depends on the work and the time. B did more work but took longer, so A (200 W) has the greater power [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation needs a judgement and a reason supported by your numbers; "B did more work" alone only repeats the claim.

**11. (a)** W = F × s = 1,500 × 9.0 [1]. **13,500 J** [1]
**(b)** GPE = m × g × h = 400 × 10 × 3.0 [1]. **12,000 J** [1]
**(c)** 13,500 − 12,000 = **1,500 J** [1]. It is transferred to thermal energy stores (friction between boat and slipway, and in the winch) [1]
**(d)** P = W ÷ t = 13,500 ÷ 15 [1]. **900 W** [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Using the vertical height for GPE and the distance along the slope for work done is the key choice here; mixing them gives a different, wrong comparison.

**12. (a)** KE = 0.5 × 900 × 12² [1]. **64,800 J** [1]
**(b)** s = W ÷ F = 64,800 ÷ 2,700 [1]. **24 m** [1]
**(c)** P = W ÷ t = 64,800 ÷ 4.0 [1]. **16,200 W** (16.2 kW) [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the time given in the question, and check that your answer is in watts; mistakes in part (c) often come from dividing by the distance instead of the time.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out units, or using N, N m or W when J is needed.
- Not converting kJ to J before substituting.
- Using mass as a force without multiplying by g.
- Using the slope length where the vertical height is needed, and the other way round.
- Writing that energy is "lost" or "used up" when the answer needs a transfer to thermal stores.
- Treating power as energy, or not linking it to time.
- Not explaining the right angle between force and movement when asked why no work is done.
- Carrying rounded values into later steps and ending with a different third significant figure.
