These questions cover section 3 of Unit M2: Mechanics 2 in Pearson Edexcel International A Level Mathematics (YMA01): kinetic and potential energy, work and power, the work–energy principle and the conservation of mechanical energy, with constant resistances and inclined planes. Take g = 9.8 m s⁻² and give answers that use g to 3 significant figures.

Related: [M2 work and energy study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-mathematics-yma01-m2-work-and-energy-edexcel-alevel-maths/) and [M2 work and energy revision notes](/resources/edexcel-a-level-mathematics-yma01-m2-work-and-energy-edexcel-alevel-maths-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** A particle of mass 0.5 kg moves at 6 m s⁻¹. Find its kinetic energy, and the potential energy it gains if it rises a vertical distance of 3 m. **[2]**

**2.** A box is dragged 15 m along a horizontal floor by a rope that makes 25° with the horizontal. The tension in the rope is a constant 40 N. Find the work done by the tension. **[2]**

**3.** A crane lifts a load of mass 500 kg vertically at a constant speed of 0.4 m s⁻¹. Find the power developed by the crane, ignoring resistances. **[2]**

**4.** A car's engine has a maximum power of 24 kW. The resistance to motion is a constant 800 N. Find the maximum speed of the car on a level road. **[2]**

**5.** A ball is dropped from rest from a height of 12 m. Use conservation of mechanical energy to find its speed when it hits the ground, ignoring air resistance. **[2]**

**6.** A block of mass 3 kg slides 5 m across a rough horizontal floor, with coefficient of friction 0.25. Find the work done against friction. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A particle of mass 1.5 kg is projected with speed 10 m s⁻¹ up a line of greatest slope of a rough plane inclined at α to the horizontal, where sin α = 3/5. The coefficient of friction is 0.5.

**(a)** Use the work–energy principle to find the distance the particle travels up the plane before coming to rest. **[5]**

**(b)** Show that the particle then slides back down the plane. **[1]**

**(c)** Find the speed of the particle when it returns to its point of projection. **[4]**

**8.** A child of mass 30 kg slides from rest down a smooth curved slide. The top of the slide is 4 m above the bottom. At the bottom, the child moves onto a rough horizontal mat, with coefficient of friction 0.35.

**(a)** Find the child's speed at the bottom of the slide. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the distance the child slides along the mat before stopping. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why the speed at the bottom of the slide cannot be found using the constant acceleration formulae. **[1]**

**9.** A lorry of mass 4000 kg has a maximum power of 60 kW. The resistance to motion is a constant 2000 N.

**(a)** Find the acceleration of the lorry on a level road at the instant when it is moving at 10 m s⁻¹ at full power. **[3]**

**(b)** The lorry climbs a hill inclined at α to the horizontal, where sin α = 1/25. Find its maximum speed up the hill. **[4]**

**10.** A cyclist and bicycle have total mass 80 kg. The cyclist freewheels (does no work) 200 m down a straight road inclined at α to the horizontal, where sin α = 1/20. Her speed increases from 4 m s⁻¹ to 10 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Find the work done against resistance. **[3]**

**(b)** Assuming the resistance is constant, find its size. **[2]**

**11.** A box of mass 50 kg is pulled from rest up a line of greatest slope of a rough plane inclined at 20° to the horizontal. The pulling force is 350 N, parallel to the slope. The coefficient of friction is 0.3. Use the work–energy principle to find the speed of the box after it has moved 8 m. **[6]**

**12.** A car of mass 900 kg moves along a level road. Its engine works at 18 kW. At the instant the car is moving at 15 m s⁻¹, its acceleration is 0.5 m s⁻².

**(a)** Find the resistance to motion, assuming it is constant. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the maximum speed of the car with the engine at 18 kW. **[2]**

---

## Answers

**1.** KE = ½(0.5)(36) = **9 J** [1]. PE gained = 0.5 × 9.8 × 3 = **14.7 J** [1].

**2.** Work = 40 cos 25° × 15 [1] = **544 J** [1].

**3.** The lifting force equals the weight, 500 × 9.8 = 4900 N [1]. P = Fv = 4900 × 0.4 = **1960 W** [1].

**4.** At maximum speed, driving force = resistance: 24000/v = 800 [1], so **v = 30 m s⁻¹** [1].

**5.** mgh = ½mv², so v² = 2 × 9.8 × 12 = 235.2 [1], and **v = 15.3 m s⁻¹** [1].

**6.** Friction = 0.25 × 3 × 9.8 = 7.35 N [1]. Work = 7.35 × 5 = **36.75 J** (36.8 J) [1].

**7. (a)** cos α = 4/5. R = 1.5g(4/5) = 11.76 N [1], so friction = 0.5 × 11.76 = 5.88 N [1].

Energy: ½(1.5)(10²) = 1.5g(d sin α) + 5.88d [1].

75 = 8.82d + 5.88d = 14.7d [1], so **d = 5.10 m** [1].

**(b)** The weight component down the slope is 8.82 N, greater than the greatest friction 5.88 N (equivalently μ = 0.5 < tan α = 0.75), so the particle slides back [1].

**(c)** Friction now acts up the slope [1].

PE lost = KE gained + work against friction: 8.82 × 5.102 = ½(1.5)v² + 5.88 × 5.102 [1].

0.75v² = 2.94 × 5.102 = 15.0 [1], so v² = 20 and **v = 4.47 m s⁻¹** [1].

**8. (a)** The slide is smooth, so mechanical energy is conserved [1]. 30g(4) = ½(30)v² [1], so v = √78.4 = **8.85 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** Friction = 0.35 × 30g = 102.9 N [1]. ½(30)(78.4) = 102.9d [1], so 1176 = 102.9d and **d = 11.4 m** [1].

**(c)** The slide is curved, so the component of the weight along it changes and the acceleration is not constant [1].

**9. (a)** F = 60000/10 = 6000 N [1]. 6000 − 2000 = 4000a [1], so **a = 1 m s⁻²** [1].

**(b)** Component of weight down the hill = 4000 × 9.8 × (1/25) = 1568 N [1].

At maximum speed: 60000/v = 2000 + 1568 [2], so v = 60000/3568 = **16.8 m s⁻¹** [1].

**10. (a)** PE lost = 80 × 9.8 × (200 × 1/20) = 7840 J [1].

KE gained = ½(80)(10² − 4²) = 3360 J [1].

Work against resistance = 7840 − 3360 = **4480 J** [1].

**(b)** 4480 = R × 200 [1], so **R = 22.4 N** [1].

**11.** Work done by the pulling force = 350 × 8 = 2800 J [1].

PE gained = 50 × 9.8 × 8 sin 20° = 1340.7 J [1].

R = 50g cos 20° = 460.4 N, so friction = 0.3 × 460.4 = 138.1 N [1], and work against friction = 138.1 × 8 = 1105.1 J [1].

2800 = ½(50)v² + 1340.7 + 1105.1, so ½(50)v² = 354.2 [1].

v² = 14.17, so **v = 3.76 m s⁻¹** [1].

**12. (a)** Driving force = 18000/15 = 1200 N [1].

1200 − R = 900 × 0.5 [1], so **R = 750 N** [1].

**(b)** At maximum speed, 18000/v = 750 [1], so **v = 24 m s⁻¹** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the distance along the slope instead of the vertical height in mgh.
- Taking R = mg on a slope, so the friction is too large.
- Leaving out friction on the return journey down a slope.
- Setting driving force equal to resistance when the vehicle is still accelerating.
- Forgetting to convert kilowatts to watts.
- Solving with Newton's second law when the question says "use the work–energy principle".
