Revise first with the [atomic structure study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-atomic-structure/) and the [atomic structure revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-atomic-structure-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** State what is meant by an atom. **[1]**

**2.** Methane has the formula CH₄. State the number of atoms in one molecule of methane and explain why methane is a compound. **[2]**

**3.** State where in an atom most of its mass is found, and explain why. **[2]**

**4.** State what is meant by atomic number. **[1]**

**5.** Give the number of neutrons in an atom of ¹²⁷₅₃I. **[1]**

**6.** Copy and complete the table. **[3]**

| Species | Protons | Neutrons | Electrons |
|---|---|---|---|
| ¹⁹₉F | | | |
| ⁴⁰₂₀Ca²⁺ | | | |
| ³¹₁₅P³⁻ | | | |

**7.** Hydrogen has three isotopes: hydrogen-1, hydrogen-2 and hydrogen-3.

**(a)** State one way in which atoms of these isotopes are the same and one way in which they are different. **[2]**
**(b)** State the number of neutrons in an atom of hydrogen-3. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**8.** The table describes four particles, J, K, L and M.

| Particle | Protons | Neutrons | Electrons |
|---|---|---|---|
| J | 11 | 12 | 10 |
| K | 17 | 18 | 17 |
| L | 17 | 20 | 18 |
| M | 18 | 22 | 18 |

**(a)** Identify the two particles that are isotopes of the same element. Give a reason. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify the particle that is a positive ion and state its charge. **[2]**
**(c)** Write the symbol for L, showing its mass number, atomic number and charge. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why K and M are atoms of different elements. **[1]**

**9.** A sample of strontium contains these isotopes.

| Isotope | Percentage abundance |
|---|---|
| Strontium-86 | 10.0 |
| Strontium-87 | 7.0 |
| Strontium-88 | 83.0 |

**(a)** Calculate the relative atomic mass of strontium. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why your answer is closer to 88 than to 86. **[1]**
**(c)** State the number of protons in an atom of strontium-88. (Atomic number of strontium = 38.) **[1]**
**(d)** State the number of neutrons in an atom of strontium-86. **[1]**

**10.** A sample of iridium contains iridium-191 and iridium-193 in the ratio 3 : 5.

**(a)** Calculate the relative atomic mass of iridium. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. **[2]**
**(b)** A student divides by 100 instead. Explain how you can tell from the size of the answer that this is wrong. **[1]**

**11.** Silver has a relative atomic mass of 107.9. It has two isotopes, silver-107 and silver-109.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage abundance of silver-107. **[3]**
**(b)** State what is meant by relative atomic mass. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the two isotopes of silver have the same chemical properties. **[2]**

**12.** A scientist compares an atom of argon-40 with an atom of potassium-40 and a potassium ion, K⁺. (Atomic numbers: Ar = 18, K = 19.)

**(a)** Show that the argon atom and the potassium atom have the same mass number but different numbers of neutrons. **[2]**
**(b)** State the number of electrons in K⁺ and name the noble gas atom that has the same number. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why K⁺ has a positive charge. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** The smallest particle of an element that has the chemical properties of that element [1].

**2.** 5 atoms [1]. It contains two different elements (carbon and hydrogen) chemically bonded together [1].

**3.** In the nucleus [1], because protons and neutrons each have relative mass 1 whereas electrons have a negligible mass (about 1/1840) [1].

**4.** The number of protons in the nucleus of an atom [1].

**5.** 127 − 53 = 74 [1].

**6.** One mark per correct row [3]:
- ¹⁹₉F: 9, 10, 9.
- ⁴⁰₂₀Ca²⁺: 20, 20, 18.
- ³¹₁₅P³⁻: 15, 16, 18.
*Marking note:* For P³⁻, adding the 3 extra electrons (15 + 3) is the step most often reversed.

**7. (a)** Same: number of protons / atomic number / number of electrons [1]. Different: number of neutrons / mass number [1].
**(b)** 3 − 1 = 2 [1].

**8. (a)** K and L [1]: same number of protons (17) but different numbers of neutrons [1].
**(b)** J [1]; charge +1 (11 protons − 10 electrons) [1].
**(c)** ³⁷₁₇Cl⁻: mass number 37 and atomic number 17 [1]; charge 1− and symbol Cl [1].
**(d)** They have different numbers of protons / different atomic numbers (17 and 18) [1].

**9. (a)** (86 × 10.0 + 87 × 7.0 + 88 × 83.0) ÷ 100 = (860 + 609 + 7304) ÷ 100 [1] = 87.73 = **87.7** [1].
**(b)** Strontium-88 is by far the most abundant isotope, so it contributes most to the weighted mean [1].
**(c)** 38 [1].
**(d)** 86 − 38 = 48 [1].

**10. (a)** (191 × 3 + 193 × 5) ÷ 8 = (573 + 965) ÷ 8 = 1538 ÷ 8 [1] = 192.25 = **192.3** [1].
**(b)** It gives 15.4, which is far smaller than the mass number of either isotope; Ar must lie between 191 and 193 [1].

**11. (a)** Let silver-107 = x%: 107x + 109(100 − x) = 10 790 [1]. 10 900 − 2x = 10 790, so 2x = 110 [1]. x = **55%** [1].
**(b)** The weighted mean mass of the atoms of the element [1], compared with 1/12 of the mass of an atom of carbon-12 [1].
**(c)** They have the same number of protons, so the same number of electrons and the same electron arrangement [1]. Chemical reactions involve the electrons [1].
*Marking note:* In (a), the check (107 × 55 + 109 × 45) ÷ 100 = 107.9 confirms the answer.

**12. (a)** Both have mass number 40 [1]. Argon has 40 − 18 = 22 neutrons; potassium has 40 − 19 = 21 neutrons [1].
**(b)** 18 electrons [1]; argon [1].
**(c)** It has 19 protons but only 18 electrons / it has lost one electron [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "protons and electrons" for mass number.
- Forgetting that a negative ion has **more** electrons than protons.
- Calling two particles with different proton numbers isotopes because their masses are similar.
- Dividing by 100 when abundances are given as a ratio.
- Not checking a reverse calculation by substituting back.
