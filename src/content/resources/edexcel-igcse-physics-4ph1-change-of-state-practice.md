is Physics-only content (5.8P to 5.14P), assessed on Paper 2. The specific heat capacity of water is 4200 J/kg °C.

## Section A

**1.** State the unit of specific heat capacity. **[1]**

**2.** Describe the motion of the particles in a gas. **[2]**

**3.** State two changes that would make a puddle of water evaporate faster. **[2]**

**4.** Calculate the energy needed to raise the temperature of 2.5 kg of oil by 8.0 °C. The specific heat capacity of the oil is 2000 J/kg °C. **[2]**

**5.** Water is used to carry energy in central heating systems. State the property of water that makes it suitable for this. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** A student heats a solid in a test tube in a water bath and records its temperature every minute.

| Time / min | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature / °C | 20 | 35 | 48 | 55 | 55 | 55 | 55 | 63 | 72 |

**(a)** State the melting point of the substance. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the temperature stays constant between 3 and 6 minutes even though the substance is still being heated. **[2]**
**(c)** State the physical state of the substance at 2 minutes and at 8 minutes. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest why the student stirs the water bath. **[1]**

**7.** In hot countries, drinking water is sometimes kept cool in a clay pot. Water seeps through the clay and evaporates from the outside surface. Explain how this keeps the water in the pot cool. **[3]**

**8.** A student investigates the specific heat capacity of water using an immersion heater and a joulemeter.

**(a)** Describe the method the student should follow. **[4]**
**(b)** The student heats 0.50 kg of water. The joulemeter shows 25 200 J and the temperature rises by 11.0 °C. Calculate the specific heat capacity from these results. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why the result is higher than 4200 J/kg °C and suggest one improvement. **[2]**

**9.** A 1.8 kW kettle heats 1.2 kg of water from 20 °C to 100 °C.

**(a)** Calculate the minimum time this takes, assuming all the energy is transferred to the water. **[4]**
**(b)** The kettle is left switched on after the water reaches 100 °C. Explain why the water temperature does not rise any further. **[2]**

**10.** 9000 J is transferred to each of two 0.50 kg blocks, one of copper (c = 390 J/kg °C) and one of aluminium (c = 900 J/kg °C).

**(a)** Calculate the temperature rise of each block. **[3]**
**(b)** State which block would warm up more quickly if both were heated at the same rate. **[1]**

**11.** Ice at −15 °C is taken from a freezer and heated steadily until it is water at 30 °C. Describe and explain the shape of the temperature-time graph. **[5]**

**12.** An aluminium saucepan of mass 0.20 kg contains 0.80 kg of water. Both are heated from 20 °C to 70 °C. The specific heat capacity of aluminium is 900 J/kg °C. Calculate the total energy transferred to the saucepan and water. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** J/kg °C (joules per kilogram degree Celsius) [1].

**2.** The particles move quickly [1] and randomly in all directions, far apart from each other [1].

**3.** Any two: raise the temperature (warmer day); increase the surface area (spread the water out); have air moving over it (wind); lower the humidity of the air [1] [1].

**4.** ΔQ = m × c × ΔT = 2.5 × 2000 × 8.0 [1] = 40 000 J [1].

**5.** It has a high specific heat capacity, so each kilogram carries a lot of energy for each degree it cools [1].

**6. (a)** 55 °C [1].
**(b)** The substance is melting [1]; the energy supplied is used to overcome the forces between particles, not to increase their kinetic energy, so the temperature does not rise [1].
**(c)** At 2 minutes: solid [1]. At 8 minutes: liquid [1].
**(d)** So that the temperature is the same throughout the water bath, giving even heating [1].

**7.** The fastest-moving water particles escape from the outside surface of the pot [1]. This lowers the average kinetic energy of the particles left behind, so the water in the clay cools [1]. Energy is then transferred by heating from the water inside the pot to the cooler clay, so the drinking water cools too [1].

**8. (a)** Measure the mass of water in an insulated beaker using a balance [1]. Place the immersion heater and a thermometer in the water and record the starting temperature [1]. Switch on the heater through the joulemeter, stir the water, and switch off after a few minutes [1]. Record the highest temperature reached and the energy shown on the joulemeter; calculate c = E ÷ (m × ΔT) [1].
**(b)** c = ΔQ ÷ (m × ΔT) = 25 200 ÷ (0.50 × 11.0) [1] = 4582 [1], which rounds to 4600 J/kg °C (2 s.f., with the unit) [1].
**(c)** Some of the energy supplied heats the beaker and the surrounding air instead of the water, so the temperature rise is smaller than expected, giving a value of c that is too high [1]. Any one improvement: more insulation around the beaker; a lid; stir throughout [1].

**9. (a)** ΔQ = m × c × ΔT = 1.2 × 4200 × 80 [1] = 403 200 J [1]. time = energy ÷ power = 403 200 ÷ 1800 [1] = 224 s [1].
**(b)** The water is boiling [1]; the energy supplied is used to turn the water into steam (separating the particles), not to raise its temperature [1].

**10. (a)** Copper: ΔT = 9000 ÷ (0.50 × 390) = 46 °C (46.2 °C) [1]. Aluminium: ΔT = 9000 ÷ (0.50 × 900) [1] = 20 °C [1].
**(b)** The copper block [1].

**11.** From −15 °C the temperature rises steadily as the ice warms [1]. It then stays constant at 0 °C, giving a flat section [1], because the ice is melting and the energy is used to overcome forces between particles rather than increase their kinetic energy [1]. When all the ice has melted, the temperature rises again from 0 °C [1] up to 30 °C, as the energy now increases the kinetic energy of the water particles [1].

**12.** Saucepan: 0.20 × 900 × 50 = 9000 J [1]. Water: 0.80 × 4200 × 50 = 168 000 J [1]. Total = 177 000 J [1].

## Frequently asked questions

**Is specific latent heat tested here?**
No. Statement 5.13P uses only ΔQ = m × c × ΔT, which applies while the temperature is changing. Changes of state are
described and explained in words, without a latent heat calculation.

**Are these from past papers?**
No. They are original questions written for practice on sub-topic 5(c). Use them alongside the official Pearson
