## The Five Pillars

| Pillar | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Shahadah** | Declaration of faith — belief in one God and in Muhammad as His Prophet |
| **Salah** | Five daily prayers |
| **Zakat** | Annual almsgiving, traditionally 2.5% of qualifying savings |
| **Sawm** | Fasting during Ramadan |
| **Hajj** | Pilgrimage to Makkah, once in a lifetime if able |

**Shahadah is the foundation**, because accepting it is what makes a person a Muslim; the other four are practices that follow from that belief. Questions on "which pillar is most important" reward that reasoning rather than a bare assertion.

## Salah

Five prayers daily: **Fajr** (dawn), **Zuhr** (midday), **Asr** (afternoon), **Maghrib** (sunset), **Isha** (night).

**Wudu** — ritual washing before prayer, expressing purity of body and intention.

Prayer is performed facing the **Qiblah** (the direction of the Ka'bah in Makkah), on a clean surface, in sequences of movements and recitations called **rak'ahs**.

**Friday prayer (Jumu'ah)** is congregational, held at the mosque, and includes a **khutbah** (sermon). Its significance is communal — it unites the community and provides teaching, which individual prayer does not.

**Significance:** regular remembrance of God, discipline, equality (all stand in rows regardless of status), and unity across the world through the shared direction and timing.

## Zakat and Sawm

**Zakat** purifies wealth and reduces inequality. It is an obligation rather than voluntary charity — **sadaqah** is the voluntary form. Distinguishing the two is a common question.

**Sawm** — fasting from dawn to sunset during Ramadan: no food, drink or marital relations. Exemptions apply to the young, elderly, ill, pregnant, nursing and travelling.

**Significance:** self-discipline, empathy with the poor, spiritual focus, and the commemoration of the first revelation of the Qur'an, which occurred in Ramadan — **Laylat al-Qadr**, the Night of Power.

## Hajj

Performed in Dhul-Hijjah. Key rites: **Ihram** (state of purity and simple white garments), **Tawaf** (seven circuits of the Ka'bah), **Sa'i** (walking between Safa and Marwah), standing at **Arafat**, stoning the pillars at Mina, and the sacrifice.

**The white ihram garments carry the central meaning**: all pilgrims dress identically, so wealth and status are invisible and all stand equal before God. That symbolism is what "explain the significance of Hajj" answers should reach.

Standing at **Arafat** is the essential rite, commemorating the Prophet's Farewell Sermon.

## Festivals

- **Eid-ul-Fitr** — marks the end of Ramadan; charity (Zakat-ul-Fitr) is given before the prayer.
- **Eid-ul-Adha** — during Hajj; commemorates Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice his son, marked by sacrifice with the meat shared with the poor.

## Sources of authority

**The Qur'an** — the direct word of God, revealed to Muhammad through the angel Jibril. **The Sunnah and Hadith** — the practice and sayings of the Prophet, which explain and apply the Qur'an.

**The relationship matters:** the Qur'an gives the command to pray but not its detailed form; the Sunnah supplies that. This is why both sources are needed and why Hadith authenticity is carefully assessed.

## Exam traps

- Confusing Zakat (obligatory) with sadaqah (voluntary).
- Confusing Eid-ul-Fitr with Eid-ul-Adha.
- Describing a practice without explaining its significance.
- Giving the pillars without saying why Shahadah is foundational.
- Omitting Qur'anic or Hadith support where the question asks for it.
- In evaluation questions, giving one viewpoint only — both sides and a judgement are required.

## Self-test

1. Name the Five Pillars.
2. Why is Shahadah considered foundational?
3. Distinguish Zakat from sadaqah.
4. What is the significance of the ihram garments?
5. How do the Qur'an and Sunnah relate as sources of authority?

**Answers:** 1. Shahadah, Salah, Zakat, Sawm, Hajj. 2. Accepting and declaring it is what makes a person a Muslim; the other pillars are practices that follow from that belief. 3. Zakat is an obligatory annual payment on qualifying wealth; sadaqah is voluntary charity given at any time in any amount. 4. All pilgrims wear identical simple white garments, so wealth and social status are indistinguishable and all stand equal before God. 5. The Qur'an gives the commands, while the Sunnah and Hadith show how the Prophet applied them in practice, supplying detail the Qur'an does not specify.
