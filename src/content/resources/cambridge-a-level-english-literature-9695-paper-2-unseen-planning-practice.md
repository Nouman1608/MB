## Section A: Recall

**2.** How many unseen questions do you answer, and from how many? **[2]**

## Section B: Unseen prose (original)

> The station had been closed for years, but Edna still swept the platform on Thursdays. No one had told her to stop, and no one had told her to start. The broom made the same dry sound it always had. When the 4.10 went through without slowing, she lifted her head, as if it might change its mind.

**4.** Comment on how the writer uses detail, sentence structure and tone to present Edna. **[plan only: name the text type; 4 points with evidence]**

## Section C: Unseen poem (original)

> **Inventory**
> One chair, its back warm from the afternoon.
> One kettle, listening.
> A window with a view of other windows,
> and in each of them, someone not looking back.

**5.** Discuss how the poem uses structure and imagery to create meaning. **[plan only: name the text type; 4 points with evidence]**

## Section D: Prose set text planning (apply to your own set text)

**6.** Plan an answer to: "Examine how the writer presents a turning point in your set text." Give a one-sentence argument and three pieces of evidence, each with a named choice.

**7.** Choose a short passage from your set text and write a plan for a (b)-style question: "Analyse how the writer creates tension in this passage and consider its importance in the novel or collection as a whole."

## Guidance (indicative, not a mark scheme)

**1.** 50 marks [1]; Section A 25 and Section B 25 [1].

**2.** One [1], from a choice of two [1].

**3.** No [1]. The syllabus says such knowledge is not expected.

**4.** Possible points: prose, with narrative voice in the third person; routine detail ("swept ... on Thursdays") shows habit and devotion; repeated "no one had told her" suggests absence of instruction and her own choice; the plain sentences and dry sound create a bleak, patient tone; "as if it might change its mind" personifies the train and shows her hope; the closed station shows the past persisting. Credit supported readings.

**5.** Possible points: poem, short lines, a list structure ("One chair ... One kettle"); the personified chair and listening kettle suggest loneliness softened by small comforts; the shift to the windows widens the poem from a room to a city; "someone not looking back" ends on isolation; the title frames the speaker's life as a count of objects. Credit supported readings.

**6.** and **7.** Self-marked: argument answers the question; evidence is specific; each choice is named and its effect explained; for 7, the link to the whole text is explicit.
