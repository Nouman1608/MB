## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by intensive agriculture. **[1]**

**2.** Distinguish between arable and pastoral agriculture. **[2]**

**3.** Describe what is meant by a genetically modified organism. **[2]**

**4.** Name the **three** types of organic fertiliser listed in the syllabus. **[3]**

**5.** Give **two** reasons why urban farming can increase food production. **[2]**

**6.** State what a herbicide controls, and explain why using it can increase crop yield. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Two fields on a farm are each 15 hectares. Field A grows maize in a rotation that includes beans. Field B has grown maize every year for ten years. Last year Field A produced 54 tonnes and Field B produced 42 tonnes. The figures are invented for practice.

**(a)** Calculate the yield of each field in tonnes per hectare. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate how much higher, as a percentage, the yield of Field A is than that of Field B. Give your answer to one decimal place. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain the difference in yield between the two fields. **[3]**

**8.** Describe hydroponics and aeroponics, and give **one** benefit and **one** limitation of growing crops in controlled environments. **[4]**

**9.** A farmer has sprayed the same insecticide on a crop every year. It now kills far fewer of the pests.

**(a)** Explain how the pests have become resistant to the insecticide. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain how biological control could help the farmer. **[2]**

**10.** Explain how growing cash crops such as palm oil or cotton for export can lead to food shortages in a country. **[3]**

**11.** Explain how overuse of insecticides can harm pollinators and reduce the yield of crops that depend on them. **[3]**

**12.** A government is encouraging small farms in a rural region to buy tractors and combine harvesters. Discuss the benefits and limitations of mechanisation for this region. **[6]**

**13.** A farmer in a region with a short rainy season and long dry periods wants to increase crop yield. Suggest and explain **three** strategies from the syllabus that would suit this farm. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Farming with high inputs of labour, money, machinery or chemicals per unit area to obtain high output [1].

**2.** Arable agriculture grows crops [1]; pastoral agriculture rears livestock [1].

**3.** An organism whose genetic material has been changed by removing, changing or inserting individual genes [1] to give favourable characteristics, such as pest resistance or drought tolerance [1].

**4.** Crop residue [1]; manure [1]; mulch [1].

**5.** Any two: uses land in cities that would otherwise produce no food, such as rooftops [1]; vertical farms grow crops in layers in a small area [1]; food is grown close to consumers, so less is wasted in transport [1]; gardens and allotments add to total production [1].

**6.** Weeds [1]; weeds compete with the crop for light, water and nutrients, so removing them leaves more for the crop [1].

**7. (a)** Field A: 54 ÷ 15 = 3.6 t/ha [1]. Field B: 42 ÷ 15 = 2.8 t/ha [1].

**(b)** (3.6 − 2.8) ÷ 2.8 × 100 [1] = 28.6% [1].

**(c)** Any three:
- Growing maize every year in Field B has exhausted the nutrients maize needs [1].
- Pests and diseases of maize have built up in Field B because their host is always present [1].
- In Field A, beans are legumes whose root nodules add nitrogen to the soil [1].
- Rotation breaks the life cycle of maize pests, so fewer survive in Field A [1].

**8.** Hydroponics: plants grow with their roots in a nutrient solution, without soil [1]. Aeroponics: roots hang in the air and are sprayed with a nutrient mist [1]. Benefit, any one: exact control of nutrients, water or temperature; little water used; crops grown all year; pests excluded [1]. Limitation, any one: high cost of equipment; needs energy for pumps, lighting or heating; requires technical skill [1].

**9. (a)** The insecticide killed most of the pests, but a few had a natural resistance [1]; these survived and bred [1], passing on resistance, so over the years most of the population became resistant [1].

**(b)** A natural predator or parasite of the pest is introduced, such as ladybirds to eat aphids [1]; it reduces the pest population without chemicals, and the pest cannot become resistant to being eaten in the same way [1].

**10.** Any three: land used for cash crops is no longer used to grow food for local people [1]; farmers earn money from exports but food must then be bought, often at higher prices [1]; if export prices fall, farmers may not earn enough to buy food [1]; the best land is often used for cash crops, pushing food crops onto poorer land [1].

**11.** Insecticides kill or harm bees and other insect pollinators as well as pests [1]. Fewer pollinators means fewer flowers are pollinated [1], so crops such as fruit trees produce fewer fruits and seeds, lowering yield [1].

**12.** Levels-based. Credit up to 6 from:
- **Benefits**: larger areas can be ploughed and harvested [1]; work is done quickly, so crops are planted and harvested at the best time [1]; less physical labour needed [1]; higher production per worker can increase income [1].
- **Limitations**: machines are expensive for small farms to buy and maintain [1]; fuel costs, and fuel may be hard to obtain in rural areas [1]; fewer farm jobs, so rural unemployment may rise [1]; heavy machines compact the soil [1]; small fields may be hard to work with large machines [1].
- **Judgement**: for example, sharing machines between farms through a co-operative reduces costs [1].

Maximum 6; at least one benefit and one limitation are needed for more than 3 marks.

**13.** Three suitable strategies, each with an explanation linked to the dry conditions, 2 marks each:
- **Trickle/drip irrigation**: delivers water directly to the roots [1], so little is lost to evaporation in the dry heat [1].
- **Rainwater harvesting**: collects water in the rainy season [1] and stores it for use in the dry periods [1].
- **Mulch (organic fertiliser)**: covers the soil surface [1], reducing evaporation and adding organic matter that helps the soil hold water [1].
- **GMOs**: drought-tolerant varieties [1] can grow with less water [1].
- **Automated watering**: soil moisture sensors water only when needed [1], so scarce water is not wasted [1].
- **Agroforestry**: trees give shade and shelter [1], reducing evaporation from the soil and crops [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Question 1**: describing intensive agriculture as a large farm rather than high inputs per area.
- **Question 7(b)**: dividing by Field A's yield instead of Field B's. "Higher than Field B" means Field B is the starting value.
- **Question 9(a)**: saying the pests "got used to" the insecticide. Resistance comes from survivors breeding.
- **Question 12**: discussing only benefits.
- **Question 13**: choosing strategies such as greenhouses without linking them to the dry periods in the question.
