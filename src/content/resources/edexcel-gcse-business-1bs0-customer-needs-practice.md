## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a customer need named in the specification? **[1]**

A: Profit

B: Market share

C: Convenience

D: Independence

**2.** A supermarket stocks 40 types of breakfast cereal. Which customer need is it mainly meeting? **[1]**

A: Choice

B: Price

C: Convenience

D: Quality

**3.** Define the term customer needs. **[1]**

**4.** Give **two** reasons from the specification why it is important for a business to identify and understand its customers. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why a business may not be able to offer customers both the lowest price and the highest quality. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **one** reason why failing to understand customer needs could threaten the survival of a new business. **[3]**

### Section B: case-study questions

**7.** *Velvet Comb* is a hair salon in a town centre. The owner, Priya, asked 200 customers which need matters most to them when choosing a salon.

| Need | Customers |
|---|---|
| Price | 54 |
| Quality | 72 |
| Choice | 18 |
| Convenience | 56 |

**(a)** Identify the need that matters most to Velvet Comb's customers. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage of customers who chose convenience. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the difference, in percentage points, between the share choosing quality and the share choosing price. **[2]**

**(d)** Outline **one** way Velvet Comb could meet its customers' need for convenience. **[2]**

**8.** Priya is considering two changes to increase sales at Velvet Comb.

Option 1: cut the price of a standard cut from £18.50 to £16.65.

Option 2: open late, until 9pm, on Thursday and Friday evenings. Many customers work in offices in the town until 5.30pm.

Velvet Comb currently gives 1,100 standard cuts a year.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage price cut in Option 1. **[2]**

**(b)** Justify which option Priya should choose. **[9]**

**9.** *Elmstead Garden Centre* has seen sales fall since a large DIY store opened nearby selling cheaper plants. Elmstead's customers often say its staff give excellent advice. Analyse how understanding customer needs could help Elmstead Garden Centre generate sales. **[6]**

**10.** Explain **one** reason why a business's customer needs may change over time. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** C: Convenience [1]. **[1]**

**2.** A: Choice [1]. **[1]**

**3.** What customers want from a product or service when they buy it, such as a fair price, good quality, choice or convenience [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Generating sales [1]; business survival [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Offering higher quality usually means using better materials or more skilled staff [1], which raises the business's costs [1]. To cover those costs and still make a profit, the business has to charge a higher price, so it cannot also be the cheapest [1]. **[3]**

**6.** A new business has little cash to fall back on [1]. If it offers products that do not meet customers' needs, sales will be low and stock may go unsold [1], so revenue may not cover costs and the business could run out of money and close [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** Quality (72 customers) [1].

**(b)** 56 ÷ 200 × 100 [1] = **28%** [1].

**(c)** Quality = 72 ÷ 200 × 100 = 36%; price = 54 ÷ 200 × 100 = 27% [1]. Difference = 36 − 27 = **9 percentage points** [1].

**(d)** Velvet Comb could let customers book appointments online at any time [1], so they do not need to phone during working hours, which saves busy customers time and makes them more likely to book [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Percentage points are the difference between two percentages. Writing "9%" is usually accepted at GCSE, but the precise term shows understanding.

**8. (a)** Price cut = £18.50 − £16.65 = £1.85 [1]. £1.85 ÷ £18.50 × 100 = **10%** [1].

**(b)** Option 1: only 27% of customers rank price as their main need [1], so a 10% cut may attract few extra customers. At 1,100 cuts, revenue would fall from £20,350 to £18,315 unless the number of cuts rises [1], a loss of £2,035 that must be made up by new customers [1].

Option 2: 28% of customers rank convenience first [1], and many work in offices until 5.30pm, so they find it hard to visit during normal hours [1]. Opening late on Thursday and Friday could attract these customers without lowering the price of any cut [1]. However, late opening means paying staff and heating for extra hours [1], so it will only add profit if enough customers book evening appointments.

Judgement: Priya should choose Option 2, because her own survey shows convenience matters slightly more than price and the office workers are a clear group whose need is not being met [1]. It also keeps the price of every existing cut unchanged, whereas Option 1 reduces revenue from every current customer [1]. **[11]**
*Examiner insight:* Strong Justify answers use the information given: here the survey percentages, the office hours and the revenue figures.

**9.** The DIY store attracts customers who mainly want a low price [1], and Elmstead is unlikely to win them by price because it cannot match a large store's costs [1]. Customers praise Elmstead's expert advice, so its customers value quality of service [1]. By focusing on this need, for example running free planting talks or offering a plant guarantee, it gives customers a reason to choose it over the cheaper store [1]. Satisfied customers are more likely to return and recommend Elmstead to friends [1], so sales can rise even without cutting prices, helping it recover from the fall in sales [1]. **[6]**

**10.** Customers' circumstances change, for example more people working from home [1]. Their needs change with them, such as wanting delivery rather than visiting a shop (convenience) [1], so a business that does not keep checking may offer what customers used to want and lose sales to rivals that adapt [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Identifying the need with the lowest figure instead of the highest.
- Dividing by the wrong total when calculating a percentage.
- Treating a 10% price cut as certain to raise revenue.
- Naming convenience without a practical example linked to the customers.
- In Analyse answers, writing about customers in general rather than those in the case.
