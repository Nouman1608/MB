## Section A

**1.** In a market, demand decreases and supply increases at the same time. What must happen?

A: Equilibrium price falls

B: Equilibrium price rises

C: Equilibrium quantity falls

D: Equilibrium quantity rises

**[1]**

**2.** Wool and lamb meat are produced from the same sheep. This is an example of

A: alternative demand

B: derived demand

C: joint demand

D: joint supply

**[1]**

**3.** Define market disequilibrium. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why the demand for cement is a derived demand. **[2]**

**5.** Explain what happens to a shortage at a price below equilibrium if price is free to change. **[3]**

**6.** Explain the rationing function of price. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** In an invented market for phone cases, Qd = 500 - 20P and Qs = 30P - 100, where P is in cu and Q is hundreds a week.

**(a)** Calculate the equilibrium price and quantity. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the excess demand or excess supply at a price of 8 cu and at a price of 15 cu. **[2]**

**(c)** Demand increases by 100 at every price. Calculate the new equilibrium. **[2]**

**(d)** Starting again from the original curves, supply instead increases by 50 at every price. Calculate the new equilibrium. **[2]**

**(e)** Calculate the equilibrium if both shifts in (c) and (d) happen together, and explain why the change in price is uncertain before calculation. **[3]**

**8.** A fall in the price of games consoles is followed by a rise in the price of console games.

**(a)** Identify the relationship between the two goods. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain, using the idea of shifts and movements, why the price of games rose. **[3]**

**9.** A drought reduces the coffee harvest in a major producing country, while a rise in incomes increases demand for coffee in importing countries.

**(a)** State the effect on the equilibrium price of coffee and explain why it is certain. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the effect on equilibrium quantity is uncertain. **[2]**

**(c)** Tea is a substitute for coffee. Explain the likely effect on the tea market. **[2]**

**10.** Demand for air travel rises sharply after a period of restrictions is lifted.

**(a)** Explain how the signalling function of price works in this market. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain how the incentive function of price leads to a reallocation of resources. **[3]**

**(c)** Comment on one reason why the price mechanism might not remove the shortage quickly. **[2]**

**11.** Explain how a rise in demand for petrol could affect the market for diesel. **[4]**

**12.** Explain why a rise in demand for new houses could raise the wages of electricians. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **A** [1]. Both shifts push price down; the change in quantity depends on their sizes.

**2.** **D** [1]. One production process yields both products.

**3.** A situation in which **quantity demanded and quantity supplied are not equal** at the current price [1], so there is excess demand or excess supply and pressure for price to change [1].

**4.** Cement is not wanted for itself but as an input into **buildings and roads** [1], so demand for cement depends on, and rises or falls with, **demand for construction** [1].

**5.** At a price below equilibrium, **quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied** [1]. Buyers compete for the limited goods and sellers raise the price [1]. As price rises, quantity demanded **contracts** and quantity supplied **extends** until they are equal and the shortage is removed [1].

**6.** When a good is scarce, the price rises until **quantity demanded falls to the quantity available** [1], so the good goes to those **willing and able to pay** the price [1].

**7. (a)** 500 - 20P = 30P - 100, so 600 = 50P and **P = 12 cu** [1]. Q = 500 - 240 = **260 hundred** [1].

**(b)** At 8 cu: Qd = 340, Qs = 140, **excess demand of 200** [1]. At 15 cu: Qd = 200, Qs = 350, **excess supply of 150** [1].

**(c)** Qd = 600 - 20P. 600 - 20P = 30P - 100, so 700 = 50P, **P = 14 cu** [1], **Q = 320 hundred** [1].

**(d)** Qs = 30P - 50. 500 - 20P = 30P - 50, so 550 = 50P, **P = 11 cu** [1], **Q = 280 hundred** [1].

**(e)** 600 - 20P = 30P - 50, so 650 = 50P, **P = 13 cu** and **Q = 340 hundred** [1]. The demand increase raises price while the supply increase lowers it [1], so the net effect depends on which shift is larger; here the demand shift dominates and price rises from 12 to 13 cu [1].

**8. (a)** **Joint demand**: consoles and games are complements [1].

**(b)** The lower console price causes an **extension in quantity demanded of consoles**, a movement along their demand curve [1]. More console owners want games, so the **demand curve for games shifts right** [1]. At the old price of games there is excess demand, so the **price of games rises** to a new equilibrium [1].

**9. (a)** Price **rises** [1]. The drought shifts supply left and higher incomes shift demand right; **both shifts raise price** [1].

**(b)** The supply shift **reduces quantity** while the demand shift **raises it** [1], so the final change depends on the **relative size of the two shifts** [1].

**(c)** Higher coffee prices lead some consumers to switch, so **demand for tea shifts right** [1], raising the equilibrium price and quantity of tea [1].

**10. (a)** Excess demand at the old price lets airlines **raise fares** [1]. The higher fares tell airlines and airports that consumers now **value air travel more** and that seats are scarce [1].

**(b)** Higher fares raise **expected profit** on flights [1], so airlines add routes, return aircraft to service and hire crew [1]. Resources such as labour and capital **move into air travel** from less profitable uses [1].

**(c)** Supply is **inelastic in the short run** [1]: aircraft, trained pilots and airport slots cannot be added quickly, so the shortage and high fares may last for some time [1].

**11.** Higher demand for petrol raises its price and leads refineries to **process more crude oil** [1]. Refining produces petrol and diesel together, so this is **joint supply** [1]. The **supply of diesel shifts right** [1]. If demand for diesel is unchanged, its **price falls and quantity rises** [1].

**12.** Electricians are needed to build houses, so demand for their labour is a **derived demand** [1]. More house building **shifts the demand for electricians right** [1]. With the supply of electricians unchanged, their **equilibrium wage rises** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Claiming a definite change in quantity in Question 9(b).
- Describing the games market in Question 8 as a movement rather than a shift.
- Calculating excess demand as quantity supplied minus quantity demanded and losing the sign meaning.
- Listing the functions of price in Question 10 without applying them to air travel.
