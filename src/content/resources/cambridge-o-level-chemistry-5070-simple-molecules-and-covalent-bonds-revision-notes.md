These notes condense section 2.5, Simple molecules and covalent bonds, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). The method and worked dot-and-cross examples are in the [simple molecules and covalent bonds study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-simple-molecules-and-covalent-bonds/); exam-style questions are in the [simple molecules and covalent bonds practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-simple-molecules-and-covalent-bonds-practice/).

## Definition (learn exactly)

A **covalent bond** is formed when a **pair of electrons is shared** between two atoms, leading to **noble gas electronic configurations**.

- Forms between **non-metal** atoms.
- Each atom gives one electron to each shared pair.
- Single bond = 1 shared pair; double = 2; triple = 3.

## Bonds each atom forms

| Atom | Outer electrons | Bonds needed | Non-bonding pairs (when all bonds single) |
|---|---|---|---|
| H | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| C | 4 | 4 | 0 |
| N | 5 | 3 | 1 |
| O | 6 | 2 | 2 |
| Cl | 7 | 1 | 3 |

Each **double** bond uses up two of an atom's bonds; each **triple** bond uses three.

## The named molecules at a glance

| Molecule | Structural formula | Shared pairs | Non-bonding pairs |
|---|---|---|---|
| H₂ | H–H | 1 | 0 |
| Cl₂ | Cl–Cl | 1 | 6 (3 each) |
| HCl | H–Cl | 1 | 3 |
| H₂O | H–O–H | 2 | 2 |
| NH₃ | N bonded to 3 H | 3 | 1 |
| CH₄ | C bonded to 4 H | 4 | 0 |
| O₂ | O=O | 2 | 4 (2 each) |
| CO₂ | O=C=O | 4 | 4 (2 on each O) |
| N₂ | N≡N | 3 | 2 (1 each) |
| C₂H₄ | H₂C=CH₂ | 6 | 0 |
| CH₃OH | H₃C–O–H | 5 | 2 (on O) |

**Counting rule:** for each atom, (2 × number of shared pairs it is in) + (2 × its non-bonding pairs) = 8 (or 2 for H).

## Dot-and-cross checklist

- Overlapping circles for outer shells (only outer shells needed).
- Each shared pair in the overlap: one dot and one cross.
- Remaining outer electrons as pairs outside the overlap.
- A key if more than two kinds of atom (or use a third symbol).
- Final count: 8 around every atom except H (2).

## "Similar molecules": copy the pattern

Atoms in the same group form the same number of bonds, so an unfamiliar molecule usually matches a named one.

| Unfamiliar molecule | Same pattern as | Why |
|---|---|---|
| H₂S | H₂O | S and O both Group VI: 2 bonds, 2 non-bonding pairs |
| PH₃ | NH₃ | P and N both Group V: 3 bonds, 1 non-bonding pair |
| SiH₄, CCl₄ | CH₄ | central atom forms 4 single bonds |
| HBr, HF | HCl | halogen forms 1 bond, keeps 3 non-bonding pairs |
| F₂, Br₂ | Cl₂ | one single bond, 3 non-bonding pairs on each atom |

## Properties of simple molecular compounds

| Property | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Low melting and boiling points | only **weak intermolecular forces** between molecules are overcome; **little energy** needed; covalent bonds **not broken** |
| Often gases or liquids at r.t.p. | consequence of the weak forces between molecules |
| Poor electrical conductivity | molecules are neutral: **no ions** and **no delocalised electrons** free to move |

Specific types of intermolecular force are not required for 5070.

Approximate boiling points to illustrate: methane −162 °C; ammonia −33 °C; methanol 65 °C; water 100 °C.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Covalent bond | a shared pair of electrons between two atoms |
| Molecule | a group of atoms held together by covalent bonds |
| Simple molecular substance | made of small, separate molecules |
| Intermolecular forces | weak forces of attraction between separate molecules |
| Non-bonding pair | pair of outer electrons not involved in a bond |
| Double bond | two shared pairs between the same two atoms |
| Triple bond | three shared pairs between the same two atoms |

## Exam traps

- **Weak forces between molecules**, not weak covalent bonds.
- Boiling water gives steam made of H₂O molecules: no bonds inside the molecule break.
- Ammonia's nitrogen has a non-bonding pair; water's oxygen has two.
- O₂ has a double bond, N₂ a triple bond, CO₂ two double bonds.
- Ethene has a C=C double bond; methanol has only single bonds.
- "No electrons to move" is wrong: there are electrons, but none are delocalised, and there are no ions.

## Self-test

1. Define a covalent bond.
2. How many covalent bonds does a nitrogen atom form in ammonia?
3. How many non-bonding pairs are on the oxygen atom in methanol?
4. Describe the bonding in a nitrogen molecule.
5. How many electrons are shared between the two carbon atoms in ethene?
6. Show that the carbon atom in carbon dioxide has a noble gas configuration.
7. How many non-bonding pairs are there in a chlorine molecule altogether?
8. Predict the number of bonds and non-bonding pairs on the phosphorus atom in PH₃ (phosphorus is in Group V).
9. Explain why chlorine is a gas at room temperature.
10. Why does liquid methanol not conduct electricity?
11. When ammonia boils, what is overcome?
12. Write the structural formula of oxygen, O₂, showing the bond.

## Answers

1. A pair of electrons shared between two atoms, leading to noble gas electronic configurations.
2. Three.
3. Two.
4. Three shared pairs of electrons (a triple bond) between the two atoms, with one non-bonding pair on each nitrogen.
5. Four (two shared pairs).
6. It is in two double bonds: 4 shared pairs = 8 electrons.
7. Six (three on each atom).
8. Three bonds and one non-bonding pair, like nitrogen in ammonia.
9. The forces between Cl₂ molecules are weak, so little energy is needed to separate them; the boiling point is below room temperature.
10. It contains neutral molecules: no ions and no delocalised electrons free to move.
11. The weak intermolecular forces between ammonia molecules (not the N–H covalent bonds).
12. O=O.
