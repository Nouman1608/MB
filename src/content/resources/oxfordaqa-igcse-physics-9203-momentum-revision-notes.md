Condensed recall for **3.1.4 Momentum** (P content, full Physics award) in OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203), for exams May/June 2018 onwards. Worked examples and full explanations are in the [Momentum study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-momentum/); exam-style questions are in the [Momentum practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-momentum-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Momentum, p | Mass × velocity; a vector, unit kg m/s |
| Closed system | A group of objects on which no external force acts |
| Conservation of momentum | In a closed system, total momentum before an interaction equals total momentum after |
| Collision | An interaction in which objects hit each other; they may stick together or separate |
| Explosion | An interaction in which objects at rest push apart; total momentum stays zero |
| Change in momentum, Δp | Final momentum − initial momentum |
| Rate of change of momentum | Change in momentum ÷ time taken; equals the force |
| Recoil | The backward motion of one object when another is pushed away from it |

## Equations

```
p = m x v
F = Δp / t      (so Δp = F x t)
```

Units: p and Δp in kg m/s, m in kg, v in m/s, F in N, t in s. A newton second (N s) is the same as a kg m/s.

## Collision method in five lines

1. Choose and state a positive direction.
2. Give velocities in the other direction a minus sign.
3. Total momentum before = sum of m × v for both objects.
4. Total momentum after = the same expression with the after-velocities.
5. Set them equal and solve; interpret the sign of the answer.

If the objects stick together, use their combined mass with one shared velocity.

## Explosions and recoil

- Start at rest, so total momentum before = 0.
- After: m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = 0, so the momenta are equal in size and opposite in direction.
- Speed is inversely related to mass: the lighter object moves faster.

## Rebounds

- The velocity reverses, so the final momentum has the opposite sign.
- Size of Δp = m × (speed before + speed after).
- Example: 0.20 kg ball, 8.0 m/s in, 6.0 m/s out: Δp = 0.20 × 14 = 2.8 kg m/s.

## Safety features (explain qualitatively)

The four-link chain: momentum must change by a fixed amount → the feature **increases the time** of the change → the **rate of change of momentum** is smaller → the **force** on the person is smaller.

| Feature | What lengthens the time |
|---|---|
| Air bag | Deflates as the occupant moves into it; also spreads the force over a larger area |
| Seat belt | Stretches slightly; also stops the occupant hitting the windscreen |
| Crumple zone | Crushes in a controlled way |
| Side impact bar | Spreads the force through the car body and slows the door's intrusion |
| Crash mat | Compresses as the gymnast lands |
| Playground surface | Compresses as a child lands |
| Cycle helmet | Foam liner crushes |

## Exam traps

- **Signs.** One missing minus sign in a head-on collision wrecks the answer.
- **Rebounds.** Add the speeds to find the size of Δp; do not subtract them.
- **Zero total momentum** after an explosion does not mean the objects are still.
- **Wrong words.** Features do not "absorb momentum" or "reduce momentum"; they increase the time and reduce the force.
- **Milliseconds.** Convert ms to s before dividing.
- **Closed system.** Friction or air resistance makes a system not closed; give this as the reason for experimental differences.
- **Third Law link.** In a collision the two objects feel forces of equal size for the same time, so their momentum changes are equal and opposite.

## Self-test

**1.** Calculate the momentum of a 0.16 kg ball moving at 25 m/s.

**2.** Give the unit of momentum.

**3.** State the condition for momentum to be conserved.

**4.** A 3.0 kg trolley moving at 2.0 m/s hits a stationary 1.0 kg trolley and they stick. Find their velocity.

**5.** Two trolleys at rest spring apart. One has momentum 4.5 kg m/s to the left. State the momentum of the other.

**6.** A 70 kg person jumps from a 140 kg boat at rest, moving forwards at 3.0 m/s. Find the boat's velocity.

**7.** A 0.50 kg ball hits a wall at 4.0 m/s and rebounds at 3.0 m/s. Find the size of the change in momentum.

**8.** A force of 200 N acts for 0.15 s. Find the change in momentum.

**9.** A momentum change of 36 kg m/s takes 0.090 s. Find the average force.

**10.** Explain in one chain of reasoning how a cycle helmet reduces injury.

**11.** Why can a crash test with a car on a track give a slightly smaller total momentum after the collision than before?

**12.** Car X (1000 kg, 20 m/s east) and car Y (1500 kg, 10 m/s west) collide and stick. Find their velocity.

## Self-test answers

**1.** p = 0.16 × 25 = 4.0 kg m/s.

**2.** kg m/s (or N s).

**3.** The system must be closed: no external forces act.

**4.** 3.0 × 2.0 = 6.0 kg m/s; v = 6.0 ÷ 4.0 = 1.5 m/s in the original direction.

**5.** 4.5 kg m/s to the right.

**6.** 70 × 3.0 = 210 kg m/s forwards for the person; boat: 210 ÷ 140 = 1.5 m/s backwards.

**7.** Δp = 0.50 × (4.0 + 3.0) = 3.5 kg m/s.

**8.** Δp = 200 × 0.15 = 30 kg m/s.

**9.** F = 36 ÷ 0.090 = 400 N.

**10.** The foam crushes, so the head's momentum changes to zero over a longer time; the rate of change of momentum is smaller, so the force on the head is smaller.

**11.** External forces such as friction from the track and air resistance act, so the system is not perfectly closed.

**12.** East positive: 20 000 − 15 000 = 5000 kg m/s; v = 5000 ÷ 2500 = 2.0 m/s east.
