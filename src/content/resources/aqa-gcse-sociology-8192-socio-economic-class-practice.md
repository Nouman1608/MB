## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** In Marx's theory, which group owns small businesses?

A. Bourgeoisie
B. Petty bourgeoisie
C. Proletariat
D. Lumpenproletariat

**[1]**

**2.** For Weber, a group of people who share a similar level of prestige or esteem share a similar:

A. Class situation
B. Market situation
C. Status situation
D. Party membership

**[1]**

**3.** Explain the difference between income and wealth. **[2]**

**4.** Describe what Marx meant by false class consciousness. **[2]**

**5.** Identify and explain **one** reason why Weber did not expect society to polarise into two classes. **[3]**

**6.** Describe Weber's idea of social closure, using an example. **[3]**

### Section B: longer and structured questions

**7.** Read the item.

> Kai is an electrician who runs his own business with two employees. His sister Mira is a hospital consultant earning a high salary. Their cousin Ben works on a supermarket checkout on a zero-hours contract. All three say they are "working class, like our parents".

**(a)** Using the item, explain how Marx would classify Kai and Mira. **[2]**
**(b)** Using the item, explain how Weber's idea of market situation would distinguish Mira from Ben. **[2]**

**8.** Explain **two** ways in which feminists have criticised traditional approaches to socio-economic class. **[4]**

**9.** Study the table. The data are fictional.

> **Brackley (a fictional town): adults' own description of their class, and their official occupational class, 2025**

| Official occupational class | Adults surveyed | Described themselves as working class |
|---|---|---|
| Professional and managerial | 200 | 40 |
| Intermediate | 150 | 60 |
| Routine and manual | 250 | 200 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of professional and managerial adults who described themselves as working class. **[1]**
**(b)** Using the table, explain what is meant by subjective class. **[3]**

**10.** Identify and explain **two** differences between Marx's and Weber's views of socio-economic class. **[4]**

**11.** Discuss how far sociologists would agree that Marx's theory of class still explains class divisions in the UK today. **[12]**

**12.** Discuss how far sociologists would agree that social class is the most important form of inequality in the UK. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** B. Petty bourgeoisie [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* the lumpenproletariat option is a common wrong answer; it refers to those at the very bottom, not small owners.

**2.** C. Status situation [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* market situation is about economic position; prestige belongs to status.

**3.** Income is a flow of money received over a period, such as wages or benefits [1], whereas wealth is a stock of assets owned, such as property, land, shares or savings [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* the "whereas" earns the mark for difference; two separate definitions with no contrast can still score, but an example of each helps.

**4.** False class consciousness is when members of the subject class accept ruling class ideology [1], seeing capitalism as normal and natural even though it exploits them, so they do not challenge it [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* link it to ideology; "workers are confused" is too vague.

**5.** Weber argued that the middle class expands rather than contracts as capitalism develops [1], because there is growing demand for professional and other skilled services [1], so more people hold well-paid non-owning positions between the two extremes instead of being pushed into the proletariat [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* a mark comes from the reason (demand for skills), not just the claim.

**6.** Social closure is the process by which some individuals are excluded from membership of a status group [1], so that its prestige and advantages are kept for insiders [1]. For example, in the caste system people cannot move into a higher caste or marry outside their own [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* the caste example is in the specification's own summary; a professional body limiting entry is also acceptable.

**7. (a)** Kai owns his business and employs others, so Marx would place him in the petty bourgeoisie, a small owner [1]. Mira, though highly paid, does not own the means of production and sells her labour, so she would be part of the proletariat or non-owning class [1].
**(b)** Mira's scarce, highly qualified skills give her a strong market situation, with high pay and security [1], whereas Ben's job needs few qualifications and is insecure, so his market situation and life chances are much weaker, placing them in different classes [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* the point of the item is that all three call themselves working class; strong answers notice that self-description and objective class can differ.

**8.** First, traditional class schemes placed a woman according to her husband's or father's occupation [1], which hid her own job, pay and position in the labour market [1]. Second, class analysis ignored unpaid domestic labour [1], even though Marxist feminists argue this work benefits capitalism by caring for and reproducing the workforce at no cost to employers [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* each point needs its own consequence; do not repeat "it ignores women" twice.

**9. (a)** 40 ÷ 200 × 100 = 20% [1]
**(b)** Subjective class is the class people believe they belong to [1], which may differ from their official occupational class [1]. In Brackley, 20% of professional and managerial adults described themselves as working class, even though their occupation places them in a higher class [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* use a figure from the table in part (b); otherwise the application mark is lost.

**10.** First, Marx defined class by ownership of the means of production [1], whereas Weber defined it by market situation, so non-owners can be divided into several classes [1]. Second, Marx expected polarisation and proletarian revolution [1], whereas Weber saw the middle class expanding and rejected the idea that revolution was inevitable [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* another valid difference is that Weber saw status and party as separate from class, while Marx saw political power as coming from economic power.

**11.** The real exam marks extended answers using levels of response, not point by point. The points below show indicative creditworthy content.

- Marx saw class as based on ownership of the means of production [1].
- The bourgeoisie exploit the proletariat; political power comes from economic power [1].
- Ruling class ideology and false class consciousness keep the system stable [1].
- For: inherited wealth and the ownership of land and large businesses remain concentrated in few hands [1].
- For: many workers in insecure, low-paid jobs, such as zero-hours work, still sell their labour with little control [1].
- Against: the middle class has expanded, as Weber argued, rather than disappearing [1].
- Against: no proletarian revolution has taken place in Britain [1].
- Weber: non-owners are divided by market situation; professionals have very different life chances from routine workers [1].
- Weber: status and party, such as ethnicity, religion or pressure groups, also shape inequality [1].
- New Right: Marx ignores the social mobility and opportunities created by capitalism [1].
- Feminist: Marx's model focuses on class and overlooks women's unpaid work and gender inequality [1].
- Judgement: Marx explains the continuing divide between owners and workers, but Weber's more complex picture fits the growth of the middle class better, so Marx explains class divisions only partly [1]. **[12]**

*Examiner insight:* "still" and "today" require contemporary examples; describe a modern case, not only nineteenth-century factories.

**12.** The real exam marks extended answers using levels of response, not point by point. The points below show indicative creditworthy content.

- Marxists argue class is the most important inequality because it is based on ownership and exploitation [1].
- Class shapes life chances such as health, education, housing and work [1].
- Weber links shared class situation to shared life chances [1].
- Class gaps in education, such as those found by Halsey, Heath and Ridge, support the importance of class [1].
- Against: Weber argues status inequalities, from ethnicity, religion or lifestyle, can matter independently [1].
- Feminists argue gender is at least as important; Walby shows patriarchy across paid work, the home and the state [1].
- Women are concentrated in lower-paid work and face the glass ceiling across all classes [1].
- Racial discrimination and institutional racism can limit life chances within every class [1].
- Functionalists argue class differences reflect talent and are not a form of injustice [1].
- New Right theorists stress social mobility, suggesting class is less fixed than Marxists claim [1].
- Inequalities overlap: a working-class woman from an ethnic minority may face several disadvantages at once [1].
- Judgement: class remains a powerful influence, but gender and ethnicity cut across it, so it is one of several key inequalities rather than clearly the most important [1]. **[12]**

*Examiner insight:* the question invites comparison, so name at least one alternative inequality and argue which carries more weight.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating status and class as the same thing.
- Placing every high earner in the bourgeoisie, even if they own nothing.
- Leaving out Weber's agreement with Marx about ownership.
- Ignoring figures in a table question.
- Writing about Marx without evaluating his predictions against what happened.
