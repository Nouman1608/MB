These notes condense **3.2.3.1 Sources of finance** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Business (9725) specification, Version 1.1 (International AS, Unit 2). The [sources of finance study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-sources-of-finance-9725/) has the worked examples on sale and leaseback, working capital and dilution; the [sources of finance practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-sources-of-finance-9725-practice/) test them. Figures are invented.

## Recall in one minute

- **Internal (4):** retained profits, sale of assets, sale and leaseback, working capital.
- **External (7):** trade credit, share capital, overdraft, loans, business angels, private equity, crowd funding.
- **Appropriateness** in different situations, for example a start-up.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Internal finance | Money raised from within the business |
| External finance | Money raised from outside the business |
| Retained profits | Profit kept in the business rather than paid to owners |
| Sale and leaseback | Selling an asset and renting it back to keep using it |
| Working capital | Money available for day-to-day operations; cash can be released from inventory and receivables |
| Trade credit | Time allowed by suppliers before payment is due |
| Overdraft | Permission to take a bank account below zero up to a limit |
| Loan | A fixed sum borrowed and repaid with interest over an agreed period |
| Share capital | Money raised by selling shares in a limited company |
| Business angel | A wealthy individual investing their own money in a small business in exchange for shares |
| Private equity | Investment firms taking large stakes in established private companies, aiming to sell later at a profit |
| Crowd funding | Raising money from many small backers, usually online |
| Security (collateral) | An asset a lender can claim if a loan is not repaid |
| Dilution | The fall in existing owners' percentage stake when new shares are issued |

## Sources at a glance

| Source | Main plus | Main minus | Typical term |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retained profits | No interest; no loss of control | Needs past profit; smaller dividends | Any |
| Sale of assets | Turns idle assets into cash | Asset gone; may sell cheaply | One-off |
| Sale and leaseback | Large sum; keep using asset | Rent for ever; lose any rise in value | Long |
| Working capital | No interest | Limited; risk of stock-outs or upsetting customers | Short |
| Trade credit | Usually free | Late payment harms relations | Short |
| Overdraft | Flexible; pay interest only on use | High rates; can be withdrawn | Short |
| Loan | Predictable; no loss of control | Interest; security; must repay | Medium/long |
| Share capital | No repayment or interest | Dilutes control; dividends expected | Permanent |
| Business angels | Money plus expertise | Give up shares and some control | Long |
| Private equity | Large sums; management input | May demand control and quick returns | Medium |
| Crowd funding | Tests demand; many backers | May miss target; idea made public | One-off |

## Quick calculations

```
Interest for part of a year = amount × annual rate × months/12
Cash released from working capital = old level − new level (inventory or receivables)
New investor's % = new shares ÷ total shares after issue × 100
Implied value of business = amount invested ÷ % stake bought
Crowd funding raised = number of backers × average pledge
```

## Matching sources to situations

- **Start-up**: owner's capital, business angels, crowd funding; loans hard without a record or security.
- **Short-term gap**: overdraft, trade credit, working capital.
- **Long-life asset**: loan, retained profits, sale and leaseback.
- **Rapid growth, large sums**: share capital, private equity.
- **Struggling firm**: sale of assets, sale and leaseback.

## Exam traps

- Match the **term** of the finance to the **life** of the need.
- Retained profits are not "free": owners give up dividends.
- Equity sources dilute **control and future profits**.
- Check that the business can actually obtain the source (sole traders cannot issue shares).
- Sale and leaseback adds a permanent fixed cost.
- Give a recommendation with reasons when the question says "evaluate" or "assess".

## Self-test

1. Name the four internal sources of finance in the specification.
2. Give one advantage of an overdraft over a loan.
3. Calculate the interest on a $24,000 overdraft for 3 months at 10% a year.
4. A firm cuts inventory from $65,000 to $40,000. How much cash is released?
5. A founder owns 60,000 shares; 15,000 new shares are sold to an angel. What percentage does the angel own?
6. An angel invests $50,000 for a 20% stake. What value does this place on the business?
7. Why might a bank refuse a loan to a start-up?
8. Give one disadvantage of sale and leaseback.
9. Give one advantage of crowd funding beyond the money raised.
10. Why can a sole trader not use share capital?
11. Which source suits a firm that must pay suppliers before customers pay it?
12. Give one disadvantage of private equity for the original owners.

### Answers

1. Retained profits, sale of assets, sale and leaseback, working capital.
2. Flexible, and interest is paid only on the amount used (any one).
3. 24,000 × 10% × 3/12 = **$600**.
4. 65,000 − 40,000 = **$25,000**.
5. 15,000 ÷ 75,000 × 100 = **20%**.
6. 50,000 ÷ 20% = **$250,000**.
7. No trading record and little security, so the risk of default is high.
8. Rent must be paid every year, raising fixed costs (or the firm loses any rise in the asset's value).
9. It tests demand and builds a group of early customers (any valid point).
10. A sole trader is not a limited company, so it has no shares to sell.
11. Trade credit or an overdraft (working capital improvement also credited).
12. They may lose control of decisions and face pressure for fast returns.
