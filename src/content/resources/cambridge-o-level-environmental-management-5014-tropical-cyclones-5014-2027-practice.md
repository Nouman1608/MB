These questions cover sub-topic 6.2, Tropical cyclones, of the 2027-2029 Cambridge O Level Environmental
Management (5014) syllabus. The contexts are a set of ocean monitoring sites, a low-lying delta coast and
an island state hit by a cyclone.

## Section A

**1.** State the minimum ocean temperature needed for a tropical cyclone to form. **[1]**

**2.** Name **two** other terms that the syllabus groups under *tropical cyclones*. **[2]**

**3.** State the range of latitudes in which tropical cyclones form. **[1]**

**4.** Name **one** strategy for managing the impacts of a tropical cyclone that is used after the event. **[1]**

## Section B

**5.** The table shows data for three ocean monitoring sites. *The data are invented for practice.*

| Site | Latitude | Sea temperature / °C | Ocean depth / m |
|---|---|---|---|
| P | 8° S | 29 | 35 |
| Q | 20° N | 28 | 300 |
| R | 33° N | 27 | 500 |

(a) Identify the site where a tropical cyclone is most likely to form. **[1]**
(b) For each of the other **two** sites, explain why a tropical cyclone is unlikely to form. **[2]**

**6.** Explain how a tropical cyclone gains its energy from the ocean. **[3]**

**7.** A tropical cyclone crosses a low-lying delta coast where many people farm rice.
(a) Describe **three** impacts of the cyclone on the farming community. **[3]**
(b) Explain why landslides may occur in hills inland of the delta. **[2]**

**8.** Explain why contamination of drinking-water supplies is common after a tropical cyclone. **[2]**

**9.** Describe how the structure of buildings can be improved to reduce damage from tropical cyclones.
**[3]**

**10.** Monitoring shows that a cyclone will reach an island state in 48 hours. Describe **three** actions the
government should take in that time. **[3]**

**11.** In an evacuation, 18 000 of a district's 24 000 residents moved to shelters.
(a) Calculate the percentage of residents who were evacuated. **[1]**
(b) Suggest **two** reasons why some residents did not evacuate. **[2]**

**12.** Explain **two** reasons why tropical cyclones cause large financial losses. **[4]**

## Section C

**13.** Discuss the benefits and limitations of disaster preparation and international aid for managing the
impacts of a tropical cyclone in a low-income country. **[6]**

**14.** "Strategies used before a tropical cyclone are more important than those used afterwards." Discuss.
**[6]**

## Answers

**1.** 27 °C. **[1]**

**2.** Any two: storms **[1]**; hurricanes **[1]**; typhoons **[1]**. **[max 2]**

**3.** Between 5° and 30° north or south of the Equator. **[1]**

**4.** Rebuilding of damaged areas or international aid. **[1]**

**5.** (a) Q. **[1]**
(b) P: the ocean is too shallow (35 m, below 60 m) **[1]**; R: the latitude of 33° N is outside the 5°-30° range
**[1]**.

**6.** Warm water (at least 27 °C) evaporates, so warm, moist air rises **[1]**; as it rises the water vapour
condenses, releasing heat **[1]**, which makes the air rise faster, lowers the pressure and draws in more
moist air, so the storm strengthens **[1]**.

**7.** (a) Any three: rice crops flattened by wind or drowned by flooding **[1]**; storm surge brings salt water
onto fields, making soil unusable **[1]**; livestock drowned or lost **[1]**; homes and stores of seed or grain
destroyed **[1]**; farmers lose income (financial losses) **[1]**. **[max 3]**
(b) Heavy rain saturates the soil on slopes **[1]**, making it heavy and weakening it so it slides downhill
**[1]**.

**8.** Floodwater mixes with sewage from damaged or overflowing sewers and toilets **[1]** and enters wells,
pipes and rivers used for drinking water **[1]**.

**9.** Any three: roofs firmly tied to walls so they are not lifted off **[1]**; storm shutters over windows **[1]**;
strong materials such as reinforced concrete **[1]**; raised floors or buildings on stilts in surge areas **[1]**;
deep foundations **[1]**. **[max 3]**

**10.** Any three: issue warnings by radio, phone and loudspeaker **[1]**; organise evacuation of coastal and
low-lying areas, including livestock **[1]**; open and stock shelters **[1]**; place emergency rescue teams
and supplies ready **[1]**; tell people to secure property and store water **[1]**. **[max 3]**

**11.** (a) 18 000 / 24 000 x 100 = **75%**. **[1]**
(b) Any two: did not receive or believe the warning **[1]**; wanted to protect homes, property or livestock
**[1]**; no transport, or elderly or ill **[1]**; shelters too far away or feared overcrowded **[1]**. **[max 2]**

**12.** Two explained reasons, two marks each. Damage to buildings and infrastructure such as ports, roads
and power lines **[1]** costs a great deal to repair and stops businesses working **[1]**. Loss of crops and
livestock **[1]** removes farmers' income and export earnings, and may need imports of food **[1]**. (Accept
lost tourism income with development.)

**13.** **[6]** One mark per point, up to six. Disaster preparation: plans and drills mean people know where to
go **[1]**; emergency supplies and rescue teams are in place before the storm **[1]**; it is relatively cheap
compared with rebuilding **[1]**; but it needs organisation and regular practice that may be lacking **[1]**.
International aid: brings money, food, clean water and medical teams **[1]**; but it may arrive days later or
fail to reach remote areas **[1]**. Conclusion: preparation saves more lives; aid is vital for recovery in a
low-income country **[1]**. **[max 6]**

**14.** **[6]** One mark per point, up to six. Before: monitoring and warning give days of notice **[1]**,
allowing evacuation that prevents most deaths from storm surge **[1]**; stronger buildings reduce damage
**[1]**. After: rebuilding restores homes and services and can build back stronger **[1]**; international aid
helps where the country cannot cope **[1]**; after-event strategies matter most for long-term recovery and
financial losses **[1]**. Conclusion: before-event strategies are more important for saving lives, but both
are needed **[1]**. **[max 6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving formation conditions without the syllabus numbers.
- Rejecting a site without saying which condition it fails and by how much.
- Writing "the cyclone causes flooding" without saying whether rain or storm surge is the cause.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is storm surge in the syllabus?**
Flooding is a named impact of tropical cyclones in 6.2.2, and storm surges are a named cause of flooding in
6.3.1, so you can use storm surge to explain coastal flooding.

**Is land use zoning a cyclone strategy in this syllabus?**
No. The 6.2.3 list is monitoring and warning, structure of buildings, disaster preparation, evacuation,
shelters, rebuilding and international aid.
