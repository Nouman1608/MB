These questions test every learning outcome in Topic 4, The atmosphere and human activities, of the
2027-2029 Cambridge O Level Environmental Management (5014) syllabus. They use a farming region, a city
and an industrial valley as contexts, and include data handling and "discuss" questions.

## Section A

**1.** Name the layer of the atmosphere directly above the troposphere. **[1]**

**2.** State the **two** gases that make up most of clean air. **[1]**

**3.** Name the gas released when wetlands are drained. **[1]**

**4.** State the chemicals that cause ozone depletion and **one** product they were used in. **[2]**

## Section B

**5.** Explain why the Earth would be too cold to support most life without the natural greenhouse effect.
**[3]**

**6.** A rice-growing region keeps large herds of cattle.
(a) Describe **three** ways farming in this region adds methane to the atmosphere. **[3]**
(b) Suggest **two** changes that would reduce the region's carbon footprint. **[2]**

**7.** The table shows estimated sea-level rise at a coastal station.

| Year | 1990 | 2005 | 2020 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rise since 1990 (mm) | 0 | 45 | 100 |

(a) Calculate the mean rate of rise per year between 1990 and 2020. **[2]**
(b) Explain **two** causes of the rise. **[2]**
(c) Describe **two** impacts of continued sea-level rise on the coastal population. **[2]**

**8.** Explain how climate change can lead to food shortages. **[3]**

**9.** Describe **three** strategies a city government could use to reduce carbon dioxide emissions from
transport and buildings. **[3]**

**10.** Explain how using artificial intelligence and climate models can help manage climate change.
**[2]**

**11.** An industrial valley has coal-fired factories and heavy traffic. Forests on the hills above are
losing their leaves and fish have disappeared from a lake.
(a) Explain how the factories and traffic produce acid rain. **[4]**
(b) Explain why fish have disappeared from the lake. **[2]**
(c) Describe how flue-gas desulfurisation works. **[2]**

**12.** Describe **three** impacts of ozone depletion. **[3]**

## Section C

**13.** Discuss the benefits and limitations of adaptation strategies for a low-lying coastal country. **[6]**

**14.** Discuss the benefits and limitations of reducing livestock farming and changing diets as ways to
limit climate change. **[6]**

**15.** Discuss the benefits and limitations of the strategies used to manage ozone depletion. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Stratosphere. **[1]**

**2.** Nitrogen and oxygen. **[1]**

**3.** Methane. **[1]**

**4.** Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) **[1]**; aerosols or refrigerants **[1]**.

**5.** Some solar radiation reaching the Earth would otherwise escape back to space **[1]**; greenhouse gases
and clouds absorb radiation and re-emit it back to the surface **[1]**, keeping the Earth warm enough to
support life **[1]**.

**6.** (a) Digestion by cattle **[1]**; decomposition of manure **[1]**; flooded rice fields **[1]**.
(b) Any two: reduce livestock numbers **[1]**; encourage plant-based diets **[1]**; reforestation of unused
land **[1]**; increase energy efficiency of farm machinery **[1]**; buy locally produced, seasonal food
**[1]**. **[max 2]**

**7.** (a) 100 mm / 30 years = **3.3 mm per year**. **[2]**
(b) Melting ice sheets and glaciers add water to the oceans **[1]**; warming of the ocean surface (thermal
expansion is accepted) **[1]**.
(c) Any two: flooding and loss of land **[1]**; damage to homes and infrastructure **[1]**; contamination of
fresh water and farmland with salt **[1]**; forced migration **[1]**. **[max 2]**

**8.** Higher temperatures and more droughts or floods **[1]** reduce crop yields and kill livestock **[1]**;
increased pests and invasive species damage crops, so less food is available **[1]**.

**9.** Any three: transport policies such as public transport, cycle lanes or congestion charges **[1]**;
increasing energy efficiency of buildings with insulation **[1]**; switching from fossil fuels to
renewable electricity **[1]**; taxation of high-emission vehicles **[1]**. **[max 3]**

**10.** They predict future impacts such as flooding or crop loss **[1]**, so governments can plan and test
the most effective strategies before investing **[1]**.

**11.** (a) Coal contains sulfur compounds, so burning it releases sulfur dioxide **[1]**; in hot vehicle
engines nitrogen reacts with oxygen to form oxides of nitrogen **[1]**; these gases react with oxygen
**[1]** and water vapour in the atmosphere to form acid rain **[1]**.
(b) Acid rain acidifies the lake **[1]**, so fish cannot survive and the aquatic food web is disrupted
**[1]**.
(c) Waste gases pass through calcium carbonate (limestone) or calcium oxide **[1]**, which reacts with sulfur
dioxide and removes it before the gases leave the chimney **[1]**.

**12.** Higher levels of solar radiation reach the surface **[1]**; increased rates of skin cancer and
cataracts **[1]**; damage to vegetation and reduced crop yields **[1]**.

**13.** **[6]** Strategies: improved flood defences; laws on building near coasts and floodplains; building
design and materials; crops that resist wetter or saltier conditions. Benefits: protect people and
property now; keep farming viable. Limitations: sea walls are very expensive for a low-income country;
laws may force people to move; adaptation does not stop sea-level rise, so defences may be overtopped
later. Conclusion: adaptation is essential but must be combined with global emission cuts.

**14.** **[6]** Benefits: less methane from cattle and manure; less deforestation for pasture and feed;
plant-based, seasonal and local food also reduces transport emissions. Limitations: livestock provides
income and protein for many families; diets are cultural; individual change is slow and voluntary; global
demand for meat is rising. Conclusion: helpful but must be part of wider strategies such as reduced fossil
fuel use.

**15.** **[6]** Benefits: the Montreal Protocol (1987) gave a single global agreement; CFCs were banned and
replaced with alternatives; safe disposal of old fridges prevents release. Limitations: CFCs stay in the
atmosphere for decades, so recovery is slow; some alternatives were themselves greenhouse gases; illegal
production and poor disposal still occur. Conclusion: largely successful, showing what international
cooperation can achieve.

## Where marks are usually lost

- In 11(a), forgetting the final step: the gases react with oxygen and water vapour in the atmosphere.
- Giving one-word strategies without describing them.
- Not quoting figures with units in data questions.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between global warming and climate change?**
Global warming is the rise in average
temperature caused by the enhanced greenhouse effect. Climate change is the resulting long-term change in
weather patterns.

**Which acid rain strategies are on the 5014 syllabus?**
Flue-gas desulfurisation, catalytic converters and
transport policies.

**Do I need to explain thermal expansion?**
No. The syllabus lists warming of the ocean surface and melting
ice; thermal expansion is accepted but not required.
