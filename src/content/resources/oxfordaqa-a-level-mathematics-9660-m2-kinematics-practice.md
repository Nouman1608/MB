These questions cover section M2.2 (Kinematics) of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Mathematics (9660) specification, Version 5.2, in International A-level Unit M2. Distances are in metres and times in seconds; i is east and j is north. Give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures. Read the [kinematics study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-mathematics-9660-m2-kinematics/) first, or refresh the methods with the [kinematics revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-mathematics-9660-m2-kinematics-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** A particle has position vector r = (2t³ − t)i + (5 − t²)j. Find its velocity and its acceleration when t = 2. **[3]**

**2.** A particle has velocity v = 4e⁻ᵗ i + 3j. Find its speed when t = 0. **[2]**

**3.** A particle moves in a straight line with velocity v = 3 sin 2t m s⁻¹. Find its acceleration when t = π/6. **[2]**

**4.** A particle has acceleration a = 4i − 6tj. When t = 0 its velocity is 2i + j. Find its velocity at time t. **[3]**

**5.** A particle has velocity v = −2i + 2√3 j. Find the angle between its direction of motion and the vector i. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A particle starts from rest at O and moves along a straight line with velocity v = 10 − 10e^(−0.2t) m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Find its initial acceleration. **[2]**

**(b)** Show that its displacement from O is s = 10t + 50e^(−0.2t) − 50. **[3]**

**(c)** Find its displacement from O when t = 5. **[1]**

**7.** A particle starts at O and moves along a straight line with velocity v = 6 − 3e^(0.25t) m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Find the exact time at which the particle changes direction. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the total distance travelled in the first 6 seconds. **[5]**

**8.** A particle has position vector r = 4 sin 3t i + 4 cos 3t j.

**(a)** Show that the particle moves at constant speed, and find that speed. **[3]**

**(b)** Show that a = −9r. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the magnitude of the acceleration. **[1]**

**9.** A particle has position vector r = (t² − 6t)i + (t² − 2t)j, for t ≥ 0.

**(a)** Find the times at which the particle is moving parallel to i and parallel to j. **[2]**

**(b)** By finding when the velocity is perpendicular to the acceleration, find the least speed of the particle. **[3]**

**(c)** Describe the direction of motion at the time of least speed. **[1]**

**10.** A particle has velocity v = 2e^(0.5t) i + (3 − 2t) j m s⁻¹. When t = 0 its position vector is −4i + 2j.

**(a)** Find its position vector at time t. **[4]**

**(b)** Find the time at which the particle is due north of O, and its distance from O at that time. **[3]**

**11.** A particle has position vector r = 3t i + (t² − 1) j + (2t − t²) k.

**(a)** Find its speed when t = 2. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the least speed of the particle. **[4]**

**12.** Two particles A and B have position vectors r_A = (2 + t)i + (5 − t)j and r_B = (3t − 2)i + (t + 1)j.

**(a)** Find the vector from A to B in terms of t. **[2]**

**(b)** Show that the particles collide, and find the position vector of the point where they collide. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** v = (6t² − 1)i − 2tj and a = 12ti − 2j [1]. At t = 2: **v = 23i − 4j** [1] and **a = 24i − 2j** [1]. Total: 3

**2.** At t = 0, v = 4i + 3j [1], so the speed is √(16 + 9) = **5 m s⁻¹** [1]. Total: 2

**3.** a = dv/dt = 6 cos 2t [1]. At t = π/6, a = 6 cos(π/3) = **3 m s⁻²** [1]. Total: 2

**4.** v = ∫a dt = 4ti − 3t²j + c [1]. At t = 0, c = 2i + j [1]. **v = (4t + 2)i + (1 − 3t²)j** [1]. Total: 3

**5.** The velocity has a negative i component and a positive j component, so it points into the second quadrant [1]. tan α = 2√3/2 = √3 gives 60° from the negative i direction, so the angle with i is **120°** [1]. Total: 2

**6. (a)** a = dv/dt = 2e^(−0.2t) [1], so a = **2 m s⁻²** at t = 0 [1].

**(b)** s = ∫(10 − 10e^(−0.2t)) dt = 10t + 50e^(−0.2t) + c [1]. At t = 0, s = 0, so 0 = 50 + c [1] and c = −50, giving **s = 10t + 50e^(−0.2t) − 50** [1].

**(c)** s = 50 + 50e⁻¹ − 50 = **18.4 m** [1]. Total: 6

**7. (a)** v = 0 when 3e^(0.25t) = 6, so e^(0.25t) = 2 [1] and **t = 4 ln 2** (≈ 2.77 s) [1].

**(b)** s = ∫(6 − 3e^(0.25t)) dt = 6t − 12e^(0.25t) + c, and s(0) = 0 gives c = 12 [1].
s(4 ln 2) = 24 ln 2 − 24 + 12 = 24 ln 2 − 12 ≈ 4.636 m [1].
s(6) = 36 − 12e^1.5 + 12 ≈ −5.780 m [1].
Distance = 4.636 + (4.636 + 5.780) [1] = **15.1 m** [1]. Total: 7

**8. (a)** v = 12 cos 3t i − 12 sin 3t j [1]. |v|² = 144 cos²3t + 144 sin²3t = 144 [1], so the speed is constant at **12 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** a = −36 sin 3t i − 36 cos 3t j [1] = −9(4 sin 3t i + 4 cos 3t j) = **−9r** [1].

**(c)** |a| = 9|r| = 9 × 4 = **36 m s⁻²** [1]. Total: 6

**9. (a)** v = (2t − 6)i + (2t − 2)j. Parallel to i when 2t − 2 = 0: **t = 1**. Parallel to j when 2t − 6 = 0: **t = 3** [1] [1].

**(b)** a = 2i + 2j. v · a = 2(2t − 6) + 2(2t − 2) = 8t − 16 = 0, so t = 2 [1]. Then v = −2i + 2j [1], and the least speed is √8 = **2√2 ≈ 2.83 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(c)** v = −2i + 2j points north-west, at **135° to i** [1]. Total: 6

**10. (a)** r = ∫v dt = 4e^(0.5t) i + (3t − t²) j + c [1] [1]. At t = 0: 4i + c = −4i + 2j, so c = −8i + 2j [1].
**r = (4e^(0.5t) − 8)i + (3t − t² + 2)j** [1].

**(b)** Due north: 4e^(0.5t) − 8 = 0, so e^(0.5t) = 2 and **t = 2 ln 2 ≈ 1.39 s** [1]. The j component is 3(1.386) − 1.386² + 2 ≈ 4.24 [1], which is positive, so the particle is due north at a distance of **4.24 m** [1]. Total: 7

**11. (a)** v = 3i + 2tj + (2 − 2t)k [1]. At t = 2, v = 3i + 4j − 2k, so the speed is √29 = **5.39 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** |v|² = 9 + 4t² + (2 − 2t)² = 8t² − 8t + 13 [1]. d(|v|²)/dt = 16t − 8 = 0 gives t = 0.5 [1]. |v|² = 2 − 4 + 13 = 11 [1], so the least speed is √11 = **3.32 m s⁻¹** [1]. Total: 6

**12. (a)** r_B − r_A = (3t − 2 − 2 − t)i + (t + 1 − 5 + t)j [1] = **(2t − 4)i + (2t − 4)j** [1].

**(b)** Both components are zero when t = 2 [1], so the particles are at the same point at the same time and collide [1]. r_A(2) = **4i + 3j** [1]. Total: 5

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out the constant vector of integration, or assuming it is zero.
- Missing the factor from the chain rule, such as the 3 in the derivative of sin 3t.
- Integrating from 0 to 6 in one step in Question 7 and calling the result the distance.
- Giving the angle −60° in Question 5 without checking the quadrant.
- Claiming a collision when the two components are zero at different times.
- Finding the time of least speed but not the speed itself.
