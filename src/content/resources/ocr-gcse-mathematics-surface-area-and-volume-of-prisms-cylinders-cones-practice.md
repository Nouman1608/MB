## Questions

**1.** A cuboid measures 14 cm by 9 cm by 6.5 cm. Find (a) its volume **[1]** and (b) its total surface area **[2]**.

**2.** A prism has a right-angled triangle as its cross-section. The shorter sides of the triangle are 8 cm and 15 cm. The prism is 25 cm long. Find (a) the volume **[2]** and (b) the total surface area **[3]**.

**3.** A closed cylindrical tin has radius 3.5 cm and height 12 cm. Find (a) its volume in terms of π **[2]**, (b) the area of the paper label that wraps once round the curved surface, in terms of π **[1]**, and (c) the total surface area to 1 decimal place **[2]**.

**4.** A cylinder has radius 25 cm. It stands upright and 150 litres of water are poured in. Find the depth of the water to 1 decimal place. **[4]**

**5.** (a) A ball is a sphere of radius 4.5 cm. Find its volume and its surface area, in terms of π. **[3]**
(b) A solid hemisphere has volume 18π cm³. Find its radius. **[3]**

**6.** A cone has base radius 7.5 cm and perpendicular height 10 cm. Find (a) the slant height **[2]**, (b) the volume in terms of π **[2]** and (c) the total surface area in terms of π **[2]**.

**7.** A square-based pyramid has base side 18 cm and perpendicular height 12 cm. Find (a) its volume **[2]** and (b) its total surface area **[4]**.

**8.** A toy is a cone placed on a solid hemisphere. They have the same radius, 6 cm, and the cone has perpendicular height 8 cm. Find (a) the volume of the toy in terms of π **[3]** and (b) the surface area of the toy in terms of π **[3]**.

**9.** A solid metal sphere of radius 6 cm is melted and recast into small solid cylinders, each of radius 2 cm and height 9 cm. No metal is lost. How many cylinders can be made? **[4]**

**10.** A square-based pyramid has volume 192 cm³ and base side 6 cm. (a) Find its perpendicular height. **[2]** (b) A cuboid has the same square base and the same height. How many times larger is its volume than the pyramid's? **[2]**

**11.** A storage tank is a cylinder with a cone-shaped roof on top. The cylinder has radius 2.5 m and wall height 4 m. The cone has the same radius and perpendicular height 6 m. The floor is not painted.
**(a)** Find the volume of the whole tank in terms of π. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the slant height of the roof. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the area of wall and roof together, in terms of π. **[2]**
**(d)** One litre of paint covers 8 m². Two coats are applied. Tins hold 5 litres. How many tins are needed? **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 14 × 9 × 6.5 = 819 [1] **819 cm³** **[1]**
**(b)** Faces: 14 × 9 = 126, 14 × 6.5 = 91, 9 × 6.5 = 58.5 [1]. 2 × (126 + 91 + 58.5) = **551 cm²** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Writing the three face areas before doubling makes it easier to spot a slip in one product.

**2. (a)** Area of triangle = ½ × 8 × 15 = 60 [1]. Volume = 60 × 25 = **1500 cm³** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Hypotenuse = √(64 + 225) = √289 = 17 cm [1]. Rectangles: (8 + 15 + 17) × 25 = 1000 [1]. Total = 2 × 60 + 1000 = **1120 cm²** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) needs the hypotenuse; an answer that includes only two rectangles will lose most of the credit for the area.

**3. (a)** V = π × 3.5² × 12 [1] = **147π cm³** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Label = 2π × 3.5 × 12 = **84π cm²** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** Ends: 2 × π × 3.5² = 24.5π [1]. Total = 24.5π + 84π = 108.5π = **340.9 cm²** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) asks for the curved surface only, so adding the circles there is a common way to throw away a mark.

**4.** 150 litres = 150 000 cm³ [1]. 150 000 = π × 25² × h [1]. h = 150 000 ÷ (625π) = 240/π [1]. **h = 76.4 cm** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the conversion from litres to cm³ as its own line; a depth that is a factor of 1000 out is hard to credit without it.

**5. (a)** V = (4/3) × π × 4.5³ = (4/3) × 91.125π [1] = **121.5π cm³** [1]. Surface area = 4 × π × 4.5² = **81π cm²** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** (2/3)πr³ = 18π [1]. r³ = 18 × 3/2 = 27 [1]. **r = 3 cm** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* In a working-backwards question, cancel π from both sides first; it keeps the cube root simple.

**6. (a)** l = √(7.5² + 10²) = √(56.25 + 100) [1] = √156.25 = **12.5 cm** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** V = (1/3) × π × 7.5² × 10 [1] = **187.5π cm³** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Curved = π × 7.5 × 12.5 = 93.75π [1]. Base = 56.25π. Total = **150π cm²** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the perpendicular height in part (b) and the slant height in part (c); swapping them is a frequent and avoidable error.

**7. (a)** V = (1/3) × 18² × 12 [1] = **1296 cm³** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Half the base side = 9 cm [1]. Slant height = √(12² + 9²) = 15 cm [1]. One triangle = ½ × 18 × 15 = 135, so four = 540 [1]. Total = 324 + 540 = **864 cm²** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A sketch of the right-angled triangle inside the pyramid (height, half-side, slant) makes the slant height easy to find.

**8. (a)** Hemisphere = (2/3) × π × 6³ = 144π [1]. Cone = (1/3) × π × 6² × 8 = 96π [1]. Total = **240π cm³** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** Slant height = √(36 + 64) = 10 cm [1]. Curved hemisphere = 2π × 36 = 72π, curved cone = π × 6 × 10 = 60π [1]. Total = **132π cm²** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The circle where the two solids join is hidden, so including it in part (b) gives an answer that is too large.

**9.** Sphere = (4/3) × π × 6³ = 288π [1]. Cylinder = π × 2² × 9 = 36π [1]. 288π ÷ 36π [1] = **8 cylinders** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Dividing the volumes shows π cancelling, so no decimal is needed; this is the sort of step a paper without a calculator rewards.

**10. (a)** 192 = (1/3) × 6² × h [1]. 192 = 12h, so **h = 16 cm** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Cuboid = 6 × 6 × 16 = 576 cm³ [1]. 576 ÷ 192 = **3 times** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Explaining that the pyramid is one third of the matching cuboid is a good check on any pyramid volume.

**11. (a)** Cylinder = π × 2.5² × 4 = 25π [1]. Cone = (1/3) × π × 2.5² × 6 = 12.5π [1]. Total = **37.5π m³** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** l = √(2.5² + 6²) = √42.25 = **6.5 m** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** Wall = 2π × 2.5 × 4 = 20π; roof = π × 2.5 × 6.5 = 16.25π [1]. Total = **36.25π m²** (≈ 113.9 m²) [1] **[2]**
**(d)** Area to cover = 2 × 113.88 = 227.77 m² [1]. Paint = 227.77 ÷ 8 = 28.47 litres [1]. 28.47 ÷ 5 = 5.69, so **6 tins** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The last step is a rounding-up decision; writing "5.69, so 6 tins" shows why you did not give 5.7.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the slant height where the perpendicular height is needed, and the reverse.
- Forgetting the hypotenuse when finding the perimeter of a triangular cross-section.
- Including a circle that is not on the outside of a composite solid, or leaving out a base that is.
- Using the diameter in place of the radius.
- Not converting litres to cm³, or converting with the wrong power of ten.
- Rounding in the middle of a calculation and ending with a final digit that is out.
- Giving a decimal when the question asks for the answer in terms of π.
- Rounding down on a "how many tins" question when you need to round up.
