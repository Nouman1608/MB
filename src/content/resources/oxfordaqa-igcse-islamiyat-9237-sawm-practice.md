## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. In the 4-mark questions, one mark for each point identified and one for developing it, up to two marks for each of two points (question 5: up to four marks for a developed explanation). The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks on that pattern plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general, accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The 12-mark question is marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |

### Section A

**1.** Ramadhan, the ninth month [1].

**2.** Iftar [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: the ill; travellers; the elderly; menstruating or post-natal women; pregnant or breastfeeding women if fasting would cause harm; young children.

**4.** Any two, [1] each: eating or drinking on purpose; sexual relations during the day; deliberately vomiting; menstruation or post-natal bleeding beginning during the day; abandoning the intention.

**5.** Laylat ul-Qadr is the Night of Power or Decree [1], the night on which the Qur'an was first revealed [1]. Surah al-Qadr says it is better than a thousand months [1]. It falls in the last ten nights of Ramadhan, most likely on the odd nights, and Muslims spend it in prayer and seeking forgiveness [1].

**6.** For each of two benefits: benefit identified [1] and developed [1].

- **Empathy with the poor** [1]: feeling hunger helps Muslims understand those who go without, which encourages giving [1].
- **Generosity** [1]: more charity is given in Ramadhan, including Zakat-ul-Fitr before Eid [1].
- **Unity** [1]: the whole Ummah fasts and breaks the fast together, which creates a shared experience [1].
- **Family and community bonds** [1]: iftar is often shared with relatives and in mosques [1].

### Section B

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, the reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- **It is commanded by Allah** [1]: it is one of the Five Pillars and an act of obedience [1].
- **It builds taqwa** [1]: giving up lawful things for Allah's sake develops awareness of Him [1].
- **It develops character** [1]: self-control, patience and honesty are trained [1].
- **It strengthens the community** [1]: Muslims fast together and care for the poor [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Decreed upon you is fasting ... that you may become righteous" (Qur'an 2:183) [2].
- The Hadith that whoever fasts Ramadhan with faith and hope of reward has past sins forgiven (Bukhari and Muslim) [2].

**8.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Ways (AO1):

- **Itikaaf** [1]: staying in the mosque for worship, leaving only for needs [1].
- **Seeking Laylat ul-Qadr** [1]: praying on the odd nights, because the exact night is unknown [1].
- **Reciting the Qur'an** [1]: the Qur'an was revealed in Ramadhan, so many aim to complete it [1].
- **Giving charity** [1]: acts of kindness are especially encouraged, including Zakat-ul-Fitr before Eid [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "The Night of Decree is better than a thousand months" (Qur'an 97:3) [2].
- The Prophet's (pbuh) advice to seek the night in the odd nights of the last ten (Sahih al-Bukhari) [2].

**9.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- The passage gives the purpose of fasting: "that you may become righteous", meaning taqwa.
- Self-control, patience and honesty are trained over a month.
- Empathy and generosity improve a person's behaviour towards others.
- The fast is linked with the Qur'an, which is "a guidance for the people".
- **Against the statement:** fasting does not automatically make a person better; the Prophet (pbuh) warned that a person who does not give up false speech gains nothing from hunger; the effect depends on sincerity and continued effort after Ramadhan; the passage also shows that fasting is an act of obedience to Allah whether or not the person feels improved.
- **Judgement:** fasting is intended to improve a person, and the passage says so, but the result depends on intention, effort and what follows the month.

*Use of the passage*

- "that you may become righteous" is the direct support.
- "Allah intends for you ease and does not intend for you hardship" shows that the aim is growth, not harm.
- "in which was revealed the Qur'an, a guidance for the people" links the fast with guidance.
- "ill or on a journey ... an equal number of days" shows compassion.
- "perhaps you will be grateful" shows that gratitude is a result.
