Condensed recall for **1.6 Urban Settlements** (Cambridge IGCSE Geography 0460, Theme 1). For the
full explanation, read the
[Population and Settlement study guide](/resources/igcse-geography-population-and-settlement/).

## Land-use zones, centre to edge

| Zone | Typical features |
|---|---|
| **CBD** | Shops, offices, highest land values, tallest buildings, best accessibility |
| **Inner city** | Older terraced housing, former industry, often run-down |
| **Suburbs** | Newer, lower-density housing, gardens, more space |
| **Rural-urban fringe** | Where the city meets the countryside; large new developments, open land |

**Why are land values highest in the CBD?** It is the most accessible point, with the best transport
links, so demand for space is greatest and the space is the most expensive. High prices push
buildings upwards.

Real cities only roughly follow this pattern: relief, rivers, transport routes and planning
distort it.

## Problems by zone

- **Inner city:** old and poor-quality housing, derelict land, unemployment, crime, traffic
  congestion, poor environment.
- **Rural-urban fringe:** loss of farmland and green space through **urban sprawl**, more commuting
  and traffic. Cheap, spacious sites attract developers.
- **Rapid growth in developing countries:** shortage of housing, congestion, pollution and strain
  on water and sanitation.

## Brownfield and greenfield

| | Brownfield | Greenfield |
|---|---|---|
| Land | Previously built on, now reused | Not built on before, often at the edge |
| Advantage | Saves countryside; services already nearby | Cheap and spacious |
| Disadvantage | Possible contamination, higher cost | Loses farmland and green space |

## Squatter settlements

A **squatter settlement** is housing built illegally on land the residents do not own, usually from
poor materials and often without water, sewage or electricity. They grow where migrants arrive
faster than formal housing is built.

**Responses:** self-help schemes (residents improve their own homes with support),
site-and-service schemes (the authority provides plots with basic services and residents build),
improving services and transport.

## Exam traps

- Placing the zones in the wrong order, or calling the inner city the newest part of town.
- Giving "expensive" as the reason for high land values without saying why (accessibility, demand).
- Mixing up brownfield and greenfield.
- Describing squatter settlements without a response, or without saying what a response cannot fix.
- Forgetting that real cities only roughly follow the zone pattern.

## Self-test

1. Name the four zones from the centre outwards.
2. Why are land values highest in the CBD?
3. Give two problems of the inner city.
4. Define a brownfield site.
5. Name one response to squatter settlement growth.

**Answers:** 1. CBD, inner city, suburbs, rural-urban fringe. 2. It is the most accessible place, so
demand is highest. 3. For example old housing and unemployment. 4. Land that was built on before and
is reused. 5. For example a site-and-service scheme or a self-help scheme.
