# Global contexts: one-page recall

Examples are written in English. Do any task that asks you to produce language in the language your school course uses, and ask your school teacher to check it. MYP has no prescribed content list: schools design their own units, so ask your teacher which concepts and contexts your units use.

Companion to the reviewed [global contexts revision notes](/resources/ib-myp-language-acquisition-global-contexts-and-topics-revision-notes/). Course hub: [/boards/ib/ib-myp/myp-language-acquisition/](/boards/ib/ib-myp/myp-language-acquisition/).

## The six (IB brief)

1. Identities and relationships
2. Orientation in space and time
3. Personal and cultural expression
4. Scientific and technical innovation
5. Globalization and sustainability
6. Fairness and development

## Rules of thumb

- A context is a lens, not a topic.
- One topic can sit under several contexts.
- Criterion C: personal, local and global interest and significance.
- The brief does not print a topic list; ask your teacher.

## Match the topic to a lens (Marlbridge examples)

| Topic | Likely lens |
|---|---|
| Moving to a new city | Orientation in space and time |
| Street art | Personal and cultural expression |
| Fast fashion | Globalization and sustainability |
| School rules | Fairness and development |
| Social media | Scientific and technical innovation |
| Family roles | Identities and relationships |

## Three scales

Personal: my family. Local: my town. Global: the wider world. Give one sentence at each scale in a speaking task.

## Self-test

1. Name the context for "access to education". 2. Which criterion mentions personal, local and global interest? 3. Can one topic fit two contexts?

**Answers:** 1. Fairness and development. 2. Criterion C (Speaking). 3. Yes.
