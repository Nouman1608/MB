These notes summarise section 4.7, Circle theorems I, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Full explanations are in the [circle theorems I study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-circle-theorems-i/), and exam-style questions are in the [circle theorems I practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-circle-theorems-i-practice/).

## What is examined

Calculate unknown angles and give explanations using:

1. the angle in a semicircle is 90°,
2. the angle between a tangent and a radius is 90°,
3. the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference,
4. angles in the same segment are equal,
5. opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180° (supplementary),
6. the alternate segment theorem.

You are expected to use the properties in your reasons.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Circumference | the curve of the circle |
| Chord | a line joining two points on the circumference |
| Diameter | a chord through the centre |
| Tangent | a line that touches the circle at one point |
| Arc | a part of the circumference |
| Segment | the region between a chord and an arc |
| Cyclic quadrilateral | a quadrilateral with all four vertices on a circle |
| Subtend | to stand on: an arc subtends an angle at a point |
| Alternate segment | the segment on the opposite side of the chord from the angle |

## The six theorems in one table

| Theorem | What to write as the reason |
|---|---|
| Diameter and a point C on the circle: angle ACB = 90° | angle in a semicircle is 90° |
| Tangent meets radius at 90° | angle between a tangent and a radius is 90° |
| Angle at centre = 2 × angle at circumference | angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference |
| Angles from the same arc, same side | angles in the same segment are equal |
| Opposite angles of ABCD on a circle add to 180° | opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180° |
| Tangent-chord angle = angle in the opposite segment | angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment |

## Extra facts used with the theorems

- Radii of a circle are equal, so a triangle with two radii as sides is isosceles.
- Angles in a triangle sum to 180°.
- Angles at a point sum to 360°.
- Angles on a straight line sum to 180°.
- The exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral equals the interior opposite angle.

## Method box: finding angles in a circle diagram

1. Look for a diameter (angle in a semicircle) and for tangents (right angles with radii).
2. Draw in radii if a centre is shown. They create isosceles triangles.
3. Match angles that stand on the same arc: same side means equal, centre means double.
4. Look for four points on a circle forming a quadrilateral: opposite angles supplementary.
5. Write each angle with its reason before moving on.
6. Check the angle sum of each triangle and quadrilateral at the end.

## Worked patterns

```
Triangle in a semicircle, angle at A = 36°:  angle at C = 90°, angle at B = 54°
Tangent from P, angle at P = 28°:            angle at centre = 62°
Angle at circumference 62°:                  angle at centre = 124°
Cyclic quadrilateral angles 95°, 78°:        opposite angles 85°, 102°
Tangent-chord angle 64°:                     angle in alternate segment = 64°
```

## Reflex angles at the centre

If the angle at the circumference is obtuse, the angle at the centre on the same arc is greater than 180° (reflex). For example, an angle of 130° at the circumference means 260° at the centre, so the angle at the centre on the other side is 360° − 260° = 100°.

## Must-know distinctions

| Idea | Be clear that |
|---|---|
| Centre vs circumference | the centre angle is the larger, double |
| Same segment | same side of the chord, so equal |
| Opposite segments | opposite sides of the chord, so supplementary angles in a cyclic quadrilateral |
| Alternate segment | opposite side from the tangent-chord angle |

## Exam traps

- Reasons that name the shape instead of the fact, such as "circle theorem".
- Using double or half the wrong way round.
- Adding adjacent angles of a cyclic quadrilateral to 180°.
- Picking the wrong segment for the alternate segment theorem.
- Forgetting the radius in the isosceles triangle.
- Using angles standing on different arcs.

## Self-test

1. AB is a diameter and C is on the circle. Angle CAB = 40°. Find angle ABC.
2. A tangent touches a circle, centre O, at T. P is outside the circle and angle TPO = 35°. Find angle TOP.
3. Angle ACB = 41° at the circumference. Find the angle AOB at the centre on the same arc.
4. Angle AOB = 150° at the centre. Find the angle at the circumference on the major arc.
5. Two angles in the same segment: one is 54°. What is the other?
6. ABCD is cyclic with angle ABC = 97°. Find angle ADC.
7. The tangent-chord angle is 71°. State the angle in the alternate segment.
8. O is the centre and angle ACB = 110° with C on the minor arc. Find the obtuse angle AOB.
9. In a cyclic quadrilateral, opposite angles are (3x + 20)° and (2x + 10)°. Find x.
10. The exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral is 75°. Find the interior opposite angle.

### Answers

1. 180 − 90 − 40 = **50°**.
2. 180 − 90 − 35 = **55°**.
3. 2 × 41 = **82°**.
4. 150 ÷ 2 = **75°**.
5. **54°** (angles in the same segment are equal).
6. 180 − 97 = **83°**.
7. **71°**.
8. Reflex angle = 220°, so angle AOB = 360 − 220 = **140°**.
9. 5x + 30 = 180, so **x = 30** (angles 110° and 70°).
10. **75°**.
