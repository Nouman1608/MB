**Below are two Paper 2 style questions on Section 7 Transport and telecommunications, each worth
25 marks in parts (a) to (d).** Question 1 focuses on internal and international transport;
Question 2 on dry ports, airports and telecommunications. Keep a blank outline map of Pakistan
beside you for the map parts.

## Question 1: Routes across and beyond Pakistan

**Fig. 1.1** (described): a map of Pakistan showing the railway network. Lines are close together
in central and northern Punjab, one line runs along each side of the Indus through Sindh, a single
line runs from Sibi through the Bolan Pass to Quetta and on to Taftan, and there are no lines in
Gilgit-Baltistan, Makran or the Thar desert.

**(a) (i)** Using Fig. 1.1, describe the pattern of the railway network in Sindh. **[2]**

**(ii)** Using Fig. 1.1, name the country reached by the railway west of Quetta. **[1]**

**(iii)** Name the pass used by the road from Pakistan to China. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the railway network is sparse in Balochistan. **[4]**

**(c) (i)** Compare road and rail for carrying cotton bales from Multan to Karachi. **[4]**

**(ii)** On an outline map, mark and label Keamari, Port Qasim and Gwadar. **[3]**

**(d)** The government plans to upgrade the main Karachi–Peshawar railway line. Evaluate the
advantages and disadvantages of this scheme for Pakistan's development. **[10]**

## Question 2: Dry ports, airports and telecommunications

**(a) (i)** Using Fig. 2.1, calculate the increase in broadband users from Year 1 to Year 7.

**(ii)** Using Fig. 2.1, compare the growth of mobile subscriptions with that of broadband users.
**[2]**

**(b) (i)** What is meant by a *dry port*? **[2]**

**(ii)** Explain why dry ports have been developed in cities such as Sialkot and Faisalabad. **[3]**

## Mark scheme: Question 1

**(a) (i)** Linear [1], running north–south along both banks of the Indus, with few east–west
links [1].

**(ii)** Iran [1].

**(iii)** Khunjerab Pass [1].

**(b)** Any four: mountainous and plateau terrain makes building costly [1]; long distances
between scattered settlements [1]; low population density means little traffic and revenue [1];
deserts with shifting sand and extreme heat [1]; flash floods wash out track [1]; security
problems have deterred investment [1]; few industries or bulk resources to carry, apart from
minerals [1].

**(c) (i)** Comparison required. Up to 4: rail is cheaper per tonne over this long distance,
whereas road costs more in fuel [1]; road can collect from the ginning factory and deliver to the
mill or port door-to-door, whereas rail needs loading at stations [1]; rail is less flexible and
can be slower because of delays, whereas trucks on the M-5 and M-9 are quicker [1]; trucks add to
congestion and road damage, whereas rail reduces these [1].

**(ii)** One mark each for correct location: Keamari at Karachi's harbour [1]; Port Qasim east of
Karachi [1]; Gwadar on the Makran coast near the Iranian border [1].

**(d)** Levels: Level 3 (8–10) balanced and developed with a judgement; Level 2 (4–7); Level 1
(1–3).

**Advantages:** faster, safer passenger trains; more freight moved by rail from Karachi's ports to
Punjab and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, cutting road congestion and fuel imports; lower transport costs for
exporters; construction jobs; links to dry ports and to CPEC. **Disadvantages:** very high cost and
foreign loans add to debt; years of disruption while track is rebuilt; Pakistan Railways' losses
may continue if management does not improve; land acquisition and resettlement along the route;
benefits concentrated on the Indus corridor, not Balochistan or the north. A Level 3 judgement
weighs long-term benefits against cost and risk.

## Mark scheme: Question 2

**(a) (i)** 95 million [1].

**(ii)** Mobile rose by 50 million, about a third more, whereas broadband more than tripled [1];
broadband grew faster in proportion, though mobile numbers remain higher [1].

**(b) (i)** An inland terminal linked to a seaport by rail or road [1] where goods are cleared
through customs and loaded into or out of sealed containers [1].

**(ii)** Any three: Sialkot and Faisalabad are major exporting cities far from the coast [1];
clearing customs locally saves time and cost [1]; sealed containers reduce theft and damage [1];
reduces congestion at Karachi's seaports [1]; allows cheaper rail haulage of containers [1].

**(c) (i)** Roads such as the Karakoram Highway are long, winding and often blocked by landslides
or snow [1]; a flight takes about an hour instead of a long road journey [1]; air carries
tourists, officials and urgent supplies, and evacuates the sick [1].

**(ii)** Any four: steep terrain makes building masts and laying cable costly [1]; valleys block
signals, so many masts are needed [1]; scattered, small communities give low revenue [1]; snow,
landslides and earthquakes damage equipment [1]; unreliable power supply for masts [1]; difficult
access for maintenance in winter [1].

**(d)** Levels: Level 3 (8–10); Level 2 (4–7); Level 1 (1–3).

**For telecommunications:** cheap to spread compared with roads; online education (Virtual
University, open-university broadcasts, TV lessons in 2020); mobile banking and e-commerce reach
people without bank branches; IT exports and freelancing create jobs; farmers get prices and
weather warnings; exporters deal directly with foreign buyers. **For motorways:** goods still need
physical transport; motorways such as the M-2 and M-5 cut journey times, lower costs and support
industry and trade; they attract industrial estates and service areas; telecoms cannot move cotton
or cement. **Limits on both:** rural and female digital gaps; motorway tolls and costs. A Level 3
answer reaches a judgement, for example that each does different work and they reinforce each
other.

## Frequently asked questions

**How precise must map answers be?**
Place each feature in the right position relative to the coast, cities and borders. Label clearly;
an unlabelled dot scores nothing.

**What is the difference between "describe the pattern" and "describe the distribution"?**
Pattern refers to shapes such as linear, grid or radial; distribution refers to where features are
relative to provinces, rivers and other features and how dense they are.

**How should I use the data in Fig. 2.1 in part (d)?**
Quote one or two figures to show how fast telecommunications use has grown, then move on to
evaluation; part (d) rewards judgement more than recall.
