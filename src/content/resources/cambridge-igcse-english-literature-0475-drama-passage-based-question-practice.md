**How to use this set.** Paper 2 offers "either (a) a passage-based question or (b) an essay
question" on each text, and you must answer one of each. This set practises the (a) question on two
original scenes. Read the context note before each scene: in the exam, you supply that context from
your knowledge of the play.

**Scene 1: from *The Inheritance*** *(original scene)*. *Context: the audience has just seen
Clara, the younger sister, hide their late mother's ring in her pocket. Her elder brother Victor has
been searching for it all day.*

> *The sitting room after the funeral. Cups on every surface. VICTOR stands by the window.
> CLARA sits very straight on the sofa.*
>
> **VICTOR:** Mother always said the ring would go to whoever loved her most.
>
> **CLARA:** She said a lot of things.
>
> **VICTOR:** She meant that one. *(He turns.)* You were with her at the end.
>
> **CLARA:** Someone had to be.
>
> **VICTOR:** And she said nothing? About the ring?
>
> **CLARA:** *(her hand moves towards her pocket, then stops)* Nothing.
>
> *The doorbell rings. Neither moves. It rings again.*
>
> **VICTOR:** That'll be the solicitor. *(He crosses to the door, then pauses beside her.)* He'll
> want everything on the table, Clara. Everything.
>
> *He exits. CLARA takes out the ring, looks at it, and closes her fist around it.*

**Scene 2: from *Market Day*** *(original scene)*. *Context: a comedy. Mr Patel has boasted all
week that his prize marrow will win the village show. The audience knows his neighbour's goat has
eaten it overnight.*

> *The show tent. MR PATEL enters carrying an empty basket covered with a cloth, beaming.*
>
> **MR PATEL:** Ladies and gentlemen, prepare yourselves.
>
> **JUDGE:** We are prepared, Mr Patel. We have been prepared since Tuesday.
>
> **MR PATEL:** Sixty days of care. Rainwater only. I sang to it.
>
> **JUDGE:** You sang to it.
>
> **MR PATEL:** Every evening. *(He grips the cloth.)* Behold!
>
> *He whips away the cloth. The basket is empty except for one chewed stalk. Silence. A goat bleats
> outside.*

## Section A

**1.** In Scene 1, identify **two** stage directions and explain what each shows the audience. **[4]**

**2.** Explain how dramatic irony works in Scene 1, using one line of Victor's dialogue. **[3]**

**3.** In Scene 1, what is the effect of the doorbell ringing twice while "Neither moves"? **[2]**

**4.** In Scene 2, explain how the Judge's repetition "You sang to it." creates humour. **[2]**

**5.** Rewrite this feature-spotting sentence as analysis: "In Scene 2 there is a stage direction
saying 'Silence.'" **[3]**

**6.** Explain why it is better to write "the audience" than "the reader" in an answer on a play,
using Scene 2 as your example. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Read Scene 1.
**(a)** How does the playwright make this such a tense moment? **[8]**
**(b)** Explore the effect of the scene's final stage direction. **[3]**

**8.** Write the introduction to an answer on: "How does the playwright make this moment in Scene 2
so comic?" Include a sentence placing the scene. **[4]**

**9.** Read Scene 2. Explore how the playwright builds up to the moment when the cloth is removed.
**[6]**

**10.** Divide Scene 1 into **three** stages and give each a heading that describes how the tension
changes. **[3]**

**11.** A candidate's answer on Scene 1 spends its first page describing Victor's behaviour across the
whole play. Explain **two** problems with this approach and how to fix them. **[4]**

**12.** Victor says: "He'll want everything on the table, Clara. Everything." Give **two**
interpretations of this line and explain how an actor's delivery could support one of them. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two, [2] each (one for identification, one for what it shows):
- "CLARA sits very straight" [1]: her rigid posture suggests tension and guilt held under control [1].
- "(He turns.)" [1]: Victor faces her as he mentions the end of their mother's life, showing that his
  remark is pointed [1].
- "(her hand moves towards her pocket, then stops)" [1]: the audience sees her almost betray herself
  [1].
- "He exits. CLARA takes out the ring…" [1]: alone, she reveals the truth to the audience only [1].
Maximum 4.

**2.** The audience knows Clara has the ring, but Victor appears not to [1]. When he says "the ring
would go to whoever loved her most", the audience hears the line as a challenge Clara cannot answer
honestly [1], so his words create tension because we watch her lie while knowing the truth [1].

**3.** The repeated ring interrupts the confrontation but neither character can break away [1], so the
moment is prolonged and the audience is held in suspense about whether Clara will confess [1].

**4.** The flat repetition shows the Judge's disbelief and boredom [1], and it deflates Mr Patel's
proud claim, so the audience laughs at the contrast between his enthusiasm and her dry response [1].

**5.** A point about effect [1], the stage direction quoted [1], the effect on the audience explained
[1]. For example: "After the cloth is whipped away, 'Silence.' holds the audience on the empty basket;
the pause lets the joke land fully, and the goat's bleat that follows breaks it with the answer we
already knew."

**6.** A play is written to be performed, so its effects depend on what is seen and heard [1]; the
humour of Scene 2 depends on the visual reveal of the empty basket and the sound of the goat, which an
audience experiences in the theatre rather than reads [1].

**7. (a)** Any four developed points, [2] each:
- The setting "after the funeral" with "Cups on every surface" creates a strained, crowded
  atmosphere after grief [2].
- Dramatic irony: the audience knows about the ring, so Victor's lines about it press on Clara's
  secret [2].
- Short, evasive replies ("She said a lot of things." / "Someone had to be.") show Clara avoiding the
  question and keep the conflict beneath the surface [2].
- Victor's direct question "About the ring?" brings the conflict into the open [2].
- The near-gesture towards her pocket is the moment of greatest risk, shown only through stage
  directions [2].
- The repeated doorbell and stillness prolong the tension [2].
- "Everything." as a one-word sentence works as a warning, suggesting Victor may suspect her [2].
Maximum 8.
**(b)** Any developed point, up to [3]: Clara is alone, so the audience is now the only witness [1];
taking out the ring confirms what the audience knew [1]; closing her fist suggests she has decided to
keep it, so the scene ends on a hardened choice rather than a confession [1].

**8.** A direct answer to the question [1]; a sentence placing the scene using the context [1]; a focus
on "comic" [1]; a sense of the argument to follow [1]. For example: "This moment comes after Mr Patel
has spent the week boasting about a marrow that the audience knows the goat has eaten. Its comedy
comes from dramatic irony: every proud line he speaks brings him closer to a humiliation we can see
coming. I will show how the Judge's dry replies, his growing excitement and the final silent reveal
make the scene funny."

**9.** Any three developed points, [2] each:
- His entrance "beaming" with a covered basket raises expectation, while the audience knows it is
  empty [2].
- His grand opening "prepare yourselves" is undercut by the Judge's weary reply [2].
- The list "Sixty days of care. Rainwater only. I sang to it." builds his pride in stages, so the
  coming fall is greater [2].
- "(He grips the cloth.)" and "Behold!" slow the action before the reveal, heightening suspense and
  comedy [2].
Maximum 6.

**10.** One mark for each sensible staged heading [3], for example: 1. Veiled challenge (the ring and
"whoever loved her most"); 2. Near-exposure (the direct question and Clara's hand); 3. Interrupted
then sealed (doorbell, warning, Clara alone closing her fist).

**11.** Two problems, [2] each: the answer ignores the printed extract, which the question is about
[1]; fix it by placing the scene in two sentences and then analysing the extract's language and stage
directions [1]. The answer delays any analysis of method [1]; fix it by starting with a direct answer
to the question and moving straight to quotation and effect [1].

**12.** Two interpretations, [1] each: a practical remark about the will and the solicitor's
procedure; a veiled warning that he suspects Clara of hiding the ring. Delivery, up to [2]: a pause
before "Everything" [1] and holding Clara's gaze [1] would make the warning reading clear to the
audience. Maximum 4.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Discussing dialogue but ignoring stage directions.
- Explaining the plot rather than how the playwright creates the effect.
- Writing about "the reader".
- Missing the final stage direction, where both scenes turn.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why do the scenes come with context notes?**
In the exam you supply the context from your knowledge of the play; the notes stand in for that
knowledge here.

**How much of my answer should be about the rest of the play?**
Enough to explain why the moment matters, usually a few sentences; the extract is the focus.
