This guide covers sub-topic **16.1 Asexual reproduction** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Outcomes 1 and 2 are Core. Outcome 3, discussing the advantages and disadvantages of asexual reproduction, is Supplement content and is marked **Extended only**.

## What the syllabus asks for

| Outcome | What you must be able to do | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| 16.1.1 | Describe asexual reproduction as a process resulting in the production of genetically identical offspring from one parent | Core |
| 16.1.2 | Identify examples of asexual reproduction in diagrams, images and information provided | Core |
| 16.1.3 | Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of asexual reproduction (a) to a population of a species in the wild and (b) to crop production | Extended only |

## The definition, word by word

> Asexual reproduction is a process resulting in the production of **genetically identical offspring** from **one parent**.

Two ideas earn the marks, and both are needed:

- **One parent.** No second individual is involved, so there are no gametes and no fertilisation.
- **Genetically identical offspring.** The offspring have the same genes as the parent and as each other. Offspring that are genetically identical to each other are often called clones.

A definition that says only "reproduction without a partner" misses the genetic point. One that says "offspring look the same" is weaker than "genetically identical", because appearance can also be changed by the environment.

Extended candidates link this to topic 17: the cells that form the new individual are produced by **mitosis**, which gives genetically identical cells. The syllabus lists asexual reproduction as one of the roles of mitosis.

## Recognising asexual reproduction (Core)

Outcome 16.1.2 means you will often meet an organism you have never studied. You are not expected to know its name in advance. Instead, look for these clues in the diagram or text.

| Clue | Points to asexual reproduction | Points to sexual reproduction |
|---|---|---|
| Number of parents | one | usually two (or gametes from one) |
| Structures | part of the parent grows into a new individual | flowers, seeds, sperm, eggs, pollen |
| Events | splitting, budding, growing from a stem or leaf | pollination, fertilisation, a zygote |
| Offspring | genetically identical to the parent | genetically different from each other |

### Examples you may be shown

- **Bacteria** divide into two identical cells (binary fission).
- **Yeast** cells grow a small outgrowth, a bud, that separates as a new cell.
- **Potato tubers** are swollen underground stems; each can grow into a new plant.
- **Strawberry runners** are side stems that grow along the ground; plantlets form along them and root.
- **Bulbs**, such as onions and daffodils, produce new bulbs beside the old one.
- **Plantlets** form along the leaf edges of some plants, such as *Bryophyllum*, and drop off to root.
- **Fungi** such as bread mould produce spores asexually.
- **Cuttings** taken by gardeners are an artificial form of asexual reproduction: a piece of stem roots and grows into a new plant.

### Worked example 1: sorting unfamiliar examples

*For each of these, decide whether it is asexual reproduction.*

| Information given | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| A sea anemone tears in half and each half grows into a new anemone. | asexual | one parent; new individuals from parts of it |
| A tomato plant pollinates itself and sets seed. | sexual | seeds form after pollination and fertilisation, even with one parent plant |
| A gardener plants a piece of geranium stem, which roots. | asexual | one parent; no gametes |
| A *Hydra* grows a bud on its side that breaks off as a small new *Hydra*. | asexual | one parent; the bud is part of the parent's body |
| Identical human twins develop from one zygote that splits. | not asexual reproduction | the zygote came from fertilisation of an egg by a sperm |

The second and last rows are the common traps. Self-pollination still involves gametes and fertilisation, so it is sexual reproduction. Identical twins are genetically identical to each other, but they come from a zygote, so they are the result of sexual reproduction.

## Why asexual reproduction can be so fast

A single parent does not need to find a mate, and there is no pollination or fertilisation step. For organisms such as bacteria, one cell can become two in a short time.

### Worked example 2: a bacterial population

*In this example, a bacterium divides every 20 minutes. Starting with one cell, how many cells are there after 3 hours, if none die?*

```
3 hours = 180 minutes
Number of divisions = 180 ÷ 20 = 9
Number of cells = 2 × 2 × ... nine times = 2⁹ = 512
```

**Answer: 512 cells**, all genetically identical to the first. The calculation shows both sides of the argument: rapid population growth, and no variation among the offspring.

## Advantages and disadvantages (Extended only)

"Discuss" in the outcome means you must give both sides and relate them to the context in the question. The syllabus names two contexts, and examiners expect you to use the right one.

### (a) A population of a species in the wild

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Only one parent is needed, so an isolated individual can reproduce. | No genetic variation, so the population has little ability to adapt if the environment changes. |
| No energy is spent finding a mate or on pollination. | A disease that can kill one individual can kill them all, because all have the same genes. |
| Reproduction is quick, so a newly reached area can be colonised fast. | Offspring often grow close to the parent and compete with it for light, water or nutrients. |
| Offspring keep all the features of a parent that is well suited to a stable environment. | Harmful features are passed to every offspring. |

### (b) Crop production

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Every plant has the same desirable features, such as yield, flavour or disease resistance. | A single new disease or pest can destroy the whole crop, because no plant has different genes that might protect it. |
| Crops are uniform, so they ripen at the same time and can be harvested and sold together. | No new varieties arise; growers must use sexual reproduction to breed improved plants. |
| Plants can be produced quickly, without waiting for flowering, pollination and seeds. | Problems caused by the environment, such as a dry season, affect all plants equally. |
| Varieties that make few or no seeds can still be grown. | Large areas of one clone depend entirely on the one variety continuing to grow well. |

### Annotated model answer

*A farmer grows a field of potatoes from tubers taken from one high-yielding plant. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of this method.* **[4]**

> All the plants are genetically identical to the high-yielding parent, so the whole field should give a high yield [1]. It is quicker than growing from seed, and the crop is uniform, so it can be harvested at the same time [1]. However, there is no variation [1], so if a disease that can infect this variety arrives, all the plants may be affected and the crop may fail [1].

The answer stays in the crop context throughout. A list of advantages to wild populations, such as "no need to find a mate", would gain little here, because the farmer is not trying to find mates for potatoes.

## Common mistakes

- Defining asexual reproduction as "reproduction with one parent" without "genetically identical offspring".
- Calling self-pollination asexual. Seeds are always the product of sexual reproduction.
- Saying the offspring are "the same" instead of "genetically identical".
- Giving only advantages when the question says "discuss".
- Mixing up the contexts: using crop points such as uniform harvests for a wild population.
- Writing "asexual reproduction causes disease". Lack of variation makes a population **more vulnerable** to disease.

## Exam technique

- **Describe** asexual reproduction: give both parts of the definition.
- **Identify** an example: name it and give the clue from the material, such as "only one parent" or "plantlets grow from the leaf".
- **Discuss** (Extended): at least one advantage and one disadvantage, each with a reason, in the context named in the question.
- **Suggest** why a species uses asexual reproduction in a particular place: link speed or single-parent reproduction to the situation described.
