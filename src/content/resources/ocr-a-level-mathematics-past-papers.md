This page is a guide to OCR A Level Mathematics A (H240) past papers from June 2024 to June 2026. For each series you get the grade thresholds, copied from OCR's grade-boundary documents, and for June 2025 and June 2024 a short account of what the examiners saw, written by us from OCR's examiner reports. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on OCR's own site or its teacher platform.

- [OCR A Level Mathematics A assessment page, where examiner reports are listed](https://www.ocr.org.uk/qualifications/as-and-a-level/mathematics-a-h230-h240-from-2017/assessment/)
- [OCR past paper finder](https://www.ocr.org.uk/qualifications/past-paper-finder/)
- [OCR grade boundaries page](https://www.ocr.org.uk/administration/grade-boundaries/)

OCR publishes question papers and mark schemes through its past paper finder, and grade boundaries on the page above. Examiner reports for each series are listed on the assessment page for the qualification. The full question papers and mark schemes that the reports refer to are held on Teach Cambridge, OCR's teacher site, so ask your teacher or exams officer if you cannot open them.

Use the three documents together. Sit the paper first, under timed conditions. Mark it strictly with the mark scheme, which tells you what each mark needs. Then read the examiner report for that paper: it tells you why answers that looked right still lost marks. Our notes for each series below are a quick way into that report.

Check which specification a paper was set on. H240 is a linear qualification first assessed in 2018 and still current. All three papers are 2 hours and 100 marks, each worth a third of the A Level. Paper 1 (H240/01) is Pure Mathematics. Paper 2 (H240/02) is Pure Mathematics and Statistics, and Paper 3 (H240/03) is Pure Mathematics and Mechanics, each roughly half pure and half applied. Pure content can appear on any of the three papers, and Paper 2 includes questions on a pre-release data set. Candidates sit all three, so the total is 300 marks.

## June 2025

The June 2025 reports stress method and communication more than new content. The same message runs through all three papers: read the command word, show enough working for the mark that the command word asks for, and keep notation exact. The Paper 2 report says performance was clearly better than in 2023 and 2024, and the statistics section was a little higher than in earlier years.

**Where marks were lost – H240/01 (Pure Mathematics)**

- Standard techniques: candidates who struggled on routine questions often tried more complicated methods on simple topics met at GCSE or AS, and some did not use the formulae given at the front of the paper.
- Command words: an answer that stated a result without the reasoning, or that did not show a given answer convincingly, lost method marks. Several parts linked to the part before, and candidates who did not connect them missed easy marks.
- Vectors: the convention for the direction of a vector was misunderstood, with bearings or negative angles given instead of an angle in the range 0 to 360 degrees from the positive x-axis, and some did not explicitly compare a magnitude with 1.
- Modelling: explanations of why a model would not fit a real person, or would stop fitting over time, were vague, and some candidates gave a standard learnt response instead of answering the situation in the question.
- Series and algebra: a geometric series sum to infinity was set up with the wrong first term, and conditions for validity of a binomial expansion were often missing or incomplete.
- Trigonometry and calculus: a harmonic form was handled well but the smallest solution in the range was often missed, a transformation of a graph needed matching to an exponential form, and some candidates did not work out the area asked for from an integral.
- Proof and differential equations: a proof by contradiction was started well but few candidates explained why a square that is a multiple of 5 means the number itself is a multiple of 5, and a separable differential equation was often spoiled by sign errors or keeping the constant after removing logarithms.

**Where marks were lost – H240/02 (Pure Mathematics and Statistics)**

- Working: answers that skipped steps or gave only a value lost marks where the question asked for detailed reasoning, including when solving a quadratic. Rounding too early and giving less than 3 significant figures also cost marks.
- Proof: argument in "show that" questions lacked rigour, with steps left out between the start and the given result.
- Pure content: numerical methods were found challenging, and only about a quarter of the candidates integrated by parts correctly in the last pure question.
- Statistics: probability questions were the most demanding, and a normal distribution calculation lost both marks when the inverse step was left out. Contextual reasons about assumptions for a binomial model needed to link to one of the required assumptions.
- Candidates tended to prefer either the pure or the statistics section and showed a gap in the other.

**Where marks were lost – H240/03 (Pure Mathematics and Mechanics)**

- Pure: careless algebra and loose notation cost accuracy marks even when the method was right. Completing the square lost a mark when brackets were missing, and the idea that the normal to a curve at its minimum is the line of symmetry was not recognised by many.
- Graphs and inequalities: a modulus graph was drawn with the cusp or the extent of the curve wrong, and negative terms in an inequality were mishandled. Where detailed reasoning was demanded, the solution of a quadratic inequality was not always shown properly.
- Trigonometry and radians: a proof of an identity lacked detail, calculators were left in degree mode in a radians question, and an area problem was hindered by not seeing a length expressed through a tangent.
- Mechanics: forces on a connected particle were incomplete (the weight forgotten) with sign errors in the acceleration; moments about a point were set up with a missing distance or the wrong angle; the contact force at a wall was treated as horizontal only. A friction problem with two blocks was very demanding, and only the strongest candidates made progress on the final part.
- Calculus in kinematics earned marks, but detailed reasoning needed the explicit substitution of a value for time into the integrated expression.

## June 2024

The June 2024 reports describe a paper where good candidates showed wide technique and presentation, while weaker scripts lost marks on algebra and on missing the demands of command words. The Paper 2 report says performance was slightly better than in 2023.

**Where marks were lost – H240/01 (Pure Mathematics)**

- Algebra: manipulation slips and difficulty with problems involving letters rather than numbers separated the grades. Some candidates chose unsuitable methods of differentiation or integration.
- Reasoning: statements needed to be justified with evidence, and solutions were sometimes discarded without a reason. Several steps were crowded into one line of working in "show that" questions.
- Functions and logarithms: a question on where a logarithmic function is defined was very challenging, and the reasoning that the stationary points were all maxima was often thin or left blank.
- Coordinate geometry: a point was found by assuming it was a midpoint when it was not. The last two questions were challenging, and a good number of blank responses on the final one suggested time pressure.
- Bright spots: a longer unstructured question was generally well attempted, and the link between parts of a polynomial question was often spotted.

**Where marks were lost – H240/02 (Pure Mathematics and Statistics)**

- Command words: key instructions were missed, generic facts were given in "explain" questions, and not enough working was shown on "determine", "show that" and detailed reasoning questions.
- Pure section: basic algebraic errors such as rearranging an equation, and misreading information given in a sequence question. Very few candidates scored on a follow-up part that depended only on a result already given.
- Interpreting given information: on a question about a graph with a parameter, the most common errors came with the later figures, where candidates gave no value or the wrong value.
- On a late multi-part question, many candidates chose sensible starting estimates but then could not set up the equation needed to continue.

**Where marks were lost – H240/03 (Pure Mathematics and Mechanics)**

- Pure: careless algebra and notation that was not concise. A binomial expansion was done well by most, but the range of validity was often wrong, with the most common wrong answers using the wrong bound.
- Mechanics: moments about a point to find a contact force were set up with geometry errors, and in one late mechanics part a common wrong answer appeared while the correct value was rarely seen, with many candidates using calculus where a simpler argument was available.
- Detailed reasoning, "show that" and "determine" appeared in many questions, and candidates who gave correct working that did not answer the question as set lost marks.

## What examiners keep saying

Across the two series with reports, the same points come up on all three papers.

- Respond to the command word. Detailed reasoning, determine and show that each ask for a full, convincing method. A calculator can find values, but the working must still be visible.
- Use correct notation and brackets, and write in a way that lets the examiner follow the line of argument.
- Link the parts of a question. Where a later part builds on an earlier one, use the earlier result.
- Answer the context. In modelling and mechanics questions, address what the question asks about the situation rather than quoting a learnt response.
- Keep to the accuracy required: three significant figures unless told otherwise, with no premature rounding.
- Revise the whole specification, including GCSE content such as mensuration in non-right-angled triangles.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find OCR A Level Mathematics A (H240) past papers?**
OCR publishes question papers and mark schemes through its past paper finder. Grade boundaries are on OCR's grade boundaries page, and examiner reports are listed on the assessment page for the qualification.

**Which H240 papers should I sit?**
All three. Paper 1 (Pure Mathematics), Paper 2 (Pure Mathematics and Statistics) and Paper 3 (Pure Mathematics and Mechanics) are each 100 marks and 2 hours.

**How are the grade thresholds used?**
The overall grade comes from adding the three paper marks, out of 300, and comparing the total with that series' boundaries. The paper rows show how each paper performed on its own.

**What mistakes do H240 examiners report most often?**
Not showing enough working when the command word asks for it, careless algebra, imprecise notation, and answering a general question when the context was asked about.

**Can grade thresholds tell me what mark I need next time?**
No. Thresholds change every series and should only be used as a rough guide for the series they belong to, never to predict future ones.
