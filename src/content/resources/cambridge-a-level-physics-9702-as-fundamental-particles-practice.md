## Section A

**1.** Name the six flavours of quark and state the charge of each, in units of e. **[3]**

**2.** Describe the quark composition of a proton and of a neutron, and show that each composition gives the correct charge. **[3]**

**3.** State what is meant by a hadron, and name the two types of hadron, giving the composition of each. **[3]**

**4.** State the charge of an anti-strange quark and of an anti-top quark. **[2]**

**5.** State the name given to the family of fundamental particles that includes the electron and the neutrino. **[1]**

**6.** Explain why the proton is not a fundamental particle. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Particle X has the quark composition d d s. Particle Y has the composition s ū.

**(a)** State whether each particle is a baryon or a meson. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the charge of X and of Y. **[2]**

**(c)** Give the quark composition and charge of the antiparticle of Y. **[2]**

**8.** In β⁻ decay a neutron in a nucleus changes into a proton.

**(a)** State the change in quark composition. **[1]**

**(b)** Write an equation for the change at quark level, including all particles emitted, and show that charge is conserved. **[3]**

**(c)** State which of the particles emitted are leptons or antiparticles of leptons. **[1]**

**9.** In β⁺ decay a proton in a nucleus changes into a neutron.

**(a)** State the change in quark composition. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the two other particles emitted, and state whether each is a particle or an antiparticle. **[2]**

**(c)** Show that charge is conserved at the quark level. **[2]**

**10.** A student claims to have identified three new particles: a meson of charge +2, a baryon of charge −2, and a particle made of two quarks with charge +1.

**(a)** Show that a meson cannot have a charge of +2. **[2]**

**(b)** Show that a baryon cannot have a charge of −2. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why two quarks without an antiquark cannot form a particle of charge +1. **[2]**

**11.** A meson has the composition u d̄.

**(a)** Give the composition and charge of its antiparticle. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why a meson made of an up quark and an anti-up quark has the same quark content as its own antiparticle. **[1]**

**12.** Classify each of the following as a quark, a lepton (or antiparticle of a lepton), a baryon (or antibaryon) or a meson: proton; electron antineutrino; strange quark; a particle of composition d s̄; electron; a particle of composition ū ū d̄. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Up (+⅔), charm (+⅔), top (+⅔) [1]; down (−⅓), strange (−⅓), bottom (−⅓) [1]; all six names given correctly [1].

**2.** Proton: uud [1], charge = +⅔ + ⅔ − ⅓ = +1. Neutron: udd [1], charge = +⅔ − ⅓ − ⅓ = 0 [1 for both charge sums shown].

**3.** A hadron is a particle made of quarks [1]. Baryon: three quarks [1]. Meson: one quark and one antiquark [1].

**4.** Anti-strange: +⅓ [1]. Anti-top: −⅔ [1].

**5.** Leptons [1].

**6.** It is made of smaller particles: three quarks (uud) [1].

**7. (a)** X: baryon (three quarks) [1]. Y: meson (quark and antiquark) [1].

**(b)** X: −⅓ − ⅓ − ⅓ = −1 [1]. Y: −⅓ − ⅔ = −1 [1].

**(c)** s̄ u [1]; charge +⅓ + ⅔ = +1 [1].

**8. (a)** A down quark changes into an up quark (udd → uud) [1].

**(b)** d → u + e⁻ + ν̄ [1 for products, 1 for the antineutrino]. Charges: −⅓ on the left; +⅔ + (−1) + 0 = −⅓ on the right, so charge is conserved [1].

**(c)** The electron is a lepton; the electron antineutrino is the antiparticle of a lepton [1].

**9. (a)** An up quark changes into a down quark (uud → udd) [1].

**(b)** A positron, which is an antiparticle (of the electron) [1]; an electron neutrino, which is a particle [1].

**(c)** u → d + e⁺ + ν [1]; +⅔ on the left; −⅓ + 1 + 0 = +⅔ on the right [1].

**10. (a)** A meson is a quark plus an antiquark [1]; the largest charge possible is a +⅔ quark with a +⅓ antiquark, giving +1, so +2 is impossible [1].

**(b)** A baryon is three quarks, each of charge +⅔ or −⅓ [1]; the most negative possible is three −⅓ quarks, giving −1, so −2 is impossible [1].

**(c)** Two quarks can give +⅔ + ⅔ = +4/3, +⅔ − ⅓ = +⅓ or −⅓ − ⅓ = −⅔ [1]; none of these is +1 (none is a whole number) [1].

**11. (a)** d ū [1]; charge −⅓ − ⅔ = −1 [1].

**(b)** Replacing each quark by its antiquark turns u ū into ū u, which is the same pair of quarks [1].

**12.** Proton: baryon; strange quark: quark [1]. Electron antineutrino: antiparticle of a lepton; electron: lepton [1]. d s̄: meson; ū ū d̄: antibaryon [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving the strange or bottom quark a charge of +⅔.
- Building a meson from two quarks, or a baryon from a quark and an antiquark.
- Writing u → d for β⁻ decay, or d → u for β⁺.
- Omitting the antineutrino or neutrino from a quark-level equation.
- Calling the proton fundamental, or the electron a hadron.
- Forgetting to reverse every quark when writing an antiparticle.
