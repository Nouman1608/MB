Take k = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹, R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ and N_A = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹. Treat helium and argon as monatomic ideal gases.

---

## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by the internal energy of a system. **[2]**

**2.** Explain why the internal energy of an ideal gas consists only of kinetic energy. **[2]**

**3.** A sealed flask of gas is carried from the ground floor to the top of a building. The temperature of the gas does not change. State and explain whether the internal energy of the gas changes. **[2]**

**4.** Calculate the mean translational kinetic energy of a molecule of an ideal gas at 27 °C. **[2]**

**5.** Ice at 0 °C is heated until it has all melted to water at 0 °C. Explain, in terms of the energies of the molecules, what happens to the internal energy during melting. **[3]**

**6.** Two samples of argon are at the same temperature. Sample X contains three times as many atoms as sample Y. Compare

**(a)** the mean kinetic energy of the atoms in X and Y

**(b)** the internal energies of X and Y. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A sealed rigid cylinder contains 0.20 mol of helium at 300 K.

**(a)** Calculate the number of helium atoms in the cylinder. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the internal energy of the gas. **[2]**

**(c)** The gas is heated to 450 K. Calculate the increase in internal energy. **[2]**

**(d)** State how much energy was transferred to the gas by heating, and explain your answer. **[2]**

**8.** A solid substance is heated at a constant rate. It melts, and the liquid is then heated further. The temperature–time graph has three sections: a rising section (solid), a flat section (melting) and a second rising section (liquid).

For each section, state what happens to the mean random kinetic energy and to the potential energy of the molecules. **[5]**

**9.** 0.050 kg of water at 100 °C and 0.050 kg of steam at 100 °C are compared.

**(a)** State which has the greater internal energy, and explain why in terms of molecules. **[3]**

**(b)** The specific latent heat of vaporisation of water is 2.26 × 10⁶ J kg⁻¹. Calculate the energy that must be supplied by heating to turn the water into steam at 100 °C. **[1]**

**10.** A ball of modelling clay of mass 0.30 kg is dropped and hits the floor at 6.0 m s⁻¹. It does not bounce.

**(a)** Explain why the kinetic energy of the falling ball is not part of its internal energy. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the kinetic energy of the ball just before impact, and state what happens to this energy after impact. **[3]**

**11.** Two blocks of the same metal are at the same temperature. Block P has mass 1.0 kg and block Q has mass 3.0 kg. A student says: "They are at the same temperature, so they have the same internal energy." Explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**

**12.** A fixed mass of helium is in state A, at pressure 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa and volume 2.4 × 10⁻³ m³.

**(a)** Using pV = nRT and U = (3/2)nRT, show that the internal energy of the gas is U = (3/2)pV, and calculate U in state A. **[3]**

**(b)** The gas is taken to state B, at 2.0 × 10⁵ Pa and 1.8 × 10⁻³ m³. Calculate the change in internal energy. **[2]**

**(c)** The gas can be taken from A to B by two different sequences of processes. Explain why the change in internal energy is the same for both. **[2]**

---

## Answers

**1.** The **sum** of the **random** kinetic and potential energies [1] of the **molecules** of the system [1].

**2.** There are **no intermolecular forces** in an ideal gas, except during collisions [1], so the molecules have **no potential energy**; all the internal energy is random kinetic energy [1].

**3.** **No change** [1]. The temperature (and volume) of the gas are unchanged, so its state, and hence its internal energy, is unchanged; the gain in gravitational potential energy belongs to the flask as a whole and is not random molecular energy [1].

**4.** T = 27 + 273 = 300 K [1]. Mean KE = (3/2)kT = 1.5 × 1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 300 = **6.2 × 10⁻²¹ J** [1].

**5.** Temperature is constant, so the **mean kinetic energy** of the molecules is **unchanged** [1]. Energy is used to separate the molecules against their attractive forces, so the **potential energy increases** [1]. The internal energy therefore **increases** [1].

**6. (a)** The **same**, because the temperature is the same [1].

**(b)** X has **three times** the internal energy of Y, because internal energy is the sum over all the atoms [1].

**7. (a)** N = nN_A = 0.20 × 6.02 × 10²³ = **1.20 × 10²³** [1].

**(b)** U = (3/2)nRT = 1.5 × 0.20 × 8.31 × 300 [1] = **748 J** [1].

**(c)** ΔU = (3/2)nRΔT = 1.5 × 0.20 × 8.31 × 150 [1] = **374 J** [1].

**(d)** **374 J** [1]. The cylinder is rigid, so the volume is constant and no work is done on or by the gas; all the increase in internal energy comes from heating [1].

**8.** Solid rising section: mean kinetic energy **increases** [1] and potential energy increases slightly as the mean separation grows [1].

Melting: mean kinetic energy **constant** [1] and potential energy **increases** [1].

Liquid rising section: mean kinetic energy **increases** (with a small increase in potential energy) [1].

**9. (a)** The **steam** [1]. The mean kinetic energy of the molecules is the same because the temperature is the same [1], but the steam molecules are much further apart, so their potential energy is much greater (less negative) [1].

**(b)** Q = mL = 0.050 × 2.26 × 10⁶ = **1.13 × 10⁵ J** [1].

**10. (a)** Internal energy is **random** molecular energy [1]; while falling, all the molecules share the same velocity, so this is **ordered** kinetic energy of the ball as a whole [1].

**(b)** E_k = ½mv² = 0.5 × 0.30 × 6.0² [1] = **5.4 J** [1]. It is transferred into random kinetic and potential energies of the molecules of the clay and floor, increasing their internal energy (with a small amount as sound) [1].

**11.** Same temperature means the same **mean** kinetic energy per molecule [1], but Q contains three times as many molecules, so its internal energy, the **sum** over all molecules, is about **three times** that of P [1].

**12. (a)** pV = nRT, so nRT = pV [1], and U = (3/2)nRT = (3/2)pV [1]. U_A = 1.5 × 1.0 × 10⁵ × 2.4 × 10⁻³ = **360 J** [1].

**(b)** U_B = 1.5 × 2.0 × 10⁵ × 1.8 × 10⁻³ = 540 J [1]. ΔU = 540 − 360 = **+180 J** [1].

**(c)** Internal energy is a **function of state** [1]; it depends only on the start and end states (A and B), not on the route between them, even though the heating and work done differ between routes [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "kinetic energy of the molecules" without "random" or without potential energy.
- Describing internal energy as an average rather than a sum.
- Forgetting to convert °C to K before using (3/2)kT.
- Saying the temperature or kinetic energy rises during melting or boiling.
- Counting the motion or height of the whole object as internal energy.
- Ignoring that a rigid container means no work is done, so all the energy came from heating.
