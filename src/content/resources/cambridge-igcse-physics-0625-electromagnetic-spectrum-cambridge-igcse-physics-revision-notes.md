## The order

| Region | Frequency | Wavelength |
|---|---|---|
| Radio waves | lowest | longest |
| Microwaves | ↑ | ↓ |
| Infrared | ↑ | ↓ |
| Visible light (red → violet) | ↑ | ↓ |
| Ultraviolet | ↑ | ↓ |
| X-rays | ↑ | ↓ |
| Gamma rays | highest | shortest |

- Asked for increasing wavelength? Start at gamma rays.
- All regions are transverse and travel at the **same speed in a vacuum**.
- **Extended only:** that speed is **3.0 × 10⁸ m/s**, about the same in air.
- Same speed, so higher frequency means shorter wavelength: v = fλ.
- Prefixes: k = 10³, M = 10⁶, G = 10⁹.

## Uses (Core)

| Region | Uses named in the syllabus | Property behind it |
|---|---|---|
| Radio | Radio and television transmissions, astronomy, RFID | Long range; detected without contact |
| Microwaves | Satellite television, mobile phones, microwave ovens | Absorbed by water (heating); carry signals |
| Infrared | Electric grills, remote controllers, intruder alarms, thermal imaging, optical fibres | Emitted by warm objects; heats |
| Visible | Vision, photography, illumination | Detected by eyes and cameras |
| Ultraviolet | Security marking, detecting fake bank notes, sterilising water | Makes some materials glow; kills microorganisms |
| X-rays | Medical scanning, security scanners | Pass through soft tissue, absorbed by bone and metal |
| Gamma | Sterilising food and medical equipment, detecting and treating cancer | Very penetrating; kills cells |

## Hazards (Core)

- Microwaves: **internal heating of body cells**.
- Infrared: **skin burns**.
- Ultraviolet: **damage to surface cells and eyes**, leading to skin cancer and eye conditions.
- X-rays and gamma rays: **mutation or damage to cells in the body**.
- Radio waves and visible light: no harmful effect is named in the syllabus.

## Satellites (Core)

- Communication with artificial satellites is **mainly by microwaves**.
- **Low orbit** satellites: used by some satellite phones; close, so short signal paths; move across the sky, so many are needed.
- **Geostationary** satellites: used by some satellite phones and direct broadcast satellite television; orbit once in 24 hours above the equator, so they stay above one point and a fixed dish can be used.

## Communication systems (Extended only)

- Mobile phones and wireless internet: **microwaves**, which penetrate some walls and need only a short aerial.
- Bluetooth: **radio waves**, which pass through walls but are weakened in doing so.
- Optical fibres (cable television, high-speed broadband): **visible light or infrared**, because glass is transparent to them and they carry high data rates.

## Digital and analogue (Extended only)

| | Analogue | Digital |
|---|---|---|
| Values | Any value in a continuous range | A few set values, usually two (0 and 1) |
| Noise over distance | Amplified along with the signal | Removed when the signal is regenerated |
| Data rate | Lower | Higher |

- A sound can be sent as either an analogue or a digital signal.
- Benefits of digital: **increased rate of data transmission**; **increased range due to accurate signal regeneration**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Electromagnetic spectrum | The family of transverse waves from radio to gamma, all with the same speed in a vacuum |
| RFID | Radio frequency identification: tags read using radio waves |
| Geostationary satellite | Stays above the same point on the Earth's surface |
| Low orbit satellite | Orbits close to the Earth and moves across the sky |
| Analogue signal (Extended only) | Varies continuously |
| Digital signal (Extended only) | Takes only a few discrete values |
| Regeneration (Extended only) | Restoring a weakened, noisy digital signal to its exact original values |

## Exam traps

- Infrared is **below** visible light in frequency; ultraviolet is **above** it.
- "Gamma rays are faster" is wrong: every region has the same speed in a vacuum.
- The microwave hazard is internal heating, not cancer.
- The ultraviolet hazard is to **surface** cells and eyes.
- Satellites communicate mainly by **microwaves**, not radio waves.
- Bluetooth's reason includes the weakening through walls.
- "Digital is better quality" is too vague; name regeneration or data rate.

## Self-test

1. Name the region between microwaves and visible light.
2. List the regions in order of increasing wavelength.
3. State how the speed of X-rays in a vacuum compares with that of radio waves.
4. Give one use of ultraviolet radiation and one of its harmful effects.
5. Why are X-rays used for scanning bones?
6. State the region used for thermal imaging.
7. State why a geostationary satellite suits satellite television.
8. *(Extended)* State the speed of electromagnetic waves in a vacuum.
9. *(Extended)* Find the wavelength of a 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi signal.
10. *(Extended)* Find the frequency of X-rays of wavelength 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ m.
11. *(Extended)* Why does Bluetooth use radio waves?
12. *(Extended)* Give two benefits of digital signalling.

### Answers

1. **Infrared**.
2. Gamma rays, X-rays, ultraviolet, visible light, infrared, microwaves, radio waves.
3. **The same**.
4. Security marking, detecting fake bank notes or sterilising water; damage to surface cells and eyes (skin cancer, eye conditions).
5. They pass through soft tissue but are **absorbed by bone**.
6. **Infrared**.
7. It stays above the same point on the Earth, so a **fixed dish** can point at it.
8. **3.0 × 10⁸ m/s**.
9. λ = 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 2.4 × 10⁹ = **0.125 m** (12.5 cm).
10. f = 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ = **3.0 × 10¹⁸ Hz**.
11. Radio waves pass through walls, but the signal is **weakened**, which suits short-range links.
12. **Higher data rate**; **greater range** because the signal can be regenerated accurately.
