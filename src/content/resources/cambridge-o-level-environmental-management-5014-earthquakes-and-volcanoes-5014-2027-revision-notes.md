## Layers and continents

- **Crust**: thin, rigid; oceanic (thin, dense) and continental (thick, less dense); broken into plates.
- **Mantle**: thick layer of hot rock that flows very slowly; convection currents.
- **Core**: mainly iron and nickel; source of heat.
- Continents: **Africa, Antarctica, Asia, Europe, North America, Oceania, South America**.
  Hook: "Three A's, two Americas, Europe and Oceania."

## Convection and boundaries

Core heats base of mantle → hot rock rises → spreads under crust → cools and sinks → plates dragged.

| Boundary | Movement | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Divergent / constructive | Apart | Magma rises: volcanic activity and earthquakes |
| Convergent / destructive | Together; subduction | Earthquakes and a line of volcanoes |
| Conservative | Slide past | Earthquakes only |

**Hotspot**: a plume of hot mantle under a plate, away from boundaries; a moving plate gives a chain of
volcanoes.

## Earthquake features

- **Release of energy**: stress builds as plates lock; rocks slip suddenly.
- **Focus**: point underground where slip starts.
- **Epicentre**: point on the surface directly above the focus.
- **Magnitude**: energy released, measured on the **Moment Magnitude Scale**; +1 ≈ 32 times the energy;
  +2 ≈ 1 000 times.

## Eruption features

Magma (underground) → lava (at surface); ash; gases (**water vapour, carbon dioxide, sulfur dioxide**);
acid rain (from sulfur dioxide); volcanic bombs (lava lumps that harden in the air); pyroclastic flows (fast
currents of hot gas, ash and rock).

## Ten impacts

Damage to buildings and infrastructure · loss of crops, livestock and habitats · evacuation of people and
livestock · fire · tsunamis · landslides · contamination of drinking-water supplies · water-related disease ·
financial losses · human health.

**Chain to learn:** broken pipes → contaminated water → crowded camps → water-related disease.

## Eight management strategies

| Before | During | After |
|---|---|---|
| Monitoring and warning | Evacuation | Rebuilding of damaged areas |
| Land use zoning | Shelters | International aid |
| Structure of buildings | | |
| Disaster preparation: plans, drills, emergency supplies, emergency rescue teams | | |

**Benefits and limitations in one line each**
- Monitoring and warning: good for volcanoes; earthquakes cannot be timed.
- Land use zoning: keeps people out of danger; land is scarce and people already live there.
- Structure of buildings: saves lives; costly; old buildings hard to fix.
- Disaster preparation: people react quickly; needs money and practice.
- Evacuation: removes people; needs warning, transport and trust.
- Shelters: protect people; crowding spreads disease.
- Rebuilding: restores services; slow, expensive.
- International aid: fills gaps; may be late or poorly distributed.

## Five opportunities

Fertile soils · extraction of minerals · building materials · geothermal power · tourism.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Tectonic plate | A large rigid section of the Earth's crust and upper mantle |
| Convection current | Circulation of heated mantle rock that rises, spreads, cools and sinks |
| Subduction | One plate sinking beneath another at a convergent boundary |
| Hotspot | A place where a plume of hot mantle rises beneath a plate |
| Focus | Point underground where an earthquake starts |
| Epicentre | Point on the surface directly above the focus |
| Magnitude | Energy released by an earthquake |
| Magma / lava | Molten rock underground / at the surface |
| Pyroclastic flow | Fast-moving current of hot gas, ash and rock |
| Volcanic bomb | Lump of lava that cools and hardens in the air |
| Tsunami | Series of large sea waves caused by displaced water |
| Geothermal power | Electricity or heat from hot rocks |

## Exam traps

- Focus and epicentre swapped.
- Volcanoes at conservative boundaries (there are none).
- Moment magnitude treated as a linear scale.
- "Earthquakes can be predicted." Only the likely areas are known.
- Acid rain from carbon dioxide. The syllabus links acid rain to sulfur dioxide.
- Management strategies listed without saying how they reduce a named impact.

## Self-test

1. Which layer of the Earth contains convection currents?
2. Name the seven continents.
3. Explain why volcanoes form at convergent boundaries.
4. What type of boundary produces earthquakes but no volcanoes?
5. Define the epicentre.
6. How much more energy, roughly, does a magnitude 8.0 release than a 6.0?
7. Name the gas that causes acid rain after an eruption.
8. What is a pyroclastic flow?
9. Explain how an earthquake can lead to water-related disease.
10. Give two strategies used before a tectonic event and one used after.
11. Give one limitation of land use zoning.
12. Explain why geothermal power is an opportunity in volcanic areas.

### Answers

1. The mantle.
2. Africa, Antarctica, Asia, Europe, North America, Oceania, South America.
3. The denser plate is subducted and melts; the magma rises through the overlying plate to form volcanoes.
4. Conservative.
5. The point on the Earth's surface directly above the focus.
6. About 32 x 32 ≈ 1 000 times.
7. Sulfur dioxide.
8. A fast-moving current of hot gas, ash and rock flowing down a volcano.
9. Water and sewage pipes break, drinking water is contaminated, and disease spreads in crowded shelters.
10. Before: any two of monitoring and warning, land use zoning, structure of buildings, disaster
    preparation. After: rebuilding or international aid.
11. Land may be scarce, or people already live in the danger zone and do not want to move.
12. Hot rocks near the surface heat water to steam, which drives turbines to generate electricity.
