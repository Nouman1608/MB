## Questions

**1.** State what is meant by a community, an ecosystem and a niche. **[3]**

**2.** A woodland of 2400 m² is sampled for bluebells using twenty quadrats, each 0.5 m by 0.5 m. The counts per quadrat were 2, 5, 0, 3, 4, 1, 6, 2, 0, 3, 5, 4, 1, 2, 7, 3, 0, 4, 2 and 3.

**(a)** Estimate the number of bluebells in the woodland. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the frequency of bluebells. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe how the positions of the quadrats should be chosen. **[2]**

**3.** A population of snails in a hedge bank is estimated by mark-release-recapture. 48 snails are marked with a dot of paint on the shell and released. The next day, 60 snails are caught, of which 8 are marked.

**(a)** Calculate the estimated population. **[2]**
**(b)** State two assumptions made in this method. **[2]**
**(c)** Thrushes find brightly painted snails easier to see. Explain the effect on the estimate. **[2]**

**4.** The table shows mean numbers of field voles (per hectare) and stoats (per km²).

| Year | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Voles | 40 | 110 | 70 | 25 | 30 | 95 | 80 | 30 | 35 |
| Stoats | 3 | 5 | 9 | 6 | 3 | 4 | 8 | 7 | 3 |

**(a)** Give the years between the vole peaks, and the delay from a vole peak to the next stoat peak. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the pattern in the data. **[3]**
**(c)** Define carrying capacity and suggest one abiotic factor that could lower it for voles. **[2]**

**5.** Grass species A and B were grown in pots of five plants. The table shows the mean dry mass at eight weeks of one plant, by its four neighbours.

| Plant | Neighbours | Mean dry mass (g) |
|---|---|---|
| A | four A | 3.6 |
| A | four B | 4.8 |
| B | four B | 3.4 |
| B | four A | 2.2 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease in the mass of B when its neighbours are A instead of B. **[1]**
**(b)** Use the data to decide which is the stronger competitor. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why A grows less with four A neighbours than with four B neighbours. **[2]**

**6.** Explain how bare rock covered with lichens can come to support grasses and shrubs, and why the lichens then decline. **[6]**

**7.** A glacier retreated and the land was studied at different times afterwards.

| Years since retreat | 0 | 10 | 40 | 100 | 200 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Soil depth (mm) | 0 | 5 | 30 | 90 | 150 |
| Plant species per 100 m² | 2 | 6 | 14 | 22 | 18 |
| Light reaching the ground (%) | 100 | 95 | 70 | 25 | 8 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in soil depth between 40 and 100 years. **[1]**
**(b)** Give two ways in which organisms increase soil depth. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the fall in species number between 100 and 200 years. **[2]**
**(d)** State what mainly determines the type of climax community. **[1]**

**8.** On a chalk hillside reserve, sheep graze some plots and others are fenced. After six years, grazed plots had 14.2 plant species per m² and scrub covered 3%. Ungrazed plots had 8.6 species per m² and scrub covered 41%.

**(a)** Explain why scrub cover is higher in ungrazed plots. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage more species in grazed plots. **[1]**
**(c)** The farmer wants to stop grazing because it loses money. Suggest a compromise that meets both aims. **[2]**

**9.** A field-margin scheme was studied for skylarks. Study 1, run by a conservation charity on six reserves for ten years, found mean pairs per reserve rose from 40 to 52. Study 2, run by a farmers' union on two farms for two years, found pairs fell from 15 to 12.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in each study. **[2]**
**(b)** Evaluate the evidence and suggest why the studies conflict. **[4]**

**10.** A student investigated the effect of light on a fern along a transect from open ground into woodland. At seven quadrats the light intensity, as a percentage of full daylight, was 8, 15, 22, 35, 48, 66 and 90. Fern frequency (%) at the same points was 80, 75, 60, 50, 30, 25 and 5.

**(a)** Describe how the student should carry out the investigation. **[6]**
**(b)** The correlation coefficient is −0.98. The critical value for seven pairs at the 5% level is 0.754. Interpret this result. **[3]**

**11.** Yeast cells per cm³ in a flask of broth were counted: 2.5 × 10⁴ at 0 hours, 1.0 × 10⁵ at 2, 4.0 × 10⁵ at 4, 1.6 × 10⁶ at 6, 2.4 × 10⁶ at 8 and 2.5 × 10⁶ at 10 hours.

**(a)** Calculate the doubling time between 0 and 6 hours. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why a logarithmic scale suits these data. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain the change after 6 hours. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** A community is the populations of different species in one place [1]. An ecosystem is a community and the non-living components of its environment [1]. A niche is how a species fits into its habitat, governed by its adaptation to abiotic and biotic conditions [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Define niche by role and adaptation, not by where an organism lives.

**2. (a)** Mean = 57 ÷ 20 = 2.85 per quadrat [1]. Density = 2.85 ÷ 0.25 = 11.4 per m² [1]. Estimate = 11.4 × 2400 = **27 360** (about 27 000) [1].
**(b)** 17 of 20 quadrats contain bluebells, so frequency = **85%** [1].
**(c)** Lay tapes as axes [1] and use random numbers as coordinates [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert the quadrat area to m² before scaling up.

**3. (a)** N = (48 × 60) ÷ 8 [1] = **360 snails** [1].
**(b)** Any two from: no migration, births or deaths between samples; marked snails mix randomly; marking does not affect survival or behaviour; the mark is not lost [1] [1].
**(c)** More marked snails are eaten, so fewer are recaptured [1]. m is too low, so the estimate is too high [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the number that changes (m) and say which way the estimate moves.

**4. (a)** Vole peaks in years 2 and 6, so 4 years apart [1]. Stoat peaks follow one year later [1].
**(b)** More voles give stoats more food, so stoats increase after a delay while they breed [1]. More stoats eat more voles, so voles fall [1]. Stoats then have less food and decline, so voles recover [1].
**(c)** The size of population an ecosystem can support [1]. For example, a severe frost or drought reduces the vegetation voles eat [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Describe the delay, then explain it.

**5. (a)** (3.4 − 2.2) ÷ 3.4 × 100 = **35.3%** [1].
**(b)** A is the stronger competitor [1]. A is heavier with B neighbours (4.8 g) than with A neighbours (3.6 g), while B is lighter with A neighbours (2.2 g) than with B neighbours (3.4 g) [1].
**(c)** Neighbours of the same species have identical requirements for light, water and mineral ions [1]. Intraspecific competition is stronger, so each plant gets fewer resources [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Use figures from both pairs of rows. One pair alone does not show which species is stronger.

**6.** Lichens are pioneers that survive on bare rock with little water or nutrients [1]. They weather the rock slightly and, when they die, decay and add organic matter [1]. A thin soil forms that holds water and nutrients, so the environment is less hostile [1]. Grasses and shrubs, with different adaptations such as deeper roots, colonise [1]. They shade the lichens and out-compete them for light [1]. The environment is now less suitable for lichens, so they decline as biodiversity increases [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Name a change in the abiotic environment at each stage. A list of species does not explain replacement.

**7. (a)** (90 − 30) ÷ 30 × 100 = **200%** [1].
**(b)** Dead organisms decay and add organic matter [1]. Roots and pioneers break rock into fragments [1].
**(c)** Light reaching the ground falls from 25% to 8% [1]. Species that need light are out-competed under the canopy [1].
**(d)** Climate [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In "explain" parts based on a table, quote a figure and give the biological reason.

**8. (a)** Without grazing, succession continues [1]. Woody plants grow because no grazers remove seedlings, and they shade out grassland species [1].
**(b)** (14.2 − 8.6) ÷ 8.6 × 100 = **65%** [1].
**(c)** For example, a payment for grazing at a lower stocking density [1] that keeps scrub down while cutting the farmer's losses [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A compromise must meet both aims. "Fence the reserve" ignores the farmer.

**9. (a)** Study 1: (52 − 40) ÷ 40 × 100 = **+30%** [1]. Study 2: (12 − 15) ÷ 15 × 100 = **−20%** [1].
**(b)** Study 1 has more sites and a longer period, so is likely to be more reliable [1]. Study 2 has only two farms and two years, so chance could explain the fall [1]. Both funders have an interest in the result [1]. Site, weather or how the scheme was run may also explain the difference, so neither proves the scheme caused the change [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Tie each criticism to the study: "two farms is too small to rule out a chance fall" is stronger than "more repeats".

**10. (a)** Lay a transect from open ground into woodland [1]. Place same-size quadrats at regular intervals, for example every 5 m [1]. Record fern frequency or percentage cover in each [1]. Measure light intensity with a light meter at each quadrat at the same time of day [1]. Control or record other factors, such as soil moisture [1]. Repeat on a second transect, take means and carry out a risk assessment [1].
**(b)** Fern frequency falls as light rises, a strong negative correlation [1]. 0.98 is greater than 0.754, so the correlation is significant and unlikely to be chance [1]. It does not prove light is the cause, because soil moisture or temperature may also vary with shade [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the variable measured at each quadrat and say how other factors are controlled; "use quadrats" alone is too general.

**11. (a)** The ratio is 64 = 2⁶, so 6 doublings [1]. Doubling time = 6 ÷ 6 = **1 hour** [1].
**(b)** The counts span several orders of magnitude, and a log scale shows steady exponential growth as a straight line [1].
**(c)** Growth slows and levels off as nutrients become limiting and waste products build up [1]. The population has reached the carrying capacity of the flask [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show how you reached six doublings, so that working can be credited.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "niche" as "habitat".
- Reporting the quadrat mean as the population without scaling to the area.
- Stating assumptions without saying how a failure changes the estimate.
- Confusing frequency with percentage cover.
- Listing succession species without the abiotic change.
- Giving generic evaluation points that ignore the study given.
- Treating a significant correlation as proof of cause.
