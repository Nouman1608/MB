This guide covers **Paper 1 – Depth Studies**, for Pearson Edexcel
International GCSE History (4HI1), specification Issue 5. Paper 1
offers eight Depth Study options, of which candidates study at least
two — a school's choice of which two (or more) is set by the centre,
not left to individual candidates. The eight options span roughly two
centuries of world history, from the late 18th-century French
Revolution through to the end of Apartheid in the 1990s, and no
candidate is expected to know all eight — only the specific options
their own school has chosen to teach.

## Where this fits in 4HI1

Each Depth Study is a self-contained period of focused historical
study, examined in detail rather than surveyed broadly, and is
independent of the others — a candidate studying The French Revolution
does not need knowledge of Dictatorship in the USSR unless their centre
has chosen that combination. Because of this structure, the specific
content that matters to any individual candidate depends entirely on
which two (or more) options their school has selected to teach. This
paper sits alongside the rest of 4HI1's assessment structure, so confirm
directly with your centre or teacher exactly how the full qualification
is assessed for your own examination series before finalising a
revision plan.

## Syllabus coverage

**PEARSON EDEXCEL INTERNATIONAL GCSE HISTORY (4HI1) — PAPER 1 DEPTH
STUDIES (CHOOSE AT LEAST 2 OF 8)**

- 1.1 The French Revolution, c1780–99
- 1.2 Development of a Nation: Unification of Italy, 1848–70
- 1.3 Germany: Development of Dictatorship, 1918–45
- 1.4 Colonial Rule and the Nationalist Challenge in India, 1919–47
- 1.5 Dictatorship and Conflict in the USSR, 1924–53
- 1.6 A World Divided: Superpower Relations, 1943–72
- 1.7 A Divided Union: Civil Rights in the USA, 1945–74
- 1.8 South Africa: From Union to the End of Apartheid, 1948–94

## How to approach it

Because only a subset of these eight options is actually taught at any
given school, the first and most important step is confirming exactly
which Depth Studies your centre has chosen — general Depth Study
technique matters, but revising the wrong period entirely is an
avoidable and serious error. Within whichever options are studied,
Depth Study questions test detailed causation and consequence within a
narrow, defined timeframe, so revision should prioritise depth over
breadth: knowing the specific sequence of events, key individuals, and
their motivations within the chosen period matters more than a broad
overview. Because each Depth Study is examined independently, building
a structured set of notes per option — key events in order, causes,
consequences, and named individuals — makes each one easier to revise
in isolation without the material from one bleeding confusingly into
another. Past-paper practice is especially valuable here, since
question phrasing and mark-scheme expectations are broadly consistent
across the eight options even though the historical content differs
completely between them.

## Why Depth Studies are examined this way

Unlike a broad survey paper covering centuries of change, a Depth Study is designed to test genuine
historical depth within a tightly bounded period — usually a few decades. This format rewards
detailed, specific knowledge (named individuals, exact sequences of events, precise causation)
rather than broad thematic sweep, and it means the skills tested — explaining why a specific event
happened, or how far a specific development was significant — transfer consistently across all
eight options even though the underlying content is entirely different from one option to another.
A candidate confident with the general shape of Depth Study questions (cause, consequence,
significance, change over time within the period) can apply that same exam technique regardless of
which two options their centre happens to teach.

| 1.4 India: Nationalist Challenge | 1919-47 | Colonial rule and the movement toward independence |
| 1.5 USSR: Dictatorship and Conflict | 1924-53 | Stalinist rule and its consequences |
| 1.6 Superpower Relations | 1943-72 | Cold War tensions between the USA and USSR |

Confirm with your centre which two (or more) rows above you are actually being taught before using
this table to plan detailed revision — studying the wrong option, even briefly, wastes time that
could otherwise deepen knowledge of the period that will genuinely appear in your exam.

## Building period-specific knowledge efficiently

For whichever Depth Study options a centre teaches, an effective revision structure separates each
option into: a chronological outline of major events, the key individuals involved and their roles,
the causes of major developments within the period, and the consequences or significance of those
developments. Building this structure separately for each studied option — rather than one combined
set of notes — keeps the periods from bleeding into one another, since candidates sometimes lose
marks by unintentionally applying facts from one Depth Study to a question about a different one,
particularly where two studied options share superficial similarities (for instance, both involving
a shift toward authoritarian rule).
