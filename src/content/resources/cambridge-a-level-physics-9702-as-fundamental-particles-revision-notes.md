Subtopic 11.2 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702 (2025–2027) covers the six quark flavours and their charges, antiquarks, the quark composition of protons and neutrons, baryons and mesons, quark changes in β⁻ and β⁺ decay, and leptons. The syllabus requires no quark property other than charge. These notes are for recall; see the [Fundamental particles study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-as-fundamental-particles/) for worked examples and the [Fundamental particles practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-as-fundamental-particles-practice/) to test yourself.

## The six quarks

| Charge +⅔ | Charge −⅓ |
|---|---|
| up (u) | down (d) |
| charm (c) | strange (s) |
| top (t) | bottom (b) |

- Antiquarks: same symbol with a bar, **opposite charge** (ū = −⅔, d̄ = +⅓, s̄ = +⅓, c̄ = −⅔, t̄ = −⅔, b̄ = +⅓).
- Quarks are **fundamental**.

## Hadrons

| Type | Made of | Examples | Possible charges |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baryon | 3 quarks | proton uud (+1), neutron udd (0) | −1, 0, +1, +2 |
| Meson | 1 quark + 1 antiquark | u d̄ (+1), d ū (−1) | −1, 0, +1 |
| Antibaryon | 3 antiquarks | antiproton ūūd̄ (−1) | −2, −1, 0, +1 |

- Protons and neutrons are **not** fundamental.
- Two quarks without an antiquark give +4/3, +1/3 or −2/3: not a whole number, so not a hadron.
- An antiparticle: replace every quark with its antiquark; charge reverses.

## Leptons

- **Electrons and neutrinos** are fundamental particles called **leptons**.
- They are not made of quarks.
- Their antiparticles (positron, antineutrino) are also fundamental.

## Classification map

```
fundamental particles ── quarks (u, d, s, c, t, b) and antiquarks
                      └─ leptons (electron, neutrino) and their antiparticles
hadrons (made of quarks) ── baryons (qqq): proton, neutron
                         └─ mesons (q q̄)
```

## β decay at quark level

| Decay | Nucleon change | Quark change | Emitted |
|---|---|---|---|
| β⁻ | n → p (udd → uud) | d → u | electron + electron antineutrino |
| β⁺ | p → n (uud → udd) | u → d | positron + electron neutrino |

Charge checks:

```
β⁻:  d → u + e⁻ + ν̄      −⅓ = +⅔ − 1 + 0
β⁺:  u → d + e⁺ + ν      +⅔ = −⅓ + 1 + 0
```

## Charge arithmetic shortcuts

- Count the u-type (+⅔) and d-type (−⅓) quarks; antiquarks flip the sign.
- uud: 2(+⅔) + (−⅓) = +1.
- For a meson, the only whole-number totals are −1, 0, +1.
- For a baryon, ddd (−1) is the lowest and uuu (+2) the highest.

## Worked charge checks

| Composition | Count | Type | Charge |
|---|---|---|---|
| u u d | 2 × (+⅔) + (−⅓) | Baryon | +1 (proton) |
| u d d | (+⅔) + 2 × (−⅓) | Baryon | 0 (neutron) |
| d d s | 3 × (−⅓) | Baryon | −1 |
| u c s | (+⅔) + (+⅔) + (−⅓) | Baryon | +1 |
| s ū | (−⅓) + (−⅔) | Meson | −1 |
| c d̄ | (+⅔) + (+⅓) | Meson | +1 |
| b b̄ | (−⅓) + (+⅓) | Meson | 0 |

Method: write each charge in thirds, add the numerators, divide by three. A non-integer answer means the combination is not a hadron.

## Linking 11.1 and 11.2

| Nuclear-level description (11.1) | Quark-level description (11.2) |
|---|---|
| β⁻: neutron → proton; Z increases by 1 | d → u inside the nucleon |
| β⁺: proton → neutron; Z decreases by 1 | u → d inside the nucleon |
| Electron emitted in β⁻ | The electron is a lepton, created in the decay |
| Positron emitted in β⁺ | The positron is the antiparticle of the electron |
| Antineutrino (β⁻), neutrino (β⁺) | Neutrinos are leptons; the antineutrino is the antiparticle |

In both descriptions the nucleon number is unchanged: a baryon (three quarks) becomes another baryon (three quarks).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fundamental particle | Has no internal structure |
| Quark flavour | Type of quark: up, down, strange, charm, top, bottom |
| Antiquark | Antiparticle of a quark: same mass, opposite charge |
| Hadron | Particle made of quarks |
| Baryon | Hadron of three quarks |
| Meson | Hadron of one quark and one antiquark |
| Lepton | Fundamental particle such as the electron or neutrino |

## Exam traps

- Calling protons or neutrons fundamental.
- Giving s or b a charge of +⅔.
- Making a meson from two quarks.
- Writing u → d for β⁻ (it is d → u).
- Classing positrons as baryons because they are positive.
- Forgetting the (anti)neutrino in quark-level β equations.
- Adding properties of quarks other than charge (not required).

## Self-test

2. Which three quarks have charge −⅓?
3. What is the charge of a c̄?
4. Give the quark compositions of the proton and the neutron.
5. Define a baryon and a meson.
6. Find the charge of a d s̄ meson.
7. Why can a meson not have a charge of +2?
8. Which quark change happens in β⁺ decay?
9. Name two leptons.
10. Is a neutron fundamental? Explain.
11. What is the composition of the antiparticle of the neutron?
12. Find the charge of an s s s baryon.

The largest possible is a +⅔ quark with a +⅓ antiquark, which gives +1. 8. An up quark changes to a down quark. 9. Electron and neutrino. 10.
