> **Syllabus edition note.** These notes follow the Cambridge O Level Environmental Management 5014
> syllabus for exams in 2027, 2028 and 2029, sub-topic 7.2.

Sub-topic 7.2 has two outcomes: the factors that change human populations (five named) and the impacts
of population growth (twelve named). Learn the measure the syllabus attaches to each factor, then group
the impacts.

Full explanations: [human population size study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-environmental-management-5014-human-population-size-5014-2027/).
Test yourself: [human population size practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-environmental-management-5014-human-population-size-5014-2027-practice/).

## The equation

**Population change = (births - deaths) + (immigrants - emigrants)**
- Births - deaths = **natural change** (increase if positive, decrease if negative).
- Immigrants - emigrants = **net migration**.
- Growth rate (%) = total change / starting population x 100.

## Five factors (7.2.1)

| Factor | Syllabus measure or detail | Raises population when... | Lowers it when... |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Birth rates | Children per woman | High child mortality; children work; little contraception; early marriage | Girls educated; women in paid work; contraception; city living costs |
| (b) Death rates | Life expectancy | Death rates fall: food, water, sanitation, vaccines, healthcare | Famine, conflict, epidemics |
| (c) Migration | Emigration, immigration, push and pull | Immigration exceeds emigration | Emigration exceeds immigration |
| (d) Rural and urban migration | Movement between countryside and towns | Cities gain migrants | Rural areas lose young adults |
| (e) Urbanisation | Rising urban share | Cities grow by migration and natural increase | — |

**Replacement level**: about 2.1 children per woman keeps a population stable without migration.

**Key sequence**: death rates fall first → births still high → rapid growth → birth rates fall later →
growth slows.

## Push and pull

| Push (away) | Pull (towards) |
|---|---|
| Unemployment, poverty | Jobs, higher wages |
| Conflict, persecution | Safety, freedom |
| Natural hazards, drought, soil erosion | Reliable water, fertile land |
| Few schools, clinics | Education, healthcare |
| Land shortage | Family and friends already there |

Migrants are mostly **young adults**: the receiving area gains workers and later births; the sending area
ages.

## Urbanisation versus urban growth

- **Urbanisation**: rising **proportion** urban (for example 35% → 50%).
- **Urban growth**: rising **number** of urban people.
- A country can have urban growth without urbanisation if rural areas grow just as fast.

## Twelve impacts of growth (7.2.2)

| Group | Impacts |
|---|---|
| Where people live | Informal urban settlements; megacities; availability of land |
| Resources | Availability of resources; energy usage; water supply |
| Services | Sanitation; management of waste; facilities and infrastructure |
| Daily life and environment | Air, land, light, noise, visual and water pollution; traffic congestion; changes in employment opportunities |

**Chains**
- Growth > housing → informal settlements on floodplains and slopes → hazard risk.
- Growth > sanitation → sewage in water → water-related disease.
- Growth > roads → traffic congestion → air and noise pollution.
- Growth > jobs → unemployment and informal work; or new workers attract industry → more jobs.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Birth rate | Here measured as children per woman |
| Children per woman | Average number of children born to a woman in her lifetime |
| Death rate | Rate at which people die; reflected in life expectancy |
| Life expectancy | Average number of years a person can expect to live |
| Natural change | Births minus deaths |
| Emigration / immigration | Leaving / arriving in a country |
| Net migration | Immigrants minus emigrants |
| Push factor | Something that encourages people to leave |
| Pull factor | Something that attracts people to a place |
| Rural-urban migration | Movement from countryside to towns and cities |
| Urbanisation | Increase in the proportion of people living in urban areas |
| Informal urban settlement | Housing built without legal permission or services |
| Megacity | A city with more than 10 million people |

## Exam traps

- Mixing up push and pull.
- Saying life expectancy falls as death rates fall; it rises.
- Using urbanisation to mean urban growth.
- Leaving out migration in a population-change calculation for a city or country.
- Listing impacts with no reason; always link to growth outpacing provision.
- Forgetting the positive impact: employment opportunities can grow.

## Self-test

1. Write the population change equation.
2. A country has 1.8 children per woman and no migration. What will happen to its population over time?
3. Explain why improved sanitation lowers death rates.
4. Why do death rates often fall before birth rates?
5. Give two push factors that lead to rural-urban migration.
6. Explain how migration can change the structure of the sending area's population.
7. Define urbanisation.
8. A city's urban share rises from 42% to 56%. Is this urbanisation or urban growth?
9. Name three impacts of growth on services.
10. Explain how population growth can cause traffic congestion.
11. Births 12 000, deaths 4 000, immigrants 3 000, emigrants 5 000. Calculate the change.
12. A region of 400 000 grows by 14 000 in a year. Calculate the growth rate.

### Answers

1. Change = (births - deaths) + (immigrants - emigrants).
2. Below the replacement level of about 2.1, so it will eventually decline.
3. Fewer people drink water contaminated with sewage, so fewer die of water-related diseases.
4. Healthcare, clean water and food improve quickly, but attitudes and family sizes change more slowly.
5. Any two: unemployment; low farm incomes; lack of services; land shortage; drought or soil erosion.
6. Young adults leave, so the area has a larger share of children and elderly people.
7. An increase in the proportion of a country's population living in urban areas.
8. Urbanisation (the proportion has increased).
9. Any three: sanitation; management of waste; facilities and infrastructure (also water supply).
10. More people own and use vehicles, but roads and public transport are not expanded fast enough.
11. Natural change +8 000; net migration -2 000; total +6 000.
12. 14 000 / 400 000 x 100 = 3.5%.
