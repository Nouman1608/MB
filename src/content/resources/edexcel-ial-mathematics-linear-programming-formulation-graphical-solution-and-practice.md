## Questions

**1.** A shop sells x hardback and y paperback books. Write each condition as an inequality in x and y.

**(a)** At least 15 hardbacks are in stock. **[1]**
**(b)** The shop holds at most 120 books in total. **[1]**
**(c)** Hardbacks are no more than 25% of all the books. **[1]**
**(d)** Hardbacks need 3 cm of shelf, paperbacks 2 cm, and 240 cm is available. **[1]**

**2.** A tutoring centre runs x group sessions and y one-to-one sessions each week. A group session needs 2 tutor-hours and a one-to-one session needs 1 tutor-hour. There are 36 tutor-hours. At most 30 sessions can be held in total. At least 5 group sessions must run. There must be no more than twice as many one-to-one sessions as group sessions. Income is £60 per group session and £35 per one-to-one session. Formulate a linear program to maximise income. **[7]**

**3.** Maximise P = 5x + 3y subject to 2x + y ≤ 16, x + 2y ≤ 14 and x + y ≥ 9. **[5]**

**4.** The feasible region for a problem has vertices (0, 0), (10, 0), (9, 3), (3, 9) and (0, 10). It is bounded by x + y ≤ 12, 3x + y ≤ 30 and x + 3y ≤ 30. The objective is to maximise P = 7x + 4y.

**(a)** Find the gradient of the line 7x + 4y = 28. **[1]**
**(b)** Use the ruler method to explain which vertex is optimal. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the maximum value of P. **[1]**

**5.** A café blends x kg of bean A and y kg of bean B. Minimise the cost C = 8x + 6y pounds, subject to 3x + y ≥ 18, x + 2y ≥ 16 and x + y ≤ 20. **[6]**

**6.** A club hires x large boats and y small boats. The cost in pounds is C = 40x + 30y, to be minimised, subject to 3x + y ≥ 30, x + 2y ≥ 24 and x + y ≤ 20. The numbers of boats are integers.

**(a)** Ignoring the integer condition, find the minimum cost and the point where it occurs. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the minimum cost when x and y must be integers, justifying your answer. **[3]**

**7.** A caterer bakes x small, y medium and z large cakes. Oven time is 4, 6 and 10 minutes per cake respectively, with 480 minutes available. Mixing time is 5, 8 and 12 minutes per cake, with 600 minutes available. At least half of all the cakes must be small. Profit is 60p, 100p and 170p per cake respectively. Formulate a linear program to maximise profit. **[6]**

**8.** Maximise P = 6x + 5y subject to 3x + 2y ≤ 48 (machine hours), x + y ≤ 20 (labour hours), x ≥ 4 (contract) and x + 3y ≤ 50 (material). The optimal solution is x = 8, y = 12.

**(a)** Show that (8, 12) satisfies all four constraints and state which are binding. **[2]**
**(b)** State how much material is unused and by how much x exceeds its minimum. **[2]**

**9.** A bakery makes x batches of bread and y batches of cakes each day. Bread needs 3 oven-hours and 1 preparation-hour per batch. Cakes need 2 oven-hours and 3 preparation-hours per batch. There are 60 oven-hours and 45 preparation-hours. At least 12 batches must be made. Profit is £8 per batch of bread and £9 per batch of cakes.

**(a)** Formulate a linear program to maximise profit P. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the coordinates of the vertices of the feasible region. **[3]**
**(c)** Ignoring the integer condition, find the maximum profit. **[2]**
**(d)** The batches are whole numbers. Find the best production plan and its profit, justifying your answer. **[3]**

**10.** A gardener buys x bags of compost A and y bags of compost B. Compost A has 5 units of nitrogen and 2 units of potash per bag. Compost B has 2 units of nitrogen and 3 units of potash per bag. At least 60 units of nitrogen and 48 units of potash are needed. Storage holds at most 30 bags. Bag A costs £7 and bag B costs £5.

**(a)** Formulate a linear program to minimise the cost C. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the minimum cost when fractions of a bag are allowed. **[3]**
**(c)** The bags are sold whole. Find the cheapest whole-number purchase, justifying your answer. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** x ≥ 15 [1] **[1]**
**(b)** x + y ≤ 120 [1] **[1]**
**(c)** x ≤ 0.25(x + y), so 4x ≤ x + y, giving 3x ≤ y [1] **[1]**
**(d)** 3x + 2y ≤ 240 [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* For a percentage condition, write the total x + y before simplifying.

**2.** Let x = group sessions and y = one-to-one sessions per week [1].
Maximise I = 60x + 35y [1].
Tutor time: 2x + y ≤ 36 [1]. Total: x + y ≤ 30 [1]. Minimum: x ≥ 5 [1]. Ratio: y ≤ 2x [1].
x and y are non-negative integers [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Each sentence usually gives one constraint, so tick them off. The integer condition is easy to forget.

**3.** (4, 5) from x + 2y = 14 and x + y = 9 [1]; (6, 4) from 2x + y = 16 and x + 2y = 14 [1]; (7, 2) from 2x + y = 16 and x + y = 9 [1].
P: (4, 5) gives 35; (6, 4) gives 42; (7, 2) gives 41 [1].
The maximum is **P = 42 at x = 6, y = 4** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the pair of lines used for each vertex, so the coordinates can be followed.

**4. (a)** Gradient = −7/4 [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** The line 7x + 4y = 28 meets the axes at (4, 0) and (0, 7); slide a ruler parallel to it away from the origin [1]. Its gradient −7/4 lies between −3 (3x + y = 30) and −1 (x + y = 12), so the last vertex touched is **(9, 3)** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** P = 7(9) + 4(3) = **75** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Say which way you move the ruler and why the last point touched is the answer.

**5.** From the intercepts, the vertices of R are (0, 18), (0, 20), (4, 6), (16, 0) and (20, 0) [1].
(4, 6) from x = 16 − 2y: 48 − 6y + y = 18, so y = 6 and x = 4 [1] [1].
C: (0, 18) gives 108; (0, 20) gives 120; (16, 0) gives 128; (20, 0) gives 160; (4, 6) gives 68 [1].
The minimum is C = 68 [1], by blending **4 kg of A and 6 kg of B at a cost of £68** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* With "at least" constraints, check that your vertex list includes those on the axes.

**6. (a)** Solve 3x + y = 30 and x + 2y = 24: y = 30 − 3x, so x + 60 − 6x = 24, giving x = 7.2 [1], y = 8.4 [1]. Cost = 40(7.2) + 30(8.4) = **£540** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** (7, 8) gives 3x + y = 29 < 30, not feasible. (8, 8) is feasible with cost 560, and (7, 9) is feasible with cost 550 [1]. Every other feasible point costs more than 540, and costs are multiples of 10, so no integer plan can cost less than 550 [1]. The minimum is **£550, with 7 large and 9 small boats** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Rounding to (7, 8) is infeasible and (8, 8) is dearer. State why 550 cannot be beaten.

**7.** Let x, y, z be the numbers of small, medium and large cakes [1].
Maximise P = 60x + 100y + 170z [1].
Oven: 4x + 6y + 10z ≤ 480 [1]. Mixing: 5x + 8y + 12z ≤ 600 [1].
"At least half small": x ≥ ½(x + y + z), so x ≥ y + z [1].
x, y, z non-negative integers [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* If you simplify the oven inequality, divide every term, giving 2x + 3y + 5z ≤ 240.

**8. (a)** 3(8) + 2(12) = 48; 8 + 12 = 20; 8 ≥ 4; 8 + 3(12) = 44 ≤ 50, so all are satisfied [1]. Machine hours and labour hours hold with equality, so they are **binding** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Material unused: 50 − 44 = **6** [1]. x exceeds its minimum by 8 − 4 = **4** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Interpret in context, using units from the question.

**9. (a)** Maximise P = 8x + 9y [1]. Oven: 3x + 2y ≤ 60 [1]. Preparation: x + 3y ≤ 45 [1]. Total: x + y ≥ 12, and x, y non-negative integers [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** Axis intercepts: (12, 0) and (0, 12) from x + y = 12; (20, 0) from the oven line; (0, 15) from the preparation line [1]. Solve 3x + 2y = 60 and x + 3y = 45: x = 45 − 3y, so 135 − 9y + 2y = 60 [1], giving y = 75/7 and x = 90/7 [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** P: (12, 0) gives 96; (0, 12) gives 108; (0, 15) gives 135; (20, 0) gives 160; (90/7, 75/7) gives 1395/7 ≈ 199.29 [1]. The maximum is **£199.29** at that vertex [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** The nearest integer point (13, 11) fails the oven limit: 3(13) + 2(11) = 61 > 60 [1]. (12, 11) is feasible: 58 ≤ 60, 45 ≤ 45, 23 ≥ 12, and P = 195 [1]. A better plan would have P from 196 to 199; every integer point on those lines breaks a limit, such as (11, 12) on 196, where 47 > 45. So the best plan is **12 batches of bread and 11 of cakes, profit £195** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show one infeasible rounded point, the best feasible point, and why nothing between it and the bound works.

**10. (a)** Minimise C = 7x + 5y [1]. Nitrogen: 5x + 2y ≥ 60 [1]. Potash: 2x + 3y ≥ 48 [1]. Storage: x + y ≤ 30, with x, y non-negative integers [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** The vertices are (0, 30), (24, 0), (30, 0) and the meeting of the nitrogen and potash lines. Eliminating y gives 11x = 84 [1], giving x = 84/11 and y = 120/11 [1]. C: (0, 30) gives 150; (24, 0) gives 168; (30, 0) gives 210; the other vertex gives **£108** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** Since the minimum 108 occurs only at a non-integer point, an integer plan costs at least £109. The integer points on 7x + 5y = 109 and 110 all fail the nitrogen or potash need, such as (7, 12) on 109, which gives 5x + 2y = 59 < 60 [1]. (8, 11) is feasible: 62 ≥ 60, 49 ≥ 48, 19 ≤ 30 [1]. Cost 7(8) + 5(11), so the cheapest is **8 bags of A and 11 of B, £111** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Check every constraint for your chosen point, including storage, and state the purchase.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out the definition of the variables in a formulation.
- Reversing a ratio, for example writing y ≤ 2x as 2y ≤ x.
- Missing the non-negativity and integer conditions.
- Missing a vertex on an axis when the constraints are "at least".
- Moving the ruler towards the origin when maximising, or away from it when minimising.
- Rounding to an integer without testing every constraint.
- Stopping at the objective value without stating the plan.
