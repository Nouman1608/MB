These questions cover sub-topic **8.1 Xylem and phloem** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Statements 1 and 2 are Core. Questions on statement 3, the structure of xylem vessels, are labelled **(Extended)**. The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question. Sections are described in words; all come from non-woody dicotyledonous plants.

Revise first with the [xylem and phloem study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-xylem-and-phloem-cambridge-igcse-biology/) and the [xylem and phloem revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-xylem-and-phloem-cambridge-igcse-biology-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Which substances are transported in phloem?

A: sucrose and amino acids

B: water and mineral ions

C: glucose and starch

D: water and sucrose **[1]**

**2.** State **two** functions of xylem. **[2]**

**3.** In a section through a stem, state whether the xylem of each vascular bundle faces the centre or the outside of the stem. **[1]**

**4.** In a leaf vein, which tissue is nearer the upper surface of the leaf? **[1]**

**5.** Describe the position of the xylem and the phloem in a section through a root. **[2]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Name the substance in the walls of xylem vessels that makes them strong. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Three sections, P, Q and R, are described.

- P: thin and flat, with a thick central region containing one vascular bundle.
- Q: round, with one central mass of transport tissue shaped like a cross.
- R: round, with eight separate bundles arranged in a ring near the edge.

**(a)** Identify the organ that each section comes from.

**(b)** For section R, describe where the xylem and the phloem are in each bundle.

**(c)** For section P, state where the phloem is in the bundle. **[6]**

**8.** Compare xylem and phloem. Refer to the substances transported, the direction of transport and support. **[4]**

**9.** A student stands a leafy stem of a white-flowered plant in water containing a red dye that moves only in xylem. After four hours she cuts sections through the stem and a leaf.

**(a)** Predict and explain where the dye appears in the stem section.

**(b)** Predict where the dye appears in the leaf section.

**(c)** Suggest why the petals of the flower turn red. **[5]**

**10.** *(Extended)* Explain how the structure of xylem vessels is related to their function. **[3]**

**11.** *(Extended)* A student writes: "Xylem vessels are dead and empty, so they are no use to the plant except for carrying water."

**(a)** Explain why having no cell contents is an advantage for transport.

**(b)** Explain why the student is wrong to say xylem has no other use.

**(c)** Explain why it matters that xylem vessel cells have no cross walls. **[5]**

**12.** A gardener notices that a young sunflower plant, whose stem was partly crushed at its base, wilts on a warm day even though the soil is wet. Its leaves were not damaged.

**(a)** Suggest which tissue in the stem was damaged and explain why the plant wilted.

**(b)** Suggest **one** other effect of the damage to this tissue on the plant, apart from wilting.

**(c)** The phloem in the stem was also damaged. Suggest how this would affect the roots. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** **A** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**2.** Transport of water and mineral ions [1]; support [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**3.** The centre [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**4.** Xylem [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**5.** Xylem is in the centre, in a star or cross shape [1]; phloem is in groups between the arms of the xylem [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**6.** *(Extended)* Lignin [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**7. (a)** P leaf [1]; Q root [1]; R stem [1].

**(b)** Xylem on the inner side, towards the centre of the stem [1]; phloem on the outer side, towards the edge [1].

**(c)** On the lower side of the bundle [1]. Total: 6 marks.

*Examiner insight:* A round section with a central cross is a root, not a stem; use the arrangement of transport tissue, not the outline.

**8.** Xylem transports water and mineral ions, whereas phloem transports sucrose and amino acids [1] [1]. Transport in xylem is upwards only, but phloem can transport up or down [1]. Xylem also supports the plant; support is not a function of phloem [1]. Total: 4 marks.

**9. (a)** As red spots in a ring near the edge of the stem, on the inner side of each vascular bundle [1], because that is where the xylem is and the dye moves in xylem with the water [1].

**(b)** In the veins, on the upper side of each bundle [1].

**(c)** Xylem carries water containing the dye up the stem into the flower [1], so the dye reaches the petals through their xylem [1]. Total: 5 marks.

**10.** *(Extended)* Thick walls with lignin make vessels strong, so they do not collapse and they support the plant [1]. No cell contents, so nothing obstructs the flow of water and mineral ions [1]. Cells joined end to end with no cross walls form a long continuous tube [1]. Total: 3 marks.

**11.** *(Extended)* **(a)** There is no cytoplasm or nucleus in the way [1], so water and mineral ions flow through freely [1].

**(b)** Xylem also transports mineral ions [1] and supports the plant, because its walls are thickened with lignin [1].

**(c)** The vessels form one continuous tube, so water can move from the roots to the leaves without interruption [1]. Total: 5 marks.

**12. (a)** Xylem [1]. Less water could pass up the stem to the leaves [1], so the leaves lost water vapour faster than water reached them [1].

**(b)** Less mineral ion transport to the leaves / less support for the stem, so it bends / less water for photosynthesis [1].

**(c)** Less sucrose and fewer amino acids would reach the roots [1], so root growth would be slower or the roots could not respire as much [1]. Total: 6 marks.

*Examiner insight:* "Suggest" questions reward a sensible link from the damaged tissue's function to the effect; naming the tissue alone earns only one mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing glucose instead of sucrose for phloem.
- Leaving support or mineral ions out of the functions of xylem.
- Reversing the positions in a stem bundle or a leaf vein.
- Identifying an organ from its outline instead of its arrangement of transport tissue.
- Comparing tissues in two separate lists rather than point by point.
- (Extended) Naming features of xylem vessels without saying how each helps.
