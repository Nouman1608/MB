These notes condense subtopic **1.16 Money** of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Core and Extended candidates learn the same two statements: calculate with money, and convert from one currency to another. Worked examples are in the [money study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-money/), and exam-style questions are in the [money practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-money-practice/). Every price and exchange rate below is invented for practice.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Exchange rate | How much of one currency you get for one unit of another |
| Fixed charge | A set amount paid regardless of use, such as a monthly standing charge |
| Unit charge | A price for each unit used, such as each kWh of electricity |
| Tariff | The set of charges that make up a bill |
| Commission or fee | A charge made for changing money |
| Change | Money given back when you pay more than the cost |

## Writing amounts

| Display or working | Write |
|---|---|
| 4.5 dollars | $4.50 |
| 385 cents | $3.85 |
| 12 dollars | $12 or $12.00 |
| 16.684 dollars, nearest cent | $16.68 |

- Money is written to 2 decimal places (or as a whole number of dollars).
- Make the units match before adding: cents with cents, or dollars with dollars.

## Calculating with money

**Bills**

```
bill = fixed charge + (units used × unit charge)
units used = later meter reading - earlier meter reading
```

**Offers**

| Offer | What you pay for |
|---|---|
| 3 for the price of 2 | 2 items in every complete group of 3 |
| Buy 2, get 1 free | 2 items in every complete group of 3 |
| 25% off | 75% of the total price |
| Half price on the second item | 1.5 items for every pair |

Any items that do not make a complete group are paid for in full.

**Rounding in context**

- Items you can **afford** or **buy**: round **down** (you cannot buy part of a ticket).
- A cost **shared** so that the bill is paid: round **up** to the next cent.
- Otherwise: round to the nearest cent at the end.

## Currency conversion

With **1 unit of A = r units of B**:

```
A -> B:  multiply by r
B -> A:  divide by r
```

**Through a third currency:** convert to the middle currency, keep the unrounded value, then convert again.

**Comparing prices:** convert both into the same currency first.

**Fees:** subtract a fee in the currency it is charged in, then convert what is left.

**Sense check:** if 1 unit of A buys more than 1 unit of B, an amount in B must be a **bigger** number than the same amount in A.

## Worked reminder: comparing two offers

A shop sells cans at $0.85 each with "buy 4, get 2 free". Another sells the same cans at $0.80 each with no offer. For 12 cans:

```
Offer shop: 12 cans = 2 groups of 6, pay for 8: 8 × 0.85 = $6.80
Other shop: 12 × 0.80 = $9.60
The offer shop is cheaper by $2.80.
```

## Exam traps

- $4.5 written instead of $4.50.
- Multiplying instead of dividing when converting back, so the answer goes the wrong way.
- Rounding the middle currency in a two-step conversion.
- "3 for 2" applied to 7 items as if you pay for 4.67.
- A shared bill rounded down, so it is not fully paid.
- Number of tickets rounded up beyond the budget.
- Fixed charge forgotten in a bill.
- Prices in two currencies compared directly.

## Self-test

1. Write 4.5 dollars correctly.
2. Write 385 cents in dollars.
3. Find the cost of 18 items at $3.75 each.
4. Sam buys a book for $12.60 and a pen for $4.85. He pays with a $20 note. How much change does he get?
5. A gas bill is $23.50 plus $0.142 per unit. Find the bill for 375 units.
6. Items cost $8.75 each with "buy 2, get 1 free". Find the cost of 9 items.
7. On one day £1 = $1.25. Convert (a) £450 to dollars, (b) $1000 to pounds.
8. On one day 1 euro = 16.4 pesos. Convert (a) 250 euros to pesos, (b) 3000 pesos to euros, to the nearest cent.
9. On one day £1 = $1.25 and $1 = 0.92 euros. Convert £200 to euros.
10. A club has $120 to spend on tickets costing $9.40 each. How many tickets can it buy?
11. Five friends share a bill of $83.42 equally, paying the same whole number of cents each. How much must each person pay so that the bill is covered?
12. A jacket costs £72 in one shop and $95 in another. On one day £1 = $1.25. Which is cheaper, and by how much in dollars?

### Answers

1. **$4.50**
2. **$3.85**
3. 18 × 3.75 = **$67.50**
4. 12.60 + 4.85 = 17.45; 20 − 17.45 = **$2.55**
5. 375 × 0.142 = 53.25; 23.50 + 53.25 = **$76.75**
6. 9 items = 3 groups of 3, so pay for 6: 6 × 8.75 = **$52.50**
7. (a) 450 × 1.25 = **$562.50** (b) 1000 ÷ 1.25 = **£800**
8. (a) 250 × 16.4 = **4100 pesos** (b) 3000 ÷ 16.4 = 182.926... = **182.93 euros**
9. 200 × 1.25 = $250; 250 × 0.92 = **230 euros**
10. 120 ÷ 9.40 = 12.76..., so **12 tickets** (12 × 9.40 = $112.80)
11. 83.42 ÷ 5 = 16.684, rounded up: **$16.69**
12. £72 = 72 × 1.25 = $90, so the **£72 jacket** is cheaper by **$5**
