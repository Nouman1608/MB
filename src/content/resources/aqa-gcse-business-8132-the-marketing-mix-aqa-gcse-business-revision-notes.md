## Price

| Method | Use when | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| Skimming: high launch price, lowered later | Product is new, unique or high-tech; early buyers will pay more | Attracts rivals; puts off price-sensitive buyers |
| Penetration: low launch price, raised later | Many rivals; the business needs share quickly | Low profit per unit; customers may resist later rises |
| Competitive: close to rivals' prices | Many similar products | Little freedom; must compete on other Ps |
| Loss leader: below cost to attract shoppers | Shoppers will buy other, profitable items too | Loss must be recovered elsewhere |
| Cost-plus: unit cost plus a mark-up | Costs are known and customers accept the price | Ignores what customers will pay and rivals' prices |

**Four factors:** costs; nature of the market; degree of competition; product life cycle stage.

**Price and demand:** higher price, demand likely to fall; lower price, demand likely to rise. A price cut raises revenue only if sales rise enough.

**Calculations:** revenue = price × quantity. Profit per unit = price − unit cost. Cost-plus price = unit cost × (1 + mark-up %). A £6 unit cost with a 50% mark-up gives £6 × 1.5 = £9.

## Product

- **New products:** benefits are new revenue, fresh image and an edge over rivals; risks are development cost and failure.
- **Design and image** should match the target market's needs.
- **Differentiation:** a USP or brand image makes the product stand out, so it can charge more and keep loyal customers.

### Product life cycle

Research and development → introduction → growth → maturity → decline.

| Extension strategy (all five named) | Fits when |
|---|---|
| Updating packaging | Product looks dated |
| Adding more or different features | Rivals offer more |
| Changing target market | Current market saturated |
| Advertising | Customers have forgotten the product |
| Price reduction | Product now standard and rivals cheaper |

### Boston Matrix

| | High market growth | Low market growth |
|---|---|---|
| **High market share** | Star: invest | Cash cow: keep, use its cash |
| **Low market share** | Question mark: invest or drop | Dog: often withdraw |

Classify by **share and growth**, not by sales revenue.

## Promotion

**Reasons:** to inform or remind; to create or increase sales; to create or change image; to persuade.

| Method | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Advertising (press, TV, internet, billboards) | Wide reach; internet ads can be targeted | TV is costly; easily ignored |
| PR | Cheap, seen as more believable | No control over what is published |
| Sales promotion (offers, samples, coupons, competitions, point-of-sale displays) | Quick sales boost | May only attract bargain hunters; cuts margin |
| Sponsorship | Links the brand to a popular event or team | Expensive; harmed if the event goes badly |
| Social media | Low cost, interactive, targeted | Negative comments spread fast |

**Five factors for the promotional mix:** finance available; competitor actions; nature of the product or service; nature of the market; target market.

## Place

| Channel | Suits |
|---|---|
| Retailers | Products customers want to see before buying |
| Wholesalers | Makers wanting to reach many small shops |
| Telesales | Products needing explanation; repeat business orders |
| E-commerce | Wide reach, including international markets, open all hours |
| M-commerce | Customers buying on phones, often on impulse |

More steps in a channel = more businesses taking a margin, less control for the maker.

## The integrated mix

All four Ps must fit the target market and each other. The mix changes over the life cycle: heavy informative promotion at introduction, competitive pricing in maturity, reduced price and dropped channels in decline.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Marketing mix | The combination of price, product, promotion and place |
| USP | A feature that makes a product different from its rivals |
| Brand image | The way customers see a brand |
| Product life cycle | The stages a product passes through from development to decline |
| Extension strategy | An action to extend the life of a product and delay its decline |
| Product portfolio | The range of products a business sells |
| Boston Matrix | A model classifying products by market share and market growth |
| Sales promotion | Short-term incentives to buy, such as offers and coupons |
| Channel of distribution | The route a product takes from maker to customer |
| M-commerce | Buying and selling using mobile devices |

## Exam traps

- Skimming is high, penetration is low: do not swap them.
- A loss leader is meant to raise total profit through other purchases.
- Choose an extension strategy that fixes the cause of falling sales.
- Boston Matrix uses share and growth.
- Justify promotion with the five factors, not personal preference.
- Every P must fit the target market; a clash loses marks in a "recommend the mix" answer.

## Self-test

1. What is price skimming?
2. State the four factors influencing pricing named in the specification.
3. A product costs £8 to make and is priced with a 25% mark-up. What is the price?
4. Name the five extension strategies.
5. A product has a high market share in a low-growth market. Which Boston Matrix category is it?
6. Give one benefit and one risk of launching a new product.
7. What is a USP?
8. State the five factors influencing the promotional mix.
9. Give one weakness of PR.
10. Why might a small producer sell through a wholesaler?
11. How does e-commerce help a business reach international markets?
12. Give one way the marketing mix changes when a product enters decline.

### Answers

1. Setting a high price at launch and lowering it later.
2. Costs, nature of the market, degree of competition, product life cycle.
3. £8 × 1.25 = **£10**.
4. Updating packaging, adding more or different features, changing target market, advertising, price reduction.
5. Cash cow.
6. Benefit: new revenue, fresh image or competitive edge. Risk: development costs or the product failing.
7. A unique selling point: a feature that makes a product different from rivals.
8. Finance available, competitor actions, nature of the product or service, nature of the market, target market.
9. The business cannot control what is published.
10. The wholesaler buys in bulk and reaches many shops, saving the producer time and delivery costs.
11. Customers anywhere can buy online, so the business can sell abroad without opening shops there.
12. For example, the price is reduced, promotion spending is cut, or weak channels are dropped.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I recommend a marketing mix in an exam?**
State the target market from the case, choose each of the 4Ps to suit it, and justify each choice with a fact from the case.

**Which pricing method suits a market with many rivals?**
Usually competitive pricing, because prices well above rivals' lose customers and prices well below them cut profit.
