## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Alternative hypothesis | A testable prediction that there will be a difference or relationship |
| Null hypothesis | A prediction that there will be no difference or relationship |
| Operationalise | Define a variable so it can be measured, e.g. "words recalled out of 20" |
| Extraneous variable | Any variable other than the IV that could affect the DV |
| Target population | The whole group the researcher wants the findings to apply to |
| Standardised procedure | Every participant experiences the same steps and instructions |
| Randomisation | Using chance, e.g. to allocate participants to conditions |
| Counterbalancing | Half do condition A then B, half B then A, to balance order effects |
| Reliability | Consistency: the same result if the study is repeated |
| Validity | Whether a study measures what it claims to measure |
| Interobserver reliability | The extent to which two observers record the same behaviour in the same way |
| Primary data | Collected first-hand by the researcher |
| Secondary data | Collected by someone else, e.g. published statistics |

## Methods at a glance

| Method | Key feature | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Laboratory experiment | IV changed in a controlled setting | Control; cause and effect | Artificial setting |
| Field experiment | IV changed in a natural setting | More natural behaviour | Less control |
| Natural experiment | IV occurs naturally, not changed by researcher | Studies things that could not be manipulated ethically | No control over allocation |
| Structured interview | Same pre-set questions for all | Easy to compare answers | Cannot follow up interesting replies |
| Unstructured interview | Questions develop like a conversation | Rich, detailed answers | Hard to compare; interviewer bias |
| Questionnaire | Written questions; **open** (own words) or **closed** (fixed options) | Large samples quickly | Social desirability bias |
| Case study | In-depth study of one person or small group | Rich detail; rare cases | Hard to generalise |
| Observation | Behaviour watched and recorded using **behaviour categories** | Real behaviour, not reported | Observer bias; cannot show why |
| Correlation | Measures the relationship between two co-variables | Shows links that can be studied further | Cannot show cause |

**Correlations:** **positive** (both rise together), **negative** (one rises, the other falls), **no correlation** (no relationship).

## Procedures and planning

- **Standardised instructions** and **standardised procedures** make a study **reliable** (replicable).
- **Random allocation** spreads participant variables across conditions.
- **Counterbalancing** deals with **order effects** (practice and fatigue) in repeated measures.
- When planning, consider the **reliability and validity** of the sampling method, design and method chosen.

## Ethics: issue and how to deal with it

| Issue | How to deal with it |
|---|---|
| Consent | Get informed consent; parental consent for under-16s |
| Deception | Only if justified; debrief fully and offer withdrawal of data |
| Confidentiality | Keep data anonymous, e.g. use numbers, not names |
| Right to withdraw | Tell participants they can leave at any time, including later withdrawing their data |
| Protection from harm | No more risk than in everyday life; stop if distress occurs; debrief |

## Data handling and maths

- **Quantitative** data are numbers; **qualitative** data are words or descriptions.
- **Mean:** total divided by number of scores (uses all data; distorted by extreme scores).
- **Median:** middle value in order (not affected by extremes).
- **Mode:** most frequent value (can be more than one).
- **Range:** highest minus lowest (a measure of spread).
- **Percentage:** part ÷ whole × 100. **Ratio:** simplify by dividing by a common factor (18:6 = 3:1).
- **Standard form:** 0.0032 = 3.2 × 10⁻³; 45,000 = 4.5 × 10⁴.
- **Significant figures:** 9.375 to 2 s.f. = 9.4.
- **Displays:** bar chart (separate categories, gaps between bars); histogram (continuous data, no gaps); scatter diagram (correlations); tables of results.
- **Normal distribution:** symmetrical, bell-shaped; mean, median and mode are equal and at the centre; most scores near the middle, fewer at the extremes.

## Self-test

1. Write a null hypothesis for a study testing whether chewing gum affects memory.
2. Which method of dealing with order effects gives half the participants condition B first?
3. What is the difference between a field and a natural experiment?
4. Find the mean, median, mode and range of: 4, 6, 6, 7, 12.
5. Why is a histogram used rather than a bar chart for reaction times?
6. How could a researcher check interobserver reliability?
7. Give the mean, median and mode of a normal distribution in relation to one another.

**Answers:**
1. "There will be no difference in the number of words recalled between participants who chew gum and those who do not."
2. Counterbalancing.
3. In a field experiment the researcher changes the IV in a natural setting; in a natural experiment the IV changes naturally, without the researcher.
4. Mean 7 (35 ÷ 5); median 6; mode 6; range 8.
5. Reaction time is continuous data, so the bars should touch.
6. Two observers record the same behaviour separately using the same categories, then compare records; a high level of agreement means good reliability.
7. They are all the same value, at the centre of the curve.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between reliability and validity?**
Reliability is consistency, getting the same result when a study is repeated; validity is whether the study measures what it claims to measure.
