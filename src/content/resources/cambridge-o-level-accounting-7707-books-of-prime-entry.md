This guide covers section **2.3 Books of prime entry** of the Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus for examination in 2027, 2028 and 2029. The syllabus lists the books as the "cash book, petty cash book, sales journal, purchases journal, sales returns journal, purchases returns journal and the general journal". It also covers posting from them, trade and cash discount, the dual function of the cash book, recording receipts and payments by different methods, the imprest system, and the benefits and limitations of keeping cash at the business property and of manual and digital methods of original entry. If you sit an earlier series, check the syllabus for your exam year.

Use it with the [books of prime entry revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-books-of-prime-entry-revision-notes/) and the [books of prime entry practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-books-of-prime-entry-practice/). The documents that feed these books are in the [business documents study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-business-documents/), and posting rules are in the [double entry study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-the-double-entry-system-of-book-keeping/). Examples follow Larchmont Gifts, an invented business. Amounts are in dollars.

## 1. What a book of prime entry is

A book of prime entry records a transaction **for the first time**, from its source document. Similar transactions are listed together, so that the ledger receives one total instead of hundreds of separate entries.

| Book | Records | Source document |
|---|---|---|
| Sales journal | Credit sales | Copy of sales invoice |
| Purchases journal | Credit purchases of goods for resale | Purchase invoice |
| Sales returns journal | Goods returned by credit customers | Copy of credit note issued |
| Purchases returns journal | Goods returned to credit suppliers | Credit note received |
| Cash book | All cash and bank receipts and payments | Receipts, counterfoils, paying-in slips, bank statement |
| Petty cash book | Small cash payments | Petty cash vouchers |
| General journal | Anything not in the other books | Memos, letters, invoices for non-current assets |

**Sales journal**

|---|---|---|---|
| Jun 3 | T Okafor (list 800 less 20% trade discount) | 412 | 640 |
| Jun 10 | Bayview Hotel | 413 | 1,200 |
| | Total posted to sales account | | 1,840 |

**Purchases journal**

| Date | Supplier | $ |
|---|---|---|
| Jun 5 | Northfield Crafts (list 2,500 less 20% trade discount) | 2,000 |
| Jun 18 | Ridley Limited | 950 |
| | Total posted to purchases account | 2,950 |

**Sales returns journal**: Jun 14, T Okafor, 80. **Purchases returns journal**: Jun 9, Northfield Crafts, 150.

**Posting the day books**

| Book | Each line posted to | Total posted to |
|---|---|---|
| Sales journal | Debit each customer (sales ledger) | Credit sales account |
| Purchases journal | Credit each supplier (purchases ledger) | Debit purchases account |
| Sales returns journal | Credit each customer | Debit sales returns account |
| Purchases returns journal | Debit each supplier | Credit purchases returns account |

The journals record only **net** amounts: trade discount is deducted on the invoice and never appears in the ledger.

## 3. Trade discount and cash discount

| | Trade discount | Cash discount |
|---|---|---|
| Given for | Trade customers, bulk orders | Paying within a stated time |
| Shown on the invoice? | Yes, deducted | Only as terms, not deducted |
| Recorded in the books? | No | Yes: discount allowed (an expense) or discount received (income) |

T Okafor owes 640 − 80 = 560 and pays within the terms, which allow 5%: discount allowed 560 × 5% = 28, so Okafor pays 532. Larchmont owes Northfield 2,000 − 150 = 1,850 and earns 2%: discount received 37, so it pays 1,813.

## 4. The cash book and its dual function

The cash book is both:

- a **book of prime entry**, recording every receipt and payment for the first time, and
- a **ledger account**: its cash and bank columns are the cash account and bank account. No separate cash or bank account is opened in the ledger.

So only the **other** side of each cash book entry is posted to the ledger.

**Three-column cash book, June** (discount columns are memorandum columns, totalled and posted)

| Date | Details | Disc. | Cash | Bank | Date | Details | Disc. | Cash | Bank |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jun 1 | Balances b/d | | 300 | 4,200 | Jun 8 | Rent (online transfer) | | | 700 |
| Jun 6 | Sales (cash) | | 520 | | Jun 16 | Northfield Crafts (cheque) | 37 | | 1,813 |
| Jun 12 | Sales (card payments) | | | 1,350 | Jun 22 | Bank (contra) | | 600 | |
| Jun 20 | T Okafor (bank transfer) | 28 | | 532 | Jun 25 | Wages (bank transfer) | | | 900 |
| Jun 22 | Cash (contra) | | | 600 | Jun 30 | Petty cash (restore imprest) | | | 145 |
| | | | | | Jun 30 | Balances c/d | | 220 | 3,124 |
| | | **28** | **820** | **6,682** | | | **37** | **820** | **6,682** |

Check: cash 820 − 600 = 220; bank 6,682 − 3,558 = 3,124.

Posting: Okafor credited with 532 and 28; Northfield debited with 1,813 and 37; discount allowed account debited with the column total 28; discount received account credited with 37. The contra (cash paid into the bank) needs no ledger posting, because both sides are in the cash book. Okafor's account is now clear (640 − 80 − 532 − 28 = 0), as is Northfield's.

### Receipts and payments by different methods

| Method | Column |
|---|---|
| Notes and coins | Cash |
| Cheques received or paid | Bank (a cheque received is banked) |
| Debit and credit card sales | Bank, because the money goes into the bank account |
| Card payments made by the business | Bank |
| Online and bank transfers, standing orders, direct debits | Bank |

## 5. The petty cash book and the imprest system

Small payments such as postage and cleaning are recorded in a petty cash book, usually kept by a junior member of staff, with analysis columns for each type of expense.

The **imprest system** works like this:

1. The petty cashier is given a fixed float, the **imprest**, say 200.
2. Each payment is supported by a voucher and recorded.
3. At the end of the period the amount spent is **restored**, so the float returns to 200.

| Larchmont's June petty cash | $ |
|---|---|
| Postage | 38 |
| Cleaning | 45 |
| Stationery | 62 |
| Total spent | 145 |
| Cash remaining | 200 − 145 = 55 |
| Restored from bank on 30 June | 145 |

**Purpose:** it frees senior staff from small payments; the float limits how much can go missing; at any time cash plus vouchers should equal the imprest, which makes checking easy; analysis column totals post neatly to expense accounts. Each analysis total (postage 38, cleaning 45, stationery 62) is debited to its expense account.

## 7. Keeping cash at the business property

| Benefits | Limitations |
|---|---|
| Available for small payments and change for customers | Risk of theft, loss and fraud |
| No trips to the bank for small amounts | Need for safes, security and insurance |
| Immediate access when the bank is closed | Counting errors; harder to control |
| | Earns no interest |

Limit the cash kept, bank it regularly, use the imprest system and count it often.

## 8. Manual and digital original entry

| | Manual | Digital |
|---|---|---|
| Benefits | Cheap; no equipment or power needed; easy to learn | Fast; totals and balances automatic; entries post to the ledger directly; easy to back up and search |
| Limitations | Slow; addition and copying errors; totals must be posted by hand; paper can be lost or damaged | Cost and training; security risks; a keying error flows through every record; depends on equipment |

## Common mistakes

- Recording trade discount, or treating cash discount as a deduction on the invoice.
- Opening a separate bank account in the ledger when the cash book already serves as one.
- Posting a contra entry to the ledger.
- Putting card sales in the cash column.
- Recording credit purchases of non-current assets in the purchases journal instead of the general journal.
- Restoring the imprest by the float amount instead of the amount spent.

## Exam technique

- **Prepare** a book of prime entry: date, details, amounts, totals; a three-column cash book needs balances c/d and b/d.
- **Explain** the dual function, imprest system or a discount: say what it is and why it is used.
- **State** the posting: the account debited and the account credited, including the totals.
- **Discuss** cash on the premises, or manual against digital entry: benefits, limitations, judgement.
