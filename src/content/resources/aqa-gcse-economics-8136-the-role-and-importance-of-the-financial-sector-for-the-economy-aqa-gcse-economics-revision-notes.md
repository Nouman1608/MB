## The three content points

1. **The financial sector** and its main agents: the Bank of England, commercial banks, building societies.
2. **The role of the Bank of England:** influencing interest rates; ensuring the stability of the financial system.
3. **The role of other institutions:** high street banks help fund investment and provide a service for savers and borrowers.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Financial sector | Institutions that hold savings, lend, provide payments and manage financial risk |
| Financial intermediary | An institution that channels money from savers to borrowers |
| Central bank | The bank that sets interest rates and oversees the banking system: in the UK, the Bank of England |
| Commercial (high street) bank | A shareholder-owned bank offering accounts, loans, mortgages and payments |
| Building society | A mutual, owned by its members, mainly offering savings and mortgages |
| Bank Rate | The official interest rate set by the Bank of England's Monetary Policy Committee |
| Bank run | Many depositors trying to withdraw their money at once because they fear a bank will fail |
| Lender of last resort | The central bank's willingness to lend to banks in difficulty to stop a crisis spreading |
| Mortgage | A long-term loan to buy property, secured on that property |
| Overdraft | A short-term facility to spend more than the balance in a current account |

## Intermediation in four verbs

**Collect** deposits → **assess** borrowers → **lend** in larger sums → **spread** risk. Banks pay savers interest and charge borrowers more; the gap covers costs and profit.

## The Bank of England

**Role 1: influencing interest rates**

- The Monetary Policy Committee sets Bank Rate.
- Aim: the government's 2% CPI inflation target.
- Independent in setting rates since 1997.

**Chain for a rise in Bank Rate:** banks raise their rates → borrowing dearer, saving more rewarding → less consumer spending on credit, mortgage payers have less to spend, firms invest less → demand grows more slowly → inflation falls (but growth slows and unemployment may rise).

**Role 2: ensuring stability of the financial system**

- Regulates and supervises banks, building societies and insurers (Prudential Regulation Authority).
- Requires banks to hold enough capital to absorb losses.
- Watches risks to the whole system (Financial Policy Committee).
- Lender of last resort in a crisis.

**Other roles:** issues banknotes in England and Wales; acts as the government's bank.

**Why stability matters:** if banks fail or stop lending, firms cannot invest and households cannot borrow, so output and jobs fall, as in the 2007–08 financial crisis.

## High street banks and building societies

| Service for | What banks provide | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Savers | Safe accounts, interest | Rewards saving; savings become funds for lending |
| Borrowers | Mortgages, loans, overdrafts, credit cards | Households can buy homes and goods before saving the full price |
| Firms | Business loans and overdrafts | Fund investment in capital and cover cash-flow gaps |
| Everyone | Current accounts, cards, transfers | A fast, safe payment system |

**Bank vs building society:** a bank is owned by shareholders; a building society is owned by its members and concentrates on savings and mortgages.

## Importance to consumers, producers and government

- **Consumers:** save and earn interest; borrow for homes and cars; pay by card.
- **Producers:** borrow to invest and to cover cash-flow gaps; pay suppliers and staff.
- **Government:** borrows by selling bonds (gilts), often bought by pension funds and insurers; banks at the Bank of England.
- **Whole economy:** investment funded by banks raises productive capacity and long-run growth; financial services are a large UK employer and export.

## Calculation patterns

**Interest on a loan:** £80,000 at 5% for one year = 0.05 × 80,000 = **£4,000**.

**Is an investment worthwhile?** Extra profit £9,000 a year; loan £120,000 at 7% costs £8,400 a year; gain after interest = **£600**. At 8% the interest is £9,600, so the investment would make a **loss of £600** a year.

**Bank's interest margin:** lends £50m at 6.5%, pays savers 2% on £50m: 3.25m − 1m = **£2.25m**.

## Exam traps

- The **Bank of England**, not the government, sets Bank Rate.
- The Bank of England lends to **banks**, not to the public.
- "Ensuring stability" is not the same as "keeping prices stable"; it means preventing bank failures and crises.
- A building society is a **mutual**: name who owns it.
- In an interest rate question, give the full chain to spending or investment, not just "people borrow less".
- Higher rates help **savers** and hurt **borrowers**; say which group you mean.

### Answers

1. The Bank of England, commercial banks and building societies.
2. An institution that channels money from savers to borrowers.
3. The Monetary Policy Committee.
4. 2% inflation, measured by the CPI.
5. Influencing interest rates and ensuring the stability of the financial system.
6. Many depositors withdrawing money at once because they fear the bank will fail.
7. The Bank of England lends to banks in difficulty when no one else will, to stop a crisis spreading.
8. A building society is owned by its members; a bank is owned by shareholders.
9. Savers: safe keeping and interest. Borrowers: mortgages, loans or overdrafts.
10. They lend savers' deposits to firms, which use the money to buy capital such as machinery.
11. 0.04 × 60,000 = **£2,400**.
12. By selling bonds (gilts), which financial institutions such as pension funds buy.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is the financial sector important for economic growth?**
It turns savings into loans that fund firms' investment, which raises the economy's capacity to produce.

**What happens if the financial system becomes unstable?**
Banks may fail or stop lending, so spending and investment fall and a recession can follow.
