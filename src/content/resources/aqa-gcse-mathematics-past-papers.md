This page is a guide to AQA GCSE Mathematics (8300) past papers from June 2024 to June 2026, including the November series. For each series you get the grade thresholds, copied from AQA's grade-boundary documents, and, where we have summarised one, a short account of what the examiners saw, written by us from AQA's reports on the examination. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on AQA's own site, linked below.

## Where to get the official papers

- [AQA GCSE Mathematics (8300) subject page](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/mathematics/gcse/mathematics-8300)
- [AQA GCSE Mathematics (8300) assessment resources](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/mathematics/gcse/mathematics-8300/assessment-resources), where question papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination are listed by series and tier
- [AQA GCSE Mathematics (8300) specification at a glance](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/mathematics/gcse/mathematics-8300/specification/specification-at-a-glance)
- [AQA grade boundaries](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries) and the [grade-boundary archive](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries/archive)

Use the three documents together. Sit the paper first, under timed conditions. Mark it strictly with the mark scheme, which shows what each mark needs. Then read the report on the examination for that paper: it explains why answers that looked reasonable still lost marks. Our notes for each series below are a quick way into that report.

Check which specification a paper was set on. Every paper on this page was set on the 8300 specification, first taught in September 2015 and first examined in June 2017, so older papers cover the same content. It is a linear, tiered qualification: Foundation candidates (grades 1 to 5) and Higher candidates (grades 4 to 9) each sit three papers of 1 hour 30 minutes and 80 marks, one third of the GCSE each. Paper 1 is non-calculator; Papers 2 and 3 allow a calculator. Match the paper to your tier, for example 8300/1F or 8300/1H.

## November 2025

AQA's November boundary document covers GCSE English Language and Mathematics. The reports describe a Foundation Paper 2 where a significant minority of candidates appeared to lack a calculator, and a Higher Paper 2 where many candidates found the second half hard, with a high proportion of unanswered questions.

**Where marks were lost: Foundation**

- Paper 1: forming a set of numbers with a given range, naming a shape, using a probability tree, terminating decimals to a stated number of places, using one sector of a pie chart to find another, and the area of a quadrant from its perimeter.
- Paper 2: map scales, graphical simultaneous equations, congruent triangles, and circumference with speed, distance and time.
- Paper 3: proportion problems, bearings, square and cube number reasoning, ratio with fractions and percentages, term-to-term rules written with algebra, finding a side with sine, density units, relative frequency and the equation of a line given gradient and intercept. The report also notes that working was not always shown and that non-calculator methods were used for percentages.

**Where marks were lost: Higher**

- Paper 1: reasoning about terminating decimals, interquartile range, lengths of lines on coordinates, explaining why an answer is an estimate, solving a quadratic equation and proof.
- Paper 2: time series, map scales, congruent triangles, area of sectors, functions and a ruler-and-compasses construction. Three questions shared with Foundation were done poorly.
- Paper 3: density, naming a polygon from criteria, completing a relative frequency graph, giving reasons in geometric proof, perpendicular gradients, midpoints, probability from a Venn diagram, average speed from a speed-time graph and an iterative formula taken to five decimal places. Digits 4 and 9 were again hard to tell apart, and recall of facts not on the insert was weak.

## June 2025

The reports describe Foundation Paper 1 as little changed from the previous year, Higher Paper 1 as accessible, and Higher Paper 2 as similar in standard to the year before. Poor presentation made some reasoning answers hard to read, and the Higher Paper 3 report singles out recall of the cylinder volume formula.

**Where marks were lost: Foundation**

- Paper 1: working backwards from an answer and remainder to find the starting number, the volume of a sphere, standard form, the value of sin 90 degrees, and finding a side of a rectangle from its area and two algebraic lengths.
- Paper 2: angles in similar shapes, probability of combined events, bounds and a circle problem. The report adds that some candidates used non-calculator methods on a calculator paper and made arithmetic slips.
- Paper 3: modal value from coins, forming an equation from a perimeter, relative frequency, pie charts, large scale factors, bearings, Venn diagram problems, Pythagoras in context and equations of lines. Working was not always shown when asked.

**Where marks were lost: Higher**

- Paper 1: inequalities, interquartile range and algebraic proof. Candidates who multiplied the middle of a range rather than the bounds lost marks on bounds work.
- Paper 2: the gradient of a horizontal line, a probability coin problem, the equation of a circle, the equation of a tangent to a circle and a proof with composite and inverse functions.
- Paper 3: converting centimetres to kilometres, one number as a fraction of another, a missing value from a Venn diagram, a parallel line, drawing a smooth curve through six points, an upper bound, algebraic arguments, dotted or solid lines for inequalities, the cylinder volume formula and regions for a quadratic inequality.

## June 2024

The reports describe most Foundation candidates reaching the lower-demand questions, Higher Paper 1 with a slightly higher mean than the year before, and Higher Paper 2 as very similar to June 2023, with the questions shared with Foundation found harder than a year earlier.

**Where marks were lost: Foundation**

- Paper 1: the link between range and consistency, showing a number cannot come from a formula, area of a triangle, vectors, upper and lower bounds, the area of a circle as the base of a cone, improper fractions, number lines, inequalities and describing an enlargement.
- Paper 2: tree diagrams, reverse percentages and standard form.
- Paper 3: edges of a solid, number-machine deductions, reflections in a given line, ratio in the form 1 : n, nth term problems, percentage decrease, elevations, speed and time conversions and compound percentage change.

**Where marks were lost: Higher**

- Paper 1: algebraic proof, identities, simplifying fractions with decimals and square roots, equations of circles and enlargements with fractional scale factors. Some working and explanations were too poorly written to credit.
- Paper 2: a spinner probability problem, best estimates of probability with a reason, how a change to a straight line or an exponential curve alters a coordinate, and a ratio, volume and surface-area problem.
- Paper 3: using a tree diagram, roots from a quadratic graph, the equation of a perpendicular line, comparing relative frequencies, identifying triangles that are not congruent, equal-volume ratios and the product rule for counting. Candidates miscopied numbers from the question and made basic arithmetic errors that suggest the calculator was not used.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find AQA GCSE Mathematics 8300 past papers?**
AQA lists question papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination on the 8300 assessment-resources page, by series and tier. Grade boundaries are on AQA's grade-boundaries page and its archive.

**Which papers should I sit for a full practice run?**
Sit all three papers for your tier: 8300/1F, 2F and 3F for Foundation, or 8300/1H, 2H and 3H for Higher. Paper 1 is non-calculator and Papers 2 and 3 allow a calculator.

**Are there GCSE Mathematics papers in November as well as June?**
Yes. AQA publishes November papers, boundaries and reports for GCSE Mathematics, and this page includes the November 2024 and November 2025 series.

**Are the paper-level grade thresholds official grades?**
No. AQA states that component boundaries are notional and for illustration only. The grade comes from the total mark out of 240 and the subject boundaries for your tier.

**Can grade thresholds tell me what mark I need next time?**
No. Thresholds change every series and should only be used as a rough guide for the series they belong to, never to predict future ones.
