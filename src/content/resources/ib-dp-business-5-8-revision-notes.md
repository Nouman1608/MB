## The four content points

| Content point | Depth | Recall |
|---|---|---|
| Importance of R&D for a business | AO3 | New products, advantage, lower costs, new markets; but certain cost, uncertain return |
| Developing goods and services that address unmet needs | AO2 | Known needs found by research; latent needs revealed by products |
| Intellectual property protection: copyrights, patents, trademarks | AO2 | Match the form to the asset; protection has costs and limits |
| Innovation: incremental and disruptive | AO2 | Small steps versus market-changing products or models |

## Key terms

| Term | Working definition |
|---|---|
| Research | Work to discover new knowledge |
| Development | Turning knowledge into a usable, producible product, service or process |
| Innovation | Successful commercial use of a new idea |
| Unmet need | A customer want that no current product satisfies |
| Latent need | An unmet need customers are not aware of until a product reveals it |
| Market pull | Developing a product in response to evidence of customer need |
| Technology push | Developing a capability first, then finding a market use |
| Copyright | Protection for original creative works such as text, code, images and music |
| Patent | Protection for a new invention, product or process, for a limited period |
| Trademark | Protection for a sign that shows the origin of goods or services |
| Incremental innovation | Small improvements to an existing product or process |
| Disruptive innovation | A new product or model that changes how a market works |

## The pipeline in one line

Ideas, screening, concept testing, prototype, test marketing, launch. Most ideas are dropped along the way, so the few launched products must cover the cost of all the failures.

Mini reminder with new figures: 30 ideas cost 1,500 each to screen (45,000); 6 are concept tested at 20,000 each (120,000); 2 prototypes cost 200,000 each (400,000); 1 is launched. Total = 45,000 + 120,000 + 400,000 = 565,000 for one launch, and 1 in 30 ideas (3.3%) succeeded.

## Why R&D matters, and its limits

| Benefit | Limit |
|---|---|
| Replaces products in decline | Spending is certain, results are not |
| Creates hard-to-copy advantage | Advantage fades when protection ends or rivals catch up |
| Better processes lower costs | Payback can take years |
| Opens new markets | Cuts profit and dividends now |
| Supports premium prices | Needs skilled, expensive staff |

## Drivers of R&D spending

- Industry and pace of change.
- Competitors' launches.
- Available finance and retained profit.
- Shareholders' time horizon.
- Position of products in their life cycle.
- Government grants or tax incentives in some countries.

## Matching IP to the asset

| Asset in the case | Form |
|---|---|
| New production process or device | Patent |
| Software code, manuals, packaging artwork, recipes written as text | Copyright |
| Brand name, logo, slogan | Trademark |

Limits to quote: cost of applying and enforcing; hard to enforce in other countries; patents publish the invention so rivals can design around it; protection is time-limited (except renewable trademarks).

## Innovation comparison

| | Incremental | Disruptive |
|---|---|---|
| Risk | Low | High |
| Cost | Lower | Higher |
| Customers | Existing | Often new or ignored ones first |
| Danger | Overtaken by a disruptor | Cash runs out; own products made obsolete |

## Exam traps

- Calling any invention an innovation. It must be used commercially.
- Giving a patent for a logo, or a trademark for a process.
- Writing "a patent guarantees profit". It only stops copying for a time.
- Saying disruptive innovation is always better.
- Judging R&D on one project instead of the whole pipeline.
- Forgetting the HL-only label: SL candidates are not examined on 5.8.
- Listing benefits of R&D without the uncertainty of returns in an AO3 answer.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish between research and development.
2. Define innovation.
3. What is a latent need?
4. Give one difference between market pull and technology push.
5. State the form of IP that would protect (a) a new battery design, (b) a firm's mascot logo, (c) its app code.
6. Give two limits of patent protection.
7. A firm screened 50 ideas and launched 2. What percentage reached launch?
8. Give two factors that affect how much a business spends on R&D.
9. Give one risk of relying only on incremental innovation.
10. Give one risk of pursuing disruptive innovation.
11. Why might shareholders oppose a large rise in R&D spending?
12. Give one reason a product may need copyright, a patent and a trademark at once.

### Answers

1. Research discovers new knowledge; development turns it into a working, producible product, service or process.
2. The successful commercial use of a new idea.
3. A need customers do not realize they have until a product reveals it.
4. Market pull starts from evidence of customer need; technology push starts from a new capability and then looks for a use.
5. (a) Patent, (b) trademark, (c) copyright.
6. Any two: cost to obtain and enforce; hard to enforce abroad; the invention is published so rivals can design around it; it lasts only a limited period.
7. 2 ÷ 50 × 100 = 4%.
8. Any two: industry, competition, finance, shareholders' outlook, product life cycle, government incentives.
9. A rival with a disruptive product may take the market while the firm only improves old products.
10. High cost and uncertain demand; it may make the firm's own products obsolete.
11. R&D cuts profit and dividends now, and payback is slow and uncertain.
12. Different parts of the product are different assets: an invention, creative content and a brand name.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is section 5.8 HL only?**
Yes. Research and development is HL only in the IB Business Management guide, first assessment 2024.

**What is the difference between incremental and disruptive innovation?**
Incremental innovation improves existing products or processes in small steps. Disruptive innovation creates a product or model that changes how a market works.

**Which content point in 5.8 is set at AO3?**
The importance of research and development for a business. The other three points are set at AO2.
