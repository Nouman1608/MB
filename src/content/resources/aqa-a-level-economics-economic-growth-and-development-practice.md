## Questions

**1.** Distinguish between economic growth and economic development. **[3]**

**2.** In illustrative Country P, real GDP rises by 4% and population rises by 1.5% in a year. Calculate the growth rate of real GDP per capita, to two decimal places. **[3]**

**3.** The three dimension indices for Country Q are health 0.70, education 0.55 and income 0.60.

**(a)** Calculate an HDI-style value using a geometric mean, to three decimal places. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why a geometric mean is used rather than a simple average. **[2]**

**4.** Explain two characteristics that are common in less-developed economies. **[4]**

**5.** A firm in Country R plans a project costing 800 that earns 96 a year. Officials demand informal payments of 24 a year. The firm can earn 11% elsewhere.

**(a)** Calculate the return before the payments. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the return after the payments. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain what this shows about the effect of corruption on growth. **[2]**

**6.** Country S has GDP of 40,000m and wants growth of 5%. Its capital-output ratio is 3.5 and domestic saving is 12% of GDP.

**(a)** Calculate the investment needed, as a share of GDP and in millions. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the shortfall in millions. **[2]**
**(c)** If 20% of any aid is lost to leakage, calculate the aid needed to close the shortfall. **[2]**

**7.** A trainee in Country T pays 3,000 in total costs for a course (fees and lost earnings). Afterwards, earnings are 450 a year higher.

**(a)** Calculate the simple annual return and the payback period. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why a government might subsidise training even when the return is high. **[2]**

**8.** Compare market-based and interventionist strategies for promoting growth in a low-income economy. **[6]**

**9.** In Country U, one commodity is 60% of export revenue and exports are 20% of GDP. The commodity's world price falls 25%, with the volume exported unchanged.

**(a)** Calculate the fall in total export revenue. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the direct effect on GDP, as a percentage of GDP. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain two policies that could reduce the economy's vulnerability. **[4]**

**10.** Evaluate the view that aid is the most effective way to promote development. **[9]**

**11.** Farmers in Country V sell a crop in the city for 50 a unit. Transport costs 12 a unit. A new road cuts this to 5. Price elasticity of supply is 1.2.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in the farm-gate price and in output, to one decimal place. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the private sector may under-provide such a road. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Economic growth is a rise in the value of real output across time, tracked through real GDP or real GDP per head [1]. Development means a broader gain in how people live, taking in health, schooling, poverty and personal freedoms [1]. An economy can grow while health, education and the position of the poor change little, for example when growth is concentrated in one mining sector [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers gain credit when both terms are defined and the difference is shown with a contrast; two definitions with no link between them are weaker.

**2.** Per capita growth is approximately real GDP growth divided by population growth [1]. 1.04 / 1.015 = 1.02463 [1]. **2.46%** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Subtracting gives 2.5%, which is close but not the figure asked for to two decimal places; show the ratio method when precision is requested.

**3. (a)** Product = 0.70 x 0.55 x 0.60 = 0.231 [1]. Cube root = **0.614** [1].
**(b)** A geometric mean is lower when the dimensions are unbalanced [1], so a high value in one dimension cannot fully make up for a low value in another [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) is rewarded for the effect of imbalance. Saying only "it is more accurate" gives little to credit.

**4.** Any two, each explained. Low productivity agriculture: a large share of workers in farming, where output per worker is low, keeps average incomes low [1] and limits tax revenue and saving [1]. Dependence on a few primary exports: export earnings are volatile as commodity prices change [1], which makes investment and government budgets unstable [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each characteristic needs a consequence. A bare list of features earns less than two that are developed.

**5. (a)** 96 / 800 = **12%** [1].
**(b)** Net income = 96 - 24 = 72 [1]. 72 / 800 = **9%** [1].
**(c)** The net return of 9% is below the alternative of 11%, so the firm does not invest [1]. Corruption acts like a tax on investment, lowering capital formation and future productive capacity and growth [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Compare the net return with the alternative explicitly; the conclusion that the project is not built is the point of the calculation.

**6. (a)** Investment share = 5 x 3.5 = **17.5%** of GDP [1]. 0.175 x 40,000 = **7,000m** [1].
**(b)** Saving = 0.12 x 40,000 = 4,800m [1]. Shortfall = 7,000 - 4,800 = **2,200m** [1].
**(c)** Only 80% of aid arrives, so aid needed = 2,200 / 0.8 [1] = **2,750m** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Dividing by 0.8 rather than multiplying by 1.2 is the usual slip; check that 80% of your answer returns the shortfall.

**7. (a)** Return = 450 / 3,000 = **15%** a year [1]. Payback = 3,000 / 450 = 6.67 years [1], about **6.7 years** [1].
**(b)** Individuals may be unable to borrow or afford fees and lost earnings, so they under-invest [1]. Training also gives external benefits such as higher productivity for other workers and better health, which are not reflected in private returns [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A strong answer links the subsidy to a market failure (credit constraints or externalities), not just to the idea that education is good.

**8.** Market-based: trade liberalisation, privatisation and secure property rights raise competition and incentives [1]. Private firms respond to prices and may invest more efficiently [1]. Weakness: public goods, equity and the environment may be neglected [1]. Interventionist: the state can build roads, schools and clinics that firms will not provide [1], and protect infant industries while they grow [1]. Weakness: government failure, corruption and poor information may waste funds. Judgement: success depends on institutions and state capacity, and many strategies combine both [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison with a reasoned judgement tends to do better than two separate lists.

**9. (a)** Fall = 0.60 x 25% [1] = **15%** [1].
**(b)** 15% x 20% of GDP [1] = **3% of GDP** [1].
**(c)** Diversification, such as subsidising or training for manufacturing exports, reduces dependence on one price [1] but takes time and may need investment finance [1]. A stabilisation scheme or reserve fund saves in high-price years and spends in low-price years [1], but needs good governance and funds to be kept safe [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Each policy should be tied to the vulnerability described, and the limit of the policy adds the evaluation.

**10.** Case for aid: it can fill the gap between required and domestic investment [1], fund public goods like health and schooling that markets under-provide [1], and give urgent relief in a crisis [1]. Case against: leakage through corruption cuts what reaches projects [1], tied aid raises costs [1], dependency can reduce incentives to raise tax revenue [1], and loans add to debt [1]. Compare with trade: export growth can raise incomes without donor conditions, but needs infrastructure and open markets [1]. Judgement: aid works best when it is untied, targeted at health, schooling or infrastructure and used in a country with good institutions; it is rarely sufficient alone [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation gains credit when the judgement states the conditions under which aid is more or less effective, rather than a general "it depends".

**11. (a)** Farm-gate price before = 50 - 12 = 38 and after = 50 - 5 = 45 [1]. Change = 45 / 38 - 1 = **18.4%** [1]. Output change = 1.2 x 18.4% = **22.1%** [1].
**(b)** Roads are costly and benefit many users who do not pay, so private firms cannot easily charge for the full benefit [1]. Free riders and positive externalities make returns to a private builder low relative to social benefit [1]. The government may therefore need to provide or fund the road [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Percentage change must be taken on the original farm-gate price, not on the market price; this is the common error.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating growth and development as synonyms.
- Subtracting growth rates when exact per capita growth is asked.
- Stating that HDI includes inequality or the environment.
- Listing a characteristic without saying what it does to income or investment.
- Skipping the comparison step in a calculation, such as net return against the alternative.
- Writing one-sided verdicts about aid or trade with no conditions.
- Describing a policy but not its limit, so evaluation is missing.
- Ignoring the context given in the question.
