These notes cover the topic **Political power and action** in the Cambridge IGCSE Global Perspectives (0457) syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027, Version 2 (published April 2025), sections 2 to 4. The topic list applies to all three components: the Written Exam (Component 1), the Individual Report (Component 2) and the Team Project (Component 3). **Knowledge of content is not assessed.** These notes show how to use the topic to practise the assessed skills: research, analysis, evaluation, reflection, communication and collaboration.

Test yourself afterwards with the [practice questions](/resources/igcse-global-perspectives-0457-political-power-and-action-practice/). Course overview: [hub](/boards/cambridge/igcse/global-perspectives/) and [checklist](/checklists/cambridge/igcse/global-perspectives/).

## Real examples at a glance

Use these to show perspectives and courses of action. They are context, not content you will be tested on.

| Example | Level | What happened | One evaluation point |
|---|---|---|---|
| UN Security Council veto | Global | Five permanent members (China, France, Russia, UK, US) can each block a resolution | Supporters say it keeps major powers involved; critics say it lets one state block action |
| Compulsory voting, Australia | National | Required in federal elections since 1924 | High turnout, but compulsion does not guarantee informed votes |
| Votes at 16, Austria | National | Voting age lowered to 16 for national elections in 2007 | Years of data, but results may not transfer to other countries |
| Votes at 16, Scotland | National | 16-year-olds vote in Scottish Parliament and local elections | Supporters cite it as a working model; critics ask whether early turnout lasts as voters get older |
| Popular initiative, Switzerland | National | 100,000 signatures in 18 months can force a national vote on a constitutional change | Direct power for citizens, but critics argue well-funded campaigns find signatures easier |
| Petitions, UK Parliament | National | Government response at 10,000 signatures; debate considered at 100,000 | Open and cheap, but a debate is not a decision |
| Participatory budgeting, Porto Alegre | Local | Residents helped decide spending from 1989 | Direct power, but participants may not represent everyone |

## Judgement words for Component 1

The syllabus lists eight: **bias, claim, fact, generalisation, opinion, prediction, value, vested interest**.

- **Fact v opinion:** a fact can be checked ("turnout was 48%"); an opinion cannot ("the result was a disgrace").
- **Claim v fact:** a claim is put forward as true but still needs evidence ("votes at 16 build a voting habit").
- **Prediction:** a claim about the future ("turnout will rise next time"). It cannot yet be checked.
- **Generalisation:** a claim stretched to a whole group ("young people don't care about politics").
- **Value:** what someone believes matters (freedom, equality, order). Values explain why perspectives differ.
- **Vested interest v bias:** a vested interest is what the author gains; bias is one-sided presentation. A vested interest makes bias more likely, but you still have to show it in the source.

## Perspectives map: one issue, three levels

Issue: *should young people have more direct power over local decisions?*

| Level | Who | Likely perspective | Why they hold it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Personal | A 17-year-old student | Wants a say on spending that affects her | Values fairness; directly affected |
| Local | A councillor | Supports consultation but wants final control | Elected and accountable for the budget |
| National | A government minister | Cautious about changing who can vote | Values consistency in the law across the country |
| Global | An international youth organisation | Supports wider youth participation | Its purpose is to promote young people's voice |

## Must-know distinctions

- **Topic v issue v question.** Topic: political power and action. Issue: whether voting should be compulsory. Question: "Should voting be compulsory in democratic countries?"
- **Perspective v opinion.** A perspective rests on evidence and reasoning; a bare opinion does not.
- **Global v local issue.** The Individual Report needs a global issue with local and/or national and global perspectives. The Team Project needs a local issue.
- **Intention v action.** "82% say they will vote" is not the same as 82% voting.
- **Participation v influence.** Signing a petition is participation; whether it changes a decision is a separate question.
- **Analysing an argument v agreeing with it.** In Question 3 you judge how well an argument is supported, not whether you like its conclusion.

## Mini essay plan: assessing actions (Question 4 style)

Task: *a fictional town's young people rarely vote in local elections. Assess which action would best increase their participation.*

1. **Set out the issue** from the sources: who is affected, how big the problem is, and why it matters (legitimacy of the council).
2. **Action one: votes at 16 for local elections.** Evidence for (new voters, habit-forming). Evidence against (overall turnout can fall when a low-turnout group is added). Who benefits.
3. **Action two: a youth council with a real budget.** Strength: direct, visible results. Limit: only a few young people take part.
4. **Action three: easier registration and school sessions on how to vote.** Strength: cheap, removes a practical barrier. Limit: depends on schools' time.
5. **Compare** against the same criteria: effectiveness, cost, fairness, sustainability.
6. **Judgement:** choose one, explain why it beats the others, and state one condition under which you would change your mind.

The real exam marks extended answers with levels of response. A clear, supported judgement that weighs actions against each other reaches the top levels; a list of actions does not.

## Quick self-test

1. What does the syllabus say about knowledge of content?
2. Give the syllabus's meaning of "perspective".
3. Name the eight terms Component 1 says candidates will benefit from understanding.
4. Is "If the voting age is lowered, turnout will rise" a fact, prediction or opinion?
5. What is the difference between a vested interest and bias?
6. Which assessment objective is not assessed in the Written Exam?
7. Name one country that lowered the voting age to 16 for national elections, and the year.
8. Why is "Democracy" a weak Individual Report title?
9. What kind of issue must a Team Project address?
10. Give one limitation of participatory budgeting as a course of action.
11. Why is a survey of intentions weaker evidence of turnout than official results?

### Answers

1. Knowledge of content is not assessed in this syllabus.
2. A viewpoint on an issue based on evidence and reasoning.
3. Bias, claim, fact, generalisation, opinion, prediction, value and vested interest.
4. A prediction (it is about the future and cannot yet be checked).
5. A vested interest is what the author stands to gain; bias is one-sided presentation in the source itself.
6. AO2 Reflection (Component 1 is 100% AO1).
7. Austria, in 2007.
8. It is a topic, not a question; it does not divide opinion or point to perspectives.
9. A local issue.
10. Turnout can be low, and those who take part may not represent everyone affected.
11. People often say they will vote and then do not; official results record what actually happened.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing what you know about governments instead of using the sources provided.
- Labelling a source "biased" without quoting the words or data that show the bias.
- Mixing up a claim and a fact: if it still needs evidence, it is a claim.
- Ignoring sample size and who carried out a survey when evaluating research.
- Suggesting a way to test a claim without naming a method, a sample and a comparison.
- Listing courses of action without comparing them against the same criteria.
- Giving a party-political view in place of a judgement about the quality of arguments.
- Choosing an Individual Report question with only one possible answer.
- Planning a Team Project action with no way to measure success.
