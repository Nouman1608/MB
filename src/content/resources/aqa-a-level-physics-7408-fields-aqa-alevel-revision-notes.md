Condensed notes for sub-section 3.7.1 Fields of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408, AS and A-level exams June 2016 onwards). It is A-level only and assessed in Paper 2. For explanations and worked examples use the [fields study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-fields-aqa-alevel/); then try the [fields practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-fields-aqa-alevel-practice/). The whole of section 3.7 is summarised in the [Fields and their consequences revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-fields-and-their-consequences-revision-notes/).

## The core statements

- **Force field**: a region in which a body experiences a **non-contact** force.
- A force field can be represented as a **vector**; its direction is found **by inspection**.
- Sources: **mass** (gravitational), **static charge** (electric), **moving charges** (magnetic).
- **Similarities** (gravity and electrostatics): inverse-square force laws; field lines; potential; equipotential surfaces.
- **Difference**: masses always attract; charges may attract or repel.

## Field strength and direction

| Field | Definition | Direction by inspection | Unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gravitational | g = F/m | towards the mass | N kg⁻¹ |
| Electric | E = F/Q (positive test charge) | away from +, towards − | N C⁻¹ = V m⁻¹ |
| Magnetic | force on moving charge or current | from 3.7.5 rules | T |

**Test object**: a mass or positive charge small enough not to disturb the field.

**Resultant field**: find each magnitude; decide each direction; add as vectors (along a line, add or subtract).

## Field lines

- Arrow = direction of force on test object.
- Line density = field strength.
- Lines never cross.
- **Uniform**: parallel, equally spaced (parallel plates; near Earth's surface over a small region).
- **Radial**: along radii from a point or spherical source.

## Equipotentials

- Surfaces of equal potential, **perpendicular** to field lines.
- **No work** is done moving along one.
- Uniform field: equally spaced planes.
- Radial field: concentric spheres, spacing **increases** outwards for equal steps of V.
- Potential is a **scalar**: add the potentials from several sources directly, with sign.

## Comparison table

| | Gravitational | Electrostatic |
|---|---|---|
| Property | mass m | charge Q |
| Constant | G | 1/4πε₀ |
| F (point sources) | Gm₁m₂/r² | Q₁Q₂/4πε₀r² |
| Radial field strength | GM/r² | Q/4πε₀r² |
| Radial potential | −GM/r | Q/4πε₀r |
| Work | mΔV | QΔV |
| Gradient of V–r | −g | E (magnitude) |
| Area under field–r graph | ΔV | ΔV |
| Can repel? | no | yes |
| Sign of V | always negative | sign of source |

## Neutral points

- Exist only where two fields point in **opposite** directions.
- Two masses: between them, closer to the smaller mass.
- Two **like** charges: between them, closer to the smaller charge.
- Two **unlike** charges: not between them (fields add there); for unequal charges, on the line outside, beyond the smaller charge.
- Method: set the two magnitudes equal, take square roots, solve for distance.
- Earth–Moon: about 0.90 of the separation from the Earth (mass ratio about 81, √81 = 9).

## Zero field versus zero potential

- Midway between **+Q and −Q**: V = 0 (contributions cancel as scalars) but E is not zero (both fields point towards −Q).
- Midway between **+Q and +Q**: E = 0 (fields cancel as vectors) but V is not zero (both potentials positive).
- Between two masses: g can be zero, V never is (all gravitational potentials are negative).

## Scale of the forces

- Electrostatic/gravitational ratio between two protons: about 10³⁶; two electrons: about 10⁴²; two alpha particles: about 10³⁵.
- The separation cancels in the ratio, so it is distance-independent.
- Gravity dominates astronomically because large bodies are electrically neutral overall.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Force field | Region where a body experiences a non-contact force |
| Field strength | Force per unit mass or per unit positive charge |
| Uniform field | Same strength and direction everywhere |
| Radial field | Field directed along radii from a point source |
| Potential | Work done per unit mass or charge bringing it from infinity |
| Equipotential surface | Surface on which potential is constant |
| Neutral point | Point where the resultant field strength is zero |
| Inverse-square law | Force or field proportional to 1/r² |

## Exam traps

- A contact force is not a field force.
- Gravitational field lines point **inwards** to the mass.
- Equipotentials drawn at an angle other than 90° to field lines lose the mark.
- No neutral point between unlike charges.
- Potentials add as scalars; field strengths add as vectors.
- "Both obey inverse-square laws" is a similarity, not a difference.

## Self-test

**1.** Define a force field.
**2.** Name the three sources of force fields given in the specification.
**3.** State two similarities between gravitational and electrostatic forces.
**4.** State the key difference.
**5.** Why can field lines never cross?
**6.** What angle do equipotentials make with field lines?
**7.** How much work is done moving a charge along an equipotential?
**8.** Masses of 2.0 kg and 8.0 kg are 0.60 m apart. How far from the 2.0 kg mass is the neutral point?
**9.** If the distance between two point charges triples, what happens to the force?
**10.** Is there a neutral point between +Q and −Q? Explain.
**11.** Why is gravitational potential always negative?
**12.** Why does gravity dominate on astronomical scales?

## Self-test answers

**1.** A region in which a body experiences a non-contact force.
**2.** Mass, static charge, and moving charges.
**3.** Any two: inverse-square force law; field lines; potential; equipotential surfaces.
**4.** Masses always attract; charges may attract or repel.
**5.** The field has only one direction at each point.
**6.** 90°.
**7.** None.
**8.** √(2/8) = 0.5, so x/(0.60 − x) = 0.5, x = 0.20 m.
**9.** It falls to one-ninth.
**10.** No: between them both fields point towards −Q, so they add.
**11.** Gravity is attractive, so work must be done on a mass to move it to infinity, where V = 0.
**12.** Large bodies have huge mass but are electrically neutral overall.
