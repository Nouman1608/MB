For explanations and worked examples, read the [sexual reproduction study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-sexual-reproduction-cambridge-igcse-biology/). ## Core recall

**Sexual reproduction (16.2.1):** a process involving the **fusion of the nuclei of two gametes** to form a **zygote**, and the production of offspring that are **genetically different from each other**.

**Fertilisation (16.2.2):** the **fusion of the nuclei of gametes**.

| Term | Humans | Flowering plants |
|---|---|---|
| Male gamete | sperm | nucleus in pollen grain |
| Female gamete | egg cell | nucleus in ovule |
| Fertilisation happens | in the oviduct | in the ovule |
| Zygote becomes | embryo | embryo in the seed |

**Order of events:** gametes made → gametes meet → nuclei fuse (fertilisation) → zygote → embryo.

**Why offspring differ:** gametes carry different combinations of alleles, and which gametes fuse is random. Every zygote is a new combination, half from each parent.

## Haploid and diploid -- Extended only

| Nucleus | Sets of chromosomes | Found in |
|---|---|---|
| Haploid | one | gametes |
| Diploid | two | zygote and body cells |

**Rule:** haploid + haploid → diploid.

| Species | Body cell | Gamete | Zygote |
|---|---|---|---|
| Human | 46 | 23 | 46 |
| Fruit fly | 8 | 4 | 8 |
| Garden pea | 14 | 7 | 14 |
| Dog | 78 | 39 | 78 |

**Why gametes are haploid:** so that the zygote has the normal diploid number, and the chromosome number stays constant from generation to generation. Without halving, the number would double every generation.

## Advantages and disadvantages -- Extended only

**Wild population**

- Advantage: **variation**, so some offspring may survive a change in environment (disease, climate, predator), and the population can adapt by natural selection.
- Advantage: seeds and young can **disperse** to new areas, away from competition with parents.
- Disadvantage: usually needs **two parents**; energy and time spent finding a mate.
- Disadvantage: plants may rely on **pollinators** or wind.
- Disadvantage: **slower** than asexual reproduction.

**Crop production**

- Advantage: breeders can **cross varieties** to combine useful features.
- Advantage: variation reduces the chance that **one disease** destroys the whole crop.
- Advantage: **seeds** store and transport well.
- Disadvantage: crop is **less uniform** (yield, size, ripening).
- Disadvantage: a good **combination of features can be lost**.
- Disadvantage: needs **pollination** and time to flower and set seed.

**One-line summary:** sexual reproduction trades **speed and certainty** for **variation**.

## Sexual and asexual side by side

| Feature | Sexual | Asexual |
|---|---|---|
| Parents | usually two | one |
| Gametes and fertilisation | yes | no |
| Offspring | genetically different from each other | genetically identical |
| Speed | slower | faster |
| Population's ability to adapt | higher | lower |

**Chromosome-number method (Extended):** gamete = body cell ÷ 2; zygote = gamete + gamete. Check that the zygote number equals the body-cell number.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Gamete | a sex cell, such as a sperm, an egg cell or the male nucleus of a pollen grain |
| Fertilisation | the fusion of the nuclei of gametes |
| Zygote | the cell formed when two gamete nuclei fuse |
| Embryo | the ball of cells formed when the zygote divides |
| Haploid nucleus | a nucleus containing a single set of chromosomes (Extended) |
| Diploid nucleus | a nucleus containing two sets of chromosomes (Extended) |
| Variation | differences between individuals of the same species |
| Genetically different | having different combinations of alleles |

## Exam traps

- Fusion of **nuclei**, not just gametes or cells.
- Offspring are genetically different **from each other**: use the syllabus phrase.
- The zygote is **diploid**, not haploid.
- Halve **once** for a gamete: 14 → 7.
- Self-pollination and other single-parent gamete fusion are still **sexual**.
- "Discuss" needs both sides and the context named in the question.
- Variation is an advantage only because it can **help some individuals survive**; say how.

## Self-test

**1.** Describe sexual reproduction.

**2.** Define fertilisation.

**3.** Name the cell formed by fertilisation.

**4.** Name the male and female gametes in humans.

**5.** Give two reasons why offspring of the same parents are genetically different.

**6.** *(Extended)* State the meaning of haploid.

**7.** *(Extended)* A horse has 64 chromosomes in its body cells. State the number in a horse egg cell and in a horse zygote.

**8.** *(Extended)* Explain why gametes must be haploid.

**9.** *(Extended)* Give one advantage of sexual reproduction to a wild population.

**10.** *(Extended)* Give one disadvantage of sexual reproduction to crop production.

**11.** *(Extended)* Explain why sexual reproduction helps a population survive a new disease.

**12.** A plant pollinates itself and makes seeds. Is this sexual reproduction? Explain.

### Answers

**1.** The fusion of the nuclei of two gametes to form a zygote, producing offspring that are genetically different from each other.

**2.** The fusion of the nuclei of gametes.

**3.** The zygote.

**4.** Sperm (male) and egg cell (female).

**5.** Gametes carry different combinations of alleles; which gametes fuse is random.

**6.** A nucleus containing a single set of chromosomes.

**7.** Egg cell 32; zygote 64.

**8.** So that when two gametes fuse the zygote has the diploid number, keeping the chromosome number the same in each generation.

**9.** Variation, so some individuals may survive a change in the environment and the population can adapt.

**10.** Any one of: crop less uniform; good combinations of features may be lost; slower; depends on pollination.

**11.** Offspring vary, so some may have alleles that make them resistant; these survive and reproduce.

**12.** Yes. Gametes are made and their nuclei fuse at fertilisation, even though only one plant is involved.
