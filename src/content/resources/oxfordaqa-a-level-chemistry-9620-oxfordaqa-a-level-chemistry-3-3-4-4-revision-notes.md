## Specification in brief

- Production by **partial oxidation of ethene**, and the **hazards** of the process.
- Reactions with **water and alcohols**; **uses** of the products.
- Explain the **high reactivity** of epoxyethane.
- **Equations** and **mechanism** for reactions with water and alcohols.
- **Economic and environmental importance** of products, including **surfactants and antifreeze**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Epoxyethane | C₂H₄O; a three-membered ring of two CH₂ groups and one O |
| Partial oxidation | Adding one O atom per ethene without full combustion |
| Ring strain | Instability caused by bond angles (about 60°) far from the normal angle (about 109.5°) |
| Ring opening | Breaking one ring C–O bond, relieving strain |
| Nucleophile | Lone pair donor (here, the O of water or an alcohol) |
| Ethane-1,2-diol | HOCH₂CH₂OH; product with water; antifreeze |
| Glycol ether | Product with a simple alcohol, such as CH₃OCH₂CH₂OH; solvent |
| Surfactant | Molecule with a non-polar part and a polar part; lowers surface tension, used in detergents |

## Recall: production and hazards

**Equation:** 2CH₂=CH₂ + O₂ → 2C₂H₄O (silver catalyst). Atom economy 100 %.

**Competing reaction:** CH₂=CH₂ + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 2H₂O (much more exothermic).

| Hazard | Reason | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Explosion / fire | Ethene and epoxyethane are flammable; vapour–oxygen mixtures can explode | control the ethene : oxygen ratio |
| Runaway heating | Both reactions exothermic; combustion speeds up as temperature rises | cooling; careful temperature control |
| Toxicity | Epoxyethane is toxic and a carcinogen | sealed plant; leak detection |
| Unwanted reactions | Highly reactive ring | keep away from water and other contaminants in storage |

## Recall: reactivity

1. Bond angles in the ring about **60°**, against about **109.5°** normally → **ring strain** → weak ring C–O bonds.
2. C–O bonds are **polar** → ring carbons δ+.
3. A nucleophile attacks a ring carbon; one C–O bond breaks; strain is relieved.

## Recall: reactions

| Nucleophile | Equation | Product |
|---|---|---|
| H₂O | C₂H₄O + H₂O → HOCH₂CH₂OH | ethane-1,2-diol |
| CH₃OH | C₂H₄O + CH₃OH → CH₃OCH₂CH₂OH | 2-methoxyethanol |
| C₂H₅OH | C₂H₄O + C₂H₅OH → C₂H₅OCH₂CH₂OH | 2-ethoxyethanol |
| HOCH₂CH₂OH | C₂H₄O + HOCH₂CH₂OH → HOCH₂CH₂OCH₂CH₂OH | product still has –OH (chain can grow) |
| ROH (long chain) | ROH + nC₂H₄O → R(OCH₂CH₂)ₙOH | non-ionic surfactant |

**Mechanism (four arrows):**

1. Lone pair on O of H₂O/ROH → ring carbon.
2. Ring C–O bond → ring O (ring opens; O⁻ forms; attacking O becomes O⁺–H).
3. Lone pair on O⁻ → H on O⁺.
4. O⁺–H bond → O⁺ (neutral product).

**To make mainly ethane-1,2-diol:** use a large **excess of water**, so the diol is less likely to meet more epoxyethane.

**Counting units in a surfactant:** n = (Mr product − Mr alcohol) ÷ 44.0.

## Recall: importance of products

- **Ethane-1,2-diol:** antifreeze (lowers the freezing point of engine coolant); monomer for polyesters. Toxic if swallowed, so careful disposal matters.
- **Surfactants:** non-polar tail attracts grease; polar polyether head attracts water; used in detergents. Can harm aquatic life, so ready biodegradability is important.
- **Glycol ethers:** solvents for paints and inks.
- **Economic:** cheap ethene, 100 % atom economy, several high-volume products.

## Exam traps

- Water and alcohols are **nucleophiles**, not electrophiles.
- Reactivity needs **ring strain**, not just polarity.
- Ring angle is **about 60°**.
- Show the charges in the intermediate: **+ on the attacking O**, **− on the former ring O**.
- Products of ring opening all keep an **–OH**.
- Hazards need a **reason**, not just "dangerous".

## Self-test

1. Write the equation for making epoxyethane from ethene.
2. Name the catalyst used.
3. What is the atom economy of this reaction, and why?
4. Give two hazards of the process, each with a reason.
5. Explain why epoxyethane is much more reactive than ethoxyethane (an ordinary ether).
6. Write the equation for the reaction of epoxyethane with water and name the product.
7. Give the structure of the product with propan-1-ol.
8. Describe the first two curly arrows in the reaction of epoxyethane with methanol.
9. Why is a large excess of water used when making ethane-1,2-diol?
10. Explain why ethane-1,2-diol is used in car cooling systems.
11. A surfactant made from dodecan-1-ol (Mr 186.0) has Mr 406.0. How many epoxyethane units are present?
12. Explain how the structure of such a surfactant allows it to remove grease.

### Answers

1. **2CH₂=CH₂ + O₂ → 2C₂H₄O**
2. **Silver.**
3. **100 %**: every reactant atom ends up in the only product.
4. Any two: **explosion/fire** (flammable gases mixed with oxygen); **overheating** (exothermic reactions, combustion speeds up); **toxicity** (epoxyethane is toxic and carcinogenic).
5. Epoxyethane's ring has bond angles of about **60°**, so it is **strained** and its C–O bonds are weak; breaking one relieves strain. Ethoxyethane has **no ring strain**.
6. **C₂H₄O + H₂O → HOCH₂CH₂OH**, **ethane-1,2-diol**.
7. **CH₃CH₂CH₂OCH₂CH₂OH** (2-propoxyethanol).
8. From a **lone pair on the methanol O to a ring carbon**; from the **ring C–O bond to the ring O**.
9. The diol still has –OH groups and can react with more epoxyethane; **excess water** makes reaction with water much more likely.
10. It **lowers the freezing point** of the water in the coolant, so it does not freeze in cold weather.
11. n = (406.0 − 186.0) ÷ 44.0 = **5**.
12. The **non-polar hydrocarbon chain** dissolves in grease; the **polar polyether chain ending in –OH** is attracted to water, so grease is carried into the water.
