These notes condense **1.1.2 Implementing** in OCR A Level Chemistry A (H432), Version 3.1 (May 2026). The three learning outcomes are (a) how to use a wide range of practical apparatus and techniques correctly (HSW4), (b) appropriate units for measurements (M0.0), and (c) presenting observations and data in an appropriate format (HSW8). Section 1.1 is assessed in the written papers.

Worked examples are in the [1.1.2 study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-h432-ocr-a-level-chemistry-1-1-2/). Exam-style questions are in the [1.1.2 practice questions](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-h432-ocr-a-level-chemistry-1-1-2-practice/). For the steps in each technique, use the [1.2.2 apparatus and techniques revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-h432-ocr-a-level-chemistry-1-2-2-revision-notes/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Resolution | The smallest change an instrument can show |
| Meniscus | The curved surface of a liquid in a narrow tube; read the bottom of it for aqueous solutions |
| Parallax error | A reading error from viewing a scale at an angle instead of at eye level |
| Tare (zero) | Set a balance to read zero before weighing |
| Titre | Final burette reading minus initial burette reading |
| Concordant titres | Titres that agree closely, typically within 0.10 cm³ |
| Raw data | Readings as taken from the instrument |
| Processed data | Values calculated from raw data, such as a mean or 1/time |
| Solidus heading | A table heading in the form quantity / unit, such as "mass / g" |
| Qualitative observation | A description of what is seen, heard or smelt, such as a colour change |

## Reading and recording: instrument by instrument

| Instrument | How to read | How to record |
|---|---|---|
| Burette | Bottom of meniscus, eye level, scale reads downwards | 2 d.p., last digit 0 or 5 (e.g. 23.45 cm³) |
| Pipette | Bottom of meniscus on the mark at eye level | The printed volume (e.g. 25.0 cm³) |
| Volumetric flask | Bottom of meniscus on the mark, then invert to mix | The printed volume (e.g. 250.0 cm³) |
| Thermometer (1 °C divisions) | Eye level; bulb fully in liquid, not touching the container | To the nearest 0.5 °C (e.g. 21.5 °C) |
| Balance | Tare first; wait for a steady reading | Every digit shown (e.g. 1.20 g) |
| Gas syringe | At the end of the plunger, once it stops moving | To the scale's resolution |

Trailing zeros matter. 22.60 cm³ says the reading was made to 0.05 cm³; 22.6 cm³ does not.

## Units: the M0.0 checklist

**Conversions.**

```
cm³ → dm³    ÷ 1000            dm³ → m³    ÷ 1000
cm³ → m³     × 10⁻⁶            kPa → Pa    × 1000
J → kJ       ÷ 1000            °C → K      + 273
mol dm⁻³ → g dm⁻³   × M        mg → g      ÷ 1000
```

**Where conversions are needed.**

- **n = cV:** V in dm³ when c is in mol dm⁻³.
- **pV = nRT:** p in Pa, V in m³, T in K, with R = 8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹.
- **q = mcΔT:** q comes out in J; divide by 1000 before quoting ΔH in kJ mol⁻¹.
- **ΔG = ΔH − TΔS:** ΔS is usually in J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ and ΔH in kJ mol⁻¹; convert ΔS to kJ first.

**Units of Kc.** Substitute mol dm⁻³ for each concentration and cancel.

| Equilibrium | Kc expression | Units |
|---|---|---|
| H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI | [HI]² ÷ ([H₂][I₂]) | none |
| 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃ | [SO₃]² ÷ ([SO₂]²[O₂]) | dm³ mol⁻¹ |
| N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ | [NH₃]² ÷ ([N₂][H₂]³) | mol⁻² dm⁶ |

**Units of a rate constant.** Divide the rate units (mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹) by the concentration terms.

| Overall order | Units of k |
|---|---|
| 1 | s⁻¹ |
| 2 | dm³ mol⁻¹ s⁻¹ |
| 3 | dm⁶ mol⁻² s⁻¹ |

## Presenting data: the table checklist

1. Independent variable in the first column.
2. Headings as quantity / unit.
3. No units in the body.
4. Same number of decimal places down each column, matching the resolution.
5. Raw and processed data in separate, labelled columns.
6. Titration tables: final reading, initial reading and titre for the rough and each accurate run.

## Presenting observations

- Record what you see, not what it means.
- Colour change: give **both** colours, "from orange to green".
- Precipitate: give its colour and the word "precipitate".
- Gas: "effervescence" or "bubbles", plus the result of any test on the gas.
- Temperature: give the readings, not "it got hot".
- With "a few drops" then "excess", record both observations.

## Exam traps

- **Burette to 1 d.p.**, or a second decimal place that is not 0 or 5.
- **Dropped trailing zeros** in readings or titres.
- **cm³ in pV = nRT.** Convert to m³ (× 10⁻⁶).
- **kJ and J mixed** in ΔG = ΔH − TΔS.
- **Units of Kc guessed** instead of worked out; some equilibria have no units.
- **Units in table cells** instead of in the heading.
- **Conclusions written as observations.**
- **Final colour only** in a colour change.

## Quick self-test

1. The bottom of the meniscus is exactly on the 15.3 cm³ mark of a burette. Record the reading.
2. Initial burette reading 1.15 cm³, final reading 24.70 cm³. Find the titre.
3. Convert 37.5 cm³ to dm³.
4. Express 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide (M = 40.0 g mol⁻¹) in g dm⁻³.
5. Give the units of Kc for H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g).
6. Give the units of Kc for 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g).
7. Give the units of k for rate = k[A]².
8. Give the units of k for a first-order reaction.
9. Convert ΔS = −120 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ into kJ K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.
10. Convert 150 kPa and 250 cm³ into the units needed for pV = nRT.
11. Rewrite "it fizzed and went clear" as a precise observation for a solid carbonate added to acid.
12. Write a correct table heading for times measured in seconds.

### Answers

1. **15.30 cm³**.
2. 24.70 − 1.15 = **23.55 cm³**.
3. 37.5 ÷ 1000 = **0.0375 dm³**.
4. 0.0500 × 40.0 = **2.00 g dm⁻³**.
5. **No units**: (mol dm⁻³)² ÷ (mol dm⁻³)² cancels.
6. (mol dm⁻³)² ÷ (mol dm⁻³)³ = (mol dm⁻³)⁻¹ = **dm³ mol⁻¹**.
7. mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ ÷ (mol dm⁻³)² = **dm³ mol⁻¹ s⁻¹**.
8. **s⁻¹**.
9. **−0.120 kJ K⁻¹ mol⁻¹**.
10. **1.50 × 10⁵ Pa** and **2.50 × 10⁻⁴ m³**.
11. "Effervescence; the solid dissolved to give a colourless solution." (Add the limewater result if the gas was tested.)
12. **time / s**.
