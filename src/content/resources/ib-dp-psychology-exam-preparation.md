The [assessment revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-psychology-assessment-revision-notes/) set out what
each paper weighs and tests. These notes turn that into an exam-day plan -- integrating the three
approaches on Paper 1, preparing both HL options equally for Paper 2, HL Paper 3's separate
methodology focus -- with a worked example and a checklist, alongside the
[full syllabus guide](/resources/ib-dp-psychology-syllabus-guide/) already on the site.

## Paper 1 (50% SL / 40% HL): integrate the three approaches, don't answer from one alone

Paper 1's core-approaches essay specifically rewards integrating the biological, cognitive and
sociocultural approaches where relevant to the question, rather than answering from a single
approach in isolation. **Exam-preparation priority**: when planning a Paper 1 essay, deliberately
check whether at least two of the three approaches can support the argument before writing, since
essays that stay inside one approach lose marks even when well-structured and factually accurate.

## Paper 2 (25% SL / 20% HL): prepare both HL options equally

SL students study one option and answer one essay question on it. HL students study two options,
and Paper 2 requires one essay question from *each* of the two studied options, not a single essay
covering one option only. **Exam-preparation priority**: because option content roughly doubles in
hours between SL (20) and HL (40), HL students should revise their second option with the same depth
as their first, rather than treating it as a lighter add-on -- Paper 2 allocates equal marks to both.

## HL Paper 3 (20%): methodology is its own revision strand, not an extension of Papers 1-2

Paper 3 tests research methodology specifically, requiring HL students to analyse
qualitative/quantitative methods and evaluate research scenarios methodologically and ethically.
**Exam-preparation priority**: because this is content and skill genuinely separate from Papers 1-2's
focus on approaches and options, revise Paper 3 as its own strand -- reviewing methodological
strengths and weaknesses of specific study designs and ethical considerations directly -- rather than
assuming strong Paper 1/2 preparation transfers automatically.

## The internal assessment: the write-up must be individual, even though the study usually isn't

The experimental study is usually designed and carried out in a small group, but the write-up,
analysis and discussion must be entirely the student's own individual work. **Exam-preparation
priority**: when drafting the IA, avoid closely mirroring a group member's structure or wording even
where the underlying data and design are shared, since close similarity risks being flagged during
external moderation -- the analysis, discussion and evaluation should reflect the individual
student's own critical thinking about the study.

## Command terms and how each paper uses them

**Outline**, **describe** and **explain** call for content-based recall and understanding.
**Evaluate**, **discuss** and **contrast** require a reasoned judgement comparing theories, studies
or approaches. **To what extent** expects a weighed, evidence-based conclusion rather than a
one-sided answer.

## Worked practice scenario: an evaluate-style Paper 1 answer plan

Question: "Evaluate one theory of the biological approach to explaining behaviour."

```
Step 1 - state the theory clearly and its key claim (biological approach):
[named theory] proposes that [named behaviour] can be explained by
[named biological mechanism, e.g. hormone, brain structure, genetics].

Step 2 - support with study evidence:
Cite a named study supporting the theory, briefly describing its
method and finding.

Step 3 - evaluate using a second approach (integration, not repetition):
Contrast with a cognitive or sociocultural explanation of the same
behaviour -- does it compete with, or complement, the biological
explanation?

Step 4 - evaluate methodologically:
Comment on a strength or limitation of the supporting study's method
(e.g. correlational vs experimental design, ecological validity,
sample generalisability) -- distinct from simply restating findings.

Step 5 - conclude with a judgement:
State, briefly, how convincing the theory is overall, given both the
supporting evidence and its limitations.
```

This five-step structure keeps the answer from staying inside a single approach (Step 3) and from
stopping at description (Steps 4-5) -- exactly the combination "evaluate" is testing, and the same
structure that Paper 2's option essays and HL Paper 3's methodological evaluations reuse.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exam**: practise Paper 1 essay plans that deliberately bring in a second approach;
  HL students should revise both Paper 2 options with equal depth, not just the first one studied;
  HL students should revise Paper 3 methodology as its own strand; check the IA write-up for
  independent wording and analysis distinct from any group member's version.
- **During Paper 1**: before finishing an essay, check whether a second approach has been brought in
  to support or contrast the argument.
- **During Paper 2**: confirm which two options you are answering on (HL) and allocate time evenly
  between them.
- **During HL Paper 3**: treat each research scenario methodologically and ethically, not just
  descriptively.
- **On every paper**: match the response to the command term -- "outline" is not "evaluate."

## Self-test

1. What must a strong Paper 1 essay integrate, beyond a single approach?
2. How many options does an HL student study for Paper 2, and how many essay questions does Paper 2
   require from them?
3. Why must the internal assessment write-up be individual, even though the experiment is often
   conducted in a group?
4. What does HL Paper 3 specifically test, that Papers 1 and 2 do not?

**Answers:** 1. At least one other approach (biological, cognitive or sociocultural) beyond the one
the essay is primarily framed around, since Paper 1 rewards integration rather than a single-approach
answer. 2. Two options; one essay question from *each* of the two studied options, not a single essay
covering one option only. 3. Because the write-up, analysis and discussion must reflect the
individual student's own work; closely mirroring a group member's write-up risks being flagged during
external moderation, even where the underlying study was conducted together. 4. Research
methodology -- analysing qualitative versus quantitative methods and evaluating research scenarios
methodologically and ethically, as a distinct skill from applying content knowledge.
