## Section B

**7.** *(Extended)* Explain, in terms of particles, why increasing the temperature increases the rate of a reaction. **[3]**

**8.** A student reacted calcium carbonate chips with excess dilute hydrochloric acid in a flask on a balance. The flask had a cotton wool plug.

| Time / min | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mass lost / g | 0.00 | 0.18 | 0.29 | 0.36 | 0.40 | 0.42 | 0.42 |

**(a)** Write the balanced equation for the reaction, with state symbols. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the mass of the flask and contents decreases. **[1]**

**(c)** State the purpose of the cotton wool plug. **[1]**

**(d)** Calculate the mean rate of mass loss over the first 2 minutes. Give the unit. **[2]**

**(e)** State the time at which the reaction stopped and explain why it stopped. **[2]**

**9.** *(Extended)* Explain why the rate of reaction between magnesium and dilute sulfuric acid decreases as the reaction proceeds. **[2]**

**10.** *(Extended)* A student wants to measure the rate of the reaction between magnesium ribbon and hydrochloric acid by measuring the loss in mass of the flask.

**(a)** Evaluate this method. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest a better method and give one reason it is better than collecting the gas over water. **[2]**

**11.** In experiment 1, 0.12 g of magnesium ribbon reacted with excess dilute hydrochloric acid at 20 °C; 120 cm³ of hydrogen was collected in total, and 72 cm³ had been collected after 30 s.

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of hydrogen production over the first 30 s. **[2]**

**(b)** Experiment 2 used 0.12 g of magnesium **powder** under the same conditions. Describe how the volume–time curve for experiment 2 compares with experiment 1. **[2]**

**(c)** Experiment 3 used 0.06 g of magnesium ribbon under the same conditions as experiment 1. State the total volume of hydrogen collected. **[1]**

**12.** *(Extended)* Hydrogen peroxide decomposes slowly at room temperature. Adding a small amount of a solid catalyst makes oxygen form quickly. Describe how you could show that the solid is a catalyst and not a reactant. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Rate decreases [1].

**(b)** Rate decreases [1].

**2.** Its mass is unchanged / it is still present [1]; it is chemically unchanged [1].

**3.** **B** [1]. Pressure affects only reactions involving gaseous reactants; neither reactant here is a gas.

**4.** The minimum energy colliding particles must have in order to react [1].

**5. (a)** Gas syringe (or upturned measuring cylinder / burette over water) [1].

**(b)** Stop-watch [1].

**6.** It decreases (lowers) the activation energy [1].

**7.** Particles gain kinetic energy and move faster [1]; collisions are more frequent [1]; a greater proportion of collisions have energy equal to or greater than the activation energy [1].

**8. (a)** CaCO₃(s) + 2HCl(aq) → CaCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g): correct formulae [1]; balanced with correct state symbols [1].

**(b)** Carbon dioxide gas is produced and escapes from the flask [1].

**(c)** Lets gas escape but stops acid spray / liquid being lost from the flask [1].

**(d)** 0.29 ÷ 2 [1] = 0.145 g/min (accept 0.15 g/min) with unit [1].

**(e)** At 5 minutes (mass loss stops changing) [1]; the calcium carbonate has been used up / it is the limiting reactant [1].

**9.** The concentration of acid decreases as it is used up / magnesium is used up [1]; so there are fewer particles per unit volume and collisions are less frequent [1].

**10. (a)** Hydrogen has a very low mass, so the mass lost is very small [1]; a typical balance cannot measure such a small change accurately, so the results would be unreliable [1].

**(b)** Collect the gas in a gas syringe and record the volume at regular intervals [1]; a syringe is more precise / no gas is lost by dissolving in water / easier to read [1].

**11. (a)** 72 ÷ 30 [1] = 2.4 cm³/s [1].

**(b)** Steeper at the start / faster initial rate [1]; levels off sooner at the same final volume of 120 cm³ [1].

**(c)** 60 cm³ [1].

**12.** Weigh the solid before adding it [1]; at the end, filter off the solid, wash and dry it [1]; show its mass is unchanged (and that it speeds up a fresh sample of hydrogen peroxide again) [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "more collisions" instead of "more frequent collisions" in an explanation.
- Giving only the collision-frequency point for temperature and leaving out the activation-energy point.
- Saying a faster reaction gives "more product" when the amount of limiting reactant is unchanged.
- Leaving out the unit on a mean rate, or using the wrong one (g/min when the data are in seconds).
- Missing carbon dioxide or the state symbols in the calcium carbonate equation.
- Describing a mass-loss experiment for a reaction that gives hydrogen without commenting on the tiny mass change.
