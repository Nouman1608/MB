## Questions

**1.** Define economic growth. **[1]**

**2.** State the difference between GDP and real GDP. **[2]**

**3.** Country S had a nominal GDP of 720 billion units in Year 1 and 792 billion units in Year 2. Prices rose by 6% between the two years.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in nominal GDP. **[2]**
**(b)** Estimate the rate of real economic growth. **[1]**

**4.** Country T has a GDP of 96 billion units and a population of 3.2 million. Country V has a GDP of 450 billion units and a population of 25 million.
**(a)** Calculate GDP per capita for each country. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain which country is likely to have the higher average standard of living. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **two** causes of economic growth. **[4]**

**6.** Explain one cost of economic growth for people living in a fast-growing city. **[3]**

**7.** Country W has 24.0 million people in work and 1.2 million unemployed. Calculate the unemployment rate to one decimal place. **[2]**

**8.** Identify the type of unemployment in each case.
**(a)** A ski instructor without work in summer. **[1]**
**(b)** A steelworker whose plant closed after demand moved to imported steel. **[1]**
**(c)** A nurse who has left one hospital and is a month away from starting at another. **[1]**
**(d)** Shop workers laid off across the country during a recession. **[1]**

**9.** Explain why the claimant count may give a lower figure for unemployment than a survey of households. **[2]**

**10.** Explain **two** consequences of high unemployment for the government. **[4]**

**11.** Explain how investment in education and training could reduce structural unemployment. **[3]**

**12.** Country X has rising cyclical unemployment and low inflation. The government is considering either cutting interest rates or increasing spending on new roads. Evaluate which policy is more likely to reduce unemployment. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** An increase in the quantity of goods and services produced by an economy over time (an increase in real GDP) [1]. **[1]**

**2.** GDP is the total value of goods and services produced in a year at current prices [1]; real GDP is adjusted for inflation so it measures changes in output only [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** (792 − 720) ÷ 720 × 100 [1] = **10%** [1].
**(b)** About 10% − 6% = **4%** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the formula. A precise method (792 ÷ 1.06 = 747.2, growth 3.8%) also earns the mark.

**4. (a)** T: 96 billion ÷ 3.2 million = **30,000** [1]. V: 450 billion ÷ 25 million = **18,000** [1].
**(b)** Country T [1], because each person has a larger share of output on average, even though V's total GDP is bigger [1]. **[4]**

**5.** Any two, each with development: more investment in capital such as machinery [1], which lets workers produce more output per hour [1]; a larger or better-skilled workforce [1], which raises the amount the economy can produce [1]; new technology [1], so more is produced from the same inputs [1]; higher spending such as exports [1], so firms raise output to meet demand [1]. **[4]**

**6.** More traffic, pollution or housing pressure [1]; for example, more production and car use raise air pollution [1], which can harm residents' health, so their quality of life may fall even as incomes rise [1]. **[3]**

**7.** Labour force = 24.0 + 1.2 = 25.2 million [1]. Rate = 1.2 ÷ 25.2 × 100 = **4.8%** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Dividing by 24.0 (the employed only) is the most common error and gives 5.0%.

**8. (a)** Seasonal [1]. **(b)** Structural [1]. **(c)** Frictional [1]. **(d)** Cyclical [1]. **[4]**

**9.** The claimant count only includes people claiming unemployment-related benefits [1]; people seeking work who are not eligible for, or choose not to claim, benefits are left out, but a survey would count them [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Any two developed points: tax revenue falls [1] because unemployed people pay less income tax and spend less, so less VAT is collected [1]; spending on benefits rises [1], so the government may have to borrow more or cut other spending [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Structural unemployment exists because workers' skills do not match the jobs available [1]. Training gives them skills that growing industries need [1], so they can move into those jobs and the mismatch shrinks [1]. **[3]**

**12.** *Interest-rate cut:*
- Cheaper borrowing encourages households to spend and firms to invest [1], raising demand so firms hire more workers [1].
- Low inflation means there is little risk in raising demand [1]. It can be done quickly [1].
- But if confidence is low, people may not borrow even at lower rates [1].

*Road spending:*
- Creates jobs directly in construction [1] and raises demand further as those workers spend their wages [1].
- It also improves infrastructure, which could raise long-run growth [1].
- But it takes time to plan and increases government borrowing [1].

*Judgement:* A reasoned conclusion, for example that the interest-rate cut acts faster but road spending is more certain to create jobs when confidence is weak, so the best choice depends on business and consumer confidence [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the context: low inflation is the reason demand-side policy is safe here. A conclusion with no reason scores little.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using nominal rather than real GDP to measure growth.
- Calculating the unemployment rate out of the employed or the whole population.
- Naming a type of unemployment without linking it to the cause in the question.
- Writing "unemployment is bad for the economy" with no chain of reasoning.
- Ending a 9-mark answer with a summary instead of a judgement.

## Frequently asked questions

**Should growth be measured with nominal or real GDP?**
Use real GDP, which removes the effect of inflation. Using nominal GDP as evidence that output rose is a common way to lose marks.

**What do you divide by to find the unemployment rate?**
Divide the number unemployed by the labour force, the employed plus the unemployed, not by the number employed or the whole population.

**How should I finish a 9-mark answer in AQA GCSE Economics?**
End with a judgement that answers the question, not a summary of the points already made.
