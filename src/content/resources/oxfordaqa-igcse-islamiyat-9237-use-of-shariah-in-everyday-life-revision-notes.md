**These notes condense content area 3.2.2.7 of 9237: "Following the Qur'an and Sunnah in everyday life, including Halal and Haram, dietary laws and dress laws for men and women. The role of parents including religious upbringing and developing good morals and character."** The full explanation is in the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-islamiyat-9237-use-of-shariah-in-everyday-life/).

## The five grades of action

| Grade | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Wajib / fard | Required | Five daily prayers |
| Mustahabb | Recommended | Voluntary fasts |
| Mubah | Permitted | Choosing a colour of clothes |
| Makruh | Disliked | Wasting water |
| Haram | Forbidden | Pork, alcohol, interest |

## Halal and Haram

- **Halal:** lawful. **Haram:** unlawful.
- Basic rule: everything is halal unless a text forbids it.
- **Doubtful matters:** the Hadith in Bukhari and Muslim says halal is clear, haram is clear, and between them are doubtful things to avoid.
- **Halal earnings:** honest work; no interest, theft, fraud or gambling.
- **Why follow:** obedience to Allah, protection from harm, discipline, a sign of faith.

## Dietary laws

| Forbidden | Source |
|---|---|
| Dead animals, flowing blood, pork, food dedicated to other than Allah | Qur'an 2:173; 5:3 |
| Animals killed by strangling, a blow, a fall or goring | Qur'an 5:3 |
| Alcohol and intoxicants | Qur'an 5:90 |

**Halal slaughter (dhabihah):** healthy animal; name of Allah said; throat cut quickly with a very sharp knife; blood drained.

**Kindness:** Allah has prescribed excellence in everything; sharpen the knife and spare suffering (Muslim).

**Necessity:** forced by hunger, no desire to disobey, no excess (Qur'an 2:173).

**Food of the People of the Book:** permitted (Qur'an 5:5).

**In practice:** reading ingredients (gelatine, alcohol, animal fat); halal certification; Bismillah before eating; right hand; avoiding waste.

## Dress laws

| | Men | Women |
|---|---|---|
| Covering | At least navel to knees (most scholars) | Body except face and hands (most scholars) |
| Qur'an | Lower the gaze, guard modesty (24:30) | Lower the gaze; draw the head covering over the chest (24:31); outer garments (33:59) |
| Other | Silk and gold jewellery forbidden for men in Hadith; no pride in dress | Loose clothing; head covering |

**Both:** modesty is part of faith (Bukhari); clean, simple, dignified dress.

**Reasons:** modesty, dignity, identity, protection, obedience.

**Variety:** the principles are fixed, the styles vary by culture; scholars differ on details such as covering the face.

## Role of parents

| Area | What parents do |
|---|---|
| Religious upbringing | Teach the Shahadah and basics of belief; give a good name; teach prayer from about seven; teach the Qur'an; take children to the mosque; set an example |
| Good morals and character | Teach honesty, kindness, respect, gratitude; use Luqman's advice (31:13-19) as a model |
| Fairness | Treat children justly |
| Responsibility | "Each of you is a shepherd" (Bukhari); "protect yourselves and your families from a Fire" (66:6) |

## The prescribed passage: Sahih al-Bukhari 5027

"The best among you (Muslims) are those who learn the Qur'an and teach it."

| Phrase | Use |
|---|---|
| "The best among you" | Excellence is in knowledge and service, not status |
| "learn the Qur'an" | Following Shariah starts with learning it |
| "teach it" | The duty passes on, especially from parents to children |

## Links between the parts

- **Halal and Haram** are the commands of the Qur'an and Sunnah applied to daily choices.
- **Dietary and dress laws** are the best-known examples.
- **Parents** pass these habits on, so Shariah continues from one generation to the next.

## Model answer: a 6-mark question

**Question:** Explain **two** reasons why Muslims follow dietary laws. Use a source of authority. **[6]**

> Muslims follow dietary laws because they are commands of Allah in the Qur'an [1]. Pork, blood and dead animals are forbidden, so eating halal is a way of obeying Allah [1]. The laws also teach discipline and kindness [1]; halal slaughter requires a sharp knife and the name of Allah, so the animal is treated with respect [1]. Qur'an 5:3 lists the forbidden foods and also allows an exception for severe hunger, which shows that the rules are firm but merciful [2].

## A day in practice

| Time | Shariah in action |
|---|---|
| Morning | Fajr prayer; Bismillah before breakfast; halal food |
| School or work | Honest effort; modest dress; kind speech; avoiding haram earnings |
| Afternoon | Prayers at their times; help for family and neighbours |
| Evening | Family meal; Qur'an recitation; teaching children |

**Point to make:** Shariah is a way of living all of life, not a set of rules for special occasions.

## Exam traps

- Halal and Haram cover income and conduct, not only food.
- Men also have dress rules.
- Say "most scholars" when giving covering rules.
- Include the exception for necessity.
- Connect parents' duties to a verse or Hadith.

## Self-test

1. What does Halal mean?
2. Give two foods forbidden in Qur'an 5:3.
3. Name two conditions of halal slaughter.
4. In which verse are women told to draw their head coverings over their chests?
5. Give one dress rule for men.
6. What is the exception for necessity?
7. Who gave advice to his son in Surah Luqman?
8. What does the Hadith in Bukhari 5027 say about the best of Muslims?
9. Give two ways parents give a religious upbringing.
10. Why do Muslims avoid doubtful matters?

**Answers:** 1. Lawful and permitted. 2. Any two: dead animals; blood; pork; food dedicated to other than Allah. 3. Any two: healthy animal; the name of Allah said; quick cut with a sharp knife; blood drained. 4. Qur'an 24:31. 5. Any one: cover at least navel to knees; no silk or gold; no pride in dress. 6. A person forced by hunger may eat forbidden food without desire and without excess. 7. Luqman. 8. They are those who learn the Qur'an and teach it. 9. Any two: teach prayer; teach the Qur'an; take them to the mosque; give a good example. 10. To protect their religion and honour from sin.
