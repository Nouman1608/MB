## Questions

**1.** Give the number of protons, neutrons and electrons in each species.

**(a)** ²⁷Al³⁺ (atomic number 13) **[3]**
**(b)** ⁷⁹Se²⁻ (atomic number 34) **[3]**

**2.** Carbon-13 and carbon-14 are isotopes of carbon (atomic number 6).

**(a)** State what is the same and what is different in the nuclei of these two isotopes. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the two isotopes react in the same way chemically. **[2]**

**3.** Define each term.

**(a)** relative isotopic mass **[2]**
**(b)** relative atomic mass **[2]**

**4.** A mass spectrum of an element X shows four peaks, from singly charged ions.

| m/z | 64 | 66 | 67 | 68 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Relative abundance (%) | 48.0 | 28.0 | 4.0 | 20.0 |

**(a)** State what the m/z value of each peak represents. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the relative atomic mass of X. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**5.** Element M has two isotopes, of relative isotopic mass 121 and 123. Its relative atomic mass is 121.9. Calculate the percentage abundance of each isotope. **[3]**

**6.** Ar values: P = 31.0, O = 16.0, Al = 27.0, S = 32.1.

**(a)** Calculate the relative molecular mass of P₄O₁₀. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the relative formula mass of Al₂(SO₄)₃. **[2]**
**(c)** State why the term "relative formula mass" is used for Al₂(SO₄)₃ and not "relative molecular mass". **[1]**

**7.** **(a)** Predict the charge on the ion formed by each element: strontium, phosphorus and bromine. **[3]**
**(b)** Write the formula of each compound.

- strontium phosphide **[1]**
- aluminium nitrate **[1]**
- ammonium carbonate **[1]**
- silver sulfate **[1]**

**8.** Give the formula, including the charge, of each ion: **[4]**

nitrate, hydroxide, zinc, ammonium

**9.** Balance each equation and add state symbols where they are missing.

**(a)** C₄H₁₀(g) + O₂(g) → CO₂(g) + H₂O(l) **[2]**
**(b)** Mg(OH)₂(s) + HNO₃(aq) → Mg(NO₃)₂(aq) + H₂O(l) **[2]**
**(c)** Al + HCl → AlCl₃ + H₂, where aluminium is a solid, the acid is aqueous, aluminium chloride is dissolved and hydrogen is a gas **[2]**

**10.** When aqueous lead(II) nitrate is added to aqueous potassium iodide, a yellow precipitate of lead(II) iodide forms. Potassium nitrate remains in solution. The lead(II) ion is Pb²⁺.

**(a)** Write the full balanced equation with state symbols. **[2]**
**(b)** Write the ionic equation with state symbols. **[2]**
**(c)** Identify the spectator ions. **[1]**

**11.** A Group 2 element M has atomic number 38. A sample has the following isotopes, from singly charged ions.

| m/z | 86 | 87 | 88 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relative abundance (%) | 10.0 | 6.0 | 84.0 |

**(a)** Calculate the relative atomic mass of M to 3 significant figures. **[3]**
**(b)** Deduce the number of protons, neutrons and electrons in an ion of the isotope of mass 88 in the compound M(NO₃)₂. **[2]**
**(c)** Use your answer to (a) and the Ar values N = 14.0 and O = 16.0 to calculate the relative formula mass of M(NO₃)₂. **[2]**
**(d)** Aqueous M(NO₃)₂ reacts with aqueous sodium sulfate to give a white precipitate of the sulfate of M. Write the ionic equation with state symbols, using M²⁺ for the metal ion. **[2]**

**12.** Early models of the atom pictured electrons embedded in a sphere of positive charge. In alpha-particle scattering experiments, most alpha particles passed straight through a thin metal foil, and a very small fraction were deflected through large angles.

**(a)** Explain how this evidence led to the model being rejected and replaced. **[3]**
**(b)** Name one phenomenon that a model with electrons in shells can explain. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Protons = atomic number = 13. [1] Neutrons = 27 − 13 = 14. [1] Electrons = 13 − 3 = 10. [1] **13 p, 14 n, 10 e** **[3]**
**(b)** Protons = 34. [1] Neutrons = 79 − 34 = 45. [1] Electrons = 34 + 2 = 36. [1] **34 p, 45 n, 36 e** **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers tend to gain credit for showing the subtraction, and lose it by treating the charge the wrong way round: cations have lost electrons, so they hold fewer electrons than protons, while anions hold extra.

**2. (a)** The number of protons is the same (6). [1] The number of neutrons differs: 7 in carbon-13 and 8 in carbon-14. [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Chemical reactions depend on the electrons, particularly the outer ones. [1] Equal proton numbers mean the two isotopes have the same electron arrangement. [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A reason that mentions electron arrangement or number of electrons tends to be needed. Saying only that they are the same element gives little to build on.

**3. (a)** The mass of an atom of an isotope [1] compared with 1/12th the mass of an atom of carbon-12. [1] **[2]**
**(b)** The mean mass of an atom of the element, weighted by the abundance of each isotope, [1] compared with 1/12th the mass of an atom of carbon-12. [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The two definitions share the same standard, so answers are distinguished by "isotope" in the first and "weighted mean" in the second.

**4. (a)** The relative isotopic mass of that isotope, since the ions carry a single charge. [1] **[1]**
**(b)** Sum of mass × abundance = 64 × 48.0 + 66 × 28.0 + 67 × 4.0 + 68 × 20.0 = 3072 + 1848 + 268 + 1360 = 6548. [1] Divide by the total abundance, 100. [1] 6548 / 100 = 65.48, which is **65.5** to 3 s.f. [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each product. A value outside 64 to 68 signals an error.

**5.** Let the abundance of the 123 isotope be x%. Then the abundance of the 121 isotope is (100 − x)%. [1] (121 × (100 − x) + 123 × x) / 100 = 121.9, so 12100 + 2x = 12190. [1] x = 45, so **45% of the 123 isotope and 55% of the 121 isotope**. [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Define the unknown before solving. A quick check helps: 121.9 is nearer 121 than 123, so the lighter isotope should be the more abundant.

**6. (a)** 4 × 31.0 + 10 × 16.0 = 124.0 + 160.0. [1] **284.0** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** 2 × 27.0 + 3 × (32.1 + 4 × 16.0) = 54.0 + 3 × 96.1. [1] 54.0 + 288.3 = **342.3** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Al₂(SO₄)₃ is an ionic compound with a giant structure, not made of discrete molecules. [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Multiplying the contents of a bracket by the subscript outside it is the point that most often goes wrong. Linking the term to "giant structure" gains credit that "it is ionic" alone may not.

**7. (a)** Strontium is in Group 2: **Sr²⁺**. [1] Phosphorus is in Group 15: **P³⁻**. [1] Bromine is in Group 17: **Br⁻**. [1] **[3]**
**(b)** Sr²⁺ and P³⁻: three Sr²⁺ (+6) balance two P³⁻ (−6): **Sr₃P₂**. [1] Al³⁺ and NO₃⁻: **Al(NO₃)₃**. [1] NH₄⁺ and CO₃²⁻: **(NH₄)₂CO₃**. [1] Ag⁺ and SO₄²⁻: **Ag₂SO₄**. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Brackets are part of the formula. "AlNO33" does not show three nitrate ions, so a formula needs to be unambiguous.

**8.** **NO₃⁻** [1], **OH⁻** [1], **Zn²⁺** [1], **NH₄⁺** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Both formula and charge are asked for, so an uncharged formula such as NO₃ leaves out part of the answer.

**9. (a)** 2C₄H₁₀(g) + 13O₂(g) → 8CO₂(g) + 10H₂O(l) [1] correct carbon and hydrogen balance, [1] oxygen balanced (26 on each side) **[2]**
**(b)** Mg(OH)₂(s) + 2HNO₃(aq) → Mg(NO₃)₂(aq) + 2H₂O(l) [1] for 2HNO₃, [1] for 2H₂O **[2]**
**(c)** 2Al(s) + 6HCl(aq) → 2AlCl₃(aq) + 3H₂(g) [1] balance, [1] state symbols **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Balance by adding coefficients only. Check each element on both sides at the end, particularly oxygen in (a) and hydrogen in (c), and make sure every species carries a state symbol.

**10. (a)** Pb(NO₃)₂(aq) + 2KI(aq) → PbI₂(s) + 2KNO₃(aq) [1] correct formulae, [1] balance and state symbols **[2]**
**(b)** Pb²⁺(aq) + 2I⁻(aq) → PbI₂(s) [1] correct species and states, [1] balance of atoms and charge **[2]**
**(c)** **K⁺ and NO₃⁻** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* The ionic equation must balance in charge as well as atoms: here 2+ and 2− on the left total zero, matching the uncharged solid. Spectator ions are those that appear unchanged on both sides.

**11. (a)** 86 × 10.0 + 87 × 6.0 + 88 × 84.0 = 860 + 522 + 7392 = 8774. [1] 8774 / 100 = 87.74. [1] **87.7** to 3 s.f. [1] **[3]**
**(b)** Protons = 38 and neutrons = 88 − 38 = 50. [1] The ion is M²⁺, so electrons = 38 − 2 = 36. [1] **38 p, 50 n, 36 e** **[2]**
**(c)** M(NO₃)₂: 87.7 + 2 × (14.0 + 3 × 16.0) = 87.7 + 124.0. [1] **211.7** [1] **[2]**
**(d)** M²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) → MSO₄(s) [1] correct species, [1] balance and state symbols **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (c) uses your part (a) value, so write it clearly. In (b), state the 2+ charge, because the compound contains the ion and not the neutral atom.

**12. (a)** Most alpha particles passed straight through, so the atom must be mostly empty space. [1] A few were deflected through large angles, so there must be a small region of concentrated positive charge. [1] An evenly spread positive sphere would not deflect them like this, so the model was rejected and replaced by one with a small, dense, positive nucleus. [1] **[3]**
**(b)** Any one of: periodic properties, such as the repeating pattern in ionisation energies or in reactivity across a period or down a group. [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each observation to a conclusion about the structure. Listing what was seen without saying what it shows tends to gain less credit.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding the charge to the electron count of a cation, or subtracting it for an anion.
- Giving the mass number as the number of neutrons.
- Writing a definition of relative atomic mass without "weighted mean" or without carbon-12.
- Dividing a weighted sum by the number of isotopes instead of the total abundance.
- Not multiplying the contents of a bracket by the subscript when finding relative formula mass.
- Using "relative molecular mass" for an ionic compound.
- Forgetting brackets in formulae such as Al(NO₃)₃ and (NH₄)₂CO₃.
- Writing an ionic equation that balances in atoms but not in charge.
- Omitting state symbols when the question asks for them.
