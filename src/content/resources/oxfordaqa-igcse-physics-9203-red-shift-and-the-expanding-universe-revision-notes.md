These notes condense section 3.8.3 (Red shift and the expanding universe) of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203) specification, for exams May/June 2018 onwards. The section is marked P, so it is examined in the full Physics award only. For explanations and worked data, read the [red shift study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-red-shift-and-the-expanding-universe/); then test yourself with the [red shift practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-red-shift-and-the-expanding-universe-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Doppler effect | A change in the observed wavelength and frequency when a wave source moves relative to an observer |
| Red shift | The observed increase in the wavelength of light from most distant galaxies |
| Big Bang model | The universe began from a very small initial point and has expanded ever since |
| Expanding universe | Space itself is stretching, carrying galaxies apart |
| CMBR | Cosmic microwave background radiation: electromagnetic radiation filling the universe, from radiation present shortly after the beginning |
| Age of the universe | About 14 thousand million years (1.4 × 10¹⁰ years) |

## Doppler effect in one table

| Source | Wavelength observed | Frequency observed |
|---|---|---|
| Moving away | Longer (increases) | Lower (decreases) |
| Moving towards | Shorter (decreases) | Higher (increases) |
| Not moving relative to observer | Same as emitted | Same as emitted |

- Works for any wave source: the specification names light, sound and microwaves.
- The wave speed does not change. Use wave speed = frequency × wavelength to link the two changes.
- **Why:** a source moving towards you emits each crest from a point closer to you, so crests bunch up; a receding source spreads them out.

**Worked reminder.** A source emits 500 Hz sound; sound travels at 340 m/s. Emitted wavelength = 340 ÷ 500 = 0.680 m. Moving away, an observer hears 470 Hz: observed wavelength = 340 ÷ 470 = 0.723 m, an increase of 0.043 m.

## Red shift observations to learn word for word

1. There is an observed increase in the wavelength of light from most distant galaxies.
2. The further away the galaxies, the faster they are moving.
3. The further away the galaxies, the bigger the observed increase in wavelength.

How it is measured: elements produce light at fixed wavelengths measured on Earth. The same pattern appears in light from a galaxy, but shifted to longer wavelengths. Shift = observed wavelength − laboratory wavelength.

## Evidence chain for the Big Bang

1. Red shift shows distant galaxies are moving away.
2. More distant galaxies are moving away faster.
3. This suggests space itself is expanding.
4. So the universe was smaller in the past.
5. Going back far enough, it began from a very small initial point: the Big Bang.

Then add the second line of evidence:

6. CMBR fills the universe; it comes from radiation present shortly after the beginning.
7. The Big Bang theory is currently the only theory that can explain the existence of CMBR.

## Which evidence shows what?

| Question asks about... | Use |
|---|---|
| Galaxies moving apart | Red shift |
| Space expanding | Red shift increasing with distance |
| A hot, early universe that left radiation behind | CMBR |
| Why the Big Bang is preferred over other theories | CMBR: only the Big Bang currently explains it |

## Exam traps

- "Red-shifted galaxies look red." Their wavelengths are longer; colour is not the point.
- "The speed of light changes." Only wavelength and frequency change.
- "Moving away gives higher frequency." Opposite: lower frequency.
- "We are at the centre of the expansion." Every observer sees the same pattern.
- "Red shift proves the Big Bang." It supports the model.
- "CMBR is starlight" or "CMBR is sound". It is microwave electromagnetic radiation left from shortly after the beginning.
- "14 million years." It is 14 thousand million years.
- Giving only red shift when the question asks about two kinds of evidence.

## Quick self-test

1. Define the Doppler effect.
2. A source of microwaves moves towards a detector. What happens to the observed wavelength and frequency?
3. What is meant by red shift?
4. What does a larger red shift tell you about a galaxy's motion?
5. How is a galaxy's distance related to the size of its red shift?
6. A line has a laboratory wavelength of 486.1 nm. In a galaxy's light it is measured at 489.5 nm. Calculate the red shift as a change in wavelength.
7. Why does the observed red shift suggest that space itself is expanding?
8. Explain how the expansion leads to the idea of a Big Bang.
9. What is CMBR?
10. Why is CMBR important evidence for the Big Bang theory?
11. How old is the universe thought to be?
12. A car horn emits 400 Hz; sound travels at 340 m/s. Calculate the wavelength heard by a listener the car is approaching if the listener hears 425 Hz.

### Answers

1. A change in the observed wavelength and frequency of waves when the source moves relative to the observer.
2. The wavelength decreases and the frequency increases.
3. The observed increase in the wavelength of light from most distant galaxies.
4. It is moving away faster.
5. The further away the galaxy, the bigger the red shift.
6. 489.5 − 486.1 = **3.4 nm** increase.
7. Most distant galaxies are moving away, and the further away they are, the faster they move; this pattern fits space itself stretching and carrying galaxies apart.
8. If the universe is expanding now, it was smaller in the past; tracing back leads to a very small initial point from which it began.
9. Electromagnetic radiation in the microwave region that fills the universe, coming from radiation present shortly after the beginning.
10. The Big Bang theory is currently the only theory that can explain its existence.
11. About 14 thousand million years.
12. 340 ÷ 425 = **0.800 m** (shorter than the emitted 340 ÷ 400 = 0.850 m).

## Frequently asked questions

**Does the Doppler effect only apply to light?**
No. The specification says the wave source could be light, sound or microwaves. Red shift is the name used for light from distant galaxies.

**What are the two pieces of evidence for the Big Bang in OxfordAQA 9203?**
Red shift of light from distant galaxies, which shows the universe is expanding, and CMBR, which the Big Bang theory is currently the only theory to explain.
