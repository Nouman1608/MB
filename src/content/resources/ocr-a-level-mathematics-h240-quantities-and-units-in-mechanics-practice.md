These questions cover section 3.01, Quantities and Units in Mechanics, of OCR A Level Mathematics A (H240), specification Version 3.1 (August 2026): references 3.01a and 3.01b (Stage 1) and 3.01c (Stage 2, A Level only). Question 10 uses the unit for moment, which is A Level only. Take g = 9.8 m s⁻² unless a question says otherwise, and give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures.

Review the ideas in the [quantities and units study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-mathematics-h240-quantities-and-units-in-mechanics/) or the [quantities and units revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-mathematics-h240-quantities-and-units-in-mechanics-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** State the S.I. unit of each quantity.

**(a)** mass **[1]**

**(b)** acceleration **[1]**

**(c)** weight **[1]**

**2.** Express the newton in terms of the S.I. base units, showing how you obtain your answer. **[2]**

**3.** Convert each quantity to S.I. units.

**(a)** 126 km h⁻¹ **[1]**

**(b)** 2.4 tonnes **[1]**

**(c)** 80 cm s⁻² **[1]**

**4.** A parcel has mass 3.5 kg. Find its weight, stating the unit. **[2]**

**5.** A stone has weight 29.4 N on Earth.

**(a)** Find its mass. **[1]**

**(b)** Find its weight on a planet where the acceleration due to gravity is 3.7 m s⁻². **[1]**

**6.** A student writes: "The box has a weight of 12 kg." Explain what is wrong with this statement and rewrite it correctly. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A train of mass 240 tonnes slows down uniformly from 108 km h⁻¹ to 36 km h⁻¹ in 2.5 minutes.

**(a)** Find the deceleration of the train in m s⁻². **[3]**

**(b)** Find the magnitude of the resultant force on the train. **[2]**

**8.** The air resistance on a cyclist is modelled as R = kv², where R is in newtons and v is the speed in m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Find the S.I. unit of k, giving your answer in base units. **[2]**

**(b)** R = 18 N when v = 6 m s⁻¹. Find the value of k, with its unit. **[2]**

**9.** A particle starts from rest and moves with constant acceleration a until it reaches speed v. A student suggests three expressions for the time taken: t = v/a, t = va and t = v²/a. Use units to decide which of the three could be correct, showing your reasoning for each. **[3]**

**10.** (A Level only) A force of 45 N acts at right angles to a door at a point 85 cm from the line of the hinges.

**(a)** Find the moment of the force about the hinge line, stating the unit. **[2]**

**(b)** Write this unit in S.I. base units. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the unit should not be written as mN. **[1]**

**11.** A particle of mass 400 g moves in a straight line with constant acceleration. Its speed increases from 2 m s⁻¹ to 9.2 m s⁻¹ while it travels 18 m.

**(a)** Show that the formula v² = u² + 2as is consistent in units. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the acceleration of the particle. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the resultant force acting on it. **[2]**

**12.** A ball is projected vertically upwards with speed u. A model gives its greatest height as h = u²/(2g). Show that the right-hand side has the unit of length. **[2]**

**13.** A car travels at 47 km h⁻¹ when the driver brakes. The car decelerates uniformly at 6.5 m s⁻² until it stops. The stopping distance is d = v²/(2a).

**(a)** Convert 47 km h⁻¹ to m s⁻¹, giving your answer to 3 significant figures. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the stopping distance using the unrounded speed. **[1]**

**(c)** Find the stopping distance using your rounded answer to part (a), and explain why the two answers differ. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **kg** (kilogram) [1].

**(b)** **m s⁻²** (or m/s²) [1].

**(c)** **N** (newton) [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Weight is a force, so its unit is the newton; writing kg for weight is the most common slip in this section.

**2.** F = ma, so the unit of force is the unit of mass times the unit of acceleration [1]. 1 N = 1 kg × 1 m s⁻² = **1 kg m s⁻²** [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Quote the defining equation; the base units then follow in one line.

**3. (a)** 126 ÷ 3.6 = **35 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** 2.4 × 1000 = **2400 kg** [1].

**(c)** 80 ÷ 100 = **0.8 m s⁻²** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Only the length part of cm s⁻² changes, so the factor is 100; the seconds are already S.I.

**4.** W = mg = 3.5 × 9.8 [1] = **34.3 N** [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The unit mark depends on writing N, not kg.

**5. (a)** m = W ÷ g = 29.4 ÷ 9.8 = **3 kg** [1].

**(b)** W = 3 × 3.7 = **11.1 N** [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Mass is the same on both bodies; only g, and so the weight, changes.

**6.** Weight is a force and is measured in newtons, so "12 kg" is a mass, not a weight [1]. Correct version: "The box has a mass of 12 kg and a weight of 12 × 9.8 = **117.6 N** (118 N to 3 s.f.)." [1] **[2]**

**7. (a)** u = 108 ÷ 3.6 = 30 m s⁻¹ and v = 36 ÷ 3.6 = 10 m s⁻¹ [1]. t = 2.5 × 60 = 150 s [1]. a = (10 − 30) ÷ 150 = −0.1333..., so the deceleration is **0.133 m s⁻²** (3 s.f.) [1].

**(b)** m = 240 000 kg [1]. F = ma = 240 000 × 20/150 = **32 000 N** [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Three conversions are needed before any formula is used: speed, time and mass. Keeping a = 2/15 exactly gives the force as an exact 32 000 N.

**8. (a)** k = R/v², so its unit is N ÷ (m s⁻¹)² [1] = kg m s⁻² ÷ m² s⁻² = **kg m⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** k = 18 ÷ 6² = 18 ÷ 36 [1] = **0.5 kg m⁻¹** [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* An answer to (b) needs the unit found in (a); "0.5" alone does not finish the question.

**9.** v/a: (m s⁻¹) ÷ (m s⁻²) = s, a time, so it **could be correct** [1]. va: m s⁻¹ × m s⁻² = m² s⁻³, not a time, so it is **wrong** [1]. v²/a: m² s⁻² ÷ m s⁻² = m, a length, so it is **wrong** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* "Could be correct" is the right conclusion: a unit check rules formulae out but cannot prove one right.

**10. (a)** 85 cm = 0.85 m [1]. Moment = 45 × 0.85 = **38.25 N m** (38.3 N m to 3 s.f.) [1].

**(b)** N m = kg m s⁻² × m = **kg m² s⁻²** [1].

**(c)** mN means millinewtons (thousandths of a newton), a unit of force, so the product of newtons and metres is written N m with a space [1]. **[4]**

**11. (a)** v² and u² have unit (m s⁻¹)² = m² s⁻² [1]. 2as has unit m s⁻² × m = m² s⁻², so every term matches [1].

**(b)** 9.2² = 2² + 2 × a × 18, so 84.64 − 4 = 36a [1]. a = 80.64 ÷ 36 = **2.24 m s⁻²** [1].

**(c)** m = 400 g = 0.4 kg [1]. F = 0.4 × 2.24 = **0.896 N** [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* The mass is given in grams to test 3.01a; using 400 in F = ma gives an answer a thousand times too big.

**12.** u² has unit (m s⁻¹)² = m² s⁻², and 2g has unit m s⁻² (2 has no unit) [1]. u²/(2g) has unit m² s⁻² ÷ m s⁻² = **m**, a length [1]. **[2]**

**13. (a)** 47 ÷ 3.6 = 13.0555... = **13.1 m s⁻¹** (3 s.f.) [1].

**(b)** d = 13.0555...² ÷ 13 = **13.1 m** (3 s.f.) [1].

**(c)** Using 13.1: d = 13.1² ÷ 13 = 13.2 m (3 s.f.) [1]. The rounded speed is too large and the error is increased by squaring, so rounding before the final step changes the third significant figure; keep full values until the end [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Round only the final answer. Store intermediate values in the calculator memory rather than copying rounded figures forward.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing weight in kg, or mass in N.
- Substituting km h⁻¹, minutes, grams or tonnes directly into a formula.
- Leaving the unit off the final answer, or giving N for a moment.
- Concluding that a formula is correct because its units balance.
- Rounding a converted value early and carrying the rounded figure into later working.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to give units in every answer in OCR A Level Mathematics mechanics?**
Yes, whenever the answer is a physical quantity. Section 3.01 expects you to add the appropriate unit to a quantity, so a final answer such as "34.3" for a weight is incomplete without N.

**What value of g should I use?**
Use g = 9.8 m s⁻² unless the question gives a different value. The specification notes that g depends on location, so a question about another planet or the Moon will state its own value.

**Why is the moment question marked A Level only?**
The unit for moment, N m, is reference 3.01c, which is Stage 2 content in H240 and does not appear in AS Level H230.
