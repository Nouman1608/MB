## Questions

**1.** Predict the charge on the ion formed by each element: rubidium, selenium, nitrogen and aluminium. **[4]**

**2.** Write the formula of each compound.

**(a)** zinc nitrate **[1]**

**(b)** ammonium carbonate **[1]**

**(c)** silver sulfate **[1]**

**(d)** aluminium hydroxide **[1]**

**(e)** magnesium nitride **[1]**

**3.** Use the charges given to write the formula of each compound.

**(a)** iron(III) sulfate (Fe³⁺) **[1]**

**(b)** chromium(III) oxide (Cr³⁺) **[1]**

**4.** A student writes three formulae: CaOH₂ for calcium hydroxide, NH₄SO₄ for ammonium sulfate and AgO for silver oxide. Correct each formula and explain the error. **[3]**

**5.** Write a balanced equation, with state symbols, for each reaction.

**(a)** Aluminium reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid to form aluminium chloride solution and hydrogen. **[2]**

**(b)** Barium reacts with water to form barium hydroxide solution and hydrogen. **[1]**

**6.** Propan-1-ol, C₃H₇OH, burns completely in oxygen. Write a balanced equation using the smallest whole numbers. **[2]**

**7.** Write an ionic equation, with state symbols, for each reaction.

**(a)** Potassium hydroxide solution neutralises dilute nitric acid. **[1]**

**(b)** Silver nitrate solution is added to sodium bromide solution, and a cream precipitate forms. **[1]**

**(c)** Solid zinc carbonate reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid. **[2]**

**8.** Aqueous barium chloride is added to aqueous sodium sulfate, and a white precipitate forms.

**(a)** Write the full balanced equation with state symbols. **[2]**

**(b)** Write the ionic equation. **[1]**

**(c)** Name the spectator ions. **[1]**

**9.** Lithium nitride, Li₃N, reacts with water to form lithium hydroxide solution and ammonia gas. Write a balanced equation with state symbols. **[2]**

**10.** Chlorine reacts with hot aqueous sodium hydroxide to form sodium chloride, sodium chlorate(V), NaClO₃, and water.

**(a)** Write a balanced equation. **[2]**

**(b)** Write the ionic equation. The chlorate(V) ion is ClO₃⁻. **[1]**

**11.** Copper(II) oxide, a black solid, reacts with warm dilute sulfuric acid to form a blue solution.

**(a)** Write the full balanced equation with state symbols. **[1]**

**(b)** Write the ionic equation. **[1]**

**(c)** Write an equation, with state symbols, for the thermal decomposition of solid zinc carbonate into zinc oxide and carbon dioxide. **[1]**

**12.** Gallium is in Group 13, below aluminium.

**(a)** Predict the formula of gallium oxide. **[1]**

**(b)** Predict the formula of gallium nitrate. **[1]**

**13.** **(a)** Ammonia gas and hydrogen chloride gas react to form white smoke of solid ammonium chloride. Write the equation with state symbols. **[1]**

**(b)** Aqueous ammonia neutralises dilute sulfuric acid. Write the balanced equation with state symbols. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Rb⁺ [1]; Se²⁻ [1]; N³⁻ [1]; Al³⁺ [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Group 15 and 16 non-metals gain three and two electrons; writing N⁵⁺ or Se⁶⁺ counts outer electrons without asking whether they are gained or lost.

**2. (a)** Zn(NO₃)₂ [1]

**(b)** (NH₄)₂CO₃ [1]

**(c)** Ag₂SO₄ [1]

**(d)** Al(OH)₃ [1]

**(e)** Mg₃N₂ [1] **[5]**

**3. (a)** Fe₂(SO₄)₃ [1]

**(b)** Cr₂O₃ [1] **[2]**

**4.** Ca(OH)₂: brackets are needed because there are two hydroxide ions [1]. (NH₄)₂SO₄: two NH₄⁺ ions balance one SO₄²⁻ [1]. Ag₂O: silver forms Ag⁺, so two are needed for one O²⁻ [1] **[3]**

**5. (a)** 2Al(s) + 6HCl(aq) → 2AlCl₃(aq) + 3H₂(g): correct formulae [1]; balancing and state symbols [1]

**(b)** Ba(s) + 2H₂O(l) → Ba(OH)₂(aq) + H₂(g) [1] **[3]**

**6.** One C₃H₇OH gives 3CO₂ + 4H₂O, which hold 10 O atoms; 1 comes from the alcohol, so 9 come from 4.5 O₂ [1]. 2C₃H₇OH + 9O₂ → 6CO₂ + 8H₂O [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Count the oxygen atom in the alcohol before deciding how much O₂ is needed.

**7. (a)** H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l) [1]

**(b)** Ag⁺(aq) + Br⁻(aq) → AgBr(s) [1]

**(c)** ZnCO₃(s) + 2H⁺(aq) → Zn²⁺(aq) + CO₂(g) + H₂O(l): ZnCO₃ kept whole as a solid [1]; balanced with state symbols [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* In (c) the carbonate is a solid, so it is not split into Zn²⁺ and CO₃²⁻.

**8. (a)** BaCl₂(aq) + Na₂SO₄(aq) → BaSO₄(s) + 2NaCl(aq): formulae [1]; balance and state symbols [1]

**(b)** Ba²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) → BaSO₄(s) [1]

**(c)** Na⁺ and Cl⁻ [1] **[4]**

**9.** Li₃N(s) + 3H₂O(l) → 3LiOH(aq) + NH₃(g): formulae [1]; balance and state symbols [1] **[2]**

**10. (a)** Formulae: Cl₂ + NaOH → NaCl + NaClO₃ + H₂O [1]. Balanced: 3Cl₂ + 6NaOH → 5NaCl + NaClO₃ + 3H₂O [1]

**(b)** 3Cl₂ + 6OH⁻ → 5Cl⁻ + ClO₃⁻ + 3H₂O [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Check charge in (b): 6 negative charges on the left, 5 + 1 = 6 on the right.

**11. (a)** CuO(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → CuSO₄(aq) + H₂O(l) [1]

**(b)** CuO(s) + 2H⁺(aq) → Cu²⁺(aq) + H₂O(l) [1]

**(c)** ZnCO₃(s) → ZnO(s) + CO₂(g) [1] **[3]**

**12. (a)** Ga₂O₃, because Ga forms Ga³⁺ like aluminium [1]

**(b)** Ga(NO₃)₃ [1] **[2]**

**13. (a)** NH₃(g) + HCl(g) → NH₄Cl(s) [1]

**(b)** Product (NH₄)₂SO₄ [1]; 2NH₃(aq) + H₂SO₄(aq) → (NH₄)₂SO₄(aq) [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Ammonia forms an ammonium salt with no water produced; writing water as a product treats ammonia as if it were a hydroxide.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Missing brackets round a molecular ion that appears more than once.
- Changing a formula to make an equation balance.
- Ignoring the oxygen already present in an organic fuel.
- Splitting solids, gases or water into ions in an ionic equation.
- Leaving out state symbols when the question asks for them.
- Balancing atoms but not checking the total charge in an ionic equation.
