## Questions

**1.** An effervescent vitamin tablet fizzes in water as an acid and a carbonate in it react. Explain, using collision theory, why the fizzing is faster if:

**(a)** the tablet is crushed into powder first **[2]**
**(b)** the water is warm instead of cold. **[2]**

**2.** A pile of dry wood in a shed does not burn at room temperature, but it keeps burning once it has been lit with a match. Explain these observations in terms of activation energy. **[2]**

**3.** Equal lengths of magnesium ribbon are added to excess hydrochloric acid of different concentrations at one temperature. The time for the ribbon to disappear is recorded.

| [HCl] / mol dm⁻³ | 0.50 | 1.00 | 1.50 | 2.00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Time / s | 160 | 82 | 54 | 41 |

**(a)** Calculate the rate, as 1/time, for each run. Give units. **[2]**
**(b)** Use your answers to deduce how the rate depends on the acid concentration. **[1]**

**4.** Hydrogen peroxide solution decomposes when manganese(IV) oxide is added: 2H₂O₂(aq) → 2H₂O(l) + O₂(g). The volume of oxygen is plotted against time. The tangent at t = 0 goes through (0 s, 0 cm³) and (20 s, 36 cm³). The tangent at t = 60 s goes through (0 s, 30 cm³) and (120 s, 78 cm³).

**(a)** Calculate the initial rate of reaction, with units. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the rate at 60 s. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the rate at 60 s is lower than the initial rate. **[2]**

**5.** A sample of gas is warmed from T₁ to a higher temperature T₂. Describe how the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution at T₂ differs from that at T₁, and use it to explain why the rate of reaction increases. **[4]**

**6.** A gas reaction has an uncatalysed activation energy of 150 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔH = −60 kJ mol⁻¹. With a catalyst the reaction takes place in two steps. Step 1 has a barrier 52 kJ mol⁻¹ above the reactants and forms an intermediate 20 kJ mol⁻¹ above the reactants. Step 2 has a barrier 45 kJ mol⁻¹ above the intermediate.

**(a)** Deduce the activation energy of the catalysed route. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how much lower this is than the uncatalysed activation energy. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe the reaction profile for the catalysed route, stating the energy of each feature relative to the reactants. **[2]**

**7.** Peroxodisulfate ions oxidise iodide ions slowly in aqueous solution: S₂O₈²⁻(aq) + 2I⁻(aq) → 2SO₄²⁻(aq) + I₂(aq). A few drops of iron(III) solution make the reaction much faster. Use the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution to explain how the iron ions increase the rate at constant temperature. **[3]**

**8.** Methanol can be made over a copper-based catalyst by two routes.

Route A, from carbon dioxide: CO₂ + 3H₂ → CH₃OH + H₂O
Route B, from carbon monoxide: CO + 2H₂ → CH₃OH

**(a)** Calculate the atom economy of route A for making methanol. **[2]**
**(b)** State the atom economy of route B, and give a reason. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain two ways in which catalysts help make industrial processes more sustainable. **[2]**

**9.** A sealed glass bulb at 450 °C holds an equilibrium mixture of hydrogen, iodine vapour and hydrogen iodide: H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g). Iodine vapour is purple; the other two gases are colourless.

**(a)** The purple colour does not change over time. Explain, in terms of rates and concentrations, why the system is in dynamic equilibrium. **[2]**
**(b)** More hydrogen is injected at constant temperature and volume. Predict and explain the change in colour. **[2]**

**10.** Phenylethene (styrene) is made by removing hydrogen from ethylbenzene over a catalyst. The forward reaction is endothermic.

C₆H₅CH₂CH₃(g) ⇌ C₆H₅CH=CH₂(g) + H₂(g)

Predict and justify the effect on the equilibrium yield of phenylethene of:

**(a)** raising the temperature **[2]**
**(b)** raising the pressure **[2]**
**(c)** removing hydrogen from the mixture as it forms. **[2]**

**11.** Gas Z is made over a catalyst: X(g) + 2Y(g) ⇌ Z(g). The forward reaction is exothermic. The data are illustrative.

| Temperature / °C | 200 | 250 | 300 | 350 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Yield of Z at 5 MPa / % | 38 | 24 | 13 | 6 |
| Yield of Z at 20 MPa / % | 62 | 47 | 31 | 18 |
| Time to reach equilibrium / min | 240 | 45 | 10 | 2 |

**(a)** How does temperature affect the yield of Z? Explain your answer. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the effect of pressure on the yield of Z. **[2]**
**(c)** A manufacturer chooses 300 °C. Use the data to justify this as a compromise. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest one reason why the manufacturer might choose 5 MPa, even though 20 MPa gives a higher yield. **[1]**
**(e)** State the effect of the catalyst on the yield of Z and on the time taken to reach equilibrium. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Crushing gives a larger surface area, so more acid and carbonate particles are exposed to the water [1]. Collisions between the reacting particles become more frequent, so the rate increases [1].
**(b)** In warm water the particles have more kinetic energy, so a larger fraction of collisions reach the activation energy [1]. Successful collisions happen more often [1].
*Examiner insight:* For (b), collision frequency alone usually scores less than the fraction of particles with at least Ea.

**2.** At room temperature very few molecules have energy at or above the activation energy, so the rate is negligible [1]. The match supplies energy to overcome Ea; the exothermic combustion then releases enough heat to keep nearby molecules above Ea [1].
*Examiner insight:* "The wood needs energy", with no mention of activation energy, tends to miss the first mark.

**3. (a)** 1/160 = 0.00625; 1/82 = 0.0122; 1/54 = 0.0185; 1/41 = 0.0244 [1]; unit s⁻¹ [1].
**(b)** Rate ÷ concentration is roughly constant (about 0.0122 to 0.0125), so **rate is directly proportional to [HCl]** [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Rate increases with concentration" is too vague for the deduction mark; show the constant ratio or that doubling concentration doubles the rate.

**4. (a)** Gradient = (36 − 0) ÷ (20 − 0) [1] = **1.8 cm³ s⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** (78 − 30) ÷ (120 − 0) = **0.40 cm³ s⁻¹** [1].
**(c)** Hydrogen peroxide is used up, so its concentration falls [1]. Collisions between H₂O₂ molecules and the catalyst surface become less frequent, so the rate falls [1].
*Examiner insight:* Take both points from the tangent; points on the curve give an average rate, not the rate at that instant.

**5.** At T₂ the peak is lower [1] and at a higher energy [1]. The area under the curve is unchanged, as the number of molecules is the same [1]. A much greater proportion of molecules have energy at or above Ea, so more collisions succeed and the rate increases [1].
*Examiner insight:* Mark Ea on the energy axis; without it, the curve is not linked to the rate.

**6. (a)** Second barrier = 20 + 45 = 65 kJ mol⁻¹ above the reactants [1]. This is higher than the first barrier (52), so catalysed Ea = **65 kJ mol⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** 150 − 65 = **85 kJ mol⁻¹** [1].
**(c)** First hump at +52, dip for the intermediate at +20 and second, higher hump at +65 [1]; products at −60, the same level as for the uncatalysed route [1].
*Examiner insight:* Ea is taken to the highest point on the catalysed route; choosing the first barrier gives 52, which is wrong here.

**7.** The iron ions provide an alternative route with a lower activation energy [1]. The distribution is unchanged at constant temperature, but the lower Ea lies further left, so more ions have energy at or above it [1]. A greater proportion of collisions succeed, giving more successful collisions per unit time [1].
*Examiner insight:* Saying the catalyst "gives the ions more energy" loses credit.

**8. (a)** Mr(CH₃OH) = 32.0; Mr(H₂O) = 18.0; sum of products = 32.0 + 18.0 = 50.0 [1]. Atom economy = 32.0 ÷ 50.0 × 100 = **64.0%** [1].
**(b)** **100%**, because methanol is the only product [1].
**(c)** Lower Ea allows a useful rate at a lower temperature, so less fuel is used [1]. A catalyst can allow a route with fewer by-products, so atom economy is higher and there is less waste [1].
*Examiner insight:* Include water in the denominator; omitting it wrongly gives 100% for route A.

**9. (a)** The forward and backward reactions continue at equal rates [1], so the concentrations of H₂, I₂ and HI stay constant and the colour does not change [1].
**(b)** The purple colour becomes paler [1]. The added hydrogen moves the position to the right to use it up, and this also uses up iodine [1].
*Examiner insight:* Writing that the concentrations are "equal" loses the mark; they are constant.

**10. (a)** The forward reaction is endothermic [1], so raising the temperature moves the position to the right to absorb the heat; the yield of phenylethene **increases** [1].
**(b)** 1 gas molecule on the left and 2 on the right [1]. Raising the pressure moves the position to the left, the side with fewer gas molecules; the yield **decreases** [1].
**(c)** Removing a product moves the position right to replace it [1]; the yield **increases** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Count gas molecules from the coefficients; "more molecules on the right" without numbers can lose the justification mark.

**11. (a)** As temperature rises the yield falls, for example from 38% to 6% at 5 MPa [1]. The forward reaction is exothermic, so higher temperature moves the position to the left, in the endothermic direction [1].
**(b)** Higher pressure gives a higher yield at every temperature [1], because the right side has fewer gas molecules (1 compared with 3) [1].
**(c)** At 300 °C equilibrium takes 10 minutes, against 240 minutes at 200 °C, so the rate is much faster [1]. The yield (13% or 31%) is still higher than at 350 °C, so 300 °C balances yield against rate [1].
**(d)** Any one: high pressure needs stronger, more expensive equipment; more energy is used to compress the gases; higher pressure is a greater safety risk [1].
**(e)** No effect on the yield; equilibrium is reached in a shorter time [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Use the data" means quote figures from the table; general statements without numbers usually score only part of the marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "more collisions" instead of "more frequent collisions".
- Explaining a temperature effect by collision frequency only.
- Taking tangent readings from the curve itself, or leaving out units.
- Measuring the catalysed Ea to the first hump or the intermediate, not the highest point.
- Drawing a new Maxwell-Boltzmann curve for a catalyst.
- Leaving a by-product such as water out of an atom economy calculation.
- Saying concentrations are "equal" at equilibrium.
- Predicting a shift with no reason.
- Claiming a catalyst increases the yield.
- Not quoting figures in a "use the data" question.
