Topic 1 key concepts that apply here: scarcity and choice; the margin and decision-making; time.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Scarcity | Wants exceed the resources available to satisfy them |
| Resources | Factors of production: land, labour, capital, enterprise |
| Needs | Goods and services required for survival |
| Wants | Goods and services desired beyond needs |
| Choice | Selecting one use of resources over others |
| Opportunity cost | The next best alternative forgone when a choice is made |
| Shortage | Quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied at the current price |
| Marginal decision | Choosing whether to have one more or one less unit |
| Labour-intensive | A method using a high proportion of labour relative to capital |
| Capital-intensive | A method using a high proportion of capital relative to labour |
| Resource allocation | How resources are divided between different uses |

## Scarcity: three contrasts

- **Scarcity versus shortage.** A shortage is temporary and can be removed by a price rise; scarcity is permanent.
- **Rich and poor.** All agents face scarcity; the poor face scarcity of needs, the rich of time and of ever-higher wants.
- **Now and later.** Technology can ease scarcity; depletion of resources can worsen it.

## Choices at every level

| Agent | Scarce resource | Typical choice |
|---|---|---|
| Individual | Income, time | Work or leisure; spend or save |
| Firm | Budget, capital, labour | Which products to make; which method to use |
| Government | Revenue, borrowing | Which services to fund; how to tax |

**The margin.** Compare the extra benefit of one more unit with its extra cost. Keep going while extra benefit ≥ extra cost.

**Quick example.** A café owner can open one more hour each evening. Extra revenue per extra hour: first hour 60 cu, second 45 cu, third 30 cu. Extra cost (staff, power) is 40 cu an hour. She opens 2 extra hours (60 and 45 both exceed 40; 30 does not).

## Opportunity cost: the rules

1. Next best alternative only, never the sum of all alternatives.
2. Can be measured in goods, time or money.
3. Forward-looking: unrecoverable past spending does not count.
4. Every choice has one, including choices by governments that seem "free" to users.

**Quick example.** A town council can use a plot of land for a car park, a market or a playground. It ranks them car park, then market, then playground. Choosing the car park has an opportunity cost of the market only.

**Time and opportunity cost.** Choosing investment (capital goods) now gives up consumption today for more output in future. Choosing consumption now gives up future output.

## What, how and for whom

| Question | Decides | Depends on |
|---|---|---|
| What? | Which goods and services, in what quantities | Consumer demand, government priorities |
| How? | Which methods and factor combinations | Relative factor prices, technology |
| For whom? | Who receives the output | Incomes, government policy, rationing rules |

**How: quick example.** Method A uses 30 days of labour at 50 cu a day = 1,500 cu. Method B uses 10 days of labour plus equipment at 800 cu = 500 + 800 = 1,300 cu. Method B is cheaper. If wages fell to 25 cu a day, A would cost 750 and B 1,050, so A would be cheaper.

## Exam traps

- Leaving out "next best".
- Summing all rejected options.
- Counting unrecoverable past spending.
- Using "scarcity" and "shortage" as if they meant the same thing.
- Claiming free healthcare or schooling has no opportunity cost.
- Naming what, how and for whom without saying what each decides.
- Ignoring the time dimension of a choice when asked to assess it.

## Self-test

1. Define scarcity.
2. Distinguish scarcity from a shortage.
3. Why does a wealthy country still face scarcity?
4. Define opportunity cost.
5. A student ranks her Saturday options: paid work (first), revision (second), sport (third). She works. What is her opportunity cost?
6. A firm spent 20,000 cu on a machine last year that has no resale value. Is that 20,000 cu part of the opportunity cost of using it today? Why?
7. Give an example of a marginal decision by a firm.
8. Extra revenue from each extra hour of opening is 70, 50 and 35 cu; the extra cost is 45 cu an hour. How many extra hours should open?
9. State the three basic questions of resource allocation.
10. Method A costs 900 cu and method B costs 1,100 cu for the same output. Which answers "how to produce" more efficiently, and why?
11. Why does "free" state education have an opportunity cost?
12. Explain how time affects the opportunity cost of building a new port.

### Answers

1. Wants exceed the resources available to satisfy them.
2. A shortage is excess demand at the current price, removable by a price rise; scarcity is the permanent gap between unlimited wants and limited resources.
3. Wants keep growing and time and resources are still limited, so it cannot provide everything its people want.
4. The next best alternative forgone when a choice is made.
5. Revision, the next best alternative; sport is not included.
6. No. It cannot be recovered whatever the firm decides now, so it is not an alternative forgone.
7. For example, whether to run a machine for one more shift.
8. Two: 70 and 50 exceed 45; 35 does not.
9. What to produce, how to produce and for whom to produce.
10. Method A, because it uses fewer resources (lower cost) for the same output.
11. The resources used (teachers, buildings, materials) could have been used for something else, such as healthcare, paid for through taxes.
12. The port gives up other goods now but raises trade and output for many years, so the present cost must be weighed against future benefits.
