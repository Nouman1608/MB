Pearson Edexcel International GCSE English Language (4EA1) develops the two
skills at the heart of the subject: reading a range of texts closely and
writing clearly for different purposes. Students read non-fiction, poetry
and prose, and write both transactional pieces (writing with a practical
purpose and audience) and imaginative pieces. Everyone takes Component 1, a
135-minute exam worth 90 marks and 60%. The remaining 40% comes from either
Component 2, a 90-minute exam, or Component 3, a non-examined assessment;
both alternatives carry 60 marks. The specification was first taught in
September 2016 and first examined in June 2018.

## How the course is organised

The course is built from three components plus an optional endorsement.

- **Component 1: Non-fiction Texts and Transactional Writing** is
  compulsory. Section A is about reading non-fiction texts; Section B is
  transactional writing.
- **Component 2: Poetry and Prose Texts and Imaginative Writing** is the
  examined option. Section A asks for a response to poetry and prose texts;
  Section B is imaginative writing.
- **Component 3: Poetry and Prose Texts and Imaginative Writing** is the
  non-examined alternative to Component 2. It covers the same content
  through two assignments: Assignment A on poetry and prose texts and
  Assignment B on imaginative writing.
- **Spoken Language Endorsement** is optional and sits outside the weighted
  components.

So the content splits neatly into two halves. Component 1 deals with
non-fiction reading and writing for a real-world purpose. Components 2 and 3
deal with literary reading and creative writing, and your centre chooses
whether that half is examined or assessed through coursework.

## How it's assessed

4EA1 is untiered. Each student completes Component 1 plus either Component 2
or Component 3.

| Component | Paper code | Format | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Component 1: Non-fiction Texts and Transactional Writing | 4EA1/01 | Written exam, externally assessed | 135 minutes (2h15) | 90 | 60% |
| Component 2: Poetry and Prose Texts and Imaginative Writing | 4EA1/02 | Written exam, externally assessed | 90 minutes (1h30) | 60 | 40% |
| Component 3: Poetry and Prose Texts and Imaginative Writing (non-examined assessment) | 4EA1/03 | Coursework, internally assessed and externally moderated | Not timed | 60 | 40% |

Inside Component 1, Section A (reading, a response to a non-fiction text) and
Section B (transactional writing) are worth 45 marks each. Inside
Component 2, Section A (a reading essay) and Section B (imaginative writing)
are worth 30 marks each. Because Components 2 and 3 have identical marks and
weighting, the choice between them changes the way the work is done, not its
value in the final grade.

Pearson also lists a separate modular version of the qualification (4XEA1,
first teaching September 2025). It is not described in the 4EA1
specification and is not covered on this page.

## How the parts fit together

Every part of the course pairs a reading task with a writing task, and the
two are meant to inform each other. Close reading of non-fiction in
Component 1 shows how writers shape arguments, select detail and address an
audience, which is exactly what transactional writing asks you to do
yourself. In the same way, analysing how poets and prose writers create
effects in Component 2 or 3 gives you techniques to use in imaginative
writing.

Some habits run through the whole qualification: reading the question
carefully, supporting points with precise reference to the text, explaining
the effect of a writer's choices rather than just naming them, and planning
writing before starting it. Clear, controlled expression helps in every
writing task. Since Component 1 alone is worth 60%, non-fiction
reading and transactional writing deserve steady attention even when class
time is focused on literary texts.

## Planning your study

Find out early whether your centre has chosen Component 2 or Component 3, as
this shapes the year. If you are taking Component 3, the two assignments are
completed during the course on a schedule your centre sets, so ask about
deadlines and the rules for the work at the start. If you are taking
Component 2, the poetry and prose work is revised for a timed paper.

A typical two-year plan begins with a broad reading programme across
non-fiction, poetry and prose, building analytical vocabulary. Short
writing tasks for different audiences and purposes can run alongside from
the start. In the second year, focus on Component 1 practice under timed
conditions and complete either the Component 3 assignments or Component 2
revision. If your centre offers the Spoken Language Endorsement, plan it
with your teacher so it does not clash with written deadlines.

Begin full practice papers for Component 1 (and Component 2, if relevant)
in the final two terms. Advice on how to divide time within each paper is on
the exam-preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**What components do I take in Pearson Edexcel IGCSE English Language 4EA1?**
Everyone takes Component 1, then either Component 2 (an exam) or Component 3 (non-examined assessment). Your centre decides which of the two you follow.

**How much is each component worth?**
Component 1 is a 135-minute exam worth 90 marks and 60%. Component 2 is a 90-minute exam worth 60 marks and 40%, and Component 3 is coursework worth 60 marks and 40%.

**Is Component 3 coursework easier than the Component 2 exam?**
Neither is weighted more heavily: both carry 60 marks and 40% and cover the same content. Component 3 is internally assessed and externally moderated, while Component 2 is an externally assessed exam.

**Is the Spoken Language Endorsement compulsory?**
No. It is an optional endorsement and is not one of the weighted components.

**Is IGCSE English Language 4EA1 tiered?**
No. The qualification is untiered.
