Learn the content first from the [Momentum study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-momentum/) and the [Momentum revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-momentum-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Calculate the momentum of a 1800 kg van travelling at 22 m/s. **[2]**

**2.** A bullet of mass 0.012 kg has a momentum of 4.8 kg m/s. Calculate its velocity. **[2]**

**3.** State the principle of conservation of momentum. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why momentum is a vector quantity. **[1]**

**5.** A resultant force of 1500 N acts on a car for 4.0 s. Calculate the change in the car's momentum. **[2]**

**6.** A footballer kicks a stationary 0.45 kg ball, which leaves the boot at 24 m/s. The boot is in contact with the ball for 0.0090 s. Calculate the average force on the ball. **[3]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A railway wagon of mass 8000 kg moving at 3.0 m/s collides with a stationary wagon of mass 4000 kg. The wagons couple together.

**(a)** Calculate the total momentum before the collision. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the velocity of the coupled wagons. **[2]**

**(c)** State **one** assumption you have made. **[1]**

**8.** Two dodgem cars collide head-on. Car P (total mass 250 kg) moves east at 3.0 m/s. Car Q (total mass 200 kg) moves west at 2.5 m/s. After the collision, car P moves east at 0.40 m/s.

**(a)** Calculate the total momentum before the collision, taking east as positive. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the velocity of car Q after the collision. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain how your answer to (b) shows the direction of car Q. **[1]**

**9.** A cannon of mass 600 kg is at rest on a smooth platform. It fires a shell of mass 5.0 kg horizontally at 180 m/s.

**(a)** State the total momentum of the cannon and shell before firing. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the recoil velocity of the cannon. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why the cannon moves much more slowly than the shell. **[2]**

**10.** A squash ball of mass 0.024 kg travels at 30 m/s towards a wall. It rebounds at 22 m/s along the same line. It is in contact with the wall for 0.0030 s.

**(a)** Calculate the size of the change in momentum of the ball. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the average force the wall exerts on the ball. **[2]**

**(c)** State the size and direction of the force the ball exerts on the wall. **[2]**

**11.** Explain how a crumple zone and a seat belt together reduce the risk of injury to a car driver in a frontal collision. **[5]**

**12.** A child of mass 30 kg falls from a climbing frame and hits the ground at 5.0 m/s.

**(a)** Calculate the child's change in momentum when she stops. **[2]**

**(b)** On concrete she stops in 0.010 s. On a rubber playground surface she stops in 0.12 s. Calculate the average force in each case. **[2]**

**(c)** Use your answers to explain why cushioned surfaces are used in playgrounds. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** p = m × v = 1800 × 22 [1] = **39 600 kg m/s** [1]. **[2]**

**2.** v = p ÷ m = 4.8 ÷ 0.012 [1] = **400 m/s** [1]. **[2]**

**3.** In a closed system (no external forces) [1], the total momentum before an interaction equals the total momentum after it [1]. **[2]**

**4.** It has a direction (the same as the velocity) as well as a magnitude [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Δp = F × t = 1500 × 4.0 [1] = **6000 kg m/s** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Δp = 0.45 × 24 = 10.8 kg m/s [1]; F = Δp ÷ t = 10.8 ÷ 0.0090 [1] = **1200 N** [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** 8000 × 3.0 = **24 000 kg m/s** [1].

**(b)** 24 000 = (8000 + 4000) × v [1]; v = **2.0 m/s** in the original direction [1].

**(c)** No external forces act (friction from the track is negligible) [1]. **[4]**

**8. (a)** 250 × 3.0 + 200 × (−2.5) [1] = 750 − 500 = **250 kg m/s** east [1].

**(b)** After: 250 × 0.40 + 200 v = 100 + 200 v [1]; 100 + 200 v = 250 [1]; v = **0.75 m/s** [1].

**(c)** The answer is positive, so car Q moves east: it has reversed direction [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* the sign of a calculated velocity is information; say what it means.

**9. (a)** Zero [1].

**(b)** 5.0 × 180 + 600 v = 0 [1]; 600 v = −900 [1]; v = −1.5 m/s, so **1.5 m/s backwards** [1].

**(c)** The cannon and shell have momenta of equal size [1]; the cannon's mass is far larger (120 times), so its velocity is far smaller [1]. **[6]**

**10. (a)** Δp = 0.024 × (30 + 22) [1] = **1.248 kg m/s**, about 1.2 kg m/s [1].

**(b)** F = 1.248 ÷ 0.0030 [1] = **416 N**, about 420 N [1].

**(c)** 416 N (the same size) [1], towards the wall [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* using 30 − 22 = 8 m/s gives a force less than a sixth of the correct value. The velocity reverses, so the speeds add.

**11.** The driver's momentum must fall to zero in the collision [1]. The crumple zone crushes in a controlled way, so the car takes longer to stop [1]. The seat belt stretches slightly, so the driver takes longer still to stop [1]. A longer time means a smaller rate of change of momentum [1], so a smaller force acts on the driver and injuries are less likely [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* the words "time", "rate of change of momentum" and "force" all need to appear; "the crumple zone absorbs the impact" gains nothing on its own.

**12. (a)** Δp = 30 × 5.0 [1] = **150 kg m/s** [1].

**(b)** Concrete: 150 ÷ 0.010 = **15 000 N** [1]; rubber: 150 ÷ 0.12 = **1250 N** [1].

**(c)** The rubber surface increases the stopping time (twelve times longer) [1], so the force on the child is much smaller (twelve times smaller), reducing the chance of injury [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- A missing minus sign for motion in the opposite direction.
- Subtracting speeds for a rebound.
- Safety explanations that never mention time or rate of change of momentum.
- Forgetting the combined mass when objects stick together.
