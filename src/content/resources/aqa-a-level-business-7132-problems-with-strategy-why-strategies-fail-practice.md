These questions cover section 3.10.4 problems with strategy and why strategies fail of the AQA A-level Business (7132) specification. All businesses and figures are invented. Revise first with the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-problems-with-strategy-why-strategies-fail/) and the [managing strategic change revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-managing-strategic-change-revision-notes/).

## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** A firm makes small, steady improvements to its current products while its customers move to a different technology. Which term best describes this? **[1]**

A: Emergent strategy

B: Strategic drift

C: Contingency planning

D: Market development

**2.** Which **one** of the following is a plan for dealing with an event that might happen but is not expected? **[1]**

A: A contingency plan

B: A mission statement

C: A corporate objective

D: A SWOT analysis

**3.** Define "emergent strategy". **[2]**

**4.** State **two** reasons why strategic decisions are difficult to make. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why poor communication can cause a strategy to fail. **[3]**

## Section B: Structured questions

Dalrymple Dairies set the following targets for Year 3 of its five-year strategy. The table shows the results.

| Measure | Target | Actual |
|---|---|---|
| Operating margin | 10% | 7.5% |
| Market share | 18% | 16% |
| Staff turnover | 10% | 14% |
| Customer satisfaction (index) | 80 | 83 |

**6.** Calculate the variance (actual minus target) for each measure. **[4]**

**7.** Evaluate how well Dalrymple's strategy is performing. **[6]**

**8.** Dalrymple's milk sales are £80m and have been falling by 10% a year. The managers have responded by making production more efficient but have not changed the product range. Calculate the value of milk sales after three years, and the percentage fall, and explain why this suggests strategic drift. **[5]**

**9.** Explain **two** reasons why Dalrymple's managers may have failed to respond to the fall in milk sales sooner. **[4]**

**10.** Dalrymple estimates the following risks. Calculate the expected loss for each (probability × impact) and identify the risk that deserves most attention.

| Risk | Probability | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| Fire at the main plant | 2% | £12m |
| Failure of a key supplier | 8% | £5m |
| Strike | 15% | £2.4m |
| Data breach | 5% | £9m |

**[5]**

**11.** Analyse the value to Dalrymple of having a contingency plan for a product contamination crisis. **[6]**

**12.** Evaluate the view that a business should rely on a detailed strategic plan rather than allow strategy to emerge over time. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. The strategy gradually moves away from the needs of the environment. **[1]**

**2.** **A** [1]. A contingency plan prepares for "what if" events. **[1]**

**3.** A strategy that develops over time [1] through a series of decisions, experiments and responses to events, rather than being fully planned in advance [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: uncertainty about the future [1]; incomplete information [1]; high cost and irreversibility [1]; complexity and conflicting stakeholder interests [1]; managers' biases [1]. **[2]**

**5.** If staff do not understand what the strategy is or why it matters [1], they may resist it or take actions that do not support it [1], and different departments may work towards different goals, so the strategy is not carried out as intended [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Operating margin: 7.5 − 10 = −2.5 percentage points [1]. Market share: 16 − 18 = −2 percentage points [1]. Staff turnover: 14 − 10 = +4 percentage points (worse than target) [1]. Customer satisfaction: 83 − 80 = +3 [1]. **[4]**

**7.** Operating margin is 25% below target (2.5 ÷ 10) [1], market share is 11% below target (2 ÷ 18) [1], and staff turnover is 40% above target, which is a poor result [1]. Customer satisfaction beats the target, so the product itself is liked [1]. Overall, the strategy is partly working: customers are happy but profit, share and staff retention are behind [1]. High turnover may be driving higher costs and lower margin. Judgement: it is too early to abandon the strategy, but managers should investigate the causes before deciding [1]. **[6]**

**8.** After one year £72m, after two £64.8m, after three £58.32m [1]. Fall = 80 − 58.32 = £21.68m [1], which is 27.1% [1]. The firm is putting effort into producing a product that fewer people want [1], and has not changed the product range to match changing demand, so its strategy is drifting away from the market [1]. **[5]**

**9.** Reason 1: a strong existing culture [1]. Managers may believe "we are a milk business" and not see a need for new products [1]. Reason 2: poor scanning of the environment [1]. If the firm did not track customers' changing habits, it would not see the trend until sales fell sharply [1]. (Credit also: past success; defensive leaders; slow structure.) **[4]**

**10.** Fire: 2% × £12m = £0.24m [1]. Supplier: 8% × £5m = £0.40m [1]. Strike: 15% × £2.4m = £0.36m [1]. Data breach: 5% × £9m = £0.45m [1]. The data breach has the highest expected loss, so it deserves the most attention [1]. **[5]**

**11.** Value 1: speed and calm in the response [1]. A prepared plan sets out who does what, so recall of products and statements to the public happen quickly, reducing harm [1]. Value 2: protecting reputation and sales [1]. Customers trust a firm that is seen to act promptly and honestly [1]. Value 3: limiting costs [1], as faster action reduces the number of affected products and legal claims [1]. **[6]**

**12.** For planning: it gives clear direction and co-ordinates departments [1], ensures resources are allocated to priorities [1], forces managers to analyse the environment and risks [1], and gives targets against which performance can be judged [1]. Against: forecasts can be wrong, and a rigid plan may stop managers reacting to new information [1]. Plans take time to make and can discourage initiative. Emergent strategy uses staff knowledge and allows the firm to learn from experience [1] and adapt quickly in changing markets [1]. But it can lack direction and be inconsistent [1]. Factors: how stable the market is [1], the size of the commitments being made [1], and how well the firm can learn and adapt. Judgement: most firms need a mix, with a plan for the main direction and flexibility to adapt [1]; the more uncertain the environment, the more the plan should be treated as a guide that is reviewed regularly [1]. **[12]**

*Examiner insight:* The strongest answers use the figures from the case, and weigh causes against each other rather than listing them.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating planned and emergent strategy as incompatible.
- Confusing strategic drift with a sudden shock.
- Listing reasons for failure without choosing the most important.
- Quoting variances without saying what they mean.
- Forgetting that expected loss uses probability and impact.
