These questions follow OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560, Version 2.1 (August 2026), section 9.03, Plane vector geometry. Questions 1 to 9 are for both tiers. Questions 10 to 13 use vectors in geometric arguments and proofs, which are **Higher tier only**, and are labelled. Column vectors are written (a over b); single-letter vectors are in bold. Sketch each diagram from its description. All questions can be answered without a calculator.

Learn the methods first in the [plane vector geometry study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-plane-vector-geometry-ocr-gcse-maths/) or the [plane vector geometry revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-plane-vector-geometry-ocr-gcse-maths-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** A is (−1, 3) and B is (4, −2).

**(a)** Write down the column vector **AB**. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the column vector **BA**. **[1]**

**2.** **a** = (3 over 1) and **b** = (−2 over 4).

**(a)** Find **a** + **b**. **[1]**

**(b)** Find 3**a** − 2**b**. **[2]**

**3.** **c** = (−3 over 4).

**(a)** Describe how to draw **c** on a square grid. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down −**c**. **[1]**

**4.** Here are four vectors.

P: (3 over −2)

Q: (−9 over 6)

R: (4 over −6)

S: (12 over 8)

Which of them are parallel to (6 over −4)? **[2]**

**5.** Find the value of k so that (k over 15) is parallel to (−2 over 5). **[2]**

**6.** C is the point (2, −3) and **CD** = (−4 over 7).

**(a)** Find the coordinates of D. **[1]**

**(b)** E is the point with **CE** = 2**CD**. Find the coordinates of E. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** **a** = (1 over 2) and **b** = (3 over −1). Find the numbers p and q so that p**a** + q**b** = (9 over 4). **[4]**

**8.** O is the origin, **OA** = **a** and **OB** = **b**.

**(a)** Write **AB** in terms of **a** and **b**. **[1]**

**(b)** M is the midpoint of AB. Find **OM** in terms of **a** and **b**. **[2]**

**(c)** P is the point on AB with AP : PB = 3 : 1. Find **OP** in terms of **a** and **b**. **[2]**

**9.** A is (1, 1), B is (5, 3) and C is (7, 8).

**(a)** Write down **AB**. **[1]**

**(b)** ABCD is a parallelogram. Find the coordinates of D. **[2]**

**(c)** Write down **BD**. **[1]**

**10.** **(Higher tier only)** OPQR is a parallelogram with **OP** = **p** and **OR** = **r**.

**(a)** Write **OQ** and **PR** in terms of **p** and **r**. **[2]**

**(b)** X is the midpoint of OQ. Find **PX** in terms of **p** and **r**. **[2]**

**(c)** Use your answer to part (b) to explain what this shows about the diagonals of the parallelogram. **[1]**

**11.** **(Higher tier only)** In triangle OAB, **OA** = **a** and **OB** = **b**. P is the point on OA with OP = ⅓OA, and Q is the point on OB with OQ = ⅓OB. Prove that PQ is parallel to AB, and find the ratio PQ : AB. **[3]**

**12.** **(Higher tier only)** **OA** = **a**, **OB** = **b** and **OC** = 3**b** − 2**a**.

**(a)** Show that A, B and C lie on a straight line. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the ratio AB : BC. **[1]**

**13.** **(Higher tier only)** In triangle OAB, **OA** = **a** and **OB** = **b**. M is the midpoint of AB. G is the point on OM with OG : GM = 2 : 1. N is the midpoint of OA.

**(a)** Find **OG** in terms of **a** and **b**. **[2]**

**(b)** Prove that B, G and N are collinear. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** (4 − (−1) over −2 − 3) = **(5 over −5)** [1]

**(b)** **(−5 over 5)** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* End minus start. Working out A − B instead gives **BA**, which points the wrong way.

**2. (a)** **(1 over 5)** [1]

**(b)** 3**a** = (9 over 3) and 2**b** = (−4 over 8) [1]; 3**a** − 2**b** = **(13 over −5)** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Subtracting −4 gives +4 in the top component. Write the brackets in full to keep the signs right.

**3. (a)** From any starting point, move **3 left and 4 up**, and draw an arrow to the finishing point with the **arrowhead at the finish** [1]

**(b)** **(3 over −4)** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* A line without an arrowhead does not show a vector's direction.

**4.** P = ½ × (6 over −4) and Q = −1.5 × (6 over −4) [1]; **P and Q** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* R has the numbers the wrong way round and S has the wrong sign, so neither is a multiple of (6 over −4).

**5.** 15 = 3 × 5, so the multiplier is 3 [1]; k = 3 × (−2) = **−6** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Use the component you know in both vectors to find the multiplier, then apply it to the other component.

**6. (a)** (2 − 4, −3 + 7) = **(−2, 4)** [1]

**(b)** 2**CD** = (−8 over 14), so E = (2 − 8, −3 + 14) = **(−6, 11)** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Coordinates of the end point = coordinates of the start point + the vector.

**7.** p + 3q = 9 and 2p − q = 4 [1]; q = 2p − 4, so p + 6p − 12 = 9 [1]; **p = 3** [1]; **q = 2** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Check: 3(1 over 2) + 2(3 over −1) = (3 + 6 over 6 − 2) = (9 over 4).

**8. (a)** **AB** = **b** − **a** [1]

**(b)** **OM** = **a** + ½(**b** − **a**) [1]; = **½(a + b)** [1]

**(c)** **AP** = ¾**AB**, so **OP** = **a** + ¾(**b** − **a**) [1]; = **¼a + ¾b** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* AP : PB = 3 : 1 splits AB into 4 equal parts, so AP is three quarters of AB, not three thirds.

**9. (a)** **(4 over 2)** [1]

**(b)** **DC** = **AB**, so D = C − (4 over 2) [1]; **D = (3, 6)** [1]

**(c)** **(−2 over 3)** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* In parallelogram ABCD the letters go in order round the shape, so AB and DC are the equal, parallel pair.

**10. (a)** **OQ** = **p** + **r** [1]; **PR** = **r** − **p** [1]

**(b)** **OX** = ½(**p** + **r**) [1]; **PX** = **OX** − **OP** = ½**r** − ½**p** = **½(r − p)** [1]

**(c)** **PX** = ½**PR**, so X lies on PR at its midpoint: the diagonals **bisect each other** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* X was defined as the midpoint of OQ, and part (b) shows it is also the midpoint of PR. That is the whole proof.

**11.** **OP** = ⅓**a** and **OQ** = ⅓**b**, so **PQ** = ⅓**b** − ⅓**a** = ⅓(**b** − **a**) [1]; **AB** = **b** − **a**, so **PQ** = ⅓**AB**, which means PQ is parallel to AB [1]; **PQ : AB = 1 : 3** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The scalar ⅓ gives both facts at once: parallel, and one third of the length.

**12. (a)** **AB** = **b** − **a** [1]; **BC** = **OC** − **OB** = 2**b** − 2**a** = 2(**b** − **a**) = 2**AB** [1]; so AB and BC are parallel, and they share the point B, so A, B and C are collinear [1]

**(b)** **AB : BC = 1 : 2** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Parallel vectors alone could describe two separate parallel lines. The shared point B is what puts all three points on one line.

**13. (a)** **OM** = ½(**a** + **b**) [1]; **OG** = ⅔**OM** = **⅓(a + b)** [1]

**(b)** **BG** = **OG** − **OB** = ⅓**a** − ⅔**b** [1]; **BN** = **ON** − **OB** = ½**a** − **b** [1]; **BG** = ⅔(½**a** − **b**) = ⅔**BN**, so BG and BN are parallel and share the point B: B, G and N are collinear [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* This proves that the line from B to the midpoint of OA passes through G. Write the final conclusion in words.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Subtracting coordinates in the wrong order.
- Applying a scalar to only one component.
- Writing **AB** as **a** − **b**.
- Converting a ratio m : n into the fraction m/n instead of m/(m + n).
- Claiming collinearity without the shared point.
- Ending a proof with algebra and no geometrical conclusion.
