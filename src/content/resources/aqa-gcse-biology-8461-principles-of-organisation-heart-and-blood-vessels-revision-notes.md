## 4.2.1 Levels of organisation

```
cell -> tissue -> organ -> organ system -> organism
```

| Term | Specification definition | Circulation example |
|---|---|---|
| Cell | The basic building block of all living organisms | Cardiac muscle cell |
| Tissue | A group of cells with a similar structure and function | Muscle tissue in the heart wall |
| Organ | An aggregation of tissues performing specific functions | Heart |
| Organ system | Organs organised to work together, forming the organism | Circulatory system (heart, blood vessels, blood) |

**Test for an organ:** does it contain more than one type of tissue? If yes, and it does a specific job, it is
an organ.

### Size and scale

| Level | Typical unit |
|---|---|
| Cell | µm (micrometres) |
| Tissue layer | µm to mm |
| Organ | cm |
| Organism | m |

1 mm = 1000 µm. 1 cm = 10 000 µm. Convert to one unit **before** comparing sizes.

## 4.2.2.2 Heart structure

- Four chambers: **right atrium, right ventricle, left atrium, left ventricle**.
- Atria receive blood. Ventricles pump it out.
- A wall of muscle separates the right and left sides, so the two kinds of blood do not mix.
- Valves stop backflow. You do **not** need the valve names.
- **Left ventricle wall is thickest**: it pumps blood around the whole body. The right ventricle pumps only to
  the nearby lungs.

### The five vessels you must name

| Vessel | From | To | Blood |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vena cava | Body | Right atrium | Deoxygenated |
| Pulmonary artery | Right ventricle | Lungs | Deoxygenated |
| Pulmonary vein | Lungs | Left atrium | Oxygenated |
| Aorta | Left ventricle | Body | Oxygenated |
| Coronary arteries | Aorta (base) | Heart muscle | Oxygenated |

**Memory hook:** **A**rteries go **A**way from the heart. Veins go back **in** to the heart.

### Method in steps: tracing the route

```
1. Start at the vena cava.
2. Right atrium -> right ventricle.
3. Pulmonary artery -> lungs (pick up O2, lose CO2).
4. Pulmonary vein -> left atrium -> left ventricle.
5. Aorta -> body.
```

Check: every chamber appears once, and the route crosses the lungs between the right and left sides.

## Double circulation

- Blood passes through the heart **twice** in each complete circuit.
- Right ventricle -> lungs (gas exchange).
- Left ventricle -> rest of the body.
- Blood coming back from the lungs is pumped a second time by the left ventricle, so it reaches the body
  under higher pressure than if it went straight on from the lungs.
- Trace it as a figure of eight: body -> right side -> lungs -> left side -> body.

## Pacemaker

- Natural resting heart rate is controlled by a group of cells in the **right atrium** acting as a pacemaker.
- **Artificial pacemakers** are electrical devices that correct irregularities in the heart rate.

## Lungs

Only four structures are needed: **trachea, bronchi, alveoli, capillary network** around the alveoli.

| Adaptation | Effect |
|---|---|
| Many alveoli | Large surface area |
| Thin alveolus walls | Short diffusion distance |
| Capillary network with good blood supply | Keeps the concentration gradient steep |
| Ventilation | Keeps the concentration gradient steep |

Gases: O₂ diffuses alveolus -> blood. CO₂ diffuses blood -> alveolus.

Air route: trachea -> bronchi -> alveoli. Blood route: pulmonary artery (deoxygenated) -> capillaries around the
alveoli -> pulmonary vein (oxygenated).

## Blood vessels: structure to function

| Vessel | Key structure | Link to function |
|---|---|---|
| Artery | Thick muscular, elastic wall; narrow lumen | Withstands and maintains high pressure from the ventricles |
| Vein | Thin wall; wide lumen; valves | Low pressure, so valves stop backflow; wide lumen eases flow back to the heart |
| Capillary | Wall one cell thick; narrow | Short diffusion distance for exchange with body cells |

## Blood-flow rates

| Quantity | Formula | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Heart rate | beats counted / time in s x 60 | beats per minute |
| Volume pumped per minute | volume per beat x heart rate | cm³/min |
| Flow rate | volume / time | cm³/s or cm³/min |
| Speed of blood | distance / time | mm/s |

1 dm³ = 1000 cm³. 60 s = 1 min.

**Small reminder.** 450 cm³ in 30 s gives 450 / 30 = 15 cm³/s. Dividing 30 by 450 gives a meaningless number.

### Method in steps: a rate calculation

```
1. Write the formula in words (rate = amount / time).
2. Convert units first (dm3 -> cm3, mm -> um, minutes -> seconds).
3. Substitute and calculate.
4. Give the unit the question asks for.
```

**Worked reminder.** A heart pumps 4.2 dm³ of blood in one minute at 60 beats per minute. Volume per beat =
4200 cm³ / 60 = 70 cm³. Here you rearrange the same relationship: volume per beat = volume per minute / heart rate.

## Method in steps: a structure-function explanation

Six-mark questions on vessels or lungs reward linked pairs. Use this frame for each point:

```
Feature  ->  what it does  ->  why that matters for the function
```

**Worked reminder (capillary).** Wall one cell thick -> short diffusion distance -> oxygen and glucose reach
body cells quickly.

**Worked reminder (vein).** Valves -> stop blood flowing backwards -> blood still returns to the heart even
though the pressure is low.

**Worked reminder (alveoli).** Surrounded by capillaries -> blood keeps carrying oxygen away -> the
concentration gradient stays steep, so diffusion stays fast.

A feature with no "why" usually earns half the available credit at most. Two features with full links beat four
features listed bare.

## Size and scale reminder

To compare a tissue layer 0.5 mm thick with cells 20 µm wide: 0.5 mm = 500 µm, and 500 / 20 = 25, so the layer
is about 25 cells thick. Always convert first, then divide the larger by the smaller.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Artery vs vein:** defined by direction (away from / towards the heart), not by oxygen content.
- **Pulmonary artery vs pulmonary vein:** the artery carries deoxygenated blood; the vein carries oxygenated blood.
- **Right vs left ventricle:** right to the lungs, left to the body. Left wall thicker.
- **Atrium vs ventricle:** atria receive, ventricles pump out.
- **Tissue vs organ:** a group of similar cells with one function vs several tissues working together.
- **Natural vs artificial pacemaker:** cells in the right atrium vs an electrical device.

## Quick self-test

1. Define a tissue.
2. Why is the heart an organ and not a tissue?
3. Which chamber does the vena cava empty into?
4. Name the vessel that carries blood from the left ventricle.
5. Which vessel carries deoxygenated blood away from the heart?
6. What do the coronary arteries supply?
7. Where is the natural pacemaker?
8. Give two adaptations of alveoli and the effect of each.
9. Why do veins have valves but arteries do not need them along their length?
10. A student counts 27 beats in 20 seconds. Calculate the heart rate.
11. A heart pumps 65 cm³ per beat at 80 beats per minute. Calculate the volume pumped per minute.
12. A red blood cell moves 2 mm along a capillary in 4 s. Calculate its speed. How many cells 25 µm wide fit in a line 1 mm long?

### Answers

1. A group of cells with a similar structure and function.
2. It is made of several different tissues (such as muscle tissue) working together to perform a specific function.
3. The right atrium.
4. The aorta.
5. The pulmonary artery.
6. The heart muscle, with oxygenated blood (oxygen and glucose for respiration).
7. In the right atrium.
8. Any two: many alveoli give a large surface area; thin walls give a short diffusion distance; a capillary
   network keeps a steep concentration gradient.
9. Blood in veins is at low pressure, so valves prevent it flowing backwards. Blood in arteries is at high
   pressure from the ventricles, so it is pushed forwards.
10. 27 / 20 x 60 = **81 beats per minute**.
11. 65 x 80 = **5200 cm³ per minute** (5.2 dm³ per minute).
12. 2 / 4 = **0.5 mm/s**. 1 mm = 1000 µm, so 1000 / 25 = **40 cells**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "the heart pumps blood to the lungs" without saying it is the **right ventricle**.
- Stating that arteries carry oxygenated blood as a definition. It fails for the pulmonary artery.
- Putting the pacemaker in the left atrium or the ventricles.
- Saying the left ventricle is thicker "because it pumps harder" without linking it to pumping around the **whole body**.
- Describing capillary walls as "thin" instead of **one cell thick**, with no mention of diffusion distance.
- Listing an alveolus adaptation without the effect on diffusion.
- Saying gas exchange happens in the trachea or bronchi. It happens in the **alveoli**.
- Missing units in rate answers, or forgetting to multiply by 60 to get beats per minute.
- Comparing sizes in different units (mm against µm) without converting.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the levels of organisation in AQA GCSE Biology?**
Cell, tissue, organ, organ system, organism. An organ contains more than one type of tissue.

**What is double circulation?**
Blood passes through the heart twice in each complete circuit: the right ventricle pumps blood to the lungs, and the left ventricle pumps blood to the rest of the body.

**What controls the natural heart rate?**
A group of cells in the right atrium acts as a pacemaker. Artificial pacemakers are electrical devices that correct irregularities in the heart rate.

**Why is the left ventricle wall thickest?**
The left ventricle pumps blood around the whole body, so it needs a thicker muscular wall than the right ventricle, which pumps blood only to the lungs.

**Do I need to know the names of the heart valves?**
No. You need to know that valves stop backflow, but not the names of the valves.
