The specification labels Units 1 and 2 as the International AS and Units 3 and 4 as the extra International A2 content, so this is Unit 4 material for the full International A Level only. ## Questions

**1.** Country B cuts its tariff on imported tractors from 25% to 10%. The world price is 300.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage fall in the landed price. **[2]**
**(b)** Price elasticity of demand for imported tractors is -2. Calculate the percentage change in quantity demanded. **[1]**

**2.** A floating currency moves from 20 to 25 local currency units (LCU) per dollar.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage fall in the dollar value of one LCU. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the new LCU price of an import costing 40 dollars. **[1]**

**3.** A microfinance lender gives a loan of 500, repaid in six monthly instalments of 95.

**(a)** Calculate the total interest and the flat interest rate. **[2]**
**(b)** The borrower's small business earns an extra 105 a month. Calculate the monthly net gain. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the flat rate understates the true cost of borrowing. **[1]**

**4.** A state railway is sold for 900m. The government borrows at 4%. The buyer expects annual profit of 117m.

**(a)** Calculate the annual interest the government saves. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the buyer's expected return on the price paid. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the annual amount the government gives up if the forecast is right. **[1]**
**(d)** State one policy to protect consumers after the sale. **[1]**

**5.** Country C trains technicians at a cost of 2,000 each. Each earns an extra 360 a year for 12 years. Use a 6% discount rate.

**(a)** Calculate the annuity factor and the present value of one technician's extra earnings. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the net present value per technician. **[1]**
**(c)** State what your answer shows. **[1]**

**6.** A road costs 450m and brings benefits of 70m a year for 12 years. Use a 7% discount rate.

**(a)** Calculate the net present value. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the net present value if costs overrun by 30%. **[1]**
**(c)** Give one benefit of the road not captured in the 70m. **[1]**

**7.** Country D pegs its currency at 8 LCU per dollar. The market rate would be 10.

**(a)** An export is priced at 2,000 LCU. Calculate its dollar price at the peg and at the market rate, and the percentage by which the peg raises the price. **[2]**
**(b)** The central bank loses 400m dollars of reserves a year and holds 1,600m. State how many years the reserves last. **[1]**
**(c)** State one way to support the peg other than using reserves. **[1]**

**8.** A buffer stock authority faces demand Qd = 600 - 4P.

**(a)** After a good harvest, supply is 200 + 4P and the floor price is 56. Calculate the quantity bought and the cost. **[3]**
**(b)** After a poor harvest, supply is 40 + 4P and the ceiling price is 64. Calculate the quantity released and the revenue. **[3]**

**9.** A joint venture in Country E earns pre-tax profit of 360m. Tax is 30%. The local firm owns 45% and a TNC owns 55%.

**(a)** Calculate profit after tax and the TNC's share. **[2]**
**(b)** Show that a larger share of pre-tax profit stays in Country E than if the plant were wholly foreign-owned. **[3]**

**10.** Country F puts a 40% tariff on imported car parts. The world price is 50. The domestic firm's unit cost is 70 and falls 10% a year.

**(a)** Show that the tariff makes the landed price equal to the firm's cost. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the first year in which the firm's cost is below the world price. **[2]**
**(c)** Evaluate protectionism as a strategy for this industry. **[6]**

**11.** Country A: 70% of export earnings come from one crop with volatile prices. Fuel subsidies cost 18% of government revenue. Average schooling is 5 years. Only 12% of adults hold a bank account. Many state firms make losses, and rural roads are poor.

Assess whether market-orientated or interventionist strategies would do more for development in Country A. **[12]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Before: 300 x 1.25 = 375. [1] After: 300 x 1.10 = 330, so the change is (330 - 375)/375 = **-12%**, a fall of 12%. [1]
**(b)** -2 x -12% = **+24%**. [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show both landed prices before the percentage. A bare 12% invites doubt about whether you used the tariff-inclusive price or the world price.

**2. (a)** 1/20 = 0.05 and 1/25 = 0.04 dollars. [1] (0.04 - 0.05)/0.05 = **-20%**. [1]
**(b)** 40 x 25 = **1,000 LCU**, up from 800. [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The fall in the currency (20%) is not the same as the rise in the price of the dollar (25%). Say which one you are calculating.

**3. (a)** Total repaid 6 x 95 = 570, so interest = **70**. [1] 70/500 = **14%** flat. [1]
**(b)** 105 - 95 = **10 a month**. [1]
**(c)** The balance owed falls each month, so the borrower has the use of less than 500 on average; the true annual rate is higher than 14%. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Explanations are stronger when they say why the rate is higher, not just that it is. Mention the falling balance.

**4. (a)** 0.04 x 900m = **36m**. [1]
**(b)** 117/900 = **13%**. [1]
**(c)** 117m - 36m = **81m a year**. [1]
**(d)** A price cap set by an independent regulator. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A policy to protect consumers must address the monopoly problem. "Fair prices" alone is too vague.

**5. (a)** Annuity factor = (1 - 1.06^-12)/0.06 = **8.384**. [1] PV = 360 x 8.384 = **3,018**. [1]
**(b)** NPV = 3,018 - 2,000 = **+1,018**. [1]
**(c)** Discounted benefits exceed the cost, so training is worthwhile on this basis. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Interpret the sign. A calculated NPV with no comment is weaker than one that links the sign to the decision.

**6. (a)** Annuity factor = (1 - 1.07^-12)/0.07 = 7.943. [1] PV = 70 x 7.943 = 556. [1] NPV = 556 - 450 = **+106m**. [1]
**(b)** Cost 450 x 1.3 = 585, so NPV = 556 - 585 = **-29m**. [1]
**(c)** For example, access for rural households or the attraction of FDI. [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Name a specific benefit with a clear link to development. "It helps the economy" is too general.

**7. (a)** Peg: 2,000/8 = 250 dollars. Market: 2,000/10 = 200 dollars. [1] The peg raises the price by 50/200 = **25%**. [1]
**(b)** 1,600/400 = **4 years**. [1]
**(c)** Raise interest rates, or use capital controls. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Work out which way the peg is out of line. Here the currency is overvalued, so exports are dearer, not cheaper.

**8. (a)** At 56: Qd = 600 - 224 = 376. [1] Qs = 200 + 224 = 424, so the authority buys **48**. [1] Cost = 48 x 56 = **2,688**. [1]
**(b)** At 64: Qd = 600 - 256 = 344 and Qs = 40 + 256 = 296. [1] Release **48**. [1] Revenue = 48 x 64 = **3,072**. [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the support price, not the free-market price, to find both quantities. Using the market equilibrium is a common slip.

**9. (a)** Tax = 108m, so profit after tax = **252m**. [1] TNC share = 0.55 x 252 = **138.6m**. [1]
**(b)** Local share = 0.45 x 252 = 113.4m. [1] Stays in the country = 108 + 113.4 = 221.4m, which is 221.4/360 = **61.5%**. [1] A wholly foreign-owned plant leaves only the tax, **30%**. [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "Show that" needs both figures and a comparison. Giving only 61.5% leaves the claim unproven.

**10. (a)** 50 x 1.40 = **70**, equal to the unit cost. [1]
**(b)** Costs: year 3 = 70 x 0.9^3 = 51.0 (above 50); year 4 = 70 x 0.9^4 = 45.9 (below 50). [1] The first year is **year 4**. [1]
**(c)** Credit these six points:
- Tariff gives time for learning to cut costs; the cost falls 10% a year. [1]
- The tariff needed falls each year, about 40%, 26%, 13%, 2%, so it can be phased out. [1]
- Consumers pay 20 extra per unit in year 0 (70 against 50). [1]
- Risk: firms lobby to keep protection and never become efficient. [1]
- Retaliation could hurt exporters, and other users of car parts face higher costs. [1]
- Judgement: justified if time-limited and the industry shows falling costs; weaker if cost falls stop. [1]
**[9]**
*Examiner insight:* A strong evaluation uses the data (the falling cost) and reaches a judgement conditional on time limits and results.

**11.** Credit developed points such as:
- Removing fuel subsidies frees 18% of revenue for schools or roads. [1]
- It raises prices for poor households, so cash transfers are needed. [1]
- Compensation requires administration that may not exist. [1]
- Privatising loss-making firms may cut losses and raise efficiency. [1]
- A private monopoly needs regulation, and job losses may follow. [1]
- Microfinance reaches the 88% without bank accounts, but rates are high and loans small. [1]
- A buffer stock band for the main crop stabilises export earnings and farm incomes. [1]
- Funds, storage and forecasting limit it, and a floor set too high builds stock. [1]
- Education spending raises productivity, but payback is slow and skilled workers may emigrate. [1]
- Road building lowers costs and links farms to markets, but is costly and prone to corruption. [1]
- Judgement: a mix, with subsidy reform helping to pay for schooling and roads, and a price band for the crop. [1]
- Justification: the crop dependence and low schooling are the binding constraints in Country A, so interventionist measures carry more weight, while market reforms fund them. [1]
**[12]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the data in the stem (70% crop dependence, 5 years schooling, 88% without accounts) and make a judgement about priority rather than listing strategies.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the world price instead of the tariff-inclusive landed price.
- Confusing the fall in a currency's value with the rise in the price of the dollar.
- Quoting a flat microfinance rate as the true cost.
- Using the market price instead of the floor or ceiling in buffer stock questions.
- Giving a sign for NPV without saying what it means for the decision.
- Treating all of a TNC's profit as lost to the country, ignoring tax and local ownership.
- Describing strategies in turn with no comparison or judgement for the country in the stem.
- Forgetting that the effects depend on time, institutions and the size of the economy.
