## Condensed recall

### Distribution

- Oceans: about 97% of all water.
- Freshwater: about two-thirds ice, almost all the rest groundwater; lakes, rivers, soil, air and organisms about 1%.
- **Blue water**: liquid freshwater in rivers, lakes and aquifers (irrigation draws on it).
- **Green water**: soil water used by plants and returned by evapotranspiration (rain-fed farming and natural ecosystems use it).

### Drainage basin budget

```
P = Q + E ± ΔS
```

- Positive ΔS: stores (soil, groundwater) are filling.
- Negative ΔS: stores are draining; baseflow keeps the river running in dry spells.
- Long-term ΔS ≈ 0 unless groundwater is over-abstracted.
- Volume = depth (m) × area (m²). 1 km² = 10⁶ m²; 1 mm = 0.001 m.

Short reminder: P = 700 mm, E = 450 mm, Q = 280 mm gives ΔS = 700 − 280 − 450 = **−30 mm** (storage fell).

### Storm hydrograph

| Part | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Rising limb | Discharge rising as quick flows arrive |
| Peak discharge | Highest flow after the storm |
| Lag time | Peak rainfall to peak discharge |
| Falling (recession) limb | Discharge declining as stores drain |
| Baseflow | Groundwater-fed flow between storms |

- **Flashier** (short lag, high peak): urban surfaces, drains, steep slopes, saturated or frozen soil, deforestation, high stream density.
- **Slower** (long lag, low peak): forest, permeable rock and soil, gentle slopes, wetlands.

### Ocean circulation

- **Surface currents**: wind-driven; deflected by the Coriolis effect into **gyres** (clockwise north, anticlockwise south).
- Warm currents carry heat poleward; the Gulf Stream and North Atlantic Drift give north-west Europe milder winters.
- **Thermohaline circulation**: density-driven by temperature and salinity; cold, salty water sinks in the North Atlantic; one loop takes centuries.
- Greenland meltwater freshens surface water and could weaken the Atlantic circulation.

### Upwelling and ENSO

- **Upwelling**: offshore winds move surface water away; cold, nutrient-rich water rises; high productivity and fisheries (e.g. Peru).
- **El Niño**: trade winds weaken; warm water spreads east; upwelling off Peru weakens; fish catches fall; flooding in western South America, drought in parts of Australia and Indonesia.
- **La Niña**: trade winds strengthen; upwelling increases.

### Stratification

- Water is densest at about 4 °C.
- Summer layers: **epilimnion** (warm, mixed, oxygenated), **thermocline** (rapid temperature fall, blocks mixing), **hypolimnion** (cold, dark, oxygen used by decomposers and not replaced).
- **Turnover** (autumn, often spring): uniform density lets wind mix the whole lake; oxygen goes down, nutrients come up, phytoplankton bloom follows.
- Ocean warming strengthens stratification: less oxygen at depth, fewer nutrients at the surface, lower productivity.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Drainage basin | Area drained by a river and its tributaries |
| Watershed | Boundary between drainage basins |
| Water budget | Balance of precipitation, runoff, evapotranspiration and storage change |
| Baseflow | River flow supplied by groundwater |
| Lag time | Time from peak rainfall to peak discharge |
| Gyre | Large circular system of wind-driven surface currents |
| Coriolis effect | Deflection of moving air and water caused by Earth's rotation |
| Thermohaline circulation | Deep ocean circulation driven by differences in temperature and salinity |
| Upwelling | Rise of cold, nutrient-rich deep water to the surface |
| El Niño | Periodic weakening of Pacific trade winds and warming of the eastern Pacific |
| Thermocline | Layer of rapid temperature change with depth |
| Turnover | Seasonal mixing of a whole lake once it has a uniform density |

## Exam traps

- Leaving out ΔS or giving it the wrong sign.
- Unit slips: km² → m² is × 10⁶; mm → m is ÷ 1000.
- Measuring lag time from the start of rainfall instead of its peak.
- "The thermohaline circulation is driven by wind." It is driven by density.
- El Niño answers that describe warm water but not the loss of upwelling and its effect on productivity.
- Saying the hypolimnion loses oxygen because it is cold: it loses oxygen because mixing stops while decomposition continues.
- Melting sea ice does not raise sea level directly (it already floats); melting land ice does.

## Self-test

1. Write the water budget equation and define each term.
2. A basin receives 920 mm of rain; evapotranspiration is 610 mm and runoff 350 mm. Calculate ΔS and state what it shows.
3. Convert a storage gain of 25 mm over a 400 km² basin into cubic metres.
4. Define lag time.
5. Give three basin features that shorten lag time.
6. Why does a river keep flowing after weeks without rain?
7. What drives surface currents, and what drives the thermohaline circulation?
8. Why does the North Atlantic Drift matter to north-west Europe?
9. Explain why upwelling zones support large fisheries.
10. What happens to upwelling off Peru during El Niño?
11. Name the three layers of a stratified lake in summer.
12. What does autumn turnover do to oxygen and nutrients?

### Answers

1. P = Q + E ± ΔS: precipitation equals runoff plus evapotranspiration plus or minus change in storage.
2. ΔS = 920 − 350 − 610 = **−40 mm**; storage fell, so soils and groundwater lost water.
3. 0.025 m × 400 × 10⁶ m² = **10 × 10⁶ m³** (10 million m³).
4. The time between peak rainfall and peak discharge.
5. Any three: impermeable surfaces; drains; steep slopes; saturated or frozen soil; thin soil; deforestation; high stream density.
6. Groundwater continues to seep into the channel as baseflow.
7. Surface currents: wind (deflected by the Coriolis effect). Thermohaline: density differences from temperature and salinity.
8. It carries warm water north-east, giving milder winters than other places at the same latitude.
9. Rising deep water brings nutrients to the sunlit surface, so phytoplankton productivity is high, supporting large food webs.
10. It weakens, so nutrient supply falls and fish catches drop.
11. Epilimnion, thermocline (metalimnion), hypolimnion.
12. It mixes oxygen down to the bottom water and brings nutrients up to the surface.
