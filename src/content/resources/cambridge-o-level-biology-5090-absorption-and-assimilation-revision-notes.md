These notes condense subtopic **8.3 Absorption and assimilation** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series: where absorption happens, how it differs from assimilation, the structure of a villus, why villi and microvilli matter, water absorption, and the hepatic portal vein. The full explanations and the worked surface area model are in the [8.3 study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-absorption-and-assimilation/). Then try the [8.3 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-absorption-and-assimilation-practice/).

## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning in exam wording |
|---|---|
| Absorption | Movement of nutrients from the intestines into the cells lining the digestive system and then into the blood |
| Assimilation | Uptake and use by cells of nutrients from the blood |
| Villus | Finger-like projection of the ileum wall that increases surface area |
| Lacteal | Lymph vessel in the centre of a villus that absorbs fatty acids and glycerol |
| Hepatic portal vein | Vein that carries most of the molecules and ions absorbed from the ileum to the liver |

## Processes of absorption

| Process | Example |
|---|---|
| Diffusion | Glucose and amino acids move down a concentration gradient into the blood |
| Osmosis | Water moves from the gut lumen into the cells and blood |
| Active transport | Glucose absorbed against its gradient, using energy from respiration |

## Villus: structure and function

| Feature | Function |
|---|---|
| Large number of villi | Increase internal surface area of the ileum |
| Microvilli on epithelial cells | Increase surface area further |
| Wall one cell thick | Short diffusion distance |
| Capillary network | Absorbs glucose, amino acids, ions and water-soluble vitamins; carries them away, keeping the concentration gradient steep |
| Lacteal | Absorbs fatty acids and glycerol |
| Many mitochondria in the cells | Energy for active transport |

## Why a large surface area?

More nutrients can be absorbed in the same time, so the body gets its nutrients quickly and efficiently.

Model: a 10 mm by 10 mm gut wall (100 mm squared) carrying 2000 cylindrical villi of length 1 mm and diameter 0.2 mm gains 2000 x 3.14 x 0.2 x 1 = 1257 mm squared, giving a total of 1357 mm squared. That is about 13.6 times larger.

## Water absorption

- Water is absorbed from the **small intestine** and the **colon**.
- **Most** absorption of water takes place in the **small intestine**.
- The colon absorbs most of the water that remains.
- Water moves by osmosis.

## Hepatic portal vein

- Carries blood from the ileum to the **liver**.
- Carries most absorbed molecules and ions (not fatty acids and glycerol, which pass through the lacteals).
- The liver can then store glucose as glycogen, process amino acids and remove harmful substances.

## Assimilation

| Nutrient | Use |
|---|---|
| Glucose | Respiration; stored as glycogen |
| Amino acids | Make proteins |
| Fatty acids and glycerol | Make lipids; respiration |
| Ions and vitamins | Specific jobs (for example iron for haemoglobin) |

## The journey of glucose

1. Starch is digested to glucose in the small intestine.
2. Glucose is absorbed into the capillaries of a villus.
3. It travels in the **hepatic portal vein** to the liver.
4. The liver may store it as glycogen or release it into the blood.
5. Cells take up glucose from the blood (assimilation) and use it in respiration.

## Two routes out of a villus

| Route | Nutrients |
|---|---|
| Capillaries (then hepatic portal vein to liver) | Glucose, amino acids, ions, water-soluble vitamins, water |
| Lacteal (lymph) | Fatty acids and glycerol |

## Short model answers

**Explain how the structure of a villus is adapted for absorption.** Its wall is one cell thick, so the diffusion distance is short. It has microvilli, which increase the surface area. It has capillaries, which carry away glucose and amino acids and keep the concentration gradient steep. It has a lacteal for fatty acids and glycerol.

**State what is meant by assimilation.** The uptake and use by body cells of nutrients from the blood.

**State the function of the hepatic portal vein.** It carries most of the molecules and ions absorbed from the ileum to the liver.

**Explain why most water absorption happens in the small intestine.** Large volumes of water enter the gut in food, drink and secretions, and the small intestine has a large surface area, so most of the water is absorbed there before it reaches the colon.

## Exam traps

- Absorption is into the **blood**; assimilation is **use by cells**.
- Glucose and amino acids go into the **capillaries**; fatty acids and glycerol go into the **lacteal**.
- The hepatic portal vein goes **to the liver**.
- Most water is absorbed in the **small intestine**, not the colon.
- A villus wall is **one cell thick**.
- Do not say that the stomach is the main site of absorption.

## Self-test

1. Which region of the gut is the main site of absorption?
2. Define absorption.
3. Define assimilation.
4. Name the three processes that move nutrients across the gut wall.
5. Why is the wall of a villus only one cell thick?
6. What is the function of the capillaries in a villus?
7. What is the function of a lacteal?
8. Why do villi and microvilli matter?
9. Where is most water absorbed?
10. Name the vessel that carries absorbed glucose to the liver.
11. State one use of amino acids after assimilation.
12. The ileum has a wall area of 200 mm squared before villi and 2800 mm squared with villi. How many times larger is it?

### Answers

1. The small intestine (the ileum).
2. Movement of nutrients from the intestines into the cells lining the digestive system and then into the blood.
3. Uptake and use by cells of nutrients from the blood.
4. Diffusion, osmosis and active transport.
5. To give a short diffusion distance.
6. To absorb glucose, amino acids, ions and vitamins, and carry them away in the blood.
7. To absorb fatty acids and glycerol.
8. They increase the internal surface area, so more nutrients are absorbed at a time.
9. In the small intestine.
10. The hepatic portal vein.
11. To make proteins (for example enzymes or muscle).
12. 2800 divided by 200 = 14 times larger.
