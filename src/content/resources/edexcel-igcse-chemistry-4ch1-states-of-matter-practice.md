These questions cover sub-topic 1(a) States of matter of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) specification, Issue 3 (September 2024): points 1.1 to 1.7C. Questions marked **(Paper 2 only)** test the C-referenced points 1.5C to 1.7C. ## Section A: short questions

**1.** Describe the arrangement and movement of the particles in a solid. **[2]**

**2.** Explain why a liquid takes the shape of its container but a solid does not. **[2]**

**3.** Name the process in each case.

**(a)** Water vapour forming droplets on the inside of a cold window. **[1]**
**(b)** A puddle drying up on a warm day without reaching 100 °C. **[1]**
**(c)** Molten candle wax becoming solid. **[1]**

**4.** The table gives the melting and boiling points of four substances.

| Substance | Melting point / °C | Boiling point / °C |
|---|---|---|
| A | −117 | 78 |
| B | 1083 | 2567 |
| C | −218 | −183 |
| D | 44 | 280 |

**(a)** Identify the substance that is a gas at 25 °C. **[1]**
**(b)** Identify the substance that is a liquid at 60 °C but a solid at 25 °C. **[1]**
**(c)** Give the physical state of A at −150 °C. **[1]**

**5.** A student takes 1 cm³ of a blue copper(II) sulfate solution and makes it up to 10 cm³ with water. She repeats this twice more with each new solution.

**(a)** Calculate the fraction of the original concentration left after the three dilutions. **[1]**
**(b)** The final solution is very pale blue. State what this suggests about the size of the particles in copper(II) sulfate. **[1]**

**6.** State what is meant by each term.

**(a)** solute **[1]**
**(b)** saturated solution **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** Liquid substance Y is cooled from 95 °C. Its temperature is recorded every minute.

| Time / min | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature / °C | 95 | 86 | 80 | 80 | 80 | 80 | 72 | 65 |

**(a)** State the freezing point of Y. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the temperature does not change between 2 and 5 minutes. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe how the arrangement and energy of the particles at 7 minutes differ from those at 0 minutes. **[2]**
**(d)** A second sample of Y contains an impurity. Suggest how its cooling curve would differ. **[1]**

**8.** A long glass tube is clamped horizontally. Cotton wool soaked in concentrated ammonia solution is put in one end and cotton wool soaked in concentrated hydrochloric acid in the other end at the same time. Both ends are sealed. (Mr: NH₃ = 17, HCl = 36.5)

**(a)** A white solid forms inside the tube. Name the white solid and write a word equation for its formation. **[2]**
**(b)** State and explain where in the tube the white solid forms. **[3]**
**(c)** The experiment is repeated with the tube in a warm room. Predict and explain the effect on the time taken for the white solid to appear. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why the ends are sealed. **[1]**

**9.** A gas jar of air is placed upside down on a gas jar of brown nitrogen dioxide gas, with a glass plate between them. The plate is removed.

**(a)** Describe what is seen after some time. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain, in terms of particles, why the colour spreads even though nitrogen dioxide is denser than air. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the colour takes several minutes to spread rather than filling the top jar at once. **[1]**

**10.** Explain, in terms of particles and energy, what happens when ice at −10 °C is heated until it becomes steam at 110 °C. Refer to the stages where the temperature stays constant. **[6]**

**11.** **(Paper 2 only)** The solubility of potassium chlorate in water is shown.

| Temperature / °C | 10 | 30 | 50 | 70 | 90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Solubility / g per 100 g water | 5 | 10 | 19 | 30 | 46 |

**(a)** Describe how a student should plot these results as a solubility curve. **[3]**
**(b)** A solution contains 6.0 g of potassium chlorate in 20 g of water at 90 °C. Show whether the solution is saturated or unsaturated at 90 °C. **[2]**
**(c)** The solution in (b) is cooled. Use the table to determine the temperature at which crystals first appear. **[1]**
**(d)** The solution is cooled further to 10 °C. Calculate the mass of crystals that forms. **[2]**

**12.** **(Paper 2 only)** A student investigates the solubility of a salt, W, in water at 30 °C.

| Measurement | Mass / g |
|---|---|
| Empty evaporating basin | 28.40 |
| Basin + saturated solution | 54.40 |
| Basin + dry solid (constant mass) | 35.40 |

**(a)** Calculate the solubility of W at 30 °C in g per 100 g of water. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the saturated solution must be made in a water bath. **[1]**
**(c)** Some undissolved solid was poured into the basin with the solution. State and explain the effect on the calculated solubility. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Particles are close together in a regular arrangement [1]. They vibrate about fixed positions [1].
*Marking note:* "Do not move" scores zero for the movement mark.

**2.** In a liquid the particles can move past one another, so the liquid flows [1]. In a solid the particles are held in fixed positions by strong forces, so the shape cannot change [1].

**3. (a)** Condensing [1].
**(b)** Evaporation [1].
**(c)** Freezing [1].
*Marking note:* "Boiling" for (b) is wrong: the water never reaches its boiling point and only surface particles escape.

**4. (a)** C [1].
**(b)** D [1].
**(c)** Solid [1] (−150 °C is below its melting point of −117 °C).

**5. (a)** 1/1000 [1].
**(b)** The particles are very small (and there are a very large number of them in a small mass) [1].

**6. (a)** The substance that dissolves in a solvent [1].
**(b)** A solution in which no more solute can dissolve at that temperature [1].

**7. (a)** 80 °C [1].
**(b)** Y is freezing [1]. Energy is released as forces of attraction form between particles, which balances the energy lost to the surroundings [1].
**(c)** At 7 minutes the particles are in a regular arrangement in fixed positions (at 0 minutes they were irregular and moving past each other) [1]. They have less energy [1].
**(d)** It would freeze over a range of temperatures / no sharp flat section (or a sloping section, starting at a different temperature) [1].

**8. (a)** Ammonium chloride [1]. Ammonia + hydrogen chloride → ammonium chloride [1].
**(b)** Nearer the hydrochloric acid end [1]. Ammonia particles have a lower Mr / are lighter [1], so they move faster and diffuse further in the same time [1].
**(c)** Less time [1]. The particles have more energy and move faster, so they meet sooner [1].
**(d)** To stop the gases escaping (both are harmful / irritant) [1].
*Marking note:* In (b) the location mark is not awarded with a reason that says the heavier gas moves faster.

**9. (a)** Both jars become the same pale brown colour [1].
**(b)** Gas particles move randomly in all directions [1], so there is net movement of nitrogen dioxide particles from high concentration to low concentration / they diffuse upwards [1].
**(c)** Nitrogen dioxide particles collide with air particles and change direction many times [1].

**10.** One mark per point, up to 6:
- From −10 °C to 0 °C the ice particles gain energy and vibrate more; temperature rises [1].
- At 0 °C the ice melts; temperature stays constant [1].
- During melting the energy taken in is used to overcome some forces of attraction, so particles can move past one another [1].
- From 0 °C to 100 °C the water particles gain energy and move faster; temperature rises [1].
- At 100 °C the water boils; temperature stays constant while energy overcomes the remaining forces of attraction [1].
- Particles move far apart and randomly as steam; above 100 °C they gain energy and move faster still [1].
*Marking note:* A six-mark answer needs both constant-temperature stages explained, not just named.

**11. (a)** Temperature (°C) on the x-axis and solubility (g per 100 g water) on the y-axis, both labelled with units [1]. A scale that uses more than half the grid, with points plotted accurately [1]. A smooth curve of best fit [1].
**(b)** 6.0 × 100 ÷ 20 = 30 g per 100 g water [1]. This is less than 46 g per 100 g at 90 °C, so the solution is unsaturated [1].
**(c)** 70 °C [1].
**(d)** Mass that stays dissolved at 10 °C = 5 × 20 ÷ 100 = 1.0 g [1]. Crystals = 6.0 − 1.0 = 5.0 g [1].
*Marking note:* In (d), using 46 g instead of the 6.0 g actually present gives the wrong answer because the solution was never saturated at 90 °C.

**12. (a)** Mass of solid = 35.40 − 28.40 = 7.00 g [1]. Mass of water = 54.40 − 35.40 = 19.00 g [1]. Solubility = 7.00 ÷ 19.00 × 100 = 36.8 g per 100 g of water [1].
**(b)** Solubility depends on temperature, so the temperature must be kept at 30 °C [1].
**(c)** The calculated solubility would be too high [1], because the undissolved solid adds to the mass of solid but did not dissolve in the water [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "the particles stop moving" for a solid, or "the particles get bigger" when heated.
- Naming a flat section on a curve without explaining where the energy goes.
- Forgetting to convert to g per 100 g of water before reading a solubility curve.
- Using the solubility at the higher temperature when the solution was not saturated there.
- Giving a solubility without units or temperature.
