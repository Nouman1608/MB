## Section B

**6.** A student used a potometer to measure the distance moved by an air bubble in 5 minutes under different conditions. The results are in the table.

| Condition | Distance moved by bubble (mm) |
|---|---|
| Still air, shade, 20 degrees Celsius | 15 |
| Still air, bright light, 20 degrees Celsius | 25 |
| Moving air, bright light, 20 degrees Celsius | 45 |
| Still air, bright light, 30 degrees Celsius | 40 |

**(a)** Calculate the rate of movement of the bubble, in mm per minute, in moving air and bright light. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in rate when the plant moves from shade to bright light in still air. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why moving air increases the rate of transpiration. **[3]**

**(d)** Explain why the rate was higher at 30 degrees Celsius than at 20 degrees Celsius. **[2]**

**(e)** State **two** precautions that should be taken when setting up a potometer. **[2]**

**7.** Explain how water moves from the soil up to the top of a tall tree, starting from the leaf. **[4]**

**8.** On a hot, dry afternoon a pot plant wilts, but it recovers by the next morning.

**(a)** Explain why the plant wilts. **[3]**

**(b)** Suggest how closing the stomata helps the plant. **[2]**

**9.** The diagram description below shows a transverse section of a stem. It shows vascular bundles arranged in a ring. In each bundle there is a larger tissue towards the centre of the stem and a smaller tissue towards the outside.

**(a)** Identify the tissue towards the centre of each bundle and the tissue towards the outside. **[2]**

**(b)** State the position of the cortex. **[1]**

**(c)** State where the xylem would be in a root. **[1]**

**10.** A potato tuber stores starch. Explain, using the terms source and sink, how the potato tuber can be both. **[3]**

**11.** Describe three differences between the way substances are moved in the xylem and the way they are moved in the phloem. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** C [1]. **[1]**

**3.** The loss of water [1] vapour from the leaves of a plant [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Transport of water and mineral ions [1]; support [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Thick walls containing lignin make it strong, giving support and stopping it collapsing [1]. It has no cell contents, so water can flow freely [1]. The cells are joined end to end with no cross walls, forming a long continuous tube [1]. **[3]**

**6.**

**(a)** Rate = 45 divided by 5 [1] = 9 mm per minute [1].

**(b)** Shade rate = 15 divided by 5 = 3 mm per minute; light rate = 25 divided by 5 = 5 mm per minute [1]. Percentage increase = (5 - 3) divided by 3 x 100 = 66.7 per cent (accept 67) [1].

**(c)** Moving air carries away water vapour from around the leaf [1], keeping the concentration of water vapour outside the leaf low [1], so the concentration gradient stays steeper and more water vapour diffuses out [1].

**(d)** Water evaporates faster at the higher temperature [1], and water vapour molecules have more kinetic energy so they diffuse faster [1].

**(e)** Any two of: cut the shoot under water; make sure there are no air bubbles in the xylem; seal joints with grease; dry the leaves; keep other factors constant [1 each]. **[11]**

**7.** Water evaporates from the mesophyll cells and diffuses out through the stomata [1]. This removes water from the top of the xylem, which pulls water up (transpiration pull) [1]. The water molecules are held together by forces of attraction, so the water moves as a continuous column [1]. Water is drawn into the root from the soil to replace it [1]. **[4]**

**8.**

**(a)** Water loss by transpiration is faster than the uptake by the roots [1]. The cells lose water and turgor pressure falls [1]. The cells become flaccid and no longer support the plant, which droops [1].

**(b)** Closing the stomata reduces the loss of water vapour [1], so less water is lost and the cells can recover turgor [1]. **[5]**

**9.**

**(a)** Xylem towards the centre [1]. Phloem towards the outside [1].

**(b)** Between the ring of bundles and the epidermis (outside the bundles) [1].

**(c)** In the centre of the root [1]. **[4]**

**10.** A source releases sucrose or amino acids and a sink uses or stores them [1]. In summer, the tuber stores starch made from sucrose delivered by the phloem, so it is a sink [1]. In spring it breaks the starch down and sends sugars to the growing shoots, so it is a source [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Any three of: xylem carries water and mineral ions but phloem carries sucrose and amino acids [1]; xylem moves substances upwards only but phloem moves substances up and down [1]; xylem vessels are dead but phloem cells are living [1]; xylem movement is driven by transpiration pull and needs no energy from the plant, but phloem transport uses energy [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving the effect of a factor on transpiration with no explanation.
- Saying humidity increases the rate.
- Mixing up the positions of xylem and phloem.
- Writing that water evaporates "from the stomata".
- Missing out forces of attraction in the explanation of transpiration pull.
