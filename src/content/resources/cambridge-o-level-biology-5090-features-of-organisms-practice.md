## Section A

**1.** Which feature is found in a prokaryote but not in a plant cell?

A: a cell wall

B: DNA

C: no nucleus

D: ribosomes

**[1]**

**2.** An animal has six legs, three body parts and one pair of antennae. To which group does it belong?

A: arachnids

B: crustaceans

C: insects

D: myriapods

**[1]**

**3.** State **two** features of the fungus kingdom. **[2]**

**4.** State **two** features that all vertebrates share and that distinguish them from arthropods. **[2]**

**5.** Describe the main features of a virus. **[3]**

## Section B

**6.** The table gives features of four vertebrates.

| Animal | Body covering | Eggs | Gills |
|---|---|---|---|
| J | feathers | hard shell, on land | no |
| K | moist skin | jelly, laid in water | in young only |
| L | dry scales | leathery shell, on land | no |
| M | hair | none; young born alive | no |

**(a)** Identify the vertebrate group of each of J, K, L and M. **[4]**

**(b)** State one feature, other than those in the table, that M has and J does not. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why K must lay its eggs in water. **[2]**

**7.** A student examines three organisms.

Organism R is made of one cell and has no nucleus. Organism S has many cells, cell walls of cellulose and chloroplasts. Organism T is made of threads, has no chlorophyll and absorbs food from rotting bread.

**(a)** Identify the kingdom of each organism. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain how you decided that T is not a plant. **[2]**

**8.** A leaf has parallel veins and the flower has petals in threes.

**(a)** State whether the plant is a monocotyledon or a dicotyledon. **[1]**

**(b)** State how many seed leaves its seed would contain. **[1]**

**(c)** Describe one way a fern differs in reproduction from this plant. **[2]**

**9.** A spider is often called an insect.

**(a)** Explain why this is incorrect, using two features. **[3]**

**(b)** State the name of the group to which the spider belongs. **[1]**

**10.** Explain why viruses can only replicate in living cells. **[2]**

**11.** *Plasmodium* is a single-celled organism with a nucleus that causes malaria. Identify its kingdom and explain your answer. **[3]**

**12.** A scientist finds a new organism. It is made of one cell, has a cell wall and has circular DNA but no nucleus.

**(a)** Identify the kingdom to which it belongs. **[1]**

**(b)** State **two** further features you would expect it to have, or not have. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how you would show that it is not a fungus. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** C [1]. **[1]**

**2.** C [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Any two of: nucleus present [1]; cell walls not made of cellulose (chitin) [1]; no chlorophyll [1]; made of threads (hyphae) in many fungi [1]; feeds by releasing enzymes and absorbing the digested food [1]; yeast is single-celled [1]. **[2]**

**4.** They have a backbone [1] and an internal skeleton; they do not have an external skeleton (exoskeleton) [1]. **[2]**

**5.** A virus has a protein coat [1] and genetic material (DNA or RNA) inside it [1]. It is not made of cells (it has no nucleus, cytoplasm or membrane) [1]. **[3]**

**6.**

**(a)** J: bird [1]. K: amphibian [1]. L: reptile [1]. M: mammal [1].

**(b)** Any one of: mammary glands to feed young with milk; external ears; teeth [1].

**(c)** The eggs have only a jelly covering and no shell [1], so they would dry out on land [1]. **[7]**

**7.**

**(a)** R: prokaryote [1]. S: plant [1]. T: fungus [1].

**(b)** T has no chlorophyll, so it cannot photosynthesise [1]. It feeds on rotting bread, absorbing food that it has digested outside its body, which is not how plants feed [1]. **[5]**

**8.**

**(a)** Monocotyledon [1].

**(b)** One [1].

**(c)** A fern reproduces by spores [1], whereas the flowering plant reproduces using flowers that produce seeds [1]. **[4]**

**9.**

**(a)** Insects have six legs and a spider has eight [1]. Insects have three body parts but a spider has two [1]. Insects usually have antennae and wings, but a spider has none [1].

**(b)** Arachnids [1]. **[4]**

**10.** A virus is not made of cells and has no machinery of its own [1], so it must enter a host cell and use the cell's own machinery to make new virus particles [1]. **[2]**

**11.** Protoctist [1]. It has a nucleus, so it is not a prokaryote [1], and it is single-celled, so it is not a plant, an animal or a many-celled fungus [1]. **[3]**

**12.**

**(a)** Prokaryote [1].

**(b)** Any two of: ribosomes [1]; plasmids (possibly) [1]; cell membrane and cytoplasm [1]; no mitochondria, chloroplasts or nucleus [1].

**(c)** A fungus has a nucleus [1], but this organism has no nucleus, only circular DNA in the cytoplasm [1]. **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling a virus a cell or placing it in a kingdom.
- Writing "skin" instead of the exact covering (fur, feathers, scales).
- Mixing up the leaf vein patterns of monocotyledons and dicotyledons.
- Naming a spider as an insect.
- Saying that bacteria have no DNA.
