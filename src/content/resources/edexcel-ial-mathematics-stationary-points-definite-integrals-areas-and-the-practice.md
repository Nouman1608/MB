## Questions

**1.** Find the coordinates of the stationary points of the curve y = x³ + 3x² − 45x + 2 and determine their nature. **[4]**

**2.** The function f is given by f(x) = 2x³ − 9x² − 24x. State the range of x over which f is increasing. **[4]**

**3.** A curve has equation y = 3x + 12/x for x ≠ 0. Find the coordinates of its stationary points and determine their nature. **[6]**

**4.** The curve C has equation y = (x + 1)²(x − 2).

**(a)** Find where C crosses or touches each axis, giving coordinates. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the coordinates of the stationary points of C and determine their nature. **[3]**
**(c)** Sketch C, labelling the points found in (a) and (b). **[1]**

**5.** A closed storage box has height h cm and a square base with sides of length x cm. Its six faces have a total area of 600 cm².

**(a)** Show that the volume of the box is V = 150x − x³/2 cm³. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the value of x for which V is a maximum, justify that it is a maximum, and find this greatest volume. **[4]**

**6.** Evaluate ∫ from 1 to 9 of (x + 4)/√x dx. **[4]**

**7.** Given that k > 1 and ∫ from 1 to k of (4x − 3) dx = 10, find the value of k. **[4]**

**8.** The curve with equation y = 9x − x² and the line with equation y = 2x + 10 intersect at two points. Find the area of the finite region enclosed between them. **[6]**

**9.** Find the total area of the finite regions enclosed between the curve y = x(x + 1)(x − 3) and the x-axis. **[6]**

**10.** The curves y = 2x² − 6x and y = 6x − x² intersect at two points.

**(a)** Find the x-coordinates of the points of intersection. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the area of the region enclosed between the curves. **[4]**

**11.** (a) Use the trapezium rule with 4 strips to estimate ∫ from 0 to 2 of x³ dx. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the exact value of the integral and state, with a reason, whether your answer to (a) is an over-estimate or an under-estimate. **[2]**
**(c)** With 2 strips the trapezium rule gives 5. Compare the errors for 2 and 4 strips and say what happens to the error when the number of strips is doubled. **[2]**

**12.** The depth of a river, in metres, is measured at 1 m intervals across its 6 m width, starting at one bank.

| Distance from bank (m) | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Depth (m) | 0 | 1.2 | 2.1 | 2.6 | 2.4 | 1.5 | 0 |

**(a)** Use the trapezium rule with 6 strips to estimate the cross-sectional area of the river. **[3]**
**(b)** Use the trapezium rule with 3 strips, using only the depths at 0, 2, 4 and 6 m, to give a second estimate. **[2]**
**(c)** Comment on the difference between your two estimates. **[2]**
**(d)** Give one reason why the trapezium rule gives only an approximation here. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** dy/dx = 3x² + 6x − 45 = 3(x + 5)(x − 3) [1]
dy/dx = 0 gives x = 3 or x = −5 [1]
y = −79 when x = 3 and y = 177 when x = −5 [1]
d²y/dx² = 6x + 6, which is 24 > 0 at x = 3 and −24 < 0 at x = −5. **Minimum at (3, −79); maximum at (−5, 177)** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give both coordinates and say how you know the nature. A statement such as "it is a minimum" with no sign of d²y/dx² or of the gradient gives no evidence for the claim.

**2.** f′(x) = 6x² − 18x − 24 = 6(x − 4)(x + 1) [2]
f is increasing where f′(x) > 0 [1]
The quadratic opens upwards, so **x < −1 or x > 4** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Increasing" means f′(x) > 0, so say so. A single inequality such as −1 < x < 4 is the decreasing interval, and the answer must list two separate ranges.

**3.** y = 3x + 12x⁻¹, so dy/dx = 3 − 12x⁻² [1]
3 − 12/x² = 0 gives x² = 4, so x = ±2 [1]
At x = 2, y = 12; at x = −2, y = −12 [1]
d²y/dx² = 24x⁻³ = 24/x³ [1]
At x = 2 it is 3 > 0, so **(2, 12) is a minimum** [1]
At x = −2 it is −3 < 0, so **(−2, −12) is a maximum** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Both roots of x² = 4 are needed. Notice the "maximum" is lower than the "minimum": these are local turning points, not the greatest and least values of y.

**4. (a)** y = 0 at x = −1 and x = 2 [1]; y = (1)(−2) = −2 when x = 0 [1]
**(b)** y = x³ − 3x − 2, so dy/dx = 3x² − 3 = 3(x − 1)(x + 1) [1]
Stationary points at x = 1, y = −4 and x = −1, y = 0 [1]
d²y/dx² = 6x: −6 at x = −1 (maximum), 6 at x = 1 (minimum). **Maximum (−1, 0); minimum (1, −4)** [1]
**(c)** Cubic with positive x³ coefficient; touches the x-axis at (−1, 0), crosses at (2, 0), passes through (0, −2) with minimum (1, −4) [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A repeated root means the curve touches the axis at that point, and that point is also the turning point. In a sketch, shape and labelled features matter more than scale.

**5. (a)** Surface area: 2x² + 4xh = 600 [1]
h = (300 − x²)/(2x) [1]
V = x²h = x(300 − x²)/2 = **150x − x³/2** [1]
**(b)** dV/dx = 150 − 3x²/2 [1]
dV/dx = 0 gives x² = 100, so **x = 10** (x > 0) [1]
d²V/dx² = −3x = −30 < 0, so V is a maximum [1]
V = 1500 − 500 = **1000 cm³** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* A "show that" needs every step written, including where h comes from. In the second part, justifying the maximum is a separate requirement from solving dV/dx = 0.

**6.** (x + 4)/√x = x^(1/2) + 4x^(−1/2) [1]
Antiderivative: (2/3)x^(3/2) + 8x^(1/2) [1]
At 9: 18 + 24 = 42; at 1: 2/3 + 8 = 26/3 [1]
42 − 26/3 = **100/3** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Split the fraction before integrating. Substitute both limits, and leave the exact fraction unless a decimal is asked for.

**7.** [2x² − 3x] from 1 to k = (2k² − 3k) − (−1) [2]
2k² − 3k + 1 = 10 gives 2k² − 3k − 9 = 0 [1]
(2k + 3)(k − 3) = 0, and k > 1, so **k = 3** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The condition k > 1 is there to reject k = −3/2, so reject it explicitly. Leaving both roots usually loses the final credit.

**8.** 9x − x² = 2x + 10 gives x² − 7x + 10 = 0, so x = 2 and x = 5 [2]
At x = 3 the curve gives 18 and the line gives 16, so the curve is above [1]
Integrand: (9x − x²) − (2x + 10) = 7x − x² − 10 [1]
[7x²/2 − x³/3 − 10x] from 2 to 5 = −25/6 − (−26/3) [1]
Area = **9/2** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Check which graph is on top with a test value. Subtracting the wrong way gives a negative area, which signals the error but still has to be corrected.

**9.** Roots at x = −1, 0 and 3 [1]
y = x³ − 2x² − 3x [1]
F(x) = x⁴/4 − 2x³/3 − 3x²/2 [1]
From −1 to 0: 0 − F(−1) = 0 − (−7/12) = 7/12, above the axis [1]
From 0 to 3: F(3) − 0 = −45/4, below the axis, so area 45/4 [1]
Total area = 7/12 + 135/12 = **71/6** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A single integral from −1 to 3 gives −32/3, which is the signed value and not an area. Treat each region separately so that the positive and negative parts do not cancel.

**10. (a)** 2x² − 6x = 6x − x² gives 3x² − 12x = 0, so **x = 0 and x = 4** [2]
**(b)** At x = 1 the curves give −4 and 5, so y = 6x − x² is the upper curve [1]
Integrand: 12x − 3x² [1]
[6x² − x³] from 0 to 4 = 96 − 64 [1]
Area = **32** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Simplify (upper − lower) before integrating, because it halves the work. Make clear which curve you took as upper.

**11. (a)** h = 0.5, with y-values 0, 0.125, 1, 3.375, 8 [1]
(0.5/2)[0 + 8 + 2(0.125 + 1 + 3.375)] [1]
= 0.25 × 17 = **4.25** [1]
**(b)** Exact: [x⁴/4] from 0 to 2 = **4** [1]
4.25 > 4, so **over-estimate**, because the curve bends upwards (y″ = 6x > 0) and the chords lie above it [1]
**(c)** Error with 2 strips = 5 − 4 = 1; error with 4 strips = 0.25 [1]
Doubling the strips **cut the error to about a quarter** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Give a reason tied to the shape of the curve. Saying "trapezia always overshoot" is wrong, because for a curve bending downwards the rule under-estimates.

**12. (a)** h = 1 [1]
(1/2)[0 + 0 + 2(1.2 + 2.1 + 2.6 + 2.4 + 1.5)] [1]
= **9.8 m²** [1]
**(b)** h = 2 and (2/2)[0 + 0 + 2(2.1 + 2.4)] [1]
= **9.0 m²** [1]
**(c)** The estimates differ by 0.8 m² [1]
The 6-strip value uses more measurements, so it is likely to be closer to the true area and the 3-strip value probably underestimates by about 0.8 m² [1]
**(d)** The bed between measurements is curved, but the rule joins the points with straight lines. [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A comment should refer to the numbers and to why one estimate is better. "The more accurate one is bigger" without a reason about strips does not use the information given.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving x-values for stationary points and not the y-coordinates.
- Claiming a nature without a second derivative sign or a gradient check.
- Writing one inequality for an increasing function that has two ranges.
- Forgetting to rewrite 1/x or 1/√x as powers before differentiating or integrating.
- Substituting only the upper limit, or subtracting the limits the wrong way.
- Integrating over a root of the curve and quoting the signed value as an area.
- Omitting the intersection points, or integrating (lower − upper).
- Not justifying a minimum in an optimisation problem.
- Using the wrong number of y-values or the wrong h in the trapezium rule.
- Stating over- or under-estimate with no reason connected to the curve.
