The specification has five statements: isotopes (a), counting particles in atoms and ions (b), the definitions of relative isotopic mass and relative atomic mass (c), using mass spectrometry to find isotopic masses, abundances and relative atomic mass (d), and the terms relative molecular mass and relative formula mass (e).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to give in an answer |
|---|---|
| Atomic number (Z) | Number of protons in the nucleus |
| Mass number (A) | Number of protons plus neutrons |
| Isotopes | Atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons and different masses |
| Relative isotopic mass | Mass of an atom of an isotope compared with 1/12th of the mass of an atom of carbon-12 |
| Relative atomic mass, Ar | Weighted mean mass of an atom of an element compared with 1/12th of the mass of an atom of carbon-12 |
| Relative abundance | Proportion of each isotope in a sample, as a percentage or as relative peak heights |
| Relative molecular mass, Mr | Sum of Ar values for a simple molecule |
| Relative formula mass | Sum of Ar values for a formula unit of a giant structure |

The specification says the two definitions in statement (c) are required. Definitions of relative molecular mass and relative formula mass are not.

## 2.1.1(a) and (b): particles in atoms and ions

| Particle | Relative mass | Relative charge | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proton | 1 | +1 | Nucleus |
| Neutron | 1 | 0 | Nucleus |
| Electron | 1/1836 | −1 | Shells around the nucleus |

**Counting rules**

- Protons = atomic number. This fixes the element.
- Neutrons = mass number − atomic number.
- Electrons in a neutral atom = protons.
- Positive ion: electrons = protons − charge. Negative ion: electrons = protons + charge.

### Worked reminders

| Species | Protons | Neutrons | Electrons |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⁵⁷Fe²⁺ (Z = 26) | 26 | 31 | 24 |
| ⁷⁹Se²⁻ (Z = 34) | 34 | 45 | 36 |
| ³⁹K⁺ (Z = 19) | 19 | 20 | 18 |

**Isotopes and properties.** Isotopes of an element have the same number of protons and electrons, so the same electron arrangement and the same chemical reactions. Their masses differ, so physical properties that depend on mass, such as density, differ slightly.

**Models of the atom.** The specification's guidance (HSW1, HSW7) asks you to see that different models explain different things, and that models change as evidence arrives. Learn the order and the evidence that forced each change:

1. Solid indivisible spheres.
2. Electrons embedded in a positive sphere, after the discovery of the electron.
3. A small dense positive nucleus, after alpha particles were scattered through large angles by metal foil.
4. Electrons in fixed shells (the Bohr model), which explains periodic properties such as similar reactions down a group.

## 2.1.1(c): the carbon-12 standard

- Both definitions compare with **1/12th of the mass of one carbon-12 atom**, so the relative isotopic mass of carbon-12 is exactly 12.
- Relative isotopic mass refers to **one isotope**.
- Relative atomic mass is a **weighted mean** over all the isotopes of an element, weighted by abundance. That is why Ar values are rarely whole numbers.
- Neither quantity has units: each is a ratio of two masses.

## 2.1.1(d): mass spectrum calculations

The specification limits questions to **ions with single charges**, so each peak's m/z equals the relative isotopic mass of that isotope. Knowledge of the mass spectrometer itself is not required. Three types of calculation appear.

### Type 1: percentage abundances given

Ar = Σ(isotopic mass × % abundance) ÷ 100.

Boron: ¹⁰B 19.9%, ¹¹B 80.1%. Ar = (10 × 19.9 + 11 × 80.1) ÷ 100 = **10.8**.

### Type 2: relative peak heights given

Divide by the **sum of the heights**, not by 100.

Magnesium peaks at m/z 24, 25 and 26 with heights 7.9, 1.0 and 1.1.
Ar = (24 × 7.9 + 25 × 1.0 + 26 × 1.1) ÷ (7.9 + 1.0 + 1.1) = 243.2 ÷ 10.0 = **24.3**.

### Type 3: Ar given, abundances unknown

Let one abundance be x% and the other (100 − x)%.

Copper (Ar 63.5) has isotopes of mass 63 and 65, using whole-number masses.
63(100 − x) + 65x = 6350, so 6300 + 2x = 6350 and x = 25.
**75% copper-63 and 25% copper-65.**

With three isotopes and one abundance missing, first find it from 100% minus the others.

### Checks that catch errors

- Ar must lie between the lightest and heaviest isotopic masses.
- Ar lies closer to the mass of the most abundant isotope.
- Give Ar to the precision asked, usually one decimal place.

## 2.1.1(e): Mr and relative formula mass

- Use **relative molecular mass** for simple molecules: H₂O, CO₂, C₆H₁₂O₆.
- Use **relative formula mass** for giant structures: NaCl, MgO, SiO₂.
- Both are calculated by adding Ar values. Multiply bracketed groups and water of crystallisation through.

| Substance | Working | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Ca(NO₃)₂ | 40.1 + 2(14.0 + 3 × 16.0) | 164.1 |
| CuSO₄·5H₂O | 63.5 + 32.1 + 4 × 16.0 + 5 × 18.0 | 249.6 |

## Exam traps

- Writing "same number of protons but different mass number" without mentioning **neutrons**: the definition in statement (a) is in terms of neutrons.
- Leaving "weighted mean" or "1/12th of carbon-12" out of the Ar definition.
- Defining relative atomic mass as "the mass of an atom", which describes isotopic mass.
- Dividing by 100 when the data are peak heights that do not sum to 100.
- Adding electrons for a positive ion.
- Calling the mass of SiO₂ a relative molecular mass.

## Quick self-test

1. Give the numbers of protons, neutrons and electrons in ³⁴S²⁻ (Z = 16).
2. A species has 12 protons, 13 neutrons and 10 electrons. Write its symbol with mass number and charge.
3. Define relative isotopic mass.
4. Why does the definition of relative atomic mass include "weighted mean"?
5. Why do chlorine-35 and chlorine-37 react in the same way?
6. Gallium is 60.1% gallium-69 and 39.9% gallium-71. Calculate Ar using whole-number isotopic masses.
7. Rubidium peaks at m/z 85 and 87 have heights 2.6 and 1.0. Calculate Ar.
8. Element X (Ar 10.8) has isotopes of mass 10 and 11. Find the percentage of mass 10.
9. Why are questions limited to singly charged ions?
10. Which term is used for the mass of MgO: relative molecular mass or relative formula mass?
11. Calculate the relative formula mass of Al₂(SO₄)₃ (Al = 27.0, S = 32.1, O = 16.0).

### Answers

1. 16 protons, 18 neutrons, 18 electrons.
2. ²⁵Mg²⁺.
3. The mass of an atom of an isotope compared with 1/12th of the mass of an atom of carbon-12.
4. Isotopes have different masses and abundances, so each mass is weighted by how common the isotope is.
5. Same number of electrons and the same electron arrangement.
6. (69 × 60.1 + 71 × 39.9) ÷ 100 = **69.8**.
7. (85 × 2.6 + 87 × 1.0) ÷ 3.6 = **85.6**.
8. 10x + 11(100 − x) = 1080, so x = **20%**.
9. Then each m/z value equals the relative isotopic mass, so peaks can be read directly.
10. Relative formula mass, because MgO has a giant ionic structure.
11. 2 × 27.0 + 3 × (32.1 + 64.0) = 54.0 + 288.3 = **342.3**.
