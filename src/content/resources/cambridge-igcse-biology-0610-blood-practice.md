These questions cover sub-topic **9.4 Blood** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Parts marked **(Extended)** test Supplement content; Core candidates can skip them. Counts and data in the questions are invented for practice.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which component of blood transports urea?

A: platelets

B: plasma

C: red blood cells

D: white blood cells

**[1]**

**2.** List the **four** components of blood. **[2]**

**3.** Name the protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen. **[1]**

**4.** State **two** roles of blood clotting. **[2]**

**5.** *(Extended)* State the function of lymphocytes. **[1]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Name the insoluble protein that forms the mesh of a blood clot. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A diagram of a blood smear shows three kinds of structure. Structure J has no nucleus and is very common. Structure K has a nucleus and is larger than J. Structure L is much smaller than J and has no nucleus.

**(a)** Identify J, K and L. **[3]**

**(b)** State the function of J and explain how it carries out this function. **[3]**

**(c)** *(Extended)* Structure K has a lobed nucleus and a large amount of cytoplasm. Identify K and describe its function. **[2]**

**8.** Plasma transports several substances.

**(a)** Complete the sentences.

Carbon dioxide is carried in plasma from respiring cells to the ................

Urea is carried in plasma from the ................ to the kidneys.

Hormones are carried in plasma from glands to ................ organs. **[3]**

**(b)** A student writes, "Plasma carries oxygen from the lungs to the cells." Explain why this is incorrect. **[1]**

**9.** A student counts cells in five fields of view of a blood smear.

| Field of view | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Red blood cells | 412 | 398 | 405 | 420 | 390 |
| White blood cells | 1 | 2 | 0 | 3 | 1 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean number of red blood cells per field of view. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the mean number of white blood cells per field of view. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the student counted five fields rather than one. **[2]**

**10.** A person cuts a finger on a piece of glass.

**(a)** Explain why it is important that the blood clots quickly. **[2]**

**(b)** *(Extended)* Describe the process of clotting. **[3]**

**(c)** A person has a condition in which their blood contains very few platelets. Suggest **one** problem this could cause. **[1]**

**11.** *(Extended)* Blood is taken from a patient with an infection. The number of lymphocytes and phagocytes is higher than normal.

**(a)** Describe how you would distinguish a lymphocyte from a phagocyte in a photomicrograph. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain how the increase in each cell type helps the patient. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest why a patient with fewer red blood cells than normal may become breathless during exercise. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1].

**2.** Red blood cells, white blood cells, platelets and plasma [2]. All four correct scores 2 marks; two or three correct scores 1 mark.

*Examiner insight:* "Blood cells" as one item does not count as two components; name red and white cells separately.

**3.** Haemoglobin [1].

**4.** Prevents blood loss [1]; prevents the entry of pathogens [1].

*Examiner insight:* "Stops bleeding" and "stops blood loss" are the same idea and score once; the second mark needs pathogens.

**5.** Antibody production [1].

**6.** Fibrin [1].

*Examiner insight:* "Fibrinogen" scores zero here; it is the soluble protein that is converted.

**7. (a)** J is a red blood cell [1]; K is a white blood cell [1]; L is a platelet [1].

**(b)** J transports oxygen [1]. It contains haemoglobin [1], which combines with oxygen in the lungs and releases it in respiring tissues [1].

**(c)** K is a phagocyte [1]; it engulfs pathogens by phagocytosis [1].

**8. (a)** lungs [1]; liver [1]; target [1].

**(b)** Oxygen is carried by haemoglobin in red blood cells, not by plasma [1].

**9. (a)** (412 + 398 + 405 + 420 + 390) ÷ 5 = 2025 ÷ 5 [1] = **405** [1].

**(b)** (1 + 2 + 0 + 3 + 1) ÷ 5 = 7 ÷ 5 = **1.4** [1].

**(c)** The numbers vary between fields, especially for the few white cells [1]; a mean of several fields is more representative / reliable [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (b), a mean does not have to be a whole number; rounding 1.4 to 1 loses the mark.

**10. (a)** To prevent blood loss [1] and to prevent pathogens entering through the cut [1].

**(b)** Fibrinogen (soluble) [1] is converted to fibrin (insoluble) [1]; fibrin forms a mesh that traps blood cells / forms a clot [1].

**(c)** Blood would take longer to clot, so there could be more blood loss / more chance of infection [1].

**11. (a)** A lymphocyte has a large round nucleus that fills most of the cell [1]; a phagocyte has a lobed nucleus and more cytoplasm [1].

**(b)** More lymphocytes produce more antibodies [1]; more phagocytes engulf more pathogens by phagocytosis [1].

**(c)** Fewer red blood cells means less haemoglobin, so less oxygen is carried [1]; less oxygen reaches the respiring muscles [1]; the person breathes faster to try to take in more oxygen / less aerobic respiration releases less energy [1].

*Examiner insight:* Build the chain: component, function, effect on cells, symptom. Jumping straight to "they cannot breathe" misses the middle marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming plasma as the carrier of oxygen.
- Identifying blood cells by colour instead of by nucleus and size.
- Writing "white cells fight germs" with no process named.
- Confusing fibrinogen and fibrin.
- Rounding a mean to a whole number when the data do not justify it.
