All data and emission factors below are invented.

## Questions

**1.** Distinguish between natural capital and natural income. **[2]**

**2.** Classify each ecosystem service as provisioning, regulating, cultural or supporting:
(i) mangroves reducing storm surge, (ii) honey from wild bees, (iii) nutrient cycling in soil,
(iv) birdwatching tourism. **[2]**

**3.** An aquifer stores 1,500 million m³. Recharge is 40 million m³ per year; abstraction is
55 million m³ per year.

**(a)** Calculate the net annual change in storage. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate how long the aquifer would last at this rate. **[1]**
**(c)** State the maximum abstraction that would be sustainable, with a reason. **[2]**

**4.** Country T has 25 million people and a biocapacity of 2.4 gha per person. Its footprint
components (gha per person) are: carbon 3.4, cropland 1.1, grazing land 0.5, forest products 0.6,
fishing grounds 0.2, built-up land 0.2.

**(a)** Calculate the ecological footprint per person. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the ecological deficit per person and in total. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage of the footprint due to carbon. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest **one** policy that would most reduce Country T's footprint, with a reason. **[1]**

**5.** A school records: heating 120,000 kWh (0.18 kg CO₂e per kWh); electricity 80,000 kWh
(0.20 kg CO₂e per kWh); school buses 30,000 km (1.1 kg CO₂e per km).

**(a)** Calculate the school's carbon footprint in tonnes of CO₂e. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage due to school buses. **[1]**
**(c)** Identify **one** source of emissions not included in this footprint. **[1]**

**6.** Distinguish between blue water and grey water in a water footprint. **[2]**

**7.** Explain why a country with a falling ecological footprint may not be reducing its global
environmental impact. **[3]**

**8.** Outline the two boundaries of the doughnut economics model. **[2]**

**9.** Explain how crossing a planetary boundary could increase the risk of a tipping point. **[3]**

**10.** Using an example, explain what is meant by environmental justice. **[3]**

**11.** Outline how a circular economy differs from a linear economy for mobile phones. **[3]**

**12.** Evaluate the use of environmental impact assessments in deciding whether a large
development should go ahead. **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** Natural capital is the stock of natural resources, such as a forest or fish stock [1]; natural
income is the yield from that stock that can be taken without reducing it [1]. **[2]**

**2.** (i) Regulating; (ii) provisioning; (iii) supporting; (iv) cultural. All four correct [2]; two
or three correct [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** 40 − 55 = **−15 million m³ per year** [1]
**(b)** 1500 / 15 = **100 years** [1]
**(c)** 40 million m³ per year [1], because abstraction equal to recharge (natural income) keeps the
storage constant [1]. **[4]**

**4. (a)** 3.4 + 1.1 + 0.5 + 0.6 + 0.2 + 0.2 = **6.0 gha per person** [1]
**(b)** 6.0 − 2.4 = **3.6 gha per person** [1]; 3.6 × 25 million = **90 million gha** [1]
**(c)** 3.4 / 6.0 × 100 = **56.7%** (3 s.f.) [1]
**(d)** A policy that cuts fossil-fuel use, such as expanding renewable electricity or public
transport, because carbon is the largest component [1]. **[5]**

**5. (a)** 120000 × 0.18 = 21600 kg; 80000 × 0.20 = 16000 kg; 30000 × 1.1 = 33000 kg [1]; total =
70600 kg = **70.6 t CO₂e** [1]
**(b)** 33000 / 70600 × 100 = **46.7%** (3 s.f.) [1]
**(c)** Any one: food in the canteen; purchased goods such as paper and computers; staff commuting
by car; waste disposal [1]. **[4]**

**6.** Blue water is surface or groundwater withdrawn and used, for example for irrigation or
processing [1]; grey water is the volume of fresh water needed to dilute pollutants to acceptable
standards [1]. **[2]**

**7.** The country may import more goods instead of producing them [1], so land use and emissions
move to the exporting countries [1]; the domestic footprint falls but total consumption, and global
impact, may be unchanged or higher [1]. **[3]**

**8.** An inner social foundation of basic needs such as food, water, health and education [1]; an
outer ecological ceiling based on planetary boundaries [1]. **[2]**

**9.** A boundary marks the edge of the safe operating space for an Earth-system process [1]. Beyond
it, pressures such as nitrogen run-off or greenhouse gas concentrations push systems closer to
thresholds [1], where positive feedback can drive a large, hard-to-reverse shift, such as lake
eutrophication or ice-sheet loss [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Environmental justice is the fair distribution of environmental benefits and harms [1], and
fair participation in decisions about them [1]. Example: a waste incinerator sited next to a
low-income neighbourhood whose residents were not consulted, while wealthier areas that produce more
waste are unaffected [1]. **[3]**

**11.** A linear economy makes, uses and discards phones [1]. A circular economy designs phones to be
repaired and upgraded [1], and recovers parts and metals through take-back, refurbishment and
recycling so materials stay in use [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Award up to 7: strengths, up to 3; limitations, up to 3; a judgement [1].
- *Strengths:* a baseline study records conditions before change, so impacts can be measured later
  [1]; predicts impacts so they can be mitigated or the project redesigned [1]; consultation gives
  local communities a voice [1]; conditions and monitoring can be enforced after approval [1].
- *Limitations:* baselines may be too short to capture seasonal or yearly variation [1]; predictions
  are uncertain and cumulative or indirect impacts are hard to assess [1]; reports are often paid
  for by the developer, raising questions of bias [1]; decision-makers can still approve despite
  serious impacts if economic benefits are valued more [1].
- *Judgement:* for example, EIAs improve decisions by making impacts visible, but their value
  depends on independent review, meaningful consultation and enforcement of monitoring [1]. **[7]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Comparing a total footprint with per-person biocapacity.
- Dropping the minus sign or units from a net change.
- Writing "recycling" as the whole definition of a circular economy.
- Listing EIA stages when the question asks for evaluation.
