Topic 4 of Cambridge IGCSE Environmental Management (0680) is about how the atmosphere works and how
human activities change it. You need to describe its layers and composition, explain the natural and
enhanced greenhouse effect, and describe the causes, impacts and management of climate change, acid
rain and ozone depletion. Most longer questions end with "discuss the benefits and limitations" of a
strategy, so learn each strategy with one benefit and one limitation.

For the full explanation use the
[atmosphere study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-environmental-management-the-atmosphere-and-human-activities-study-guide/),
then test yourself with the
[atmosphere practice questions](/resources/igcse-environmental-management-atmosphere-practice/).

## What is the structure of the atmosphere? (4.1)

From the ground up: **troposphere → stratosphere → mesosphere → thermosphere**. The **ozone layer** is
in the **lower stratosphere**.

| Gas in clean air | Approximate share |
|---|---|
| Nitrogen | 78% |
| Oxygen | 21% |
| Argon, carbon dioxide, water vapour | The remaining ~1% |

**Climate** = the weather conditions in a location based on the weather over many years (weather is
day to day; climate is the long-term pattern).

**Natural greenhouse effect** in five steps:
1. Solar radiation passes through the atmosphere.
2. Some is absorbed by land and oceans, heating the Earth.
3. Some is reflected back into space.
4. Some is absorbed and re-emitted back to the surface by greenhouse gases and clouds.
5. This keeps the Earth warm enough to support life.

## What causes climate change? (4.2)

**Greenhouse gas** = a gas that absorbs radiation and emits the energy as thermal (heat) energy. Named
examples: **carbon dioxide (CO2), water vapour (H2O), methane (CH4)**.

| Human activity | Gas | How |
|---|---|---|
| Burning fossil fuels | CO2 | Combustion releases carbon stored for millions of years |
| Agriculture | CH4 | Cattle digestion, manure decomposition, flooded rice fields |
| Deforestation | CO2 | Fewer trees removing CO2 by photosynthesis |
| Wetland drainage | CH4 | Change in land use releases methane |
| Cement manufacture | CO2 | Limestone (calcium carbonate) heated to lime (calcium oxide) |
| Population growth and energy use | CO2 | More people using more energy |

**Enhanced greenhouse effect**: higher concentrations of greenhouse gases absorb more radiation →
**global warming** → **climate change**.

**Impacts to learn (12)**: warmer land and ocean surface; melting ice sheets, glaciers and permafrost →
sea-level rise; ocean acidification from extra CO2; disrupted food chains; change in biodiversity; loss
of habitat; forced migration of people and animals; more frequent and severe extreme weather (floods,
loss of land, drought, wildfires); changed crop yields; more pests and invasive species; food shortages.

## How can carbon footprints be reduced? (4.2.6)

| Strategy | One benefit | One limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Reduce fossil fuel use | Cuts the largest source of CO2 | Renewables need investment and storage |
| Sustainable food choices (plant-based, seasonal, local) | Lower emissions from transport and livestock | Depends on individual choice and cost |
| Less livestock farming | Less methane | Loss of income for farmers |
| Fewer children per woman | Lower future demand | Slow to take effect; cultural and ethical issues |
| Energy efficiency | Saves money as well as emissions | Upfront cost of insulation or appliances |
| Reforestation and afforestation | Trees store carbon | Takes decades; needs land |
| Carbon sequestration (capture, remove, store) | Tackles emissions from industry | Expensive; storage sites must not leak |
| Transport policies | Fewer cars, less congestion | Needs good public transport |
| International agreements | Shared targets, such as the Paris Agreement (2015) | Hard to enforce |
| Taxation | Makes polluting more expensive | Can hit poorer households hardest |
| Research and climate models | Better predictions guide policy | Models carry uncertainty |
| Artificial intelligence | Predicts impacts and tests strategies quickly | Relies on good data; uses energy itself |

**Climate change adaptation** = altering behaviour, practices and infrastructure to adapt to the
impacts of climate change. Examples: crops that resist warmer, drier or wetter conditions; improved
flood defences; laws on building near coasts and on floodplains; building design and materials.

## How does acid rain form and how is it managed? (4.3)

**Formation**
- Sulfur dioxide from burning fossil fuels that contain sulfur compounds, and from volcanic gases.
- Oxides of nitrogen form when atmospheric nitrogen reacts with oxygen at the high temperatures in
  vehicle engines.
- These gases react with oxygen and water vapour in the atmosphere to form acid rain.

**Impacts**: acidified lakes and rivers; fewer fish; damaged aquatic food webs; acidified soils;
defoliation and lower crop yields; damage to buildings.

| Strategy | Benefit | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Flue-gas desulfurisation (reacts SO2 with calcium carbonate or calcium oxide) | Removes most SO2 from power-station exhaust | Costly; needs quarried limestone |
| Catalytic converters | Remove oxides of nitrogen from vehicle exhaust | Do not reduce CO2; need maintenance |
| Transport policies | Fewer vehicles, less NOx | Need public support and alternatives |

## What causes ozone depletion? (4.4)

- Cause: **chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)** in aerosols and refrigerants.
- Impacts: more solar (ultraviolet) radiation reaching the surface; higher rates of skin cancer and
  cataracts; damage to vegetation and lower crop yields.
- Strategies: international agreement (the Montreal Protocol, 1987); CFC ban and alternatives; safe
  disposal of CFCs in old fridges and freezers.
- Benefit: the agreement was adopted worldwide and CFC use fell sharply. Limitation: CFCs last a long
  time in the atmosphere, so recovery takes decades, and illegal use is hard to police.

## Worked example: reading a CO2 graph

A graph shows atmospheric CO2 rising from 340 ppm in 1980 to 420 ppm in 2023.

- Increase = 420 - 340 = **80 ppm**.
- Percentage increase = 80 / 340 x 100 = **23.5%**.
- Mean rate = 80 / 43 years = **about 1.9 ppm per year**.

Exam tip: quote figures from the graph with units, then give a reason (fossil fuel combustion).

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- Putting the ozone layer in the troposphere - it is in the lower stratosphere.
- Mixing up ozone depletion and global warming: CFCs thin the ozone layer; greenhouse gases warm the
  planet.
- Writing "pollution causes acid rain" without naming sulfur dioxide and oxides of nitrogen.
- Saying catalytic converters remove sulfur dioxide - they remove oxides of nitrogen.
- Listing a strategy in a "discuss" question without a benefit and a limitation.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which greenhouse gases does 0680 require?**
Carbon dioxide, water vapour and methane. Learn one human
source of each that the syllabus names.

**What is the difference between mitigation and adaptation?**
Reducing carbon footprints tackles the cause
of climate change. Adaptation changes behaviour, practices and infrastructure to cope with its impacts.

**Do I need to know El Nino?**
No. The 0680 syllabus states that knowledge of ENSO, El Nino and La Nina is
not required for ocean surface warming.
