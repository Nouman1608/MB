These questions cover key question 2.3 of Cambridge IGCSE History 0470: "How far was Hitler's foreign policy to blame
for the outbreak of war in Europe in 1939?"

## Section A

**1.** State **three** of Hitler's foreign policy aims. **[3]**

**2.** Describe the remilitarisation of the Rhineland in 1936. **[4]**

**3.** Identify **two** ways in which Germany and Italy were involved in the Spanish Civil War. **[2]**

**4.** Describe the events of the Anschluss in 1938. **[4]**

**5.** State **three** terms of the Nazi-Soviet Pact, including its secret protocol. **[3]**

**6.** Describe the crisis over the Sudetenland in September 1938. **[4]**

## Section B

**7.** Why did Britain and France fail to stop German rearmament between 1933 and 1936? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**8.** Why did Britain and France declare war on Germany in September 1939? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**9.** "Appeasement was a sensible policy in 1938." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

**10.** "The Nazi-Soviet Pact was the most important cause of the outbreak of war in 1939." How far do you agree?
Explain your answer. **[10]**

**11.** Study the source below, which is invented for practice.

*Source A (invented)*: from a letter written by a British shopkeeper to a newspaper, October 1938. "My father fell
at the Somme. My son is nineteen. Mr Chamberlain has given me the one thing I asked for: that my boy will not follow
his grandfather. If the price is a quarrel in a far-off country, I will pay it gladly."

Why was this letter written in October 1938? Use the source and your knowledge to explain your answer. **[6]**

**12.** Study the source below, which is also invented for practice.

*Source B (invented)*: a description of an imaginary cartoon of August 1939, written for this page. Hitler and
Stalin, smiling, shake hands over a map of Poland. Behind their backs, each holds a knife.

What is the message of the cartoon described in Source B? Use the source and your knowledge to explain your
answer. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any three, 1 mark each: overturn the Treaty of Versailles (1); unite all German speakers in one Reich (1); gain
Lebensraum in Eastern Europe (1); destroy communism (1); rebuild Germany's armed forces (1); make Germany a great power
again (1). **[3]**

**2.** Any four, 1 mark each: the Rhineland had been demilitarised by Versailles (1); German troops entered it in March
1936 (1); Hitler claimed the Franco-Soviet treaty of 1935 threatened Germany (1); it was a gamble, as German forces
were not ready for war (1); attention was focused on Abyssinia (1); France was divided and unwilling to act without
Britain (1); Britain thought Germany was only entering its own territory (1); Britain and France protested but took
no action (1). **[4]**

**3.** Any two, 1 mark each: they supported Franco's Nationalists (1); Germany sent the Condor Legion, with aircraft
(1); the bombing of Guernica in 1937 (1); Italy sent large numbers of troops (1); both tested weapons and tactics (1);
both ignored the non-intervention agreement (1). **[2]**

**4.** Any four, 1 mark each: Anschluss had been banned by Versailles (1); Austrian Nazis, encouraged by Hitler,
caused unrest (1); Chancellor Schuschnigg met Hitler and was pressured to give Nazis posts in government (1);
Schuschnigg called a vote on Austrian independence (1); Hitler demanded it be cancelled and Schuschnigg resigned (1);
German troops entered Austria in March 1938 (1); a controlled plebiscite approved the union (1); Britain and France
did nothing (1). **[4]**

**5.** Any three, 1 mark each: signed on 23 August 1939 (1); Germany and the USSR would not attack each other (1); they
would stay neutral if the other went to war (1); the secret protocol divided Poland between them (1); it placed the
Baltic states and other areas in a Soviet sphere (1). **[3]**

**6.** Any four, 1 mark each: about three million German speakers lived in the Sudetenland (1); Henlein's Sudeten German
Party, backed by Hitler, demanded union with Germany (1); Hitler threatened war (1); Chamberlain met Hitler at
Berchtesgaden (1); and again at Bad Godesberg, where Hitler raised his demands (1); war seemed close (1); at Munich on
29-30 September Britain, France, Germany and Italy agreed that Germany would take the Sudetenland (1); Czechoslovakia
was not invited (1). **[4]**

**7.** Levels. Level 4 (6): explains two reasons. Level 3 (4-5): explains one reason; 5 for a developed explanation
with specific support. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes reasons. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative
content: **sympathy for Germany**, because many in Britain thought Versailles too harsh and German rearmament fair;
**divisions**, because Britain signed the Anglo-German Naval Agreement in 1935, breaking up the Stresa Front;
**distraction**, because the Abyssinian crisis absorbed the League, Britain and France; **unwillingness to fight**,
because of memories of the First World War and the cost of war during the Depression; **fear of communism**, because
some saw a strong Germany as a barrier to the USSR. **[6]**

**8.** Levels as for Question 7. Indicative content: **the guarantee to Poland**, given on 31 March 1939 and followed
by the Anglo-Polish alliance of 25 August, committed them; **Hitler's broken promises**, because the occupation of the
Czech lands in March 1939 showed he could not be trusted and his aims were not limited to German speakers; **public
opinion** had turned against appeasement; **the balance of power**, because Germany's expansion threatened Britain and
France themselves; **the ultimatum**, because Germany ignored the demand to withdraw from Poland. **[6]**

**9.** Levels. Level 5 (10): explains both sides and reaches a supported judgement on "how far". Level 4 (7-9):
explains both sides (7 for one explanation on each side, 1 more for each additional explanation). Level 3 (4-6):
explains one side. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative content. **Agree:**
British rearmament was incomplete and time was needed for fighters and radar; public opinion and the Dominions opposed
war; many thought the Sudeten Germans' demand for self-determination fair; the USA would not help; fear of the USSR.
**Disagree:** it encouraged Hitler to take more; Czechoslovakia's army and border defences were given up; the USSR was
excluded, pushing Stalin towards Hitler; Hitler's aims in *Mein Kampf* showed that concessions would not satisfy him;
Germany also used the time to rearm. **Judgement:** for example, sensible given Britain's position in 1938, but it rested
on a false belief about Hitler's aims. **[10]**

**10.** Levels as for Question 9. Indicative content. **Agree:** it removed the danger of a two-front war, so Hitler felt
free to invade Poland a week later; it made the British and French guarantee to Poland almost impossible to carry out;
the secret protocol planned Poland's division. **Disagree:** Hitler's aim of Lebensraum meant war in the east was likely
anyway; appeasement had already encouraged him; the League's failures had destroyed collective security; Versailles
had created the Polish Corridor and Danzig issues; the pact itself was partly a result of Munich. **Judgement:** for
example, the pact decided the timing of war, but Hitler's aims explain why war came at all. **[10]**

**11.** Levels. Level 3 (5-6): explains the purpose in context, using the source and knowledge. Level 2 (3-4): explains
the purpose using the source only, or the context without the purpose. Level 1 (1-2): describes the source. Indicative
content: **purpose**: to support Chamberlain and the Munich Agreement publicly, and to persuade other readers that
avoiding war was worth the cost. **Context**: the Munich Agreement (29-30 September 1938) gave the Sudetenland to
Germany; Chamberlain returned claiming "peace for our time" and was widely welcomed; memories of the First World War,
such as the Somme, made many families fear another war; critics in Britain attacked the agreement, so supporters wrote
to defend it. The phrase "a far-off country" echoes the view that Czechoslovakia was not Britain's concern. **[6]**

**12.** Levels. Level 3 (5-6): explains the message in context, using the source and knowledge. Level 2 (3-4): explains
the message using the source only. Level 1 (1-2): describes the source. Indicative content: the message is that the
**Nazi-Soviet Pact was a cynical deal between enemies**: they shake hands over Poland because the pact planned to
divide it; the **hidden knives** show that neither trusted the other and each would betray the other in time.
Knowledge: the pact was signed on 23 August 1939; its secret protocol divided Poland; Hitler and Stalin were
ideological enemies; Germany invaded Poland on 1 September and the USSR from the east later that month. **[6]**
