This guide covers subtopic **14.2, Alkenes**, from Topic 14, Hydrocarbons, of Cambridge International AS
& A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027 series. It is AS Level content. The
[Hydrocarbons: Alkanes and Alkenes study guide](/resources/as-hydrocarbons-alkanes-and-alkenes/)
summarises the reactions and works the propene/HBr example; this page adds the mechanism with bromine
in full, alkenes that give more than one product on formation, structure deduction from hot KMnO₄
products, and a tertiary carbocation case.

## What the syllabus asks for

1. recall how alkenes are made: (a) **elimination** of HX from a halogenoalkane, ethanolic NaOH and heat;
   (b) **dehydration** of an alcohol, heated Al₂O₃ or a concentrated acid such as concentrated H₂SO₄;
   (c) **cracking**
2. describe (a) **electrophilic addition** of (i) H₂, Pt/Ni catalyst and heat; (ii) steam, H₂O(g) with
   H₃PO₄ catalyst; (iii) HX(g) at room temperature; (iv) X₂; (b) oxidation by **cold dilute acidified
   KMnO₄** to a diol; (c) oxidation by **hot concentrated acidified KMnO₄**, breaking the C=C, and using
   the products to locate the double bond; (d) **addition polymerisation** of ethene and propene
3. describe the use of **aqueous bromine** to show a C=C
4. describe the mechanism of electrophilic addition, using **bromine/ethene** and **HBr/propene**
5. describe and explain the **inductive effects** of alkyl groups on the stability of primary, secondary
   and tertiary cations, and use this to explain **Markovnikov addition**

## Making alkenes, and when you get more than one

**Elimination.** Heating a halogenoalkane with NaOH dissolved in ethanol removes H and X from adjacent
carbons:

    CH₃CH₂Br + NaOH → CH₂=CH₂ + NaBr + H₂O     (ethanolic, heat)

**Dehydration.** Passing alcohol vapour over heated Al₂O₃, or heating with concentrated H₂SO₄, removes
H₂O:

    CH₃CH₂CH₂OH → CH₃CH=CH₂ + H₂O

If the carbon carrying X or OH has **different** neighbouring carbons with hydrogens, more than one
alkene forms. 2-bromobutane or butan-2-ol can lose H from C1 or from C3:

- loss from C1 gives **but-1-ene**, CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃
- loss from C3 gives **but-2-ene**, which forms as both **cis** and **trans** isomers

So three alkenes are possible. Use the isomerism skills from the
[Isomerism study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-chemistry-9701-as-isomerism/) whenever a question
asks for "all the possible organic products".

## The addition reactions

| Reagent | Conditions | Product from propene |
|---|---|---|
| H₂(g) | Pt or Ni catalyst, heat | propane |
| H₂O(g), steam | H₃PO₄ catalyst | propan-2-ol (major) |
| HBr(g) | room temperature | 2-bromopropane (major) |
| Cl₂ or Br₂ | room temperature | 1,2-dichloropropane or 1,2-dibromopropane |

With a symmetrical reagent (H₂, X₂) there is only one product. With an unsymmetrical reagent (H₂O, HX)
added to an unsymmetrical alkene, two products are possible, and carbocation stability decides the major
one.

## Mechanism 1: bromine and ethene

1. As a Br₂ molecule approaches the electron-rich π bond, the π electrons repel the electrons in Br–Br,
   so the bromine molecule becomes **polarised** (an induced dipole): Brᵟ⁺–Brᵟ⁻.
2. A curly arrow goes **from the C=C π bond to the δ+ bromine atom**. A second curly arrow goes **from the
   Br–Br bond to the δ− bromine**, which leaves as Br⁻ (heterolytic fission).
3. This gives a **carbocation**, CH₂Br–CH₂⁺, and a bromide ion.
4. A curly arrow goes **from a lone pair on Br⁻ to the positive carbon**, giving
   **1,2-dibromoethane**, CH₂BrCH₂Br.

## Mechanism 2: hydrogen bromide and propene

HBr is already polar, Hᵟ⁺–Brᵟ⁻. The π bond attacks the δ+ hydrogen; the H–Br bonding pair moves onto Br,
which leaves as Br⁻. The hydrogen can join C1 or C2, so two carbocations are possible: the secondary
CH₃CH⁺CH₃ or the primary CH₃CH₂CH₂⁺. Br⁻ then bonds to the positive carbon.

## Inductive effects and Markovnikov addition

Alkyl groups are **electron-donating** by the inductive effect: they push electron density along the σ
bonds towards a positively charged carbon, spreading out the charge and stabilising the cation. The more
alkyl groups attached to the positive carbon, the more stable the cation:

**tertiary > secondary > primary**

The more stable cation forms faster (lower activation energy), so it leads to the **major product**. This
is **Markovnikov addition**: the hydrogen adds to the C=C carbon that already has more hydrogens.

**Worked example 1.** Predict the major product of 2-methylpropene, (CH₃)₂C=CH₂, with HBr, and explain.

If H⁺ adds to the CH₂ carbon, the positive charge sits on the central carbon, which carries three methyl
groups: a **tertiary** cation, (CH₃)₃C⁺. If H⁺ adds to the central carbon, the charge sits on the end
carbon, with only one alkyl group: a **primary** cation, (CH₃)₂CHCH₂⁺. Three electron-donating methyl
groups stabilise the tertiary cation much more, so it forms preferentially, and Br⁻ attacks it. Major
product: **2-bromo-2-methylpropane**, (CH₃)₃CBr. Minor product: 1-bromo-2-methylpropane.

## Oxidation with KMnO₄

**Cold, dilute, acidified KMnO₄** adds an OH group to each carbon of the C=C, forming a **diol**; the
purple solution is decolourised. Propene gives propane-1,2-diol, CH₃CH(OH)CH₂OH.

**Hot, concentrated, acidified KMnO₄** breaks the C=C completely. Each half is oxidised according to what
was attached to it:

| Group at one end of the C=C | Product from that end |
|---|---|
| =CH₂ | CO₂ (and H₂O) |
| =CHR | carboxylic acid, RCOOH |
| =CRR′ | ketone, RCOR′ |

**Worked example 2.** Alkene Z, C₆H₁₂, is heated with hot concentrated acidified KMnO₄. The only products
are propanone and propanoic acid. Deduce the structure of Z.

Propanone, CH₃COCH₃, comes from an end with two alkyl groups: (CH₃)₂C=. Propanoic acid, CH₃CH₂COOH,
comes from an end with one H and one ethyl group: =CHCH₂CH₃. Joining the two ends across the double bond
gives **(CH₃)₂C=CHCH₂CH₃, 2-methylpent-2-ene**. Check: 3 + 3 = 6 carbons, and the formula is C₆H₁₂.

## Addition polymerisation

The π bond of each monomer opens and the monomers join into a long chain, with no other product.

- ethene, CH₂=CH₂, gives poly(ethene), repeat unit –CH₂–CH₂–
- propene, CH₂=CHCH₃, gives poly(propene), repeat unit –CH₂–CH(CH₃)–

The methyl group of propene becomes a side branch on every second carbon of the chain; it is not part of
the main chain.

## The bromine water test

Shake the compound with **aqueous bromine** at room temperature. An alkene **decolourises** the orange
solution rapidly. An alkane leaves it orange (it reacts with bromine only in UV light).

## Common mistakes

- **Using aqueous NaOH for elimination.** Aqueous NaOH gives an alcohol by substitution; elimination
  needs **ethanolic** NaOH.
- **Starting the first curly arrow from a bromine atom.** It starts at the C=C (the π bond).
- **Drawing the carbocation positive charge on the wrong carbon**, or forgetting to show Br⁻ with a lone
  pair.
- **Saying the secondary cation is "more stable because it is secondary".** Explain: more electron-donating
  alkyl groups spread the positive charge.
- **Mixing up cold dilute and hot concentrated KMnO₄** products.
- **Putting CH₃ into the main chain** of the poly(propene) repeat unit.

## Exam technique for the command words

- **Describe the mechanism:** show the dipole, both curly arrows in step 1, the carbocation and the
  final curly arrow from the anion's lone pair.
- **Explain** Markovnikov addition: inductive effect, cation stability order, the more stable cation
  gives the major product.
- **Deduce** the alkene from oxidation products: identify each end separately, then join them.
- **Describe** a test: reagent, conditions and the observation for both possible results.
