Quick recall for section 2.2, Recruitment and selection process, of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Business (4BS1) specification, Issue 2, February 2026. Worked examples are in the [recruitment and selection study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-recruitment-and-selection-process/); exam-style questions are in the [recruitment and selection practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-recruitment-and-selection-process-practice/). Descriptions are standard ones, as the specification names the items without describing them. Figures are invented and in dollars.

## What the specification lists

- 2.2.1 Types of employment: full-time, part-time, job share, casual/seasonal/temporary.
- 2.2.2 Recruitment documents: job description, person specification, application form, CV/résumé.
- 2.2.3 Internal and external recruitment: job advertisement, shortlisting, interviewing.
- 2.2.4 Legal controls over employment and their effects: equal opportunities (gender, race, disability, religion, sexual preference, age); minimum wage laws.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Recruitment | Attracting suitable applicants for a vacancy |
| Selection | Choosing the best applicant |
| Job description | Document describing the job: title, duties, hours, who it reports to |
| Person specification | Document describing the person wanted: qualifications, skills, experience, qualities (essential and desirable) |
| Application form | Form designed by the employer, with the same questions for every applicant |
| CV/résumé | The applicant's own summary of education, experience and skills |
| Internal recruitment | Filling a post from existing staff |
| External recruitment | Filling a post from outside the business |
| Shortlisting | Reducing applicants to those who best meet the person specification |
| Job share | Two or more people sharing one full-time job |

## The process

Vacancy → type of employment → job description and person specification → internal or external → advertisement → applications → shortlist → interview and tests → appoint.

## Types of employment

| Type | Use when | Drawback |
|---|---|---|
| Full-time | Steady work all year | Paid in quiet periods too |
| Part-time | Peaks at certain times | More people to manage |
| Job share | Full-time cover needed, staff want fewer hours | Handover |
| Casual/seasonal/temporary | Short peaks or one-off tasks | Less commitment, repeated training |

Reminder: 3 seasonal workers × 10 weeks × 25 hours × 10.00 = 7,500.

## Internal or external

| | Internal | External |
|---|---|---|
| Plus | Cheaper, quicker, known person, motivates staff | Wider choice, new ideas, no knock-on vacancy |
| Minus | Small pool, no new ideas, another vacancy | Costly, slower, riskier, longer induction |

Reminder: advertising 6,000 plus agency fees 14,000 for 8 appointments: 20,000 ÷ 8 = 2,500 per appointment.

## Application form or CV?

- Application form: same questions for everyone, easy to compare, designed by the employer; suits large numbers of applicants.
- CV: the applicant's own layout and emphasis, shows initiative; harder to compare; common in small businesses.

## Shortlisting and interviewing

- Reject anyone missing an essential criterion.
- Score the rest against the person specification.
- Ask every candidate the same core questions.
- Add practical tests where the job needs a skill that can be shown.
- Keep records of decisions.

## Legal controls

- Equal opportunities: no less favourable treatment because of gender, race, disability, religion, sexual preference or age, at any stage.
- Effects: fair wording of adverts and person specifications; job-related interview questions; reasonable adjustments for disabled applicants; training and records; risk of fines, compensation and bad publicity if broken; benefit of a wider talent pool.
- Minimum wage laws: no worker, including part-time and casual staff, may be paid below the legal rate. Effects: higher labour costs for low-paid roles; prices may rise or profit fall; staff motivation and retention may improve.

Reminder: rate rises by 0.50; 10 workers × 25 hours × 52 weeks × 0.50 = 6,500 a year.

## Small and large businesses

- Paper 1 (small): owner recruits personally; often accepts CVs; uses local adverts and word of mouth; one poor appointment has a big effect.
- Paper 2 (large): human resources department; application forms and online systems; many vacancies; may recruit in several countries.

## Exam traps

- Job description and person specification swapped.
- Recruitment confused with selection.
- One-sided points on internal or external recruitment.
- Equal opportunities described with no effect on the business.
- Suggesting lower pay than the minimum for part-time or casual staff.
- A person specification that includes age or gender requirements.

## Self-test

1. Define person specification.
2. What does a job description contain?
3. Give one benefit of an application form over a CV for the employer.
4. Give one benefit of internal recruitment.
5. Give one limitation of external recruitment.
6. A shop needs cover from 9am to 9pm. Which type of employment suits the evening hours, and why?
7. Define job share.
8. What is shortlisting?
9. Name the six grounds covered by equal opportunities in the specification.
10. Why might an advert saying "aged 20 to 30" be a problem?
11. Recruitment costs 12,000 for 5 appointments. Calculate the cost per appointment.
12. Four part-time staff each work 15 hours a week. The minimum wage rises by 0.40 an hour. Calculate the extra cost per week.

### Answers

1. A document listing the qualifications, skills, experience and qualities wanted in the person appointed.
2. Job title, duties and responsibilities, hours, place of work and who the post reports to.
3. Every applicant answers the same questions, so they are easier to compare.
4. Cheaper and quicker (or the person knows the business; or it motivates staff).
5. More costly and slower (or more risk; or longer induction).
6. Part-time, because the business pays only for the extra hours it needs.
7. Two or more people sharing one full-time job.
8. Reducing applicants to the few who best meet the person specification.
9. Gender, race, disability, religion, sexual preference, age.
10. It excludes applicants because of age, which equal opportunities law does not allow.
11. 12,000 ÷ 5 = 2,400.
12. 4 × 15 × 0.40 = 24.
