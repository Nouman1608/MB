## Section A

**1.** Explain, in terms of molecules, how a gas exerts a pressure on the walls of its container. **[2]**

**2.** Which pair of conditions makes a real gas behave most like an ideal gas?

A: high temperature and high pressure

B: high temperature and low pressure

C: low temperature and high pressure

D: low temperature and low pressure **[1]**

**3.** Suggest why helium behaves more like an ideal gas than ammonia under the same conditions. **[2]**

**4.** 3.20 g of oxygen gas, O₂, is held in a 4.00 dm³ container at 300 K. Calculate the pressure in Pa. **[3]**

**5.** Calculate the volume, in cm³, occupied by 0.0250 mol of an ideal gas at 400 K and 1.50 × 10⁵ Pa. **[2]**

**6.** 0.0500 mol of gas occupies 1.50 dm³ at 1.00 × 10⁵ Pa. Calculate the temperature in K. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A liquid hydrocarbon, empirical formula C₃H₇, is vaporised in a gas syringe. 0.285 g of the liquid gives 98.0 cm³ of vapour at 90 °C and 102 kPa.

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in mol, of vapour. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the relative molecular mass of the hydrocarbon. **[1]**

**(c)** Deduce its molecular formula. **[1]**

**(d)** Some vapour condensed on the cooler end of the syringe before the volume was read. State and explain the effect on the calculated Mr. **[2]**

**8.** A gas has a density of 2.86 g dm⁻³ at 273 K and 101 kPa.

**(a)** Calculate the relative molecular mass of the gas. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest, with a reason, which of N₂, CO₂ and SO₂ the gas is. **[1]**

**9.** 0.120 g of magnesium reacts with excess dilute hydrochloric acid. The hydrogen is collected at 298 K and 100 kPa.

**(a)** Write the equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the amount, in mol, of hydrogen formed. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the volume of hydrogen in cm³. **[2]**

**10.** Real gases do not obey pV = nRT exactly.

**(a)** Explain why real gases deviate from ideal behaviour at high pressure. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why real gases deviate from ideal behaviour at low temperature. **[2]**

**11.** A sealed rigid container of gas is at 1.00 × 10⁵ Pa and 300 K. It is heated to 450 K.

**(a)** Explain, in terms of molecular collisions, why the pressure increases. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the new pressure. **[1]**

**12.** An evacuated 250 cm³ flask has a mass of 105.462 g. When filled with a gas at 20 °C and 1.01 × 10⁵ Pa, its mass is 105.918 g.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of gas in the flask. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the relative molecular mass of the gas. **[3]**

**(c)** The gas turns limewater milky. Identify it. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Gas molecules move randomly and collide with the walls of the container [1]; each collision exerts a force on the wall, and pressure is the total force per unit area [1].

**2.** B [1].

**3.** Helium atoms have very weak intermolecular forces (id-id only), whereas ammonia molecules attract each other by hydrogen bonds [1]; helium atoms are also smaller, so their own volume matters less [1].

**4.** n = 3.20 / 32.0 = 0.100 mol [1]; V = 4.00 × 10⁻³ m³ [1]; p = nRT/V = (0.100 × 8.31 × 300) / (4.00 × 10⁻³) = 6.23 × 10⁴ Pa [1].

**5.** V = nRT/p = (0.0250 × 8.31 × 400) / (1.50 × 10⁵) = 5.54 × 10⁻⁴ m³ [1] = 554 cm³ [1].

**6.** T = pV/nR [1] = (1.00 × 10⁵ × 1.50 × 10⁻³) / (0.0500 × 8.31) = 361 K [1].

**7. (a)** T = 363 K, V = 9.80 × 10⁻⁵ m³, p = 1.02 × 10⁵ Pa [1]; n = pV/RT = (1.02 × 10⁵ × 9.80 × 10⁻⁵) / (8.31 × 363) = 3.31 × 10⁻³ mol [1].

**(b)** Mr = 0.285 / 3.314 × 10⁻³ = 86.0 [1].

**(c)** M(C₃H₇) = 43.0, and 86.0 / 43.0 = 2, so C₆H₁₄ [1].

**(d)** The measured volume is too small, so the calculated n is too small [1], and Mr (mass ÷ n) is too high [1].

**8. (a)** ρ = 2860 g m⁻³ [1]; Mr = ρRT/p = (2860 × 8.31 × 273) / (1.01 × 10⁵) = 64.2 [1].

**(b)** SO₂, because its Mr is 64.1; N₂ is 28.0 and CO₂ is 44.0 [1].

**9. (a)** Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂ [1].

**(b)** n(H₂) = n(Mg) = 0.120 / 24.3 = 4.94 × 10⁻³ mol [1].

**(c)** V = nRT/p = (4.938 × 10⁻³ × 8.31 × 298) / (1.00 × 10⁵) = 1.22 × 10⁻⁴ m³ [1] = 122 cm³ [1].

**10. (a)** At high pressure the molecules are close together [1], so the volume of the molecules is no longer negligible compared with the container volume, and intermolecular attractions become significant [1].

**(b)** At low temperature the molecules move more slowly [1], so intermolecular attractions have a greater effect, pulling molecules together and reducing the pressure compared with an ideal gas [1].

**11. (a)** At a higher temperature the molecules move faster [1], so they hit the walls more often and with greater force, increasing the force per unit area [1].

**(b)** p ∝ T at constant n and V: 1.00 × 10⁵ × 450 / 300 = 1.50 × 10⁵ Pa [1].

**12. (a)** 105.918 − 105.462 = 0.456 g [1].

**(b)** T = 293 K and V = 2.50 × 10⁻⁴ m³ [1]; n = (1.01 × 10⁵ × 2.50 × 10⁻⁴) / (8.31 × 293) = 1.037 × 10⁻² mol [1]; Mr = 0.456 / 1.037 × 10⁻² = 44.0 [1].

**(c)** Carbon dioxide, CO₂ [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not converting cm³ or dm³ to m³, kPa to Pa, or °C to K before substituting.
- Rounding n too early, which shifts Mr in the third figure.
- Saying ideal gas particles have "very small" volume instead of zero volume.
- Getting the direction of an experimental error on Mr the wrong way round.
- Leaving out units on a final answer.

## Frequently asked questions

**Should I use 24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹ or pV = nRT?**
Use the molar volume only when the question states room conditions and allows it. If a temperature and pressure are given, use pV = nRT.
