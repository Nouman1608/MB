Subtopic 5.1 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 asks you to know and understand "the effects of using microprocessor-controlled devices". The syllabus splits this into two settings. In the home you weigh effects on lifestyle, leisure time, physical fitness, security of data and the degree of social interaction. In transport you weigh security of data, autonomous vehicles and transport safety. This guide goes beyond the [Effects of Using IT study guide](/resources/igcse-ict-effects-of-using-it/) by looking at how the devices work, which device suits which point, and how to build answers that score.

## What a microprocessor-controlled device is

A **microprocessor** is a small processor built into a device to run one set of tasks. It follows a stored program, reads inputs and switches outputs. Many devices also contain **sensors** (temperature, light, motion, pressure) and **actuators** (motors, heaters, valves, pumps) that the microprocessor drives.

A **smart device** is a microprocessor-controlled device that is also connected to a network, usually the home's wireless router and the internet. This lets the owner monitor and control it from a smartphone app, and lets the device send data back to the manufacturer.

The difference matters in exams. A basic washing machine with a microprocessor saves effort, but it cannot be hacked from outside the home. A smart washing machine on the home network can, which is why "security of data" only becomes a strong point once a device is connected.

### Monitoring and controlling

The syllabus says "monitoring and controlling". **Monitoring** means the device collects and reports data without acting on it, for example a smart doorbell that records who calls. **Controlling** means the device changes an output in response to its data, for example a smart heating system that switches the boiler on when the room is colder than the preset value.

## Devices in the home

| Device | What the microprocessor does |
|---|---|
| Washing machine and dishwasher | Runs the chosen programme, controls water level, temperature and timing |
| Robot vacuum cleaner | Uses sensors to detect walls and stairs and plans a cleaning route |
| Microwave and smart oven | Times cooking; a smart oven can be switched on remotely from an app |
| Smart fridge | Monitors temperature; some models track contents and suggest shopping |
| Burglar alarm and smart camera | Detects movement and sends an alert or live video to a phone |
| Smart lighting | Switches lights on and off by time, movement or remote command |
| Smart TV and speaker | Streams media and responds to voice commands |

### Effects in the home, dimension by dimension

**Lifestyle.** Positive: labour-saving devices remove repetitive chores, and remote control means a meal can be cooking or the home warming before someone arrives. Negative: people may rely on convenience food and ready-made settings, and become dependent on devices that may break or need costly repair.

**Leisure time.** Positive: time once spent on chores such as washing up or vacuuming becomes free time for hobbies or family. Negative: much of the gained time may be spent on screens, such as streaming on a smart TV, rather than on varied activities.

**Physical fitness.** Positive: fitness trackers and smart watches monitor steps and heart rate and can encourage exercise. Negative: when devices do the moving, people walk and stand less; a robot vacuum or remote-controlled lighting removes small amounts of daily activity, and a less active lifestyle can contribute to weight gain.

**Security of data.** Positive: smart alarms and cameras improve the physical security of the home and let the owner check it while away. Negative: every connected device is a possible way into the home network. A hacker could view camera footage, learn when the house is empty from heating or lighting patterns, or steal account details stored in the app. Weak default passwords and software that is never updated make this more likely.

**Degree of social interaction.** Positive: video calls on smart TVs and speakers help people keep in touch with relatives who live far away. Negative: people may spend more time alone at home, and fewer everyday tasks bring a household together, so face-to-face contact can fall.

## Devices in transport

Microprocessors are found throughout modern transport:

- **In vehicles**: engine management, anti-lock braking, cruise control, parking sensors, airbag control, satellite navigation and lane-assist systems.
- **On roads**: smart traffic lights that change their timing according to traffic detected by sensors, and signs that display variable speed limits.
- **On railways and in the air**: train control and signalling systems, driverless metro trains, and autopilot systems in aircraft.

### Security of data

Connected vehicles store and transmit data such as location history, journey times and driving behaviour. Positive: this data can locate a stolen car, help emergency services after a crash and let a garage diagnose faults. Negative: if the data is intercepted or the manufacturer's server is hacked, a person's movements can be tracked, and in a worst case a vehicle's systems could be interfered with remotely.

### Autonomous vehicles

An **autonomous vehicle** uses sensors, cameras and GPS so that the microprocessor-controlled system steers, brakes and accelerates without a human driver. Positive effects include fewer accidents caused by tiredness, distraction or alcohol, smoother driving that uses fuel more efficiently, and independent travel for people who cannot drive. Negative effects include the high cost of the vehicles, the risk of sensor failure in heavy rain, snow or poor road markings, the risk of hacking, uncertainty about who is responsible after a crash, and fewer jobs for drivers.

### Transport safety

Systems such as anti-lock braking, collision warning and automatic emergency braking react faster than a human. Smart traffic lights reduce queues at junctions. The risk is over-reliance: a driver who trusts lane assist may stop paying attention, and a software fault could affect many vehicles at once.

## Worked example: an annotated answer

**Question:** A family has installed a robot vacuum cleaner, a smart oven and a smart security camera. Discuss the effects of these devices on the family's physical fitness and the security of their data. **[6]**

**Model answer:**

The robot vacuum cleaner does the vacuuming, so family members no longer walk around the house pushing a vacuum cleaner. *(Names the device and the mechanism.)* This removes a regular form of light exercise, so their physical fitness may fall over time. *(States the effect on the named dimension.)* However, the time saved could be used for exercise such as going for a run. *(Gives the other side.)*

The smart oven and camera are connected to the internet through an app. *(Explains why data is at risk.)* If the account is hacked, someone could watch the camera's live video or see when the oven is not in use, and so work out when the house is empty. *(A specific consequence, not just "it could be hacked".)* On the other hand, the camera lets the family check the house while away and sends alerts if someone breaks in. *(Balance.)* The risks can be reduced with strong, unique passwords and regular software updates, so overall the devices are beneficial if the family secures them. *(Brief judgement.)*

The answer earns its marks by tying every point to a named device and a named dimension, and by giving both sides.

## Common mistakes

- Writing "it makes people lazy" without saying which device removes which activity.
- Saying any device "can be hacked" when it is not connected to a network.
- Treating autonomous vehicles only as a safety improvement and forgetting cost, hacking, job losses and sensor failure.
- Mixing up the two lists: social interaction and leisure time are home dimensions; transport safety is a transport heading.
- Giving only positive effects in a "discuss" question.

## Exam technique

- **State** or **identify**: a short phrase, such as "less physical activity".
- **Describe**: say what happens, for example how a smart oven is switched on from an app.
- **Explain**: give a reason that links the device to the effect, using "so" or "because".
- **Discuss** or **evaluate**: give positive and negative effects for the dimensions named, then a short conclusion if the question asks for one.

Before writing, underline the setting (home or transport) and the dimensions in the question, and answer only those.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does every microprocessor-controlled device raise data security issues?**
No. The issue is strongest for devices connected to a network. A stand-alone microwave has no route for data to leave the home.

**Do I need to know how autonomous vehicles work in detail?**
You need enough to explain their effects: they use sensors, cameras and GPS so that a computer system controls steering, braking and acceleration.
