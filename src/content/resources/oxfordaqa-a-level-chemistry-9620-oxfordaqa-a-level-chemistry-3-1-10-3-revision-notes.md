## Core recall

- Water is **slightly dissociated**: H₂O ⇌ H⁺ + OH⁻.
- **Kw is derived from Kc** for this dissociation: Kc = [H⁺][OH⁻] / [H₂O].
- [H₂O] is effectively constant (about 55.6 mol dm⁻³), so Kc × [H₂O] = **Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻]**.
- Units: **mol² dm⁻⁶**. At 25 °C, Kw = **1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ mol² dm⁻⁶**.
- **Kw varies with temperature**: it increases as temperature rises.
- Skill: use Kw to calculate the **pH of a strong base** from its concentration.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ionic product of water, Kw | [H⁺][OH⁻] in any aqueous solution at a given temperature |
| Neutral | [H⁺] = [OH⁻] (pH 7 only at 25 °C) |
| Acidic | [H⁺] > [OH⁻] |
| Alkaline | [H⁺] < [OH⁻] |
| Strong base | Completely dissociated, so [OH⁻] follows from its concentration |
| Endothermic ionisation | Water's dissociation absorbs heat, so Kw rises with temperature |

## Why [H₂O] can be folded into the constant

- Only about 1 water molecule in 5.6 × 10⁸ is ionised at 25 °C.
- So [H₂O] barely changes and is treated as a constant.
- That constant is combined with Kc: Kc = 1.80 × 10⁻¹⁶ mol dm⁻³, and 1.80 × 10⁻¹⁶ × 55.6 = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ mol² dm⁻⁶.

## Temperature and neutrality

| Temperature / °C | Kw / mol² dm⁻⁶ | pH of pure water | Neutral? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 2.92 × 10⁻¹⁵ | 7.27 | Yes |
| 25 | 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ | 7.00 | Yes |
| 40 | 2.92 × 10⁻¹⁴ | 6.77 | Yes |

- Kw rises with temperature → equilibrium moves right on heating → ionisation is **endothermic**.
- In pure water, [H⁺] = √Kw at any temperature.
- Pure water is **always neutral**, whatever its pH.

## Method boxes

**[OH⁻] from [H⁺] (or pH)**

1. [H⁺] = 10^(−pH) if needed.
2. [OH⁻] = Kw ÷ [H⁺].

**pH of a strong base**

1. [OH⁻] = concentration × number of OH⁻ per formula unit (×2 for Ba(OH)₂, Sr(OH)₂, Ca(OH)₂).
2. [H⁺] = Kw ÷ [OH⁻] (Kw for the stated temperature).
3. pH = −log₁₀[H⁺].

**Strong base from a mass**

1. n = mass ÷ Mr; c = n ÷ V (dm³).
2. Continue as above.

**Base in excess after mixing with a strong acid**

1. Leftover n(OH⁻) = n(OH⁻) − n(H⁺).
2. Divide by the **total** volume.
3. Use Kw to find [H⁺], then pH.

## Shortcuts that check your answer

- At 25 °C, a strong base with [OH⁻] = 10⁻ⁿ has pH = 14 − n (for example, 0.0100 mol dm⁻³ NaOH has pH 12.00).
- Tenfold dilution of a strong base **lowers** the pH by 1.
- Raising the temperature **lowers** the pH of a strong base of fixed concentration, because Kw increases.

## Exam traps

- [H₂O] left in the Kw expression.
- Kw units given as mol dm⁻³.
- [OH⁻] not doubled for a Group 2 hydroxide.
- −log₁₀[OH⁻] reported as the pH.
- 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ used at a temperature where a different Kw is given.
- "Pure water at 40 °C is acidic": wrong; it is neutral.
- Explaining neutrality by pH value rather than by [H⁺] = [OH⁻].

## Quick self-test

Use Kw = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ mol² dm⁻⁶ at 25 °C unless told otherwise.

1. Write the expression for Kw and give its units.
2. Why can [H₂O] be treated as constant?
3. Calculate [OH⁻] when [H⁺] = 1.5 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³.
4. Calculate the pH of 0.0045 mol dm⁻³ NaOH.
5. Calculate the pH of 0.0045 mol dm⁻³ Ba(OH)₂.
6. 0.370 g of Ca(OH)₂ (Mr = 74.1) is dissolved and made up to 500 cm³. Assuming it is fully dissolved and dissociated, calculate the pH.
7. At 50 °C, Kw = 5.48 × 10⁻¹⁴ mol² dm⁻⁶. Calculate the pH of pure water and say whether it is neutral.
8. Calculate the pH of 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ KOH at 50 °C.
9. A solution has pH 11.80 at 25 °C. Calculate [OH⁻].
10. What does the increase of Kw with temperature show about the ionisation of water?
11. A strong base is diluted by a factor of 10. How does the pH change?
12. Explain why water at pH 6.77 can still be neutral.

### Answers

1. Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻]; **mol² dm⁻⁶**.
2. So few molecules ionise that the concentration of water hardly changes.
3. [OH⁻] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ ÷ 1.5 × 10⁻³ = **6.67 × 10⁻¹² mol dm⁻³**.
4. [H⁺] = 2.22 × 10⁻¹² mol dm⁻³; pH = **11.65**.
5. [OH⁻] = 0.0090 mol dm⁻³; [H⁺] = 1.11 × 10⁻¹² mol dm⁻³; pH = **11.95**.
6. n = 4.99 × 10⁻³ mol; c = 9.99 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³; [OH⁻] = 0.0200 mol dm⁻³; pH = **12.30**.
7. [H⁺] = √(5.48 × 10⁻¹⁴) = 2.34 × 10⁻⁷ mol dm⁻³; pH = **6.63**; **neutral**, because [H⁺] = [OH⁻].
8. [H⁺] = 5.48 × 10⁻¹⁴ ÷ 0.0500 = 1.10 × 10⁻¹² mol dm⁻³; pH = **11.96**.
9. [H⁺] = 1.58 × 10⁻¹² mol dm⁻³; [OH⁻] = **6.31 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³**.
10. It is **endothermic**: heating moves the equilibrium to the right.
11. It **falls by 1**.
12. Neutral means [H⁺] = [OH⁻]. At the temperature where pH 6.77 is the pH of pure water, Kw is larger, but the two concentrations are still equal.
