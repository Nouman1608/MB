**These notes condense Section 4 Agricultural development of Cambridge O Level Pakistan Studies
2059 Paper 2.** Expect a map of crop areas, a photograph to identify, a farming-system diagram or a
water-table cross-section, then a part (d) judgement on how to raise output sustainably. For the
full explanations, see the
[Agricultural Development study guide](/resources/o-level-pakistan-studies-agricultural-development/).

## Syllabus checklist (2059, 2026)

- ☐ 4(a) Subsistence, cash crop and livestock farming as input–process–output systems
- ☐ 4(b) Map areas and photographs of cotton, rice, sugar cane, wheat; buffalo, cattle, goats, sheep, poultry
- ☐ 4(b) Uses of crops and livestock products; areas, reasons and uses of twelve other crops
- ☐ 4(c) Natural and human factors; three subsistence case studies; four cash crops
- ☐ 4(c) Livestock on small farms; nomadic herding and transhumance
- ☐ 4(c) Karez, inundation and perennial canals; Persian wheel and tubewell; dams, barrages, link and distribution canals, field channels, bunds
- ☐ 4(c) Waterlogging and salinity: causes, restoration, prevention
- ☐ 4(c) Government action; possibilities, problems and sustainability

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Subsistence farming | Growing food mainly for the farmer's own household |
| Cash cropping | Growing crops mainly for sale |
| Kharif | Summer-sown, autumn-harvested season (cotton, rice, cane, maize) |
| Rabi | Winter-sown, spring-harvested season (wheat, gram, oilseeds) |
| Barani | Rain-fed land without irrigation |
| Fragmented holding | A farm split into scattered plots, often by inheritance |
| Karez | Underground gravity channel carrying groundwater to an oasis |
| Barrage | A low dam across a river that raises water level to feed canals |
| Link canal | Canal transferring water from one river to another |
| Bund | Earth ridge holding water on a field |
| Waterlogging | Soil saturated because the water table has risen near the surface |
| Salinity | Salt left in topsoil when groundwater evaporates |
| Transhumance | Seasonal movement of herds between summer and winter pastures |

## The four main crops at a glance

| Crop | Season | Climate need | Example districts | Leading use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cotton | Kharif | Long, hot, frost-free summer; dry at picking | Vehari, Bahawalnagar, Sanghar | Lint to textile mills |
| Rice | Kharif | 20°C+, standing water, clay soil | Sheikhupura, Narowal; Larkana | Food and export (basmati) |
| Sugar cane | Kharif (12–18 months in ground) | Heat, heavy water use | Rahim Yar Khan, Mardan, Badin | Sugar, gur, ethanol |
| Wheat | Rabi | Cool growing, warm dry ripening | All Punjab and Sindh plains; Chakwal barani | Flour (staple) |

**Memory hook for the other twelve crops:** "Cold north, hot south, sand in the middle."
Apples (Quetta, Swat) and apricots (Hunza, Skardu) need cold winters; bananas (Thatta), dates
(Khairpur, Turbat) and mangoes (Multan, Mirpur Khas) need great heat; millet and gram survive on
the sandy Thal and Thar margins; kinnow oranges cluster around Sargodha; tobacco and maize centre
on Mardan, Swabi and the Peshawar valley; vegetables ring the big cities because they spoil
quickly.

## Three subsistence case studies

| Case | Natural factor | Human factor | Problem |
|---|---|---|---|
| Traditional rice | Clay soils, monsoon heat | Hand transplanting, family labour | Small, fragmented plots; little capital |
| Barani wheat (Potwar) | Variable winter rain; gullied land | Low inputs because of risk | Yields swing year to year |
| Karez oasis (Balochistan) | Aquifer at hill foot; extreme heat | Community maintains tunnel | Tubewells lower water table |

## Irrigation in one chain

River → **dam** stores water → **barrage** raises level → **link canal** shifts water between
rivers → **distribution canal** → **watercourse** → **field channel** → field held by **bunds**.
Lift irrigation (Persian wheel, tubewell) draws on groundwater instead.

## Waterlogging and salinity cross-section (draw this)

Label: unlined canal → seepage arrows downward → rising water table → capillary rise in hot season
→ evaporation → white salt crust on surface. Add a tubewell pumping water down and a drain
removing saline water to show the solution.

**Solutions:** SCARP tubewells; drains such as the Left Bank Outfall Drain; lining watercourses;
laser levelling; gypsum for sodic soils; salt-tolerant crops; drip and sprinkler irrigation.

## Government action: evaluate, do not list

| Action | Gain | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Land reforms (1959, 1972, 1977) | Ceilings on holdings; tenant rights | Evaded; many large estates remained |
| High-yielding seeds | Green Revolution wheat yields rose | Need water and fertiliser the poor cannot buy |
| Credit (Zarai Taraqiati Bank) | Loans for inputs and machines | Collateral rules exclude tenants |
| Research and extension | New varieties, pest advice | Too few extension workers reach small farmers |
| Subsidised fertiliser and solar tubewells | Cheaper inputs | Overuse; falling water tables |

## Exam traps

- **Calling sugar cane a rabi crop.** It is planted in the kharif cycle and occupies land for most
  of a year.
- **Mixing up the karez and the Persian wheel.** The karez is gravity-fed and underground; the
  Persian wheel lifts water from a well.
- **Explaining salinity without evaporation.** Salt only appears when water evaporates at the
  surface.
- **Ignoring the photograph.** If asked to identify a crop, say what in the photo shows it, such as
  flooded fields with seedlings in rows for rice.
- **"Government helped farmers" with no example.** Name a reform, a bank or a seed programme.

## Self-test

1. Name one district famous for basmati rice.
2. Which crop is ginned before it reaches a mill?
3. What is the role of a barrage?
4. Give two ways land damaged by salinity can be restored.
5. State one reason vegetables are grown close to cities.
6. Name one Pakistani buffalo breed.

**Answers:** 1. Sheikhupura, Narowal, Hafizabad or another Kallar tract district. 2. Cotton.
3. It raises river level so water can be diverted into canals. 4. Any two of tubewell pumping,
surface or tile drains, flushing, adding gypsum, salt-tolerant crops. 5. They are perishable, so
they must reach markets quickly. 6. Nili-Ravi or Kundi.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many crops do I need to know for 2059 Section 4?**
Four main crops in depth (cotton, rice, sugar cane, wheat) and twelve others by area, reason for
location and one use.

**What is the best way to revise crop locations?**
Shade them on a blank map of Pakistan's provinces and rivers, because the exam often gives a map
and asks you to describe a distribution.

**Why is waterlogging worst in the Indus Plain?**
It has one of the world's largest continuous canal networks on flat land, so seepage is huge and drainage
is slow, while hot summers draw salt to the surface.
