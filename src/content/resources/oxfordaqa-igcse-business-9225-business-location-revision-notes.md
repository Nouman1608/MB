Condensed recall for section 3.1.5 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Business (9225). For full explanations and worked examples, use the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-9225-business-location/). Figures are invented.

## What the specification asks

Understand the factors that influence where a business is located:

- **proximity to the market**
- **availability of raw materials**
- **labour**
- **competition**
- **costs**
- **government influence**.

## The six factors at a glance

| Factor | What to say | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Proximity to the market | Close to customers means more sales and lower delivery cost | Cafe on a busy street; bakery near homes |
| Raw materials | Close to supplies cuts transport cost, especially bulky or perishable items | Sawmill near forests; fish processing near ports |
| Labour | Needs enough workers with the right skills at an affordable wage | Call centre near a town with many job seekers |
| Competition | Clustering draws customers; isolation avoids rivals | Restaurant street; only petrol station in a village |
| Costs | Rent, wages, rates and transport add up; compare totals | Edge-of-town warehouse cheaper than city centre |
| Government influence | Grants, tax breaks, planning rules and infrastructure | Grant for firms in a high-unemployment area |

## Which factor matters most?

| Business type | Key factors |
|---|---|
| Shop or restaurant | Market (footfall), competition |
| Factory using bulky raw materials | Raw materials, transport costs |
| Call centre or office | Labour, costs |
| Online retailer | Costs, transport links, labour |
| Fresh food producer | Market and raw materials (products spoil) |

## Calculations

**Total cost of a site** = rent + wages + transport + other costs.

Site A: $3,500 + $9,000 + $1,200 = $13,700 a month. Site B: $2,000 + $7,500 + $3,000 = $12,500 a month. Difference: $1,200 a month, or $14,400 a year.

**Transport cost per unit** = total transport cost / units. $18,000 / 6,000 tonnes = $3.00 a tonne. If transport cost falls to $4,500, then $4,500 / 6,000 = $0.75 a tonne.

**Customers from footfall** = people passing x percentage entering. 1,200 x 5% = 60 customers a day.

## How governments influence location

- **Grants and cheap loans** for areas with high unemployment.
- **Lower taxes** in special zones.
- **Planning rules** on what can be built where.
- **Infrastructure**: roads, rail, ports and broadband.
- **Restrictions** on noisy or polluting businesses near homes.

## Competition: near or far?

| Locate near rivals | Locate away from rivals |
|---|---|
| Customers come to compare | Less rivalry for local customers |
| Area becomes known for that type of shop | Business is the only supplier in the area |
| Shared reputation | Can charge higher prices |
| Risk: customers may choose a rival | Risk: fewer passing customers |

## Evaluating a choice

1. Decide what the business needs most.
2. Compare the sites on those factors.
3. Check costs as a total, not one by one.
4. Consider the longer term: can the site support growth?
5. Make a clear recommendation and give a reason.

## Exam traps

- Do not list all six factors for every business; choose the relevant ones.
- Cheap rent does not make a site cheap if wages, transport or lost sales are higher.
- Competition can be a benefit as well as a problem.
- Online businesses depend less on proximity to customers.
- Always use case details such as the type of goods, the staff needed and the sites offered.
- Government influence means grants, planning rules, taxes and infrastructure.

## Model answer outline: a 9-mark "evaluate" question

Question style: "Evaluate whether a furniture maker should locate in the town centre or on an industrial estate."

- **Identify what matters most:** the firm makes heavy goods and needs timber, so raw materials and costs are likely to matter more than footfall.
- **Estate:** lower rent and wages, closer to the sawmill, possible government grant.
- **Town centre:** more customers passing and a larger labour pool, but higher costs.
- **Calculation:** compare total monthly costs for both sites and annualise the difference.
- **Judgement:** recommend one site, say why the other is weaker for this business, and add a condition (for example "provided the grant lasts and customers can still reach the showroom").

## Revision checklist

- I can list the six location factors.
- I can say which factors matter most for a shop, a factory, a call centre and an online seller.
- I can add up the costs of two sites and compare them.
- I can calculate transport cost per unit.
- I can explain why a business might locate near or away from competitors.
- I can give four ways a government can influence location.
- I can write a recommendation that uses the facts in the case.

## Self-test

1. List the six factors that influence business location.
2. Explain why a bakery wants to be near its customers.
3. Explain why a quarry is located near its raw materials.
4. Give one reason why a call centre might locate in an area with lower wages.
5. State one benefit of locating near competitors.
6. Name two ways in which a government can influence where a business locates.
7. Site A costs $5,000 rent, $8,000 wages and $1,000 transport a month. Site B costs $3,000, $8,500 and $2,500. Which is cheaper and by how much a month?
8. A firm pays $12,000 a month to transport 4,000 units. Calculate the transport cost per unit.
9. 800 people pass a shop each day and 3% enter. How many customers enter?
10. Why might an online retailer choose a cheap warehouse far from its customers?
11. Explain one drawback of a city-centre location.
12. A farm shop is deciding between a busy main road and a quiet village lane. Give one factor in favour of each.

### Answers

1. Proximity to the market, raw materials, labour, competition, costs, government influence.
2. Bread must be fresh and customers usually buy it close to home, so being near them gives more sales and short, cheap deliveries.
3. Stone is heavy and bulky, so moving it long distances is expensive.
4. A call centre employs many people, so lower wages cut its largest cost, and customers do not visit.
5. Customers come to the area to compare, which brings more passing trade.
6. Any two: grants; lower taxes; planning rules; infrastructure; restrictions on pollution.
7. Site A = 5,000 + 8,000 + 1,000 = $14,000; Site B = 3,000 + 8,500 + 2,500 = $14,000. They cost the **same**, a difference of **$0** a month, so other factors would decide.
8. $12,000 / 4,000 = **$3** per unit.
9. 3% x 800 = **24** customers.
10. Customers order online, so they do not visit the warehouse and a low-cost site reduces its costs.
11. High rent and rates, which reduce profit unless sales rise enough.
12. Main road: more passing customers. Lane: lower rent and less competition from other shops.
