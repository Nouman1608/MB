These questions cover section M1.3 of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Mathematics (9660), version 5.2. All forces are horizontal or vertical, so no resolving is needed. Take g = 9.8 m s⁻² and give answers to 3 significant figures where they are not exact. Strings are light and inextensible, and pulleys are smooth.

---

## Section A

**1.** A particle of mass 3.5 kg rests on a smooth horizontal surface. A horizontal force of 14 N acts on it. Find its weight and its acceleration. **[2]**

**2.** A van of mass 2000 kg travels at a constant speed along a level road with a driving force of 1600 N. State the total resistance to its motion and give a reason. **[2]**

**3.** A box of mass 5 kg is raised vertically by a rope with an upward acceleration of 0.4 m s⁻². Find the tension in the rope. **[2]**

**4.** A stone of mass 0.2 kg falls vertically through the air with acceleration 8.5 m s⁻². Find the air resistance, assumed constant. **[2]**

**5.** A crate of mass 15 kg is pushed across a rough horizontal floor at constant velocity by a horizontal force of 90 N. Find the coefficient of friction. **[3]**

## Section B

**6.** A lift of mass 500 kg carries two passengers with a total mass of 150 kg. One passenger has mass 70 kg.

**(a)** The tension in the cable is 6890 N. Find the acceleration of the lift. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the normal reaction on the 70 kg passenger during this acceleration. **[2]**

**(c)** Later, while moving upwards at 2 m s⁻¹, the lift slows uniformly to rest in 2.5 s. Find the tension in the cable during this stage. **[3]**

**7.** A bucket of mass 6 kg is raised by a vertical rope. A stone of mass 2 kg hangs below the bucket from a light vertical string. The tension in the rope is 100 N.

**(a)** Find the acceleration of the system. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the tension in the string. **[2]**

**8.** Block P, of mass 4 kg, lies on a rough horizontal table with coefficient of friction 0.2, at a distance of 2 m from a pulley at the edge of the table. A string attached to P passes over the pulley to block Q, of mass 3 kg, which hangs 0.8 m above the floor. The system is released from rest.

**(a)** Find the acceleration of the system and the tension in the string. **[4]**

**(b)** Find the speed of Q as it hits the floor. **[2]**

**(c)** Q does not rebound. Determine whether P reaches the pulley. **[3]**

**9.** Particles A, of mass 5 kg, and B, of mass m kg, hang at the ends of a string over a pulley. When released from rest, A moves downwards with acceleration 1.4 m s⁻².

**(a)** Find the tension in the string. **[1]**

**(b)** Find m. **[3]**

**10.** A locomotive of mass 60 000 kg pulls three wagons, each of mass 20 000 kg, along a straight level track. The couplings are light and horizontal. The driving force is 72 000 N. The resistance is 6000 N on the locomotive and 2000 N on each wagon.

**(a)** Find the acceleration. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the tension in the coupling between the locomotive and the first wagon. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why this coupling has the greatest tension of the three. **[1]**

**11.** A car of mass 1100 kg tows a caravan of mass 700 kg along a level road, joined by a light rigid tow bar. With the engine off, the car's brakes provide a braking force on the car only. Resistances are 250 N on the car and 350 N on the caravan. Car and caravan decelerate together at 1.5 m s⁻² from 20 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Find the braking force. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the force in the tow bar and state whether it is a tension or a thrust. **[3]**

**(c)** Find the distance travelled before coming to rest. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain why a tow rope could not replace the tow bar in this situation. **[1]**

**12.** A book of mass 1.2 kg rests on a horizontal table.

**(a)** Identify the force that forms a Newton's third law pair with the book's weight, giving its size and direction. **[2]**

**(b)** A student presses down vertically on the book with a force of 8 N. Find the normal reaction from the table on the book. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** W = 3.5 × 9.8 = **34.3 N** [1]. a = 14 ÷ 3.5 = **4 m s⁻²** [1].

**2.** **1600 N** [1]. The speed is constant, so the acceleration is zero and the resultant force is zero (Newton's first law): resistance equals the driving force [1].

**3.** T − 5g = 5 × 0.4 [1], so T = 5 × 10.2 = **51 N** [1].

**4.** Down positive: 0.2g − D = 0.2 × 8.5 [1], so D = 0.2 × 1.3 = **0.26 N** [1].

**5.** Constant velocity, so friction = 90 N [1]. R = 15 × 9.8 = 147 N [1]. μ = 90 ÷ 147 = **0.612** [1].

**6. (a)** Whole lift, mass 650 kg: 6890 − 650 × 9.8 = 650a [1]. 6890 − 6370 = 520 [1], so a = **0.8 m s⁻² upwards** [1].

**(b)** R − 70g = 70 × 0.8 [1], so R = 70 × 10.6 = **742 N** [1].

**(c)** a = (0 − 2) ÷ 2.5 = −0.8 m s⁻² [1]. T − 650g = 650 × (−0.8) [1], so T = 650 × 9 = **5850 N** [1].

**7. (a)** Whole system, mass 8 kg: 100 − 8 × 9.8 = 8a [1], so a = 21.6 ÷ 8 = **2.7 m s⁻²** [1].

**(b)** Stone: T − 2g = 2 × 2.7 [1], so T = 2 × 12.5 = **25 N** [1].

**8. (a)** R = 4g = 39.2 N, so friction = 0.2 × 39.2 = 7.84 N [1]. P: T − 7.84 = 4a. Q: 3g − T = 3a [1]. Adding: 29.4 − 7.84 = 7a, so **a = 3.08 m s⁻²** [1]. T = 7.84 + 4 × 3.08 = **20.16 N (20.2 N)** [1].

**(b)** v² = 2 × 3.08 × 0.8 = 4.928 [1], so v = **2.22 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(c)** After Q lands the string is slack; friction alone decelerates P at 0.2 × 9.8 = 1.96 m s⁻² [1]. Distance to stop = 4.928 ÷ (2 × 1.96) = 1.257 m [1]. P has 2 − 0.8 = 1.2 m left to the pulley, and 1.257 > 1.2, so **P reaches the pulley** [1].

**9. (a)** A: 5g − T = 5 × 1.4, so T = 5 × 8.4 = **42 N** [1].

**(b)** B moves up with acceleration 1.4 m s⁻²: T − mg = 1.4m [1]. 42 = m(9.8 + 1.4) = 11.2m [1], so **m = 3.75** [1].

**10. (a)** Total mass 120 000 kg, total resistance 12 000 N: 72 000 − 12 000 = 120 000a [1], so **a = 0.5 m s⁻²** [1].

**(b)** The three wagons together: T − 6000 = 60 000 × 0.5 [1], so **T = 36 000 N** [1]. Check with the locomotive: 72 000 − 6000 − 36 000 = 30 000 = 60 000 × 0.5.

**(c)** It must accelerate all three wagons and overcome all their resistances, while each coupling further back pulls fewer wagons [1].

**11. (a)** Whole system, deceleration 1.5 m s⁻²: B + 250 + 350 = 1800 × 1.5 [1], so **B = 2100 N** [1].

**(b)** The caravan needs a backward resultant of 700 × 1.5 = 1050 N [1]. Its resistance gives 350 N, so the bar must push it backwards with **700 N** [1]. The bar pushes, so it is a **thrust** [1].

**(c)** 0 = 400 − 3s, so s = **133 m** [1].

**(d)** The link must push the caravan backwards, and a rope can only pull, so it would go slack [1].

**12. (a)** The gravitational force of the book on the Earth [1], of size 1.2 × 9.8 = 11.76 N, acting upwards on the Earth [1].

**(b)** R = 11.76 + 8 = **19.76 N (19.8 N)** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Taking R = mg in a lift or when a vertical push or pull acts.
- Writing a single-body equation with the whole system's mass.
- Treating tension as equal to the hanging weight while the system accelerates.
- Keeping the stage 1 acceleration after the string goes slack.
- Naming the normal reaction as the third law partner of the weight.
- Saying a tow bar is in tension during braking without checking the sign of the force.
