## Puberty -- Core

| | Testosterone | Oestrogen |
|---|---|---|
| Made in | testes | ovaries |
| Changes at puberty | facial and body hair; deeper voice; muscle development; growth of penis and testes; sperm production starts | breasts develop; hips widen; pubic and underarm hair; menstrual cycle starts |

- **Secondary sexual characteristics** develop at puberty; the hormones also **regulate** (maintain) them afterwards.
- Primary sexual characteristics are the reproductive organs present from birth.

## Menstrual cycle -- Core

Day 1 = first day of menstruation. Typical length about 28 days; real cycles vary.

| Days | Ovary | Uterus lining |
|---|---|---|
| 1–5 | follicle starts developing; egg matures | **menstruation**: lining breaks down and is lost |
| 6–13 | follicle grows | lining **repairs and thickens** |
| ~14 | **ovulation**: egg released into oviduct | thick |
| 15–28 | corpus luteum forms | lining **maintained** for implantation |
| end | corpus luteum breaks down (no pregnancy) | lining breaks down → new cycle |

**Date rule:** in a question that says ovulation is 14 days before the next menstruation, ovulation day = cycle length + 1 − 14.

## Hormone sources -- Extended only

| Hormone | Menstrual cycle | Pregnancy |
|---|---|---|
| FSH | pituitary gland | (inhibited) |
| LH | pituitary gland | (inhibited) |
| Oestrogen | ovaries (developing follicle) | placenta |
| Progesterone | corpus luteum (ovary) | corpus luteum at first, then placenta |

## Hormone roles -- Extended only

| Hormone | Roles |
|---|---|
| FSH | causes a follicle to develop and an egg to mature; stimulates oestrogen production |
| Oestrogen | repairs and thickens lining; inhibits FSH; high levels stimulate LH release |
| LH | surge causes ovulation; follicle becomes corpus luteum |
| Progesterone | maintains lining; inhibits FSH and LH |

**The chain:** FSH ↑ → follicle + oestrogen ↑ → lining thickens, FSH ↓, LH surge → ovulation → corpus luteum → progesterone ↑ → lining kept, FSH and LH ↓ → (no pregnancy) corpus luteum breaks down → progesterone ↓ → menstruation, FSH ↑ again.

**Pregnancy:** progesterone stays high (corpus luteum, then placenta) → lining maintained, no menstruation; FSH and LH inhibited → no ovulation.

## Reading hormone graphs

- LH peak → ovulation (about day 14 in a 28-day cycle).
- Oestrogen peaks **just before** the LH surge.
- Progesterone is highest in the **second half** of the cycle.
- Progesterone falling at the end → no pregnancy; menstruation follows.
- Progesterone staying high → pregnancy.

## Worked recall: a 26-day cycle

Menstruation starts on 10 March; the question says ovulation is 14 days before the next menstruation.

```
Next day 1 = day 27 = 10 March + 26 days = 5 April
Ovulation  = day 27 − 14 = day 13
Day 13     = 10 March + 12 days = 22 March
```

## Answer frames for "explain" questions -- Extended only

- **Why no ovulation in pregnancy:** progesterone high (corpus luteum, then placenta) → inhibits FSH and LH → no follicle develops, no LH surge.
- **Why menstruation follows if no pregnancy:** corpus luteum breaks down → progesterone falls → lining not maintained → lining lost.
- **Why the lining thickens in the first half:** FSH stimulates oestrogen production → oestrogen repairs and thickens the lining.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Puberty | the time when secondary sexual characteristics develop |
| Secondary sexual characteristics | features that develop at puberty under the control of sex hormones |
| Menstruation | breakdown and loss of the uterus lining |
| Ovulation | release of an egg cell from the ovary |
| Follicle | structure in the ovary in which an egg cell matures (Extended) |
| Corpus luteum | the empty follicle after ovulation; secretes progesterone (Extended) |
| Pituitary gland | gland in the brain that secretes FSH and LH (Extended) |

## Exam traps

- Menstruation is **not** ovulation.
- Not every cycle is 28 days: follow the rule in the question.
- Name **specific** changes: "facial hair", not just "hair".
- (Extended) FSH and LH come from the **pituitary gland**, not the ovary.
- (Extended) **LH** causes ovulation, not progesterone or oestrogen.
- (Extended) In pregnancy the **placenta** makes progesterone and oestrogen.
- (Extended) Say **which** hormones progesterone inhibits.

## Self-test

**1.** Where is testosterone made?

**2.** Give two secondary sexual characteristics caused by oestrogen.

**3.** On which day of the cycle does menstruation begin?

**4.** What happens to the uterus lining between days 6 and 13?

**5.** What is ovulation?

**6.** Why is the uterus lining kept thick after ovulation?

**7.** *(Extended)* Name the gland that secretes FSH.

**8.** *(Extended)* Where is progesterone made in the second half of the cycle?

**9.** *(Extended)* Which hormone causes ovulation?

**10.** *(Extended)* State two effects of progesterone.

**11.** *(Extended)* Where is progesterone made in late pregnancy?

**12.** *(Extended)* Explain why menstruation starts when the corpus luteum breaks down.

### Answers

**1.** In the testes.

**2.** Any two of: breast development; widening of hips; pubic or underarm hair; start of the menstrual cycle.

**3.** Day 1.

**4.** It is repaired and thickens.

**5.** The release of an egg cell from the ovary.

**6.** So that an embryo can implant if fertilisation happens.

**7.** The pituitary gland.

**8.** The corpus luteum in the ovary.

**9.** LH.

**10.** Maintains the uterus lining; inhibits FSH and LH.

**11.** The placenta.

**12.** Progesterone falls, so the lining is no longer maintained and breaks down.
