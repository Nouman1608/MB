## Section A

**1.** Which change happens when a person breathes in?

A: the diaphragm relaxes and rises

B: the external intercostal muscles contract

C: the ribs move down and in

D: the volume of the thorax decreases

A: alveolar cells

B: ciliated cells

C: goblet cells

D: red blood cells

**[1]**

**3.** State the approximate percentage of oxygen in inspired air. **[1]**

**4.** State **two** features of the alveoli that make them good gas exchange surfaces. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** The table shows the composition of inspired and expired air.

| Gas | Inspired air (%) | Expired air (%) |
|---|---|---|
| Oxygen | 21 | 16 |
| Carbon dioxide | 0.04 | 4 |
| Nitrogen | 78 | 78 |

**(a)** Calculate how many times more carbon dioxide there is in expired air than in inspired air. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the percentage of oxygen is lower in expired air. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe an experiment to show that expired air contains more carbon dioxide than inspired air. **[3]**

**(d)** State **two** other ways in which expired air differs from inspired air. **[2]**

**7.** Explain how the movements of the ribs and diaphragm cause air to enter the lungs during inspiration. **[5]**

**8.** A student measured her breathing at rest and after exercise.

| Condition | Breaths per minute | Volume per breath (dm cubed) |
|---|---|---|
| At rest | 15 | 0.4 |
| After exercise | 30 | 1.0 |

**(a)** Calculate the volume of air breathed per minute at rest and after exercise. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate how many times greater the volume breathed per minute is after exercise. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why breathing changes during exercise. **[3]**

**9.** Describe the roles of goblet cells and ciliated cells in protecting the lungs. **[4]**

**10.** The wall of an alveolus and the wall of a capillary are each one cell thick.

**(a)** Explain why this is an advantage for gas exchange. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the blood supply to the alveoli is important. **[2]**

**11.** A person with a damaged diaphragm cannot breathe in properly. Explain why. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**

**3.** 21% [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Any two of: large surface area; thin walls (one cell thick); moist lining; good blood supply; good air supply [1 each]. **[2]**

**5.** The bronchus (bronchi) [1]. **[1]**

**6.**

**(a)** 4 divided by 0.04 [1] = 100 times [1].

**(b)** Oxygen is absorbed from the alveoli into the blood [1] and used by the body cells in aerobic respiration [1].

**(c)** Bubble inspired air and expired air through separate tubes of limewater [1]. Use the same volume of limewater and the same number of breaths [1]. The limewater through which expired air is breathed goes cloudy (milky) faster [1].

**(d)** Any two of: contains more water vapour; is warmer [1 each]. **[9]**

**7.** The external intercostal muscles contract, which moves the ribs up and out [1]. The diaphragm contracts and flattens [1]. The volume of the thorax increases [1]. The pressure in the lungs falls below the atmospheric pressure [1]. Air moves in from outside because it moves from a region of high pressure to a region of low pressure [1]. **[5]**

**8.**

**(a)** At rest: 15 x 0.4 = 6.0 dm cubed per minute [1]. After exercise: 30 x 1.0 = 30 dm cubed per minute [1].

**(b)** 30 divided by 6.0 = 5 times [1].

**(c)** During exercise the muscle cells respire faster [1], using more oxygen and producing more carbon dioxide [1]. The brain detects the rise in carbon dioxide and increases the rate and depth of breathing, to take in more oxygen and remove the carbon dioxide [1]. **[6]**

**9.** Goblet cells make mucus [1], which traps dust and pathogens [1]. Ciliated cells have cilia that beat [1] and move the mucus up to the throat so that it can be swallowed or coughed out [1]. **[4]**

**10.**

**(a)** The diffusion distance between the air and the blood is very short [1], so oxygen and carbon dioxide diffuse across quickly [1].

**(b)** Blood carries oxygen away from the alveoli, and brings carbon dioxide to them [1], which keeps a steep concentration gradient so that diffusion continues [1]. **[4]**

**11.** The diaphragm contracts and flattens to increase the volume of the thorax [1]. If it is damaged, the volume does not increase enough [1], so the pressure does not fall enough and less air enters the lungs [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out the pressure step in an explanation of breathing.
- Saying that the diaphragm rises when breathing in.
- Writing "the lungs expand by themselves".
- Not saying that mucus traps pathogens and cilia move it.
- Stating the percentage of expired oxygen as zero.
