**Section B of AQA GCSE History Paper 1 is the wider world depth study: one international conflict studied in detail.** Schools choose one of five *Conflict and tension* options -- the First World War (1894-1918), the inter-war years (1918-1939), East and West (1945-1972), Asia (1950-1975), or the Gulf and Afghanistan (1990-2009). You answer four questions: two on sources, a narrative account and an extended essay, for 40 marks plus 4 for spelling, punctuation and grammar.

## Where this fits in 8145

Section B is half of Paper 1, *Understanding the modern world* (2 hours, 84 marks, 50% of the GCSE); the other half is the period study in Section A. All three parts of the option's content are tested in each series. Related pages: [Wider world depth studies revision notes](/resources/gcse-history-wider-world-depth-studies-revision-notes/), [Wider world depth studies practice](/resources/gcse-history-wider-world-depth-studies-practice/), [Section A period studies guide](/resources/gcse-history-period-studies-study-guide/) and the [Paper 1 options overview](/resources/gcse-history-understanding-the-modern-world-options/).

## How is Section B examined?

| Question | Type | Marks | Skill |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Source analysis ("How do you know?") | 4 | AO3: one source -- content and provenance |
| 2 | How useful are Sources B and C? | 12 | AO3: content, provenance and context of two sources |
| 3 | Write an account of how... | 8 | AO1 + AO2: a narrative with cause or consequence |
| 4 | "Statement." How far do you agree? | 16 + 4 SPaG | AO1 + AO2: sustained judgement |

Sources may be written or visual, such as cartoons, posters and photographs.

## BA Conflict and tension: the First World War, 1894-1918

**Causes.** The **Triple Alliance** (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy, 1882) faced the **Franco-Russian Alliance** (1894), joined by Britain's ententes with France (1904) and Russia (1907). Crises over **Morocco** (1905, 1911) and **Bosnia** (1908-09) hardened the blocs. Britain abandoned **Splendid Isolation** as the Kaiser's **Weltpolitik**, colonial ambitions and the **naval race** (the *Dreadnought*, 1906) alarmed it. Slav nationalism set Serbia against Austria-Hungary; the assassination of **Franz Ferdinand** at Sarajevo (28 June 1914) led to the July Crisis, and the **Schlieffen Plan**'s march through **Belgium** brought Britain in.

**Stalemate.** The plan failed at the **Marne**; trench warfare, machine guns and artillery produced attrition at **Verdun**, the **Somme** and **Passchendaele** (1917). **Gallipoli** failed; at sea, **Jutland**, the **U-boat** campaign and **convoys** decided control of supply routes.

**Ending the war.** The Bolshevik Revolution took Russia out of the war, freeing German troops; **US entry** (1917) added fresh resources. **Ludendorff's Spring Offensive** (1918) failed, and the Allies' **Hundred Days** advance, using new tactics and technology, pushed Germany back. The **blockade**, the **Kaiser's abdication** and the **Armistice** followed; historians debate the contribution of **Haig** and **Foch**, supreme Allied commander from 1918.

## BB Conflict and tension: the inter-war years, 1918-1939

**Peacemaking.** **Wilson**'s Fourteen Points, **Clemenceau**'s wish to weaken Germany and **Lloyd George**'s compromise shaped the **Treaty of Versailles** (1919): territorial losses, military limits, **war guilt** and **reparations**, all imposed as a *Diktat*. New states faced ethnic minorities and border disputes.

**The League of Nations.** Its **Covenant**, Assembly, Council and agencies (such as those on health and refugees) aimed at collective security, but the USA never joined. It succeeded over the **Aaland Islands** (1921), **Upper Silesia** (1921) and the Greek-**Bulgarian** dispute (1925), but failed over **Vilna** (1920) and **Corfu** (1923). Diplomacy outside it produced **Locarno** (1925) and **Kellogg-Briand** (1928). The **Depression** weakened members; **Manchuria** (1931) and **Abyssinia** (1935-36) exposed its weakness.

**Origins of the Second World War.** Hitler's aims -- overturning Versailles, uniting Germans, *Lebensraum* -- led to the **Saar** plebiscite (1935), conscription and rearmament; Italy reacted to the **Dollfuss** affair (1934) and briefly joined the **Stresa Front** (1935), while Britain signed the **Anglo-German Naval Agreement**. The **Rhineland** (1936), the **Axis** and **Anti-Comintern Pact**, the **Anschluss** (1938), **appeasement** and **Munich** (September 1938) escalated tension. After the occupation of **Czechoslovakia** (March 1939) and the **Nazi-Soviet Pact** (August 1939), Germany invaded **Poland** on 1 September 1939. Responsibility is debated between **Hitler**, **Stalin** and **Chamberlain**.

## BC Conflict and tension between East and West, 1945-1972

**Origins.** **Yalta** and **Potsdam** (1945) divided Germany; ideological differences and the **atomic bomb** strained relations among Stalin, Churchill, Roosevelt, Attlee and Truman. Soviet expansion in Eastern Europe met the **Truman Doctrine** and **Marshall Plan** (1947); Stalin replied with **Cominform** and **Comecon**; **Yugoslavia** broke away; the **Berlin Blockade and Airlift** (1948-49) followed.

**Development.** Soviet support for **Mao**'s revolution in China (1949), the war in **Korea** and the Vietminh and Vietcong campaigns spread the Cold War to Asia. **NATO** (1949) faced the **Warsaw Pact** (1955); the **arms race** and **space race** -- **Sputnik** (1957), ICBMs, **Polaris**, **Gagarin** (1961), **Apollo** -- raised the stakes. The "Thaw" after Stalin ended with **Hungary** (1956) and the **U2** incident (1960), which wrecked the Paris summit.

**Transformation.** The **Berlin Wall** (1961) and Kennedy's response; Cuba -- **Castro**, the **Bay of Pigs** (1961) and the **Missile Crisis** (October 1962); the **Prague Spring** and **Brezhnev Doctrine** (1968); and **détente**, with **SALT 1** (1972) signed by **Nixon** and **Brezhnev**.

## BD Conflict and tension in Asia, 1950-1975

**Korea.** Korea, divided at the 38th parallel after 1945, had rival leaders **Kim Il Sung** and **Syngman Rhee**. North Korea invaded in June 1950; with the USSR boycotting the Security Council, the **UN** sent forces under **MacArthur**. The **Inchon** landings recaptured the South; the UN advance north brought **Chinese intervention** (October 1950); Truman **sacked MacArthur** (1951); stalemate led to the **armistice** (1953).

**Vietnam escalates.** The French defeat at **Dien Bien Phu** (1954) led to the **Geneva Agreement**; opposition to **Diem** and the **Vietcong**'s guerrilla war, backed by **Ho Chi Minh**, followed. The **Domino Theory** justified intervention under Eisenhower and Kennedy, including the **Strategic Hamlets** programme. Under Johnson, the **Gulf of Tonkin** (1964), mass bombing, **Search and Destroy**, **My Lai** and the **Tet Offensive** (1968) turned opinion against the war.

**Ending the conflict.** Nixon's **Vietnamisation**, chemical warfare, bombing (1970-72), opening to **China** and widening of the war into **Laos and Cambodia**; protest at **Kent State** (1970); the media's influence; the context of **Watergate**; the **Paris Peace** talks and **Kissinger**; US withdrawal (1973) and the **fall of Saigon** (1975).

## BE Conflict and tension in the Gulf and Afghanistan, 1990-2009

**Tensions in the Gulf.** The **Iran-Iraq war** (1980-88) left Iraq indebted; Western and Russian interests centred on **oil**; the **Israeli-Palestinian conflict** fuelled tension and terrorist motives. Iraq's invasion of **Kuwait** (August 1990) led **Bush** and **Thatcher** to back a **UN** coalition that expelled Iraq in early 1991, increasing US influence in the region.

**The war on Al-Qaeda.** **Osama bin Laden**'s Al-Qaeda carried out the **11 September 2001** attacks. Afghanistan under the **Taliban** was seen as a rogue state sheltering it. **Bush**'s war on terror, supported by **Blair**, overthrew the Taliban in 2001; a **UN conference** set up a new government under **Karzai**, which faced ethnic divisions and a Taliban **resurgence**.

**The Iraq War.** **Saddam Hussein**'s regime persecuted **Kurds** and **Shia** Muslims; debate raged about links to Al-Qaeda and **weapons of mass destruction**, which **IAEA** and UN inspectors were sent to find. The US-led **invasion of 2003** toppled Saddam amid international opposition and debate over Western interests, including oil.

## Worked example: a question 2 (usefulness) plan

*How useful are Sources B and C to an historian studying the Cuban Missile Crisis?* **[12]**

For each source: **content** (what it shows -- for example, an invented practice source: a US cartoon showing Kennedy and Khrushchev arm-wrestling over nuclear buttons), **provenance** (who made it, when, why -- a Western cartoonist in October 1962, writing for a public afraid of war), and **context** (your own knowledge -- the blockade, the secret deal over Turkey). Useful answers show what each source reveals *because of* who made it, not just despite it.

## Common mistakes

- Question 1: describing the source without explaining *how you know*.
- Question 2: generic comments ("it is biased") with no reference to the specific provenance.
- Question 3: a story without explaining how one event led to another.
- Question 4: no judgement, or forgetting SPaG.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should Section B take?** About an hour; give question 4 around 20-25 minutes.

**Can I use knowledge from the period study?** Only where it is relevant to the depth study question; answers must focus on international conflict.

**Is Section B the same in every year?** The question types are fixed; the focus of each question can come from any of the three parts.
