Condensed for quick recall of how the subject is assessed. For the full subject overview, use the
[IB MYP Individuals and Societies subject overview](/resources/ib-myp-individuals-and-societies-subject-guide/).

## No SL/HL split — four criteria instead

| Criterion | Focus |
|---|---|
| A — Knowing and understanding | Recall and conceptual understanding |
| B — Investigating | Research and inquiry skills |
| C — Communicating | Structuring and presenting a response |
| D — Thinking critically | Analysis, evaluation, forming judgements |

Each scored on an **8-point scale (1-8)**, equally weighted. Most assessment happens through
**classroom-based coursework**, not a single exam.

## If your school offers MYP eAssessment (history, geography or integrated humanities only)

A 2-hour on-screen exam, three tasks, **weighted by marks, not equally**:

| Task | Criteria combined | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| Investigating | A + B | 26 |
| Communicating | A + C | 18 |
| Thinking critically (extended writing) | A + C + D | 36 |

The thinking-critically task is the **largest single component** — always requires working from
source material, not recalling facts in isolation.

## Exam traps

- Assuming eAssessment is available in every I&S discipline — it's only offered in history, geography and integrated humanities.
- Underpreparing the thinking-critically task, since it's worth the most marks (36) of the three.
- Recalling facts in isolation instead of working from and referencing the provided source material, which every task requires.

## Self-test

1. What are the four MYP Individuals and Societies criteria?
2. In which three disciplines is MYP eAssessment available for this subject group?
3. Which eAssessment task is worth the most marks?
4. What kind of material must every eAssessment task engage with?

**Answers:** 1. Knowing and understanding; Investigating; Communicating; Thinking critically. 2. History, geography and integrated humanities. 3. The thinking-critically task (36 marks). 4. Source material — no task simply asks for facts recalled in isolation.
