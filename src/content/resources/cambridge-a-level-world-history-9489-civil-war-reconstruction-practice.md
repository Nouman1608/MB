## Section A

**1.** Identify two economic differences between North and South that affected their attitudes to federal policy before 1860. **[2]**

**2.** State three terms of the Compromise of 1850. **[3]**

**3.** Explain why the Lecompton Constitution damaged the Democratic Party. **[3]**

**4.** Identify three reasons why the Whig Party declined in the 1850s. **[3]**

**5.** State two ways in which the Confederacy's management of resources weakened its war effort. **[2]**

**6.** Give three reasons why Lincoln issued the Emancipation Proclamation. **[3]**

**7.** Explain two ways in which Andrew Johnson's policies angered Congress in 1865-66. **[4]**

## Section B

**8.** Explain why the Mexican War of 1846-48 increased tension between the North and the South. **[10]**

**9.** "Military leadership was the main reason why the Civil War lasted four years." How far do you agree? **[20]**

**10.** Read the two invented sources below. Compare Sources A and B as evidence about the election of 1860.

*Source A (invented)* -- From an editorial in a Charleston newspaper, November 1860. "A sectional party has elected a sectional President, without one vote from the South. He calls slavery a wrong and promises that it shall grow no further. The North has declared that we are a minority to be governed, not partners to be respected. South Carolina must act alone if need be."

*Source B (invented)* -- From a letter by a Republican voter in Ohio, November 1860. "We have won fairly and lawfully. Mr Lincoln has said a hundred times that he will not interfere with slavery in the States. All we ask is that the territories be kept free for free men. If the South will not accept an election it has lost, then it does not accept the Union at all." **[15]**

**11.** Read the two further invented sources below, together with Sources A and B in Question 10. How far do Sources A to D support the view that the defence of slavery was the main reason for secession?

*Source C (invented)* -- From a declaration adopted by a Deep South state convention, January 1861. "Our institutions rest upon the labour of the African race. The non-slaveholding States have denounced that institution as sinful, encouraged our slaves to flee, and now elected a President pledged to its ultimate extinction. We therefore resume the powers we delegated to the federal government."

*Source D (invented)* -- From a speech by a Virginia delegate, April 1861, after Lincoln's call for volunteers. "I voted against secession in February, and I would vote so again if the question were only one of the territories. But I will not raise my hand against a sister State. Mr Lincoln asks Virginia to supply men to coerce the South. Rather than that, we go." **[25]**

**12.** Explain why Reconstruction came to an end in 1877. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two, 1 mark each: the North was industrialising and wanted protective tariffs, while the cotton-exporting South saw tariffs as raising the price of goods it bought (1); the North relied on free wage labour and immigration, the South on enslaved labour (1); Northern interests wanted federal spending on internal improvements such as roads and canals, which many Southerners opposed (1). **[2]**

**2.** Any three, 1 mark each: California admitted as a free state (1); Utah and New Mexico territories to decide slavery by popular sovereignty (1); the slave trade (not slavery) abolished in the District of Columbia (1); a stronger Fugitive Slave Act (1); Texas's boundary settled with compensation for its debt (1). **[3]**

**3.** 1 mark each: President Buchanan backed admitting Kansas under the pro-slavery Lecompton Constitution (1); Stephen Douglas opposed it as a violation of popular sovereignty, because most Kansas settlers were against slavery (1); the dispute set Northern and Southern Democrats against each other, preparing the party's split in 1860 (1). **[3]**

**4.** Any three, 1 mark each: Northern and Southern Whigs disagreed over slavery and the Compromise of 1850 (1); the Kansas-Nebraska Act split the party's remaining members by section (1); the death of leaders such as Clay and Webster in 1852 (1); heavy defeat in the 1852 presidential election (1); many members moved to the Know-Nothings or the new Republican Party (1). **[3]**

**5.** Any two, 1 mark each: printing money caused severe inflation (1); the blockade cut cotton exports and imports of manufactured goods (1); impressment of food and slaves angered farmers and planters (1); state governors resisted central control over troops and supplies (1); exemptions from conscription for large slaveholders caused resentment among poorer whites (1). **[2]**

**6.** Any three, 1 mark each: to weaken the Confederacy by undermining its labour force (1); to allow Black men to serve in the Union forces (1); to make the war a moral cause, discouraging British and French intervention (1); to respond to Radical Republican and abolitionist pressure (1); Antietam (September 1862) gave him a victory from which to announce it (1). **[3]**

**7.** Two ways, 2 marks each: he pardoned large numbers of former Confederates, who were then elected to Southern governments and to Congress (2); he accepted Southern governments that passed Black Codes restricting freedpeople (2); he vetoed the Freedmen's Bureau extension and the Civil Rights Act of 1866, which Congress overrode (2); he campaigned against the 14th Amendment in 1866 (2). **[4]**

**8.** Indicative content. **New territory:** victory brought California, New Mexico and other lands, forcing the question of whether slavery would extend into them. **The Wilmot Proviso:** the attempt to ban slavery from the conquered lands passed the House on Northern votes and failed in the Senate, showing that the parties were splitting along sectional lines. **Suspicion of motives:** many Northerners saw the war, begun under the slaveholding President Polk, as a plot to expand slave territory; Southerners saw opposition to it as an attack on their rights. **Free Soil:** the Free Soil Party (1848) organised Northern voters who opposed expansion of slavery. **California:** the gold rush and California's request to enter as a free state threatened the Senate balance and led to the crisis settled in 1850. **Linking:** the land itself was not the problem -- the question of slavery in it was, and the Proviso turned that question into a sectional contest in Congress. **[10]**

**9.** Indicative content. **For:** Lee and Jackson repeatedly defeated larger Union armies in Virginia (Second Bull Run, Fredericksburg, Chancellorsville); McClellan's caution wasted Union advantages in 1862, and Lincoln changed commanders several times; the war turned once Grant and Sherman took charge in 1864. **Against:** the Confederacy's defensive position meant the Union had to conquer a huge territory; the Anaconda Plan was slow by design; the North took time to mobilise its population and industry; political leadership mattered -- Lincoln holding the Border States and Davis's disputes with the governors; the Emancipation Proclamation and Black enlistment strengthened the Union only from 1863; foreign recognition remained a Confederate hope until 1863. **Judgement:** a strong answer argues, for example, that the size of the task and the defensive advantage explain why the war was long, while leadership explains its timing -- why it did not end in 1862 and why it did end in 1865. **[20]**

**10.** Indicative content. **Similarities:** both recognise that Lincoln won without Southern support; both note that his policy was to stop the expansion of slavery. **Differences:** A sees the result as sectional domination and calls for South Carolina to act alone; B insists the election was lawful and that Lincoln would not touch slavery where it existed. A expects permanent minority status; B sees refusal to accept the result as rejection of the Union. **Explaining:** A comes from Charleston, centre of secessionist feeling, written as South Carolina prepared its December convention; B is a Northern Republican, celebrating victory and echoing Lincoln's public assurances. Knowledge: Lincoln won without any Southern electoral votes and was not even on the ballot in most Southern states; South Carolina seceded first, on 20 December 1860. **[15]**

**11.** Indicative content. **Support:** C openly states that the state's institutions rest on slavery and cites Northern hostility to it; A links secession to Lincoln's view of slavery as a wrong. Knowledge: the declarations of causes issued by several Deep South states stressed slavery; the Confederate constitution protected it. **Challenge:** D shows that Virginia seceded not to protect slavery in the territories but because of Lincoln's call to coerce the South -- states' rights and loyalty to the South; A also speaks of being "a minority to be governed", suggesting political power and honour; B suggests Southerners rejected democratic defeat itself. Knowledge: the Upper South rejected secession in early 1861 and left only after Fort Sumter. **Provenance:** C is an official justification; D is a speech meant to persuade a convention. **Judgement:** strong answers distinguish the two stages: for the Deep South, slavery was the central cause; for the Upper South, the trigger was coercion, though slavery underlay their sense of a common Southern interest. **[25]**

**12.** Indicative content. **Northern fatigue:** after a decade, many Northerners wanted to move on; the Panic of 1873 turned attention to the economy, and Democrats won the House in 1874. **Republican divisions:** the Liberal Republicans of 1872 attacked corruption in Grant's administration and favoured reconciliation with the white South; the Amnesty Act restored rights to most former Confederates. **Southern resistance:** violence by groups such as the White League and the Mississippi "plan" of 1875 overthrew Republican governments, and the government did not intervene. **The courts:** Supreme Court rulings such as the Slaughterhouse Cases (1873) narrowed the 14th Amendment. **The disputed election of 1876:** contested returns from Florida, Louisiana and South Carolina gave Democrats the bargaining power to secure the withdrawal of federal troops in return for accepting Hayes. **Linking:** loss of Northern will made Southern violence effective, and the 1876 deadlock turned that into a formal end. **[10]**
