**This guide covers content area 3.1.2.2 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), Shahadah: "the meaning, importance, and use of the Shahadah in a Muslim's life".** The prescribed passage is Qur'an 3:18-19. The Shahadah is the first of the Five Pillars. It is short, but a good answer needs three things: what the words mean, why they matter, and where Muslims use them. This guide is built around those three questions.

## The words and their meaning

The Shahadah ("testimony" or "witnessing") is usually said in Arabic:

> *Ashhadu an la ilaha illa Allah, wa ashhadu anna Muhammadan rasul Allah.*
> "I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, and I bear witness that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah."

It is sometimes called the *Kalimah* ("the word") or the *Kalimah Tayyibah* (the good word). It has two halves.

### Part 1: "There is no god but Allah"

This is the statement of **Tawhid**. It has two steps. *La ilaha* ("no god") rejects every false object of worship: idols, power, wealth, even one's own desires. *Illa Allah* ("but Allah") affirms that the One God alone is worshipped. The sentence is a negation followed by an affirmation, so that the heart is first cleared of false gods and then filled with faith in Allah. Compare Qur'an 112, which says that nothing is equal to Him.

### Part 2: "Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah"

This is the statement of **Risalah** (messengership). It means accepting that Muhammad (pbuh) is Allah's final messenger, that the Qur'an came through him, and that Muslims follow his Sunnah as the model of how to live. The Qur'an says, "Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah" (48:29).

Both halves are needed. Believing in Allah without accepting His messenger leaves a person without knowing how to worship; accepting a messenger without Tawhid would misplace worship. A person who says the Shahadah sincerely is therefore claiming to know who to worship and how.

## What "witnessing" involves

To "bear witness" is more serious than to say something. It means saying what one knows to be true. Scholars teach that the first half of the Shahadah involves conditions in the person who says it:

| Condition | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Knowledge (*ilm*) | Understanding what the words mean |
| Certainty (*yaqin*) | No doubt in the heart |
| Acceptance (*qabul*) | Accepting what the words require |
| Submission (*inqiyad*) | Obeying Allah in practice |
| Truthfulness (*sidq*) | Saying it honestly |
| Sincerity (*ikhlas*) | Doing it for Allah alone |
| Love (*mahabbah*) | Loving Allah and what pleases Him |

You do not have to know all seven by name, but they explain why the Shahadah is more than a formula. It is a commitment shown through life.

## Why the Shahadah is important

1. **It is the first pillar and the doorway to Islam.** A person becomes Muslim by sincerely declaring it. The other four pillars depend on it, because prayer, fasting, charity and pilgrimage are offered to Allah in the manner taught by His messenger.
2. **It sums up the faith.** It contains the key beliefs of Tawhid and Risalah, and the other key beliefs follow from them.
3. **It gives identity.** It marks a Muslim as someone who has chosen to worship One God and follow His final messenger.
4. **It is the highest branch of faith.** The Prophet (pbuh) said that faith has over seventy branches, the highest being the statement "La ilaha illa Allah" (Sahih Muslim).
5. **It brings hope.** The Prophet (pbuh) is reported to have said that whoever's last words are "La ilaha illa Allah" will enter Paradise (Abu Dawud).
6. **It unites all Muslims.** Whatever their school, language or country, Muslims share this declaration.

## How the Shahadah is used in a Muslim's life

The Shahadah is not said once and forgotten. It runs through Muslim life.

| Situation | How it appears |
|---|---|
| **Becoming Muslim** | A person says it sincerely, usually in front of witnesses in a mosque or a gathering. No special priest or ritual is required, though the community often welcomes the new Muslim. |
| **Birth** | The call to prayer, which contains the Shahadah, is often whispered into a newborn's ear, so that the first words heard are the declaration of faith. |
| **Daily prayer** | The Azaan and Iqamah include it. In each prayer, the Tashahhud, the sitting recitation, includes it. A Muslim says it at least five times a day in prayer. |
| **Friday sermon** | The khutbah normally begins with it. |
| **Times of trouble** | Many Muslims repeat it in fear or hardship as a statement of trust. |
| **Dying** | Family encourage the dying person to say it, so that their last words are Tawhid. |
| **Funeral** | The Janazah prayer and the burial take place in the setting of this belief. |

**Differences between Muslims.** Shi'a Muslims often add a line in the Azaan and sometimes in the prayer declaring Ali (RA) as the friend (*wali*) of Allah. Many Shi'a scholars say this addition is not part of the Shahadah itself. Sunni Muslims do not add it. All agree on the two core statements.

## Living the Shahadah

Saying the words is the start; living by them is the proof. A few everyday examples show what "bearing witness" can mean in practice.

- **Honesty at work:** a Muslim who refuses to cheat a customer, even when it would be easy, acts as someone who believes Allah alone is Lord and sees every action.
- **Fear of people:** a Muslim student who stays polite and firm when pressured to join in something harmful shows that no one else has authority over them in matters of right and wrong.
- **Following the Sunnah:** a Muslim who greets neighbours kindly, visits the sick and speaks gently follows the example of the messenger they have declared.
- **Gratitude:** a Muslim who thanks Allah after a success, and not only their own effort, shows the first half in action.

The Qur'an links the declaration with deeds: faith is joined with "righteous deeds" again and again. This is why teachers say that the Shahadah is the seed and the other pillars and good character are its growth.

## The prescribed passage: Qur'an 3:18-19

> "Allah witnesses that there is no deity except Him, and [so do] the angels and those of knowledge – [that He is] maintaining [creation] in justice. There is no deity except Him, the Exalted in Might, the Wise. Indeed, the religion in the sight of Allah is Islam. And those who were given the Scripture did not differ except after knowledge had come to them – out of jealous animosity between themselves. And whoever disbelieves in the verses of Allah, then indeed, Allah is swift in [taking] account."

| Words | What they teach |
|---|---|
| "Allah witnesses that there is no deity except Him" | Allah Himself gives the testimony, so Tawhid is certain |
| "the angels and those of knowledge" | Others join in the witness: angels and people of knowledge |
| "maintaining [creation] in justice" | Tawhid is linked to justice and order in the world |
| "the Exalted in Might, the Wise" | The names of Allah behind the declaration |
| "the religion in the sight of Allah is Islam" | The Shahadah leads into submission to Allah |
| "did not differ except after knowledge had come to them" | Differences came from jealousy, not lack of proof |
| "swift in [taking] account" | A reminder that the declaration has consequences |

**How to use it:** the verses show that the statement "there is no deity except Him" is not only a human claim; it is made by Allah, the angels and the people of knowledge. This gives the Shahadah great weight.

## Worked example: a 4-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** reasons why the Shahadah is important to Muslims. **[4]**

> The Shahadah is important because it is the way a person becomes a Muslim [1]. Without sincerely declaring belief in Allah and in Muhammad as His messenger, the other pillars would not be valid acts of worship [1]. It is also important because it expresses the central belief of Islam, Tawhid [1], so every time Muslims say it in prayer they are renewing their commitment to worship Allah alone [1].

## Worked example: a 6-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** ways in which the Shahadah is used in the life of a Muslim. Apply **one** teaching from a source of authority. **[6]**

> The Shahadah is part of the Azaan and the prayer, so a Muslim says or hears it several times a day [1]; this keeps Tawhid always in mind and reminds them of their identity [1]. It is also said at important moments in life, such as whispered to a newborn or encouraged for a dying person [1], so a Muslim's first and last words can be words of faith [1]. The Qur'an says, "Allah witnesses that there is no deity except Him, and [so do] the angels and those of knowledge" [2], which shows that when Muslims repeat the Shahadah they join a witness that Allah and the angels have also made.

## Common mistakes

- **Giving only the words.** Add meaning, importance and use.
- **Missing the second half.** Both Tawhid and Risalah are required.
- **Saying the Shahadah is just a ritual formula.** Scholars teach that it needs understanding and sincerity.
- **Saying a priest or special ceremony is needed.** It is made sincerely, ideally with witnesses.
- **Forgetting the passage.** Quote "Allah witnesses that there is no deity except Him" and "the religion in the sight of Allah is Islam".
