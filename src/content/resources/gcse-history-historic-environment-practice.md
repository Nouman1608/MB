## Section A

**1.** Choose one feature of your specified historic site (a building, a layout, an artefact, or its location). Describe the feature in your own words. **[4]**

**2.** Explain what this feature reveals about the wider historical period your depth study covers. **[8]**

## Section B

**3.** "The most important thing a historic site can show us is how ordinary people lived." How far would you agree, using your specified site as evidence? **[16]**

---

## Answers (worked approach, since Section A depends on your specified site)

**1.** A strong answer describes the feature with **specific physical detail** (size, materials, layout, decoration, location relative to the rest of the site) rather than a vague general impression. Precision here sets up the explanation in Question 2.

**2.** Use the Feature → Context → Link → Significance structure: state the feature [1], explain what was happening in the wider historical period [2], explicitly connect the two — does the feature *reflect*, *result from*, or *enable* something about that period? [3], and close with why this connection matters to understanding the period [2]. Marks are lost by describing the feature and the period separately without making the link explicit.

**3.** A top-band answer takes a clear position (agree, partly agree, or disagree) [2] and supports it with **specific evidence from the specified site** — features that do (or don't) reveal information about ordinary people's lives, contrasted with what the site reveals about other things (power, religion, defence, trade) [8]. The strongest answers weigh the site's evidence about ordinary life against its evidence about other historical themes, reaching a **substantiated judgement** rather than simply listing site features [6].

---

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a site feature without connecting it to the historical period.
- Treating the Historic Environment element as disconnected from the rest of the depth study it's drawn from.
- Judgement questions answered as description only, with no clear "how far do you agree" position taken and sustained.
- Using out-of-date knowledge of which sites are specified for the current examination series.
