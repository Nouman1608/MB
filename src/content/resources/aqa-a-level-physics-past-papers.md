This page is a guide to AQA A Level Physics (7408) past papers from June 2024 to June 2026. For each series you get the grade thresholds, copied from AQA's grade-boundary documents, and, where AQA has published them, a short account of what the examiners saw, written by us from AQA's reports on the examination. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on AQA's own site, linked below.

## Where to get the official papers

- [AQA A-level Physics (7408) subject page](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/physics/a-level/physics-7408)
- [AQA A-level Physics (7408) assessment resources](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/physics/a-level/physics-7408/assessment-resources), where question papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination are listed by series and paper
- [AQA A-level Physics (7408) specification at a glance](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/physics/a-level/physics-7408/specification/specification-at-a-glance)
- [AQA grade boundaries](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries) and the [grade-boundary archive](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries/archive)

Use the three documents together. Sit the paper first, under timed conditions. Mark it strictly with the mark scheme, which shows what each mark needs. Then read the report on the examination for that paper: it explains why answers that looked reasonable still lost marks. Our notes for each series below are a quick way into that report.

Check which specification a paper was set on. Every paper on this page was set on the 7408 specification, first taught in September 2015 and first examined in June 2017, so older papers cover the same content. The qualification is linear with three two-hour papers. Paper 1 (85 marks) covers the first part of the content, Paper 2 (85 marks) covers the later sections and assumes the earlier ones, and Paper 3 (80 marks) has a compulsory practical skills and data analysis section (Section A, 45 marks) plus one optional topic (Section B, 35 marks): Astrophysics, Medical physics, Engineering physics, Turning points in physics or Electronics. The total is 250 marks.

## June 2025

The reports describe the Paper 3 Section A as about a mark easier than in 2024, the Medical physics option as harder than in 2024, and Paper 2 as a paper where candidates coped well with contextual questions but found other questions harder.

**Where marks were lost: Paper 1 (7408/1)**

- Particle physics: labelling quark types, naming the wrong exchange particle, and vague statements about why a nucleus is unstable or how electron capture works.
- Waves: standing waves were confused with progressive waves, the term antiphase was not used correctly, candidates called the two waves coherent, and some described the direction of travel rather than energy transfer.
- Simple harmonic motion: the graph of acceleration against displacement was often drawn as a sine curve or with the wrong slope, and the following part could not be done without it.
- Materials: breaking stress and the Young modulus were treated as the same quantity, and some candidates did not show evidence for a comparison between wires.
- Circuits: combining cells in series and parallel, and forgetting to take the reciprocal when finding resistance.
- Mechanics: impulse treated as force, an extra centripetal force drawn on a car, a horizontal component wrongly given to weight on a banked track, and power-of-ten errors from millisecond scales.

**Where marks were lost: Paper 2 (7408/2)**

- Gases: kinetic-theory assumptions were stated without precision, and "using kinetic theory" was not followed. Candidates named the changing quantities but did not say which quantities stayed constant.
- Radioactivity: the energy-mass equation was applied to the whole mass of a particle, and the number of nuclei was confused with the nucleon number.
- Gravitation: forgetting to add the planet's radius, mishandling the cube and the root, and unit conversions.
- Capacitors: the definition of the dielectric constant and sketching a current-time graph for charging were weak.
- Fields: the vector nature of an electric field was a weakness, and few candidates gave the correct direction of a magnetic field to make an ion move in a circle.
- Explicit comparisons were often missing in answers that needed a conclusion drawn from two values.

**Where marks were lost: Paper 3 Section A (7408/3A)**

- Candidates truncated final answers or added an unnecessary significant figure on the answer line, and the examiners judged the answer line.
- Practical-procedure explanations were weaker for candidates who seemed to have limited hands-on experience.
- Disorganised working spread across the answer space made algebra fall apart; correct working was needed with the command words Deduce and Determine.

**Where marks were lost: Paper 3 Section B options**

- Astrophysics: black-body curves drawn too symmetrically, recognising a Sun-like star's absolute magnitude, vague answers about detection, rays drawn without a ruler, and "explain" answers that listed benefits without a reason.
- Medical physics: refraction at the cornea was often missing from ray diagrams, a multi-step lens calculation was hard, and hypermetropia was confused with myopia.
- Engineering physics: sign conventions, the distance from the axis for a braking force, the fact that there were two brake pads, and rotational as well as translational kinetic energy.
- Turning points in physics: the thermionic emission set-up was unfamiliar to a minority, and candidates used data-book values for the electron's charge and mass in a part where the question ruled them out.

## June 2024

The reports describe Paper 3 Section A as more accessible than 2023, and the Medical physics option as more accessible than the year before with a clear rise in the mean mark.

**Where marks were lost: Paper 1 (7408/1)**

- Writing electrostatic where electromagnetic was needed, and weak understanding of virtual photons as exchange particles.
- Classification tables where a particle can belong to two groups, and answers that mixed weak and strong interactions rather than applying the conservation rule to the situation.
- Diffraction-grating work: giving the slit separation instead of the number of lines per metre, and confusing accuracy with precision.
- Using the combined energy as the energy of one particle in a collision.
- Conversion of a temperature to kelvin, errors with natural logarithms, and units.
- Describing how to connect ammeters and voltmeters in a circuit.

**Where marks were lost: Paper 2 (7408/2)**

- Kinetic theory: which mass to use in the pressure equation, and forgetting that a change in temperature needed the starting value subtracted.
- Capacitor graphs: the initial gradient was not specified, and a time constant was found from a given resistance when the graph was needed.
- Magnetic flux: a question that depended on the geometry of the situation was very challenging, with a missing cosine term and the wrong area among the common errors.

**Where marks were lost: Paper 3 Section A (7408/3A)**

- Resistivity: using a given value instead of the candidate's own result, and carrying a power-of-ten error forward.
- Uncertainties: error bars were not used when drawing lines of maximum and minimum gradient, and the metre ruler was treated as a datum rather than a measuring instrument.
- Deducing base units for a constant was achieved by only a small share of candidates.
- Rounding during a multi-part calculation and answers with too many significant figures.

**Where marks were lost: Paper 3 Section B options**

- Astrophysics: telescope magnification errors, diffraction confused with refraction, the surface area of a sphere and the fourth power of temperature, and quasar formation mixed up with supernovae.
- Medical physics: intensity and intensity level were confused, answers on hearing loss were vague, and many thought total internal reflection needs a cladding.
- Engineering physics: conservation of angular momentum answers did not give the opposite direction of rotation, and a torque argument needed all peaks compared.
- Turning points in physics: descriptions of the oil-drop experiment lacked detail, and percentage uncertainties were subtracted rather than added.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find AQA A Level Physics 7408 past papers?**
AQA lists question papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination on the 7408 assessment-resources page. Grade boundaries are on AQA's grade-boundaries page and its archive.

**Which papers do I sit for a full practice run?**
All three: Paper 1, Paper 2 and Paper 3. In Paper 3 you answer Section A and the one Section B option your course teaches.

**Why are there five different subject rows in the thresholds?**
AQA gives a subject boundary for each Paper 3 option, because the choice of option affects the total mark out of 250. Use the row for your option.

**Are the paper-level grade thresholds official grades?**
No. AQA states that paper-level boundaries are notional and for illustration only. The grade comes from the total mark and the subject boundary for your option.

**Can grade thresholds tell me what mark I need next time?**
No. Thresholds change every series and should only be used as a rough guide for the series they belong to, never to predict future ones.
