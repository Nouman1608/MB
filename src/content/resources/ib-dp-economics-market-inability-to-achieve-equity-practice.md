This set covers **sub-topic 2.12, the market's inability to achieve equity**, which is **HL only**, so every question is HL. It follows the IB Diploma Programme *Economics guide* (first assessment 2022, published February 2020), the course examined from the 2022 session onwards. Read the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-economics-market-inability-to-achieve-equity/) and the [revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-economics-market-inability-to-achieve-equity-revision-notes/) first, and keep the [IB DP Economics hub](/boards/ib/ib-dp/economics/) and the [printable checklist](/checklists/ib/ib-dp/economics/) to hand. Brannoch is an invented country and its figures are illustrative. Use a calculator only where a question allows one; the guide says Paper 1 is calculator-free.

## Questions

**1.** (HL only) Distinguish between income and wealth. **[2]**

**2.** (HL only) State the four factors of production and the factor income that each earns. **[4]**

**3.** (HL only) Draw a labelled circular flow diagram for a free market economy, and use it to show where inequality in income arises. **[4]**

**4.** (HL only) Brannoch is a free market economy with five equal-sized households. Their yearly incomes in bran, an invented currency, are: A 18, B 22, C 30, D 50, E 130.

**(a)** Calculate household E's share of total income. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the combined share of households A and B. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate how many times household E's income is larger than household A's, to one decimal place. **[2]**

**5.** (HL only) In Brannoch, property income (rent, interest and profit) received by households A to E is 0, 0, 4, 10 and 46 bran.

**(a)** Calculate household E's share of all property income, to one decimal place. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of household E's own income (from question 4) that is property income, to one decimal place. **[2]**

**6.** (HL only, calculator allowed) In Brannoch, the market for cleaning staff has demand Qd = 800 - 20w and supply Qs = 60w. The market for specialist surgeons has demand Qd = 460 - 3w and supply Qs = 2w. Here w is the hourly wage in bran and Q is hours in thousands.

**(a)** Find the equilibrium wage and quantity for cleaning staff. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the equilibrium wage and quantity for surgeons. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain, with reference to the factor market, why the two wages differ so much. **[3]**

**7.** (HL only, calculator allowed) Household A in Brannoch owns wealth of 600 bran, which earns a 4% return a year. It has 100 bran of other income a year and saves 15% of its total income each year, adding the saving to its wealth at the end of the year. Household B owns wealth of 50 bran, has 30 bran of other income, the same return and the same saving rate.

**(a)** Calculate A's wealth at the start of year 3. Give your answer to two decimal places. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate B's wealth at the start of year 3 to two decimal places. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the gap between A's and B's incomes in year 3 to one decimal place. **[2]**

**8.** (HL only) Explain how unequal ownership of capital leads to unequal incomes in a free market. **[4]**

**9.** (HL only) Explain why a household that can sell only low-priced labour receives a low income in a free market. **[4]**

**10.** (HL only) Distinguish between equality and equity. **[3]**

**11.** (HL only) Explain, using a circular flow diagram, why a free market economy may result in an unequal distribution of income and wealth. **[10]**

**12.** (HL only, data response) Brannoch has 100 households. The table shows total yearly income, in millions of bran, by group.

| Group | Year 1 | Year 6 |
|---|---|---|
| Bottom 40 households | 600 | 640 |
| Middle 40 households | 1,500 | 1,640 |
| Top 20 households | 2,900 | 3,720 |

**(a)** Calculate the top group's share of total income in each year. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the average income per household in the top group divided by that in the bottom group, in each year, to one decimal place. **[3]**
**(c)** Analyse how unequal factor ownership could explain the change shown. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Income is a flow earned over a period, for example a year [1], whereas wealth is a stock, the value of assets held on one date [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A "distinguish" answer should make the difference explicit in one sentence rather than defining each term in separate sentences.

**2.** Labour earns wages [1], land earns rent [1], capital earns interest [1], enterprise earns profit [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Pair each factor with its income in the same line, so that the answer shows you know which return goes with which factor.

**3.** Households and firms drawn as two boxes [1]. A factor market and a product market named between them [1]. Arrows labelled for factor services, factor incomes, spending, and goods and services, with directions correct [1]. Wages, rent, interest and profit written on the factor income arrow, with a note that income differs because households own different factors at different prices [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Draw it in pencil with a ruler, label everything, and refer to the diagram in your text; a drawing with no labelled markets is hard to give credit to.

**4. (a)** Total income = 18 + 22 + 30 + 50 + 130 = 250 [1]. Share of E = 130 / 250 = **52%** [1].
**(b)** A + B = 18 + 22 = 40, and 40 / 250 = **16%** [1]; the working must show both the sum and the division [1].
**(c)** 130 / 18 = 7.2222 [1], which is **7.2 times** to one decimal place [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show the total before dividing, so that an error in the final figure still leaves evidence of correct method.

**5. (a)** Property income total = 0 + 0 + 4 + 10 + 46 = 60 [1]. E's share = 46 / 60 = 0.7667, so **76.7%** [1].
**(b)** E's property income as a share of E's income = 46 / 130 [1] = **35.4%** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Read the denominator carefully: part (a) divides by all property income, part (b) by the household's own income.

**6. (a)** 800 - 20w = 60w [1], so 80w = 800, **w = 10 bran** and Q = 60 x 10 = **600 thousand hours** [1].
**(b)** 460 - 3w = 2w [1], so 5w = 460, **w = 92 bran** and Q = 2 x 92 = **184 thousand hours** [1].
**(c)** The surgeon wage is 92 / 10 = 9.2 times the cleaning wage [1]. Surgeon supply is small because training is long and costly, while demand is strong, so the wage that clears the market is high [1]. Wages are factor prices set in the factor market, so households that sell different factors receive different incomes without any step in the loop evening them out [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Check each result back into both demand and supply (here 600 and 184); it takes ten seconds and catches arithmetic slips.

**7. (a)** Year 1: interest = 0.04 x 600 = 24, income = 124, saving = 0.15 x 124 = 18.6, wealth = 618.6 [1]. Year 2: income = 100 + 0.04 x 618.6 = 124.744, saving = 18.71 [1]. Wealth = 618.6 + 18.71 = **637.31 bran** [1].
**(b)** Year 1: income = 30 + 0.04 x 50 = 32, saving = 4.8, wealth = 54.8 [1]. Year 2: income = 32.192, saving = 4.83, so wealth = 54.8 + 4.83 = **59.63 bran** [1].
**(c)** Year 3 incomes: A = 100 + 0.04 x 637.31 = 125.49 and B = 30 + 0.04 x 59.63 = 32.39 [1]. Gap = 125.49 - 32.39 = **93.1 bran** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Keep the full values until the final step and round at the end, or the answer to part (c) can drift by a hundredth.

**8.** Capital earns interest or profit for its owners [1]. Households with a lot of capital receive a large property income on top of any wages [1]. Households with no capital have only labour income [1]. So where capital ownership is concentrated, income is more unequal than wages alone would be [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Finish with the effect on the distribution of income, not only the description of what capital does.

**9.** Factor prices are set by demand and supply in the factor market [1]. If many people offer similar low-skilled labour, supply is large relative to demand [1], so the equilibrium wage is low [1]. A household with no other factor to sell has no other income stream to add to that wage [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the wage to supply and demand explicitly; saying only "low-skilled workers earn less" restates the question.

**10.** Equality means shares of income or wealth are the same size [1]. Equity means fairness [1], which is a judgement, so an unequal outcome is not necessarily seen as inequitable, nor an equal one as fair [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A third mark may depend on showing that the two ideas can come apart, so add that sentence.

**11.** Indicative points only; treat this as a plan for your own answer, not a mark scheme.
- Circular flow drawn and fully labelled: households, firms, factor and product markets, four flows, four factor incomes [3].
- Households own different amounts of factors, so receive different factor incomes (wages only against wages, rent, interest and profit) [2].
- Factor prices come from demand and supply, so scarce and strongly demanded factors earn more [2].
- Wealth earns income and can be saved to build more wealth, so gaps persist and widen, with starting positions differing between households [2].
- A clearly labelled illustrative example, explained, and a point stating that the diagram contains no mechanism that reflects need [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* A good diagram earns more when your paragraphs keep pointing back to a named part of it, such as the factor market arrow.

**12. (a)** Year 1 total = 600 + 1,500 + 2,900 = 5,000, so top share = 2,900 / 5,000 = **58%** [1]. Year 6 total = 640 + 1,640 + 3,720 = 6,000, so top share = 3,720 / 6,000 = **62%** [1].
**(b)** Year 1: top average = 2,900 / 20 = 145 and bottom average = 600 / 40 = 15 [1], ratio = 9.7. Year 6: top average = 3,720 / 20 = 186 and bottom average = 640 / 40 = 16, ratio = 11.6 [1]. Answer: **9.7 in year 1 and 11.6 in year 6** [1].
**(c)** All groups gained, but the top group's income grew 28.3% against 6.7% for the bottom group [1]. If the top group owns most capital, land and businesses, it receives property income as well as wages, and that rises with the economy [1]. Wealth earns income that can be saved, so the gap widens even without any change in wage rates [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the data in the analysis, with a figure for each claim, rather than giving a generic explanation that could fit any table.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not naming the factor market on the diagram, or leaving off the four factor incomes.
- Adding a government to a free market diagram.
- Giving the result of a calculation without any working.
- Dividing by the wrong total, such as using the household's own income instead of total income.
- Treating a stock as a flow, or the reverse, when answering questions on wealth and income.
- Stating a cause with no effect on the distribution.
- Rounding too early in multi-year compounding questions.
