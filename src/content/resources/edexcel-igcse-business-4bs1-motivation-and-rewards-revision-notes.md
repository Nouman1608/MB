Quick recall for section 2.4 Motivation and rewards in Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Business (4BS1), Issue 2, February 2026, points 2.4.1 and 2.4.2. Applied examples and an annotated answer are in the [motivation and rewards study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-motivation-and-rewards/); questions are in the [motivation and rewards practice set](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-motivation-and-rewards-practice/). Figures below are invented, in dollars.

## 1. The specification list

- **2.4.1** Importance of motivation: attracting employees, retaining employees, productivity; theories of Herzberg, Maslow and Taylor.
- **2.4.2** Financial methods: remuneration, bonus, commission, promotion, fringe benefits. Non-financial methods: job rotation, job enrichment, autonomy.

## 2. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Motivation | The desire or drive to work hard and well |
| Productivity | Output per worker in a period |
| Staff turnover | The proportion of staff who leave in a period (standard form: leavers ÷ average staff × 100) |
| Remuneration | Payment for work, such as a wage or salary |
| Wage | Pay per hour or week; can vary with hours worked |
| Salary | A fixed yearly amount, usually paid monthly |
| Piece rate | Pay per item produced |
| Bonus | An extra payment for reaching a target |
| Commission | A percentage of the value of sales made |
| Promotion | Moving to a higher position with more pay and responsibility |
| Fringe benefits | Extras on top of pay, such as staff discount, health cover or a company car |
| Job rotation | Moving between tasks at a similar level |
| Job enrichment | Adding responsibility and more demanding tasks to a job |
| Autonomy | Freedom to decide how and when to do the work |
| Hygiene factor | Herzberg: a factor that causes dissatisfaction if poor but does not motivate if good |
| Motivator | Herzberg: a factor in the job itself that creates motivation |

The standard formula and definitions here are common teaching; the specification lists the terms without defining them.

## 3. Theories in one table

| | Taylor | Maslow | Herzberg |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core claim | Workers want money; pay for output and design efficient tasks | Needs form a hierarchy: physiological, safety, social, esteem, self-actualisation | Hygiene factors prevent dissatisfaction; motivators create satisfaction |
| Pay | The main motivator | Meets lower needs | A hygiene factor |
| Method it supports | Piece rate, commission, bonus | Different rewards for different levels | Job enrichment, recognition, responsibility |
| Weakness | Ignores social and esteem needs; quality may suffer | People do not always move in order | Some workers are motivated by money |

**Maslow ladder (bottom to top):** physiological → safety → social → esteem → self-actualisation.

**Herzberg lists:**
- Hygiene: pay, working conditions, job security, company policy, supervision, relations with colleagues.
- Motivators: achievement, recognition, responsibility, the work itself, advancement.

## 4. Methods matched to theory

| Method | Type | Theory link | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Remuneration (wage, salary, piece rate) | Financial | Taylor; Maslow lower levels | Fixed pay does not reward effort; piece rate can harm quality |
| Bonus | Financial | Taylor; Maslow esteem if public | Targets missed can demotivate |
| Commission | Financial | Taylor | Pressure selling; unstable income |
| Promotion | Financial | Maslow esteem; Herzberg advancement | Few posts available |
| Fringe benefits | Financial | Maslow safety (health cover, sick pay) | Only motivates those who value the benefit |
| Job rotation | Non-financial | Reduces boredom; Maslow social if team-based | Tasks still simple |
| Job enrichment | Non-financial | Herzberg motivators; Maslow higher levels | Needs training; some staff resist |
| Autonomy | Non-financial | Herzberg responsibility; Maslow self-actualisation | Mistakes; needs capable staff |

## 5. Worked reminders

**Time rate or piece rate.** A machinist works 38 hours at 11.50 an hour: 38 × 11.50 = 437 a week. On a piece rate of 0.90 per item, she must make 437 ÷ 0.90 = 485.6, so 486 items to earn at least as much. Making 520 items earns 520 × 0.90 = 468, which is 31 more.

**Commission plus bonus.** Basic pay is 1,500 a month, commission is 3% of sales and a bonus of 250 is paid when sales exceed 35,000. With sales of 40,000: commission = 0.03 × 40,000 = 1,200; total = 1,500 + 1,200 + 250 = **2,950**.

**Staff turnover.** A firm with 120 staff loses 15 in a year: 15 ÷ 120 × 100 = 12.5%. After introducing autonomy for team leaders, 9 leave: 7.5%. If each replacement costs 1,800, the saving is 6 × 1,800 = **10,800**.

## 6. Exam traps

- Describing a theory and stopping. Every theory point must lead to what the business should do.
- Writing "Herzberg says pay doesn't matter". Poor pay causes dissatisfaction.
- Placing esteem below social needs in Maslow.
- Confusing commission (percentage of sales) with a bonus (payment for a target).
- Confusing job rotation (different tasks) with job enrichment (bigger, more responsible job).
- Recommending a pay rise for a business the case shows is short of cash, without a reason it is affordable.
- Forgetting that the 4BS1 list of reasons is attracting, retaining and productivity: use those words.

## 7. Answer frames

- **Explain** one benefit of commission: commission is linked to sales made → staff try harder to sell → revenue rises for the business.
- **Analyse** using Maslow: identify the unmet level from the case → choose a method for that level → show the effect on retention or productivity.
- **Justify** choosing between two methods: cost of each, which need or factor each targets, why one fits this workforce better, decision.
- **Evaluate** a reward scheme: who it suits, its cost, risk to quality or teamwork, a judgement with "it depends on".

## Quick self-test

1. Give the three reasons the specification lists for why motivation matters.
2. Name the level directly above safety in Maslow's hierarchy.
3. Give two of Herzberg's hygiene factors.
4. What is the difference between job rotation and job enrichment?
5. Which theorist is most closely linked with piece rates?
6. A salesperson earns 5% commission on sales of 32,000. Calculate the commission.
7. A firm has 50 staff and 8 leave. Calculate staff turnover.
8. State one fringe benefit that meets safety needs.
9. Why might promotion demotivate some employees?
10. Give one limitation of autonomy.
11. Workers say pay is fair but they never hear when they do well. Which Herzberg category is missing?
12. Why can a fringe benefit be worth more to employees than it costs the business?

### Answers

1. Attracting employees, retaining employees, productivity.
2. Social (belonging) needs.
3. Any two of: pay, working conditions, job security, company policy, supervision, relations with colleagues.
4. Job rotation moves a worker between tasks of a similar level; job enrichment adds responsibility and more demanding tasks to the job itself.
5. Taylor.
6. 0.05 × 32,000 = 1,600.
7. 8 ÷ 50 × 100 = 16%.
8. Health cover or sick pay (or a pension).
9. Only a few posts are available, so those who are not promoted may feel overlooked.
10. Employees may make mistakes, or managers may lose some control over how work is done.
11. Motivators, specifically recognition.
12. The business can buy it at a lower group price or produce it cheaply, such as a staff discount, while the employee would pay the full price.
