## What quality means

**Quality:** meeting customers' expectations. A good-quality product or service is fit for purpose, free from faults, reliable and consistent, and matches what customers expect for the price.

Quality is relative to price: a cheap product can be good quality if it does what customers expect.

## Why quality matters

| Benefit of good quality | Cost of poor quality |
|---|---|
| Repeat customers and recommendations | Lost customers, who tell others |
| Strong brand; may support a higher price | Damaged reputation, poor online reviews |
| Less waste, rework and scrapping | Materials and labour wasted on rejects |
| Fewer refunds and complaints | Refunds, replacements, staff time on complaints |
| Advantage over competitors | Possible legal action if faulty goods harm customers |

## Quality control (QC) and quality assurance (QA)

| | Quality control | Quality assurance |
|---|---|---|
| Idea | Check products to find and remove faults | Organise every stage to prevent faults |
| When | After production, or at checkpoints | Throughout the process, from suppliers to delivery |
| Who is responsible | Inspectors or supervisors | Every employee |
| Typical methods | Inspecting finished items or a sample; testing; checking a finished room or report | Training; set procedures and checklists; choosing and checking suppliers; workers checking their own work |
| Advantages | Simple and quick to set up; stops faulty goods reaching customers; gives fault data; little change for staff | Fewer faults made, so less waste; consistent quality; staff involved and responsible; protects reputation |
| Disadvantages | Waste already made; inspectors add cost but not value; samples can miss faults; staff may leave quality to inspectors | Costly and slow to set up; needs all staff to cooperate; results take time; some checks still needed |

## Services

- Services are produced as the customer receives them, so faults cannot be removed before the customer sees them.
- Quality depends on staff, so training and procedures matter most.
- QA usually suits services better than QC.

## Measuring quality

```
Fault rate (%)     = faulty items / items checked x 100
Complaint rate (%) = complaints / customers x 100
```

Worked reminder: 18 faulty lamps in a sample of 450.

```
18 / 450 x 100 = 4%
```

Estimated faulty lamps in a week's output of 12,000: 4 / 100 x 12,000 = 480.

## Recommend and justify: quick guide

| Choose QC when... | Choose QA when... |
|---|---|
| The business is small with simple products | Faults are dangerous (food, medicine, toys, safety parts) |
| Faults are easy to see and cheap | Waste from rejects is costly |
| Budget and time for training are limited | It is a service delivered in front of the customer |
| A quick improvement is needed | The brand depends on reliability |

Always add a drawback of your choice and, where sensible, say some use of the other approach may still help.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Quality | Meeting customers' expectations: fit for purpose, free from faults, reliable |
| Quality control | Checking goods or services, usually at the end, to find and remove faults |
| Quality assurance | Organising the whole process so that faults are prevented at every stage |
| Defect | A fault in a product or service |
| Reject | An item that fails a quality check |
| Rework | Correcting a faulty item so it can be sold |

## Exam traps

- Quality is not the same as luxury or high price.
- QC finds faults; it does not prevent them.
- QA still uses some checks; it is not "no inspection".
- Compare fault rates as percentages when sample sizes differ.
- A recommendation must use the case: the product, its risks, the budget, the type of business.

## Quick self-test

1. Define quality.
2. Give two reasons why quality is important to a business.
3. Define quality control.
4. Define quality assurance.
5. Who is responsible for quality under quality assurance?
6. State one disadvantage of quality control.
7. State one disadvantage of quality assurance.
8. Why does quality assurance often suit service businesses?
9. A sample of 600 bottles has 15 faulty. Calculate the fault rate.
10. A café has 25 complaints from 2,000 customers. Calculate the complaint rate.
11. Supplier X: 4 faulty in 200. Supplier Y: 6 faulty in 400. Which has the lower fault rate?
12. Which approach would you recommend for a maker of car seat belts, and why?

### Answers

1. Meeting customers' expectations: fit for purpose, free from faults, reliable and consistent.
2. Any two: repeat customers; good reputation; less waste and lower costs; fewer refunds and complaints; advantage over competitors.
3. Checking goods or services, usually after production, to find and remove faults.
4. Organising every stage of production so that faults are prevented.
5. Every employee.
6. Any one: waste is already made; inspectors add cost; samples can miss faults; workers may take less care.
7. Any one: costly to set up; needs training and time; depends on all staff cooperating; results are slow.
8. The service is produced in front of the customer, so faults must be prevented rather than found afterwards.
9. 15 / 600 x 100 = 2.5%.
10. 25 / 2,000 x 100 = 1.25%.
11. X = 4 / 200 x 100 = 2%; Y = 6 / 400 x 100 = 1.5%. Supplier Y.
12. Quality assurance, because a faulty seat belt could injure people, so faults must be prevented at every stage; final testing may still be used as well.
