## The four content points at a glance

| Content point | Depth in the guide | One-line summary |
|---|---|---|
| Vision statement and mission statement | AO2 | Vision is the future picture; mission is present purpose |
| Common objectives: growth, profit, protecting shareholder value, ethical objectives | AO2 | What a business sets as targets |
| Strategic and tactical objectives | AO3 | Long-term direction versus shorter-term steps |
| Corporate social responsibility (CSR) | AO3 | Duties to society beyond making a profit |

Remember that AO3 means synthesis and evaluation: give a judgment, not just a list.

## Vision and mission

| | Vision statement | Mission statement |
|---|---|---|
| Looks at | The future | The present |
| Answers | Where do we want to be? | What do we do, for whom, and how? |
| Tone | Aspirational | Practical |
| Measurable? | Rarely | Sometimes, partly |

The guide does not give formal definitions, so these are the standard forms.

**Why they help:** shared direction for staff, a guide for decisions, a signal of values to customers and investors.

**Why they can fail:** too vague, copied from others, not followed in practice, written for show, and their effect is hard to measure.

**Must-know distinction:** a statement is not an objective. An objective is a specific target set to move toward the statement.

## Common objectives

| Objective | What it means | Typical measure | Main benefit | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Growth | Becoming larger | Sales revenue, market share, staff numbers, outlets | Economies of scale, more power in the market, spread of risk | Cash-flow pressure, loss of control, falling quality |
| Profit | Revenue exceeds costs | Net profit, net profit margin | Funds reinvestment and rewards owners | Short-termism, cutting corners |
| Protecting shareholder value | Keeping the worth of shares and dividends stable or rising | Share price, dividends, market capitalization | Keeps investors confident and the cost of raising funds down | Pressure to please investors at the expense of other stakeholders |
| Ethical objectives | Acting on moral principles beyond the legal minimum | Policies, audits, supplier standards | Trust, reputation, loyal staff and customers | Higher costs, clashes with other objectives |

### Formulas (standard forms)

| Measure | Formula |
|---|---|
| Percentage growth | (new − old) ÷ old × 100 |
| Net profit margin | net profit ÷ sales revenue × 100 |
| Return on holding a share for a year | (closing price − opening price + dividend) ÷ opening price × 100 |
| Market capitalization | number of shares × share price |

The guide does not give these formulas for 1.3. They are the usual textbook forms, so state which one you use and show the working.

### Method for a growth or profit calculation

1. Write the formula.
2. Substitute the figures from the stimulus, keeping units.
3. Calculate and give a sensible rounding, with a percent sign or currency.
4. Add one sentence saying what the figure means for the business.

**Small worked reminder.** A firm's sales rise from 80 to 92 (thousand units). Growth = (92 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100 = 15%. A net profit of 45 on revenue of 600 gives a margin of 45 ÷ 600 × 100 = 7.5%.

### Growth and profit can pull apart

- Growth bought with discounts raises sales but squeezes the margin.
- Profit pushed up by cost cuts may harm quality, so growth stalls later.
- A new business often accepts low profit to build a customer base.

## Strategic and tactical objectives

| | Strategic | Tactical |
|---|---|---|
| Time | Long term | Short to medium term |
| Scope | Whole organization | Department, product or project |
| Set by | Senior management, board | Middle managers |
| Role | Direction | Steps toward the direction |
| Flexibility | Reviewed rarely | Adjusted often |

**Key links:**
- Tactical objectives must support the strategic objective.
- If they do not, effort is spent without progress.
- Too much attention on short-run tactics can damage long-run strategy.
- If conditions change, strategic objectives may need revision. This links to the guide's inquiry statement about why objectives might change over time.

**Spotting the type in a question:** look for a time frame (five years versus this quarter), the unit it applies to (whole firm versus one team) and whether it states a destination or an action.

## CSR

**Definition (standard form):** the belief that a business has responsibilities to society and the environment, as well as to its owners, and takes those into account in its decisions.

| For | Against or limits |
|---|---|
| Better reputation and loyalty | Higher costs, lower short-term profit |
| Easier to attract and keep staff | Some owners prefer a focus on shareholder returns |
| Lower risk of scandal | Can be marketing with little real change |
| Supports long-term sustainability | Benefits are hard to measure and slow |
| May spur creativity (new materials, processes) | Different stakeholders want different things |

**Linking points for evaluation:**
- CSR links to ethical objectives: ethics is the principle, CSR is the broader approach.
- CSR can support or conflict with profit and shareholder value, depending on time frame.
- Name the stakeholder who gains or loses (see 1.4).
- The guide's inquiry statements ask about CSR and creativity and about ethical behavior and sustainability.

## Why objectives change

Objectives are not fixed. A business may revise them when:
- a new competitor or technology changes the market
- the economy slows, so growth targets become unrealistic
- ownership changes, for example when a private firm becomes publicly held and shareholder value gains weight
- stakeholders, such as customers or pressure groups, demand higher ethical or environmental standards
- the business matures and moves from a growth focus toward profit and steady dividends

In an evaluation answer, a sentence about how the objective may change over time, and what the business should do then, shows depth.

## Judging an evaluation answer

1. Make a point and tie it to the case.
2. Explain the effect with a link word (so, which means, therefore).
3. Give the other side or a limit.
4. Reach a judgment and say what it depends on, such as time frame, size of cost or the stakeholder's power.

## Quick self-test

1. State one difference between a vision statement and a mission statement.
2. Give two ways of measuring the growth of a business.
3. A firm has sales of USD 250,000 last year and USD 280,000 this year. Calculate the percentage growth.
4. A business made a net profit of USD 36,000 on sales revenue of USD 480,000. Calculate its net profit margin.
5. Why is protecting shareholder value more directly relevant to a publicly held company?
6. A share cost USD 20.00 a year ago and now costs USD 19.00. The dividend was USD 1.00. Calculate the return to the shareholder.
7. Classify as strategic or tactical: "Reduce delivery times by a day by the end of this month."
8. Classify as strategic or tactical: "Become the leading supplier in three countries within ten years."
9. Give one example of an ethical objective.
10. State two possible benefits and one possible drawback of CSR.

### Answers

1. A vision statement describes the future the organization wants; a mission statement describes what it does now, for whom and how.
2. Any two of: sales revenue, market share, number of employees, number of outlets, value of the business.
3. (280,000 − 250,000) ÷ 250,000 × 100 = **12%**.
4. 36,000 ÷ 480,000 × 100 = **7.5%**.
5. Its shares are traded openly, so a share price shows the market's view daily and shareholders can easily sell if unhappy. A private firm has no such open price.
6. (19.00 − 20.00 + 1.00) ÷ 20.00 × 100 = **0%**. The dividend exactly offsets the fall in price.
7. Tactical: short time frame, narrow scope.
8. Strategic: long time frame, whole-business direction.
9. For example, only buying from suppliers that treat their workers fairly, or refusing to advertise in a misleading way.
10. Benefits: stronger reputation, easier recruitment, lower scandal risk. Drawback: higher costs that may reduce short-term profit.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling a mission statement an objective, or swapping vision and mission.
- Saying "the objective is to make money" without naming profit, margin or growth and the measure.
- Showing a percentage with no working or no formula.
- Confusing the percentage change in profit with the profit margin.
- Describing strategic and tactical objectives in general terms without applying them to the business in the case.
- Treating CSR as only a cost, or only a benefit.
- Writing a list of points with no judgment at the end of an evaluation question.
- Leaving out the stakeholder: who gains, who loses.
- Ignoring the time frame when judging whether an objective is sensible.
