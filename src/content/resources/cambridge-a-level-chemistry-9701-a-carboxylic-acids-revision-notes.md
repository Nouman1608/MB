## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Carboxylate ion** | RCOO⁻, the anion left when a carboxylic acid loses H⁺ |
| **Inductive effect** | electron withdrawal (or donation) through σ bonds by an electronegative (or alkyl) group |
| **Acyl chloride** | RCOCl, made by replacing the –OH of a carboxylic acid with –Cl |
| **Further oxidation** | oxidation of a carboxylic acid beyond the –COOH stage, to CO₂ |
| **Self-indicating titration** | a titration in which a reactant's colour shows the end-point (MnO₄⁻) |

## Benzoic acid from an alkylbenzene

    C₆H₅CH₃ + 3[O] → C₆H₅COOH + H₂O      (hot alkaline KMnO₄, then dilute acid)

- Alkaline step gives C₆H₅COO⁻; **dilute acid** releases C₆H₅COOH (white solid).
- Purple → brown precipitate (MnO₂).
- Any alkyl side-chain → one –COOH.

## Acyl chlorides: three reagents

    3RCOOH + PCl₃ → 3RCOCl + H₃PO₃          heat
    RCOOH + PCl₅  → RCOCl + POCl₃ + HCl      room temperature, steamy fumes
    RCOOH + SOCl₂ → RCOCl + SO₂ + HCl        gaseous by-products: easy separation

All need **dry** conditions.

## Acids that oxidise further

| Acid | Why | Reagents | Products |
|---|---|---|---|
| methanoic, HCOOH | H on the carbonyl carbon (aldehyde-like) | Tollens', Fehling's, acidified KMnO₄, acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ | CO₂ + H₂O |
| ethanedioic, HOOCCOOH | C–C bond between two –COOH groups breaks | **warm** acidified KMnO₄ | CO₂ (+ H₂O) |

Observations with methanoic acid: Tollens' → silver mirror; Fehling's → red-brown precipitate; KMnO₄ purple →
colourless; K₂Cr₂O₇ orange → green.

    5HOOCCOOH + 2MnO₄⁻ + 6H⁺ → 10CO₂ + 2Mn²⁺ + 8H₂O        ratio acid : MnO₄⁻ = 5 : 2
    3HCOOH + Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 8H⁺ → 3CO₂ + 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O

## Relative acidity

**carboxylic acid > phenol > water > alcohol**

| Anion | Where is the charge? | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| RCOO⁻ | shared equally by **two** O atoms | most stabilised |
| C₆H₅O⁻ | spread from one O into ring carbons | stabilised, less so |
| RO⁻ | on one O, intensified by alkyl group | destabilised |

| Test | Carboxylic acid | Phenol | Alcohol |
|---|---|---|---|
| Na₂CO₃(aq) | effervescence (CO₂) | none | none |
| NaOH(aq) | reacts | reacts | no reaction |
| Na(s) | H₂ | H₂ | H₂ |

## Chlorine-substituted acids

- Cl withdraws electrons **inductively** → carboxylate stabilised → **stronger acid**.
- More Cl → stronger: CH₃COOH < ClCH₂COOH < Cl₂CHCOOH < Cl₃CCOOH.
- Cl closer to –COOH → stronger: 2-chloro > 3-chloro > 4-chloro.
- Larger Ka / smaller pKa = stronger acid.

## Choosing between reagents: quick decisions

| Situation | Best choice | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| want easy separation of the acyl chloride | SOCl₂ | SO₂ and HCl leave as gases |
| a test for –OH that gives visible fumes | PCl₅ | steamy HCl fumes at room temperature |
| distinguish HCOOH from CH₃COOH | Tollens' or Fehling's | only methanoic acid is oxidised |
| find the concentration of ethanedioic acid | KMnO₄ titration, warm | self-indicating, 5 : 2 ratio |

## Exam traps

- **"then dilute acid"** is part of the benzoic acid conditions.
- **PCl₃ : acid = 1 : 3**; PCl₅ and SOCl₂ are 1 : 1.
- SOCl₂ advantage: **both by-products are gases**.
- Ethanedioic acid needs **warm** acidified KMnO₄.
- Chloro-acids: **inductive effect**, not delocalisation.
- Carboxylic acid strength: say **two oxygen atoms** share the charge.
- Ethanoic acid gives **no** silver mirror; methanoic acid does.

## Self-test

1. State the reagents and conditions to make benzoic acid from ethylbenzene.
2. Write the equation for propanoic acid reacting with SOCl₂.
3. Give one advantage of SOCl₂ over PCl₅.
4. Write a balanced equation for butanoic acid reacting with PCl₃.
5. Explain why methanoic acid gives a silver mirror with Tollens' reagent but ethanoic acid does not.
6. State the products of oxidising ethanedioic acid with warm acidified KMnO₄.
7. 20.0 cm³ of 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ ethanedioic acid is titrated with 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ KMnO₄. Calculate the volume
   of KMnO₄ needed.
8. Explain why ethanoic acid is a stronger acid than phenol.
9. Put in order of increasing acidity: ethanol, ethanoic acid, phenol, chloroethanoic acid.
10. Why is 2-chlorobutanoic acid stronger than 4-chlorobutanoic acid?
11. Describe one test that distinguishes methanoic acid from ethanoic acid, with results.
12. State what is seen when acidified potassium dichromate(VI) is warmed with methanoic acid.

**Answers:**

1. Hot alkaline KMnO₄, then dilute acid.
2. CH₃CH₂COOH + SOCl₂ → CH₃CH₂COCl + SO₂ + HCl.
3. Both by-products (SO₂ and HCl) are gases, so the acyl chloride is easily separated.
4. 3CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH + PCl₃ → 3CH₃CH₂CH₂COCl + H₃PO₃.
5. Methanoic acid has an H on the carbonyl carbon, like an aldehyde, so it is oxidised (to CO₂ and H₂O) and
   reduces Ag⁺ to Ag; ethanoic acid has no such H and is not oxidised.
6. Carbon dioxide (and water); the KMnO₄ is reduced to Mn²⁺.
7. n(acid) = 1.00 × 10⁻³ mol; n(MnO₄⁻) = 1.00 × 10⁻³ × 2/5 = 4.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol; volume = 4.00 × 10⁻⁴ / 0.0200 =
   0.0200 dm³ = 20.0 cm³.
8. In ethanoate the negative charge is delocalised over two electronegative O atoms; in phenoxide it is spread
   from one O into less electronegative ring carbons, so ethanoate is the more stable anion.
9. Ethanol < phenol < ethanoic acid < chloroethanoic acid.
10. The Cl is closer to the –COOH group, so its inductive electron withdrawal stabilises the carboxylate ion
    more; the effect weakens with distance.
11. Warm with Tollens' reagent: methanoic acid gives a silver mirror; ethanoic acid gives no change. (Or
    Fehling's: red-brown precipitate with methanoic acid only.)
12. The orange solution turns green.
