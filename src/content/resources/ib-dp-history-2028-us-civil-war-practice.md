## Questions

**1.** Explain two ways in which the cotton economy shaped the political life of the USA before 1860. **[4]**

**2.** Read the extract (constructed for practice).

> "The decision of the Court says that the Black man has no rights which the white man is bound to respect. If so, then no territory can be closed to slavery, and no free state can be sure that it will remain free." (Constructed for practice: a speech by a northern politician in 1857.)

**(a)** Identify the two fears expressed in the extract. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the *Dred Scott* decision contributed to the coming of the Civil War. **[4]**

**3.** Distinguish between the aims of the abolitionists and those of the Free Soil and Republican movements. **[3]**

**4.** Analyse the importance of the Nullification Crisis as a long-term cause of the Civil War. **[6]**

**5.** Explain the role of African American participation in the outcome of the war. **[6]**

**6.** Examine the view that foreign relations were important in determining the outcome of the Civil War. **[8]**

**7.** Discuss the social impact of the Civil War on women in either the North or the South. **[8]**

**8.** To what extent was the Compromise of 1877 the main reason for the failure of Reconstruction? **[8]**

**9.** Paper 3 style essay question: "Slavery was the only significant cause of the American Civil War." To what extent do you agree? **[15]**

## Answers

**1.** Two developed points, 2 marks each (1 for the point, 1 for development). Cotton and economic power: cotton made up over half of US exports [1], giving planters great wealth and influence in Congress and the presidency [1]. Defence of slavery: the cotton economy rested on slave labour, so southern leaders defended it as a vital interest and fought to expand it into new territory [1], which made every territorial question a political conflict [1]. Links to northern industry: textile mills depended on southern cotton, which tied northern business to the South and made abolition politically harder in the North [1][1]. **[4]**

**2. (a)** The fear that Black Americans had no rights that must be respected [1]; the fear that slavery might spread to all territories and even free states [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The Supreme Court ruled in 1857 that Black people could not be citizens and that Congress could not ban slavery in the territories [1]. This overturned the Missouri Compromise line and the Republican platform of restricting slavery's expansion [1]. It convinced many northerners that a "Slave Power" controlled the government and strengthened the Republican Party [1]. It also hardened southern demands and removed room for compromise [1]. **[4]**

**3.** Abolitionists such as Garrison sought the immediate end of slavery everywhere, on moral grounds [1]. Free Soil and Republican supporters mainly sought to stop slavery spreading into the territories [1], with many accepting that slavery should continue in states where it existed, partly out of concern for white workers' opportunities and partly from concern over the Constitution [1]. **[3]**

**4.** In 1832 South Carolina declared the tariff null and void, based on states' rights ideas associated with Calhoun [1]. Jackson threatened force and the Force Bill, and a compromise tariff in 1833 ended the crisis [1]. Importance: it established a precedent and language for resisting federal law that southerners later used to justify secession [1]. It showed that the federal government would assert its authority [1]. Limits: the crisis was about tariffs, not directly slavery, and other states did not follow South Carolina [1]. Judgement that it was important in ideas but less so than slavery and territorial disputes [1]. **[6]**

**5.** About 180,000 Black men served in the Union army [1], following the Emancipation Proclamation and the recruitment of the US Colored Troops [1]. They added to Union manpower at a time when volunteering had fallen [1]. Enslaved people who escaped to Union lines weakened the Confederate labour force [1]. Participation also changed the war's purpose and gave grounds for Black citizenship claims after the war [1]. Judgement of its importance compared with industry, leadership and the blockade [1]. **[6]**

**6.** For the view: the Confederacy relied on "King Cotton" diplomacy and hoped that Britain and France would recognise it [2]; the Union stopped this, in part because Britain had other cotton sources and public opinion opposed slavery [2]. The Emancipation Proclamation made recognition of the Confederacy morally difficult [1]. Against: the war was decided by resources, the blockade and military campaigns, and the Trent Affair was settled without a break [2]. Judgement that diplomacy mattered by preventing intervention but was not the main reason [1]. **[8]**

**7.** Choose the North or South. For the South: women managed plantations and farms when men served [1]; shortages and inflation caused hardship and sometimes bread riots, such as in Richmond in 1863 [2]; they worked as nurses and in factories [1]; many lost property or family [1]. For the North: women worked in factories and government offices and ran farms [1]; they organised relief through bodies such as the Sanitary Commission [1]; nursing opened new roles, for example Clara Barton [1]; wages stayed lower than men's [1]. Impact: increased public confidence and activism but no political rights [2]. Judgement on the extent and lasting nature of the change [1]. Credit up to 8 marks for either side. **[8]**

**8.** The compromise ended troop presence and allowed southern Democrats to regain control [2]. Other reasons: violence by the Klan and other groups [2]; Northern weariness and economic depression after 1873 [1]; Supreme Court decisions limiting the 14th Amendment [1]; lack of land reform and economic dependence of freed people [1]. Judgement: the compromise was the end point of a failure already underway, so it was less a cause than a symptom [1]. **[8]**

**9.** Use the Paper 3 markbands. A top answer (13 to 15 marks) shows in-depth understanding of the argument, evaluates a range of perspectives and reaches a substantiated judgement. Slavery as central (about 4 marks): the cotton economy, the territorial disputes, the Fugitive Slave Act, *Dred Scott*, the secession declarations naming slavery. Other factors (about 4 marks): states' rights, sectional economic differences, the tariff, political failure, the election of 1860 and southern fear of losing power. Perspectives (about 3 marks): McPherson and Foner (slavery central), the "blundering generation" view (politicians failed), and Beard (economic conflict). Judgement (about 4 marks): for example, that other factors were important but nearly all were tied to slavery, so "only" is too strong while slavery was the necessary cause. **[15]**
