## Questions

**1.** Solve x² + 3x − 54 = 0. **[3]**

**2.** Solve each equation.

**(a)** 7x² − 21x = 0 **[3]**
**(b)** 25x² − 16 = 0 **[2]**

**3.** Solve 6x² − 5x − 21 = 0. **[4]**

**4.** Solve (x + 2)(x + 5) = 4x + 28. **[4]**

**5.** Here is a table of values for y = x² − 4x − 1.

| x | −1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 4 | −1 | −4 | −5 | −4 | −1 | 4 |

**(a)** Draw the graph of y = x² − 4x − 1 for values of x from −1 to 5. **[2]**
**(b)** Use your graph to estimate the solutions of x² − 4x − 1 = 0. **[2]**
**(c)** Write down the equation of a line you could draw on the graph to solve x² − 4x − 3 = 0. Show how you decide, then use your graph to estimate the solutions. **[3]**

**6.** (Extension only)

**(a)** Write x² − 8x + 6 in the form (x + p)² + q. **[2]**
**(b)** Hence solve x² − 8x + 6 = 0. Give your answers in the form a ± √b. **[2]**

**7.** (Extension only) Solve 3x² + 12x − 5 = 0 by completing the square. Give your answers correct to 2 decimal places. **[5]**

**8.** (Extension only) Use the quadratic formula to solve 4x² − 9x − 1 = 0. Round your answers to 3 significant figures. **[5]**

**9.** A rectangle has length (2x + 1) cm and width (x − 2) cm. Its area is 52 cm².

**(a)** Show that 2x² − 3x − 54 = 0. **[3]**
**(b)** Solve 2x² − 3x − 54 = 0. **[3]**
**(c)** Write down the length and width of the rectangle. **[2]**

**10.** (Extension only) A ball is thrown upwards. Its height h metres above the ground after t seconds is h = 2 + 12t − 5t².

**(a)** Find the time when the ball hits the ground. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the times when the ball is 9 metres above the ground. **[3]**

**11.** (Extension only) The number x satisfies 3/x + 2/(x + 1) = 2.

**(a)** Show that 2x² − 3x − 3 = 0. **[3]**
**(b)** Solve 2x² − 3x − 3 = 0. Round your answers to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Two numbers with product −54 and sum 3 are 9 and −6, so (x + 9)(x − 6) = 0 [1]. x = −9 [1]. x = 6 [1]. **x = −9 or x = 6** **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the brackets down before the solutions, so that your method is visible even if a later sign slips.

**2. (a)** Take out the common factor: 7x(x − 3) = 0 [1]. x = 0 [1]. x = 3 [1]. **x = 0 or x = 3**
**(b)** (5x − 4)(5x + 4) = 0 [1]. **x = 4/5 or x = −4/5** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Dividing both sides of 7x² = 21x by x gives only x = 3, so the solution x = 0 is lost; factorising keeps both.

**3.** a × c = −126, and −14 and 9 have product −126 and sum −5. 6x² − 14x + 9x − 21 = 0 [1]. 2x(3x − 7) + 3(3x − 7) = 0 [1]. (3x − 7)(2x + 3) = 0 [1]. **x = 7/3 or x = −3/2** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Expanding your brackets to check they give 6x² − 5x − 21 takes seconds and catches sign errors before they reach the solutions.

**4.** Expand: x² + 7x + 10 = 4x + 28 [1]. Rearrange: x² + 3x − 18 = 0 [1]. (x + 6)(x − 3) = 0 [1]. **x = −6 or x = 3** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Setting x + 2 and x + 5 equal to 4x or 28 does not work, because the right-hand side is not 0; this approach gives wrong solutions.

**5. (a)** All seven points plotted correctly [1]. A smooth curve through them, with a rounded minimum [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The curve crosses y = 0 between x = −1 and x = 0, and between x = 4 and x = 5 [1]. **x ≈ −0.2 and x ≈ 4.2** (answers within about 0.1 of these are reasonable) [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** x² − 4x − 3 = 0 becomes x² − 4x − 1 = 2 [1], so the line is y = 2 [1]. Reading where it meets the curve gives **x ≈ −0.6 and x ≈ 4.6** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Solutions are x-values, so mark them clearly on the x-axis or list them as x = ...; quoting the y-values of the points is a common way to lose the final mark.

**6. (a)** (x − 4)² [1] − 10, because −16 + 6 = −10 [1]. **(x − 4)² − 10** **[2]**
**(b)** (x − 4)² = 10, so x − 4 = ±√10 [1]. **x = 4 ± √10** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The form a ± √b asks for an exact answer, so giving only decimals does not match what was requested.

**7.** 3x² + 12x − 5 = 3(x² + 4x) − 5 [1] = 3[(x + 2)² − 4] − 5 = 3(x + 2)² − 17 [1]. So 3(x + 2)² = 17 and (x + 2)² = 17/3 [1]. x = −2 ± √(17/3) [1]. **x = 0.38 or x = −4.38** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* When the question names the method, an answer found by the formula alone does not show the method asked for, so show the completed-square form.

**8.** a = 4, b = −9, c = −1 [1]. b² − 4ac = 81 + 16 = 97 [1]. x = (9 ± √97)/8 [1]. **x = 2.36** [1] **or x = −0.106** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Writing the substitution with brackets, as (−9)², prevents the common slip of getting −81; a wrong discriminant is easier to spot when it is shown on its own line.

**9. (a)** (2x + 1)(x − 2) = 2x² − 4x + x − 2 [1] = 2x² − 3x − 2 [1]. Setting this equal to 52 gives 2x² − 3x − 54 = 0 [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** 2x² − 12x + 9x − 54 = 0 [1]. (2x + 9)(x − 6) = 0 [1]. **x = 6 or x = −4.5** [1] **[3]**
**(c)** A width of x − 2 must be positive, so x = 6 [1]. **Length 13 cm and width 4 cm** [1] (check: 13 × 4 = 52). **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A "show that" needs every line, including the expansion; and a context question usually expects the invalid solution to be rejected with a reason.

**10. (a)** At the ground h = 0, so 5t² − 12t − 2 = 0 [1]. t = (12 ± √(144 + 40))/10 = (12 ± √184)/10 [1]. t = 2.56 or t = −0.156 [1]. Time cannot be negative: **t = 2.56 seconds** [1] **[4]**
**(b)** 2 + 12t − 5t² = 9 gives 5t² − 12t + 7 = 0 [1]. (5t − 7)(t − 1) = 0 [1]. **t = 1 second and t = 1.4 seconds** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Two answers in part (b) make sense, since the ball passes 9 metres on the way up and on the way down; giving one value suggests the second root was overlooked.

**11. (a)** Multiply every term by x(x + 1): 3(x + 1) + 2x = 2x(x + 1) [1]. Expand: 3x + 3 + 2x = 2x² + 2x [1]. Rearrange: 2x² − 3x − 3 = 0 [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** a = 2, b = −3, c = −3, so b² − 4ac = 9 + 24 = 33 [1]. x = (3 ± √33)/4 [1]. **x = 2.19 or x = −0.686** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Both solutions are valid here because neither makes a denominator zero; it is good practice to check this rather than assume.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Solving before the equation is in the form "= 0", for example setting each bracket equal to a non-zero number.
- Dividing by x and losing the solution x = 0.
- Wrong signs when splitting the middle term of a trinomial with a ≠ 1.
- Stopping at the factorised form and not stating the values of x.
- Reading y-coordinates instead of x-coordinates from a graph.
- Choosing the wrong horizontal line when the equation has been rearranged.
- Forgetting to take out the coefficient of x² before halving when completing the square.
- Writing −b² for (−b)² in the formula when b is negative.
- Rounding early, or giving a decimal when a surd was requested.
- Leaving a negative length or time in a context question without rejecting it.
