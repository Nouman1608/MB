Use R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ throughout. Relative atomic masses for this set: hydrogen 1.0, carbon 12.0,
nitrogen 14.0, oxygen 16.0, fluorine 19.0, silicon 28.1, sulfur 32.1, vanadium 50.9, tin 118.7.

## Questions

**1.** State the ideal gas equation and give the SI unit of pressure, volume and temperature used in it. **[3]**

**2.** Define the terms *empirical formula* and *molecular formula*. **[2]**

**3.** A gas sample has a volume of 350 cm³ at 85.0 kPa and 27 °C. Convert each of these values to the units needed for the ideal gas equation. **[3]**

**4.** Calculate the volume, in cm³, occupied by 1.28 g of sulfur dioxide, SO₂ (Mr = 64.1), at 45 °C and 112 kPa. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[4]**

**5.** 2.04 g of vanadium is heated in oxygen until the mass is constant. The oxide formed has a mass of 3.64 g. Calculate the empirical formula of the oxide. **[3]**

**6.** A gaseous hydride of silicon contains 90.4% silicon and 9.6% hydrogen by mass. Its Mr is 62.2. Calculate its empirical formula and deduce its molecular formula. **[4]**

**7.** A 5.00 dm³ steel cylinder holds 0.180 mol of helium at a pressure of 92.4 kPa. Calculate the temperature of the helium in kelvin. **[3]**

**8.** An evacuated glass bulb of volume 400 cm³ is filled with an unknown gas at 20 °C and 101 kPa. The mass of the bulb increases by 0.712 g. Calculate the Mr of the gas. **[3]**

**9.** A student finds the empirical formula of an oxide of tin. Tin is warmed with concentrated nitric acid in a crucible, and the residue is then heated strongly. Results:

| Reading | Mass / g |
|---|---|
| empty crucible | 21.346 |
| crucible and tin | 22.536 |
| crucible and tin oxide (constant mass) | 22.857 |

**(a)** Use the results to work out the empirical formula of the tin oxide. **[3]**
**(b)** Give the reason for heating the residue to constant mass. **[1]**
**(c)** The balance reads to ±0.001 g. Calculate the percentage uncertainty in the mass of oxygen. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest why the reaction with nitric acid is done in a fume cupboard. **[1]**

**10.** A volatile liquid X contains 54.5% carbon, 9.1% hydrogen and 36.4% oxygen by mass. In an experiment, 0.186 g of X is injected into a gas syringe held in an oven at 110 °C. It vaporises completely, giving 67.9 cm³ of vapour at 99.0 kPa.

**(a)** Calculate the empirical formula of X. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the Mr of X from the gas data. **[3]**
**(c)** Deduce the molecular formula of X. **[1]**
**(d)** State why the oven must be above the boiling point of X, and explain the effect on the calculated Mr if some vapour leaked out of the syringe. **[2]**

**11.** A 0.520 g sample of a gaseous fluoride of nitrogen contains 0.140 g of nitrogen and 0.380 g of fluorine. The sample occupies 125 cm³ at 30 °C and 101 kPa.

**(a)** Calculate the empirical formula of the compound. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the amount, in mol, of gas in the sample. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the Mr of the compound and deduce its molecular formula. **[3]**
**(d)** The container is sealed and heated to 150 °C. Calculate the new pressure in kPa. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** pV = nRT [1]. Pressure in Pa and volume in m³ [1]. Temperature in K [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the units as named SI units; writing "kPa" or "dm³" here would contradict the requirement that every variable is in SI units.

**2.** Empirical formula: the ratio of atoms of each element in the compound, in its simplest whole-number form [1]. Molecular formula: the true number of atoms of each element present in the compound's formula [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Simplest ratio" without "whole number" is incomplete; include both words to make the definition precise.

**3.** V = 350 × 10⁻⁶ = **3.50 × 10⁻⁴ m³** [1]. p = 85.0 × 10³ = **8.50 × 10⁴ Pa** [1]. T = 27 + 273 = **300 K** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Write each conversion out on its own line so every step can be credited; dividing cm³ by 1000 gives dm³, not m³.

**4.** n = 1.28 / 64.1 = 0.01997 mol [1]. Convert: p = 112 × 10³ Pa, T = 318 K [1]. V = nRT / p = (0.01997 × 8.31 × 318) / (112 × 10³) = 4.71 × 10⁻⁴ m³ [1]. V = **471 cm³** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Converting m³ back to cm³ is part of the answer; an answer left as 4.71 × 10⁻⁴ does not answer the question as set.

**5.** Mass of oxygen = 3.64 − 2.04 = 1.60 g [1]. Moles: V = 2.04 / 50.9 = 0.0401; O = 1.60 / 16.0 = 0.100 [1]. Ratio 1 : 2.5 = 2 : 5, so **V₂O₅** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Using 3.64 g as the mass of oxygen is the usual slip; show the subtraction so the oxygen mass is clear.

**6.** Si: 90.4 / 28.1 = 3.217; H: 9.6 / 1.0 = 9.6 [1]. Ratio 1 : 2.98 ≈ 1 : 3, empirical formula SiH₃ [1]. Empirical formula mass = 28.1 + 3.0 = 31.1; 62.2 ÷ 31.1 = 2 [1]. Molecular formula **Si₂H₆** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the division 62.2 ÷ 31.1 = 2; a molecular formula stated with no link to Mr leaves the reasoning unseen.

**7.** p = 92.4 × 10³ Pa and V = 5.00 × 10⁻³ m³ [1]. T = pV / nR = (92.4 × 10³ × 5.00 × 10⁻³) / (0.180 × 8.31) [1]. T = **309 K** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* An answer near 0.309 K signals kPa left unconverted; check that a gas temperature is physically sensible before moving on.

**8.** Convert: V = 4.00 × 10⁻⁴ m³, p = 101 × 10³ Pa, T = 293 K [1]. M = mRT / pV = (0.712 × 8.31 × 293) / (101 × 10³ × 4.00 × 10⁻⁴) [1]. Mr = **42.9** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote Mr without units; if you give a molar mass instead, the unit must be g mol⁻¹.

**9. (a)** Mass of tin = 22.536 − 21.346 = 1.190 g; mass of oxygen = 22.857 − 22.536 = 0.321 g [1]. Moles: Sn = 1.190 / 118.7 = 0.01003; O = 0.321 / 16.0 = 0.02006 [1]. Ratio 1 : 2.00, so **SnO₂** [1].
**(b)** To make sure the reaction is complete and all water and other volatile matter has been driven off [1].
**(c)** Two readings give ±0.002 g; (0.002 / 0.321) × 100 = **0.62%** [1].
**(d)** Toxic gas (nitrogen dioxide) is given off [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c), a mass found by difference uses two readings, so the uncertainty is doubled before it is divided by the mass.

**10. (a)** C: 54.5 / 12.0 = 4.54; H: 9.1 / 1.0 = 9.1; O: 36.4 / 16.0 = 2.275 [1]. Divide by 2.275: 2.00 : 4.00 : 1 [1]. Empirical formula **C₂H₄O** [1].
**(b)** Convert: T = 383 K, p = 99.0 × 10³ Pa, V = 67.9 × 10⁻⁶ m³ [1]. M = mRT / pV = (0.186 × 8.31 × 383) / (99.0 × 10³ × 67.9 × 10⁻⁶) [1]. Mr = **88.1** [1].
**(c)** C₂H₄O = 44.0; 88.1 ÷ 44.0 = 2, so **C₄H₈O₂** [1].
**(d)** So that all of X is a gas and its whole mass contributes to the vapour volume [1]. A leak makes the measured volume too small, so the calculated Mr is too high [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* For (d), a direction ("too high") without the reason (V is smaller and sits on the bottom of mRT / pV) is unlikely to earn the explanation mark.

**11. (a)** N: 0.140 / 14.0 = 0.0100; F: 0.380 / 19.0 = 0.0200 [1]. Ratio 1 : 2, empirical formula **NF₂** [1].
**(b)** p = 101 × 10³ Pa, V = 1.25 × 10⁻⁴ m³, T = 303 K [1]. n = pV / RT = (101 × 10³ × 1.25 × 10⁻⁴) / (8.31 × 303) = **5.01 × 10⁻³ mol** [1].
**(c)** Mr = 0.520 ÷ (5.01 × 10⁻³) = 104 [1]. NF₂ = 14.0 + 38.0 = 52.0 [1]. 104 ÷ 52.0 = 2, so **N₂F₄** [1].
**(d)** n and V are fixed, so p is proportional to T (in kelvin) [1]. p = 101 × 423 / 303 = **141 kPa** [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* In (d), scaling by 150 / 30 in °C gives 505 kPa; both temperatures must be in kelvin before any ratio is taken.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting kPa, cm³ or °C straight into pV = nRT.
- Dividing cm³ by 1000 and calling the result m³.
- Leaving a final volume in m³ when cm³ or dm³ was asked for.
- Taking ratios of Celsius temperatures instead of kelvin temperatures.
- Rounding a 1 : 2.5 or 1 : 1.33 ratio to the nearest whole number instead of multiplying through.
- Using the mass of the oxide as the mass of oxygen.
- Ending with the empirical formula when the molecular formula is the target.
- Forgetting that a mass by difference has two readings, so twice the reading uncertainty.
- Stating that an error makes Mr "inaccurate" without saying whether it is too high or too low, and why.
