Subtopic 20.5 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702 (2025–2027) covers the definition of magnetic flux, Φ = BA, flux linkage, the experiments that demonstrate electromagnetic induction, and Faraday's and Lenz's laws. These notes are condensed for recall. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [Electromagnetic induction study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-electromagnetic-induction/); to apply the ideas, try the [Electromagnetic induction practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-electromagnetic-induction-practice/).

## Definitions and equations

**Magnetic flux**: the product of the magnetic flux density and the cross-sectional area perpendicular to the direction of the magnetic flux density.

```
Φ = BA                 (A ⊥ B)          unit: weber, 1 Wb = 1 T m²
Φ = BA cos θ           (θ between B and the normal to the coil)
Flux linkage = NΦ      unit: Wb (Wb-turns)
E = −Δ(NΦ)/Δt          (Faraday: size; minus sign: Lenz)
E = BLv                (rod of length L moving at v ⊥ B)
```

## The two laws

- **Faraday's law**: the induced e.m.f. is proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux linkage.
- **Lenz's law**: the induced e.m.f. is in such a direction as to oppose the change producing it.
- Lenz's law follows from conservation of energy: work must be done against the opposing effect, and that work becomes electrical energy.

## Orientation of a coil

| Coil position | Flux linkage |
|---|---|
| Plane ⊥ B | NBA (maximum) |
| Plane at angle α to B | NBA sin α |
| Plane parallel to B | 0 |
| Turned 180° from ⊥ | −NBA (change of 2NBA) |

## Graph rules

| Flux linkage–time graph | E.m.f. |
|---|---|
| Rising straight line | Constant, one sign |
| Flat | Zero |
| Falling straight line | Constant, opposite sign |
| Steeper | Larger |
| Area under E–t graph | Change in flux linkage |

## Experiments: what they show

| Observation | Shows |
|---|---|
| Meter deflects only while a magnet moves in or out of a coil | A **changing** flux induces an e.m.f. |
| Deflection reverses when the motion reverses | Direction depends on the direction of the change |
| Switching a current on or off in a second coil gives a brief deflection | Changing flux induces e.m.f. even without motion |
| Faster motion, stronger magnet, more turns → larger deflection | Size depends on the rate of change of flux linkage |
| Approaching N pole makes the near end of the coil a N pole | Lenz's law: the induced effect opposes the change |
| Magnet falls slowly through a copper tube | Induced currents oppose the motion |

## Lenz's law direction method

1. Is the flux through the circuit increasing or decreasing, and which way does it point?
2. Induced field opposes the change: opposite to the external field if increasing, same way if decreasing.
3. Right-hand grip rule → current direction.
4. Check: any force on the conductor opposes its motion.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Magnetic flux Φ | BA with A perpendicular to B; unit weber |
| Weber | 1 T m² |
| Flux linkage | NΦ for a coil of N turns |
| Induced e.m.f. | E.m.f. produced by a changing flux linkage |
| Faraday's law | E.m.f. ∝ rate of change of flux linkage |
| Lenz's law | Induced e.m.f. opposes the change producing it |

## Exam traps

- Using the whole area of a coil tilted to the field.
- Leaving out N.
- Treating a 180° rotation as no change.
- Linking a large e.m.f. to a large flux rather than a fast change.
- Converting cm² to m² by dividing by 100 (it is 10⁴).
- Stating Lenz's law as opposing the flux rather than the change.
- Saying there is no e.m.f. in an open circuit: the e.m.f. is induced; only the current needs a complete circuit.

## Self-test

1. Define magnetic flux.
2. State the unit of flux in terms of the tesla.
3. A coil of 500 turns and area 3.0 × 10⁻⁴ m² has its plane perpendicular to a 0.080 T field. Calculate the flux linkage.
4. The field in Question 3 falls to zero in 0.15 s. Calculate the average e.m.f.
5. A flat loop of area 1.5 × 10⁻³ m² has its normal at 60° to a field of 0.12 T. Calculate the flux.
6. State Faraday's law.
7. State Lenz's law and the conservation law it follows from.
8. A rod 0.15 m long moves at 5.0 m s⁻¹ perpendicular to a 0.40 T field. Calculate the e.m.f.
9. A graph of flux linkage against time is horizontal for 2 s. What is the e.m.f. during that time?
10. A coil is turned through 180° in a uniform field. Is the change in flux linkage zero? Explain.
11. Why does a magnet take longer to fall through a copper tube than through a plastic tube?
12. A north pole is pulled away from one end of a coil. What polarity does the induced current give that end?

**Answers:** 1. The product of the magnetic flux density and the cross-sectional area perpendicular to the field. 2. 1 Wb = 1 T m². 3. 500 × 0.080 × 3.0 × 10⁻⁴ = 0.012 Wb. 4. 0.012 / 0.15 = 0.080 V. 5. 0.12 × 1.5 × 10⁻³ × cos 60° = 9.0 × 10⁻⁵ Wb. 6. The magnitude of the induced e.m.f. is proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux linkage. 7. The induced e.m.f. acts in a direction to oppose the change producing it; conservation of energy. 8. BLv = 0.40 × 0.15 × 5.0 = 0.30 V. 9. Zero. 10. No: the flux passes through the coil the other way, so the linkage goes from +NBA to −NBA, a change of 2NBA. 11. The moving magnet induces currents in the copper; by Lenz's law these produce fields that oppose its motion. Plastic is an insulator, so no current flows. 12. South (it attracts the magnet, opposing its withdrawal).
