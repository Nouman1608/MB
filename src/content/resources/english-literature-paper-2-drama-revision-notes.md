## Format at a glance

- **1 hour 30 minutes, 50 marks, worth 50%** of the whole qualification.
- **Two questions on two set texts**, 25 marks each.
- **Closed book** — no texts in the exam; passage extracts for (a) questions are printed on the paper.
- Set texts change by year — always confirm against the current syllabus.

## The one-of-each rule

For each text, there is a choice of **(a) passage-based** or **(b) essay**. You must answer **one
passage-based and one essay question across the paper** — you cannot answer two essays or two passage
questions. Both skills must be exam-ready for **both** plays.

## The four AOs — equally weighted, 25% each

| AO | What it rewards |
|---|---|
| AO1 | Detailed knowledge of the text, supported by reference and quotation |
| AO2 | Understanding of meanings and contexts — beyond surface meaning |
| AO3 | How writers use language, structure and form to create effects |
| AO4 | A sensitive, informed **personal** response |

**A technically observant answer with no personal engagement caps itself just as surely as an
enthusiastic one with no quotation.** Every paragraph should do all four jobs: a claim, evidence, a
comment on dramatic method, and your own evaluation of its effect.

## Why Paper 2 specifically (not Paper 3/4)

Paper 2 is the single-exam, closed-book drama route. The alternative is Paper 3 (Drama, Open Text)
paired with either Paper 4 (Unseen) or Component 5 (Coursework). Choosing Paper 2 means: no unseen
paper, no coursework folder — but also no text open on the desk, and two plays held securely in memory
at once.

## Because it's closed book: build a quotation bank

Memorise **10-15 flexible lines per play**, chosen to cover several characters and themes — this will
serve almost any essay title. Open-text candidates never need to do this; closed-book candidates
cannot skip it.

## Passage vs essay — different jobs

- **Passage-based (a)**: a close-reading exercise. The extract is printed in front of you — the
  examiner expects detailed work with its specific language and stagecraft, not a general character
  survey.
- **Essay (b)**: plan for five minutes first. Three or four points arranged so the argument develops —
  not a character description that happens to mention the question's key word in each paragraph.

## Remember: these are plays, not novels

Write about **audience, staging, entrances, dramatic irony**, and what an audience sees and hears.
Treating a play like a novel — describing only plot and character — misses the genre awareness AO3
specifically rewards.

## Timing discipline

Split 90 minutes evenly: **45 minutes per question**. Two balanced 25-mark answers nearly always
outscore one long, well-developed answer and one rushed one.

## Exam traps

- Answering two essays or two passage questions instead of one of each.
- Bringing a general character survey to a passage-based question instead of close reading the printed
  extract.
- Writing about a play as if it were a novel — no staging, no audience awareness.
- Over-running the first question and leaving the second rushed.

## Why 0475 is organised by paper, not by topic list

Unlike a content-based subject with a topic-by-topic syllabus, 0475 is organised entirely around
**routes through the qualification**. Every candidate takes Paper 1 (Poetry and Prose), then completes
the qualification through one of three routes: Paper 2 Drama, OR Paper 3 Drama (Open Text) with Paper
4 Unseen, OR Paper 3 with Component 5 Coursework. Knowing which route your school has entered you for
matters more here than in most subjects, since revision materials genuinely differ by route — a Paper
3 candidate's open-text drama preparation (annotating a personal copy of the text) is not transferable
to a Paper 2 candidate's closed-book quotation-memorisation approach, even though both study drama.

## What "50% of the qualification" actually means for revision time

Because Paper 1 (Poetry and Prose) and Paper 2 (Drama) are each worth exactly 50 marks and 50% of the
final grade on this route, drama deserves equal revision time to poetry and prose combined — not a
smaller share simply because it is "one paper" against Paper 1's coverage of two genres. Candidates
who intuitively give poetry and prose more attention because Paper 1 covers more named forms often
under-prepare Paper 2 relative to its actual weighting in the final grade.

## One more distinction worth keeping straight

Do not confuse Paper 2's "essay question" with a free-choice essay — the question still names a
specific character, relationship or theme, and the mark scheme still rewards textual reference over a
general discussion of the play's themes in the abstract. Treat it as a directed essay, not an open one.

## Self-test

1. How many marks is Paper 2 worth, and what percentage of the qualification?
2. What is the one-of-each rule?
3. Name the four AOs and what each rewards.
4. Why does a closed-book route demand a quotation bank?
5. What genre-specific element should a strong drama answer always include?

**Answers:** 1. 50 marks, 50% of the qualification. 2. One passage-based question and one essay
question across the two set texts — never two of the same type. 3. AO1 knowledge/quotation, AO2
meanings/contexts, AO3 language/structure/form, AO4 personal response. 4. Because texts cannot be
brought into the exam room, so quotations must come from memory. 5. Staging, audience awareness,
dramatic irony — treating the text as a play, not a novel.
