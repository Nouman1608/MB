## Section A

**1.** Define outsourcing. **[1]**

**2.** Identify the **two** areas in which outsourcing has grown. **[2]**

**3.** State **two** services a wholesaler provides to a retailer. **[2]**

**4.** Identify the **four** types of communication. **[4]**

## Section B

**5.** A toy company designs its products in Pakistan but has them made by a factory in another country under contract. It also pays a logistics firm to store and deliver orders to customers.

**(a)** Explain **two** reasons why the company outsources its manufacturing. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain **one** risk for the company of outsourcing. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain **one** benefit of using a logistics firm for its deliveries. **[2]**

**6.** Explain the importance of the wholesaler to the manufacturer. **[6]**

**7.** Many independent wholesalers have lost trade to supermarkets that buy direct from manufacturers. Explain how cash and carry and voluntary chains help independent wholesalers survive. **[6]**

**8.** A shop owner needs to **(a)** order more stock urgently from a nearby supplier, **(b)** send a formal complaint that may be used as evidence later, and **(c)** hold a meeting with a supplier in another country. Recommend a method of communication for each and justify your choice using factors from the syllabus. **[6]**

**9.** Discuss whether a retailer should use a chatbot on its website to deal with customer questions. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** Paying another enterprise to carry out an activity the business could do itself (in-house) [1].

**2.** Contract manufacturing [1]; contract logistics [1].

**3.** Any two: breaking bulk; offering a wide range of goods; credit; delivery; advice on products or displays; packing, labelling or price-marking [1] [1].

**4.** Written [1], oral [1], electronic [1], visual [1].

**5. (a)** Any two, 2 marks each: **lower costs** — the overseas factory may have lower wages or economies of scale, so each toy costs less [1] [1]; **expertise** — a specialist factory already has the machinery and skilled workers [1] [1]; **focus on core activities** — the company can concentrate on design and marketing [1] [1]; **flexibility** — it can order more or fewer toys without building its own factory [1] [1].
**(b)** Less control over quality: if the factory makes faulty or unsafe toys, the company's brand is damaged [1] [1] (or: delays in delivery, risk of designs being copied, or unethical working conditions harming reputation).
**(c)** The logistics firm has specialist warehouses and vehicles, so orders reach customers faster or more cheaply than the company could manage itself [1] [1].

**6.** The wholesaler **buys in bulk**, giving the manufacturer large regular orders and long production runs [1] [1]; it **stores goods**, so the manufacturer saves warehousing costs [1]; it **bears the risk** of goods not selling or being damaged [1]; it **distributes to many retailers**, so the manufacturer deals with a few wholesalers rather than many shops, saving time and transport costs [1]; it **pays promptly**, helping the manufacturer's cash flow, and passes on market information [1].

**7. Cash and carry:** retailers visit the warehouse, select goods, pay at once and transport them themselves [1]; because there is no credit or delivery, the wholesaler's costs and therefore prices are lower [1], attracting small shops, cafés and restaurants that buy little and often [1]. **Voluntary chains:** independent retailers agree to buy most of their stock from one wholesaler [1]; the wholesaler's bulk buying lowers prices for them [1], and they share a common brand name and promotions, helping them and the wholesaler compete with supermarkets [1].

**8. (a)** Telephone — the matter is **urgent** and the supplier is nearby, so a quick two-way conversation confirms the order at once [1] [1]. **(b)** Letter (or email with a written record) — the message is **important** and a formal written record can be kept as evidence [1] [1]. **(c)** Videoconferencing — the **distance** is great, so it saves travel cost and time while allowing face-to-face discussion, provided **networks are available** [1] [1].

**9.** Benefits: answers simple questions instantly, 24 hours a day [1]; cheaper than employing staff for every query [1]. Limitations: cannot handle complex or unusual complaints, which may frustrate customers [1]; customers may prefer a human, and personal service is lost [1]. Judgement: use a chatbot for simple questions with an option to reach a person [1].

## Frequently asked questions

**Is web chat a type or a method of communication?** It is a method. The syllabus types are written, oral, electronic and visual; web-based chat (including chatbots) is an electronic method.

**Does a voluntary chain mean the shops are owned by the wholesaler?** No. The retailers stay independently owned; they agree to buy mainly from one wholesaler and share branding and promotions.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a reason for outsourcing without explaining how it benefits the business in the scenario.
- Describing wholesaler functions without saying who benefits.
- Recommending a communication method without naming the factor that justifies it.
