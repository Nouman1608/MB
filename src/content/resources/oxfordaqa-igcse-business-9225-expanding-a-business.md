This guide covers section **3.1.7 Expanding a business** of OxfordAQA International GCSE Business (9225), part of Topic 1, Business in the real world. The specification asks you to discuss the advantages and disadvantages of **methods of growth**: organic growth through **franchising, opening new stores and expanding through e-commerce**, **outsourcing**, and external growth through **mergers and takeovers**. You must understand **economies of scale** (including purchasing and technical economies) and **diseconomies of scale** (caused by poor communication, coordination issues and reduced staff motivation), and be able to **calculate and interpret average unit costs**. You are not expected to know merger or takeover legislation. All businesses and figures in this guide are invented.

For the whole topic, see the [Business in the Real World study guide](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-business-business-in-the-real-world/). Questions on this subtopic are in the [practice set](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-9225-expanding-a-business-practice/), and condensed notes are in the [Business in the Real World revision notes](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-business-real-world-revision-notes/).

## Why do businesses expand?

Businesses expand to earn higher profit, reach more customers, gain a larger share of the market, spread the risk across more products or places and benefit from lower costs per unit. Some owners also want the status of running a larger business. Growth carries risks, so the method chosen needs to suit the business and its situation.

## Two ways to grow

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Organic (internal) growth | The business grows from within, using its own resources and profits | Opening new stores, selling online, franchising |
| External growth | The business grows by joining with or buying another business | Merger, takeover |

Outsourcing is a method of managing growth by using other firms to carry out some tasks.

## Methods of expansion

### Opening new stores

The business builds or rents more outlets, in new towns or countries.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| The owner keeps full control and all the profit | Needs a large amount of money for premises and stock |
| Slow, steady growth is easier to manage | Slow compared with buying another firm |
| The brand and standards stay the same across stores | A poor location may not bring enough customers |
| Existing customers already know the brand | New stores may take sales from nearby existing stores |

### Expanding through e-commerce

**E-commerce** means selling goods or services online. A business can start selling to customers in other areas or other countries through a website or app.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Reaches a much larger market, including international customers | Needs investment in the website, payment security and delivery |
| No need for many new shops, so lower premises costs | Customers cannot see or try the products, so returns may be high |
| Open all day and night | Strong online competition and price comparison |
| Data on customers' buying habits | Delivery problems or website failures damage the reputation |

### Franchising

A **franchise** is an arrangement in which a business (the **franchisor**) sells the right to use its name, products and methods to another person (the **franchisee**). The franchisee usually pays an initial fee and a share of sales, and follows the franchisor's rules.

| Advantages to the franchisor | Disadvantages to the franchisor |
|---|---|
| Grows quickly using the franchisee's money | Receives only part of the profit from each outlet |
| The franchisee is motivated because they own the outlet | A poorly run franchise can damage the brand |
| Lower risk and less capital needed | Less direct control over quality and customer service |

**Worked example 1: franchise income.** A sandwich chain charges a $20,000 start-up fee and 6% of sales. A franchisee's first-year sales are $300,000.

```
Share of sales = 6% x $300,000 = $18,000
First-year income to franchisor = $20,000 + $18,000 = $38,000
```

The franchisor receives $38,000 without paying for the shop, stock or staff. The franchisee keeps the rest of the profit, but must pay these charges and follow the rules.

### Outsourcing

**Outsourcing** means paying another business to carry out some of the work, such as delivery, payroll or customer service. The growing business can then focus on its main activity.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Specialists may do the work better or more cheaply | Less control over quality and timing |
| Frees management time and capital | The supplier may fail, or stop working for the business |
| Easier to cope with a rise in demand | Risk of poor quality that damages the business's reputation |
| Avoids buying equipment or hiring extra staff | May be more expensive over time |

### Mergers and takeovers (external growth)

A **merger** is when two businesses agree to join and form one business. A **takeover** (or acquisition) is when one business buys enough shares in another to control it.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Fast growth in size and market share | Expensive, particularly takeovers |
| Removes a competitor | Workers may fear job losses, so motivation falls |
| Gains new customers, products, skills or markets | The two cultures may clash, causing conflict |
| Cost savings by combining operations | Combined firm may become too large to manage well |

**Worked example 2: market share after a merger.** Firm A has sales of $4 million and firm B has sales of $1 million in a market worth $20 million.

```
A's market share = 4 / 20 x 100 = 20%
B's market share = 1 / 20 x 100 = 5%
Combined share   = 5 / 20 x 100 = 25%
```

The merger gives the combined firm a 25% share, larger than either firm had alone, which may put it ahead of rivals with a lower share. Success is not automatic: customers may switch to rivals if the merger leads to price rises or reduced quality.

## Economies of scale

**Economies of scale** are the advantages of producing on a larger scale that reduce the average cost of each unit. The average unit cost falls as output rises.

**Average unit cost** = total cost / number of units produced.

**Worked example 3: average unit cost.** A bakery's costs at three levels of output:

| Output (loaves) | Total cost | Average unit cost |
|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | $12,000 | $12.00 |
| 4,000 | $36,000 | $9.00 |
| 10,000 | $110,000 | $11.00 |

```
1,000 loaves:  12,000 / 1,000  = $12.00
4,000 loaves:  36,000 / 4,000  = $9.00
10,000 loaves: 110,000 / 10,000 = $11.00
```

Between 1,000 and 4,000 loaves the average cost falls from $12 to $9, which is an economy of scale. Between 4,000 and 10,000 it rises from $9 to $11, which is a diseconomy of scale.

### Types of economies of scale named in the specification

- **Purchasing economies:** large orders earn discounts. A bakery buying 500 kg of flour pays $4 per kg. If it merges with another bakery and the two buy 5,000 kg together, the supplier charges $3.40 per kg. The big order costs 5,000 x $3.40 = $17,000, compared with 5,000 x $4 = $20,000 at the old price. The saving is $3,000, or 15%, because the supplier gives a discount for the large order.
- **Technical economies:** larger firms can use bigger, more efficient machines and production lines. A large factory may spread the cost of an expensive machine over a vast number of products, so each product bears less of the cost.

## Diseconomies of scale

**Diseconomies of scale** occur when growth makes average unit costs rise. The specification names three causes.

- **Poor communication:** messages take longer to pass between more layers of managers and more sites, so mistakes happen.
- **Coordination problems:** it is hard to manage many departments and locations so that they work together.
- **Reduced staff motivation:** workers in a big firm may feel that they are not valued, so they work less well, take more time off or leave.

A business can reduce the risk by dividing into smaller units, improving communication and giving staff more responsibility.

## Common mistakes

- Defining economies of scale as "making more profit". They are lower **average costs** as output rises.
- Confusing average unit cost with total cost. Divide total cost by output.
- Saying that growth always brings economies of scale. Diseconomies can follow.
- Describing a method of growth without a link to the business in the case.
- Mixing up a merger (agreement) and a takeover (purchase of control).
- Saying outsourcing is the same as franchising. In outsourcing a firm pays another to do a task; in franchising a firm sells the right to use its name.

## Exam technique for this section

For **state** questions, name the method or type of economy. For **explain** questions, give an advantage or disadvantage with a reason that fits the business. For **calculate** questions, show the formula and the working. For **evaluate** questions, such as which method of growth is best for a business, compare at least two methods against the case facts (money available, wish for control, speed of growth), then reach a justified recommendation.
