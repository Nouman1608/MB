## Section A - multiple choice

**1.** Which transport method is most suitable for moving crude oil continuously from an oilfield to a
refinery 300 km away? **[1]**
A air freight   B container ship   C pipeline   D delivery van

**2.** Which type of warehouse stores imported goods before customs duty has been paid? **[1]**
A bonded   B cash and carry   C cold storage   D regional distribution centre

**3.** Transferring a container from a large ocean ship to a smaller ship at a hub port is called: **[1]**
A logistics postponement   B transshipment   C voice picking   D quantity function

## Section B - structured questions

**4.** State the **three** functions of transport in the supply chain. **[3]**

**5.** *Kiran Steel makes steel beams in a city 600 km from a port. It exports 2 000 tonnes a month.*
(a) Identify the most suitable transport method to move the beams to the port. Justify your choice. **[3]**
(b) Explain **two** disadvantages of using road transport for this journey. **[4]**

**6.** *Medilink supplies vaccines that must be kept at 2-8 °C to hospitals in three neighbouring
countries.*
(a) Explain **two** factors Medilink should consider when choosing a transport method. **[4]**
(b) Identify the type of warehouse Medilink needs. **[1]**

**7.** Explain **two** advantages of containerisation to an exporter. **[4]**

**8.** Explain how a warehouse helps to keep prices stable for a seasonal crop such as rice. **[3]**

**9.** *FreshCart, a supermarket chain, uses a regional distribution centre (RDC) to supply 40 shops.*
(a) Describe what an RDC does. **[2]**
(b) Explain **two** benefits to FreshCart of using an RDC. **[4]**

**10.** *An online retailer is introducing robotics, automated sorting and voice picking in its warehouse.*
(a) Describe how voice picking works. **[2]**
(b) Discuss whether warehouse automation will improve customer service. **[6]**

**11.** *A furniture maker in Vietnam must deliver 800 sofas to a retailer in Germany within six weeks.
The sofas are bulky but not fragile.* Evaluate whether sea or air transport is more appropriate.
**[6]**

## Answers

**1.** C. **[1]**

**2.** A. **[1]**

**3.** B. **[1]**

**4.** Time **[1]**, place **[1]** and quantity **[1]**.

**5.** (a) Rail (freight train) **[1]**: carries very heavy, bulky loads **[1]** over long distances at lower cost
per tonne than road **[1]**.
(b) Any two explained: each truck carries a limited load, so many journeys are needed, raising cost **[2]**;
congestion and road conditions can delay deliveries to the port **[2]**; more fuel, pollution and risk of
accidents **[2]**. **[max 4]**

**6.** (a) Any two explained: nature of goods - vaccines are perishable and must stay refrigerated **[2]**;
urgency - hospitals may need them quickly **[2]**; security - high value, so theft or damage must be
avoided **[2]**; reliability of transporter - late delivery could ruin the stock **[2]**. **[max 4]**
(b) Cold storage warehouse. **[1]**

**7.** Any two explained: sealed containers reduce theft and damage **[2]**, which lowers insurance costs;
containers move between ship, rail and road without unpacking **[2]**, making loading faster and cheaper;
standard sizes allow fast handling by cranes **[2]**. **[max 4]**

**8.** Rice is harvested at one time of year **[1]**; storing it in a warehouse lets it be released gradually
**[1]**, so supply matches demand all year and prices do not fall sharply at harvest or rise sharply later
**[1]**.

**9.** (a) A large warehouse that receives goods from many suppliers **[1]** and sends mixed deliveries to the
chain's shops in a region **[1]**.
(b) Any two explained: fewer, fuller deliveries to each shop reduce transport costs **[2]**; shops need
less storage space, so more floor space for selling **[2]**; better stock control across the chain
**[2]**. **[max 4]**

**10.** (a) Workers wear a headset **[1]** and receive spoken instructions about where to go and what to pick,
confirming each pick by voice **[1]**.
(b) **[6]** Likely to improve: less human error and better picking accuracy mean customers receive the
correct items; faster sorting speeds delivery; improved inventory control means fewer "out of stock"
problems. Limits: high installation cost may raise prices; breakdowns can stop dispatch; fewer staff may
reduce flexibility for unusual orders or returns. Conclusion: service is likely to improve if systems are
reliable and staff are retrained.

**11.** **[6]** Sea: far cheaper for 800 bulky sofas; a container ship has large capacity; six weeks is
enough time for a typical Asia-Europe voyage plus road delivery; sofas are not fragile or perishable.
Air: much faster, but very expensive for bulky goods and limited cargo space would mean many flights.
Risks with sea: port delays or bad weather could threaten the deadline. Conclusion: sea transport in
containers is more appropriate, provided shipping is booked early to meet the six-week deadline.

## A note on exam technique for this topic

In "choose a method" questions, always justify the choice with at least two of the six factors in the
syllabus - nature of goods, urgency, cost, security, distance and reliability. For 6-mark evaluations,
give both sides and a clear conclusion that answers the question.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many transport methods does 0715 list?** Five: road, rail, air, sea and pipeline, each with named
examples such as couriers, freight trains, freight planes, container ships and tankers.

**What is the difference between containerisation and transshipment?** Containerisation is packing goods in
standard containers. Transshipment is moving those containers from one ship or vehicle to another on the
way to their destination.

**Which warehouse types are on the syllabus?** Bonded, cold storage, cash and carry, and regional
distribution centres.
