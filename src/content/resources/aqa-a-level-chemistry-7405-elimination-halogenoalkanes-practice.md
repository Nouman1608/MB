These questions cover section **3.3.3.2 Elimination** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2. Learn the ideas first in the [Elimination study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-elimination-halogenoalkanes/) and the [Elimination revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-elimination-halogenoalkanes-revision-notes/). Use Aᵣ values H = 1.0, C = 12.0, Br = 79.9, and a molar gas volume of 24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹ at room temperature and pressure.

## Questions

**1.** 2-bromopropane reacts with potassium hydroxide to give both propan-2-ol and propene.

**(a)** State the role of the hydroxide ion in the formation of propan-2-ol. **[1]**

**(b)** State the role of the hydroxide ion in the formation of propene. **[1]**

**(c)** Write an equation for the formation of propene. **[1]**

**2.** State the reagent and conditions that favour elimination rather than substitution when 2-bromopropane reacts with potassium hydroxide. **[2]**

**3.** Outline the mechanism for the elimination of hydrogen bromide from 2-bromopropane by hydroxide ions. Describe each curly arrow and state the products. **[4]**

**4.** 2-bromobutane is heated with ethanolic potassium hydroxide.

**(a)** Name all the alkenes that can form, including stereoisomers. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why one of these alkenes has stereoisomers and another does not. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why 1-bromopropane gives only one alkene on elimination. **[2]**

**6.** 2-bromo-2-methylpropane, (CH₃)₃CBr, is treated with ethanolic potassium hydroxide.

**(a)** Name the organic product. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why only one alkene is possible. **[2]**

**7.** 24.58 g of 2-bromopropane (Mᵣ = 122.9) reacts completely with hydroxide ions. 70 % of the molecules undergo elimination and the rest undergo substitution.

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in mol, of 2-bromopropane. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of propene (Mᵣ = 42.0) formed. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the volume of propene formed at room temperature and pressure. **[1]**

**(d)** Calculate the mass of propan-2-ol (Mᵣ = 60.0) formed. **[2]**

**8.** A student is given two samples, one of propan-2-ol and one of propene, from a reaction of 2-bromopropane. Describe a test that distinguishes them and state the result with each. **[2]**

**9.** A student heats 1-bromobutane with aqueous potassium hydroxide and obtains a lower yield of butan-1-ol than expected. Suggest, with an equation, one reason related to the reagent. **[2]**

**10.** Compare the reactions of 2-bromopropane with warm aqueous potassium hydroxide and with hot ethanolic potassium hydroxide. In your answer, give the main product and the role of the hydroxide ion in each case, and outline the first curly arrow of each mechanism. **[5]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Nucleophile [1]

**(b)** Base [1]

**(c)** CH₃CHBrCH₃ + OH⁻ → CH₃CH=CH₂ + H₂O + Br⁻ [1]. **[3]**

**2.** Potassium hydroxide dissolved in ethanol (ethanolic) [1]; heat under reflux (hot) [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Arrow from the lone pair on O of OH⁻ to a hydrogen on a carbon next to the C–Br carbon [1]; arrow from the C–H bond to the carbon–carbon bond, forming the C=C [1]; arrow from the C–Br bond to the bromine atom [1]; products propene, water and Br⁻ [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* If the first arrow goes to the carbon bonded to Br, the mechanism shown is substitution and the marks for elimination are lost.

**4. (a)** But-1-ene [1]; (E)-but-2-ene [1]; (Z)-but-2-ene [1]

**(b)** But-2-ene has a C=C with two different groups on each carbon (H and CH₃), so the groups can lie on the same side or opposite sides, giving stereoisomers [1]; but-1-ene has two hydrogens on one carbon of the double bond, so it has no stereoisomers [1]. **[5]**

**5.** The carbon bonded to Br (C1) has only one neighbouring carbon, C2 [1]; removing H from C2 can only give propene [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** 2-methylpropene [1]

**(b)** The carbon bonded to Br has three neighbouring carbons, all methyl groups [1]; removing a hydrogen from any of them gives the same alkene [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** n = 24.58 ÷ 122.9 = **0.2000 mol** [1]

**(b)** n(propene) = 0.70 × 0.2000 = 0.1400 mol [1]; mass = 0.1400 × 42.0 = **5.88 g** [1]

**(c)** V = 0.1400 × 24.0 = **3.36 dm³** [1]

**(d)** n(propan-2-ol) = 0.30 × 0.2000 = 0.0600 mol [1]; mass = 0.0600 × 60.0 = **3.60 g** [1]. **[6]**

**8.** Add bromine water to each [1]; propene decolourises it from orange to colourless, propan-2-ol gives no change [1]. **[2]**

**9.** Hydroxide also acts as a base [1]; some 1-bromobutane undergoes elimination, CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂Br + OH⁻ → CH₃CH₂CH=CH₂ + H₂O + Br⁻ [1]. **[2]**

**10.** With warm aqueous KOH the main product is propan-2-ol [1]; hydroxide acts as a nucleophile, with the first arrow from its lone pair to the δ+ carbon [1]. With hot ethanolic KOH the main product is propene [1]; hydroxide acts as a base, with the first arrow from its lone pair to a hydrogen on an adjacent carbon [1]. Both reactions occur to some extent in each case, the solvent and temperature changing the proportions [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Where the question says "compare", give a point about each side for each feature, not two separate descriptions with no link.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the carbon bonded to the halogen as the target of the first arrow in elimination.
- Leaving out water from the products.
- Missing the E/Z isomers of but-2-ene.
- Writing "aqueous" for the elimination conditions.
- Confusing a base with a nucleophile in the role description.
