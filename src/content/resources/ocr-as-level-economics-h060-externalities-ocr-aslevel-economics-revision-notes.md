These notes condense the specification requirement to explain, with the aid of a diagram, "Positive and negative externalities (external benefits and external costs); consumption and production". It is part of topic 2.7 Market failure and externalities, in Component 01, Microeconomics, of the OCR *AS Level Economics (H060)* specification, Version 3.0 (June 2026). Worked examples for all four cases are in the [externalities study guide](/resources/ocr-as-level-economics-h060-externalities-ocr-aslevel-economics/). Practise with the [externalities practice questions](/resources/ocr-as-level-economics-h060-externalities-ocr-aslevel-economics-practice/). All figures are invented.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Externality | Cost or benefit to a third party not reflected in the market price |
| Negative externality | External cost: harm to third parties |
| Positive externality | External benefit: gain to third parties |
| Production externality | Arises from producing the good; separates MSC from MPC |
| Consumption externality | Arises from consuming the good; separates MSB from MPB |
| Overproduction / overconsumption | Market output above the social optimum |
| Underproduction / underconsumption | Market output below the social optimum |
| Welfare loss | Triangle between MSB and MSC, from the optimum to the market output |

## Classify in two steps

1. Cost or benefit to the third party? Negative or positive.
2. Caused by producing or by consuming? Move the cost curve or the benefit curve.

| | Production | Consumption |
|---|---|---|
| **Negative** | MSC above MPC: overproduction (factory fumes, quarry dust) | MSB below MPB: overconsumption (loud music late at night, congestion from car use) |
| **Positive** | MSC below MPC: underproduction (firm training general skills, orchard helping beekeepers) | MSB above MPB: underconsumption (vaccination, education) |

## Diagram checklist

- Axes: price, cost and benefit (vertical); quantity (horizontal).
- Label every curve: MPB, MSB, MPC, MSC (write "MPB = MSB" if they coincide).
- Mark Q market (private curves cross) and Q optimum (social curves cross).
- Shade the welfare loss between MSB and MSC, from Q optimum to Q market.
- For a constant MEC or MEB, draw the social curve parallel to the private one.

## Worked reminders

**Negative production.** A paper mill: MPB = 120 − 3Q, MPC = 20 + 2Q, MEC = £15, so MSC = 35 + 2Q.
- Market: 120 − 3Q = 20 + 2Q, Q = **20**.
- Optimum: 120 − 3Q = 35 + 2Q, Q = **17**.
- Loss: ½ × 3 × 15 = **22.5**.

**Positive consumption.** Adult evening classes: MPB = 60 − 2Q, MEB = £9, so MSB = 69 − 2Q; MPC = MSC = 12 + Q.
- Market: 60 − 2Q = 12 + Q, Q = **16**.
- Optimum: 69 − 2Q = 12 + Q, Q = **19**.
- Loss: ½ × 3 × 9 = **13.5**.

Shortcut when the external effect is a constant amount per unit: **welfare loss = ½ × output gap × external cost or benefit per unit**.

## Contexts to use

| Context | Typical externality |
|---|---|
| Education | Positive consumption: productivity gains for employers and society |
| Health care and vaccination | Positive consumption: lower spread of disease |
| Transport | Negative consumption: congestion, fumes, accidents |
| Energy and manufacturing | Negative production: emissions, noise, waste |
| Research by firms | Positive production: knowledge used by other firms |

## Evaluation points

- External effects are hard to value in money, so the welfare loss is an estimate.
- External costs may rise with output, so a parallel MSC understates the loss.
- Third parties are often lower-income households, adding an equity issue.
- Effects may cross borders or build up over time.
- Some externalities are too small to be worth correcting.

## Exam traps

- A consumption externality never moves the cost curve.
- External cost: social curve **above** MPC (production) or **below** MPB (consumption).
- The positive externality welfare loss comes from units not produced.
- Name the third party in every explanation.
- Market price falls short of the full social cost when there is a negative production externality, so the optimum has a higher price.

## Self-test

1. Define an externality.
2. Classify the noise from a nightclub's customers leaving late at night.
3. Classify the benefit to a town when a firm restores an old building for its offices.
4. Which curve moves for a positive consumption externality, and in which direction?
5. MPC is £30 and MEC is £7. Find MSC.
6. MPB = 90 − 2Q, MPC = 10 + 2Q, MEC = £8. Find the market output and optimum.
7. In question 6, calculate the welfare loss.
8. MPB = 50 − Q, MEB = £6, MPC = MSC = 5 + 2Q. Find the market output and optimum.
9. In question 8, calculate the welfare loss.
10. Why is the market output of education likely to be too low?
11. Why might the welfare loss from pollution be larger than a parallel MSC diagram suggests?
12. Give one reason why it is hard to measure an external cost.

### Answers

1. A cost or benefit to a third party that is not reflected in the market price.
2. Negative consumption externality.
3. Positive production externality.
4. MSB, drawn above MPB.
5. 30 + 7 = **£37**.
6. Market: 90 − 2Q = 10 + 2Q, **Q = 20**. Optimum: 90 − 2Q = 18 + 2Q, **Q = 18**.
7. ½ × 2 × 8 = **8**.
8. Market: 50 − Q = 5 + 2Q, **Q = 15**. Optimum: 56 − Q = 5 + 2Q, **Q = 17**.
9. ½ × 2 × 6 = **6**.
10. Students weigh their own benefits, such as higher pay, and ignore benefits to others, such as higher productivity for employers and a better informed society, so MSB lies above MPB.
11. Damage per unit often rises as output grows, so MSC becomes steeper than MPC and the gap at the market output is larger.
12. Any one of: no market price exists for clean air or health; effects are spread over many people; effects appear years later; people value them differently.
