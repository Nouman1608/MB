## Questions

**1.** (Extension only) Two similar crates have matching lengths in the ratio 4 : 7. Write down the ratio of their surface areas and the ratio of their volumes. **[2]**

**2.** (Extension only) Two photo frames are similar. The smaller has area 96 cm² and width 12 cm. The larger has area 150 cm². Work out the width of the larger frame. **[3]**

**3.** (Extension only) Two tins are mathematically similar. Their heights are 8 cm and 14 cm. The larger tin holds 1715 cm³.

**(a)** Work out the volume of the smaller tin. **[3]**
**(b)** The paper label on the smaller tin has area 88 cm². Work out the area of the label on the larger tin. **[2]**

**4.** (Extension only) A sector has radius 15 cm and angle 48°. Give each answer in terms of π.

**(a)** Find the arc length. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the area of the sector. **[2]**

**5.** (Extension only) A sector has radius 12 cm and arc length 20 cm. Work out the angle of the sector, to 1 decimal place. **[3]**

**6.** (Extension only) A sector has angle 110° and area 55 cm².

**(a)** Work out the radius. **[3]**
**(b)** Work out the perimeter of the sector. **[3]**

**7.** (Extension only) A model of a statue is 24 cm tall. The real statue is 3.6 m tall. The model is similar to the real statue.

**(a)** The surface area of the model is 1500 cm². Work out the surface area of the real statue, in m². **[3]**
**(b)** The model holds 2400 cm³ of sand. Work out the volume of the real statue, in m³. **[3]**

**8.** (Extension only) Two vases are similar. The labels on them have areas 45 cm² and 80 cm². The smaller vase holds 405 ml. Work out the capacity of the larger vase. **[4]**

**9.** (Extension only) A garden bed is in the shape of a sector of a circle with radius 6.5 m and angle 140°.

**(a)** Work out its area. **[2]**
**(b)** Edging is fitted all the way round the bed. Work out the total length of edging. **[3]**

**10.** (Extension only) A fan-shaped window frame is made by taking a sector of radius 20 cm and angle 72°, and removing from it a concentric sector of radius 8 cm and the same angle.

**(a)** Show that the area of the frame is 67.2π cm². **[3]**
**(b)** Work out the total length of the frame's outer boundary, which has two arcs and two straight edges. **[4]**

**11.** (Extension only) Sector P has radius 8 cm and angle 135°. Sector Q has the same angle and is similar to P. The radius of Q is 14 cm.

**(a)** Find the arc length of P, in terms of π. **[2]**
**(b)** Write down the scale factor from P to Q and find the arc length of Q, in terms of π. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the area of Q to 4 significant figures, using area P = 24π cm² in your working. **[3]**

**12.** (Extension only) A pendulum has length 85 cm and swings through an angle of 24° at its lowest point.

**(a)** Work out the length of the arc travelled by the end of the pendulum. **[2]**
**(b)** On a different swing, the end travels along an arc of 50 cm. Work out the angle of the swing, to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Area ratio = 4² : 7² = 16 : 49 [1]. Volume ratio = 4³ : 7³ = **64 : 343** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* write the squared and cubed numbers separately, because it is easy to give 8 : 14 for an area ratio by only doubling the lengths.

**2.** Area ratio = 150 : 96 = 25 : 16 [1]. Length ratio = √25 : √16 = 5 : 4, so k = 5/4 [1]. Width = 12 × 5/4 = **15 cm** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* square-rooting the area ratio is the key step here. If you multiply 12 by the area scale factor instead, the working shows which idea you used.

**3. (a)** k = 14/8 = 7/4 [1]. Volume factor from small to large = (7/4)³ = 343/64 [1]. Small volume = 1715 × 64/343 = **320 cm³** [1].
**(b)** Area factor = (7/4)² = 49/16 [1]. Label = 88 × 49/16 = **269.5 cm²** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* the larger figure is given here, so you must divide by the volume factor (or multiply by its reciprocal). A smaller tin with the bigger volume is a signal that something went the wrong way round.

**4. (a)** Fraction = 48/360 = 2/15 [1]. Arc = 2/15 × 2π × 15 = **4π cm** [1].
**(b)** Area = 2/15 × π × 225 [1] = **30π cm²** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* when the answer is wanted in terms of π, cancelling the fraction first and leaving π unevaluated gives a clean exact answer. A decimal can lose credit if the question asked for π.

**5.** Circumference = 2π × 12 = 24π [1]. θ = 20/(24π) × 360 [1] = 95.49... so **95.5°** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* the fraction of the circle is arc ÷ circumference. Writing that fraction down before multiplying by 360 shows the method even if a keying slip changes the final value.

**6. (a)** 55 = 110/360 × π × r² [1]. r² = 55 × 360 / (110π) = 180/π = 57.29... [1]. r = 7.569... so **7.57 cm** [1].
**(b)** Arc = 110/360 × 2π × 7.569... = 14.53... [1]. Perimeter = arc + 2r [1] = 14.53 + 15.14 = **29.7 cm** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* keep the unrounded radius for part (b). Using 7.57 can nudge the third significant figure, and the perimeter needs both radii as well as the arc.

**7. (a)** Real height = 360 cm, so k = 360 ÷ 24 = 15 [1]. Area factor = 15² = 225 [1]. Real area = 1500 × 225 = 337 500 cm² = **33.75 m²** [1].
**(b)** Volume factor = 15³ = 3375 [1]. Real volume = 2400 × 3375 = 8 100 000 cm³ [1] = **8.1 m³** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* the lengths are in different units, so convert before dividing. A final conversion from cm² to m² uses 10 000, and from cm³ to m³ uses 1 000 000.

**8.** Area ratio = 45 : 80 = 9 : 16 [1]. Length ratio = 3 : 4, so k = 4/3 [1]. Volume factor = 64/27 [1]. Capacity = 405 × 64/27 = **960 ml** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* this question needs two changes of power in one chain: area to length, then length to volume. Writing each ratio on its own line makes the route clear.

**9. (a)** Area = 140/360 × π × 6.5² [1] = **51.6 m²** [1].
**(b)** Arc = 140/360 × 2π × 6.5 = 15.88... [1]. Straight edges = 2 × 6.5 = 13 [1]. Total = 15.88 + 13 = **28.9 m** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "all the way round" signals the perimeter. The arc alone is a common answer, so state clearly that you are adding the two radii.

**10. (a)** Area = 72/360 × π × (20² − 8²) [1]. = 1/5 × π × 336 [1] = 67.2π, as required [1].
**(b)** Outer arc = 72/360 × 2π × 20 = 8π [1]. Inner arc = 72/360 × 2π × 8 = 3.2π [1]. Straight edges = 2 × (20 − 8) = 24 [1]. Total = 11.2π + 24 = **59.2 cm** [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* on a "show that" question, the line with the exact result must appear with the working that leads to it. A bare statement of 67.2π is unlikely to earn the final step.

**11. (a)** Fraction = 135/360 = 3/8 [1]. Arc P = 3/8 × 16π = **6π cm** [1].
**(b)** k = 14/8 = **1.75** [1]. Arc Q = 6π × 1.75 = **10.5π cm** [1].
**(c)** Area factor = 1.75² = 3.0625 [1]. Area Q = 24π × 3.0625 = 73.5π [1] = **230.9 cm²** (4 s.f.) [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* sectors with the same angle are similar, so scale the arc by k and the area by k². You can check the answer by working out area Q directly from 3/8 × π × 14².

**12. (a)** Arc = 24/360 × 2π × 85 [1] = **35.6 cm** [1].
**(b)** θ = 50/(2π × 85) × 360 [1] = 33.70... so **33.7°** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* the radius is the pendulum length, not the diameter. Check that the angle in part (b) is bigger than in part (a) when the arc is longer.

## Where marks are usually lost

* Scaling an area by k instead of k², or a volume by k instead of k³.
* Forgetting to take a square root or cube root when the question gives areas or volumes and asks for a length.
* Dividing the wrong way round, so a larger figure ends up with a smaller answer.
* Leaving the two radii out of the perimeter of a sector.
* Using the diameter in a sector formula when the formula needs the radius.
* Rounding the radius or the arc early and then losing accuracy in the final answer.
* Giving a decimal when the question asks for an answer in terms of π, or the other way round.
* Forgetting to convert cm² to m² or cm³ to m³, or using 100 or 1000 as the conversion.
* Writing only a final answer on a multi-step or "show that" question, so the method cannot be seen.
