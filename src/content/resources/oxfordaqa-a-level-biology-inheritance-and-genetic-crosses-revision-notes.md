## 3.3.6.1 Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Genotype | The alleles an organism carries (its genetic constitution) |
| Phenotype | What is expressed: the genotype's effect plus the environment's |
| Allele | A version of a gene |
| Locus | Where a gene sits on a chromosome |
| Homozygous | Both alleles at the locus are the same |
| Heterozygous | The two alleles at the locus differ |
| Dominant | Shows in the heterozygote's phenotype |
| Recessive | Hidden in a heterozygote; shows only without a dominant allele |
| Codominant | Each allele shows in the heterozygote's phenotype |

A population can hold many alleles of a gene. A diploid individual carries two.

Environment can change the phenotype of a fixed genotype. Example: a plant with alleles for tallness grows shorter if short of nitrates.

## Method: a fully labelled genetic diagram

1. Key (letters, dominance).
2. Parental phenotypes and genotypes.
3. Gametes: one allele (or one allele of each gene) per gamete.
4. Offspring genotypes (use a grid if there are more than two gamete types).
5. Phenotype for each genotype, then the ratio.

## Ratios to recognise

| Parents | Offspring ratio | Clue |
|---|---|---|
| Aa × Aa (complete dominance) | 3 : 1 | Both parents show the dominant trait and some offspring do not |
| Aa × aa (test cross) | 1 : 1 | Used to find an unknown genotype |
| Codominant heterozygotes × each other | 1 : 2 : 1 | Heterozygote has its own phenotype |
| AaBb × AaBb, independent genes | 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 | Dihybrid |
| AaBb × aabb, independent genes | 1 : 1 : 1 : 1 | Dihybrid test cross |
| Linked genes in a test cross | Two large and two small classes | Recombinants are the small classes |
| Recessive epistasis | 9 : 3 : 4 | Two classes merged |
| Dominant epistasis | 12 : 3 : 1 | Two classes merged |
| Complementary genes | 9 : 7 | Both genes needed |

Check that the total is 16 for a two-gene cross between double heterozygotes.

## Multiple alleles

- Possible genotypes = n(n + 1)/2 for n alleles: 6 for three alleles, 10 for four.
- ABO: Iᴬ and Iᴮ codominant, i recessive. Group O is ii; group AB is IᴬIᴮ.
- Dominance series: write the order (for example a1 > a2 > a3) and apply it to each genotype before drawing the grid.

## Sex-linkage

- Humans and *Drosophila*: XX female, XY male. Birds: ZZ male, ZW female.
- The sex with one copy (XY male; ZW female) expresses whichever allele it has.
- X-linked recessive: an affected daughter has an affected father and a carrier or affected mother. A son gets his X from his mother only.
- X-linked dominant: an affected father passes it to all daughters and no sons.
- Reciprocal crosses give different results for a sex-linked gene and the same for an autosomal gene.
- Carrier mother × unaffected father (X-linked recessive): half the sons affected, no daughters affected, half the daughters carriers.

## Autosomal linkage

- Linked genes lie on the same chromosome and are inherited together.
- Parental combinations are the commonest classes; recombinants arise by crossing over in meiosis.
- Recombination frequency = recombinant offspring ÷ total offspring × 100.
- Loci that are close together give few recombinants. Independent assortment would give 50% recombinants in a test cross, because all four classes would be equal.

## Epistasis

- One gene masks or modifies the expression of another gene at a different locus.
- Dominance acts between alleles of the same gene; epistasis acts between genes.
- State the biochemical idea where you can: for example, an enzyme that makes a precursor is missing, so a later gene has nothing to act on.

## The chi-squared test

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | State the null hypothesis: any difference from the expected ratio is due to chance |
| 2 | Work out expected counts from the ratio |
| 3 | χ² = Σ (O − E)² / E |
| 4 | Degrees of freedom = categories − 1 |
| 5 | Critical value at p = 0.05: df 1 is 3.84, df 2 is 5.99, df 3 is 7.81, df 4 is 9.49 |
| 6 | Result below the critical value: not significant, null hypothesis stands. Equal or above: significant, reject the null hypothesis |

Use counts, never percentages. Include every category. A significant result means the ratio does not fit; it does not say why.

**Reminder.** Fowl blue × blue, 80 chicks, observed 17 black : 45 blue : 18 splashed white. Expected 20 : 40 : 20. χ² = 0.45 + 0.625 + 0.2 = 1.275, so 1.28. With df = 2 the critical value is 5.99, so the result is not significant and fits 1 : 2 : 1.

## Worked reminders

**Sex-linked cross.** Assume an X-linked recessive allele Xᵃ causes a condition. Carrier mother XᴬXᵃ × unaffected father XᴬY.

- Gametes: mother Xᴬ or Xᵃ; father Xᴬ or Y.
- Offspring: XᴬXᴬ unaffected girl, XᴬXᵃ carrier girl, XᴬY unaffected boy, XᵃY affected boy.
- Probability a child is affected: 1/4. Probability a son is affected: 1/2.

**Dihybrid expectation.** AaBb × AaBb with 480 offspring, independent genes: 9/16 × 480 = 270 double dominant, 3/16 × 480 = 90 for each single-recessive class, 1/16 × 480 = 30 double recessive.

**Linkage.** Test cross of a double heterozygote CD / cd with cd / cd. Parental classes (CD and cd) are large; recombinant classes (Cd and cD) are small and about equal, because crossing over gives each reciprocal product equally often.

**Epistasis.** If a pp genotype blocks pigment, every pp plant is white whether it is RR, Rr or rr. In PpRr × PpRr, four of the 16 boxes (one ppRR, two ppRr and one pprr) merge into one white class, so the ratio is 9 coloured with R : 3 coloured with rr : 4 white.

## Model answer shapes

- **A question asks why a dihybrid ratio is unusual:** name the pattern (linkage or epistasis), say what the observed classes show, link it to the mechanism.
- **Explain a sex-linked result:** state which sex has the single allele, say sons or daughters inherit from which parent, and compare with an autosomal prediction.
- **Interpret a chi-squared result:** give χ², degrees of freedom, critical value, comparison, then a statement about the null hypothesis in the context of the cross.

## Must-know distinctions

- Genotype versus phenotype. Codominance versus incomplete dominance: the second is not named in the specification, so describe what you see.
- Linkage versus sex-linkage: linkage is two genes on one chromosome; sex-linkage is a gene on a sex chromosome.
- Epistasis versus dominance.
- Recombinant versus parental offspring classes.

## Quick self-test

1. Define phenotype.
2. A heterozygote is crossed with a homozygous recessive. What ratio do you expect?
3. A species has four alleles of one gene. How many genotypes are possible?
4. 160 offspring from a cross expected to give 9 coloured : 7 white are 103 coloured and 57 white. Calculate χ² and say if it fits.
5. A test cross gives 24, 27, 25 and 24 in four classes. Test against 1 : 1 : 1 : 1.
6. A carrier woman has a child with an unaffected man (X-linked recessive). What is the probability that a son is affected?
7. A test cross gives 500 offspring, of which 60 are recombinants. Give the recombination frequency.
8. State why X-linked recessive conditions are commoner in males.
9. A two-gene cross gives 12 : 3 : 1. Name the pattern.
10. Give the degrees of freedom for a test on a 9 : 3 : 4 ratio.

### Answers

1. The expression of the genotype and its interaction with the environment.
2. 1 : 1.
3. 4 × 5 / 2 = 10.
4. Expected 90 and 70. χ² = (13²/90) + (13²/70) = 1.878 + 2.414 = 4.29. One degree of freedom, critical value 3.84. Greater than 3.84, so significant: the data do not fit 9 : 7.
5. Total 100, expected 25 each. χ² = 0.04 + 0.16 + 0 + 0.04 = 0.24. Three degrees of freedom, critical value 7.81. Not significant: consistent with independent assortment.
6. 1/2.
7. 60/500 × 100 = 12%.
8. A male has one X, so a single recessive allele is expressed. A female needs two.
9. Dominant epistasis.
10. 3 categories − 1 = 2.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out the key, so the examiner cannot tell which allele is dominant.
- Putting a whole genotype in a gamete.
- Giving a ratio without the phenotypes it refers to.
- Treating a recombination frequency as an expected count rather than the proportion of recombinants.
- Applying chi-squared to percentages.
- Using the total number of individuals instead of the number of categories − 1.
- Writing "the null hypothesis is accepted" or "proved" when the wording should be "not rejected".
- Not saying what the significant difference means for the cross, for example linkage or epistasis.
