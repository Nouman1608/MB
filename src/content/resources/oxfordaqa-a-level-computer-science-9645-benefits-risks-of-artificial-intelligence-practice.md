These questions practise sub-topic **3.16.3 Benefits and risks of artificial intelligence** of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Computer Science (9645), Version 1.1: the benefits of AI (consistent decision making, fast analysis of large data sets, continuous availability, lower cost, wider access) and its risks (job losses, bias, false information, plagiarism, surveillance and superintelligent systems). The figures are invented for practice.

## Section A

**1.** State **three** benefits of using artificial intelligence. **[3]**

**2.** State **three** risks of using artificial intelligence. **[3]**

**3.** Explain what is meant by **plagiarism** in the context of generative AI. **[2]**

## Section B

**4.** A finance department must check 250,000 invoices. A clerk can check 50 invoices per hour. An AI system can check 100 invoices per second.

**(a)** Calculate the number of hours of clerk time needed. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the time the AI system needs, in minutes to the nearest minute. **[2]**
**(c)** State which benefit of AI this shows. **[1]**

**5.** Handling a customer case costs £3.20 with a member of staff and £0.08 with an AI system. The company handles 40,000 cases.

**(a)** Calculate the saving from using the AI system for all the cases. **[2]**
**(b)** Give **one** reason the real saving may be smaller than your answer. **[1]**

**6.** A university used past admission decisions to train an AI system. Of 300 applicants from region R1, 150 were offered places. Of 100 applicants from region R2, 30 were offered places.

**(a)** Calculate the offer rate for each region. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how a system trained on this data could show bias. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe **one** step that would reduce the risk of bias. **[1]**

**7.** A factory has 500 workers. Robots controlled by AI take over 35% of the work.

**(a)** Calculate the number of workers whose work is no longer needed. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe **two** social impacts of this change. **[2]**

**8.** An AI system answers 10,000 questions per day. 3% of its answers contain false information because some of its training data was incorrect.

**(a)** Calculate the number of false answers per day and in 30 days. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why false answers from an AI system can be harder to spot than errors from a person. **[2]**

**9.** A city plans to use AI to analyse images from 1,500 cameras that record for 24 hours a day.

**(a)** Calculate the number of hours of footage per day, and the number of people needed to watch it all in real time on 8-hour shifts. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe **one** benefit and **two** risks of this use of AI. **[3]**

**10.** A hospital is considering using an AI system to help diagnose disease from scans. Discuss the benefits and risks of this decision. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any three of: improved and more consistent decision making **[1]**; the ability to analyse very large data sets quickly **[1]**; continuous availability **[1]**; lower cost of operation **[1]**; availability to more people and in areas where human expertise is not readily available **[1]**. **[3]**

**2.** Any three of: elimination of jobs and the social impact of this **[1]**; bias in decision making **[1]**; false information if the training data is incorrect **[1]**; plagiarism **[1]**; use in surveillance systems **[1]**; risk to human existence from superintelligent systems **[1]**. **[3]**

**3.** Plagiarism is presenting work produced by an AI system as your own **[1]** (or using AI output that copies other people's work without acknowledging the source) **[1]**. **[2]**

**4. (a)** 250,000 / 50 = **5,000 hours** **[1]**
**(b)** 250,000 / 100 = 2,500 seconds **[1]**; 2,500 / 60 = 41.7, so **42 minutes** **[1]**. **[2]**
**(c)** The ability to analyse very large data sets quickly **[1]**.

**5. (a)** Staff cost 40,000 × 3.20 = £128,000 **[1]**; AI cost 40,000 × 0.08 = £3,200; saving = **£124,800** **[1]**. **[2]**
**(b)** The system costs money to develop, train, maintain and supervise, or some cases still need staff **[1]**.

**6. (a)** R1: 150 / 300 = **50%** **[1]**; R2: 30 / 100 = **30%** **[1]**. **[2]**
**(b)** The past decisions gave region R2 a lower offer rate **[1]**; the system learns to copy this pattern, so it would favour region R1 applicants even if they were no better qualified **[1]**. **[2]**
**(c)** Any one of: use training data that is representative and free from past unfairness; test the results separately for each region; keep human review of decisions **[1]**.

**7. (a)** 500 × 0.35 = **175** workers **[1]**
**(b)** Any two of: unemployment **[1]**; lower incomes in the affected community **[1]**; the need for retraining into new roles **[1]**; new jobs may need different skills or appear in different places **[1]**. **[2]**

**8. (a)** 10,000 × 0.03 = **300** per day **[1]**; 300 × 30 = **9,000** in 30 days **[1]**. **[2]**
**(b)** An AI system gives answers in a fluent, confident way that does not show its uncertainty **[1]**; users may trust the output as accurate because it comes from a computer, so mistakes can spread without being checked **[1]**. **[2]**

**9. (a)** 1,500 × 24 = **36,000 hours** per day **[1]**; 36,000 / 8 = **4,500 people** **[1]**. **[2]**
**(b)** Benefit (any one): faster detection of incidents or missing people **[1]**. Risks (any two): loss of privacy **[1]**; people could be monitored without their knowledge **[1]**; misidentification could lead to innocent people being suspected **[1]**; bias in recognition could affect some groups more **[1]**. **[3]**

**10.** Award up to **[6]**: up to **[2]** for benefits with reasons, for example more consistent and possibly more accurate analysis of scans **[1]**, or quick analysis of many scans, availability at any hour, or access to expertise where specialists are scarce **[1]**; up to **[2]** for risks with reasons, for example bias if the training data under-represents some patients so the system works less well for them **[1]**, or false negatives and false positives affecting patients, over-reliance on a system that works only in a narrow field, or the impact on jobs **[1]**; **[1]** for applying the points to the hospital scenario (scans, diagnosis, patients); and **[1]** for a conclusion that weighs the points, for example that the system should support clinicians who make the final decision, with representative training data and regular checking. **[6]**
