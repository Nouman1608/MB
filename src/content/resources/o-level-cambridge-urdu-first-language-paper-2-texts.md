This guide covers **Paper 2 Texts**, one of two components of
Cambridge O Level First Language Urdu 3247, 2027 series (Version 1,
no significant changes affecting teaching; the syllabus has been
reformatted only). This resource covers 3247 First Language Urdu
specifically -- 3248 Second Language Urdu is a distinct specification
with its own Paper 2.

## Where this fits in 3247

Paper 1 (covered separately) is Reading and Writing, tested through a
non-literary passage and an original composition. Paper 2 Texts moves
to literature: an unseen extract, plus prescribed poetry and prose
set texts, together worth 50% of the qualification and assessed
entirely through Assessment Objective 3, Literature (texts).

## Syllabus coverage

**CAMBRIDGE O LEVEL FIRST LANGUAGE URDU 3247 — PAPER 2 TEXTS**

- Part 1 Unseen Passage (20 marks) — one passage-based question on an
  extract of poetry or prose not seen before, testing critical
  understanding of the text and the ways the writer achieves effects,
  guided by prompts in the question
- Part 2 Poetry (15 marks) — two essay questions based on the
  prescribed ghazals and nazms; candidates answer one. Set poets
  include Khwaja Haider Ali Ahtish, Parveen Shakir, Momin Khan Momin,
  Allama Iqbal and Ahmed Faraz (ten prescribed ghazals in total), plus
  three prescribed nazms: Altaf Hussain Hali's Musadas Hali (17
  selected stanzas), Syed Zameer Jafree's "Admee", and Mirza Mahmood
  Sarhadi's "Qitaat"
- Part 3 Prose (15 marks) — two essay questions based on eight
  prescribed prose articles; candidates answer one. Set writers
  include Muhammad Hussain Azad, Mirza Farhatullah Baig, Ghulam
  Abbas, Ahmad Nadeem Qasmi, Sir Syed Ahmed Khan, Mushtaq Ahmed
  Yousafi, Syed Imtiaz Ali Taj, and two of Mirza Ghalib's letters
  (Khatoot-e-Ghalib)

All three parts test Assessment Objective 3 (Literature): first-hand
knowledge of the text, understanding of literal and deeper meanings,
recognising how writers use language to create effect, and
communicating an informed, personal response.

## How to approach it

Part 1's Unseen Passage cannot be prepared by memorising a text, so
build the *skill* instead: practise reading unfamiliar Urdu poetry
and prose extracts and identifying how the writer creates effect
(word choice, imagery, structure), using the prompts in past
questions as a model for the kind of observation examiners expect.
For Parts 2 and 3, close, memorised knowledge of the prescribed
ghazals, nazms and prose pieces is essential, since both are assessed
through essay questions requiring direct textual reference and
quotation -- a response without specific reference to the text, even
if fluently written, will not reach the top levels of the mark
scheme. Because candidates choose one question from two in both Part
2 and Part 3, make sure your preparation covers enough of the
prescribed list that you are never left with only one usable option
on the day.

## What the mark schemes reward

Across all three parts of Paper 2, the levels-based mark schemes
reward the same underlying qualities in increasing depth: a personal,
informed response to the text; understanding that goes beyond literal
surface meaning to deeper implications; explicit engagement with how
the writer achieves their effects (word choice, imagery, structure,
tone); and close, well-selected reference to the text itself, not
paraphrase alone. The highest levels in both the Unseen Passage and
the Poetry/Prose mark schemes explicitly require sustained,
perceptive engagement with the writer's craft, not just accurate
comprehension.

## The set texts at a glance

| Part | Marks | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Unseen Passage | 20 | An extract not seen before (poetry or prose) |
| 2 Poetry | 15 | Ten prescribed ghazals and three prescribed nazms |
| 3 Prose | 15 | Eight prescribed prose articles, including two of Ghalib's letters |

Because Parts 2 and 3 are set-text papers, candidates should treat
each prescribed poem or article as something to know in real depth --
its themes, its language, and at least a few precise quotations that
can be deployed to support an argument -- rather than something to
recognise in outline only.

## Worked example

A Part 2 Poetry question asks candidates to discuss how a named poet
conveys a particular emotion or theme in one of the prescribed
ghazals. A strong answer identifies specific lines (sher) from the
poem, explains the imagery or word choice used, and connects that
language choice explicitly to the emotion or theme named in the
question -- rather than summarising the poem's general subject matter
without close reference to its actual wording.

## Common mistakes

Answering Part 1's Unseen Passage question as a simple comprehension
exercise, without engaging with *how* the writer creates effect.
Writing about a prescribed poem or prose piece from general memory of
its "story" without quoting or closely referencing the actual text.
Choosing a question in Part 2 or Part 3 based on which text is
easiest to summarise rather than which the candidate knows in the
most depth. Running out of time by spending too long on Part 1 and
leaving insufficient time for the two essay-based parts.

## Quick revision checklist

- Practise unseen extracts regularly, focusing on identifying and
  explaining the writer's technique, not just meaning.
- Know at least six of the ten prescribed ghazals and two of the
  three prescribed nazms in close textual detail, including specific
  lines to quote.
- Know at least five of the eight prescribed prose articles in close
  textual detail, including precise reference points.
- Practise writing essay answers that combine a clear personal
  response with sustained, specific textual reference.
- Time each part of Paper 2 in practice so no single part crowds out
  the others in the real exam.
