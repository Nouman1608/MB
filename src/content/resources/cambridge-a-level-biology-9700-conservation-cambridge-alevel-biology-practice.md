## Section A

**1.** (18.3.1) Explain how hunting by humans can cause the extinction of a large mammal that produces one offspring every three years. **[2]**

**2.** (18.3.2) Outline three different reasons for maintaining biodiversity. **[3]**

**3.** (18.3.3) Outline the role of seed banks in the conservation of endangered plant species. **[3]**

**4.** (18.3.4) Distinguish between IVF and embryo transfer. **[2]**

**5.** (18.3.6) State the main role of the IUCN and the main role of CITES. **[2]**

**6.** (18.3.5) Suggest why the numbers of an invasive alien species often rise rapidly in its new area. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** (18.3.1, 18.3.3) The table shows estimated numbers of a grassland antelope. During this period much of its habitat was converted to farmland, leaving several small, separate areas.

| Year | 1990 | 2000 | 2010 | 2020 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Estimated number | 4800 | 2100 | 640 | 210 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease from 1990 to 2020. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the remaining antelope are at greater risk of extinction than the 1990 population. **[4]**

**(c)** Suggest two conservation measures that could help this species. **[2]**

**8.** (18.3.3, 18.3.4) Only eight females of an endangered wild cat species remain in zoos.

**(a)** Describe how IVF, embryo transfer and surrogacy could be used to increase the number of offspring. **[5]**

**(b)** Explain how a frozen zoo could increase the genetic diversity of the captive population. **[2]**

**9.** (18.3.3) A zoo plans to breed an endangered bird and release it into the wild.

**(a)** Explain why the zoo keeps records of the ancestry of every bird. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest three reasons why the released birds might fail to establish a wild population. **[3]**

**10.** (18.3.5) Rats were introduced accidentally to an island where several seabird species nest on the ground. Before the rats were removed, 12% of nests produced a fledgling; five years after removal, 68% did.

**(a)** Calculate how many times greater nesting success was after removal. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain reasons for controlling invasive alien species, using this example and one other. **[4]**

**11.** (18.3.3) A marine park bans all fishing in one zone. Explain how this can benefit fish populations both inside and outside the zone. **[3]**

**12.** (18.3.1) Explain how climate change could cause a plant species that grows only near the tops of mountains to become extinct. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Adults are killed faster than they are replaced by reproduction [1]; with a low reproductive rate the population cannot recover, so numbers fall each generation until none remain [1].

**2.** Any three distinct kinds of reason, one mark each [3]:
- Ethical: species have a right to exist.
- Ecosystem services, such as pollination or nutrient cycling.
- Stable food webs: loss of one species affects others.
- Resources: food, medicines, alleles in wild crop relatives.
- Economic: ecotourism.

**3.** Seeds are collected from many plants/populations to capture genetic diversity [1]; dried and stored at low temperature so they remain viable for many years [1]; germination tested and plants regrown for reintroduction or research [1].

**4.** IVF is fertilisation of eggs by sperm outside the body, in a dish [1]. Embryo transfer is placing the resulting embryo into the uterus of a female [1].

**5.** IUCN: assesses the conservation status of species / publishes the Red List [1]. CITES: controls international trade in endangered species and their products [1].

**6.** It has no natural predators, parasites or pathogens in the new area [1]; it may out-compete native species for resources, or reproduce quickly [1].

**7. (a)** (4800 − 210) ÷ 4800 × 100 [1] = **95.6%** [1].

**(b)** Habitat loss leaves small, isolated populations with no gene flow between them [1]. Small populations have low genetic diversity [1]. Genetic drift and inbreeding increase, so harmful recessive alleles are expressed [1]. With fewer alleles the antelope are less able to survive disease or environmental change, and a single event could kill a whole small group [1].

**(c)** Any two [2]: protect remaining habitat as a conserved area / national park; create corridors linking fragments; captive breeding in zoos with reintroduction; move individuals between fragments; assisted reproduction using stored sperm; anti-poaching measures.

**8. (a)** Females are given hormones to stimulate the development of several eggs [1]. Eggs are collected [1] and fertilised with sperm in a dish (IVF) [1]. Embryos are cultured and then transferred into the uterus of a hormone-prepared female (embryo transfer) [1]. A surrogate, possibly a female of a related, more common cat species, carries the pregnancy, so the endangered female can produce more eggs [1].

**(b)** Sperm or embryos from unrelated animals, including those that died years ago or live in other countries, are stored in liquid nitrogen [1]; using them introduces alleles not present in the eight females' relatives, reducing inbreeding [1].

**9. (a)** To choose pairs that are not closely related [1], maintaining genetic diversity and avoiding inbreeding [1].

**(b)** Any three [3]: original threat (hunting, habitat loss, predators) still present; birds lack natural behaviours such as finding food or avoiding predators; not enough suitable habitat; disease; too few released to form a viable population; competition with established species.

**10. (a)** 68 ÷ 12 = **5.7 times** [1].

**(b)** Invasive species can prey on native species, as rats eat eggs and chicks of ground-nesting birds [1]. They often have no natural predators, so their numbers rise [1]. Other example, with harm, e.g. grey squirrels out-compete red squirrels and carry squirrelpox; cane toads poison native predators [1]. Control allows native populations to recover and protects biodiversity / prevents extinction [1].

**11.** Inside: no fish are removed, so more survive to reproduce and breeding adults grow larger [1]; habitat is undamaged by fishing gear [1]. Outside: fish and larvae move out of the zone, increasing stocks in surrounding fished waters [1].

**12.** Temperatures rise, so conditions near the summit are no longer within the species' tolerance range [1]. The plant cannot move to a cooler area because there is no higher ground [1]. Species from lower altitudes move upwards and compete with it [1]. Its population falls each generation, or it fails to reproduce, until it becomes extinct [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating a cause of extinction with no mechanism.
- Confusing in-place and elsewhere conservation.
- Saying the surrogate's eggs are used.
- Mixing up IUCN and CITES.
- Quoting percentage decrease from the final value instead of the starting value.

## Frequently asked questions

**How are these questions organised?**
Section A has short questions; Section B uses unfamiliar examples and data. The learning outcome is in brackets at the start of each question.

**Do I need named examples of invasive species?**
The syllabus asks you to explain reasons for control, so any accurate example helps. Make sure each example is linked to a specific harm, such as competition, predation or disease.
