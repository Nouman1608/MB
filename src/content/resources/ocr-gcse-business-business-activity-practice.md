## Questions

**1.** Identify **two** characteristics of an entrepreneur. **[2]**

**2.** Ingrid Voss leaves her job to start Saffrondale Candles, selling hand-poured candles at craft fairs. She noticed that no shop in her area stocks them. Explain **one** risk and **one** reward for Ingrid of starting the business. **[4]**

**3.** Ingrid wants a £6 000 bank loan for equipment. Explain **one** way a business plan could help her to obtain it. **[3]**

**4.** Gideon Hallowby is a sole trader who runs Torrenby Signs. The business owes £38 000 and its assets are worth £29 500.

**(a)** Calculate the shortfall. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain what unlimited liability means for Gideon. **[2]**
**(c)** A friend invested £4 000 in a private limited company that has also failed. Explain the most she can lose. **[2]**

**5.** Oakmoor Textiles has sales of £0.78 million in a market worth £6.5 million.

**(a)** Calculate its market share. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why a business may aim for survival when it starts, but market share later. **[3]**

**6.** A community repair workshop run by volunteers and a plc both operate in the same town. Explain why their objectives may differ. **[3]**

**7.** Oakmoor Textiles plans a night shift, which would mean lorries arriving after 10 pm.

**(a)** Identify **one** internal and **one** external stakeholder of Oakmoor Textiles. **[2]**
**(b)** Analyse the effect of the night shift on the local community. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain **one** way employees could affect Oakmoor Textiles. **[2]**

**8.** Kelvedon Chutneys raised output from 5 400 jars a month to 6 480 jars a month.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in output. **[2]**
**(b)** State **two** other ways in which Kelvedon Chutneys could grow organically. **[2]**

**9.** Kelvedon Chutneys is considering external growth.

**(a)** It buys a company that makes glass jars. Identify the type of growth. **[1]**
**(b)** It buys a rival chutney maker. Identify the type of growth. **[1]**
**(c)** It buys a company that sells garden tools. Identify the type of growth. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain the difference between a merger and a takeover. **[2]**

**10.** Stenmoor Brewing sells £2.4 million a year and holds £0.3 million in cash. A rival brewery is for sale at £1.8 million, and the owners want a bigger market share quickly. Evaluate whether Stenmoor Brewing should grow organically or by taking over the rival. **[9]**

**11.** Pruett and Aske, two friends, plan a catering business. Each can invest £15 000, but they will need supplier credit of £50 000 in the first year and a bank loan. They want to protect their personal savings. Recommend whether they should be a partnership or a private limited company. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two from **creativity**, **risk taking**, **determination**, **confidence** [1] [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* An identify question usually needs only the name of each characteristic, so a sentence of explanation tends to add nothing and uses time better spent elsewhere.

**2.** Risk: Ingrid gives up her **regular pay** and may lose her **start-up money** if sales at fairs are poor [1] [1]. Reward: she may earn **profit** from a gap in the market that no local shop fills, and she is her own boss [1] [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers tend to gain credit when each point is tied to Ingrid and her candles. A bare "she could lose money" is a weaker response than one that says which money and why.

**3.** The plan sets out the **market**, such as craft-fair buyers and expected sales [1]. A bank can then see that demand has been thought through [1], so it is more confident that Ingrid can repay the £6 000 and is more likely to lend [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Stopping at "it helps get finance" repeats the question. The development, which is why the lender cares, is where the extra credit usually sits.

**4. (a)** Shortfall = 38 000 - 29 500 [1] = **£8 500** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Unlimited liability means Gideon's **personal assets**, such as savings, can be used [1] to pay the £8 500 the business cannot cover [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** She can lose no more than the **£4 000** she invested [1], because limited liability keeps her personal assets separate from the company's debts [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the subtraction, and keep the £ sign. Part (b) reads best when it uses Gideon's figure, not a general definition alone.

**5. (a)** Market share = 0.78 ÷ 6.5 × 100 [1] = **12%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** A new business has no customers, so its first aim is to **stay in business** and cover costs [1]. Once it is established and survival is secure, it can afford to chase more customers [1], so the objective **changes as the business evolves** towards winning a larger share than rivals [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Both sales figures must be in the same units, here millions. Part (b) needs a reason for the change, not just two objectives listed.

**6.** The workshop's objective is likely to be **providing a service** to the community, not profit [1]. The plc has shareholders who expect **profit and growth** [1]. Objectives differ because of the **ownership, motives and type of business** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming two objectives is knowledge. The reason they differ is what turns a list into an explanation.

**7. (a)** Internal: **owners or employees**. External: **customers, suppliers, government or the local community**. [1] [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Lorries arriving after 10 pm cause **noise** [1], which can disturb sleep [1], so the community may complain and the business's image may suffer [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** Employees could **choose to leave** if they dislike night work [1], so Oakmoor would face recruitment costs and lost skills [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* An analyse answer gains from a chain, with each step leading to the next. Check that part (a) is not both internal or both external.

**8. (a)** (6 480 - 5 400) ÷ 5 400 × 100 [1] = **20%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Any two from **gaining new customers**, **developing new products**, **increasing market share** [1] [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The increase is divided by the original figure, not the new one. State needs no development.

**9. (a)** **Vertical (backward)**, because the jar maker is a supplier [1]. **(b)** **Horizontal**, because it is a rival at the same stage [1]. **(c)** **Diversification**, because it enters an unrelated market [1]. **(d)** In a **merger** two firms agree to combine [1]; in a **takeover** one firm buys another, which may not welcome it [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the direction as well as "vertical" where the case shows it, and use the case clue, such as supplier or rival, to support the label.

**10.** For takeover: gives **immediate extra output and customers**, so market share rises faster than organic growth [1]; it removes a **competitor** [1]; it fits the owners' wish for speed [1].
Against takeover: £1.8 million is far above the **£0.3 million cash**, so Stenmoor would need loans or investors [1]; combining two businesses can bring **clashes of culture and staff worries** [1]; the risk is high for a firm with sales of £2.4 million [1].
For organic growth: lower risk and **easier to control** [1], but it is **slow** and may not deliver market share quickly [1].
Judgement: with so little cash, a takeover looks too risky now, so organic growth, perhaps with a smaller deal later, is better [1] **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation answers tend to gain credit when they use the figures in the stimulus and end with a decision that follows from the argument.

**11.** Partnership: easy to set up and shares skills [1]; but liability is **unlimited**, so the friends' savings are at risk if the business cannot repay £50 000 of credit [1] [1].
Private limited company: **limited liability** protects personal savings above the £15 000 each invests [1]; a bank and suppliers may feel safer dealing with a company [1]; but there are more rules to follow and it costs more to run [1].
Recommendation: a **private limited company** [1], because their stated aim of protecting savings and the size of the credit they need outweigh the extra rules [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A recommend answer needs a clear choice and a reason tied to the stimulus. Listing features of both types without choosing between them leaves the question unanswered.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving an explanation for an identify or state question, and running out of time.
- Writing "limited liability" without saying what the owner can lose.
- Dividing a percentage change by the new figure instead of the old one.
- Mixing up internal and external stakeholders.
- Confusing horizontal and vertical growth, or merger and takeover.
- Evaluating with only one side and no judgement.
- Ignoring the numbers in the case study.
- Defining a term when the question asks you to apply it.
