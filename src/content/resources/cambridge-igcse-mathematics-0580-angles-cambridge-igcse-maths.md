This study guide teaches subtopic **4.6 Angles** from Topic 4 Geometry of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Statements 1 and 2 are identical for Core and Extended. Statement 3 differs: Core candidates use the angle properties of **regular** polygons, and Extended candidates use those of **regular and irregular** polygons. Angle questions appear on all four papers, and most of them are non-calculator in style even on Papers 3 and 4.

When you have worked through this guide, consolidate with the [angles revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-angles-cambridge-igcse-maths-revision-notes/) and test yourself with the [angles practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-angles-cambridge-igcse-maths-practice/).

## What this subtopic covers

| Statement | What you must do | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| 4.6.1 | Calculate unknown angles and give simple explanations using angles at a point (360°), angles at a point on a straight line (180°), vertically opposite angles, and the angle sums of a triangle (180°) and a quadrilateral (360°) | Core and Extended |
| 4.6.2 | Calculate unknown angles and give geometric explanations for corresponding, alternate and co-interior angles in parallel lines | Core and Extended |
| 4.6.3 | Know and use angle properties of regular polygons, including interior and exterior angles and angle sum | Core |
| 4.6.3 | The same for regular **and irregular** polygons | Extended |

The syllabus notes add two expectations: you must know **three-letter notation** (angle ABC), and you must use the **correct geometrical terminology** when giving reasons.

## Three-letter notation

Angle ABC is the angle at the **middle** letter, B, between the lines BA and BC. So angle ABC and angle CBA are the same angle, but angle BAC is a different angle, at A. When a diagram has several angles at one point, three letters are the only way to say which one you mean, and an answer that names the wrong angle cannot earn the accuracy mark even if the number is right.

## The basic angle facts

| Fact | Wording to use as a reason |
|---|---|
| Angles round a point add to 360° | angles at a point sum to 360° |
| Angles on a straight line add to 180° | angles on a straight line sum to 180° |
| Opposite angles where two lines cross are equal | vertically opposite angles are equal |
| The three angles of a triangle add to 180° | angle sum of a triangle is 180° |
| The four angles of a quadrilateral add to 360° | angle sum of a quadrilateral is 360° |

Two further facts are used constantly with these: the base angles of an **isosceles triangle** are equal, and each angle of an **equilateral triangle** is 60°. Look for equal-side marks on the diagram; they are often the only clue that a triangle is isosceles.

### Worked example 1 (non-calculator): forming an equation

Three angles meet at a point. They are (2x + 15)°, (3x − 20)° and (4x + 5)°. Find the largest angle.

```
Angles at a point sum to 360°:
  (2x + 15) + (3x − 20) + (4x + 5) = 360
  9x = 360
  x = 40
Angles: 2(40) + 15 = 95°,  3(40) − 20 = 100°,  4(40) + 5 = 165°
Check: 95 + 100 + 165 = 360
Largest angle = 165°
```

The question asks for an angle, not for x. Stopping at x = 40 is the most common way to lose the final mark.

## Angles in parallel lines

When a straight line (a transversal) crosses two parallel lines, it makes eight angles, but only two different sizes. Three relationships link them.

- **Corresponding angles are equal.** They sit in the same position at each crossing (both above the line and to the right of the transversal, for example).
- **Alternate angles are equal.** They lie between the parallel lines, on opposite sides of the transversal.
- **Co-interior angles sum to 180°.** They lie between the parallel lines, on the same side of the transversal. The syllabus also calls them supplementary.

Use these exact names. Informal labels such as "Z angles" or "F angles" are not geometrical terminology, so they may not be accepted as reasons.

### Worked example 2 (non-calculator): a point between parallel lines

PQ and RS are parallel lines, with PQ above RS and Q and S to the right of P and R. A point T lies between the two lines, to the right of P and R. Angle QPT = 38° and angle SRT = 47°. Find angle PTR, giving reasons.

```
Draw a line UTV through T parallel to PQ, with U to the left of T.
angle PTU = angle QPT = 38°   (alternate angles are equal; UV is parallel to PQ)
angle UTR = angle SRT = 47°   (alternate angles are equal; UV is parallel to RS)
angle PTR = 38° + 47° = 85°
```

Adding your own parallel line is a standard move when an angle sits between two parallel lines. Say clearly that you have drawn it and that it is parallel, so each reason refers to a real pair of parallel lines.

## Polygons

For any polygon with **n** sides:

```
sum of exterior angles = 360°
interior angle + exterior angle = 180°   (angles on a straight line)
sum of interior angles = (n − 2) × 180°
```

The interior angle sum comes from splitting the polygon into (n − 2) triangles from one vertex. For a **regular** polygon every angle is the same, so:

```
each exterior angle = 360° ÷ n
each interior angle = 180° − exterior angle
n = 360° ÷ exterior angle
```

### Worked example 3 (non-calculator): working back from an interior angle

Each interior angle of a regular polygon is 156°. Find the number of sides and the sum of the interior angles.

```
exterior angle = 180° − 156° = 24°
n = 360 ÷ 24 = 15 sides
interior angle sum = (15 − 2) × 180° = 13 × 180° = 2340°
Check: 15 × 156° = 2340°
```

Go through the exterior angle. Trying to solve (n − 2) × 180 ÷ n = 156 works, but it is slower and easier to get wrong.

### Worked example 4 (Extended, non-calculator): an irregular polygon

A hexagon has interior angles 2x, 2x, 3x, 3x, 140° and 100°. Find the largest angle.

```
interior angle sum of a hexagon = (6 − 2) × 180° = 720°
2x + 2x + 3x + 3x + 140 + 100 = 720
10x = 480
x = 48
angles: 96°, 96°, 144°, 144°, 140°, 100°
largest angle = 144°
```

For an irregular polygon you cannot divide by n, because the angles are not equal. Only the **sums** (interior angles and exterior angles) can be used.

### Worked example 5 (non-calculator): combining facts

ABCDE is a regular pentagon. A square ABFG is drawn on the side AB, outside the pentagon. Find angle AEG.

```
interior angle of a regular pentagon = 180° − 360° ÷ 5 = 108°
so angle EAB = 108°; angle BAG = 90° (angle of a square)
angle EAG = 360° − 108° − 90° = 162°   (angles at a point sum to 360°)
AE = AB = AG   (sides of the regular pentagon and of the square)
so triangle AEG is isosceles and
angle AEG = (180° − 162°) ÷ 2 = 9°   (base angles of an isosceles triangle;
                                      angle sum of a triangle is 180°)
```

Spotting the equal lengths is the key step. Questions that put two regular polygons side by side almost always hide an isosceles triangle.

## Giving reasons

The syllabus expects reasons in correct terminology. A good reason names the property **and** applies to the angles you are using.

- Write one reason per step, next to the step it justifies.
- Use the wordings in the table above: "alternate angles are equal", not "alternate".
- If you use an isosceles triangle, say which sides are equal or point to the marks on the diagram.
- "Show that" questions need every step written down, ending with the given value; the final answer is already printed, so the marks are for the working.

## Common mistakes

- Reading angle ABC as the angle at A. The angle is always at the middle letter.
- Using alternate angles when the lines are not stated or marked as parallel. Arrows on the lines, or the word "parallel", must be there.
- Calling co-interior angles equal. They add up to 180°.
- Dividing an interior angle sum by n for an irregular polygon (Extended).
- Using 360° ÷ n as the interior angle of a regular polygon. That is the exterior angle.
- Measuring with a protractor when the diagram says it is not drawn to scale.
- Giving x when the question asks for an angle.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between Core and Extended for 0580 angles?**
The only difference is in polygon angles: Core candidates use the properties of regular polygons, while Extended candidates must also work with irregular polygons, using the interior and exterior angle sums.

**Can I write "Z angles" as a reason?**
Avoid it. The syllabus expects correct geometrical terminology, so write "alternate angles are equal". Similarly, write "corresponding angles are equal" and "co-interior angles sum to 180°".

**How do I find the number of sides of a regular polygon?**
Find the exterior angle (180° minus the interior angle if needed), then divide 360° by it. The answer must be a whole number; if it is not, check your working.
