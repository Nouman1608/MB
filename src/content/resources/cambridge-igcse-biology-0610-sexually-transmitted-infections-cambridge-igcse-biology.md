This guide covers sub-topic **16.6 Sexually transmitted infections** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. All five outcomes are **Core**, so every candidate needs them, and there is no Supplement content in this sub-topic.

The [reproduction study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-reproduction/) covers this sub-topic in one paragraph and one worked answer. This page works through each outcome: why HIV is a sexually transmitted pathogen, which body fluids carry it, how to judge whether a situation carries a risk, and how to turn each control method into an "explain" answer. ## What makes an infection an STI

An **STI** is an infection that is **transmitted through sexual contact**. The pathogen passes from one person to another in the body fluids exchanged, or through close contact of body surfaces, during sex.

This sub-topic connects to two others:

- **Diseases and immunity (topic 10):** a **pathogen** is a disease-causing organism, and a **transmissible disease** is one in which the pathogen can be passed from one host to another. An STI is one type of transmissible disease.
- **Drugs (topic 15):** antibiotics kill bacteria but **do not affect viruses**. This matters because HIV is a virus.

## HIV and AIDS

**HIV** stands for **human immunodeficiency virus**. It is a **pathogen** that causes an STI.

The syllabus wording is that HIV infection **may lead to AIDS**. HIV damages part of the immune system (some of the lymphocytes you meet in topic 10). Over time, often several years, the body becomes less able to defend itself against other pathogens. **AIDS** is the condition that can develop at that stage, when other infections take hold. The word "may" is deliberate: a person can live with HIV for a long time without developing AIDS, especially with treatment.

Keep the two terms apart: **HIV is the virus; AIDS is a condition** that HIV infection may lead to. "Catching AIDS" is incorrect.

## How HIV is transmitted

HIV is carried in certain **body fluids** of an infected person: **blood**, **semen**, **vaginal fluids** and **breast milk**. Transmission happens when one of these fluids from an infected person enters another person's body.

| Method of transmission | How the virus passes |
|---|---|
| Unprotected sexual intercourse | semen or vaginal fluids pass between partners |
| Sharing needles, for example among people who inject drugs | traces of infected blood left in the needle enter the next user's blood |
| Transfusion of infected blood or blood products | infected blood is put directly into the bloodstream |
| From mother to child | across the placenta during pregnancy, during birth, or in breast milk |

HIV is **not** passed on by everyday contact. Shaking hands, hugging, sharing cups or cutlery, using the same toilet, coughing and insect bites do not transmit it, because none of them moves infected blood, semen, vaginal fluid or breast milk into another person's body.

### Worked example 1: judging the risk

*For each situation, decide whether HIV could be transmitted from an infected person, and give a reason.*

| Situation | Risk? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| A nurse is pricked by a needle used to take blood from an infected patient. | yes | infected blood can enter her bloodstream |
| Two students share a drinking bottle. | no | no blood, semen, vaginal fluid or breast milk is exchanged |
| A baby is breastfed by a mother with HIV who is not receiving treatment. | yes | HIV can pass in breast milk |
| A man has sexual intercourse with an infected partner without a condom. | yes | body fluids are exchanged during sex |
| A person swims in a pool with an infected person. | no | no exchange of the body fluids that carry HIV |

The key skill is to name the **fluid** and the **route** into the body. "Yes, because HIV is infectious" earns nothing.

## How the spread of STIs is controlled

Outcome 16.6.5 uses the command word **explain**. For every method, state the method **and** say how it reduces the chance of the pathogen passing to another person.

| Control method | How it reduces spread |
|---|---|
| Using **condoms** during sex | acts as a **barrier**, so semen and vaginal fluids are not exchanged |
| Having **fewer sexual partners**, or none | fewer contacts, so less chance of sex with an infected person |
| **Testing** people for STIs | infected people, who may have no symptoms, find out, so they can be treated and avoid passing it on |
| **Contact tracing**: informing and testing the partners of an infected person | finds other infected people before they spread the infection further |
| **Treating** infected people | for bacterial STIs, antibiotics can cure the infection; for HIV, drugs reduce the amount of virus in the body fluids, so it is less likely to be passed on |
| **Screening donated blood** and heat-treating blood products | infected blood is not given to patients |
| **Not sharing needles**; needle-exchange schemes | infected blood is not passed from one user to the next |
| Care for **mothers with HIV**: treatment in pregnancy, and avoiding breastfeeding where safe alternatives exist | reduces transmission across the placenta, at birth and in milk |
| **Education** | people learn how STIs spread and how to protect themselves, so they change their behaviour |

### Worked example 2: a full "explain" answer

*Explain how screening donated blood and providing clean needles reduce the spread of HIV.* **[4]**

> HIV can be transmitted in infected blood [1]. Screening tests donated blood for HIV, so infected blood is not used in transfusions [1]. People who inject drugs may share needles, which can contain traces of infected blood [1]. Providing clean needles means needles are not shared, so infected blood does not pass from one user to another [1].

Each method gets a "what" and a "how". The first sentence earns a mark by stating the route that both methods block.

### Worked example 3: a calculation with invented data

*In one invented example, a blood service tested 40 000 donations and found 12 that contained HIV. Calculate the percentage of donations that contained HIV.*

```
12 ÷ 40 000 × 100 = 0.03%
```

The number is small, but each of those 12 donations could have infected a patient, which is why every donation is screened.

## Common mistakes

- Writing "HIV is AIDS" or "catching AIDS". HIV is the virus; AIDS is the condition it may lead to.
- Saying antibiotics treat HIV. HIV is a **virus**, and antibiotics do not affect viruses.
- Giving everyday contact, such as kissing on the cheek or sharing cups, as a transmission method.
- Listing control methods with no explanation, such as "use condoms" alone.
- Saying condoms "kill the virus". They are a **barrier** to body fluids.
- Forgetting mother-to-child transmission, or giving only one of its three routes.

## Exam technique

- **Describe** an STI: "an infection transmitted through sexual contact". One precise sentence is enough.
- **State** facts about HIV: it is a virus, a pathogen that causes an STI, and its infection may lead to AIDS.
- **Describe** transmission: name the route and the body fluid involved.
- **Explain** control: method + how it stops the pathogen passing on. One mark is usually available for each linked point.
- **Suggest** with data: if a programme is followed by a fall in new cases, link the fall to the specific measure and avoid claiming it was the only cause.
