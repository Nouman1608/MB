## Questions

**1.** State what is meant by an ecological niche and name two abiotic factors that form part of a niche. **[3]**

**2.** Relative growth of three bacterial cultures was recorded after 24 hours at three oxygen concentrations (data supplied, arbitrary units).

| Culture | 0% oxygen | 5% oxygen | 21% oxygen |
|---|---|---|---|
| J | 7 | 0 | 0 |
| K | 0 | 5 | 9 |
| L | 5 | 8 | 9 |

**(a)** Identify the oxygen-tolerance group of each culture, with a reason for each. **[3]**
**(b)** Suggest why culture L grows faster as oxygen increases. **[1]**

**3.**
**(a)** Outline holozoic nutrition. **[2]**
**(b)** A fungus grows on a fallen log. State its mode of nutrition and explain how it obtains nutrients. **[3]**

**4.** A marine protist was grown in four conditions. The net change in cell number per day is shown (data supplied).

| Condition | Net change per day |
|---|---|
| Light, with prey | 2.8 |
| Light, no prey | -0.4 |
| Dark, with prey | -0.6 |
| Dark, no prey | -0.9 |

**(a)** Deduce, with evidence, whether the protist is an obligate or a facultative mixotroph. **[3]**
**(b)** State how the data for a facultative mixotroph would differ. **[1]**

**5.**
**(a)** State the three domains of life. **[1]**
**(b)** Outline three ways in which archaea can obtain energy for ATP production. **[3]**

**6.** Skull measurements of two hominid species are given (data supplied).

| Species | Molar occlusal area (mm²) | Incisor area (mm²) |
|---|---|---|
| P | 260 | 40 |
| Q | 150 | 70 |

**(a)** Calculate the ratio of molar area to incisor area for each species. **[2]**
**(b)** Deduce which species is likely to have ground more plant material, and which diet suits the other. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how theories made from living mammals allow diet to be deduced for extinct species. **[1]**

**7.** Seeds on plants with a high concentration of a toxic alkaloid and on plants with a low concentration were counted. Of 200 seeds from each type, 38 high-alkaloid seeds and 120 low-alkaloid seeds had been eaten.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of seeds eaten for each type. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the difference. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest a metabolic adaptation that would let an animal eat the high-alkaloid seeds. **[1]**
**(d)** Distinguish chewing mouthparts from piercing mouthparts in insects. **[2]**

**8.** Clay models of prey were placed on a forest floor. Sixty models had bold stripes copied from a venomous wasp and sixty were plain and unpatterned. Twelve striped and 33 plain models showed predator bite marks.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of each type attacked and the difference in percentage points. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the result. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest one limitation of using clay models. **[1]**
**(d)** State one behavioural adaptation of prey for resisting predation. **[1]**

**9.** Light intensity above a tropical forest canopy is 2000 units. Measurements show 5% reaches the shrub layer and 0.8% reaches the forest floor.

**(a)** Calculate the light intensity at each level. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how a liana and an epiphyte each reach the light. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest two features of a herb that survives on the forest floor. **[2]**
**(d)** Outline how a strangler epiphyte gets established. **[2]**

**10.** A stream fish tolerates 12 to 30 °C when alone. In streams with a competing species it is found only at 18 to 26 °C.

**(a)** Distinguish the fundamental niche from the realized niche. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the width of each temperature range and the realized range as a percentage of the fundamental range. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest why the realized niche is smaller. **[1]**

**11.** Two species, R and S, were grown alone and together with the same limited food supply. Population sizes (arbitrary units) are given.

| Day | R alone | S alone | R together | S together |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 10 | 10 | 10 | 10 |
| 10 | 50 | 70 | 55 | 60 |
| 20 | 170 | 190 | 180 | 120 |
| 30 | 300 | 270 | 290 | 40 |
| 40 | 310 | 280 | 310 | 0 |

**(a)** Describe the outcome for species S when grown together with R. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in S at day 30 when with R compared with S alone. **[2]**
**(c)** State the term for this outcome and explain why it happens. **[2]**
**(d)** State a different possible outcome of competition between two species. **[1]**
**(e)** Explain what the investigation suggests about the niches of R and S. **[2]**

**12.** A drawing of the mandible of a leaf-eating insect is 45 mm long. The actual mandible is 1.5 mm long.

**(a)** Calculate the magnification. **[2]**
**(b)** Compare how a grasshopper-type insect and an aphid feed. **[3]**
**(c)** Discuss how plant defences and predation shape the niche of a leaf-eating insect. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** The role of a species in its ecosystem [1], including its biotic interactions such as how it gets food [1]. Two abiotic factors, for example temperature and light (or water, pH, salinity) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Where it lives" describes a habitat only; include interactions as well as place.

**2. (a)** J grows only at 0% and not at 5% or 21%, so it is an obligate anaerobe [1]. K does not grow at 0%, so it is an obligate aerobe [1]. L grows at all concentrations, so it is a facultative anaerobe [1].
**(b)** Aerobic respiration releases more ATP per glucose than anaerobic respiration, so more energy is available for growth [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the data that supports each group; naming the group alone is a weaker answer.

**3. (a)** Food is ingested and digested inside the body [1], then absorbed and assimilated [1]. 
**(b)** Saprotrophic [1]. It secretes digestive enzymes onto the dead wood [1] and absorbs the soluble products [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "Absorbs the food" does not distinguish a saprotroph from an animal; state that the digestion happens outside the body.

**4. (a)** The protist grows only with light and prey together (2.8) [1]. In light alone (-0.4) and in the dark with prey (-0.6) numbers fall [1]. So it needs both autotrophy and heterotrophy: it is an obligate mixotroph [1].
**(b)** A facultative mixotroph would grow with light alone, or with prey alone in the dark, as well as with both [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote values from at least two rows.

**5. (a)** Archaea, Bacteria and Eukarya [1].
**(b)** From light [1], from oxidation of inorganic chemicals [1], and from oxidation of carbon compounds [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Three different energy sources are needed; rewording one source does not give a second point.

**6. (a)** P = 260 ÷ 40 = **6.5** [1]. Q = 150 ÷ 70 = **2.14** [1].
**(b)** P has the larger relative molar area, so it probably ground more plant material [1]. Q has a smaller ratio, consistent with an omnivorous diet [1].
**(c)** Observed diets of living mammals led to theories linking tooth form to diet, and these can be applied to fossil teeth [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Use cautious wording such as "suggests"; teeth alone do not prove a diet.

**7. (a)** High alkaloid 38 ÷ 200 × 100 = **19%** [1]. Low alkaloid 120 ÷ 200 × 100 = **60%** [1].
**(b)** The alkaloid is a toxic secondary compound that deters or harms herbivores [1], so fewer high-alkaloid seeds are eaten [1].
**(c)** An enzyme that detoxifies the alkaloid, for example in the liver or gut [1].
**(d)** Chewing mouthparts cut and grind pieces of tissue [1]; piercing mouthparts are inserted into a vessel to suck up sap [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both sides stated; describing only chewing mouthparts will not show the distinction.

**8. (a)** Striped 12 ÷ 60 × 100 = **20%** and plain 33 ÷ 60 × 100 = **55%** [1]. Difference = **35 percentage points** [1].
**(b)** Striped models were attacked less [1] because the pattern acts as warning colouration that predators have learned to avoid, advertising a chemical defence [1].
**(c)** Models do not taste, smell or move like real prey, so the result may not apply to living animals [1].
**(d)** For example herding, alarm calls, freezing or fleeing [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* State which group is higher and by how much; raw counts alone do not show the comparison.

**9. (a)** Shrub layer 2000 × 0.05 = **100 units** [1]. Floor 2000 × 0.008 = **16 units** [1].
**(b)** A liana roots in the soil and climbs on other plants, so it reaches the canopy with little trunk [1]. An epiphyte grows on the branches of trees, high in the light, without roots in the soil [1].
**(c)** Any two: large thin leaves [1]; efficient photosynthesis at low light [1]; a high chlorophyll content.
**(d)** It starts to grow on a branch [1], then sends roots to the ground and may engulf and kill the host [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Give a separate statement for each plant when asked about both.

**10. (a)** Fundamental niche: the potential niche set by adaptations and tolerances without competitors [1]. Realized niche: the actual niche occupied when competing species are present [1].
**(b)** Fundamental width 30 - 12 = **18 °C** [1]. Realized width 26 - 18 = **8 °C** [1]. 8 ÷ 18 × 100 = **44.4%** [1].
**(c)** Competition for the same limiting resources excludes the fish from part of its tolerable range [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each width subtraction before the percentage so your working can be followed.

**11. (a)** S rises to 120 by day 20 [1], then declines and reaches 0 by day 40 [1].
**(b)** (40 - 270) ÷ 270 × 100 = **-85.2%** [1], a fall of 85.2% [1].
**(c)** Competitive exclusion [1]. R and S use the same limited food, and R is the better competitor so S is eliminated [1].
**(d)** Both species persist, each restricted to part of its fundamental niche [1].
**(e)** Their niches overlap too much for both to persist under these conditions [1], so each species needs a different niche to coexist [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Support each point with values from the table.

**12. (a)** Magnification = image ÷ actual [1] = 45 ÷ 1.5 = **×30** [1].
**(b)** Grasshopper-type insect has chewing mouthparts and bites off leaf tissue [1]. An aphid has piercing mouthparts that enter a phloem vessel [1], taking sugar-rich sap without chewing [1].
**(c)** Plant defences such as thorns, tough leaves and toxins limit which plants it can eat [1]. Detoxifying enzymes would widen the range [1]. Predators restrict where and when it feeds, and defences such as camouflage or warning colours reduce predation [1]. The niche is the sum of food, competitors and enemies, so these interactions together define its role [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* A "discuss" question needs more than a list; link each defence or predator to an effect on feeding or survival.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining a niche as the place where a species lives.
- Naming the oxygen group without linking it to the data.
- Saying saprotrophs ingest food.
- Calling a protist facultative or obligate without quoting growth values.
- Forgetting that dentition is an inference, not proof of diet.
- Calculating percentage change without the direction.
