Cambridge IGCSE Information and Communication Technology (0417) teaches students how computer systems are used in the real world and how to use common software confidently to produce documents, databases, presentations, spreadsheets and web pages. All candidates take three externally assessed components: Paper 1, Theory (1 hour 30 minutes, 80 marks, 40%), Paper 2, Document Production, Databases and Presentations (2 hours 15 minutes, 70 marks, 30%) and Component 3, Spreadsheets and Website Authoring (2 hours 15 minutes, 70 marks, 30%). Paper 2 and Component 3 are practical tests carried out on a computer. This overview explains how the 21 sections of the syllabus are arranged and how they map onto the three components.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus has 21 numbered sections. They fall naturally into three groups.

**Sections 1-10: ICT concepts and their effects.** These cover Types and components of computer systems; Input and output devices; Storage devices and media; Networks and the effects of using them; The effects of using IT; ICT applications (from communication and modelling to booking systems, banking, medicine, retail, recognition systems and satellite systems); The systems life cycle (analysis, design, development and testing, implementation, documentation and evaluation); Safety and security; Audience; and Communication.

**Sections 11-16: general practical skills.** File management, Images, Layout, Styles, Proofing, and Graphs and charts. These are the skills you use whatever piece of software you are working in, such as managing and compressing files, placing and editing images, applying a corporate house style or checking your work.

**Sections 17-21: specific applications.** Document production, Databases, Presentations, Spreadsheets and Website authoring. Each has its own skills, such as pagination and gutter margins in documents, creating a database structure, using a master slide, building a spreadsheet data model, or applying stylesheets to a web page.

## How it's assessed

The syllabus is component-based and not tiered. All three components are compulsory and all are marked by Cambridge.

| Component | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting | Sections tested |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Theory | 1 hour 30 minutes | 80 | 40% | 1-21 |
| Paper 2 | Document Production, Databases and Presentations | 2 hours 15 minutes | 70 | 30% | 17-19, plus 11-16 |
| Component 3 | Spreadsheets and Website Authoring | 2 hours 15 minutes | 70 | 30% | 20-21, plus 11-16 |

Two points stand out. First, Paper 1 can draw on every section of the syllabus, including the practical ones, so theory revision should not stop at section 10. Second, the general skills in sections 11-16 are tested in both practical components, which makes them the most widely assessed part of the practical course. The syllabus covers examinations from 2026 to 2028 and was first examined in 2026.

## How the parts fit together

The theory and practical sides of 0417 support each other more than it might first appear.

The concept sections explain why the practical skills matter. Audience appreciation and copyright in section 9 shape how a presentation or a web page should be designed. Safety and security in section 8 explains why file management and data protection matter when you are handling a database. The systems life cycle in section 7 describes the analysis, design, testing and evaluation that you carry out, on a small scale, every time you complete a practical task.

In the other direction, practical work makes the theory concrete. Building a spreadsheet model helps you understand modelling applications in section 6; creating a database structure gives you a clear picture of how school management or booking systems store records. Students who keep making these links tend to write fuller, more specific answers on Paper 1.

The general skills in sections 11-16 are the glue of the practical course. A well-proofed, consistently styled, sensibly saved file is expected in every task, whichever application it is built in.

## Planning your study

A sensible plan runs theory and practical work side by side through the course, with regular time at a computer every week.

In general terms:

- **Early in the course:** cover sections 1-5 in theory lessons, and in practical lessons learn the general skills of sections 11-16 using simple documents.
- **Middle of the course:** study ICT applications, the systems life cycle and safety and security, while working through Document production, Databases and Presentations for Paper 2.
- **Later in the course:** finish Audience and Communication, then concentrate practical time on Spreadsheets and Website authoring for Component 3.

Practical skills are best kept fresh by short, frequent tasks rather than occasional long sessions. Begin topic-level questions for Paper 1 as each section is completed, and move to full practice papers for all three components in the final months. ## Frequently asked questions

**What are the components of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417?**
Paper 1 Theory (80 marks, 40%), Paper 2 Document Production, Databases and Presentations (70 marks, 30%) and Component 3 Spreadsheets and Website Authoring (70 marks, 30%). All three are compulsory.

**Which sections are tested in the practical components?**
Paper 2 tests sections 17-19 plus the general skills in sections 11-16. Component 3 tests sections 20-21 plus sections 11-16.

**Does the theory paper only cover sections 1 to 10?**
No. Paper 1 is compulsory and can draw on sections 1-21, so the practical sections can also appear in theory questions.

**How long are the practical tests?**
Paper 2 and Component 3 each last 2 hours 15 minutes and are marked out of 70.

**Is Cambridge IGCSE ICT tiered?**
No. The syllabus is not tiered, and all three components are externally assessed.
