These notes condense section **7.1 Accounting concepts** of the Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus for 2027 to 2029. The syllabus lists ten concepts: business entity, consistency, duality, going concern, historic cost, matching / accruals, materiality, money measurement, prudence and realisation. The worked profit correction is in the [accounting concepts study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-accounting-concepts/); test yourself with the [accounting concepts practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-accounting-concepts-practice/). Figures are invented and in dollars.

## Key terms

| Concept | Definition to learn | Spot it when you see |
|---|---|---|
| Business entity | The business is a separate unit from its owner | Owner's private spending, drawings, goods taken for own use |
| Consistency | The same methods are used each period | A change of depreciation or valuation method |
| Duality | Every transaction has two equal effects | Double entry, the accounting equation |
| Going concern | The business will continue for the foreseeable future | Depreciation over useful life; a business about to close |
| Historic cost | Assets are recorded at original cost | A market value higher or lower than book value |
| Matching / accruals | Income and expenses belong to the period they relate to | Accruals, prepayments, depreciation |
| Materiality | Small items may be treated simply | Cheap tools expensed though they last years |
| Money measurement | Only items measurable in money are recorded | Staff skill, reputation, morale left out |
| Prudence | Do not overstate profit or assets; recognise expected losses | Allowance for irrecoverable debts, inventory at net realisable value |
| Realisation | Revenue is recognised when goods or services pass to the customer | Orders, deposits, goods sent after the year end |

## Concept behind each procedure

- **Drawings account**: business entity.
- **Depreciation**: matching, with going concern.
- **Accrued and prepaid expenses and income**: matching / accruals.
- **Irrecoverable debts and allowance**: prudence.
- **Lower of cost and net realisable value**: prudence.
- **Income received in advance**: realisation and matching.
- **Non-current assets at cost less depreciation**: historic cost.
- **Same depreciation method each year**: consistency.
- **Capitalising only items above a limit**: materiality.

## Effect of applying a concept

| Correction | Profit | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|
| Private expense removed (business entity) | Up | Drawings up, capital down |
| Accrued expense recorded (matching) | Down | Other payables up |
| Prepaid expense recorded (matching) | Up | Other receivables up |
| Inventory written down to net realisable value (prudence) | Down | Inventory down |
| Deposit removed from revenue (realisation) | Down | Current liability up |
| Asset capitalised instead of expensed, then depreciated | Up (cost less depreciation) | Non-current assets up |

## Applying a concept in two sentences

Name the concept and what it requires, then apply it to the figures.

- **Business entity**: "The business is separate from its owner. The $600 paid for the owner's car repair is drawings, so expenses fall by $600 and profit rises by $600."
- **Matching**: "Expenses belong to the period they relate to. December's $250 gas bill is unpaid, so it is added to this year's gas expense and shown as an other payable."
- **Realisation**: "Revenue is recognised when goods pass to the customer. The $900 received for goods to be delivered next month is a liability, not this year's revenue."
- **Going concern**: "The business is expected to keep trading, so its equipment is shown at cost less depreciation. If it were closing, the expected sale value would be used instead."
- **Prudence**: "Profit and assets must not be overstated. A customer owing $500 has gone bankrupt, so the $500 is written off as an irrecoverable debt, reducing profit and trade receivables."
- **Materiality**: "Small items need not be treated as assets. The $15 calculator is charged as an expense, because depreciating it would not change any user's decision."
- **Consistency**: "The same depreciation method is used each year, so a rise in profit reflects better trading, not a change of method."

## Pairs to keep apart

- **Matching** decides which period an expense or income belongs to; **realisation** decides when revenue is earned.
- **Prudence** recognises expected losses now; **realisation** stops expected gains being recorded early.
- **Historic cost** sets the value; **going concern** justifies using it.
- **Materiality** sets a limit; **consistency** keeps the same limit each year.

## Exam traps

- "Explain" needs the concept applied to the case and its effect on the figures, not only a definition.
- Prudence means not overstating, not deliberately understating.
- Private spending is a business entity error, not an accruals error.
- A deposit for goods not yet delivered is not revenue.
- What is material depends on the size of the business.
- If a business is about to close, going concern no longer applies.

## Self-test

Name the concept applied in questions 1 to 10.

1. The owner's family holiday, paid from the business bank account, is recorded as drawings.
2. Rent paid in advance for next year is shown as a prepayment.
3. Damaged inventory is valued below cost at the amount it can be sold for.
4. An order taken in December for goods delivered in January is recorded as January revenue.
5. A stapler costing $12 is charged as an expense.
6. Land bought for $50,000 and now worth $90,000 is still shown at $50,000.
7. A business uses the same depreciation method every year.
8. A business's loyal customers are not recorded as an asset.
9. Every debit entry has an equal credit entry.
10. Machinery is depreciated over its useful life instead of being shown at what it could be sold for today.
11. Draft profit is $20,000. The owner's private insurance of $300 was charged to expenses, and wages of $450 owing are unrecorded. Calculate the corrected profit.
12. State the difference between matching and realisation.

### Answers

1. Business entity.
2. Matching / accruals.
3. Prudence.
4. Realisation.
5. Materiality.
6. Historic cost.
7. Consistency.
8. Money measurement.
9. Duality.
10. Going concern.
11. 20,000 + 300 - 450 = **$19,850**.
12. Matching places income and expenses in the period they relate to, whatever the timing of cash; realisation recognises revenue only when goods or services have passed to the customer.
