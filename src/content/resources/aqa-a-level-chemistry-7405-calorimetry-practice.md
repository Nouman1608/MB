These questions cover section **3.1.4.2 Calorimetry** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Papers 1 and 2 (and Paper 3). Learn the methods in the [Calorimetry study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-calorimetry/) and the [Calorimetry revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-calorimetry-revision-notes/). Use c = 4.18 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹ for water and solutions, and assume that 1.00 cm³ of any solution has a mass of 1.00 g. Aᵣ and Mᵣ values: H = 1.0, C = 12.0, N = 14.0, O = 16.0, Cl = 35.5.

## Questions

**1.** Calculate the heat energy, in kJ, needed to raise the temperature of 250 g of water by 12.0 °C. **[2]**

**2.** 40.0 cm³ of 1.50 mol dm⁻³ nitric acid is mixed with 40.0 cm³ of 1.50 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide solution in an insulated cup. The temperature rises by 9.5 °C. Calculate the enthalpy change of neutralisation, in kJ mol⁻¹, per mole of water formed. **[4]**

**3.** 5.35 g of ammonium chloride (Mᵣ = 53.5) is dissolved in 75.0 g of water. The temperature falls by 4.7 °C. Calculate the enthalpy change of solution in kJ mol⁻¹, including its sign. **[4]**

**4.** Excess zinc powder is added to 50.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol dm⁻³ copper(II) sulfate solution. The temperature rises by 10.4 °C.

**(a)** Explain why the amount of copper(II) sulfate, not the zinc, is used to calculate the enthalpy change. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the enthalpy change, in kJ mol⁻¹, per mole of copper(II) sulfate. **[4]**

**5.** A spirit burner containing methanol, CH₃OH (Mᵣ = 32.0), is used to heat 150 g of water. The burner loses 0.80 g in mass and the temperature of the water rises by 17.0 °C.

**(a)** Calculate the enthalpy change of combustion of methanol from these data. **[4]**

**(b)** The data-book value is −726 kJ mol⁻¹. Give two reasons why the experimental value is less negative. **[2]**

**6.** In question 5 the thermometer is read to ±0.1 °C at the start and at the end. Calculate the percentage uncertainty in the temperature rise. **[2]**

**7.** Suggest two changes that would improve the accuracy of the experiment in question 5. For each, explain the effect. **[4]**

**8.** A student uses a polystyrene cup with a lid for a neutralisation experiment, instead of a glass beaker. Explain two advantages. **[2]**

**9.** A slow reaction in solution is monitored by recording the temperature every 30 seconds after mixing. The temperature rises, reaches a maximum and then falls. Explain how the student should find the temperature rise that would have occurred with no heat loss. **[3]**

**10.** 1.25 kJ of energy raises the temperature of 50.0 g of ethanol by 10.0 K. Calculate the specific heat capacity of ethanol. **[2]**

**11.** The enthalpy change of neutralisation of a strong acid by a strong alkali is −57 kJ mol⁻¹. Predict the temperature rise when 25.0 cm³ of 1.00 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid is mixed with 25.0 cm³ of 1.00 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide. **[3]**

**12.** A student calculates the enthalpy of solution of ammonium chloride in question 3 using the mass of the solid (5.35 g) instead of the water in q = mcΔT.

**(a)** State the error. **[1]**

**(b)** State the effect on the calculated enthalpy change. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** q = 250 × 4.18 × 12.0 = 12 540 J [1] = **12.5 kJ** [1]. **[2]**

**2.** m = 80.0 g; q = 80.0 × 4.18 × 9.5 = 3177 J = 3.177 kJ [1]; n(HNO₃) = 1.50 × 0.0400 = 0.0600 mol [1]; ΔH = 3.177 ÷ 0.0600 = 52.9 [1]; the sign is negative, because the temperature rose: **−52.9 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[4]**

**3.** q = 75.0 × 4.18 × 4.7 = 1473 J = 1.473 kJ [1]; n = 5.35 ÷ 53.5 = 0.100 mol [1]; ΔH = 1.473 ÷ 0.100 = 14.7 [1]; the temperature fell, so the value is positive: **+14.7 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[4]**

**4. (a)** Zinc is in excess, so copper(II) sulfate is the limiting reagent and determines the amount that reacts [1]

**(b)** q = 50.0 × 4.18 × 10.4 = 2174 J = 2.174 kJ [1]; n = 0.200 × 0.0500 = 0.0100 mol [1]; ΔH = 2.174 ÷ 0.0100 = 217 [1]; sign negative: **−217 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[5]**

**5. (a)** q = 150 × 4.18 × 17.0 = 10 659 J = 10.66 kJ [1]; n = 0.80 ÷ 32.0 = 0.0250 mol [1]; ΔH = 10.66 ÷ 0.0250 = 426 [1]; negative: **−426 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]

**(b)** Any two of: heat is lost to the surroundings, so the temperature rise is too small; incomplete combustion gives less heat; heat is absorbed by the can or burner; methanol evaporates from the wick so the mass loss is too large; the reaction is not under standard conditions [1 each]. **[6]**

**6.** Uncertainty in ΔT = 0.1 + 0.1 = ±0.2 °C [1]; percentage uncertainty = 0.2 ÷ 17.0 × 100 = **1.2%** [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Any two pairs: use a draught shield or lid, which reduces heat loss to the air [1 + 1]; place the burner closer to the can or insulate the can, which reduces heat loss [1 + 1]; stir the water so the temperature is uniform [1 + 1]. Give 1 mark for each change and 1 mark for the stated effect. **[4]**

**8.** Polystyrene is a good insulator, so heat loss is reduced [1]; a lid reduces heat loss by evaporation and convection [1]. Accept a reference to a lower heat capacity of the cup, so less energy is absorbed by the container. **[2]**

**9.** Plot temperature against time [1]; draw a line of best fit through the cooling part of the graph and extrapolate it back to the time of mixing [1]; read the temperature at that time and subtract the starting temperature to give the corrected temperature rise [1]. **[3]**

**10.** c = q ÷ (m × ΔT) = 1250 ÷ (50.0 × 10.0) [1] = **2.50 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**

**11.** n = 1.00 × 0.0250 = 0.0250 mol; q = 57 × 0.0250 = 1.425 kJ = 1425 J [1]; m = 50.0 g, so ΔT = 1425 ÷ (50.0 × 4.18) [1] = **6.8 °C** [1]. **[3]**

**12. (a)** The mass of the solid was used, instead of the mass of the water that changed temperature [1]

**(b)** The calculated q is far too small, so the enthalpy change is far too small (about 14 times smaller in magnitude) [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the mass of the reactant instead of the water or solution in q = mcΔT.
- Not dividing by 1000 to get kJ.
- Leaving off the sign, or giving the wrong sign.
- Using the excess reagent to find n.
- Giving "heat loss" as a reason without saying which way it changes the result.
