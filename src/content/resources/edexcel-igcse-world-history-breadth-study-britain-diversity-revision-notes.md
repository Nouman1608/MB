|---|---|---|
| Representation of the People Act | 1918 | Votes for all men over 21 and women over 30 (with property qualification) |

| Aliens Restriction (Amendment) Act | 1919 | Tighter controls on foreigners |

| Education Act (Butler) | 1944 | Free secondary education for all |
| NHS begins | 1948 | Free healthcare |
| Commonwealth Immigrants Act | 1962 | Work vouchers restrict immigration |
| Race Relations Act | 1965 | Bans discrimination in public places |
| Sexual Offences Act | 1967 | Decriminalises private homosexual acts (England and Wales) |
| Chronically Sick and Disabled Persons Act | 1970 | Council services for disabled people |
| Equal Pay Act | 1970 | Equal pay for equal work |
| Immigration Act | 1971 | Further restriction of entry |
| Sex Discrimination Act | 1975 | Bans sex discrimination in jobs, education and services |
| Section 28 (Local Government Act) | 1988 | Bans councils "promoting" homosexuality |
| Disability Discrimination Act | 1995 | Outlaws disability discrimination |
| Equality Act | 2010 | Combines anti-discrimination law |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Colour bar | Refusal of service or work to black people |
| Eugenics | Attempt to "improve" the population by controlling who has children |
| Pressure group | Organisation campaigning to change law or attitudes |
| Institutional racism | Racism built into an organisation's practices |
| Third-wave feminism | Feminism from the 1990s, focusing on diversity of women's experience |

## Essay plan in outline

*"Government legislation was the main reason for improvements in equality in Britain in the years
1918–2010."*

- **For:** 1918 and 1928 votes; 1944 Acts; Race Relations Acts; Equal Pay and Sex Discrimination Acts; DDA;
  Equality Act.
- **Against:** protest and pressure (Bristol, Dagenham, Lawrence campaign, disability groups); war; immigration
  and cultural change; laws that restricted rights (1962, 1971, Section 28).
- **Judgement:** laws secured change, but campaigns usually forced governments to act.

## Frequently asked questions

**What was the significance of Windrush?**
It became the symbol of post-war Commonwealth migration that
made Britain a more diverse society.

**Why was the Macpherson Report important?**
It accepted that racism could be built into institutions,
not just individuals, and led to changes in policing and race relations law.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- **Saying all women got the vote in 1918.** Only those over 30 meeting a property qualification.
- **Forgetting restrictive laws.** 1919, 1962, 1971 and 1988 limited rights.
- **Ignoring disability and class.** Both are named themes.
- **Assuming laws changed attitudes at once.** Use Macpherson (1999) to show limits.
