Sub-topic 1.1 needs three things: the formation story of seven named rocks, the eight rock cycle
processes in the right order, and which rocks let water through. Each rock has a **parent material**, a
**process** and a **result**; learn the three together.

## Seven rocks: parent, process, result

| Rock | Type | Parent material | Process | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Granite | Igneous | Magma | Cools slowly deep underground; crystallises | Large interlocking crystals |
| Basalt | Igneous | Lava | Cools quickly at the surface; crystallises | Small crystals |
| Limestone | Sedimentary | Shells and skeletons (calcium carbonate) | Sedimentation, compaction, cementation in warm shallow seas | Layered rock with pore spaces |
| Sandstone | Sedimentary | Sand grains | Deposition, compaction, cementation | Grains with connected pore spaces |
| Shale | Sedimentary | Mud and clay | Deposited in calm water; compacted | Thin layers, tiny pores |
| Marble | Metamorphic | Limestone | Heat and/or pressure; recrystallises without melting | Hard, interlocking crystals |
| Slate | Metamorphic | Shale | Pressure (and heat); minerals line up | Splits into thin flat sheets |

**Memory hooks:** **slow** cooling = **large** crystals (granite); **fast** cooling = **small** crystals
(basalt). Limestone → marble; shale → slate.

## The eight processes

| Process | One-line meaning |
|---|---|
| Weathering | Breakdown of rock where it is (physical, chemical or biological) |
| Erosion | Removal of the broken material |
| Transportation | Carrying of material by rivers, wind, ice or the sea |
| Deposition | Dropping of material when the transporting agent loses energy |
| Sedimentation | Settling and build-up of sediment in layers |
| Compaction | Squeezing of layers by the weight above; water pushed out |
| Cementation | Minerals crystallise between grains and glue them |
| Crystallisation | Minerals form as magma or lava cools, making igneous rock |

**Sedimentary order:** weathering → erosion → transportation → deposition → sedimentation → compaction →
cementation.

**Also needed to read a diagram (not in the 1.1.2 list):** melting (rock → magma) and metamorphism (heat
and/or pressure → metamorphic rock).

## Permeability

- **Definition:** the ability of water to pass through the pore spaces of rock and soil.
- **Permeable:** most sedimentary rocks, such as limestone and sandstone.
- **Impermeable:** igneous rocks, metamorphic rocks and some sedimentary rocks, such as shale.
- **Why:** connected pore spaces between grains let water through; interlocking crystals or tightly
  packed clay particles leave no usable gaps.
- **Why it matters:** aquifers form in permeable rock above impermeable rock; reservoirs need impermeable
  floors; oil and gas are trapped under impermeable layers; shale gas needs fracking.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Igneous rock | Rock formed when magma or lava cools and crystallises |
| Sedimentary rock | Rock formed from layers of sediment that are compacted and cemented |
| Metamorphic rock | Existing rock changed by heat and/or pressure without melting |
| Magma | Molten rock below the surface |
| Lava | Molten rock that has reached the surface |
| Sediment | Loose fragments of rock or remains of organisms |
| Pore space | Gap between grains in a rock or soil |
| Permeable | Lets water pass through its pore spaces |
| Impermeable | Does not let water pass through |
| Aquifer | Permeable rock that stores ground water |

## Exam traps

- Granite cools slowly because it is **deep underground**, not because it is "in water".
- Metamorphic rock forms **without melting**; melted rock becomes igneous.
- Shale is sedimentary but **impermeable**: do not write "all sedimentary rocks are permeable".
- Weathering is breakdown **in place**; erosion is **removal**.
- "Describe the formation" questions need the parent material and the process, not just the rock type.
- For a labelled diagram, name the process for the arrow asked about, not the rock in the box.
- In data questions, quote figures with units (cm³) and calculate percentages from the original volume.

## Self-test

1. Name the **two** igneous rocks in the syllabus.
2. Why does granite have larger crystals than basalt?
3. What is limestone mainly made of?
4. Name the process that glues sediment grains together.
5. Which metamorphic rock forms from shale?
6. What changes limestone into marble?
7. Put these in order: compaction, erosion, deposition, weathering, cementation, transportation.
8. Which process forms igneous rock from lava?
9. Define permeability.
10. Classify sandstone, shale, granite and slate as permeable or impermeable.
11. Explain why sandstone is permeable but shale is not.
12. Why does natural gas stay trapped beneath a layer of shale?

**Answers:** 1. Granite and basalt. 2. Granite cools slowly deep underground, so crystals have time to
grow; basalt cools quickly at the surface. 3. Calcium carbonate, often from shells and skeletons of sea
creatures. 4. Cementation. 5. Slate. 6. Heat and/or pressure, without melting. 7. Weathering, erosion,
transportation, deposition, compaction, cementation. 8. Crystallisation. 9. The ability of water to pass
through the pore spaces of rock and soil. 10. Sandstone permeable; shale, granite and slate impermeable.
11. Sandstone has large grains with connected pore spaces; shale's clay particles are tiny and tightly
packed, so its pores are too small for water to pass. 12. Shale is impermeable, so the gas cannot pass
up through it.
