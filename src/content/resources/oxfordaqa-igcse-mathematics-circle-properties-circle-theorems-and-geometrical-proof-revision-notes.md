## Circle words (G8, Core)

| Word | Remember |
|---|---|
| Radius | Centre to circumference; all radii are equal |
| Diameter | Chord through the centre; 2 × radius; longest chord |
| Chord | Straight line joining two points on the circle |
| Arc | Part of the circumference |
| Sector | Two radii and an arc |
| Segment | A chord and an arc |
| Tangent | Touches the circle at one point only |

Two radii and a chord always make an **isosceles triangle**. If the angle at the centre is c, each base angle is (180° − c) ÷ 2.

## The eight theorems (G8, Extension only)

| No. | Theorem (reason to write) | Picture to hold in mind |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The angle subtended by an arc at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference | Centre angle on the same arc, doubled |
| 2 | The angle in a semicircle is 90° | Diameter as the base of the triangle |
| 3 | Angles in the same segment are equal | A "bow tie" from one chord |
| 4 | Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180° | Four points on the circle |
| 5 | A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact | Right angle at the contact point |
| 6 | Tangents from an external point are equal in length | Kite with two right angles |
| 7 | The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord | Right triangle with half the chord |
| 8 | Alternate segment: angle between tangent and chord equals the angle in the alternate segment | Tangent angle matches the far side |

### Method for an angle question

1. Mark every radius and every right angle you can justify.
2. Look for the centre: if one angle is at O and one is on the circle on the same arc, use theorem 1.
3. Look for a diameter: that gives 90° by theorem 2.
4. Look for four points on a circle forming a quadrilateral: theorem 4.
5. Look for two angles looking at the same chord from the same side: theorem 3.
6. If a tangent appears, think theorems 5, 6 and 8.
7. Write each angle with its reason, in the order you found it.

### Method for a length question

1. Join the centre to the point you need (a tangent contact or a chord end).
2. The right angle comes from theorem 5 or 7.
3. Use Pythagoras in that right-angled triangle.
4. Use theorem 6 for the second tangent, or double the half-chord for the whole chord.

Useful triples in circle problems: 5-12-13, 3-4-5 and 8-15-17 (and multiples). If your numbers do not fit one, check which side is the hypotenuse. In a tangent triangle, OT is the hypotenuse. In a chord triangle, the radius is.

## Small worked reminders

**Centre angle.** Angle ACB = 35° on the major arc, so angle AOB = 70°. If OA and OB are radii, angle OAB = 55°.

**Cyclic quadrilateral.** Angle A = 2x + 10 and angle C = x + 50: 3x + 60 = 180, so x = 40; A = 90°, C = 90°.

**Chord.** Radius 13 cm, chord 24 cm: half-chord 12 cm, so the distance from the centre is √(169 − 144) = 5 cm.

**Tangent.** Radius 7 cm, OT = 25 cm: TA = √(625 − 49) = 24 cm.

**Alternate segment.** The tangent makes 56° with chord AB, so the angle in the segment on the other side of AB is also 56°.

## Geometrical proof (G9, Extension only)

Every proof line is a statement plus a reason. Plan it like this.

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Define | Give a name to an angle ("let angle OAB = a") or mark equal radii |
| Reason | Quote one theorem per line |
| Link | Show how the lines combine (sum to 180°, double, subtract) |
| Conclude | Write the result in the form the question asked for |

Standard proof patterns:

- **Congruent triangles in a circle.** Two radii give one pair of equal sides. A tangent contact gives a right angle. A shared line (such as OT) gives a common side. Right angle, hypotenuse, side, or three sides, proves congruence.
- **Isosceles triangles from radii.** Base angles are equal, which links the centre angle to the circumference angle.
- **Chain through the centre.** Angle at the centre = 2 × angle on the circle, so two angles on the same arc must be equal.

Proof of cyclic quadrilateral: if angle BAD = p, the centre angle on the opposite arc is 2p, and the other centre angle is 360° − 2p, so angle BCD = 180° − p. Learn this one, and also the proof that tangents from a point are equal: right angles at A and B, OA = OB and OT common, so the triangles are congruent.

### Short reasons to reuse

- "Radii of the same circle are equal, so the triangle is isosceles."
- "Angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference."
- "Angle in a semicircle is a right angle."
- "Angles in the same segment are equal."
- "Opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°."
- "A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact."
- "Tangents from an external point are equal in length."
- "The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord."
- "Alternate segment theorem."
- "Angles in a triangle sum to 180°" (and the other basic angle facts, quoted as given).

## Must-know distinctions

| Often confused | Difference |
|---|---|
| Chord and tangent | A chord joins two points on the circle; a tangent meets it once |
| Segment and sector | Segment from a chord; sector from two radii |
| Centre theorem and same-segment | Centre theorem compares centre and circumference; same-segment compares two circumference angles |
| Opposite and adjacent angles in a cyclic quadrilateral | Only opposite angles sum to 180° |
| Semicircle and half circle | The 90° theorem needs the angle on the circumference, with the diameter as one side |

## Quick self-test

1. What is the name of the longest chord of a circle?
2. An arc makes an angle of 98° at the centre. What angle does the same arc make at the circumference?
3. Point C lies on a circle with diameter AB, and angle CAB = 27°. Find angle ABC.
4. ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral and angle DAB = 88°. Find angle BCD.
5. A tangent from T touches a circle with centre O at A. The radius is 7 cm and OT = 25 cm. Find TA.
6. A circle has radius 13 cm. A chord is 24 cm long. How far is the chord from the centre?
7. The angle between a tangent and chord AB is 56°. Name the theorem that gives another angle equal to 56°, and say where it is.
8. Tangents TA and TB meet at T, and angle ATB = 40°. Find angle TAB.
9. What reason justifies that triangle OAB, with A and B on the circle and centre O, is isosceles?
10. In cyclic quadrilateral ABCD, angle ABC = 116°. Side AB is extended to E. Find angle ADC, and say why angle CBE equals it.

### Answers

1. The diameter.
2. 49° (angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference).
3. 63° (angle ACB = 90° in a semicircle, then 180° − 90° − 27°).
4. 92° (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°).
5. 24 cm (angle OAT = 90°, then 25² − 7² = 576).
6. 5 cm (half-chord 12 cm, then 13² − 12² = 25).
7. The alternate segment theorem: the angle in the segment on the opposite side of the chord from the angle.
8. 70° (TA = TB, so triangle TAB is isosceles, and (180° − 40°) ÷ 2 = 70°).
9. OA and OB are both radii, so they are equal.
10. Angle ADC = 64° (opposite angles sum to 180°). Angle CBE = 180° − 116° = 64° (angles on a straight line), so the exterior angle equals the opposite interior angle.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing an angle with no reason when the question says "give reasons", or giving a vague reason such as "circle rule".
- Using the wrong pair of angles in a cyclic quadrilateral (adjacent instead of opposite).
- Applying the centre theorem to an angle that is not on the same arc as the centre angle.
- Reading the alternate segment as the nearer segment.
- Treating OT as a leg instead of the hypotenuse in tangent problems.
- Forgetting to double the half-chord, or to halve a full chord, in chord problems.
- Skipping the line that defines a letter, so a proof becomes hard to follow.
- Ending a proof with the working but not the stated conclusion.
- Rounding too early in a length or angle question that needs a decimal.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are circle theorems in the Core tier of OxfordAQA 9260?**
No. Circle words such as chord, arc and tangent are Core, but the eight circle theorems and geometrical proof are Extension only in OxfordAQA International GCSE Mathematics 9260.

**What is the difference between a sector and a segment?**
A sector is bounded by two radii and an arc. A segment is bounded by a chord and an arc.

**How do you find the distance from the centre of a circle to a chord?**
The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord, so use Pythagoras in the right-angled triangle formed by the radius, the half-chord and that distance.

**What does the alternate segment theorem say?**
The angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment, which is on the other side of the chord from that angle.
