Exercise 3 of **Paper 1 Reading and Writing** in Cambridge IGCSE Urdu as a Second Language (0539) is
**note-making**, worth **9 marks**. You read a longer text printed in the question paper and make
brief notes under a number of headings that the question supplies. It is the deepest reading task on
the paper, and it matters twice over: the same text is the source for the summary in Exercise 4, so
good notes here make the next 10 marks easier. This guide goes deeper on that one exercise than the
[Paper 1 Reading and Writing guide](/resources/igcse-cambridge-urdu-paper-1-reading-writing/).

## What the syllabus says

Candidates "read a longer text printed in the question paper and make brief notes under a number of
supplied headings". The objectives are **R1** (identify and select relevant information), **R2**
(understand ideas, opinions and attitudes), **R3** (understand the connections between them) and **R4**
(understand what is implied but not directly stated). The syllabus's reading content also includes
identifying "the important points or themes within an extended piece of writing", which is exactly the
skill the headings test.

Three features define the task:

- **The headings are given.** You do not decide what is important; the headings do. A true, interesting
  point that fits no heading earns nothing.
- **The notes are brief.** Full sentences are not required. A precise phrase is enough.
- **The space is a guide.** The question paper usually shows how many notes are expected under each
  heading (for example, a number of bullet points or lines). Treat that number as the number of
  separate points you need.

## What a good note looks like

A note is the shortest phrase that still carries the full point.

| Text sentence | Weak note | Good note |
|---|---|---|
| بہت سے طلبہ صبح ناشتہ کیے بغیر اسکول آ جاتے ہیں۔ | ناشتہ | بغیر ناشتے کے اسکول آنا |
| سائیکل چلانے سے فضا میں دھواں کم پھیلتا ہے۔ | سائیکل | فضائی آلودگی میں کمی |
| داخلے کے لیے پہلے سے ٹکٹ خریدنا ضروری ہے۔ | ٹکٹ | پیشگی ٹکٹ ضروری |
| روزانہ آدھا گھنٹہ چہل قدمی سے دل مضبوط ہوتا ہے۔ | چہل قدمی | دل مضبوط ہوتا ہے |

The weak notes all name a **topic** rather than a **point**. Under the heading "ورزش کے فائدے",
"چہل قدمی" names the exercise, not the benefit. The benefit is the strong heart.

Ways to shorten in Urdu:

- **Drop the helper verb.** ہوتا ہے، ہیں and جاتا ہے can usually go: "پانی کی کمی" for "یہاں پانی کم
  ہوتا جا رہا ہے".
- **Use a verbal noun** (the infinitive or a noun form): "پودوں کو پانی دینا" or "مرمت".
- **Keep the detail that makes it specific.** "کچھ وقت" is vague; "دس سال" is the point.
- **Keep negatives.** Dropping نہیں or بغیر reverses the meaning: "ٹکٹ ضروری" is not "ٹکٹ ضروری نہیں".

Lifting key words from the text is normal in note-making; this task tests whether you have found and
selected the right information, not whether you can paraphrase it. Paraphrasing becomes important in
Exercise 4.

## One note, one point

Each bullet should carry one separate point. Two common errors:

1. **Two points in one line.** If a line reads "جھولے اور سایہ دار بنچ", and the heading has two bullet
   spaces, you may have used up both points in one bullet and then repeated one in the next. Split them:
   "بچوں کے لیے جھولے" / "بزرگوں کے لیے سایہ دار بنچ".
2. **The same point twice.** "پانی کی کمی" and "پانی کم ملتا ہے" are one point written twice and will
   normally be credited once.

## Placing points under the right heading

The headings often divide one topic into contrasting parts: advantages and problems, causes and
solutions, past and present, what happened and what will happen. A correct note under the wrong heading
is not credited there.

Watch especially for:

- **Solutions written under problems.** "Ask an engineer" is advice, not a difficulty, even if it
  appears in the difficulties paragraph.
- **Points scattered across the text.** The text's paragraphs do not always line up with the headings.
  A quotation near the end may add a difficulty; a sentence in the introduction may give an advantage.
  This is the R3 and R4 part of the task.
- **Background facts.** The opening of the text often sets the scene. Unless a heading asks for it,
  background earns nothing.

## Worked example

An original short paragraph written for this guide:

> ہمارے اسکول نے پچھلے سال ردی کاغذ جمع کرنے کی مہم شروع کی۔ ہر جماعت میں ایک ڈبا رکھا گیا، اور ہر جمعے کو طلبہ کا ایک گروپ سارا کاغذ جمع کر کے ایک کارخانے کو بیچ دیتا ہے۔ اس رقم سے لائبریری کے لیے نئی کتابیں خریدی جاتی ہیں۔ تاہم کچھ طلبہ ڈبوں میں پلاسٹک اور کھانے کا بچا ہوا سامان بھی ڈال دیتے ہیں، جس سے کاغذ خراب ہو جاتا ہے۔

Headings with their bullet spaces:

**(a) مہم کا طریقہ** (2 notes) **(b) مہم کا فائدہ** (1 note) **(c) ایک مسئلہ** (1 note)

**Weak notes:**

- (a) پچھلے سال؛ ڈبا
- (b) کتابیں
- (c) طلبہ

"پچھلے سال" is when the scheme started, not how it works. "ڈبا", "کتابیں" and "طلبہ" are topics, not
points. At most one of these notes would be credited.

**Good notes:**

- (a) ہر جماعت میں کاغذ کا ڈبا
- (a) ہر جمعے کو کاغذ کارخانے کو فروخت
- (b) رقم سے لائبریری کے لیے نئی کتابیں
- (c) ڈبوں میں پلاسٹک اور بچا ہوا کھانا، جس سے کاغذ خراب

Each note is a phrase, each carries one point, and each sits under the heading it answers. The
"تاہم" (however) in the text was the signal that a problem was coming.

## A reliable method

1. **Read the headings first** and note how many points each needs. They tell you what the text is about
   and what to look for.
2. **Read the whole text once** for its overall structure.
3. **Read again with the headings in mind**, marking each relevant point in the margin with the heading
   letter (a, b, c).
4. **Check for scattered points**: quotations, the final paragraph and sentences beginning with تاہم،
   لیکن or اس کے علاوہ often hold a point for a different heading.
5. **Write the notes**: one point per bullet, specific, brief.
6. **Check** each note against its heading: does it answer that heading, and is it different from the
   other notes there?

## Exam technique and timing

Exercise 3 carries 9 marks but needs the longest reading on the paper. Allow around **15-18 minutes**,
and remember that the time also prepares you for Exercise 4. Some candidates mark the points for the
summary aspect while reading for the notes, so they do not have to read the whole text from scratch
again.

Write clearly and legibly within the space given. If you think of more points than the bullets allow,
choose the clearest and most specific ones; extra notes beyond the expected number are unlikely to earn
more and can include a wrong or repeated point.

## Common mistakes

- Writing a topic word instead of a point.
- Putting a correct point under the wrong heading.
- Writing two points in one bullet, then repeating one of them.
- Copying long sentences, which wastes time and can include material from another heading.
- Missing points that appear outside the "obvious" paragraph.
- Dropping a negative, so the note says the opposite of the text.
