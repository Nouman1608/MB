This study guide goes deeper into section 4.8, Trigonometry and Pythagoras' theorem, of topic 4 (Geometry and trigonometry) in the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) specification, Issue 2 (November 2017). The Foundation Tier content (statements A to C) is needed by both tiers. The Higher Tier adds six statements, marked **Higher tier only** below. The sine rule, cosine rule and ½ab sin C are printed on the Higher Tier formulae sheet; Pythagoras' theorem and sin, cos and tan for right-angled triangles are not printed, so learn them.

The [Geometry and trigonometry study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-maths-4ma1-geometry-and-trigonometry/) gives one example of each rule. This page concentrates on choosing the right method, rearranging when the unknown is in the denominator, the two-answer case of the sine rule and setting up 3D triangles. Use it alongside the [Trigonometry and Pythagoras revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-trigonometry-and-pythagoras-theorem-edexcel-igcse-maths-revision-notes/) and the [Trigonometry and Pythagoras practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-trigonometry-and-pythagoras-theorem-edexcel-igcse-maths-practice/).

Set your calculator to degrees before you start. A calculator in radians gives sin 30 = −0.988.

## Choosing a method

| Triangle | Know | Want | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Right-angled | Two sides | Third side | Pythagoras |
| Right-angled | A side and an angle | A side | sin, cos or tan |
| Right-angled | Two sides | An angle | sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹ or tan⁻¹ |
| Any (Higher) | A side and its opposite angle, plus one more | Side or angle | Sine rule |
| Any (Higher) | Two sides and the included angle, or three sides | Side or angle | Cosine rule |

## Pythagoras' theorem (both tiers)

In a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse c (the side opposite the right angle): **a² + b² = c²**. To find the hypotenuse, add the squares; to find a shorter side, subtract.

**Worked example 1 (distance between points).** Find the distance between P(−2, 3) and Q(4, −5).

```
across: 4 − (−2) = 6     up: −5 − 3 = −8
PQ = √(6² + 8²) = √100 = 10 units
```

**Worked example 2 (isosceles triangle).** A triangle has sides 13 cm, 13 cm and 10 cm. Find its area.

The line of symmetry from the apex meets the base at its midpoint at 90°, making two right-angled triangles with hypotenuse 13 and base 5.

```
height = √(13² − 5²) = √144 = 12 cm
area = ½ × 10 × 12 = 60 cm²
```

## Right-angled trigonometry (both tiers)

Label the sides from the angle you are using: **hypotenuse** (opposite the right angle), **opposite** (facing the angle) and **adjacent** (next to the angle, not the hypotenuse).

```
sin θ = opp / hyp     cos θ = adj / hyp     tan θ = opp / adj
```

**Worked example 3 (unknown on the bottom).** In a right-angled triangle, the angle 35° is opposite a side of 8 cm. Find the hypotenuse h.

```
sin 35° = 8 / h
h = 8 / sin 35° = 13.947... = 13.9 cm (3 s.f.)
```

A common error is h = 8 × sin 35° = 4.59 cm. A hypotenuse must be the longest side, so 4.59 cm is impossible.

**Worked example 4 (finding an angle).** A 5 m ladder stands with its foot 1.6 m from a vertical wall. Find the angle it makes with the ground.

```
cos θ = adj / hyp = 1.6 / 5 = 0.32
θ = cos⁻¹(0.32) = 71.3°
```

### Bearings (both tiers)

Draw a north line at every point. Bearings problems usually reduce to a right-angled triangle with north–south and east–west sides.

**Worked example 5.** B is 7 km east and 4 km north of A. Find the bearing of B from A.

```
angle between east and AB: tan⁻¹(4 / 7) = 29.7°
bearing = 90° − 29.7° = 060° (to the nearest degree)
```

Check: B is north of east from A, so the bearing must be between 000° and 090°.

## Obtuse angles (Higher tier only)

```
sin(180° − θ) = sin θ        cos(180° − θ) = −cos θ        tan(180° − θ) = −tan θ
```

So sin 130° = sin 50° = 0.766 and cos 130° = −cos 50° = −0.643. The cosine of an obtuse angle is negative, so if the cosine rule gives a negative cosine, the angle is obtuse. Because sin θ is the same for θ and 180° − θ, the inverse sine on a calculator only gives the acute answer.

## Elevation and depression (Higher tier only)

Both angles are measured **from the horizontal**: up for elevation, down for depression. The angle of depression from A to B equals the angle of elevation from B to A (alternate angles).

**Worked example 6.** From the top of a cliff 60 m high, the angle of depression of a boat is 18°. How far is the boat from the foot of the cliff?

```
tan 18° = 60 / d
d = 60 / tan 18° = 184.66... = 185 m (3 s.f.)
```

## Sine rule and the two-answer case (Higher tier only)

```
a / sin A = b / sin B = c / sin C       (or sin A / a = sin B / b for an angle)
```

**Worked example 7.** In triangle ABC, a = 7 cm, b = 9 cm and A = 40°. Find B.

```
sin B = 9 sin 40° / 7 = 0.8264...
B = 55.7°  or  B = 180° − 55.7° = 124.3°
```

Both are possible, because 40° + 124.3° is less than 180°. The two triangles have C = 84.3° (third side 10.8 cm) or C = 15.7° (third side 2.95 cm). An exam question will describe the triangle (for example, "angle B is obtuse") when it wants one of them. Always check whether 180° minus your answer is possible.

## Cosine rule and area (Higher tier only)

```
a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A        cos A = (b² + c² − a²) / 2bc
area = ½ab sin C
```

**Worked example 8.** A triangle has sides 8 cm, 11 cm and 15 cm. Find the largest angle and the area.

```
largest angle is opposite 15:
cos C = (8² + 11² − 15²) / (2 × 8 × 11) = −40 / 176 = −0.2272...
C = 103.1°   (negative cosine, so obtuse)
area = ½ × 8 × 11 × sin 103.1° = 42.8 cm²
```

**Worked example 9 (area backwards).** A triangle has sides 8 cm and 10 cm and area 30 cm². Find the angle between those sides.

```
30 = ½ × 8 × 10 × sin C, so sin C = 0.75
C = 48.6°  or  131.4°
```

Unless the question rules one out, both angles give an area of 30 cm².

## Three dimensions (Higher tier only)

Find a right-angled triangle **inside** the solid, draw it separately and label it. The angle between a line and a plane is the angle between the line and its projection (its "shadow") on the plane.

**Worked example 10.** A cuboid has a base 4 cm by 3 cm and a height of 12 cm. Find the length of a space diagonal and the angle it makes with the base.

```
base diagonal = √(4² + 3²) = 5 cm
space diagonal = √(5² + 12²) = 13 cm   (or √(4² + 3² + 12²) in one step)
angle with base: the projection is the base diagonal
tan θ = 12 / 5, θ = 67.4°
```

## Exam technique

- **"Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures"**: keep full calculator values in intermediate steps and round only at the end.
- **"Show that"**: give the answer to more figures than the stated value, for example 13.94... before saying it rounds to 13.9.
- **"Calculate"**: write the rule with the numbers substituted before using the calculator.
- Label your triangle with the sides you know and want before choosing sin, cos or tan.

## Common mistakes

- Adding squares when finding a shorter side.
- Multiplying when the unknown is on the bottom of the fraction.
- Labelling the adjacent and hypotenuse the wrong way round.
- Accepting only the acute answer from sin⁻¹ when an obtuse angle is possible.
- Using the cosine rule with the angle not between the two given sides.
- In 3D, using a slanted line as the projection on the base.
