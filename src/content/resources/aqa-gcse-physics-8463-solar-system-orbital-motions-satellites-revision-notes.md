## 4.8.1.1 Our solar system

- **One star** (the Sun), **eight planets**, **dwarf planets** (e.g. Pluto), and **moons** (natural satellites).
- The solar system is a **small part of the Milky Way galaxy**.
- Size order: moon < planet < Sun < solar system < galaxy.

**Formation chain**

```
nebula (dust + gas) → gravity pulls it together → particles fall in and speed up
→ centre denser and hotter → protostar → hot and dense enough → hydrogen fuses to helium
→ energy released → star shines
```

**Stability (main sequence)**

```
inwards:  gravitational collapse
outwards: expansion due to fusion energy
balanced → equilibrium → stable size for most of the star's life
```

Less fusion energy → star contracts. More fusion energy → star expands.

## 4.8.1.2 The life cycle of a star

Mass decides the path. Start for every star: **nebula → protostar → main sequence**.

```
Sun-sized:   main sequence → red giant → white dwarf → black dwarf
Massive:     main sequence → red super giant → supernova → neutron star OR black hole
```

| Stage | One-line explanation |
|---|---|
| Red giant / red super giant | Core hydrogen runs low; star expands; surface cools so it looks red |
| White dwarf | Outer layers drift off; small, hot, dense core remains |
| Black dwarf | White dwarf cooled until it gives out no significant energy |
| Supernova | Massive star collapses then explodes |
| Neutron star | Very dense remnant of a supernova |
| Black hole | Remnant of the most massive stars; not even light escapes |

**Making elements**

1. Main sequence: hydrogen → helium.
2. Later stages: fusion makes heavier elements **up to iron**.
3. **Heavier than iron → only in a supernova.**
4. Supernova **distributes** elements through the universe → new stars and planets form from it.

## 4.8.1.3 Orbits and satellites

- **Gravity** provides the force that keeps planets and satellites in circular orbits.
- No gravity → straight-line motion.

| | Planet | Moon | Artificial satellite |
|---|---|---|---|
| Orbits | Sun | A planet | Usually the Earth |
| Origin | Natural | Natural | Made and launched by people |
| Held by | Sun's gravity | Planet's gravity | Earth's gravity |

Similar: all orbit, all held by gravity, none produces its own light.

**Higher tier only: constant speed, changing velocity**

```
direction changes continuously → velocity (a vector) changes → acceleration
→ caused by gravity towards the centre → force at 90° to motion, so speed unchanged
```

**Higher tier only: speed and radius**

- Stable orbit: each radius has one speed.
- Speed changes → **radius must change**.
- Smaller radius → stronger gravity → **faster**. Larger radius → **slower**.

**Orbit calculation**

```
distance in one orbit = 2 π r
speed = 2 π r / time for one orbit   (time in seconds)
days → s: × 24 × 3600
```

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Nebula | Cloud of dust and gas from which stars form |
| Protostar | Hot, dense ball of gas before fusion starts |
| Main sequence | Stable stage when fusion balances gravitational collapse |
| Equilibrium | Inward and outward effects balanced |
| Supernova | Explosion of a massive star |
| Natural satellite | A moon |
| Artificial satellite | A satellite made and launched by people |
| Velocity | Speed in a given direction (a vector) |

## Exam traps

- Solar system and galaxy swapped.
- "The nebula exploded to form the Sun."
- Stability answers naming one effect, or omitting "balance".
- "Red giants are hotter."
- A Sun-sized star going supernova.
- All elements made in supernovae (only those heavier than iron).
- (Higher tier only) "The speed changes" in a circular orbit.
- (Higher tier only) Faster object in a larger orbit.
- Days not converted to seconds.

## Quick self-test

1. List what the solar system contains.
2. What is a nebula?
3. What force pulls a nebula together?
4. Why is a main sequence star stable?
5. Give the life cycle of a star the size of the Sun after the main sequence.
6. What can remain after a supernova?
7. Where are elements heavier than iron produced?
8. How do elements made in stars reach new planets?
9. What force keeps an artificial satellite in orbit?
10. Give one distinction between a moon and an artificial satellite.
11. (Higher tier only) Why is a satellite in a circular orbit accelerating?
12. (Higher tier only) A satellite moves to a smaller stable orbit. What happens to its speed?

### Answers

1. One star (the Sun), eight planets, dwarf planets and moons.
2. A cloud of dust and gas.
3. Gravity (gravitational attraction).
4. Gravitational collapse is balanced by expansion due to fusion energy (equilibrium).
5. Red giant → white dwarf → black dwarf.
6. A neutron star or a black hole.
7. In a supernova.
8. The supernova distributes them through the universe; new stars and planets form from that material.
9. The Earth's gravity.
10. A moon is natural; an artificial satellite is made by people (or: a moon orbits a planet; an artificial
    satellite usually orbits the Earth).
11. Its direction, and so its velocity, changes all the time; a changing velocity is an acceleration.
12. It increases.
