## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State two ways in which a potential difference can be induced across the ends of a conductor. **[2]**

**2.** A magnet rests inside a coil connected to a sensitive ammeter. What does the ammeter show?

A: a steady current

B: no current

C: a current that keeps reversing

D: a current that increases slowly

**[1]**

**3.** Explain why a wire moved along the direction of a magnetic field has no pd induced across it. **[1]**

**4.** State the type of current produced by an alternator and by a dynamo. **[2]**

**5.** An alternator produces an output with a frequency of 60 Hz. Calculate the time taken for one rotation of its coil. **[2]**

**6.** A coil is connected to a voltmeter but not to any other component. A magnet is pushed into it. State what the voltmeter shows and whether a current flows. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A student pushes the north pole of a bar magnet into a coil connected to a centre-zero ammeter. The needle moves to the right.

**(a)** State what the needle does when the magnet is pulled out again. **[1]**

**(b)** State what the needle does when the south pole is pushed in. **[1]**

**(c)** The student pushes the north pole in more quickly. Describe and explain the change in the reading. **[2]**

**(d)** Suggest two other changes that would give a larger reading. **[2]**

**8.** When the north pole of a magnet is pushed into a coil in a complete circuit, the induced current produces a magnetic field.

**(a)** State the pole formed at the end of the coil nearest the magnet. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the student feels a force opposing the movement of the magnet. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how this is consistent with the conservation of energy. **[2]**

**9.** A simple alternator has a coil rotating at a steady rate of 20 turns per second in a magnetic field.

**(a)** Calculate the period of the output. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe the shape of the graph of pd against time for two complete turns. **[2]**

**(c)** The coil is rotated at 40 turns per second. State two ways in which the graph changes. **[2]**

**(d)** State the position of the coil at which the induced pd is zero, and explain why. **[2]**

**10.** A bicycle has a dynamo driven by the wheel, which lights a lamp.

**(a)** Describe the graph of pd against time for the dynamo while the cyclist rides at a steady speed. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the lamp becomes brighter when the cyclist goes faster. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the lamp goes out when the bicycle stops. **[1]**

**11.** Describe how electricity is generated in a coal-fired power station. Your answer should include the energy transfers involved. **[5]**

**12.** A student designs a small wind generator. She can change the number of turns on the coil, the coil area and the magnet.

**(a)** Suggest three changes to her design that would increase the induced pd for the same wind speed. **[3]**

**(b)** On a calm day the generator produces no output. Explain why, using the generator effect. **[2]**

**(c)** Give one difference between how the turbine is turned in a wind generator and in a nuclear power station. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Moving the conductor relative to a magnetic field (so it cuts field lines) [1]; placing the conductor in a changing magnetic field [1]. **[2]**

**2.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** The wire does not cut any magnetic field lines (no change of field through it) [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Alternator: alternating current (ac) [1]. Dynamo: direct current (dc) [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Period = 1 ÷ frequency = 1 ÷ 60 [1] = **0.017 s** (0.0167 s) [1]. **[2]**

**6.** The voltmeter shows a reading (a pd is induced) [1], but no current flows because the circuit is not complete [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* A voltmeter takes almost no current, so this is effectively an open circuit. The induced pd exists whether or not current can flow.

**7. (a)** It moves to the left [1].

**(b)** It moves to the left [1].

**(c)** The needle moves further (a larger current) [1], because the field lines are cut faster, so a larger pd is induced [1].

**(d)** Any two: use a stronger magnet; use a coil with more turns; use a coil with a larger area [1] [1]. **[6]**

**8. (a)** A north pole [1].

**(b)** The induced current's field opposes the field of the magnet [1], so like poles face each other and repel the approaching magnet [1].

**(c)** Work must be done against the opposing force to move the magnet [1]; this work is transferred to electrical energy in the circuit, so energy is not created [1]. **[5]**

**9. (a)** Period = 1 ÷ 20 [1] = **0.050 s** [1].

**(b)** A smooth wave that rises above zero and falls below zero [1], with two complete cycles, each taking 0.050 s [1].

**(c)** The peaks are taller (larger peak pd) [1]; the waves are closer together (period halves to 0.025 s) [1].

**(d)** When the plane of the coil is perpendicular to the magnetic field [1], because the sides of the coil are moving along the field lines and are not cutting them at that instant [1]. **[8]**

**10. (a)** A series of humps [1] that are all on the same side of zero (the pd never reverses) [1].

**(b)** The dynamo turns faster, so field lines are cut faster [1], a larger pd is induced and a larger current flows through the lamp [1].

**(c)** There is no relative movement between the coil and the magnet, so no pd is induced [1]. **[5]**

**11.** Coal is burned, transferring energy from its chemical store [1] to heat water in a boiler [1]. The water becomes high-pressure steam [1], which turns a turbine (kinetic energy) [1]. The turbine turns coils of wire between magnets in the generator, inducing a pd and transferring energy electrically [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Keep the stages in order. Answers that say "the steam makes electricity" miss the turbine and generator marks.

**12. (a)** Use more turns on the coil [1]; use a coil of larger area [1]; use a stronger magnet [1].

**(b)** The blades do not turn, so there is no relative movement between the coil and the magnet [1], and no field lines are cut, so no pd is induced [1].

**(c)** In a wind generator moving air turns the turbine directly; in a nuclear power station steam from water heated by fission turns it [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying a stationary magnet in a coil produces a current.
- Confusing an induced pd with an induced current in an open circuit.
- Drawing dynamo outputs that cross the time axis.
- Changing only the peak or only the period when the rotation speed changes.
- Vague factors such as "better magnet" or "more power".
- Missing the turbine stage in a power station description.
