These notes condense section 5.3, Irrecoverable debts, from Topic 5 of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Accounting (4AC1) specification, Issue 1, October 2016. In Paper 2 these adjustments come as notes under a trial balance. Full worked examples are in the [irrecoverable debts study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-accounting-4ac1-irrecoverable-debts-4ac1-eop/); exam-style questions are in the [irrecoverable debts practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-accounting-4ac1-irrecoverable-debts-4ac1-eop-practice/). Amounts are in dollars without a sign, and all figures are invented.

## The three outcomes

- 5.3 a: explain why it is necessary to provide a provision for irrecoverable debts.
- 5.3 b: distinguish between an irrecoverable debt and a provision for an irrecoverable debt.
- 5.3 c: calculate and record both in the books of account.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Irrecoverable debt | A specific amount owed by a named customer that will not be received; written off as an expense |
| Provision for irrecoverable debts | An estimate of trade receivables that may not be collected; a credit balance deducted from trade receivables |
| Write-off | Debit irrecoverable debts, credit the customer's account |
| Increase in provision | Extra expense: debit income statement, credit provision |
| Decrease in provision | Income or reduced expense: debit provision, credit income statement |
| Net trade receivables | Trade receivables less the provision, shown in current assets |
| Allowance for doubtful debt | The newer name the specification's glossary gives for the provision |

## Order of work from a trial balance

1. Write off further debts in the notes. New trade receivables = trial balance figure − write-off.
2. Irrecoverable debts for the year = trial balance figure + write-off.
3. Provision required = rate × new trade receivables.
4. Change = provision required − trial balance provision.
5. Positive change: expense. Negative change: income.
6. Current assets: new trade receivables less provision required.

Reminder: trade receivables 30,400; write-off 400; provision 3%; opening provision 1,050. Receivables 30,000; provision 900; decrease 150; net receivables 29,100.

## Why the provision is necessary (5.3 a)

- Prudence: receivables are not shown at more than the business expects to collect, and profit is not overstated.
- Accruals: the expected loss is matched with the year of the sales that caused it.
- Users such as owners and lenders get a realistic current assets figure.

## Debt or provision (5.3 b)

| Feature | Irrecoverable debt | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Certainty | Known loss | Estimate |
| Customer | Named | None named |
| Customer's account | Closed | Unchanged |
| Income statement | Whole amount is an expense | Only the change |
| Statement of financial position | Already out of trade receivables | Deducted from trade receivables |

## Effects to state

| Adjustment | Profit | Net trade receivables |
|---|---|---|
| Write-off of a debt | Down by the debt | Down by the debt (less any provision saved) |
| Increase in provision | Down by the increase | Down by the increase |
| Decrease in provision | Up by the decrease | Up by the decrease |

Check: the change in profit from these items equals the change in net trade receivables.

## Presenting the figures

- Irrecoverable debts: one line among expenses, with the trial balance amount and any note write-off added together.
- Increase in provision: a separate expense line labelled "increase in provision for irrecoverable debts".
- Decrease in provision: added after gross profit as income, or deducted from expenses, labelled "decrease in provision"; follow the layout the question uses.
- Statement of financial position: three figures in current assets, trade receivables, less provision, and the net amount.

## Exam traps

- Charging the whole new provision when an opening provision exists.
- Applying the rate before deducting the write-off.
- Forgetting the debts already written off in the trial balance.
- Putting the provision among current liabilities.
- Treating a decrease in the provision as an expense.
- Writing a definition of each term for a "distinguish" question without contrasting them.
- Calculating a provision on other receivables; it relates only to trade receivables.

## Self-test

1. State the double entry to write off a debt of 450 owed by K. Osei.
2. Trade receivables are 24,000 after write-offs. The provision is 5% and the opening provision is 900. State the change and its effect on profit.
3. Trade receivables are 18,600 before a write-off of 600. The provision is 4%. Calculate the provision required.
4. Give two reasons why a provision is made.
5. The opening provision is 2,500. The provision required is 2,200. State the double entry for the change.
6. State where the provision appears in the statement of financial position.
7. A trial balance shows irrecoverable debts 1,140. A further 360 is written off. What is the expense for the year?
8. Year 1 provision 1,600 (first year). Year 2 required 1,750. What is charged in each year?
9. A business charges its whole new provision of 3,000 when the opening provision was 2,600. By how much is profit understated?
10. Give one feature that distinguishes a provision from an irrecoverable debt.
11. Trade receivables 40,000, provision 2,000 before adjustment. A write-off of 1,000 is made and the provision kept at 5%. Calculate the change in net trade receivables.

### Answers

1. Debit irrecoverable debts 450; credit K. Osei 450.
2. Required 1,200; increase of 300; profit falls by 300.
3. 4% × (18,600 − 600) = 4% × 18,000 = 720.
4. Prudence (do not overstate assets or profit) and accruals (match the loss to the year of the sales).
5. Debit provision for irrecoverable debts 300; credit income statement 300.
6. Deducted from trade receivables in current assets.
7. 1,140 + 360 = 1,500.
8. Year 1: 1,600. Year 2: the increase of 150.
9. By 2,600 (only the increase of 400 should be charged).
10. A provision names no customer and leaves customers' accounts unchanged (or: it is an estimate, not a known loss).
11. Receivables 39,000; provision 1,950; net 37,050 against 38,000 before, a fall of 950 (1,000 write-off less 50 fall in provision).
