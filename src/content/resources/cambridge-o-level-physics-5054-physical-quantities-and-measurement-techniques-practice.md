## Section A

**1.** State the difference between a scalar quantity and a vector quantity. **[2]**

**2.** Sort these quantities into scalars and vectors: energy, momentum, speed, weight, temperature, acceleration, time, gravitational field strength. **[4]**

**3.** A micrometer sleeve shows 4.5 mm and the thimble division on the datum line is 37.

**(a)** State the reading in millimetres. **[1]**
**(b)** Express this reading in metres. **[1]**

**4.** Name the most suitable instrument for measuring each of the following: **(a)** the length of a school corridor, **(b)** the diameter of a pencil lead, **(c)** the length of a pendulum string. **[3]**

**5.** Explain why a digital timer operated by light gates gives a more reliable value than a hand-operated stopwatch for the time a trolley takes to pass through a gate. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A student finds the volume of a small stone by displacement. The measuring cylinder reads 35.0 cm³ before the stone is added and 47.5 cm³ after.

**(a)** Calculate the volume of the stone. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe how the student should carry out the measurement to obtain an accurate result. **[3]**

**7.** A packet of 500 sheets of paper is 48.0 mm thick.

**(a)** Calculate the thickness of one sheet. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why this method gives a more precise value than measuring one sheet with a ruler. **[2]**

**8.** A student times 20 oscillations of a pendulum three times. The results are 26.2 s, 26.6 s and 26.4 s.

**(a)** State what is meant by one oscillation. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the period of the pendulum. **[3]**
**(c)** State where the student should place a marker to start and stop timing, and explain why. **[2]**

**9.** Two forces act at the same point: 9.0 N horizontally to the right and 12.0 N vertically upwards.

**(a)** Calculate the magnitude of the resultant force. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the angle between the resultant and the 9.0 N force. **[2]**

## Section C

**10.** A walker goes 240 m due east and then 70 m due south.

**(a)** State the total distance walked. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the magnitude of the walker's displacement from the start. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the direction of the displacement, as an angle south of east. **[2]**

**11.** A boat is steered straight across a river at 4.0 m/s relative to the water. The river flows at 3.0 m/s at right angles to the boat's heading. Describe how to find the boat's resultant velocity by scale drawing, and state the result you would expect. **[5]**

**12.** A student says that a force of 6 N and a force of 8 N acting at right angles to each other must have a resultant of 14 N. Explain why the student is wrong and give the correct value. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** A scalar has **magnitude (size) only** [1]; a vector has **both magnitude and direction** [1].

**2.** Scalars: energy, speed, temperature, time [2]. Vectors: momentum, weight, acceleration, gravitational field strength [2]. (Lose 1 mark per error in each group, minimum 0.)

**3. (a)** 4.5 + 37 × 0.01 = **4.87 mm** [1].
**(b)** 4.87 × 10⁻³ m (0.00487 m) [1].

**4. (a)** Tape measure [1]. **(b)** Micrometer [1]. **(c)** Metre rule [1].

**5.** A hand-operated stopwatch includes the operator's **reaction time**, a few tenths of a second, which is large compared with a short interval [1]; light gates start and stop the timer **electronically at the exact moment** the trolley interrupts the beam [1].

**6. (a)** 47.5 − 35.0 = **12.5 cm³** [1].
**(b)** Any three: read the cylinder **at eye level** from the **bottom of the meniscus** [1]; lower the stone **gently on a thread** so no water splashes out [1]; make sure the stone is **fully submerged** with no trapped air bubbles [1]; use the **narrowest cylinder** the stone fits in, for finer divisions [1].

**7. (a)** 48.0 ÷ 500 [1] = **0.096 mm** [1].
**(b)** One sheet is far thinner than the smallest ruler division (1 mm), so a direct reading is impossible [1]; measuring 500 sheets spreads the reading uncertainty over all of them, so the value for one sheet is 500 times more precise [1].

**8. (a)** A complete swing from one side, to the other side, and back to the starting position [1].
**(b)** Mean time = (26.2 + 26.6 + 26.4) ÷ 3 [1] = 26.4 s [1]; period = 26.4 ÷ 20 = **1.32 s** [1].
**(c)** At the **centre of the swing** (the lowest point) [1], because the bob moves **fastest** there, so the moment it passes is easiest to judge [1].

**9. (a)** R = √(9.0² + 12.0²) = √225 [1] = **15.0 N** [1].
**(b)** tan θ = 12.0 ÷ 9.0 = 1.33 [1]; θ = **53.1°** above the 9.0 N force [1].

**10. (a)** 240 + 70 = **310 m** [1].
**(b)** √(240² + 70²) = √62 500 [1] = **250 m** [1].
**(c)** tan θ = 70 ÷ 240 [1]; θ = **16.3° south of east** [1].

**11.** Choose and state a scale, for example 1 cm to 0.5 m/s [1]. Draw an arrow 8.0 cm long for the 4.0 m/s velocity across the river [1]. From its tip, draw an arrow 6.0 cm long at right angles for the 3.0 m/s current [1]. Join the tail of the first arrow to the tip of the second and measure its length and angle [1]. Expected result: **5.0 m/s at about 37° downstream** from the straight-across direction [1].

**12.** The forces are **not in the same direction**, so they cannot simply be added; 14 N would only be correct if they acted along the same line in the same direction [1]. At right angles they combine by Pythagoras [1]: √(6² + 8²) = √100 = **10 N** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reading the thimble as tenths of a millimetre instead of hundredths.
- Giving the final cylinder reading as the stone's volume.
- Dividing the total pendulum time by the wrong number of oscillations, or timing only one.
- Adding perpendicular vectors directly instead of using Pythagoras.
- Leaving the direction off a vector answer, or not saying which line the angle is measured from.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these questions taken from past papers?**
No. They are original questions written for practice on subtopic 1.1. They do not copy the exam's structure, question count or mark tariffs.

**Do I need trigonometry for resultant vectors?**
Yes, for the direction: tan θ = opposite ÷ adjacent. Alternatively, a careful scale drawing measured with a protractor is accepted where the question allows it.

**Why is weight a vector but mass a scalar?**
Weight is a force, so it acts in a direction (downwards, towards the centre of the Earth). Mass is an amount of matter and has no direction.
