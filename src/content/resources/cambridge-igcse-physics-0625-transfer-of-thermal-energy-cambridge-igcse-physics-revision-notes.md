These notes condense section 2.3, Transfer of thermal energy (2.3.1 to 2.3.4), of the Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Supplement content, marked **Extended only**, is examined on Papers 2 and 4. Experiments and model answers are in the [study guide on transfer of thermal energy](/resources/cambridge-igcse-physics-0625-transfer-of-thermal-energy-cambridge-igcse-physics/); the [Thermal physics revision notes](/resources/igcse-physics-thermal-physics-revision-notes/) cover the whole of Topic 2.

## The three processes at a glance

| | Conduction | Convection | Radiation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main in | solids | liquids and gases | anything; needs no medium |
| How | particles pass energy to neighbours *(Extended: lattice vibrations, free electrons)* | heated fluid expands, becomes less dense and rises; cooler fluid sinks | infrared waves emitted and absorbed |
| Through a vacuum? | no | no | yes |
| Example | metal pan base | room heater, sea breeze | warmth from the Sun |

## Conduction (2.3.1)

- Good conductors: metals. Bad conductors (insulators): plastic, wood, wool, air, water.
- Core experiments: rods of different materials heated equally (wax or pins at the far end); ice at the bottom of a tube of water heated at the top; paper wrapped round a wood-and-brass rod.

*(Extended only)*

- **All solids:** lattice vibrations passed from particle to particle.
- **Metals:** also **free (delocalised) electrons** carry energy quickly through the lattice.
- **Gases and most liquids:** particles far apart; energy passed only by occasional collisions; no lattice, no free electrons → poor conduction.
- **Intermediate solids:** glass, brick, concrete, stone conduct better than insulators, worse than metals.

## Convection (2.3.2)

1. Fluid near the source is heated and **expands**.
2. Its **density decreases**; it **rises**.
3. Cooler, **denser** fluid **sinks** to replace it.
4. A **convection current** circulates.

Experiments: coloured crystal in water heated at one side; smoke drawn down one chimney and up the other above a candle; paper spiral turning above a heater.

Convection cannot happen in solids (particles cannot flow) or in a vacuum.

## Radiation (2.3.3)

- Thermal radiation = **infrared** radiation.
- **All objects** emit it; hotter objects emit more.
- No medium needed.

| Surface | Emitter | Absorber | Reflector |
|---|---|---|---|
| dull black | best | best | worst |
| shiny white/silver | worst | worst | best |

*(Extended only)*

- **Constant temperature:** rate of receiving energy = rate of transferring it away.
- Receives faster than it loses → temperature **rises**; loses faster → temperature **falls**.
- **Rate of emission** increases with **surface temperature** and **surface area**.
- **Earth's temperature:** balance between incoming solar radiation and infrared emitted from the surface; affected by greenhouse gases, cloud cover and reflective ice and snow.
- Experiments: identical black and shiny cans of hot water cooling (emitters); identical cans of cold water near an infrared heater (absorbers); keep volume, distance and starting temperature the same.

## Applications (2.3.4)

**Core**

- Saucepan: metal base conducts; plastic or wooden handle insulates.
- Room heater: placed low; warm air rises, cool air sinks; convection current heats the room.
- Fridge freezer compartment at the top: cold, dense air sinks through the cabinet.
- Double glazing and loft insulation: trapped air is a poor conductor and cannot circulate easily.

*(Extended only)* **More than one process**

- **Fire:** radiation into the room; convection of hot gases up the chimney; conduction through the grate.
- **Car radiator:** conduction from the coolant through the metal; convection as air flows over the fins.
- **Vacuum flask:** vacuum stops conduction and convection; silvered surfaces reduce radiation; stopper reduces convection and evaporation.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Thermal conductor | Material that transfers thermal energy well by conduction |
| Thermal insulator | Material that conducts thermal energy badly |
| Convection current | Circulation of fluid caused by density differences from heating |
| Infrared radiation | Electromagnetic radiation emitted by all objects; thermal radiation |
| Lattice vibrations (Extended) | Vibrations of particles in a solid passed on to neighbours |
| Free electrons (Extended) | Delocalised electrons in metals that carry energy through the lattice |

## Exam traps

- "Heat rises": write "warm air (or water) rises because it is less dense".
- Convection in a solid: impossible.
- Radiation needs no medium; conduction and convection do.
- Black and dull: best absorber **and** best emitter; shiny: best reflector.
- Air is a poor conductor; insulation works by **trapping** air so it cannot move by convection.
- *(Extended)* Free electrons only in metals.
- *(Extended)* At constant temperature energy still flows, at equal rates in and out.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the process by which energy from the Sun reaches the Earth.
2. Why does a metal spoon in hot tea feel hot at the handle end?
3. Explain why a saucepan has a plastic handle.
4. Explain why the freezer compartment is at the top of an old-style fridge.
5. Which surface is the best emitter of infrared?
6. Why are houses in hot countries often painted white?
7. Explain why trapped air is a good insulator.
8. *(Extended)* Name the two mechanisms of conduction in a metal.
9. *(Extended)* Why is conduction poor in gases?
10. *(Extended)* State the condition for an object to stay at a constant temperature.
11. *(Extended)* Give **two** factors that increase the rate at which an object emits radiation.
12. *(Extended)* Explain how a vacuum flask reduces energy transfer by conduction and by radiation.

### Answers

1. **Radiation** (infrared).
2. Metal is a good conductor; energy is **conducted** along the spoon.
3. Plastic is a **thermal insulator**, so the handle stays cool.
4. Air cooled by the freezer becomes **denser and sinks**, setting up a **convection current** that cools the whole cabinet.
5. **Dull black.**
6. White surfaces **reflect** infrared and absorb little, so the house stays cooler.
7. Air is a **poor conductor**, and trapped air **cannot move**, so convection is prevented.
8. **Lattice vibrations** and **free (delocalised) electrons**.
9. Particles are **far apart**, so energy passes only in occasional collisions.
10. It must transfer energy away at the **same rate as it receives** energy.
11. A higher **surface temperature** and a larger **surface area**.
12. The **vacuum** stops conduction (no particles); the **silvered walls** reflect infrared, reducing radiation.
