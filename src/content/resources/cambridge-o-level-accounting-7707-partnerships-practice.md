## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** State **two** disadvantages of forming a partnership. **[2]**

**2.** Explain the importance of a partnership agreement. **[2]**

**3.** State the purpose of an appropriation account. **[2]**

**4.** Interest on capital is allowed at 6% per year. Calculate the interest for:

**(a)** a partner with capital of $45,000 **[1]**

**(b)** a partner with capital of $25,000. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why interest on a partner's loan to the business is not shown in the appropriation account. **[2]**

**6.** State where each item appears in a partnership's financial statements.

**(a)** A partner's salary **[1]**

**(b)** Interest on drawings **[1]**

**(c)** A loan from a partner, repayable in four years **[1]**

**(d)** A debit balance on a partner's current account **[1]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** Chloe and Dev are in partnership, sharing residual profits and losses in the ratio 2 : 3. Their agreement provides for:

- interest on capital at 4% per year
- a salary for Chloe of $9,000 per year
- interest on drawings: Chloe $240, Dev $360 for the year.

Their fixed capitals are Chloe $50,000 and Dev $30,000. On 1 October 2027 their current accounts had credit balances of Chloe $1,500 and Dev $900. Drawings for the year were Chloe $24,800 and Dev $18,000. The profit for the year ended 30 September 2028 was $41,000.

**(a)** Prepare the appropriation account for the year ended 30 September 2028. **[7]**

**(b)** Prepare the partners' current accounts in columnar form for the year, bringing down the balances on 1 October 2028. **[7]**

**(c)** Prepare an extract from the statement of financial position at 30 September 2028 showing the partners' capital and current accounts. **[3]**

**8.** Elif and Femi are partners. Before any charge for interest on Femi's loan, the profit for the year was $52,000. Femi lent the business $8,000 at 5% interest per year. The interest has not been paid.

**(a)** Calculate the profit for the year. **[2]**

**(b)** State where the loan from Femi appears in the statement of financial position. **[1]**

**(c)** Femi says the loan interest should be in the appropriation account. Explain why he is wrong. **[2]**

**9.** **(a)** Explain **two** differences between a partner's capital account and current account. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why partners often keep fixed capital accounts. **[2]**

**10.** Gita and Hari share residual profits and losses equally. Their agreement provides interest on capital (Gita $1,500, Hari $1,000), a salary for Hari of $7,500, and interest on drawings (Gita $100, Hari $200). The profit for the year was $6,000.

**(a)** Prepare the appropriation account. **[5]**

**(b)** Explain why the partners still receive interest on capital and Hari's salary even though the residual is a loss. **[2]**

**11.** Ivan is a sole trader running a printing business. Jana has offered to join him as a partner, bringing $20,000 of capital and marketing skills. Advise Ivan whether he should form a partnership with Jana. Justify your answer. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Profits are shared between the partners [1]. Partners may disagree, which slows decisions [1]. Also accept: each partner is bound by the others' decisions; unlimited liability for ordinary partnerships; capital still limited compared with a company. **[2]**

**2.** It sets out in advance how profits are shared and other rights, such as interest and salaries [1], which reduces the risk of disputes between partners later [1]. **[2]**

**3.** To show how the profit for the year is divided between the partners [1], after interest on capital, salaries and interest on drawings, in the agreed ratio [1]. **[2]**

**4.** **(a)** $45,000 × 6% = $2,700 [1]

**(b)** $25,000 × 6% = $1,500 [1] **[2]**

**5.** The loan is a liability of the business, so interest on it is a cost of running the business, like interest on a bank loan [1]. It is charged as an expense before profit for the year is found, not as a share of that profit [1]. **[2]**

**6.** **(a)** Appropriation account (deducted) and credited to the partner's current account [1]

**(b)** Appropriation account (added) and debited to the partner's current account [1]

**(c)** Non-current liabilities [1]

**(d)** In the capital section of the statement of financial position, deducted from the other current account balances [1] **[4]**

**8.** **(a)** Interest $8,000 × 5% = $400 [1]; profit for the year $52,000 − $400 = **$51,600** [1].

**(b)** Under non-current liabilities [1].

**(c)** The loan is a debt of the business, and interest on it is an expense that must be charged before profit is found [1]. Putting it in the appropriation account would overstate profit for the year by $400 [1]. **[5]**

**9.** **(a)** The capital account records the long-term capital each partner has invested, while the current account records yearly items such as interest, salaries, shares of profit and drawings [1] [1]. The capital account balance usually stays the same, while the current account balance changes every year and can become a debit balance [1] [1].

**(b)** Interest on capital is calculated on a stable figure [1], and the partners' long-term investment is kept separate from yearly withdrawals, so it is clear how much each has committed [1]. **[6]**

Interest on drawings added [1]; interest on capital and salary deducted [1]; residual loss $3,700 [1]; Gita $1,850 [1]; Hari $1,850 [1].

**(b)** The agreement gives interest and salaries before the residual is calculated [1]; the residual loss is then shared in the ratio and debited to the current accounts, so each partner's total share reflects the actual profit of $6,000 [1]. **[7]**

**11.** In favour: Jana's $20,000 would let Ivan buy equipment or expand without borrowing [1], and her marketing skills could increase sales, which Ivan may lack [1]. Against: Ivan would share profits and decisions, and he would be bound by Jana's business decisions under unlimited liability [1]. Judgement: form the partnership if they sign a clear agreement on profit sharing, salaries and duties, because the extra capital and skills are likely to outweigh the shared profit [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting interest on a partner's loan in the appropriation account.
- Deducting interest on drawings instead of adding it.
- Sharing the whole profit in the ratio before deducting interest and salaries.
- Showing a debit current account balance as a positive figure.
- Forgetting to bring down the current account balances.
