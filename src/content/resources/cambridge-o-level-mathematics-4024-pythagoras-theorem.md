This guide covers section 6.1, Pythagoras' theorem, in the Trigonometry topic of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The syllabus has one outcome: **know and use Pythagoras' theorem**. It is not in the List of formulas, so you must remember it. It is also the foundation for the later sections on right-angled triangles, bearings, vectors and three-dimensional problems. After this guide, use the [Pythagoras' theorem revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-pythagoras-theorem-revision-notes/) and the [Pythagoras' theorem practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-pythagoras-theorem-practice/).

## What section 6.1 asks for

1. State the theorem and apply it to find any side of a right-angled triangle.
2. Decide whether a triangle is right-angled from its three sides.
3. Use the theorem in practical and coordinate problems.
4. Leave answers as surds when asked, and give other answers to 3 significant figures.

## The theorem

In a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides.

```
a² + b² = c²
```

Here c is the **hypotenuse**: the longest side, opposite the right angle. The letters a and b are the two shorter sides. The theorem only works for right-angled triangles.

Before you start, label the sides. Find the right angle, then mark the side opposite to it as c.

## Finding the hypotenuse

Square the two shorter sides, add, then take the square root.

### Worked example 1

A right-angled triangle has shorter sides 9 cm and 12 cm. Find the hypotenuse.

```
c² = 9² + 12² = 81 + 144 = 225
c = √225 = 15 cm
```

### Worked example 2

A right-angled triangle has shorter sides 5 cm and 8 cm. Find the hypotenuse to 3 significant figures.

```
c² = 25 + 64 = 89
c = √89 = 9.43 cm
```

## Finding a shorter side

Subtract the square of the known shorter side from the square of the hypotenuse.

### Worked example 3

The hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle is 25 cm and one shorter side is 7 cm. Find the other side.

```
b² = 25² − 7² = 625 − 49 = 576
b = √576 = 24 cm
```

### Worked example 4

The hypotenuse is 12 cm and one shorter side is 7 cm. Find the other side to 3 significant figures.

```
b² = 144 − 49 = 95
b = √95 = 9.75 cm
```

The most common slip is to add when you should subtract. Check: the hypotenuse must be the longest side of the triangle.

## Pythagorean triples

Sets of whole numbers that satisfy a² + b² = c² are Pythagorean triples. The ones to recognise are:

| Triple | Check |
|---|---|
| 3, 4, 5 | 9 + 16 = 25 |
| 5, 12, 13 | 25 + 144 = 169 |
| 8, 15, 17 | 64 + 225 = 289 |
| 7, 24, 25 | 49 + 576 = 625 |
| 20, 21, 29 | 400 + 441 = 841 |

Multiples of triples are also triples: 6, 8, 10 and 9, 12, 15 and 30, 40, 50. Spotting a triple saves time and acts as a check.

## Exact answers

Sometimes the question asks for the answer as a surd, or says "exactly". Simplify by taking out square factors.

### Worked example 5

The shorter sides of a right-angled triangle are 2 cm and 6 cm. Find the hypotenuse as a simplified surd.

```
c² = 4 + 36 = 40
c = √40 = √(4 × 10) = 2√10 cm
```

The decimal value is 6.32 cm.

## Testing for a right angle

If the sides of a triangle satisfy a² + b² = c², where c is the longest side, then the triangle is right-angled. This is the converse of the theorem.

### Worked example 6

Which of these triangles is right-angled? (a) 8, 15, 17 (b) 7, 9, 12

```
(a) 8² + 15² = 64 + 225 = 289 = 17², so it is right-angled.
(b) 7² + 9² = 49 + 81 = 130, but 12² = 144, so it is not right-angled.
```

Always square the longest side separately and compare it with the sum of the other two squares.

## Using the theorem in problems

Draw a right-angled triangle, label it, and then apply the theorem.

### A ladder

### Worked example 7

A ladder 6.5 m long leans against a vertical wall. Its top reaches 6 m up the wall. How far is the foot of the ladder from the wall?

```
distance² = 6.5² − 6² = 42.25 − 36 = 6.25
distance = 2.5 m
```

### The diagonal of a rectangle

### Worked example 8

A rectangle is 24 cm by 10 cm. Find the length of its diagonal.

```
d² = 24² + 10² = 576 + 100 = 676
d = 26 cm
```

### The distance between two points

The distance between points (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂) is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle with sides |x₂ − x₁| and |y₂ − y₁|.

### Worked example 9

Find the distance between (−2, 3) and (4, −5).

```
horizontal difference = 4 − (−2) = 6
vertical difference = −5 − 3 = −8, so the side is 8
distance² = 36 + 64 = 100
distance = 10
```

### Isosceles and equilateral triangles

A line from the apex of an isosceles triangle to the midpoint of its base creates two right-angled triangles.

### Worked example 10

An isosceles triangle has sides 13 cm, 13 cm and 10 cm. Find the height and the area.

```
half the base = 5 cm
h² = 13² − 5² = 144, so h = 12 cm
area = 1/2 × 10 × 12 = 60 cm²
```

### Worked example 11

An equilateral triangle has side 8 cm. Find its height and area to 3 significant figures.

```
half the base = 4 cm
h² = 64 − 16 = 48, so h = 6.93 cm
area = 1/2 × 8 × 6.928 = 27.7 cm²
```

### A rhombus

The diagonals of a rhombus cross at right angles and bisect each other. If the diagonals are 16 cm and 30 cm, each side is the hypotenuse of a triangle with sides 8 cm and 15 cm.

```
side = √(8² + 15²) = √289 = 17 cm
```

## Squares on the sides

The theorem can be read as a statement about areas: the square drawn on the hypotenuse has the same area as the two squares on the other sides put together.

### Worked example 13

Squares are drawn on the sides of a right-angled triangle. The squares on the two shorter sides have areas 64 cm² and 36 cm². Find the side of the square on the hypotenuse.

```
area of the large square = 64 + 36 = 100 cm²
side = √100 = 10 cm
```

The shorter sides are 8 cm and 6 cm, which gives the triangle 6, 8, 10.

## Shortcuts and routes

### Worked example 14

A field is a rectangle 40 m by 30 m. A person walks from one corner to the opposite corner, first along the two edges and then straight across the diagonal. How much shorter is the diagonal route?

```
along the edges: 40 + 30 = 70 m
diagonal: √(40² + 30²) = √2500 = 50 m
saving = 70 − 50 = 20 m
```

### Worked example 15

A boat sails 12 km east and then 5 km north. How far is it from its starting point in a straight line?

```
distance² = 12² + 5² = 169
distance = 13 km
```

Directions such as east and north are at right angles to each other, so this creates a right-angled triangle automatically.

## Checking a right angle on site

Builders use the 3, 4, 5 triple to make a right angle. A corner is square if the sides 3 m and 4 m have a distance of 5 m between their ends. Any multiple works, such as 6, 8, 10 or 9, 12, 15.

## Using algebra

### Worked example 16

The sides of a right-angled triangle are x cm, (x + 7) cm and 13 cm, where 13 cm is the hypotenuse. Find x.

```
x² + (x + 7)² = 13²
x² + x² + 14x + 49 = 169
2x² + 14x − 120 = 0
x² + 7x − 60 = 0
(x + 12)(x − 5) = 0
x = 5 (the length cannot be negative)
```

The sides are 5 cm, 12 cm and 13 cm.

## Common mistakes

- Adding when you should subtract to find a shorter side.
- Forgetting the square root at the end.
- Applying the theorem to a triangle that has no right angle.
- Choosing the wrong side as the hypotenuse.
- Writing (x + 7)² as x² + 49 in an algebra problem.
- Rounding too early.
- Leaving the answer as a square when the length is required.

## Exam technique

- **Calculate** the length: show the squares, the sum or difference, and the square root.
- **Show that** a triangle is right-angled: show that a² + b² = c² exactly.
- **Give your answer as a surd**: simplify.
- Draw the right-angled triangle on diagrams that do not show one.
