Data for every question: Ar values Ag 107.87, Al 26.98, C 12.01, Cl 35.45, Cu 63.55, Fe 55.85, H 1.01, Mn 54.94, N 14.01, O 16.00. Molar volume of an ideal gas at STP = 22.7 dm³ mol⁻¹. Give numerical answers to three significant figures except where a question says otherwise.

## Questions

**1.** Solid sodium hydrogencarbonate reacts with dilute sulfuric acid, forming aqueous sodium sulfate, water and carbon dioxide. Write the balanced equation with state symbols. **[2]**

**2.** Manganese can be extracted by heating trimanganese tetroxide with aluminium powder:

3Mn₃O₄(s) + 8Al(s) → 4Al₂O₃(s) + 9Mn(l)

Calculate the mass of manganese produced from 50.0 g of Mn₃O₄, and the mass of aluminium needed to react with it completely. **[4]**

**3.** Hydrogen peroxide solution decomposes in the presence of a catalyst:

2H₂O₂(aq) → 2H₂O(l) + O₂(g)

Calculate the volume of oxygen, measured at STP, released when 50.0 cm³ of hydrogen peroxide solution, [H₂O₂] = 1.20 mol dm⁻³, decomposes completely. **[3]**

**4.** 25 cm³ of ethene is mixed with 100 cm³ of oxygen and ignited. The products return to the starting conditions, where water is a liquid.

C₂H₄(g) + 3O₂(g) → 2CO₂(g) + 2H₂O(l)

Identify the reactant in excess, state the volume of it left over, and state the volume of carbon dioxide formed. **[3]**

**5.** A 10.00 cm³ sample of phosphoric acid is titrated with sodium hydroxide solution, [NaOH] = 0.125 mol dm⁻³. The end point is reached after 24.80 cm³ has been added.

H₃PO₄(aq) + 3NaOH(aq) → Na₃PO₄(aq) + 3H₂O(l)

**(a)** Calculate [H₃PO₄] in the sample. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the concentration of sodium phosphate in the solution at the end point. **[2]**

**6.** 30.0 cm³ of silver nitrate solution, [AgNO₃] = 0.200 mol dm⁻³, is mixed with 25.0 cm³ of calcium chloride solution, [CaCl₂] = 0.100 mol dm⁻³.

2AgNO₃(aq) + CaCl₂(aq) → 2AgCl(s) + Ca(NO₃)₂(aq)

**(a)** Identify the limiting reactant. Show your working. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the theoretical yield of silver chloride in grams. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the concentration of silver ions left in solution after the precipitate forms. Assume the volumes add. **[2]**

**7.** A student makes a salt from an acid and an insoluble base, then filters and crystallises it.

**(a)** Distinguish between the theoretical yield and the experimental yield of the salt. **[1]**
**(b)** Suggest two reasons why the experimental yield could be lower than the theoretical yield. **[2]**

**8.** Urea, CO(NH₂)₂, is made industrially from ammonia and carbon dioxide:

2NH₃(g) + CO₂(g) → CO(NH₂)₂(s) + H₂O(l)

In a trial run, 34.0 kg of ammonia reacts with 66.0 kg of carbon dioxide. 51.0 kg of urea is collected.

**(a)** Show that ammonia is the limiting reactant. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the theoretical yield of urea in kg. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage yield. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the mass of carbon dioxide, in kg, left unreacted if all the ammonia reacts. **[2]**
**(e)** Calculate the atom economy of this process for urea. **[2]**

**9.** Iron(III) oxide can be reduced to iron by three different reducing agents.

Route A: Fe₂O₃(s) + 3CO(g) → 2Fe(s) + 3CO₂(g)
Route B: Fe₂O₃(s) + 2Al(s) → 2Fe(l) + Al₂O₃(s)
Route C: Fe₂O₃(s) + 3H₂(g) → 2Fe(s) + 3H₂O(g)

**(a)** Calculate the atom economy for iron in each route. Give your answers to 1 decimal place. **[3]**
**(b)** State how the atom economy of a route relates to the waste it produces. **[1]**
**(c)** Route C has the highest atom economy. Suggest two other factors a steelmaker would weigh before choosing it. **[2]**

**10.** A strip of aluminium foil of mass 0.270 g is placed in 40.0 cm³ of copper(II) chloride solution, [CuCl₂] = 0.300 mol dm⁻³, and left until no further change occurs.

2Al(s) + 3CuCl₂(aq) → 2AlCl₃(aq) + 3Cu(s)

**(a)** Show that copper(II) chloride is the limiting reactant. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the theoretical yield of copper in grams. **[1]**
**(c)** The copper is filtered off and weighed straight away. Its mass is 0.791 g. Calculate the percentage yield. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest why the value in (c) is above 100 %. **[1]**
**(e)** Calculate the mass of aluminium left unreacted. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Correct formulas with 2NaHCO₃, 2H₂O and 2CO₂ [1]; all state symbols correct [1].
**2NaHCO₃(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → Na₂SO₄(aq) + 2H₂O(l) + 2CO₂(g)** **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Check each state symbol against the wording: the solid is (s), and water at room temperature is (l), not (aq).

**2.** M(Mn₃O₄) = 3(54.94) + 4(16.00) = 228.82 g mol⁻¹, so n = 50.0 ÷ 228.82 = 0.2185 mol [1]. n(Mn) = 0.2185 × 9/3 = 0.6555 mol [1]; m(Mn) = 0.6555 × 54.94 = **36.0 g** [1]. n(Al) = 0.2185 × 8/3 = 0.5827 mol; m(Al) = 0.5827 × 26.98 = **15.7 g** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each ratio (9/3, 8/3) as its own step, so a slipped molar mass does not hide a correct method.

**3.** n(H₂O₂) = 1.20 × 0.0500 = 0.0600 mol [1]; n(O₂) = 0.0600 ÷ 2 = 0.0300 mol [1]; V = 0.0300 × 22.7 = **0.681 dm³** (681 cm³) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert 50.0 cm³ to 0.0500 dm³ first; otherwise the answer is a thousand times too large.

**4.** O₂ needed = 25 × 3 = 75 cm³ [1]; **oxygen is in excess, with 25 cm³ left over** [1]; CO₂ formed = 25 × 2 = **50 cm³** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the volume ratio directly; converting to moles and back only adds rounding.

**5. (a)** n(NaOH) = 0.125 × 0.02480 = 0.003100 mol [1]; n(H₃PO₄) = 0.003100 ÷ 3 = 0.001033 mol [1]; [H₃PO₄] = 0.001033 ÷ 0.01000 = **0.103 mol dm⁻³** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** n(Na₃PO₄) = 0.001033 mol; total volume = 10.00 + 24.80 = 34.80 cm³ [1]; concentration = 0.001033 ÷ 0.03480 = **0.0297 mol dm⁻³** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b), divide by the combined volume at the end point, not by either starting volume.

**6. (a)** n(AgNO₃) = 0.200 × 0.0300 = 0.00600 mol; n(CaCl₂) = 0.100 × 0.0250 = 0.00250 mol [1]. AgNO₃ needed for all the CaCl₂ = 2 × 0.00250 = 0.00500 mol, which is less than 0.00600 mol, so **CaCl₂ is limiting** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** n(AgCl) = 2 × 0.00250 = 0.00500 mol; M(AgCl) = 143.32 g mol⁻¹ [1]; mass = 0.00500 × 143.32 = **0.717 g** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Ag⁺ left = 0.00600 − 0.00500 = 0.00100 mol [1]; volume = 55.0 cm³, so [Ag⁺] = 0.00100 ÷ 0.0550 = **0.0182 mol dm⁻³** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a), a bare name is unsupported; compare amounts that allow for the 2 : 1 ratio.

**7. (a)** Theoretical yield is the maximum amount calculated from the limiting reactant; experimental yield is the mass of salt actually obtained [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** One reason, such as salt left dissolved after crystallisation [1]; a second, such as salt lost on the filter paper or in transfer, or incomplete reaction [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Human error" or "experimental error" is too vague; name a specific step where product is lost.

**8. (a)** n(NH₃) = 34.0 × 10³ ÷ 17.04 = 1995 mol; n(CO₂) = 66.0 × 10³ ÷ 44.01 = 1500 mol [1]. CO₂ needed for all the NH₃ = 1995 ÷ 2 = 997.7 mol [1]. 997.7 mol < 1500 mol available, so CO₂ is in excess and **NH₃ is limiting** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** n(urea) = 997.7 mol; M(urea) = 60.07 g mol⁻¹ [1]; theoretical yield = 997.7 × 60.07 = 59 930 g = **59.9 kg** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** 51.0 ÷ 59.93 × 100 = **85.1 %** [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** CO₂ left = 1500 − 997.7 = 502.0 mol [1]; mass = 502.0 × 44.01 = 22 090 g = **22.1 kg** [1]. **[2]**
**(e)** Total reactant mass = 2(17.04) + 44.01 = 78.09 g mol⁻¹ [1]; atom economy = 60.07 ÷ 78.09 × 100 = **76.9 %** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert kg to g before n = m/M, then back to kg for the answer.

**9. (a)** M(Fe₂O₃) = 159.70 g mol⁻¹ and 2Fe = 111.70 g mol⁻¹. Route A: 111.70 ÷ (159.70 + 3 × 28.01) × 100 = 111.70 ÷ 243.73 × 100 = **45.8 %** [1]. Route B: 111.70 ÷ (159.70 + 2 × 26.98) × 100 = 111.70 ÷ 213.66 × 100 = **52.3 %** [1]. Route C: 111.70 ÷ (159.70 + 3 × 2.02) × 100 = 111.70 ÷ 165.76 × 100 = **67.4 %** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** The lower the atom economy, the larger the share of reactant mass that ends up as by-products, so the more waste [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** One factor, such as the cost and energy of making the hydrogen [1]; a second, such as the rate, the hazard of a flammable gas, or the percentage yield achieved [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Include both the 2 in front of Fe and the reducing agent's coefficient; omitting either changes every value.

**10. (a)** n(Al) = 0.270 ÷ 26.98 = 0.01001 mol; n(CuCl₂) = 0.300 × 0.0400 = 0.0120 mol [1]. Al needed for all the CuCl₂ = 0.0120 × 2/3 = 0.00800 mol [1]. 0.00800 mol < 0.01001 mol, so aluminium is in excess and **CuCl₂ is limiting** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** n(Cu) = 0.0120 mol; mass = 0.0120 × 63.55 = **0.763 g** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** 0.791 ÷ 0.7626 × 100 = **104 %** [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** The copper was not dried, so its mass includes water or solution [1]. **[1]**
**(e)** Al left = 0.01001 − 0.00800 = 0.00201 mol [1]; mass = 0.00201 × 26.98 = **0.0542 g** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A yield above 100 % needs a physical cause: name the extra mass, such as water or leftover aluminium.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Altering a formula to balance an equation, or leaving out state symbols.
- Using the mole ratio the wrong way up, especially for ratios such as 9/3, 8/3 or 2/3.
- Putting cm³ into n = cV, or using one solution's volume when the question wants the combined volume.
- Naming a limiting reactant from masses or raw moles instead of moles divided by coefficients.
- Calculating a yield from the reactant in excess.
- Mixing kilograms and grams within one calculation.
- Missing a coefficient when totalling reactant molar masses for atom economy.
