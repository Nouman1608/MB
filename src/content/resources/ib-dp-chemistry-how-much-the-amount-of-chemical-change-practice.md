Use these values throughout. Ar: H 1.01, C 12.01, N 14.01, O 16.00, Al 26.98, P 30.97, S 32.07, Cl 35.45, Na 22.99, Mn 54.94, Cu 63.55, Zn 65.38, Ag 107.87, Ba 137.33. Molar volume of an ideal gas at STP = 22.7 dm³ mol⁻¹. Give numerical answers to 3 significant figures unless told otherwise.

## Questions

**1.** Solid magnesium hydroxide reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid to form aqueous magnesium chloride and water. Write a balanced equation, including state symbols. **[2]**

**2.** Manganese can be extracted by heating trimanganese tetroxide with aluminium powder:

3Mn₃O₄(s) + 8Al(s) → 4Al₂O₃(s) + 9Mn(l)

Calculate the mass of manganese produced from 50.0 g of Mn₃O₄, and the mass of aluminium needed to react with it completely. **[4]**

**3.** Hydrogen peroxide solution decomposes in the presence of a catalyst:

2H₂O₂(aq) → 2H₂O(l) + O₂(g)

Calculate the volume of oxygen, measured at STP, released when 50.0 cm³ of hydrogen peroxide solution, [H₂O₂] = 1.20 mol dm⁻³, decomposes completely. **[3]**

**4.** 25 cm³ of ethene is mixed with 100 cm³ of oxygen and ignited. The products return to the starting conditions, where water is a liquid.

C₂H₄(g) + 3O₂(g) → 2CO₂(g) + 2H₂O(l)

Identify the reactant in excess, state the volume of it left over, and state the volume of carbon dioxide formed. **[3]**

**5.** A 10.00 cm³ sample of phosphoric acid is titrated with sodium hydroxide solution, [NaOH] = 0.125 mol dm⁻³. The end point is reached after 24.80 cm³ has been added.

H₃PO₄(aq) + 3NaOH(aq) → Na₃PO₄(aq) + 3H₂O(l)

**(a)** Calculate [H₃PO₄] in the sample. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the concentration of sodium phosphate in the solution at the end point. **[2]**

**6.** 40.0 cm³ of barium chloride solution, [BaCl₂] = 0.200 mol dm⁻³, is mixed with 30.0 cm³ of sodium sulfate solution, [Na₂SO₄] = 0.250 mol dm⁻³.

BaCl₂(aq) + Na₂SO₄(aq) → BaSO₄(s) + 2NaCl(aq)

**(a)** Identify the limiting reactant. Show your working. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the theoretical yield of barium sulfate in grams. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the concentration of barium ions left in solution after the precipitate forms. Assume the volumes add. **[2]**

**7.** A student makes a salt from an acid and an insoluble base, then filters and crystallises it.

**(a)** Distinguish between the theoretical yield and the experimental yield of the salt. **[1]**
**(b)** Suggest two reasons why the experimental yield could be lower than the theoretical yield. **[2]**

**8.** Urea, CO(NH₂)₂, is made industrially from ammonia and carbon dioxide:

2NH₃(g) + CO₂(g) → CO(NH₂)₂(s) + H₂O(l)

In a trial run, 34.0 kg of ammonia reacts with 66.0 kg of carbon dioxide. 51.0 kg of urea is collected.

**(a)** Show that ammonia is the limiting reactant. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the theoretical yield of urea in kg. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage yield. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the mass of carbon dioxide, in kg, left unreacted if all the ammonia reacts. **[2]**
**(e)** Calculate the atom economy of this process for urea. **[2]**

**9.** Zinc chloride can be made from hydrochloric acid by three routes.

Route A: Zn(s) + 2HCl(aq) → ZnCl₂(aq) + H₂(g)
Route B: ZnO(s) + 2HCl(aq) → ZnCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l)
Route C: ZnCO₃(s) + 2HCl(aq) → ZnCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g)

**(a)** Calculate the atom economy of each route. Give your answers to 1 decimal place. **[3]**
**(b)** State how the atom economy of a route relates to the waste it produces. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest two reasons why a manufacturer might not choose the route with the highest atom economy. **[2]**

**10.** A piece of copper wire of mass 0.500 g is placed in 50.0 cm³ of silver nitrate solution, [AgNO₃] = 0.150 mol dm⁻³, and left until no further change occurs.

Cu(s) + 2AgNO₃(aq) → Cu(NO₃)₂(aq) + 2Ag(s)

**(a)** Show that silver nitrate is the limiting reactant. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the theoretical yield of silver in grams. **[1]**
**(c)** The silver is filtered off and weighed straight away. Its mass is 0.842 g. Calculate the percentage yield. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest why the value in (c) is above 100 %. **[1]**
**(e)** Calculate the mass of copper left unreacted. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Correct formulas and balancing with 2HCl and 2H₂O [1]; all state symbols correct [1].
**Mg(OH)₂(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl₂(aq) + 2H₂O(l)** **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Credit for state symbols needs every symbol right, so check that the solid base is (s) and the water is (l), not (aq).

**2.** M(Mn₃O₄) = 3(54.94) + 4(16.00) = 228.82 g mol⁻¹, so n = 50.0 ÷ 228.82 = 0.2185 mol [1]. n(Mn) = 0.2185 × 9/3 = 0.6555 mol [1]; m(Mn) = 0.6555 × 54.94 = **36.0 g** [1]. n(Al) = 0.2185 × 8/3 = 0.5827 mol; m(Al) = 0.5827 × 26.98 = **15.7 g** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the ratio (9/3, 8/3) on its own line so your method is visible even if a molar mass slips.

**3.** n(H₂O₂) = 1.20 × 0.0500 = 0.0600 mol [1]; n(O₂) = 0.0600 ÷ 2 = 0.0300 mol [1]; V = 0.0300 × 22.7 = **0.681 dm³** (681 cm³) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert 50.0 cm³ to 0.0500 dm³ first; otherwise the answer is a thousand times too large.

**4.** O₂ needed = 25 × 3 = 75 cm³ [1]; **oxygen is in excess, with 25 cm³ left over** [1]; CO₂ formed = 25 × 2 = **50 cm³** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* For gases at the same temperature and pressure, use the volume ratio directly; converting to moles and back only adds rounding.

**5. (a)** n(NaOH) = 0.125 × 0.02480 = 0.003100 mol [1]; n(H₃PO₄) = 0.003100 ÷ 3 = 0.001033 mol [1]; [H₃PO₄] = 0.001033 ÷ 0.01000 = **0.103 mol dm⁻³** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** n(Na₃PO₄) = 0.001033 mol; total volume = 10.00 + 24.80 = 34.80 cm³ [1]; concentration = 0.001033 ÷ 0.03480 = **0.0297 mol dm⁻³** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b), divide by the combined volume at the end point, not by either starting volume.

**6. (a)** n(BaCl₂) = 0.200 × 0.0400 = 0.00800 mol; n(Na₂SO₄) = 0.250 × 0.0300 = 0.00750 mol [1]; the ratio is 1 : 1, so the smaller amount, **Na₂SO₄, is limiting** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** n(BaSO₄) = 0.00750 mol; M(BaSO₄) = 233.40 g mol⁻¹ [1]; mass = 0.00750 × 233.40 = **1.75 g** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Ba²⁺ left = 0.00800 − 0.00750 = 0.000500 mol [1]; volume = 70.0 cm³, so [Ba²⁺] = 0.000500 ÷ 0.0700 = **0.00714 mol dm⁻³** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a), compare the amounts in moles before naming the limiting reactant; a bare name is unlikely to earn full credit.

**7. (a)** Theoretical yield is the maximum amount calculated from the limiting reactant; experimental yield is the mass of salt actually obtained [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** One reason, such as some salt staying dissolved in the liquid left after crystallisation [1]; a second reason, such as salt lost on the filter paper or when transferring between containers, or the reaction not going to completion [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Human error" or "experimental error" is too vague; name a specific step where product is lost.

**8. (a)** n(NH₃) = 34.0 × 10³ ÷ 17.04 = 1995 mol; n(CO₂) = 66.0 × 10³ ÷ 44.01 = 1500 mol [1]. CO₂ needed for all the NH₃ = 1995 ÷ 2 = 997.7 mol [1]. 997.7 mol < 1500 mol available, so CO₂ is in excess and **NH₃ is limiting** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** n(urea) = 997.7 mol; M(urea) = 60.07 g mol⁻¹ [1]; theoretical yield = 997.7 × 60.07 = 59 930 g = **59.9 kg** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** 51.0 ÷ 59.93 × 100 = **85.1 %** [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** CO₂ left = 1500 − 997.7 = 502.0 mol [1]; mass = 502.0 × 44.01 = 22 090 g = **22.1 kg** [1]. **[2]**
**(e)** Total reactant mass = 2(17.04) + 44.01 = 78.09 g mol⁻¹ [1]; atom economy = 60.07 ÷ 78.09 × 100 = **76.9 %** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert kg to g before n = m/M, then back to kg so the answer matches the unit asked for.

**9. (a)** M(ZnCl₂) = 136.28 g mol⁻¹ and 2HCl = 72.92 g mol⁻¹. Route A: 136.28 ÷ (65.38 + 72.92) × 100 = **98.5 %** [1]. Route B: 136.28 ÷ (81.38 + 72.92) × 100 = **88.3 %** [1]. Route C: 136.28 ÷ (125.39 + 72.92) × 100 = **68.7 %** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** The lower the atom economy, the more reactant mass ends up as by-products, so the more waste [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** One reason, such as route A releasing flammable hydrogen, which is a safety hazard [1]; a second reason, such as the starting material for another route being cheaper or easier to obtain, or another route giving a higher percentage yield [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Include the coefficient 2 for HCl in every denominator; without it all three values come out too high.

**10. (a)** n(Cu) = 0.500 ÷ 63.55 = 0.007868 mol; n(AgNO₃) = 0.150 × 0.0500 = 0.00750 mol [1]. Cu needed for all the AgNO₃ = 0.00750 ÷ 2 = 0.00375 mol [1]. 0.00375 mol < 0.007868 mol, so copper is in excess and **AgNO₃ is limiting** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** n(Ag) = 0.00750 mol; mass = 0.00750 × 107.87 = **0.809 g** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** 0.842 ÷ 0.809 × 100 = **104 %** [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** The silver was not dried, so its mass includes water (or solution) [1]. **[1]**
**(e)** Cu left = 0.007868 − 0.00375 = 0.004118 mol [1]; mass = 0.004118 × 63.55 = **0.262 g** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A yield above 100 % needs a physical reason: name the extra mass, such as water or copper fragments.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Altering a formula to balance an equation, or leaving out state symbols.
- Using the mole ratio the wrong way up, especially for ratios such as 9/3 or 8/3.
- Putting cm³ into n = cV, or using one solution's volume when the question wants the combined volume.
- Naming a limiting reactant from masses instead of moles divided by coefficients.
- Calculating a yield from the reactant in excess.
- Mixing kilograms and grams within one calculation.
- Missing a coefficient when totalling reactant molar masses for atom economy.
