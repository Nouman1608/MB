## Definitions to learn word for word

| Term or description | Matching term or description |
|---|---|
| Market failure | The price mechanism allocates resources in a way that is not best for society |
| Costs paid by the producer or consumer | Private costs |
| Costs dumped on an uncompensated outsider | External costs |
| Private costs plus external costs | Social costs |
| Gains enjoyed by the buyer or user | Private benefits |
| Gains that spill to someone who did not pay | External benefits |
| Private benefits plus external benefits | Social benefits |
| Public good | Non-excludable and non-rival, so private firms cannot easily charge |
| Merit good | Under-consumed because benefits are undervalued; usually has external benefits |
| Demerit good | Over-consumed because harm is underestimated; harms the user and often others |

## Causes at a glance (2.10.2)

| Cause | What goes wrong | Typical result | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public good | Free riders: people use it without paying | Not supplied privately | Defence, navigation beacons |
| Merit good | Benefits undervalued, external benefits ignored | Under-consumption | Education, healthcare |
| Demerit good | Harm underestimated | Over-consumption | Tobacco, alcohol |
| External cost | Third parties bear costs | Over-production and over-consumption | Factory smoke, noisy traffic |
| External benefit | Third parties gain for free | Under-production and under-consumption | A neighbour's well-kept garden raising local house values |
| Abuse of monopoly power | Sole seller restricts output, raises price | Higher price, lower output | A sole local water supplier |
| Factor immobility | Workers or capital cannot move to better uses | Idle resources, unfilled vacancies | A mill closes, workers lack other skills |

## Consequences (2.10.3)

| Situation | Market output vs best output | Word to use |
|---|---|---|
| Demerit good | Too high | Over-consumption |
| External cost | Too high | Over-consumption |
| Merit good | Too low | Under-consumption |
| External benefit | Too low | Under-consumption |

Misallocation means resources are in the wrong uses. Too much goes into harmful goods, and too little into beneficial ones.

## Formulae and quick checks

| Idea | Rule |
|---|---|
| Social benefit | add the external gain to the buyer's own gain |
| Social cost | add the outsider's loss to the producer's own cost |
| External cost share | external cost / social cost x 100 |
| Percentage change | change / original x 100 |
| Equal cost share | total cost / number of households |
| Unemployment rate | unemployed / labour force x 100 |
| Net social gain | social benefit - social cost |

## Method in steps

**Spotting the type of failure**
1. Ask whether non-payers can be stopped from using it. If not, think public good.
2. Ask whether the buyer misjudges the benefit or harm to themselves. If yes, merit or demerit.
3. Ask whether a third party gains or loses without payment. If yes, external benefit or cost.
4. Ask whether there is one seller. If yes, monopoly power.
5. Ask whether workers or capital are stuck. If yes, factor immobility.

**Writing a four-link chain**
1. Private decision differs from the social best.
2. So output or consumption is too high or too low.
3. So resources are misallocated.
4. So society is worse off. Name who loses.

## Worked reminders

**External cost.** A cement works in Country B has a private cost of 40 per tonne and causes dust damage to nearby homes of 12 per tonne. It makes 300 tonnes.

```
Social cost per tonne = 40 + 12 = 52
Total external cost = 12 x 300 = 3,600
Total social cost = 52 x 300 = 15,600
```

The seller counts only 40 per tonne, so the dust damage is ignored.

**External benefit.** A training course gives the trainee a private benefit of 60 and an employer an extra 25.

```
Social benefit = 60 + 25 = 85
If the course costs 70: social net gain = 85 - 70 = 15
```

If the trainee will not pay 70 for a private benefit of 60, the course is not bought. Society loses a net gain of 15.

**Unemployment from immobility.** A town with a labour force of 8,000 has 480 unemployed (6%). A plant closure adds 160 unemployed.

```
New total = 480 + 160 = 640
New rate = 640 / 8,000 = 8%
```

**Monopoly abuse.** A sole bus operator in Town S raises the fare from 50 to 56 and weekly trips fall from 900 to 720. Each trip costs 40.

```
Fare change = (56 - 50) / 50 = 12%
Trips change = (720 - 900) / 900 = -20%
Profit before = (50 - 40) x 900 = 9,000
Profit after = (56 - 40) x 720 = 11,520
```

The seller gains while riders lose trips they valued above cost. That is the failure.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Public good or merit good.** Public goods cannot be restricted to payers. Merit goods can be sold, but are bought too little.
- **Demerit good or external cost.** A demerit good is over-consumed mainly because the user misjudges harm. An external cost harms others. Tobacco can be both.
- **External benefit or private benefit.** Only gains to people who did not buy count as external.
- **Over-consumption or over-production.** Say which side you mean, though both are too much.
- **Occupational or geographical immobility.** Skills against location.

## Sentence frames for explanations

- "Because the good is non-excludable, people can use it without paying, so firms cannot earn revenue and do not supply it."
- "The buyer considers only the private benefit, so the quantity bought is below the quantity that gives the greatest social benefit."
- "The producer ignores the external cost, so the price is below the social cost and too much is produced and consumed."
- "Workers lack the skills for the vacancies, so labour is unemployed while jobs go unfilled, which wastes a factor of production."

Add the name of the good, one named person or group who gains or loses, and one number if the question gives data.

## Quick self-test

1. State what makes a good non-excludable.
2. A firm has a private cost of 34 per unit and an external cost of 9. Find the social cost per unit.
3. In question 2, what percentage of social cost is external, to 1 decimal place?
4. A vaccine gives a private benefit of 26 and an external benefit of 14. What is the social benefit?
5. A flood barrier costs 18,000 and 120 households each value it equally. What is the equal cost share?
6. In question 5, only 24 households pay the equal share. What percentage of the cost is raised?
7. Is tobacco a merit good or a demerit good? Give one reason.
8. A sole supplier raises its price from 40 to 46. What is the percentage rise?
9. Name the type of immobility when a retired miner cannot be hired as a software tester.
10. Does a good with an external benefit tend to be over-consumed or under-consumed?
11. A merit good has a price of 70, a social benefit of 85. By how much does social benefit exceed price?
12. Give one example of an external cost caused by a factory.

### Answers

1. Non-payers cannot be stopped from using it.
2. 34 + 9 = 43.
3. 9 / 43 x 100 = 20.9%.
4. 26 + 14 = 40.
5. 18,000 / 120 = 150 each.
6. 24 x 150 = 3,600, and 3,600 / 18,000 = 20%.
7. A demerit good: users underestimate the harm to their health and it also harms others.
8. (46 - 40) / 40 x 100 = 15%.
9. Occupational immobility (lack of skills).
10. Under-consumed.
11. 85 - 70 = 15.
12. For example smoke or noise that harms neighbours, or river pollution that harms downstream fishers.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a public good only by who pays for it, with no mention of non-excludable or non-rival.
- Saying "pollution" without naming who bears the cost.
- Treating external costs as part of the price the producer pays.
- Giving a government policy answer to a question that asks only for causes or consequences.
- Using a percentage change with the new value as the base.
- Confusing under-consumption with zero consumption.
- Using a famous example of the wrong area, when the syllabus asks for examples of listed areas only.
- Stating a consequence with no chain: say why the quantity is wrong and what happens to resources.
- Missing the units or the word "per" in a cost-per-unit answer.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do you calculate social cost and social benefit?**
Social cost is private cost plus external cost, and social benefit is private benefit plus external benefit. Markets work on private figures only, so market failure appears when the social and private figures differ.

**What are the causes of market failure in the O Level syllabus?**
The causes are public goods, merit goods, demerit goods, external costs, external benefits, abuse of monopoly power and factor immobility.

**What are safe examples of each type of market failure?**
Common examples are defence or lighthouses for public goods, education and healthcare for merit goods, tobacco and alcohol for demerit goods, factory smoke for external costs and one firm controlling local water supply for monopoly abuse.

**What is the consequence of market failure?**
The consequence is misallocation of resources: too many resources go into goods that harm society and too few into goods that benefit it.
