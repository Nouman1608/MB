These questions cover **section 16.9 Prototyping** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Information Technology 9626 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027 (version 4). This is **A Level only** content examined in Paper 3 (Advanced Theory), where calculators are not allowed. All questions can be answered on paper.

Revise first with the [study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-ict-9626-prototyping/) and [revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-ict-9626-prototyping-revision-notes/). The [Cambridge A Level IT hub](/boards/cambridge/a-level/ict/) lists every topic.

## Questions

**1.** State the **four** types of prototyping named in the syllabus. **[4]**

**2.** Describe what is meant by a prototype of a system. **[2]**

**3.** Give **three** reasons why a prototype is useful to a development team. **[3]**

**4.** A travel agency wants a new system for building holiday packages. The staff cannot say exactly what they need, but say they will "know it when they see it".

**(a)** Describe how a throwaway prototype would be used. **[3]**
**(b)** Give **two** advantages of this type of prototype for the travel agency. **[2]**
**(c)** Give **one** disadvantage of this type of prototype. **[1]**

**5.** Explain the difference between evolutionary prototyping and throwaway prototyping. **[3]**

**6.** A government department is building a large tax-advice system. It has three parts (an online form, a calculator and a help centre), each of which can be built by a separate team.

**(a)** Name the type of prototyping that suits this project and explain why. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe how it would be carried out. **[3]**
**(c)** Give **one** problem that might arise at the end of the project. **[1]**

**7.** Explain what is meant by rapid prototyping and give **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage. **[3]**

**8.** A bank is designing a mobile app. The requirements are changing every month as new features are suggested.

Evaluate the use of evolutionary prototyping for this project. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** Evolutionary [1]; incremental [1]; throwaway [1]; rapid. [1] **[4]**

**2.** A working model [1] of all or part of the system, built early, that users can try before the full system is built. [1] **[2]**

**3.** Any three, one mark each: users can see and try something real, so they can say what they need; misunderstandings are found early when changes are cheap; screen and report designs can be tested with users; risky ideas or technology can be tested; users are involved, so they are more likely to accept the result. **[3]**

**4. (a)** A quick prototype with sample data, such as clickable screens for building a package, is built [1]. The staff try it and say what is missing or wrong [1]. The prototype is then discarded and the real system is built using what was learned. [1] **[3]**
**(b)** Any two: the staff see something real, so they can say what they want; the requirements become clearer before the real build; the risk of building the wrong system is reduced; the prototype can be built quickly with simple tools. [1 each] **[2]**
**(c)** Any one: the effort of building the prototype is wasted; it adds time and cost; staff may think the system is nearly finished. [1] **[1]**

**5.** An evolutionary prototype is improved again and again after user feedback [1] until it becomes the finished system [1]; a throwaway prototype is built only to clarify the requirements and is discarded, and the real system is built afresh. [1] **[3]**

**6. (a)** Incremental prototyping [1], because the system divides into separate components that can be built at the same time by different teams. [1] **[2]**
**(b)** A prototype of each component (form, calculator, help centre) is built by its own team [1], tested and refined with users [1], and the finished components are combined into the whole system. [1] **[3]**
**(c)** Any one: the components may not fit together, and integration problems appear late; the way the system was split may turn out to be wrong. [1] **[1]**

**7.** Rapid prototyping is building a prototype as quickly as possible, usually with tools that produce screens and simple behaviour fast [1]. Advantage (any one): users give feedback very quickly; misunderstandings are found early; cheap to change. [1] Disadvantage (any one): quality may be poor; features such as validation and security may be missing; users may expect the real system as quickly. [1] **[3]**

**8.** Advantages (up to 2 marks): the prototype can be changed to include new features each month [1]; bank staff and customers see working versions and give feedback throughout. [1]
Disadvantages (up to 2 marks): there is no clear end date or cost, so the project may run on [1]; the structure may become messy, and security and testing might be neglected, which is serious for a bank. [1]
Judgement (1 mark): for example evolutionary prototyping is a good fit because the requirements keep changing, provided there is firm control of the scope, security and testing. [1]
Maximum 5. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The strongest answers tie each point to the bank scenario and finish with a conclusion that mentions a condition.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying every prototype is discarded.
- Mixing up incremental prototyping and incremental development.
- Writing "it is quicker" with no explanation.
- Naming a type without justifying it.
- Giving the same advantage for every type.

## Frequently asked questions

**What should I include when asked why a type of prototyping is needed?**
Say what situation it suits, such as unclear requirements for evolutionary or throwaway prototyping, or a large system split into parts for incremental prototyping.

**Is prototyping examined in Paper 3?**
Yes. Prototyping is section 16.9 of the A Level content assessed in Paper 3 Advanced Theory.
