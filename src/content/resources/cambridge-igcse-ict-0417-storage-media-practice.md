## Answers

**1.** Pits. **[1]**

**2.** CFast. **[1]**

**3.** C: DVD-RW. **[1]**

**4.** Any two: plastic strip coated with magnetic material (1); wound on reels inside a cartridge (1); serial access (1); very high capacity (1); low cost per gigabyte (1). **[2]**

**5.** Blu-ray uses a blue-violet laser with a shorter wavelength (1), so the pits are smaller and the track is packed more tightly (1). (Credit: some Blu-ray discs have more than one layer.) **[2]**

**6.** Any two: scratches on the surface (1); heat, for example being left in a hot car (1); long exposure to sunlight (1); bending or cracking (1). **[2]**

**7. (a)** CD (credit CD-ROM). **[1]**

**(b)** The data is pressed in at manufacture and cannot be changed (1), so buyers cannot delete or alter the tracks, and the album stays as the band intended (1). **[2]**

**8. (a)** Any two: very small and light, so it adds almost no weight (1); no moving parts, so it is not damaged by vibration (1); large enough capacity for high-definition video (1); fast enough to record video (1). **[2]**

**(b)** The card is removed from the drone and placed in a card reader connected to the computer (or the drone is connected by cable) (1), then the video files are copied onto the computer's storage (1). **[2]**

**(c)** Any one: the card could be lost or damaged (1); cards can fail without warning (1); frees space for the next flight (1). **[1]**

**9. (a)** The laptop's card reader may not support the xD format (1), because xD was used mainly in some older cameras, so the card cannot be read (1). **[2]**

**(b)** Any one: use an external card reader that supports xD (1); connect the old camera to the laptop with a cable (1). **[1]**

**10.** Any four, with points about both: tape has a much larger capacity per cartridge, so fewer items are needed for a large archive (1); tape has a lower cost per gigabyte (1); a BD-R cannot be changed once written, which protects the archive from accidental changes (1); Blu-ray gives direct access to a document, whereas tape needs serial access, which is slower (1); Blu-ray is not affected by magnetic fields, whereas tape is (1); tape needs a special tape drive, which may be expensive (1). **[4]**

**11. (a)** Data on a hard disk is stored as magnetised areas (1); a strong magnetic field can change their direction, corrupting the 1s and 0s (1). **[2]**

**(b)** An optical disc, because data is stored as pits and lands read by a laser, not magnetically; or a solid-state medium, such as an SD card, because data is stored in flash memory (1 for medium, 1 for reason). **[2]**

**12. (a)** A CD holds about 700 MB, which is far less than 3 GB (1). **[1]**

**(b)** DVD (DVD-R) (1), because a single-layer DVD holds about 4.7 GB, enough for the video, and discs are cheap (1). (Credit Blu-ray with a correct reason.) **[2]**

**(c)** Advantage: any one, such as faster to copy and read, or reusable, or robust (1). Disadvantage: any one, such as more expensive per student, or easily lost because of their small size (1). **[2]**

**13.** Any four, each a contrast: magnetic hard disks store data as magnetised areas, whereas memory cards use flash memory (1); hard disks are read using moving heads over spinning platters, whereas memory cards have no moving parts (1); hard disks are sealed inside a drive, whereas memory cards are removable and portable (1); hard disks have larger capacities and lower cost per gigabyte (1); hard disks can be damaged by magnetic fields or knocks, whereas memory cards are robust but easily lost (1). **[4]**

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to learn exact capacities?**
Know the order of capacity and the approximate single-layer sizes: CD about 700 MB, DVD about 4.7 GB, Blu-ray about 25 GB. Questions usually test whether a file fits, not precise figures.

**What is the difference between BD-R and BD-RE?**
BD-R can be written once; BD-RE (rewritable) can be erased and written again many times.
