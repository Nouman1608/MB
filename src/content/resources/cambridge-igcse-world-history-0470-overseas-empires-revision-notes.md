**Key question 1.5, Cambridge IGCSE History 0470:** "Why, and with what effects, did nations gain and expand their
overseas empires in the nineteenth century?" These notes add comparison grids and extra evidence to the core facts
in the [overseas empires revision notes](/resources/cambridge-world-history-overseas-empires-revision-notes/). The
analysis behind them is in the [0470 overseas empires study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-world-history-0470-overseas-empires/);
test yourself with the [0470 overseas empires practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-world-history-0470-overseas-empires-practice/).

## Motives: evidence beyond the obvious

| Motive | Extra evidence |
|---|---|
| Economic | Suez Canal shares bought by Britain (1875); Royal Niger Company on the Niger; groundnuts from Senegal |
| Military | Mahan (1890): sea power needs bases and coaling stations; Guantánamo base from the Platt Amendment |
| Geopolitical | Germany's first African colonies (1884); Fashoda crisis (1898); "effective occupation" rule of the Berlin Conference |
| Religious | Missionary societies; Samuel Ajayi Crowther, first African Anglican bishop (1864) |
| Cultural | Jules Ferry (1885): a "duty" to civilise; McKinley's "benevolent assimilation" (1898) |

**Enablers, not motives:** quinine, steamships, railways, the telegraph, the machine gun.

## Impacts on Africans compared

| | Senegal (France) | Northern Nigeria (Britain) | Congo (Leopold II) |
|---|---|---|---|
| System | Assimilation, direct rule | Indirect rule through emirs | Private colony run for profit |
| Political | Local states conquered; French officials | Emirs kept courts but obeyed British residents | Leopold's agents and the Force Publique held all power |
| Economic | Groundnut exports; taxes; labour for public works | Taxes via emirs; trade tied to Britain | Rubber and ivory quotas; forced labour |
| Social | French schooling for a small elite | Islamic law kept; missions restricted | Hostage-taking, mutilation, famine, mass deaths |
| Resistance | Umar Tall; siege of Médine (1857) | Kano and Sokoto conquered (1903) | Revolts crushed by force |

**Variation inside a colony:** in south-eastern Nigeria British "warrant chiefs" had no traditional authority and were
resented.

## India, 1857: who resisted and why

| Group | Grievance |
|---|---|
| Sepoys | Greased Enfield cartridges; pay; service overseas |
| Rulers | Doctrine of Lapse (Jhansi); Nana Sahib refused the Peshwa's pension |
| Awadh landowners | Lost estates after annexation in 1856 |
| Religious communities | Fear of conversion through missions, schools and reforms |

**Effects:** defeated by 1858, with reprisals on both sides; Crown rule (Government of India Act 1858); no more
annexations, so princely states survived; Victoria Empress of India (1877); army reorganised with more British
troops and more recruits from groups such as Punjabi Sikhs; British more cautious about religion but more separate
from Indians.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Scramble for Africa | Rapid European division of Africa, mainly after 1880 |
| Effective occupation | Berlin Conference rule that a claim needed real control on the ground |
| Assimilation | French policy of turning colonised elites into French citizens in culture |
| Indirect rule | British rule through existing local rulers |
| Warrant chiefs | Chiefs appointed by Britain where none had existed |
| Force Publique | Leopold's army in the Congo, which enforced rubber quotas |
| Doctrine of Lapse | British annexation of states whose rulers had no natural heir |
| Princely states | Indian states ruled by their own princes under British supremacy |
| Yellow press | Sensational US newspapers, such as those of Hearst and Pulitzer |
| Teller Amendment | 1898 pledge not to annex Cuba |
| Platt Amendment | 1901 terms allowing US intervention in Cuba and a base at Guantánamo |
| Benevolent assimilation | McKinley's description of US rule in the Philippines |

## Exam traps

- **Answering half the question.** Every focus point except the first asks "with what effects".
- **Motives as a list.** Link them and say whose motive each was.
- **Treating all of Nigeria as one.** Indirect rule worked differently in the north and the south-east.
- **Teller versus Platt.** Teller (1898) promised no annexation; Platt (1901) allowed intervention.
- **Ending US involvement in 1902.** The specification runs to the 1906 intervention.

## Self-test

1. Why did Britain buy Suez Canal shares in 1875? *To protect the shortest sea route to India.*
2. What did Mahan argue in 1890? *That a great power needed sea power, and so overseas bases and coaling stations.*
3. What did the Berlin Conference mean by "effective occupation"? *A power had to control territory on the ground to
   claim it.*
4. Who was Samuel Ajayi Crowther? *A formerly enslaved Yoruba man who became the first African Anglican bishop in
   1864.*
5. Which export did France encourage in Senegal? *Groundnuts (peanuts).*
6. Who fought Faidherbe at Médine in 1857? *The forces of Umar Tall.*
7. What were warrant chiefs? *Chiefs appointed by the British in areas, such as Igboland, with no traditional chiefs.*
8. Why did Nana Sahib join the rising? *The British refused him the pension of his adoptive father, the last Peshwa.*
9. Give one change to the Indian army after 1857. *More British troops, or more recruits from groups such as Punjabi
   Sikhs.*
10. What was the de Lôme letter? *A letter by the Spanish minister in Washington insulting McKinley, published in
    February 1898.*
11. When did the Philippine-American War begin? *February 1899.*
12. Why did US troops occupy Cuba again in 1906? *A revolt broke out and the USA intervened under the Platt
    Amendment.*
