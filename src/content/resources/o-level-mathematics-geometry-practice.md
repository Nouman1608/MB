These twelve questions cover angles in polygons, isosceles triangles, circle theorems, tangents and similar shapes from Cambridge O Level Mathematics 4024, topic 4. Work through them without looking at the answers. Give a reason for every angle you find, using the words of the theorem. A calculator is not needed for any question. Check each answer against the marks shown, and note any step where you lost a mark so you can revise that theorem next.

---

## Questions

**1.** Find the size of one interior angle of a regular decagon (10 sides). **[2]**

**2.** Each exterior angle of a regular polygon is 24°. **(a)** How many sides does it have? **[1]** **(b)** Find the sum of its interior angles. **[2]**

**3.** Triangle ABC is isosceles with AB = AC. Angle BAC = 48°. **(a)** Find angle ABC. **[1]** **(b)** CB is extended to D. Find angle ABD. **[1]**

**4.** PQRS is a cyclic quadrilateral. Angle PQR = 104°. Find angle PSR, giving a reason. **[2]**

**5.** A straight line touches a circle at P. A chord PS makes an angle of 37° with the tangent. Q is a point on the circle on the opposite side of PS from the 37° angle (in the alternate segment). Find angle PQS, giving a reason. **[2]**

**6.** Two tangents are drawn from a point T to a circle with centre O. They touch the circle at A and B. Angle ATB = 40°. **(a)** Find angle AOB, giving a reason. **[2]** **(b)** Find angle OAB. **[1]**

**7.** Two solids are mathematically similar. Their surface areas are 50 cm² and 200 cm². The smaller solid has a volume of 30 cm³. Find the volume of the larger solid. **[3]**

**8.** The angles of a pentagon are x°, 2x°, 2x°, 100° and 110°. **(a)** Write down the sum of the interior angles of a pentagon. **[1]** **(b)** Form an equation in x and solve it. **[2]** **(c)** Write down the size of the largest angle. **[1]**

**9.** AB is a diameter of a circle with centre O. C is a point on the circle and angle CAB = 32°. The tangent at C meets AB extended at T. **(a)** Find angle ACB, giving a reason. **[2]** **(b)** Find angle ABC. **[1]** **(c)** Find angle COB, giving a reason. **[2]** **(d)** Find angle CTO, giving your reasons. **[3]**

**10.** In triangle ABC, D is on AB and E is on AC, with DE parallel to BC. AD = 6 cm, DB = 3 cm and DE = 8 cm. **(a)** Find BC. **[2]** **(b)** Triangle ADE has area 40 cm². Find the area of quadrilateral DBCE. **[3]**

**11.** Two similar statues are made of the same material. The smaller is 12 cm tall and has mass 96 g. The larger is 30 cm tall. **(a)** Find the mass of the larger statue. **[3]** **(b)** The smaller statue has surface area 144 cm². Find the surface area of the larger one. **[2]**

**12.** A regular hexagon ABCDEF is drawn inside a circle with centre O, with all six vertices on the circle. **(a)** Find the interior angle of the hexagon. **[2]** **(b)** Explain why triangle OAB is equilateral. **[2]** **(c)** Find angle ACB, giving a reason. **[2]** **(d)** Find the acute angle between the tangent at A and the chord AB. **[2]** **(e)** Explain why angle ACD = 90°. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Exterior angle = 360° ÷ 10 = 36° [1]. Interior angle = 180° − 36° = **144°** [1].

*Examiner insight:* the exterior angle is the quicker route, and candidates who use (n − 2) × 180 ÷ n usually slip in the arithmetic.

**2.** **(a)** 360 ÷ 24 = **15 sides** [1]. **(b)** (15 − 2) × 180° [1] = **2340°** [1].

*Examiner insight:* the method mark in (b) needs n − 2 triangles; using 15 × 180 is the common loss.

**3.** **(a)** (180° − 48°) ÷ 2 = **66°** [1]. **(b)** Angle ABD = 180° − 66° = **114°** [1].

*Examiner insight:* the equal angles are opposite the equal sides, so check which two sides are equal before you halve.

**4.** 180° − 104° = **76°** [1], because opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180° [1].

*Examiner insight:* the reason is a separate mark, so write the theorem in words.

**5.** Angle PQS = **37°** [1], by the alternate segment theorem: the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment [1].

*Examiner insight:* "angles are equal" alone does not earn the reason mark; name the theorem.

**6.** **(a)** Angles OAT and OBT are 90°, because a tangent is perpendicular to the radius [1]. Angle AOB = 360° − 90° − 90° − 40° = **140°** [1]. **(b)** Triangle OAB is isosceles (OA = OB, radii), so angle OAB = (180° − 140°) ÷ 2 = **20°** [1].

*Examiner insight:* the right angles at the points of contact are the key step; candidates who miss them cannot start.

**7.** Area ratio 1 : 4, so length ratio 1 : 2 [1]. Volume ratio 1 : 8 [1]. Larger volume = 30 × 8 = **240 cm³** [1].

*Examiner insight:* go from the area ratio back to the length ratio before you cube it; cubing the area ratio is the usual error.

**8.** **(a)** (5 − 2) × 180° = **540°** [1]. **(b)** x + 2x + 2x + 100 + 110 = 540, so 5x + 210 = 540 [1] and x = **66** [1]. **(c)** 2x = **132°** [1].

*Examiner insight:* write the equation with every angle included; a missing angle gives a wrong x but still earns follow-through marks if the method is clear.

**9.** **(a)** Angle ACB = **90°** [1], because the angle in a semicircle is a right angle [1]. **(b)** 180° − 90° − 32° = **58°** [1]. **(c)** Angle COB = 2 × 32° = **64°** [1], because the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference [1]. **(d)** Angle OCT = 90°, because a tangent is perpendicular to the radius [1]. Angle CTO = 180° − 90° − 64° [1] = **26°** [1].

*Examiner insight:* in multi-part circle questions, reasons are marked part by part, so state each one even when the angle looks obvious.

**10.** **(a)** Triangles ADE and ABC are similar, with AB = 9 cm [1]. BC = 8 × 9 ÷ 6 = **12 cm** [1]. **(b)** Length ratio 6 : 9 = 2 : 3, so area ratio 4 : 9 [1]. Area of ABC = 40 × 9 ÷ 4 = 90 cm² [1]. DBCE = 90 − 40 = **50 cm²** [1].

*Examiner insight:* the scale factor must use AB, not DB; AD to DB is a part-to-part ratio.

**11.** **(a)** Length scale factor = 30 ÷ 12 = 2.5 [1]. Mass scales with volume, so 2.5³ = 15.625 [1]. Mass = 96 × 15.625 = **1500 g** [1]. **(b)** Area scale factor = 2.5² = 6.25 [1]. Surface area = 144 × 6.25 = **900 cm²** [1].

*Examiner insight:* decide first whether the quantity is a length, an area or a volume; mass is a volume quantity here.

**12.** **(a)** (6 − 2) × 180° ÷ 6 [1] = **120°** [1]. **(b)** Angle AOB = 360° ÷ 6 = 60° [1]. OA = OB (radii), so the other two angles are equal, each (180° − 60°) ÷ 2 = 60°, and all angles are 60° [1]. **(c)** Angle ACB = 60° ÷ 2 = **30°** [1], because the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference [1]. **(d)** Angle OAB = 60° and the tangent is perpendicular to OA, so the angle is 90° − 60° = 30° [1]. This agrees with the alternate segment theorem, since it equals angle ACB, so the answer is **30°** [1]. **(e)** AD passes through O, so it is a diameter, and the angle in a semicircle is 90° [1].

*Examiner insight:* "show that" and "explain why" parts need a full chain of reasons, not only a final value.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Missing the geometric reason for an angle, or writing "angles are equal" with no theorem named.
- Using the interior angle where the exterior angle is needed.
- Using the area scale factor as the volume scale factor, or using a part-to-part ratio as the scale factor.
- Forgetting that a tangent meets the radius at 90°, so the right angle is never marked.
- Applying the alternate segment theorem to an angle in the wrong segment.
- Not stating that the angle in a semicircle needs a diameter.
- Leaving out a sum of angles in a quadrilateral or polygon when forming an equation.
- Giving answers without units, such as cm² for area.
