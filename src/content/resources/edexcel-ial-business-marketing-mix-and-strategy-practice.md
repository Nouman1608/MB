## Questions

**1.** (a) Define the term marketing mix. **[2]**
(b) State two marketing objectives. **[2]**

**2.** Hollinbrook Juices had sales of £1.2m last year in a market worth £8.0m. Next year the market is forecast at £8.5m and the target is an 18% market share.

**(a)** Calculate last year's market share. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the sales needed to reach the target. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage increase in sales this requires. **[1]**

**3.** Brightholme Pet Foods uses the Boston Matrix, with a growth cut-off of 10% and a relative market share cut-off of 1.0.

| Product | Share | Largest rival | Growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adult kibble | 28% | 14% | 2% |
| Puppy mousse | 9% | 22% | 15% |
| Senior diet | 20% | 16% | 13% |
| Dried treats | 3% | 18% | 1% |

**(a)** Calculate the relative market share of puppy mousse and dried treats. **[2]**
**(b)** Classify all four products. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one decision Brightholme could make using the matrix. **[3]**

**4.** Lumisk Headsets sold the following units (thousands) in years 1 to 6: 5, 22, 60, 95, 98, 97.

**(a)** State which product life cycle stage Lumisk had reached by year 6 and give evidence from the data. **[2]**
**(b)** Analyse two extension strategies Lumisk could use. **[4]**

**5.** Larchwood Lighting makes aluminium desk lamps.

**(a)** Define the term cost/economic manufacture. **[2]**
**(b)** Analyse how concern over resource depletion could change Larchwood's design mix. **[4]**

**6.** Pendarvis Stationers prices a planner with a 40% mark-up on a unit cost of £12.50.

**(a)** Calculate the selling price. **[2]**
**(b)** Unit cost rises by 8% and the mark-up stays at 40%. Calculate the new price. **[2]**
**(c)** At the new price, monthly sales fall from 2,500 to 2,300. Calculate the price elasticity of demand. **[2]**

**7.** Tessmoor Tablets will launch a tablet with features no rival has. Rivals are expected in about nine months. Tessmoor is a new brand. Development cost is £4.2m and unit cost is £60. It is choosing between a launch price of £220 (skimming) and £120 (penetration). Evaluate which strategy Tessmoor should use. **[9]**

**8.** Alderwyn Skincare sells every cream under the Alderwyn name and runs buy-one-get-one-free offers.

**(a)** Identify one type of promotion and one type of branding Alderwyn uses. **[2]**
**(b)** State two benefits of strong branding. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain two ways Alderwyn could build its brand, one of which reflects a social trend in promotion. **[4]**

**9.** Dovecott Software sells payroll software to small firms and a budgeting app to households.

**(a)** Explain one difference between marketing to the small firms and to households. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain one advantage of a niche strategy for the payroll software. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one way Dovecott could develop customer loyalty. **[3]**

**10.** Kilverton Furniture sells a sofa through a four-stage channel at £240 to a wholesaler, who adds 20%. The retailer then adds 50%. Kilverton could instead sell online at £400, paying £60 a sofa for delivery and handling itself.

**(a)** Calculate the wholesaler's selling price and the consumer price. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the extra income per sofa Kilverton would keep by selling online. **[2]**
**(c)** Analyse one effect of this change in distribution. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) The combination of marketing decisions (product, promotion, price, place) [1] a business uses together to meet its objectives [1]. (b) Any two of: increase market share [1], increase revenue, build a brand [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Credit tends to come from saying the elements work together towards objectives, not merely listing the four Ps.

**2.** (a) 1.2 ÷ 8.0 × 100 [1] = **15%** [1]. (b) 0.18 × 8.5 [1] = **£1.53m** [1]. (c) (1.53 − 1.2) ÷ 1.2 × 100 = **27.5%** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Writing the formula line keeps your method visible, which helps if a slip occurs in an early part.

**3.** (a) Puppy mousse 9 ÷ 22 = **0.41** [1]; dried treats 3 ÷ 18 = **0.17** [1]. (b) Adult kibble (2.0, growth 2%) is a cash cow and senior diet (1.25, growth 13%) a star [1]; puppy mousse is a question mark and dried treats a dog [1]. (c) Brightholme could use cash from adult kibble [1] to fund marketing of puppy mousse, which is in a market growing at 15% [1] and could become a star if share rises. The risk is that the spending fails to win share [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers gain credit when each classification quotes both numbers against the cut-offs given in the question.

**4.** (a) **Maturity** [1], because sales growth has slowed to about 3% in year 5 (98 against 95) and then fallen by 1% in year 6 [1]. (b) New variant, such as a wireless model [1], which gives existing buyers a reason to upgrade and slows decline [1]. New market, such as exporting [1], which adds customers who have not yet bought, though it needs distribution and adaptation costs [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Evidence from the data table, such as the growth rate, usually supports the stage better than naming the stage alone.

**5.** (a) Designing a product so it can be made at a cost [1] that allows a profit at a price customers will pay [1]. (b) Waste minimisation, using less aluminium and plastic packaging [1], lowers material costs over time [1]. Designing for recycling or repair, such as parts that separate easily [1], may need redesign cost and may change the lamp's look, so Larchwood must balance cost, function and aesthetics [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Each point tends to gain credit when it names the design mix element it affects.

**6.** (a) 12.50 × 1.4 [1] = **£17.50** [1]. (b) 12.50 × 1.08 = £13.50 [1]; 13.50 × 1.4 = **£18.90** [1]. (c) Price change = 1.40 ÷ 17.50 = +8%, quantity change = −200 ÷ 2,500 = −8% [1]; PED = −8 ÷ 8 = **−1.0** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The mark-up applies to cost, not to the selling price, and showing the percentage changes before dividing makes the elasticity clear.

**7.** Contribution per unit is £160 on skimming and £60 on penetration, so £4.2m is covered after 26,250 units [1] against 70,000 [1]. The case for skimming: the unique features are a USP [1] and early buyers may be less price sensitive, so demand may be inelastic [1]. The case for penetration: Tessmoor is a new brand with no loyalty, so a low price could build share and awareness before rivals arrive in nine months [1], and volume helps spread development cost [1]. Limits: skimming risks few sales if buyers do not know the brand, and penetration leaves little margin to cover the development cost [1]. Judgement: skimming is the better choice if the features are hard to copy and early demand is inelastic, because the launch price covers costs quickly [1], and Tessmoor can lower the price when rivals appear [1]. Penetration would be better if research showed elastic demand. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation answers usually do better when the judgement is tied to a stated factor, such as elasticity or competition, rather than a general preference.

**8.** (a) Sales promotion [1]; corporate (family) branding [1]. (b) Any two of added value [1], ability to charge premium prices [1], reduced price elasticity of demand. (c) Social media: regular posts and replies to customers [1] build a community and, with shareable content that spreads virally, raise awareness cheaply [1]. A USP, such as a unique ingredient [1], differentiates the cream so customers see it as different from rivals [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Each way needs a short explanation of how it builds the brand, as a bare list of methods is likely to earn little.

**9.** (a) Small firms are B2B, so the buyer is a business with few customers and larger orders [1]. Dovecott could use personal selling and demonstrations [1], whereas households are many individuals reached by social media and app stores, with the decision more influenced by price and brand [1]. (b) A niche, such as small dental practices, is a small segment with specific needs [1], so Dovecott can add tailored features and charge a higher price than for mass-market software [1]. (c) After-sales support, such as quick help with payroll problems [1], builds trust and reduces switching [1], so customers renew each year, which lowers the cost of winning sales [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A B2B and B2C comparison is clearer when it contrasts the same feature in both, such as the number of buyers.

**10.** (a) 240 × 1.2 = **£288** [1]; 288 × 1.5 [1] = **£432** [1]. (b) 400 − 60 = £340 [1]; 340 − 240 = **£100** [1]. (c) Kilverton keeps £100 more per sofa [1] and consumers pay £32 less, which could raise sales [1], but it must run delivery, returns and storage and may upset retailers [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A change in distribution is best analysed with its effect on both the producer's income and its extra tasks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining the Boston Matrix categories but not classifying the products with the figures.
- Dividing by total market share instead of the largest rival's share for relative market share.
- Applying mark-up to price rather than to unit cost.
- Giving an elasticity value without a sign, working or meaning.
- Naming a pricing strategy without linking it to elasticity, competition, brand strength or life cycle stage.
- Listing ways to build a brand without explaining how each builds it.
- Ending an evaluation with no decision, or a decision that ignores the facts of the case.
- Treating a shorter channel as only a benefit and forgetting the extra tasks.
