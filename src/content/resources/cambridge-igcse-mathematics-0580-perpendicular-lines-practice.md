These questions cover subtopic 3.7 Perpendicular lines of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. The subtopic is **Extended only**, so every question is for Extended candidates (Papers 2 and 4). Each question is labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator). Give gradients as exact fractions unless told otherwise.

## Section A

**1.** (non-calculator) A line has gradient –4. Write down the gradient of a line perpendicular to it. **[1]**

**2.** (non-calculator) Find the gradient of a line perpendicular to 4y = 3x – 2. **[2]**

**3.** (non-calculator) Find the gradient of a line perpendicular to 6x + 2y = 7. **[2]**

**4.** (non-calculator) Show that the lines y = (2/3)x + 1 and 3x + 2y = 4 are perpendicular. **[2]**

**5.** (non-calculator) Find the equation of the line perpendicular to y = 5x – 2 that passes through (10, 3). **[2]**

**6.** (non-calculator) The line y = (k + 1)x – 4 is perpendicular to the line y = –(1/3)x + 2. Find the value of k. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** (non-calculator) P is (–3, –1) and Q is (5, 3).

**(a)** Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of PQ. **[4]**

**(b)** Show that the point (–1, 5) is the same distance from P as from Q. **[2]**

**8.** (non-calculator) A is (–2, 3), B is (1, 4) and C is (3, –2).

**(a)** Show that angle ABC is a right angle. **[3]**

**(b)** ABCD is a rectangle. Find the coordinates of D. **[1]**

**9.** (non-calculator) A circle has centre (–1, 2). The point T(2, 6) lies on the circle.

**(a)** Find the gradient of the radius to T. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the equation of the tangent to the circle at T. Give your answer in the form ax + by = c, where a, b and c are integers. **[3]**

**10.** (calculator) Line L has equation x – 2y = 4. Line M is perpendicular to L and passes through the point A(1, 3).

**(a)** Find the gradient of L. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the equation of M. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the coordinates of the point where L and M meet. **[2]**

**(d)** Hence find the shortest distance from A to line L. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**11.** (non-calculator) P(0, 0), Q(3, 1), R(4, 4) and S(1, 3) are the vertices of quadrilateral PQRS.

**(a)** Show that PQRS is a parallelogram. **[2]**

**(b)** Show that the diagonals PR and QS are perpendicular. **[2]**

**(c)** Write down the name of the special parallelogram PQRS. **[1]**

**12.** (non-calculator) A line passes through (2, 7) and (6, 5). Find the equation of the line perpendicular to it that passes through (2, 7). **[3]**

**13.** (non-calculator) A is (1, 2) and B is (5, 6).

**(a)** Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of AB. **[3]**

**(b)** The point (5, k) lies on the perpendicular bisector. Find k. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** **¼** [1]

**2.** y = (3/4)x – ½, so the gradient is 3/4 [1]; perpendicular gradient **–4/3** [1]

*Examiner insight:* starting from 3 (the coefficient of x before dividing by 4) gives –1/3 and loses both marks.

**3.** y = –3x + 3.5, gradient –3 [1]; perpendicular gradient **1/3** [1]

**4.** 3x + 2y = 4 gives y = –(3/2)x + 2, gradient –3/2 [1]; (2/3) × (–3/2) = –1, so the lines are perpendicular [1]

**5.** Gradient –1/5; 3 = –(1/5)(10) + c, so c = 5 [1]; **y = –(1/5)x + 5** (or x + 5y = 25) [1]

**6.** Perpendicular gradient is 3, so k + 1 = 3 [1]; **k = 2** [1]

**7. (a)** Midpoint (1, 1) [1]; gradient PQ = 4/8 = ½ [1]; perpendicular gradient –2 [1]; 1 = –2 + c, so **y = –2x + 3** [1]

**(b)** Distance to P: √(2² + 6²) = √40 [1]; distance to Q: √((–6)² + 2²) = √40, so the distances are equal [1]

*Examiner insight:* (–1, 5) satisfies y = –2x + 3, and every point on a perpendicular bisector is equidistant from the two ends, which is why the distances agree.

**8. (a)** Gradient AB = 1/3 [1]; gradient BC = (–2 – 4)/(3 – 1) = –3 [1]; (1/3) × (–3) = –1, so AB is perpendicular to BC and angle ABC = 90° [1]

**(b)** Step from B to C is across 2, down 6; D = A + that step = **(0, –3)** [1]

**9. (a)** (6 – 2)/(2 – (–1)) = **4/3** [1]

**(b)** Tangent gradient –3/4 [1]; 6 = –(3/4)(2) + c, so c = 7.5 [1]; **3x + 4y = 30** [1]

*Examiner insight:* check T fits: 3(2) + 4(6) = 30. A tangent through the centre instead of T is a common error.

**10. (a)** y = ½x – 2: gradient **½** [1]

**(b)** Gradient of M is –2; 3 = –2(1) + c, so c = 5 [1]; **y = –2x + 5** [1]

**(c)** ½x – 2 = –2x + 5, so 2.5x = 7 [1]; **(2.8, –0.6)** [1]

**(d)** √((2.8 – 1)² + (–0.6 – 3)²) = √(3.24 + 12.96) = √16.2 [1] = **4.02** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the shortest distance from a point to a line is along the perpendicular, which is why (c) is needed before (d).

**11. (a)** PQ and SR both have gradient 1/3 [1]; QR and PS both have gradient 3, so both pairs of opposite sides are parallel [1]

**(b)** Gradient PR = 4/4 = 1 and gradient QS = (3 – 1)/(1 – 3) = –1 [1]; 1 × (–1) = –1, so PR is perpendicular to QS [1]

**(c)** **Rhombus** [1]

*Examiner insight:* it is not a square: adjacent sides have gradients 1/3 and 3, whose product is 1, not –1.

**12.** Gradient = (5 – 7)/(6 – 2) = –½ [1]; perpendicular gradient 2 [1]; 7 = 4 + c, so **y = 2x + 3** [1]

**13. (a)** Midpoint (3, 4) and gradient AB = 1 [1]; perpendicular gradient –1 [1]; 4 = –3 + c, so **y = –x + 7** [1]

**(b)** k = –5 + 7 = **2** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Changing only the sign or only flipping the fraction.
- Reading the gradient before making y the subject.
- Using an end point instead of the midpoint for a perpendicular bisector.
- Using a tangent through the centre rather than the point of contact.
- Rounding a gradient such as –1/3 to –0.33 and carrying the error forward.
- Concluding "perpendicular" without stating that the product is –1.
