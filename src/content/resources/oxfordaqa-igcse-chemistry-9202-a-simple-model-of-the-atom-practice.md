## Section A

**1.** Which particle has a relative charge of −1?

A: electron

B: neutron

C: nucleus

D: proton

**[1]**

**2.** State what is meant by the **mass number** of an atom. **[1]**

**3.** Name the **two** types of particle found in the nucleus. **[2]**

**4.** Give the electronic structure of:

**(a)** oxygen (atomic number 8) **[1]**

**(b)** potassium (atomic number 19) **[1]**

**(c)** A student draws an atom of sodium (atomic number 11) with its electrons arranged 2,9. Explain the error and give the correct arrangement. **[2]**

**5.** State why atoms have no overall electrical charge. **[1]**

**6.** An element contains only one sort of atom. Give the chemical symbols of **two** elements whose symbols have two letters. **[1]**

**8.** Magnesium has three isotopes: ²⁴Mg, ²⁵Mg and ²⁶Mg. Magnesium has atomic number 12.

**(a)** State one way in which the three isotopes are the same. **[1]**

**(b)** State one way in which they are different. **[1]**

**(c)** Give the number of neutrons in an atom of ²⁶Mg. **[1]**

**(d)** The relative atomic mass of magnesium is given as 24.3 on some data sheets. Explain why it is not a whole number. **[2]**

**9.** An atom of element X has the electronic structure 2,8,4.

**(a)** State the atomic number of X. **[1]**

**(b)** Identify X. **[1]**

**(c)** State which energy level contains the electrons with the highest energy. **[1]**

**10.** An atom has 17 protons and 20 neutrons.

**(a)** Write the atom in the form shown in the specification, with mass number and atomic number. **[2]**

**(b)** State the number of electrons in the atom. **[1]**

**11.** The model of the atom has changed over time.

**(a)** Describe the "plum pudding" model. **[2]**

**(b)** State the main feature of the model proposed by Rutherford. **[1]**

**(c)** Name the particle discovered by Chadwick. **[1]**

**12.** Explain why ¹²C and ¹⁴C atoms are atoms of the same element even though they have different masses. **[2]**

**13.** Draw or describe the arrangement of electrons in an atom of chlorine (atomic number 17), and explain why the electrons are arranged in this way. **[3]**

**14.** Two atoms are written as ⁴⁰₁₈Ar and ⁴⁰₁₉K.

**(a)** State one way in which the two atoms are the same. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why they are atoms of different elements and not isotopes. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** A [1]. **[1]**

**2.** The total number of protons and neutrons in (the nucleus of) an atom [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Protons [1]; neutrons [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** 2,6 [1]

**(b)** 2,8,8,1 [1]

**(c)** The second shell can hold only 8 electrons, so the eleventh electron must go into the third shell [1]. Correct arrangement: 2,8,1 [1].

**[4]**

**5.** The number of electrons equals the number of protons, so the negative and positive charges cancel [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Any two correct, such as Mg, Na, Cl, Ca, He [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** 11 [1]

**(b)** 12 [1]

**(c)** 15 [1]

**(d)** ⁴⁰₁₈Ar (argon) [1]

**[4]**

**8. (a)** Same number of protons / same atomic number / same number of electrons [1].

**(b)** Different numbers of neutrons / different mass numbers [1].

**(c)** 26 − 12 = 14 [1].

**(d)** Ar is an average of the masses of the isotopes [1], taking into account how common each isotope is [1].

**[5]**

**9. (a)** 2 + 8 + 4 = 14 [1]

**(b)** Silicon [1]

**(c)** The third energy level / outer shell [1]

**[3]**

**10. (a)** ³⁷₁₇Cl: mass number 37 [1]; atomic number 17 and symbol Cl [1].

**(b)** 17 [1]

**[3]**

**11. (a)** A ball (sphere) of positive charge [1] with negative electrons embedded in it [1].

**(b)** A tiny, positively charged nucleus containing most of the mass, with electrons around it [1].

**(c)** Neutron [1]

**[4]**

**12.** Both have 6 protons / the same atomic number [1]; the element is defined by its number of protons, and only the number of neutrons is different [1]. **[2]**

**13.** 2,8,7: two electrons in the first shell, eight in the second, seven in the third [1]. Electrons occupy the lowest available energy levels [1], and the first shell is full with 2 and the second with 8 before the third starts to fill [1]. **[3]**

**14. (a)** They have the same mass number (40) / the same total number of protons and neutrons [1].

**(b)** They have different numbers of protons (18 and 19) / different atomic numbers [1]; isotopes must have the same number of protons, and the number of protons decides the element [1].

**[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reversing atomic number and mass number.
- Writing that isotopes have different numbers of protons.
- Giving potassium the structure 2,8,9.
- Explaining Ar without the word "average".
- Using the Ar from the periodic table instead of the mass number given in the question.
- Forgetting that the number of electrons in an atom equals the number of protons.
