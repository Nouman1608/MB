These questions cover section **4.7.3 Induced potential, transformers and the National Grid** of the AQA GCSE
Physics (8463) specification, for first teaching 2016, examined on Paper 2. The whole section is Higher tier only, so
questions on it are labelled **Higher tier only**. Questions 6 and 12 test only the National Grid content of
section 4.2.4.3, which is examined at both tiers. A calculator is allowed. All numerical data are invented.

Learn the content first in the
[Induced potential, transformers and the National Grid study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-8463-induced-potential-transformers-and-the-national-grid/)

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** (Higher tier only) Name the effect in which a potential difference is induced across a conductor moving relative to a magnetic
field. **[1]**

**2.** (Higher tier only) A wire is moved parallel to the field lines between two magnetic poles. State the induced p.d. and explain
your answer. **[2]**

**3.** (Higher tier only) State the part of a generator that makes it produce direct current. **[1]**

**4.** (Higher tier only) Explain why a transformer will not work from a battery. **[2]**

**5.** (Higher tier only) Give the reason a transformer core is made of iron. **[1]**

**6.** State what is meant by a step-down transformer. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** (Higher tier only) A bar magnet is pushed, S pole first, into a coil connected to a sensitive ammeter. The needle moves to the
right.

**(a)** Describe what the ammeter shows while the magnet is held still inside the coil. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe what the ammeter shows when the magnet is pulled out. **[1]**
**(c)** Give two changes that would make the needle move further. **[2]**
**(d)** State the magnetic pole formed at the end of the coil nearest the magnet as it is pushed in, and explain
why. **[2]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) An alternator's p.d.–time graph shows one complete cycle every 0.020 s, with a peak p.d. of 6.0 V.

**(a)** Calculate the number of rotations the coil makes each second. **[1]**
**(b)** The coil is turned more slowly. Describe two changes to the graph. **[2]**
**(c)** The alternator is converted to a dynamo. Describe how the graph would look, and name the part that is
changed. **[2]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) Explain how a moving-coil microphone produces an electrical signal from sound. **[4]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) A laptop charger contains a transformer that steps 230 V mains down to 19 V. The primary coil has 920 turns.

**(a)** Calculate the number of turns on the secondary coil. **[2]**
**(b)** The laptop draws 3.0 A at 19 V. Calculate the current drawn from the mains, assuming the transformer is 100%
efficient. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[3]**

**11.** (Higher tier only) A step-up transformer at a power station raises the p.d. from 25 000 V to 400 000 V. The primary coil has 500
turns. Calculate the number of turns on the secondary coil. **[2]**

**12.** A power station transmits 5.0 MW through cables with a total resistance of 8.0 Ω.

**(a)** Calculate the current in the cables if the transmission p.d. is 50 kV. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the power wasted in the cables at 50 kV. **[2]**
**(c)** The p.d. is raised to 250 kV. Calculate the new power wasted, and explain why transmitting at high p.d. makes
the National Grid more efficient. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** The generator effect [1]

**2.** Zero [1]; the wire does not cut across the field lines, so the field around it does not change [1]

**3.** Split-ring commutator [1]

**4.** A battery gives a direct current, which produces a constant magnetic field in the core [1]; a p.d. is only induced in the secondary coil by a changing field [1]

**5.** Iron is easily magnetised [1]

**6.** A transformer whose output (secondary) p.d. is lower than its input (primary) p.d. [1]

**7. (a)** Zero (the needle returns to the centre) [1]
**(b)** The needle moves to the left [1]
**(c)** Any two from: move the magnet faster [1]; use a stronger magnet [1]; use a coil with more turns [1]
**(d)** An S pole [1]; the induced current's field opposes the movement, so the coil repels the incoming S pole [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a), many write "the needle stays to the right"; no movement means no change, so no induced current.

**8. (a)** 1 ÷ 0.020 = **50 rotations per second** [1]
**(b)** The peak p.d. is lower than 6.0 V [1]; each cycle takes longer than 0.020 s (fewer cycles per second) [1]
**(c)** A series of humps all on the same side of the time axis [1]; the slip rings are replaced by a split-ring commutator [1] **[5]**

**9.** Sound waves (pressure variations) make the diaphragm vibrate [1]; the diaphragm moves a coil back and forth in the field of a permanent magnet [1]; a p.d. is induced in the coil (generator effect) [1]; the current in the circuit varies in the same pattern as the sound waves [1]

**10. (a)** ns = np × Vs / Vp = 920 × 19 / 230 [1]; = **76 turns** [1]
**(b)** Power out = 19 × 3.0 = 57 W [1]; Ip = 57 / 230 = 0.2478 A [1]; = **0.25 A** (2 s.f.) [1] **[5]**

**11.** ns = 500 × 400 000 / 25 000 [1]; = **8000 turns** [1]

**12. (a)** I = P / V = 5.0 × 10⁶ / 50 000 [1]; = **100 A** [1]
**(b)** P = I² R = 100² × 8.0 [1]; = **80 000 W** (80 kW) [1]
**(c)** I = 5.0 × 10⁶ / 250 000 = 20 A [1]; loss = 20² × 8.0 = **3200 W** [1]; a higher p.d. means a smaller current for the same power [1]; the heating loss depends on I², so far less energy is wasted in the cables and more of the power reaches consumers [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* "High voltage reduces resistance" scores zero; the resistance of the cables is unchanged.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying a magnet resting in a coil keeps inducing a current.
- Giving the wrong pole in "opposes the change" questions; pushing in gives the same pole, pulling out the opposite.
- Swapping slip rings and split-ring commutator.
- Describing only the peak height when the rotation speed changes, not the time per cycle.
- Rearranging the turns-ratio equation with p and s mixed.
- Using P = VI for the cable loss with the transmission p.d.; the loss needs I²R.
- Claiming high p.d. lowers resistance.
