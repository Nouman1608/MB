Condensed recall for section 2.3.2 of Unit 2. For explanations and worked examples, use the [Financial Planning study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-business-ybs11-financial-planning/). Figures are invented.

## Formula sheet

```
Sales revenue         = price x sales volume
Total costs           = fixed costs + variable costs
Variable costs        = variable cost per unit x output
Average cost          = total costs / output
Contribution per unit = selling price - variable cost per unit
Total contribution    = contribution per unit x units sold
Break-even output     = total fixed costs / contribution per unit
Margin of safety      = actual sales - break-even output
Profit                = total contribution - fixed costs
Net cash flow         = inflows - outflows
Closing balance       = opening balance + net cash flow
```

## Costs at a glance

- **Fixed:** do not vary with output in the short run (rent, salaries, insurance, loan interest).
- **Variable:** vary directly with output (materials, packaging, piece-rate pay).
- **Average cost** falls as output rises when fixed costs are spread over more units.
- **Improving sales:** promotion, distribution, product improvement, new markets, price change matched to PED.

## Break-even essentials

- Break-even = no profit, no loss: total revenue = total costs.
- **Price up** → contribution up → break-even down. **Variable cost up** → contribution down → break-even up. **Fixed costs up** → break-even up.
- Always round break-even output **up** to the next whole unit.
- **Chart:** fixed cost line flat; total cost starts at fixed cost; revenue starts at zero; crossing point = break-even; gap right of it = profit; distance from break-even to actual output = margin of safety.
- **Limitations:** assumes all output sold; constant price and unit cost; fixed costs truly fixed; hard with many products; only as good as the forecast.

## Cash flow essentials

- Inflows: cash sales, receipts from credit customers, loans, owner capital, asset sales.
- Outflows: suppliers, wages, rent, loan repayments, equipment, tax.
- Credit sales arrive **later** than the sale, so growing firms can run short of cash.
- **Uses:** spot shortfalls, arrange finance early, support a loan application, set targets.
- **Limitations:** relies on forecasts; late payers; unexpected costs.

## Budgets essentials

- **Purposes:** set targets, control spending, allocate resources, measure performance.
- **Historical-figures budget:** last year adjusted; quick but repeats inefficiency.
- **Zero-based budget:** every item justified from zero; removes waste but slow.
- **Favourable variance:** revenue above or cost below budget. **Adverse:** revenue below or cost above budget.
- **Difficulties:** inaccurate forecasts, unrealistic targets, events overtaking the plan, time cost.

## Linking the tools

Each tool feeds the next. The **sales forecast** sets expected volume; volume and costs give the **break-even** position and margin of safety; the same forecast, with credit terms and payment dates, builds the **cash-flow forecast**; and all of these become the **budget**, against which **variances** are measured. An error in the forecast therefore spreads into every other plan, which is the strongest evaluation point in this section.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sales volume | Number of units sold |
| Fixed costs | Costs that do not change with output in the short run |
| Variable costs | Costs that change directly with output |
| Contribution | Selling price minus variable cost per unit, the amount each unit adds towards fixed costs and profit |
| Break-even point | Output at which total revenue equals total costs |
| Margin of safety | Amount by which sales exceed break-even output |
| Cash-flow forecast | Prediction of cash inflows and outflows over future months |
| Zero-based budget | A budget built from zero, with every item justified |
| Variance | Difference between a budgeted and an actual figure |
| Adverse variance | A difference that lowers profit compared with budget |

## Exam traps

- Fixed costs ÷ **contribution**, never ÷ price.
- A margin of safety can be stated in units or in revenue; give units unless asked otherwise.
- Revenue is not cash if customers pay on credit.
- A favourable cost variance may come from lower output, not efficiency.
- A cash-flow forecast predicts; it does not show past results.
- Do not call a negative closing balance a loss: it is a cash shortfall, which may be covered by an overdraft.

## Self-test

1. Fixed costs are $12,000; variable cost is $4 a unit; output is 2,000. Calculate total costs.
2. Calculate average cost in question 1.
3. Price is $10 and variable cost $4. Calculate contribution per unit.
4. Using questions 1 and 3, calculate break-even output.
5. Sales are 2,600 units. Calculate the margin of safety.
6. State two purposes of a sales forecast.
7. Name the three factors affecting sales forecasts in the specification.
8. Opening balance $1,000; inflows $8,000; outflows $9,500. Calculate the closing balance.
9. Give one limitation of a cash-flow forecast.
10. Budgeted wages $15,000; actual $16,200. Calculate the variance and say whether it is adverse or favourable.
11. Give one advantage of zero-based budgeting.
12. What happens to the break-even point if the selling price falls?

### Answers

1. $12,000 + ($4 × 2,000) = **$20,000**.
2. $20,000 ÷ 2,000 = **$10** a unit.
3. $10 − $4 = **$6**.
4. $12,000 ÷ $6 = **2,000 units**.
5. 2,600 − 2,000 = **600 units**.
6. Any two: plan production and inventory, plan staffing, build cash-flow forecasts and budgets, support a finance application.
7. Consumer trends, economic variables, actions of competitors.
8. $1,000 + $8,000 − $9,500 = **−$500** (a cash shortfall).
9. Any one: it relies on forecasts that may be wrong; customers may pay late; unexpected costs are not included.
10. $1,200 **adverse**, because the cost is above budget.
11. Every cost must be justified, so wasteful spending carried over from previous years is removed.
12. Contribution per unit falls, so the break-even point rises.
