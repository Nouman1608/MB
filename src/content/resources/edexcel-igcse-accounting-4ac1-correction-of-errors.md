## What section 3.3 asks you to do

| Point | Specification wording |
|---|---|
| 3.3 (a) | Identify and explain errors that do and do not affect the balancing of the trial balance |
| 3.3 (b) | Prepare journal entries to correct errors |
| 3.3 (c) | Prepare a suspense account |

The specification does not name error types. The usual names (omission, commission, principle, original entry, complete reversal, compensating) are standard practice; always describe what happened as well.

## Does the error affect the trial balance?

Ask: **were equal debits and credits posted?** If yes, the totals still agree. If no, they disagree.

| What happened | Equal debits and credits? | Trial balance |
|---|---|---|
| Transaction left out completely | Yes (none at all) | Agrees |
| Right amount, wrong account of the same type | Yes | Agrees |
| Right amount, wrong class of account | Yes | Agrees |
| Wrong amount on both sides | Yes | Agrees |
| Both entries on the wrong sides | Yes | Agrees |
| Two equal errors cancelling | Yes, overall | Agrees |
| Only one side posted | No | Disagrees |
| Different amounts on each side | No | Disagrees |
| Both entries on the same side | No | Disagrees |
| A book or account added up wrongly (casting error) | No | Disagrees |
| A balance missed out of, or misplaced in, the trial balance | No | Disagrees |

## A three-step method for any correction

1. **What was done?** Write the entries actually made.
2. **What should have been done?** Write the correct entries.
3. **Correction:** the entries that turn step 1 into step 2. Where only one side was wrong, the other side of the correction goes to suspense.

### Applying it to awkward cases

**Wrong side of one account.** A receipt of 650 from a customer was debited to the bank (correct) and also debited to the customer's account (should be a credit). The customer's account is wrong by 2 × 650 = 1,300: the wrong debit must be cancelled and the right credit made. Correction: credit the customer 1,300, debit suspense 1,300.

**Casting error in a day book.** The sales day book was undercast by 400. Customers' accounts were posted from individual invoices, so they are right; only the sales account, posted from the total, is 400 too small. Correction: credit sales 400, debit suspense 400.

**Transposition in one account.** Rent paid of 1,250 was entered in the bank correctly but debited to rent as 1,520. Rent is overstated by 270. Correction: credit rent 270, debit suspense 270.

**Omitted posting.** Discount allowed of 180 was credited to the customer but never debited to the discount allowed account. Correction: debit discount allowed 180, credit suspense 180.

**Error of principle.** Office equipment of 2,400 was debited to purchases. Both sides were posted, so no suspense entry. Correction: debit office equipment 2,400, credit purchases 2,400.

## Worked example: Draycott Cycles

At 31 December 2027 the trial balance of Draycott Cycles showed debits of 88,740 and credits of 86,950. The difference of 1,790 was entered in a suspense account. Because the debit total was higher, the suspense balance is a **credit** of 1,790. The five errors above were then found.

### Journal entries

```
                                                Debit     Credit
1  Suspense                                       400
       Sales                                                 400
   Sales day book undercast

2  Suspense                                       270
       Rent                                                  270
   Rent of 1,250 posted as 1,520

3  Discount allowed                               180
       Suspense                                              180
   Discount allowed not posted to its account

4  Suspense                                     1,300
       Trade receivables (customer)                        1,300
   Receipt of 650 debited instead of credited to the customer

5  Office equipment                             2,400
       Purchases                                           2,400
   Equipment wrongly debited to purchases
```

### Suspense account

| Debit | | Credit | |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Sales | 400 | Difference per trial balance | 1,790 |
| Rent | 270 | Discount allowed | 180 |
| Trade receivables | 1,300 | | |
| | **1,970** | | **1,970** |

The account clears to nil. If it had not, at least one error would still be undiscovered.

Check the original difference: errors 2 and 4 overstated debits by 270 + 1,300 = 1,570; error 3 understated debits by 180; error 1 understated credits by 400. Debits exceeded credits by 1,570 − 180 + 400 = 1,790, matching the suspense balance.

### Effect on profit

Draycott's draft profit was 31,600. Only corrections to income statement accounts change profit.

| Error | Account corrected | Effect on profit |
|---|---|---:|
| 1 | Sales up | + 400 |
| 2 | Rent down | + 270 |
| 3 | Discount allowed up | − 180 |
| 4 | Trade receivables (statement of financial position) | 0 |
| 5 | Purchases down (no depreciation charged on the equipment this year, under the business's policy) | + 2,400 |
| | **Corrected profit** | **34,490** |

31,600 + 400 + 270 − 180 + 2,400 = 34,490. Errors 4 and the asset side of error 5 change the statement of financial position, not profit.

## Exam technique

- **Identify and explain**: say whether the trial balance agrees and why, in terms of equal or unequal debits and credits.
- **Journal**: debit line first, credit line indented, amounts in the right columns, a narrative for each entry. Use account names, not "suspense" for errors that do not affect the trial balance.
- **Suspense account**: start with the difference on the side that makes the trial balance agree (the smaller total's side), post every suspense correction, and show that it balances.
- **Effect on profit**: list each correction with a sign, including nil for statement of financial position items.

## Common errors

- Using suspense for an error of principle, omission or commission.
- Correcting a wrong-side error with the single amount instead of double.
- Correcting a casting error in a day book through the customers' accounts as well as the sales account.
- Putting the trial balance difference on the wrong side of suspense.
- Leaving out narratives.
- Changing profit for corrections that only affect assets or liabilities.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which side does the trial balance difference go in the suspense account?**
On the side of the smaller total, so that the trial balance agrees. If debits exceed credits, suspense has a credit balance.

**Why does a posting to the wrong side need double the amount to correct?**
The wrong entry must be cancelled and the correct entry made, so the correction is twice the original amount.

**Do errors of principle affect profit?**
Often yes. Charging a non-current asset to an expense understates profit, so correcting it increases profit (less any depreciation that should be charged).
