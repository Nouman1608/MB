These questions cover section 3.6 Further mechanics and thermal physics of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408, version 1.3, AS and A-level exams June 2016 onwards): 3.6.1 Periodic motion and 3.6.2 Thermal physics, including Required practicals 7 and 8. It is **A-level only**: Paper 1 assesses 6.1 and Paper 2 assesses 6.2. It is core content, not one of the options (sections 3.9 to 3.13), of which you study one for Paper 3 Section B.

Learn the content first with the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-further-mechanics-and-thermal-physics/). Take g = 9.81 m s⁻², c(water) = 4200 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹, R = 8.31 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹, k = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹ and N_A = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.

## Questions

**1.** A wheel turns at 450 revolutions per minute.

**(a)** Calculate its angular speed. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the speed of a point 0.30 m from the axle. **[1]**

**2.** A car of mass 1200 kg travels round a flat bend of radius 45 m at a constant 15 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the centripetal force on the car. **[2]**
**(b)** The maximum sideways friction the road can supply is 9000 N. Calculate the maximum speed for this bend. **[2]**
**(c)** Name the force that provides it. **[1]**

**3.**

**(a)** State the condition for an object to move with simple harmonic motion. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how a v–t graph is obtained from an x–t graph, and state the phase relationship between a and x. **[2]**

**4.** A body moves in SHM with amplitude 0.060 m and period 0.80 s. Timing starts at x = +0.060 m.

**(a)** Calculate its maximum speed. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate its maximum acceleration. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate its speed when x = 0.036 m. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the time at which it first reaches x = 0.030 m. **[2]**

**5.** In Required practical 7 you investigate how the period of a mass-spring system depends on the mass.

**(a)** Describe how to measure the period as accurately as possible. **[3]**
**(b)** A graph of T² against m is a straight line through the origin with gradient 1.58 s² kg⁻¹. Calculate the spring constant. **[2]**
**(c)** State why the pendulum part of the practical must use small amplitudes. **[1]**

**6.** A 0.10 kg mass on a spring of stiffness 40 N m⁻¹ oscillates with amplitude 0.050 m. Damping is negligible.

**(a)** Calculate the total energy of the oscillator. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the kinetic energy when the displacement is 0.030 m. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the maximum speed. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the period of oscillation and state the period of the variation of potential energy with time. **[2]**

**7.**

**(a)** Distinguish between a free vibration and a forced vibration. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain what is meant by resonance. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe how increasing the damping changes a graph of amplitude against driving frequency. **[2]**
**(d)** Give one example of resonance involving stationary waves. **[1]**

**8.** A 50 W heater warms 0.20 kg of solid wax; a data logger records the temperature. It rises by 20 K in 160 s, then stays constant for 640 s while the wax melts.

**(a)** Calculate the specific heat capacity of the solid wax. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the specific latent heat of fusion of the wax. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain, in terms of particles, why the temperature is constant while the wax melts. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest why both values are likely to be too high. **[1]**

**9.**

**(a)** Cooling water flows through an engine at 0.15 kg s⁻¹ and its temperature rises by 12 K. Calculate the rate at which energy is removed. **[2]**
**(b)** The air in a bicycle pump warms when it is compressed quickly. Explain this using the first law of thermodynamics. **[2]**

**10.** Required practical 8 investigates the gas laws.

**(a)** For trapped air at 293 K, a graph of p against 1/V has gradient 12.2 Pa m³. Calculate the number of moles of air. **[3]**
**(b)** At constant pressure, a gas has volume 50.0 cm³ at 0 °C and 68.3 cm³ at 100 °C. Use these values to estimate absolute zero in °C. **[3]**
**(c)** State one variable that must be controlled in (b). **[1]**

**11.**

**(a)** State two assumptions of the kinetic theory model of an ideal gas. **[2]**
**(b)** Derive pV = ⅓Nm(c_rms)² for N molecules, each of mass m, in a cube of side L. **[6]**

**12.** A 0.010 m³ cylinder holds argon (molar mass 0.040 kg mol⁻¹, an ideal monatomic gas) at 1.5 × 10⁷ Pa and 290 K.

**(a)** Calculate the number of moles of argon. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of argon. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the mean kinetic energy of an argon atom. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the root mean square speed of the atoms. **[2]**
**(e)** Calculate the internal energy of the gas. **[2]**
**(f)** Gas is released at constant temperature until the pressure is 6.0 × 10⁶ Pa. Calculate the mass released. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** f = 450/60 = 7.5 Hz [1]; ω = 2π × 7.5 = **47 rad s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** v = ωr = 47.1 × 0.30 = **14 m s⁻¹** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Using 450 as f loses the first mark; later marks may be allowed as error carried forward.

**2. (a)** F = mv²/r = 1200 × 15²/45 [1] = **6000 N** [1]
**(b)** v = √(Fr/m) = √(9000 × 45/1200) [1] = **18 m s⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** **Friction** between the tyres and the road [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "Centripetal force" does not answer (c); name a real force.

**3. (a)** Acceleration is proportional to displacement from the equilibrium position [1] and is always directed towards the equilibrium position [1].
**(b)** The velocity at each instant is the gradient of the x–t graph [1]. Acceleration and displacement are in antiphase (π rad apart) [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Writing only "a = −ω²x" without explaining the minus sign may not earn the second mark in (a).

**4. (a)** ω = 2π/0.80 = 7.85 rad s⁻¹ [1]; v_max = ωA = **0.47 m s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** a_max = ω²A = 7.85² × 0.060 = **3.7 m s⁻²** [1]
**(c)** v = ω√(A² − x²) = 7.85 × √(0.060² − 0.036²) = 7.85 × 0.048 [1] = **0.38 m s⁻¹** [1]
**(d)** 0.030 = 0.060 cos ωt, so ωt = π/3 rad [1]; t = (π/3)/7.85 = **0.13 s** (T/6) [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* A calculator in degrees gives ωt = 60 and a wrong time.

**5. (a)** Time at least ten oscillations and divide [1]. Time from the equilibrium position, using a marker [1]. Repeat and take a mean [1].
**(b)** T² = (4π²/k)m, so gradient = 4π²/k [1]; k = 4π²/1.58 = **25 N m⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** T = 2π√(l/g) depends on the small-angle approximation sin θ ≈ θ, so the motion is only simple harmonic for small angles [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Each mark in (a) needs a distinct technique; "be careful" earns nothing.

**6. (a)** E = ½kA² = ½ × 40 × 0.050² = **0.050 J** [1]
**(b)** E_k = ½k(A² − x²) = ½ × 40 × (0.050² − 0.030²) [1] = **0.032 J** [1]
**(c)** ½mv² = 0.050, so v = **1.0 m s⁻¹** [1]
**(d)** T = 2π√(0.10/40) = **0.31 s** [1]; potential energy varies with period **0.16 s** (half of T) [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Giving ½kx² (the potential energy) in (b) scores no accuracy mark.

**7. (a)** Free: after release, with no driving force, it oscillates at its natural frequency [1]. Forced: a periodic driving force makes it oscillate at the driving frequency [1].
**(b)** Driving frequency equals natural frequency [1]; amplitude is maximum as energy transfer is greatest [1].
**(c)** The peak is lower [1]; it is broader and at a slightly lower frequency [1].
**(d)** A string driven by a vibration generator gives large stationary waves at its harmonic frequencies [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* "Lower and broader" are separate points in (c); one alone is unlikely to gain both marks.

**8. (a)** c = Pt/(mΔθ) = (50 × 160)/(0.20 × 20) [1] = **2000 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** l = Pt/m = (50 × 640)/0.20 [1] = **1.6 × 10⁵ J kg⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** The energy increases the potential energies of the particles [1]; their mean kinetic energy, and so the temperature, does not change [1].
**(d)** Energy is lost to the surroundings, so more energy is counted than the wax absorbed [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Swapping the units of c and l usually costs the accuracy mark.

**9. (a)** P = (m/t)cΔθ = 0.15 × 4200 × 12 [1] = **7.6 × 10³ W** [1]
**(b)** Work done on the air increases its internal energy [1]. Compression is quick, so little energy leaves by heating, and the temperature rises [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "The molecules move faster" alone misses the work-done link the first law needs.

**10. (a)** pV = nRT, so p = nRT × (1/V) and gradient = nRT [1]. n = 12.2/(8.31 × 293) [1] = **5.0 × 10⁻³ mol** [1]
**(b)** Gradient = (68.3 − 50.0)/100 = 0.183 cm³ K⁻¹ [1]. V = 0 when θ = −50.0/0.183 [1] = **−273 °C** [1]
**(c)** Pressure (or the mass of gas) [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a), "gradient = nRT" is the method step; using °C loses the accuracy mark.

**11. (a)** Any two: random motion; elastic collisions; negligible molecular volume; no intermolecular forces except in collisions; negligible collision time [1] [1].
**(b)** A molecule with velocity component u hits a wall and rebounds: Δp = 2mu [1]. Time between collisions with that wall = 2L/u [1]. Force = rate of change of momentum = 2mu/(2L/u) = mu²/L [1]. For N molecules, total force = Nm(mean u²)/L [1]. With random motion, mean u² = ⅓(c_rms)², since c² = u² + v² + w² [1]. p = F/L² = Nm(c_rms)²/3L³, and V = L³, giving pV = ⅓Nm(c_rms)² [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Each derivation step must be visible; an unexplained ⅓ loses that mark.

**12. (a)** n = pV/RT = (1.5 × 10⁷ × 0.010)/(8.31 × 290) [1] = **62 mol** [1]
**(b)** Mass = 62.2 × 0.040 = **2.5 kg** [1]
**(c)** (3/2)kT = 1.5 × 1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 290 = **6.0 × 10⁻²¹ J** [1]
**(d)** Atom mass = 0.040/6.02 × 10²³ = 6.64 × 10⁻²⁶ kg [1]; c_rms = √(3kT/m) = **425 m s⁻¹** [1]
**(e)** U = (3/2)nRT = (3/2)pV [1] = 1.5 × 1.5 × 10⁷ × 0.010 = **2.3 × 10⁵ J** [1]
**(f)** n remaining = (6.0 × 10⁶ × 0.010)/(8.31 × 290) = 24.9 mol [1]; mass released = (62.2 − 24.9) × 0.040 = **1.5 kg** [1] **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Carry unrounded values of n through (f); early rounding can shift the final answer.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming "centripetal force" instead of the real force that provides it.
- Omitting "towards equilibrium" from the SHM condition.
- Confusing ½kx² (potential energy) with the kinetic energy at displacement x.
- Mixing up the units of specific heat capacity and specific latent heat.
- Using °C instead of kelvin in pV = nRT.
- Using molar mass instead of the mass of one atom when finding c_rms.
