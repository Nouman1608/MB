These questions cover section 3.11.3, Ideal gases, of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630), which is International A-level only content. Learn the content first with the [ideal gases study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-ideal-gases-oxfordaqa-alevel-physics/) and the [ideal gases revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-ideal-gases-oxfordaqa-alevel-physics-revision-notes/). Use R = 8.31 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹, k = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹ and N_A = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹. Treat all gases as ideal.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State Boyle's law, including the conditions under which it applies. **[2]**

**2.** Convert −40 °C to kelvin and 350 K to degrees Celsius. **[2]**

**3.** Calculate the number of moles and the number of atoms in 8.0 g of helium. The molar mass of helium is 4.0 g mol⁻¹. **[2]**

**4.** A fixed mass of gas in a sealed rigid container is at 300 K. Its pressure doubles. Calculate its new temperature in degrees Celsius. **[2]**

**5.** Show that the Boltzmann constant is about 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why absolute zero is found by extrapolating a graph rather than by measuring the volume of a gas at that temperature. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A sealed flask of volume 2.5 dm³ contains air at 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa and 20 °C. It is heated to 100 °C. The volume of the flask does not change.

**(a)** Calculate the new pressure. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of moles of air in the flask. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the mass of air in the flask. The molar mass of air is 0.029 kg mol⁻¹. **[1]**

**8.** In Required practical 9, a student traps air in a syringe at 295 K and records these readings.

| V / cm³ | 60 | 48 | 40 | 30 | 24 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| p / kPa | 100 | 126 | 150 | 199 | 251 |

**(a)** Use the data to show that the readings are consistent with Boyle's law. **[2]**

**(b)** State the graph the student should plot to obtain a straight line through the origin. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the number of air molecules trapped in the syringe. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why the student waits for a short time after each change in volume before reading the pressure. **[1]**

**9.** In a Charles's law investigation, a column of trapped air has a volume of 28.4 cm³ at 10 °C and 35.3 cm³ at 80 °C. The pressure is constant.

**(a)** Use these readings to estimate absolute zero in degrees Celsius. **[3]**

**(b)** Predict the volume of the air at 50 °C. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest one reason why the estimate in (a) differs from −273 °C. **[1]**

**10.** A cylinder of volume 0.020 m³ contains nitrogen at 1.2 × 10⁷ Pa and 300 K. After some gas has been used, the pressure is 4.0 × 10⁶ Pa and the temperature is 285 K. The molar mass of nitrogen is 0.028 kg mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the initial mass of nitrogen in the cylinder. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the fraction of the original gas that remains. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the mass of nitrogen used. **[1]**

**11.** A cylinder fitted with a frictionless piston holds 0.050 mol of gas at a constant 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa.

**(a)** Calculate the initial volume of the gas at 300 K. **[1]**

**(b)** The gas is heated at constant pressure to 450 K. Calculate the work done by the gas. **[2]**

**(c)** A different sample changes along a straight line on a p–V graph from 1.5 × 10⁵ Pa, 2.0 × 10⁻³ m³ to 0.60 × 10⁵ Pa, 5.0 × 10⁻³ m³. Calculate the work done by this gas. **[2]**

**(d)** Show that the sample in (c) ends at the same temperature as it started. **[1]**

**12.** Carbon dioxide has a molar mass of 0.044 kg mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of one carbon dioxide molecule. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the density of carbon dioxide at 1.01 × 10⁵ Pa and 300 K. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the number of molecules per cubic metre under these conditions. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain why air at the same pressure and temperature has the same number of molecules per cubic metre but a lower density. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** For a fixed mass of gas at constant temperature [1], the pressure is inversely proportional to the volume (pV = constant) [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Both conditions, fixed mass and constant temperature, are needed for the first mark.

**2.** −40 + 273 = 233 K [1]; 350 − 273 = 77 °C [1] **[2]**

**3.** n = 8.0/4.0 = 2.0 mol [1]; N = 2.0 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.2 × 10²⁴ atoms [1] **[2]**

**4.** p ∝ T at constant volume, so T = 600 K [1]; 600 − 273 = 327 °C [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Doubling the Celsius temperature is a common error; the proportion works only in kelvin.

**5.** k = R/N_A [1] = 8.31 / 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.380 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹ [1] **[2]**

**6.** A real gas liquefies (condenses) before reaching absolute zero [1], so its volume cannot be measured there; the straight line from higher temperatures is extended to zero volume instead [1] **[2]**

**7. (a)** p₂ = p₁T₂/T₁ with T₁ = 293 K and T₂ = 373 K [1]; p₂ = 1.0 × 10⁵ × 373/293 = 1.27 × 10⁵ Pa [1]

**(b)** V = 2.5 × 10⁻³ m³ [1]; n = pV/RT = (1.0 × 10⁵ × 2.5 × 10⁻³) / (8.31 × 293) = 0.103 mol [1]

**(c)** Mass = 0.103 × 0.029 = 3.0 × 10⁻³ kg [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* In (b) use one consistent set of p and T; either the starting or the final state gives the same n.

**8. (a)** pV for each reading: 6.00, 6.05, 6.00, 5.97, 6.02 J [1]; these agree to within about 1%, so pV is constant, as Boyle's law requires [1]

**(b)** p against 1/V [1]

**(c)** N = pV/kT [1] = 6.0 / (1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 295) = 1.5 × 10²¹ [1]

**(d)** Compressing the gas does work on it and warms it; waiting lets it return to room temperature so the temperature is constant [1] **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* "Show consistent with" needs calculated values and a comment on how closely they agree.

**9. (a)** Gradient = (35.3 − 28.4)/(80 − 10) = 0.0986 cm³ °C⁻¹ [1]; the volume reaches zero 28.4/0.0986 = 288 °C below 10 °C [1]; absolute zero ≈ 10 − 288 = −278 °C [1]

**(b)** V = 28.4 + 40 × 0.0986 = 32.3 cm³ [1]

**(c)** A long extrapolation from only two readings, so a small error in either volume shifts the intercept a lot (or the air was not at the bath temperature when read) [1] **[5]**

**10. (a)** n₁ = (1.2 × 10⁷ × 0.020)/(8.31 × 300) = 96.3 mol [1]; mass = 96.3 × 0.028 = 2.7 kg [1]

**(b)** V is fixed, so n ∝ p/T [1]; fraction = (4.0 × 10⁶/285)/(1.2 × 10⁷/300) = 0.35 [1]

**(c)** Mass used = 2.70 × (1 − 0.351) = 1.7 kg [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* The combined gas law for a fixed mass cannot be used here because gas has left the cylinder.

**11. (a)** V = nRT/p = 0.050 × 8.31 × 300 / 1.0 × 10⁵ = 1.2 × 10⁻³ m³ [1]

**(b)** At constant pressure pΔV = nRΔT [1]; work = 0.050 × 8.31 × 150 = 62 J [1]

**(c)** Area of trapezium = ½ × (1.5 × 10⁵ + 0.60 × 10⁵) × 3.0 × 10⁻³ [1] = 315 J, about 3.2 × 10² J [1]

**(d)** pV = 1.5 × 10⁵ × 2.0 × 10⁻³ = 300 J at the start and 0.60 × 10⁵ × 5.0 × 10⁻³ = 300 J at the end; pV ∝ T for a fixed mass, so T is the same [1] **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* In (c), pΔV with either pressure gives a wrong answer; the pressure is not constant.

**12. (a)** m = 0.044 / 6.02 × 10²³ = 7.3 × 10⁻²⁶ kg [1]

**(b)** ρ = pM/RT [1] = (1.01 × 10⁵ × 0.044)/(8.31 × 300) = 1.8 kg m⁻³ [1]

**(c)** N/V = p/kT = 1.01 × 10⁵ / (1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 300) = 2.4 × 10²⁵ m⁻³ [1]

**(d)** N/V = p/kT does not depend on the type of gas, so the number per m³ is the same [1]; air has a smaller molar mass (0.029 kg mol⁻¹), so each molecule is lighter and the density is lower [1] **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using Celsius temperatures in ratios or in pV = nRT.
- Applying the fixed-mass combined law when gas has been added or removed.
- Leaving volumes in cm³ or dm³, or molar mass in g mol⁻¹.
- Using pΔV for a process in which the pressure changes.
- Stating gas laws without their conditions.
