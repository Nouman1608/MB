This page is a guide to AQA A-Level Accounting (7127) past papers for the June 2024, June 2025 and June 2026 series. For each series you get the grade thresholds, copied from AQA's grade-boundary documents, and, where AQA has published its report on the examination, a short account of what the examiners saw, written by us. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on AQA's own site, linked below.

## Where to get the official papers

- [AQA A-Level Accounting (7127) subject page](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/accounting/a-level/accounting-7127)
- [AQA A-Level Accounting (7127) specification](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/accounting/a-level/accounting-7127/specification)
- [AQA A-Level Accounting (7127) assessment resources](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/accounting/a-level/accounting-7127/assessment-resources)
- [AQA past paper finder](https://www.aqa.org.uk/find-past-papers-and-mark-schemes)
- [AQA grade boundaries](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries)

AQA publishes question papers, inserts, mark schemes and reports on the examination on the assessment resources page for the subject.

Use the three documents together. Sit the paper first, under timed conditions. Mark it strictly with the mark scheme, which tells you what each mark needs. Then read the report on the examination for that paper: it explains why answers that looked reasonable still lost marks. Our notes for each series below are a quick way into that report. When you have a paper that exposes a weak topic, go back to the matching study guide, for example [budgeting](/resources/aqa-a-level-accounting-budgeting/), [standard costing and variance analysis](/resources/aqa-a-level-accounting-standard-costing-and-variance-analysis/) or [capital investment appraisal](/resources/aqa-a-level-accounting-capital-investment-appraisal/), and see the [exam preparation guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-accounting-exam-preparation/) for technique.

Check which specification a paper was set on. A-Level Accounting 7127 is a linear qualification, not tiered, first taught in September 2017 and first examined in June 2019. All the series on this page were set on this specification. There are two written papers, each 3 hours, 120 marks and 50 per cent of the A-level. Each paper has three compulsory sections: Section A (30 marks) is 10 multiple-choice questions plus short answers, Section B (40 marks) has two structured questions and Section C (50 marks) has two extended-answer questions. Paper 1 covers content sections 1 to 8 and 14 to 18, and Paper 2 covers sections 1 to 3, 8 to 13 and 17 and 18.

AQA gives one set of subject-level boundaries for the A-level (out of 240) and, for illustration, notional boundaries for each paper (out of 120). Because the qualification is linear, the subject-level boundaries decide the grade. The notional paper boundaries are the board's own indication of how the subject boundary splits across the papers. ## June 2025

The June 2025 reports describe two accessible papers. On Paper 1, extended-response answers were long and well planned, but many followed a formula: they picked an option and then gave no overall judgement with a reason. On Paper 2, numeracy was strong and every section was widely attempted. The recurring weakness on both papers was application: comments that could apply to any business scored lower than comments tied to the scenario and its numbers.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 7127/1: reasons for trade discounts given without reference to a new business, and answers drifting into cash discounts. Non-current asset extracts that ignored the purchases and sales in the additional information, and sales accounts whose labels copied the narrative instead of naming the other account.
- Paper 7127/1: a sole trader layout used for a company income statement; capital and appropriation accounts for a partnership with wrong labels, goodwill reversed, and interest and drawings worked out for a full year instead of part of one.
- Paper 7127/1: evaluation questions where students listed questions they would like answered about the data without saying why the answer would change the decision, or copied the scenario without analysis. Stronger answers used the figures, weighed financial against non-financial points and reached a justified conclusion.
- Paper 7127/2: a first-time question on the Financial Reporting Council, where most candidates mentioned a true and fair view but could not state a role and scored nothing. Variances and benefits of an investment appraisal method were the weakest multiple-choice topics.
- Paper 7127/2: profit worked out from cash flows, with the asset purchase and disposal wrongly treated; production budgets with opening and closing inventory the wrong way round; activity based costing where cost drivers and batch overheads were misunderstood and materials left out.
- Paper 7127/2: written questions that gave general points about budgeting or costing instead of the specific budget or method in the scenario, an NPV described as a profit rather than a present value of future cash flows, and a lack of understanding of credit terms in a contract decision.

## June 2024

The June 2024 reports say Paper 1 was handled well overall, with candidates writing extensively and many reaching a reasoned conclusion. Unlabelled lists of numbers were the main loss of marks in calculations. Paper 2 was also done well, but its longer, more complex calculation questions were the weakest section, and some of the weakest candidates did not attempt that section at all, which capped the grade they could reach.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 7127/1: working figures presented as unlabelled lists with no end total, or a total never used in the answer. Data limitations discussed in generic terms rather than for the scenario, and numbers not used to back up a recommendation.
- Paper 7127/1: journals that did not show both sides of the correction; an inventory account completed by finding the difference between balances instead of transferring opening and closing inventory separately; cash flow statements that covered only part of the statement.
- Paper 7127/1: a statement of financial position that was incomplete or in the wrong layout, closing inventory not derived from the ratios given, and theoretical answers on audit ethics that were not applied to the situation described.
- Paper 7127/2: general advantages of budgeting given where incremental budgeting was asked about; budgeted income statements that missed the units to buy or left delivery costs out of closing inventory; labour hours not converted from minutes.
- Paper 7127/2: material usage and labour efficiency variances worked without flexing the budget first, and the reconciliation of budgeted and actual cost left unattempted. Unbalanced answers on whether the variances were linked.
- Paper 7127/2: net present value with depreciation included as an outflow, disposal proceeds missing or fixed costs treated as project cost; payback calculated from discounted instead of net cash flows; selling-price answers that drifted into inventory valuation; ratios discussed from the business's own view instead of a customer's.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find AQA A-Level Accounting 7127 past papers?**
AQA publishes papers, inserts, mark schemes and reports on the examination on the assessment resources page for 7127, and offers a past paper finder on its website.

**Which 7127 papers should I sit when practising a series?**
Sit both papers for the series: Paper 1 and Paper 2. Each is 3 hours and 120 marks. Mark them with the official mark scheme.

**Are the paper-level thresholds the ones that decide my grade?**
No. The A-level is linear, so the subject-level boundaries out of 240 decide the grade. AQA gives the paper-level figures as notional boundaries, for illustration only.

**Why is there no examiner report summary for June 2026?**
When we checked, AQA's assessment resources page for 7127 listed reports only up to June 2025. We only summarise reports we have read, so June 2026 will be added once the board publishes them.

**Can grade thresholds tell me what mark I need next time?**
No. Thresholds change every series and should only be used as a rough guide for the series they belong to, never to predict future ones.
