This page covers the skills named in the specification for questions 1-3: "how interpretations differ, why interpretations differ" and how convincing they are. Answer the set for your school's option first, then try another for extra practice in the method.

## Section A: the method

**1.** State what question 1 asks you to compare, and explain why provenance earns no credit in it. **[2]**

**2.** An ascription reads: "From a memoir by a former Bolshevik soldier, published in Paris in 1938." Identify three details in this ascription that could help you answer question 2. **[3]**

## Section B: interpretation sets

### Set 1: AA America, 1840-1895

**Interpretation A** *(invented)* -- From a speech by an American reformer, published in a collection of speeches in 1900:
"The Dawes Act was the kindest law ever passed for the Indian. It gave each family its own farm and a path to citizenship. Once he owns land and works it, the Indian will leave the old wandering life behind and share in the progress of the nation."

**Interpretation B** *(invented)* -- From a history of the Lakota people written in 1995 by a Lakota historian:
"Allotment was a second conquest, carried out with surveyors instead of soldiers. Land that had been held in common was cut into private plots, and the 'surplus' was sold to whites. Children were sent away to schools that forbade their language. What the reformers called progress, our grandparents called the end of a nation."

**3.** How does Interpretation B differ from Interpretation A about the Dawes Act? Explain your answer using Interpretations A and B. **[4]**

**4.** Why might the authors of Interpretations A and B have a different interpretation about the Dawes Act? Explain your answer using Interpretations A and B and your contextual knowledge. **[4]**

**5.** Which interpretation gives the more convincing opinion about the Dawes Act? Explain your answer based on your contextual knowledge and what Interpretations A and B say. **[8]**

### Set 2: AB Germany, 1890-1945

**Interpretation A** *(invented)* -- From a textbook on Nazi Germany written in 1978 by a British historian:
"For the German worker the 1930s brought something he had not known for years: a secure job. Public works and rearmament swept away mass unemployment, and Strength through Joy offered cheap holidays, concerts and even cruises. Many workers were content to trade political freedom for bread and work."

**Interpretation B** *(invented)* -- From the memoir of a former trade union official, published in 1955:
"They took our unions in May 1933 and gave us the Labour Front instead, which spoke for the bosses. We worked longer hours for wages that barely moved. We paid every week into the People's Car savings scheme and never saw a car. A holiday on a Party ship did not make up for the loss of our right to strike."

**6.** How does Interpretation B differ from Interpretation A about the effects of Nazi policies on German workers? Explain your answer using Interpretations A and B. **[4]**

**7.** Why might the authors of Interpretations A and B have a different interpretation about the effects of Nazi policies on German workers? Explain your answer using Interpretations A and B and your contextual knowledge. **[4]**

**8.** Which interpretation gives the more convincing opinion about the effects of Nazi policies on German workers? Explain your answer based on your contextual knowledge and what Interpretations A and B say. **[8]**

### Set 3: AC Russia, 1894-1945

**Interpretation A** *(invented)* -- From the memoir of a former official at the royal court, published in 1924:
"The Empress, a foreigner, allowed the peasant Rasputin to choose ministers while the Tsar was at the front. Honest men were dismissed and scoundrels promoted. When the people heard what went on at court, they ceased to respect the throne. Rasputin destroyed the dynasty."

**Interpretation B** *(invented)* -- From a school textbook on Russian history published in 2005:
"Rasputin's influence damaged the monarchy's reputation, but it was the war that brought it down. Millions of soldiers were killed or captured, railways could not carry food and fuel to the cities, and prices soared. When hungry workers in Petrograd went on strike in February 1917, the soldiers refused to fire on them."

**9.** How does Interpretation B differ from Interpretation A about the reasons for the fall of the Tsar? Explain your answer using Interpretations A and B. **[4]**

**10.** Why might the authors of Interpretations A and B have a different interpretation about the reasons for the fall of the Tsar? Explain your answer using Interpretations A and B and your contextual knowledge. **[4]**

**11.** Which interpretation gives the more convincing opinion about the reasons for the fall of the Tsar? Explain your answer based on your contextual knowledge and what Interpretations A and B say. **[8]**

### Set 4: AD America, 1920-1973

**Interpretation A** *(invented)* -- From the script of an American television documentary about the 1920s, broadcast in 1966:
"Prohibition was a noble idea that became a national joke. Speakeasies opened on every city block, bootleggers grew rich, and gangsters such as Al Capone bought police and judges. Ordinary Americans learned to break the law, and in 1933 the country gave up the experiment."

**Interpretation B** *(invented)* -- From a memoir by a campaigner for the Anti-Saloon League, published in 1931:
"Since the saloons closed, I have seen men bring their wages home to their wives instead of drinking them away. Fewer families go hungry, fewer children are beaten. The cities may have their criminals, but in the small towns of America, Prohibition has done what we promised."

**12.** How does Interpretation B differ from Interpretation A about Prohibition? Explain your answer using Interpretations A and B. **[4]**

**13.** Why might the authors of Interpretations A and B have a different interpretation about Prohibition? Explain your answer using Interpretations A and B and your contextual knowledge. **[4]**

**14.** Which interpretation gives the more convincing opinion about Prohibition? Explain your answer based on your contextual knowledge and what Interpretations A and B say. **[8]**

## Answers

**How these guides work.** Question 1-style answers: main difference identified [2], supported from A [1] and from B [1]. Question 2-style answers: a reason linked to the provenance and content of A [2] and of B [2]. Question 3-style answers: A tested against contextual knowledge, for and against [3]; B tested in the same way [3]; a substantiated judgement on which is more convincing [2].

**1.** It asks you to compare **what the two interpretations say** about the issue, their content [1]. Provenance explains *why* they differ, which is the task of question 2, so it earns nothing in question 1 [1].

**2.** Any three, [1] each: the author was a Bolshevik, so he supported the revolution; he was a soldier, so he may have experienced the Civil War or the Red Army directly; it is a memoir, a personal account written from memory; it was published in 1938, during Stalin's Terror, with twenty years of hindsight; it was published in Paris, outside the USSR, so the author may have been an exile free to criticise Stalin.

**3.** Difference [2]: A sees the Dawes Act as a generous reform that offered Native Americans progress and citizenship, while B sees it as a "second conquest" that destroyed their nation. Support from A [1]: "the kindest law ever passed... a path to citizenship". Support from B [1]: land "cut into private plots" with the surplus "sold to whites".

**4.** A [2]: the author was a reformer speaking in 1900, one of the people who supported allotment; he believed owning a farm would "civilise" Native Americans, and he was trying to persuade an American audience that the policy was right, so he stresses its benefits. B [2]: the author is a Lakota historian writing in 1995, nearly a century later, with knowledge of how much land was lost; writing from the point of view of the people affected, and drawing on family memory ("our grandparents"), she stresses the loss of land, language and culture.

**5.** A tested [3]: it is accurate that the Act (1887) divided reservations into family allotments and offered citizenship to those who accepted them; reformers genuinely believed it would help. But knowledge challenges A: Plains Indians were not used to individual farming, much allotted land was poor, and many sold or lost their plots. B tested [3]: B is supported by the sale of "surplus" land to settlers, which meant Native American landholding fell by about two-thirds between 1887 and 1934; boarding schools such as Carlisle punished children for speaking their own languages. B is less complete in that some Native Americans did farm their allotments. Judgement [2]: B is more convincing, because the effects of the Act on land and culture, which A could not yet see in 1900, match the evidence; A shows the reformers' intentions rather than the results.

**6.** Difference [2]: A argues workers gained from Nazi policies through secure jobs and leisure, accepting the loss of freedom; B argues they lost far more than they gained. Support from A [1]: "a secure job"; "cheap holidays, concerts and even cruises". Support from B [1]: "longer hours for wages that barely moved"; the loss of "our right to strike".

**7.** A [2]: the author is a British historian writing a textbook in 1978, looking at national evidence such as the fall in unemployment, so stresses the material gains that explain why many workers accepted the regime. B [2]: the author was a trade union official whose union was abolished in May 1933, so he personally lost his role and freedom; writing in 1955, after the regime's defeat, he judges it by the rights it destroyed and the broken promises, such as the car savings scheme.

**8.** A tested [3]: unemployment fell from about six million in early 1933 to very low levels by the late 1930s through public works such as the autobahns and through rearmament; Strength through Joy did provide subsidised holidays and leisure. But some of the fall was hidden by conscription (from 1935), the Reich Labour Service and the removal of women and Jews from the figures. B tested [3]: trade unions were abolished on 2 May 1933 and replaced by the German Labour Front under Robert Ley; strikes were banned; working hours rose; no Volkswagen was delivered to the savers before the war. Judgement [2]: for example, B is more convincing about workers' rights and conditions, while A explains why many workers still tolerated the regime; the stronger answer decides which matters more for "the effects on workers" and explains why.

**9.** Difference [2]: A blames Rasputin for destroying the dynasty, while B argues that the war, not Rasputin, brought the monarchy down. Support from A [1]: "Rasputin destroyed the dynasty". Support from B [1]: "it was the war that brought it down", with defeats, food shortages and rising prices.

**10.** A [2]: the author was a court official who saw Rasputin's influence at first hand and may have resented an outsider who decided promotions; writing in 1924 in exile from the revolution, he may also have wished to blame one man rather than the Tsar or the system he served. B [2]: a textbook published in 2005 gives a balanced overview for students, using evidence about the economy and the army collected over eighty years, so it weighs several causes and sees Rasputin as only one.

**11.** A tested [3]: when Nicholas II took personal command of the army in September 1915, Alexandra was left in charge at home; Rasputin's influence over appointments led to rapid changes of ministers, and rumours about him damaged respect for the royal family. But Rasputin was murdered in December 1916, before the revolution, so he cannot explain events in February 1917 alone. B tested [3]: military defeats and huge losses, the breakdown of transport, food and fuel shortages in Petrograd, and strikes in February 1917 match B; crucially, troops joined the protesters, and the Tsar abdicated in March 1917. Judgement [2]: B is more convincing because it explains the immediate events of 1917 and includes Rasputin as a factor, while A reduces a complex collapse to one person.

**12.** Difference [2]: A sees Prohibition as a failure that led to lawbreaking and organised crime, while B sees it as a success that improved family life. Support from A [1]: "a national joke"; gangsters "bought police and judges". Support from B [1]: men "bring their wages home"; "Prohibition has done what we promised".

**13.** A [2]: the documentary was made in 1966, more than thirty years after repeal, so its makers knew the experiment had been abandoned in 1933; aimed at a television audience, it focuses on dramatic city crime such as Capone's. B [2]: the author campaigned for the Anti-Saloon League, which fought for Prohibition, so she wants to defend it, especially in 1931, when calls for repeal were growing; she also focuses on small towns, where she had seen improvements, rather than on the cities.

**14.** A tested [3]: illegal speakeasies and bootlegging spread; Al Capone controlled much of Chicago's illegal alcohol trade and gang violence, as in the St Valentine's Day Massacre (1929); corruption of police and officials was widespread; the 21st Amendment repealed Prohibition in 1933. B tested [3]: Prohibition was popular in rural and small-town America, especially among Protestant groups, and drinking did fall in some places; but B ignores the cities and is written by a campaigner with a strong interest. Judgement [2]: A is more convincing overall because the spread of organised crime and the decision to repeal show the policy failed nationally, though B is a reminder that its supporters did see benefits in some communities.
