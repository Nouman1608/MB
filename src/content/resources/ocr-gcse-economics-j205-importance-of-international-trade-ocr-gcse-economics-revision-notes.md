Recall notes for section 4.1 of OCR GCSE (9-1) Economics (J205, Version 2.0, June 2026), examined in Component 02. The [international trade study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-economics-j205-importance-of-international-trade-ocr-gcse-economics/) explains each point with worked examples; the [international trade practice questions](/resources/ocr-gcse-economics-j205-importance-of-international-trade-ocr-gcse-economics-practice/) test them. Figures are invented.

## What the specification asks

- Explain **why countries import and export** goods and services and the **benefits for consumers and producers**.
- Explain **free trade agreements**, including the **European Union**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Export | Good or service sold to buyers in another country; money flows in |
| Import | Good or service bought from another country; money flows out |
| Specialisation | Concentrating on producing what a country does best |
| Free trade | Trade without tariffs, quotas or other barriers |
| Tariff | A tax on imports |
| Quota | A limit on the quantity of a good that can be imported |
| Free trade agreement | Members remove or reduce barriers on trade with each other |
| Customs union | Free trade between members plus a common external tariff |
| Single market | Customs union plus shared rules and free movement of goods, services, capital and people |
| Economies of scale | Falling cost per unit as output rises |

## Goods and services

- A foreign tourist spending in the UK = **UK export** of a service.
- A UK resident's holiday abroad = **UK import** of a service.
- The UK exports many services (finance, insurance, higher education, consulting) and imports many manufactured goods, fuel and food.

## Specialisation in one example

| Per worker | Food | Machines |
|---|---|---|
| Country C | 6 | 3 |
| Country D | 2 | 5 |

Ten workers each, split 5/5 with no trade: total **40 food and 40 machines**. Specialised (C all food, D all machines): **60 food and 50 machines**. More of both from the same workers.

## Reasons for trade

| Import because | Export because |
|---|---|
| Cannot produce it (climate, resources) | Sell surplus beyond the home market |
| Cheaper abroad | Larger market, economies of scale |
| Better quality or more variety | Earn money to pay for imports |
| Need foreign inputs | Spread risk across markets |

## Who benefits and how

- **Consumers:** lower prices, more choice, goods not made at home, better quality through competition.
- **Producers:** bigger markets, economies of scale, cheaper or better inputs, pressure to innovate.

## Levels of agreement

| | Barriers between members | External tariff | Shared rules and free movement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free trade agreement | Removed or reduced | Each sets its own | No |
| Customs union | Removed | Common | No |
| Single market | Removed | Common | Yes |

## The EU and the UK: facts to know

- The EU has **27** member countries.
- It is a **customs union** and a **single market**.
- The UK left on **31 January 2020**; the transition period ended on **31 December 2020**.
- UK-EU trade now runs under the **Trade and Cooperation Agreement**: goods meeting its rules trade without tariffs or quotas, but customs checks and paperwork add costs.

## Evaluating trade agreements

| For | Against |
|---|---|
| Lower prices, more choice | Uncompetitive domestic firms may close |
| Bigger markets for exporters | Job losses concentrated in some regions |
| More competition and efficiency | Less freedom to protect industries |
| Inward investment | Shared rules limit national decisions |

**Judge by:** who gains and loses, whether displaced workers can move into growing industries, and whether gains to many consumers outweigh losses to some producers.

## Exam traps

- Services count as trade.
- Tariff = tax; quota = quantity limit.
- The EU is more than a free trade agreement.
- Say whether **consumers** or **producers** gain.
- Trade has costs as well as benefits.

## Self-test

1. Define an export.
2. A student from abroad pays fees to study at a UK university. Is this a UK export or import?
3. Give two reasons why a country imports goods.
4. Give two benefits of trade for producers.
5. Using the table above, how much extra food and how many extra machines does specialisation produce?
6. An imported good costs £250 before a 20% tariff. What is its price with the tariff?
7. Explain the difference between a tariff and a quota.
8. State one difference between a free trade agreement and a customs union.
9. When did the UK leave the EU?
10. Give one cost of joining a free trade agreement.
11. A firm imports 5,000 components a year at £4 less each than the domestic price. How much does it save?
12. Explain why trade can raise living standards.

### Answers

1. A good or service **produced in one country and sold to buyers in another**.
2. A **UK export** of a service: money comes into the UK.
3. Any two: cannot be **produced** at home; **cheaper** abroad; better **quality** or **variety**; needed as **inputs**.
4. Any two: **larger markets**; **economies of scale**; **cheaper inputs**; pressure to **innovate**.
5. **20 more food** and **10 more machines**.
6. 250 × 1.2 = **£300**.
7. A tariff is a **tax** on imports, raising their price; a quota is a **limit on the quantity** that can be imported.
8. In a customs union members charge a **common external tariff**; in a free trade agreement each sets its **own**.
9. **31 January 2020** (transition ended 31 December 2020).
10. Any one: domestic firms lose **protection** and may close; **job losses**; loss of control over trade rules.
11. 5,000 × £4 = **£20,000**.
12. Countries **specialise** in what they do best, so **total output rises**, and trade lets consumers buy more and cheaper goods than they could produce alone.
