These questions cover section 6.1 The Earth and the Solar System of the Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Where needed, use speed of light = 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s and 1 day = 86 400 s.

> **Tier note:** questions 1 to 10 are Core and every candidate should do them. Questions 11 to 13, marked *(Extended)*, use Supplement content (average orbital speed, planetary data, elliptical orbits and conservation of energy), examined on Papers 2 and 4 only. The marks shown are indicative only.

## Section A

**1.** Name:

**(a)** the planet furthest from the Sun **[1]**
**(b)** the fourth planet from the Sun. **[1]**

**2.** State what is meant by a moon. **[1]**

**3.** State the approximate time the Earth takes to orbit the Sun. **[1]**

**4.** Name the force that keeps the Earth in orbit around the Sun. **[1]**

**5.** State which object contains most of the mass of the Solar System. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** This question is about the apparent daily motion of the Sun.

**(a)** State the direction in which the Sun rises. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the Sun appears to move across the sky during the day. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why a town has night. **[1]**

**7.** This question is about the seasons.

**(a)** In June the northern hemisphere is tilted towards the Sun. State the season in the southern hemisphere in June. **[1]**
**(b)** Give **two** reasons why summer days are warmer than winter days. **[2]**
**(c)** A student says that summer happens because the Earth is closer to the Sun. Give one piece of evidence that shows this is wrong. **[1]**

**8.** This question is about the Moon.

**(a)** State the approximate time for the Moon's cycle of phases. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the positions of the Sun, Earth and Moon at (i) full Moon and (ii) new Moon. **[2]**
**(c)** The Moon does not produce its own light. Explain how we are able to see it. **[1]**

**9.** This question is about the time taken for light to travel in the Solar System.

**(a)** When Saturn is 1.4 × 10¹² m from the Earth, calculate the time for light reflected from Saturn to reach the Earth, in minutes. **[2]**
**(b)** The Moon is 3.8 × 10⁸ m from the Earth. Calculate the time for a radio signal from the Earth to reach the Moon. **[1]**

**10.** This question is about the bodies in the Solar System.

**(a)** Classify each of these bodies as a planet, a dwarf planet, a moon or a comet: (i) Venus (ii) Titan, which orbits Saturn (iii) Ceres, a minor planet in the asteroid belt that is large enough to be round (iv) a small icy body on a very stretched orbit that grows a tail near the Sun. **[4]**
**(b)** State one difference between what a moon orbits and what an asteroid orbits. **[1]**

**11.** *(Extended)* Io, a moon of Jupiter, orbits at an average radius of 4.2 × 10⁸ m with a period of 1.77 days.

**(a)** Calculate the average orbital speed of Io. **[3]**
**(b)** Another moon of Jupiter orbits at a greater radius. State how its orbital speed compares with Io's. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain your answer to (b). **[1]**

**12.** *(Extended)* The table gives invented data for four planets orbiting a distant star.

| Planet | Orbital distance / million km | Orbital period / days | Density / g/cm³ | Surface temperature / °C | Surface g / N/kg |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| W | 60 | 75 | 5.2 | 280 | 6.5 |
| X | 110 | 190 | 4.6 | 90 | 9.0 |
| Y | 420 | 1400 | 1.1 | −150 | 18 |
| Z | 900 | 4400 | 0.9 | −200 | 7.0 |

**(a)** Describe the relationship between orbital distance and surface temperature. **[1]**
**(b)** State which planets are likely to be gaseous, using data from the table. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest why Y has the largest surface gravitational field strength although it has a low density. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the average orbital speed of X in m/s. **[2]**
**(e)** State which planet has the lowest orbital speed and give a reason. **[1]**

**13.** *(Extended)* An asteroid moves round the Sun in an elliptical orbit. Point A is the point of its orbit closest to the Sun and point B is the furthest.

**(a)** State at which point the asteroid's gravitational potential energy is greatest. **[1]**
**(b)** Compare the total energy of the asteroid at A and at B. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe and explain how the asteroid's kinetic energy changes as it moves from B to A. **[2]**
**(d)** State why the Sun's gravitational field acting on the asteroid is stronger at A. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Neptune** [1].
**(b)** **Mars** [1]. **[2]**

**2.** A natural body that orbits a planet [1]. **[1]**

**3.** About **365 days** (one year) [1]. **[1]**

**4.** The **gravitational attraction of the Sun** [1]. **[1]**

**5.** The **Sun** [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** The **east** [1].
**(b)** The Earth rotates on its axis [1], once in about 24 hours, so the Sun appears to move from east to west even though it is the Earth that moves [1].
**(c)** The town has rotated to the side of the Earth facing away from the Sun [1]. **[4]**

**7. (a)** **Winter** [1].
**(b)** The Sun is higher in the sky, so each square metre of ground receives more energy [1]; days are longer than nights, so there are more hours of heating [1].
**(c)** The two hemispheres have opposite seasons at the same time, although they are the same distance from the Sun [1]. **[4]**

**8. (a)** About **one month** [1].
**(b)** (i) Full Moon: the Earth is (roughly) between the Sun and the Moon [1]. (ii) New Moon: the Moon is (roughly) between the Sun and the Earth [1].
**(c)** It reflects light from the Sun [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** t = 1.4 × 10¹² ÷ 3.0 × 10⁸ = 4700 s [1]; 4700 ÷ 60 = **78 minutes** [1].
**(b)** 3.8 × 10⁸ ÷ 3.0 × 10⁸ = **1.3 s** [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** (i) Planet [1]; (ii) moon [1]; (iii) dwarf planet [1]; (iv) comet [1].
**(b)** A moon orbits a **planet**; an asteroid orbits the **Sun** [1]. **[5]**

**11. (a)** T = 1.77 × 86 400 = 1.53 × 10⁵ s [1]; v = 2π × 4.2 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.53 × 10⁵ [1] = **1.7 × 10⁴ m/s** [1].
**(b)** It is **lower** [1].
**(c)** Jupiter's gravitational field is weaker at a greater distance [1]. **[5]**

*Common mistake:* using T = 1.77 without converting, which gives about 1.5 × 10⁹ m per day. The equation mark stands, but not the answer.

**12. (a)** The greater the orbital distance, the lower the surface temperature [1].
**(b)** **Y and Z** [1]; their densities (1.1 and 0.9 g/cm³) are much lower than those of W and X (5.2 and 4.6 g/cm³) [1].
**(c)** Y has the greatest **mass** (it is a large planet) [1].
**(d)** v = 2π × 110 × 10⁹ ÷ (190 × 86 400) [1] = **4.2 × 10⁴ m/s** [1].
**(e)** **Z**, because it is furthest from the star, where the star's gravitational field is weakest [1]. **[7]**

*Common mistake:* in (b), choosing the coldest planets without quoting densities. The reason must use the data.

**13. (a)** At **B** [1].
**(b)** The **same**: total energy is conserved [1].
**(c)** Kinetic energy **increases** [1], because gravitational potential energy is converted into kinetic energy as it moves closer to the Sun [1].
**(d)** It is closer to the Sun, and the field strength decreases with distance [1]. **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Linking seasons to distance from the Sun.
- Mixing up the Earth's rotation with its orbit.
- Placing the Earth and Moon the wrong way round for full and new Moon.
- Not converting days to seconds, or million km to metres.
- Not quoting data from a table when asked to use it.
- Explaining speed changes in an orbit with "gravity is stronger" but no energy transfer.
