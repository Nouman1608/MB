## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fluid | A liquid or a gas |
| Pressure | Force normal to a surface per unit area of that surface |
| Pascal (Pa) | Unit of pressure; 1 Pa = 1 N/m² |
| Normal | At right angles to a surface |
| Density (ρ) | Mass per unit volume, in kg/m³ |
| Upthrust (HT) | The resultant upward force on a submerged object, caused by greater pressure on its bottom surface |
| Atmosphere | A thin layer of air around the Earth that becomes less dense with altitude |
| Atmospheric pressure | Pressure caused by air molecules colliding with a surface |

## Equations

| Equation | Units | Recall or given? | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| p = F / A | Pa, N, m² | Recall | Both |
| p = h ρ g | Pa, m, kg/m³, N/kg | Equation sheet | Higher tier only |

Rearranged: F = p A, A = F / p; h = p / (ρ g).

### Area conversions

```
cm² → m²   × 1 × 10⁻⁴      (divide by 10 000)    250 cm² = 0.025 m²
mm² → m²   × 1 × 10⁻⁶      (divide by 1 000 000)  4.0 mm² = 4.0 × 10⁻⁶ m²
kPa → Pa   × 1000                                 3.5 kPa = 3500 Pa
```

*Worked reminder.* A 2.4 kN crate rests on a base of 80 cm².
A = 80 × 10⁻⁴ = 0.0080 m²; p = 2400 / 0.0080 = **300 000 Pa** (300 kPa).

## Pressure: quick ideas (both tiers)

- Fluid pressure acts **normal** to every surface it touches, in every direction.
- Same force, smaller area → larger pressure (knife edges, drawing pin points, stiletto heels).
- Same force, larger area → smaller pressure (snowshoes, tractor tyres, wide straps on a rucksack).

## Pressure in a liquid (Higher tier only)

- Deeper → taller column of liquid above → greater weight of liquid per square metre → greater pressure.
- Denser liquid → more weight in the same column → greater pressure.
- Pressure difference between two depths: Δp = Δh × ρ × g.

*Worked reminder.* A diver goes from 5.0 m to 15.0 m in seawater, ρ = 1030 kg/m³, g = 9.8 N/kg.
Δp = 10.0 × 1030 × 9.8 = **100 940 Pa** ≈ 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa.

### Upthrust chain

```
bottom surface deeper than top surface
→ greater pressure on the bottom
→ greater upward force than downward force
→ resultant upward force = upthrust
```

### Floating and sinking

| Situation | Result |
|---|---|
| Upthrust can equal the weight before the object is fully submerged | Floats, partly submerged |
| Weight greater than upthrust even when fully submerged | Sinks |
| Object less dense than the fluid | Floats |
| Object more dense than the fluid | Sinks |
| Same boat moved from seawater to fresh water | Floats lower: fresh water is less dense |

## Atmospheric pressure (both tiers)

**Model:**

- Thin layer of air, thin compared with the Earth's size.
- Less dense at higher altitude.
- Pressure comes from air molecules colliding with surfaces.

**Why it decreases with height:** fewer air molecules above a higher surface, so a smaller weight of air
above it; there is always less air above a higher surface than a lower one.

Sea-level atmospheric pressure is about 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa.

## Must-know contrasts

- **Pressure** (Pa) is not **force** (N): pressure is force per area.
- **Depth** sets liquid pressure; the **shape** or width of the container does not.
- **Upthrust** comes from a pressure **difference**, not from the pressure at one surface.
- Atmospheric pressure falls with height because of **less air above**, not "less gravity".

## Exam traps

- Dividing by 100 to convert cm² to m².
- Using the total depth in a pressure **difference** question.
- Drawing fluid pressure arrows only downwards.
- "It floats because it is light" instead of comparing weight with upthrust, or density with the fluid.
- Giving pressure in N or N/m without the area.
- Leaving out g, or using 10 N/kg when 9.8 N/kg is given.

## Quick self-test

1. State what is meant by a fluid.
2. A force of 450 N acts on an area of 0.15 m². Calculate the pressure.
3. Convert 60 cm² into m².
4. A pressure of 2500 Pa acts on 0.40 m². Calculate the force.
5. Explain why a sharp knife cuts more easily than a blunt one.
6. (Higher tier only) Calculate the pressure due to 12 m of water, ρ = 1000 kg/m³, g = 9.8 N/kg.
7. (Higher tier only) Calculate the pressure difference between 1.5 m and 4.5 m in a liquid of density
   1200 kg/m³, g = 9.8 N/kg.
8. (Higher tier only) Explain why pressure in a liquid increases with depth.
9. (Higher tier only) Explain what causes upthrust.
10. (Higher tier only) A block sinks in water but floats in a much denser salt solution. Explain why.
11. Describe a simple model of the Earth's atmosphere.
12. Explain why atmospheric pressure is lower at the top of a tall building than at its base.

### Answers

1. A liquid or a gas.
2. 450 ÷ 0.15 = **3000 Pa**.
3. 60 × 10⁻⁴ = **0.0060 m²**.
4. 2500 × 0.40 = **1000 N**.
5. Its edge has a much smaller area, so the same force gives a much larger pressure.
6. 12 × 1000 × 9.8 = **117 600 Pa** (about 1.2 × 10⁵ Pa).
7. 3.0 × 1200 × 9.8 = **35 280 Pa** (about 3.5 × 10⁴ Pa).
8. A deeper point has a taller column of liquid above it, so a greater weight of liquid acts on each square
   metre.
9. The bottom surface is deeper than the top surface, so the pressure and force on it are greater; this
   gives a resultant upward force.
10. The block is denser than water, so its weight is greater than the upthrust even when fully submerged. In
    the denser solution, pressure increases faster with depth, so the upthrust can equal its weight before
    it is fully submerged.
11. A thin layer of air around the Earth that becomes less dense with increasing altitude; air molecules
    colliding with surfaces create the pressure.
12. There are fewer air molecules, and so a smaller weight of air, above the top of the building than above
    its base.
