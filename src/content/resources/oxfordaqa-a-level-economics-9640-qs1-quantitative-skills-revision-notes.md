These notes condense quantitative skill QS1, "calculate, use and understand ratios and fractions", from section 3.5 of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Economics (9640) specification, which says the quantitative skills could be assessed in any of the assessments. For full worked examples, read the [QS1 study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-economics-9640-qs1-quantitative-skills/); to practise, use the [QS1 practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-economics-9640-qs1-quantitative-skills-practice/). All figures here are invented; "cu" means currency units.

## Core methods

| Task | Method |
|---|---|
| Share an amount in a ratio a : b : c | Divide by (a + b + c) to find one part, then multiply |
| Simplify a ratio | Divide every term by the highest common factor |
| Ratio to fraction of the whole | Part ÷ sum of all parts |
| Part : part to fraction | 3 : 5 means the first is 3/5 of the second, and 3/8 of the total |
| Fraction to percentage | Multiply by 100 |
| Compare two fractions | Convert both to decimals or percentages |
| Fraction of an amount | Multiply the amount by the fraction |

## Where QS1 appears in the specification content

| Content | The ratio or fraction |
|---|---|
| 3.1.4.4 Concentration ratios | Sales of the n largest firms ÷ total market sales |
| 3.1.1.4 and 3.4.1.2 Opportunity cost and comparative advantage | Amount of one good given up ÷ amount of the other gained |
| 3.2.2 Marginal propensities | Change in consumption (or saving, tax, imports) ÷ change in income |
| 3.2.2.4 The multiplier | 1 ÷ (1 − MPC), or 1 ÷ (MPS + MPT + MPM) |
| 3.1.3.1 Labour productivity | Output ÷ number of workers (or hours) |
| 3.2.3.4 and 3.2.4.2 Current account and national debt | Current account balance, budget deficit or debt ÷ GDP |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ratio | A comparison of two or more quantities, a : b |
| Fraction | A part of a whole, written a/b |
| n-firm concentration ratio | Combined market share of the n largest firms |
| Opportunity cost | The next best alternative given up |
| Absolute advantage | Producing more of a good with the same resources |
| Comparative advantage | Producing a good at a lower opportunity cost |
| Marginal propensity to consume | The fraction of an extra unit of income spent on consumption |
| Multiplier | The number of times a change in national income exceeds the injection that caused it |
| Labour productivity | Output per worker or per hour worked |

## Quick worked checks

**Concentration.** Five firms have sales of 40, 25, 15, 12 and 8 cu million (total 100). The two-firm ratio is 65/100 = 65%; the four-firm ratio is 92%.

**Opportunity cost.** If a worker can make 10 cars or 5 computers, one computer costs 10/5 = 2 cars and one car costs 1/2 computer. The two figures are reciprocals.

**Comparative advantage.** If cloth costs Country A 3 tonnes of wheat and Country B 2 tonnes, B has the comparative advantage in cloth. Trade at between 2 and 3 tonnes per bolt benefits both.

**Multiplier.** MPS = 0.1, MPT = 0.2, MPM = 0.3. MPW = 0.6, so the multiplier is 1/0.6 = 5/3 ≈ 1.67.

**Working backwards.** An injection of 25 cu million raises income by 100 cu million. The multiplier is 4, so MPW = 1/4 = 0.25.

## Exam traps

- **Wrong whole.** Exports : imports = 2 : 3 does not mean exports are 2/3 of trade; they are 2/5.
- **Order matters.** 4 : 5 and 5 : 4 say opposite things; label which quantity comes first.
- **Not the largest firms.** A concentration ratio uses the n largest firms only.
- **Reversed opportunity cost.** State "per unit of what" every time.
- **Absolute is not comparative.** A country can have an absolute advantage in both goods but a comparative advantage in only one.
- **Multiplier formula.** It is 1/(1 − MPC), not 1/MPC.
- **Ratio versus level.** A falling debt-to-GDP ratio can occur while debt itself rises.
- **Average versus marginal.** Output per worker is an average, not the output of the last worker hired.

## Self-test

1. Unemployed : labour force = 9 : 20. Write the unemployed as a percentage of the labour force.
2. Exports : imports = 2 : 3. What fraction of total trade is exports?
3. Five firms have sales of 40, 25, 15, 12 and 8 cu million. Find the three-firm concentration ratio.
4. A worker makes 10 cars or 5 computers a week. Find the opportunity cost of one computer.
5. In a closed economy with no government, MPC = 0.6. Find the multiplier.
6. MPS = 0.1, MPT = 0.2 and MPM = 0.3. Find the multiplier.
7. Twelve workers produce 420 units. Find labour productivity.
8. National debt is 300 cu billion and GDP is 1200 cu billion. Find the debt-to-GDP ratio as a percentage.
9. Share 360 cu million between three regions in the ratio 4 : 3 : 2.
10. Simplify 45 : 60.
11. Cloth costs Country A 3 tonnes of wheat and Country B 2 tonnes. Which country has the comparative advantage in cloth?
12. Income rises by 400 and consumption by 300. Find the MPC as a fraction.

### Answers

1. This is a part : whole ratio, so 9/20 = 45%.
2. 2/(2 + 3) = 2/5.
3. (40 + 25 + 15)/100 = 80%.
4. 10/5 = 2 cars.
5. 1/(1 − 0.6) = 2.5.
6. 1/0.6 ≈ 1.67.
7. 420/12 = 35 units per worker.
8. 300/1200 = 1/4 = 25%.
9. One part = 360/9 = 40; shares 160, 120 and 80 cu million.
10. 3 : 4.
11. Country B, because its opportunity cost of cloth is lower.
12. 300/400 = 3/4.
