## Questions

**1.** (a) Give the two situations in which a conductor develops a potential difference between its ends. **[2]**
(b) Name this effect. **[1]**

**2.** A coil is connected to a sensitive centre-zero meter. A student pushes a bar magnet into the coil, holds it still inside, then pulls it out.
(a) Describe what the meter shows at each stage. **[3]**
(b) State two changes that would increase the reading when the magnet is pushed in. **[2]**

**3.** The north pole of a bar magnet is lowered towards the top end of a vertical closed coil.
(a) State the pole that forms at the top end of the coil. **[1]**
(b) Explain why the student has to do work to push the magnet in. **[2]**
(c) The magnet is now lifted away. State the pole at the top end of the coil. **[1]**

**4.** A student drops the same magnet from the same height through four coils of the same area. A data logger records the peak pd.

| Number of turns | 50 | 100 | 150 | 200 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Peak pd (mV) | 1.2 | 2.4 | 3.6 | 4.8 |

(a) Describe how the peak pd changes with the number of turns. **[2]**
(b) Predict the peak pd for a coil of 250 turns. **[1]**
(c) State two variables that were controlled. **[2]**
(d) State one other change that would increase the peak pd. **[1]**

**5.** The pd generated by a simple alternator is recorded. The coil makes one rotation for each cycle of the output.

| Time (ms) | 0 | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 | 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pd (V) | 0 | 4.0 | 0 | -4.0 | 0 | 4.0 | 0 |

(a) State the peak pd. **[1]**
(b) Calculate the frequency of the output. **[3]**
(c) Calculate the number of rotations the coil makes in one minute. **[2]**
(d) The coil is turned twice as fast. State the new frequency and what happens to the peak pd. **[2]**

**6.** (a) Describe how the pd-time graph from a dynamo differs from the graph from an alternator. **[2]**
(b) Explain why the output of an alternator is alternating current. **[2]**

**7.** Describe how a fuel-burning power station uses a turbine to generate electricity. **[4]**

**8.** (a) State the condition under which a current-carrying wire in a magnetic field experiences no force. **[1]**
(b) State three ways of increasing the force on the wire. **[3]**

**9.** (a) Use Fleming's left-hand rule to give the direction of the force on a straight wire in each case.
(i) Field points up the page, current points out of the page. **[1]**
(ii) Field points from left to right, current points up the page. **[1]**
(iii) Field points out of the page, current points from left to right. **[1]**
(b) In case (a)(i) the current and the field are both reversed. Explain what happens to the force. **[2]**

**10.** A rectangular coil carries a current in a magnetic field and is free to turn.
(a) Explain why the coil turns. **[3]**
A straight wire of fixed length in a field is carrying different currents. The force on it is measured.

| Current (A) | 1.5 | 3.0 | 4.5 | 6.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Force (N) | 0.054 | 0.108 | 0.162 | 0.216 |

(b) Describe the relationship between the force and the current. **[2]**
(c) Predict the force when the current is 7.5 A. **[1]**
(d) State two other changes that would increase the force. **[2]**

**11.** A hand-cranked generator lights a small lamp.
(a) Suggest four changes to the generator that would increase the pd it produces. **[4]**
(b) The student notices the handle is harder to turn when the lamp is connected than when it is disconnected. Explain why. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The conductor moves relative to a magnetic field, so it cuts the field lines [1]. The magnetic field around the conductor is changing [1].
**(b)** The generator effect [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that the conductor is moving across the field, not just "in a field". A stationary conductor in a steady field gains nothing.

**2. (a)** Pushed in: the meter deflects one way [1]. Held still: zero reading [1]. Pulled out: the meter deflects the opposite way [1].
**(b)** Push the magnet in faster [1]. Use a coil with more turns, or a stronger magnet [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Give a separate statement for each of the three stages, including the direction of the deflection. A reading of zero when the magnet is still is worth stating clearly.

**3. (a)** North [1].
**(b)** The induced current has a magnetic field that opposes the field of the magnet, so the coil repels the approaching north pole [1]. Work done against this repulsion is the energy transferred to the circuit [1].
**(c)** South [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the word opposes, and link the repulsion to the movement. Answers that only name the pole for the approach do not usually explain the extra effort.

**4. (a)** A coil with more turns gives a larger peak pd [1]. It is directly proportional: 1.2 mV for every 50 turns, so doubling the turns doubles the pd [1].
**(b)** 5 × 1.2 = **6.0 mV** [1].
**(c)** Any two from: the same magnet, the same drop height (same speed), the same coil area [2].
**(d)** A stronger magnet, a larger coil area, or a faster drop (any one) [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote values to show the proportion, such as 1.2 mV per 50 turns. Saying "it goes up" alone describes less than the table shows.

**5. (a)** **4.0 V** [1].
**(b)** The peaks are at 10 ms and 50 ms, so the period is 40 ms = 0.040 s [1]. Frequency = 1 ÷ period = 1 ÷ 0.040 [1]. Frequency = **25 Hz** [1].
**(c)** 25 rotations per second [1]. 25 × 60 = **1500 rotations per minute** [1].
**(d)** The frequency doubles to **50 Hz** [1]. The peak pd increases [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert milliseconds to seconds before using the frequency relationship and show the unit. Choosing 20 ms (a half cycle) as the period is the usual slip.

**6. (a)** The dynamo graph stays on one side of the axis (the same direction) [1]. It is a series of humps that fall to zero, while the alternator graph goes above and below the axis [1].
**(b)** Each side of the coil moves upwards through the field and then downwards [1]. The polarity of the induced pd therefore flips twice per rotation [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention the reversal of direction, not just that the output changes size. Both outputs vary in size, so size alone does not separate ac from dc.

**7.** The fuel is burned to heat water [1]. The water turns to steam, which pushes the blades of the turbine [1]. The turbine turns a coil between magnets [1]. The coil cuts the magnetic field lines, so a pd is induced across it [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the steps in order, ending with the induced pd. Saying "the turbine makes the electricity" skips the physics that earns credit.

**8. (a)** The wire is parallel to the magnetic field [1].
**(b)** Increase the strength of the magnetic field [1]. Increase the current [1]. Increase the length of wire in the field [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that the field is stronger, not that the magnet is bigger. More turns is a generator factor and does not belong in this list.

**9. (a)(i)** The first finger points up the page and the second finger points out of the page. The thumb points to the **left** [1].
**(ii)** The first finger points right, the second finger points up the page, and the thumb points **into the page** [1].
**(iii)** The first finger points out of the page, the second finger points right, and the thumb points **down the page** [1].
**(b)** Reversing the current alone reverses the force [1]. Reversing the field as well reverses it again, so the force is back in the original direction [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Draw the three directions at right angles to check them. Using the right hand gives the opposite direction every time.

**10. (a)** The two long sides of the coil carry current in opposite directions [1]. Fleming's rule then gives opposite forces on those sides [1]. Together they give a turning effect [1].
**(b)** The force increases as the current increases [1]. It is directly proportional, 0.036 N for every 1.0 A [1].
**(c)** 7.5 × 0.036 = **0.270 N** [1].
**(d)** A stronger magnetic field [1]. A longer length of wire in the field [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Opposite forces on opposite sides is the key idea for part (a). Give both the pattern and a figure from the table when you describe a relationship.

**11. (a)** Turn the handle faster [1]. Use a stronger magnet [1]. Use more turns on the coil [1]. Use a larger coil [1].
**(b)** With the lamp connected the circuit is complete, so an induced current flows [1]. The current has a magnetic field that opposes the movement of the coil [1]. More work has to be done against this opposition, and that work is the energy transferred to the lamp [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) needs a complete circuit mentioned first. Without a current there is no opposing field, so the handle is easier to turn.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "the magnet makes a current" without saying the magnet or coil must be moving.
- Forgetting that a complete circuit is needed for an induced current, while the pd can exist without one.
- Describing the induced field as stopping the motion, rather than opposing it.
- Using the period as half a cycle when reading a graph, so the frequency is double the true value.
- Giving a dynamo graph that crosses the axis.
- Mixing up the first and second fingers in Fleming's rule, or using the right hand.
- Treating reversal of one factor and reversal of both as the same.
- Listing more turns or a larger coil as ways to increase the force on a straight wire.
