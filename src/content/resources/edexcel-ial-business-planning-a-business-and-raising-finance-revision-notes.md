## 1 Planning

| Point | Recall |
|---|---|
| Content (1a) | Idea and aims; market research; marketing plan; operations; owners and managers; financial information (start-up costs, forecasts, finance needed) |
| Uses (1b) | Inside: test the idea, set targets, compare actual with plan. Outside: persuade banks and investors to provide finance |
| Limits | Forecasts are estimates; poor research gives false comfort; markets change |

Method for a "use of a business plan" answer:

1. Name the user (owner, lender or investor).
2. State what that user does with the plan.
3. Link to the business in the case: a plan that shows how a loan will be repaid persuades a bank.

## 2 Internal finance

| Source (2) | Plus | Minus |
|---|---|---|
| Owner's capital: personal savings | No interest, full control | Limited amount; personal risk |
| Retained profit | No interest, no outside control | None in a new or loss-making business; slow |
| Sale of assets | Cash without borrowing | Asset lost; may sell for less than its book value |

Small reminder: Northgate Loom sells a machine with a book value of 8,000 for 6,500. It raises 6,500 of cash but receives 1,500 less than the book value.

## 3 External finance

### Sources (3a)

| Source | Key feature | Typical fit |
|---|---|---|
| Family and friends | Informal, flexible, cheap | Start-ups with little track record; risk to relationships |
| Banks | Loans and overdrafts; interest and security | Businesses with a sound plan |
| Peer-to-peer funding | Individuals lend via an online platform | Borrowers wanting an alternative to a bank |
| Business angels | Wealthy individuals invest for a share, may advise | Early-stage, high-growth ideas |
| Crowd funding | Many small contributions online | Consumer products that can attract public interest |
| Other businesses | Supplier, customer or partner lends or invests | Ventures that help the other business too |

### Methods (3b)

| Method | Key feature | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Loan | Fixed sum, interest, set repayment | Long-term purchases |
| Share capital | Sell shares; no repayment or interest; shares control | Companies seeking permanent funds |
| Venture capital | Specialist firm buys shares in a risky growth business | High growth, high risk |
| Overdraft | Borrow up to a limit; flexible; costly | Short-term cash gaps |
| Leasing | Use, not own; regular payments | Equipment, avoiding a big outlay |
| Trade credit | Pay supplier later | Everyday inventory purchases |
| Grants | Often non-repayable; conditions apply | Projects that meet a funder's purpose |

Small reminders:

- Loan interest: Sunbeam Sprouts borrows 12,000 at 10% a year on the outstanding balance and repays 6,000 of capital at the end of each of two years. Interest is 12,000 × 0.10 = 1,200, then 6,000 × 0.10 = 600, so 1,800 in total.
- Trade credit: if goods are bought on 45 days' credit and sold within 20 days, the supplier is paid 25 days after the cash arrives, so the credit is financing inventory.
- Share capital: an owner holding 80 of 100 shares who sells 25 new shares owns 80 ÷ 125 = 64%. Ownership is diluted but the company has gained funds.

Method for "suitability" answers:

1. Identify the circumstance: amount, length of time, risk, control wanted.
2. Choose the finance that fits it.
3. Give one reason it fits and one drawback.
4. Say why it is better than the nearest alternative.

## 4 Forms of business

| Form (4) | Owners and liability | Finance | One advantage | One disadvantage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sole trader | One; unlimited | Savings, retained profit, loans | Full control and all profit | Personal risk; limited funds |
| Partnership | Two or more; generally unlimited | Partners' capital, loans | Shared skills and capital | Disputes; shared profit |
| Private limited company (Ltd) | Shareholders; limited | Shares to chosen investors, loans | Limited liability | More administration; shares kept off the open market |
| Public limited company (plc) | Shareholders; limited | Shares to the public | Large capital | Cost, disclosure, takeover risk |

Other forms (4b):

- Franchising: pay a fee and often a share of revenue to use a brand and system. Reminder: a franchise charging 4% of revenue takes 6,000 from revenue of 150,000.
- Social enterprise: trades to meet social or environmental aims and reinvests most profit.
- Lifestyle business: run for a chosen way of life, with little wish to grow.
- Online business: sells through a website or app; low entry cost, high competition.

Stock market flotation (4c): selling shares to the public for the first time and getting them listed. For: large capital, profile, easier later issues. Against: cost, disclosure, shareholder pressure for profit, loss of control.

## 5 Liability

| | Unlimited | Limited |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Owner personally responsible for all business debts | Shareholder's loss limited to the amount invested |
| Who | Sole traders; partners generally | Shareholders in Ltd and plc |
| Advantage | Simple, few formalities | Protects personal assets; attracts investors |
| Disadvantage | Personal assets at risk | More paperwork and published information |
| Finance (5b) | Savings, retained profit, loans, family and friends | Also shares, angels, venture capital; plc can float |

Describe the legal position in general terms only.

Small reminder: Two partners share profit of 50,000 in the ratio 3:2. Shares are 30,000 and 20,000.

## Matching finance to circumstances

| Circumstance | Likely fit | Reason to give |
|---|---|---|
| Short gap between paying costs and receiving cash | Overdraft, trade credit | Flexible, repaid quickly, interest only on use |
| Buying machinery that lasts years | Loan, leasing, share capital | Repayment can be spread across the asset's life |
| New idea with high risk but strong growth potential | Business angel, venture capital, crowd funding | Investors accept risk for a share of the upside |
| Owner wants to keep full control | Savings, retained profit, loan | No new owners are created |
| Cannot borrow and has no savings | Family and friends, grants, crowd funding | Less reliance on credit history |
| Large expansion of an established company | Share issue, flotation as a plc | Raises large sums with no repayment date |

Always finish with a reason and a drawback. A judgement question needs both sides and a decision.

## Words to define precisely

- Business plan: a written document setting out the aims, market, operations and finances of a business and the finance it needs.
- Retained profit: the part of profit that stays in the business instead of going to owners.
- Share capital: money raised by selling shares in a company.
- Limited liability: a shareholder's loss is limited to the amount invested in shares.
- Unlimited liability: the owner is personally responsible for all the business's debts.
- Flotation: first sale of a company's shares to the public on a stock market.

## Must-know distinctions

- Internal against external finance: internal comes from the business or its owner; external from outside.
- Source against method: a bank (source) can provide a loan or an overdraft (methods).
- Share capital against loan: shares give ownership and need no repayment; a loan gives no ownership and must be repaid.
- Ltd against plc: a Ltd may not sell its shares to the public, whereas a plc may.
- Venture capital against business angel: a specialist firm against a wealthy individual.
- Leasing against buying: leasing avoids the outlay but the asset is not owned.

## Quick self-test

1. Name two items found in a business plan.
2. State one way a business plan helps a bank.
3. Give two internal sources of finance.
4. Why might a new business have no retained profit?
5. Calculate the interest in year 1 on a 20,000 loan at 8% on the outstanding balance.
6. Which method suits a short-term cash gap, an overdraft or a loan?
7. An owner holds 70 of 100 shares and 30 new shares are sold to an investor. What percentage does the owner now hold?
8. State one disadvantage of unlimited liability.
9. Name the process of selling shares to the public for the first time.
10. State one way a social enterprise differs from a conventional business.

### Answers

1. Any two from: aims, market research, marketing plan, operations, management, financial forecasts.
2. It shows the bank how much is needed, what it is for and how it will be repaid.
3. Any two of owner's capital, retained profit, sale of assets.
4. Because a new business may not yet have made a profit to keep.
5. 20,000 × 0.08 = 1,600.
6. An overdraft.
7. 70 ÷ 130 = 53.8%.
8. Business debts can be recovered from what the owner personally holds.
9. Stock market flotation.
10. It trades mainly to meet social or environmental aims and reinvests most profit, rather than maximising returns to owners.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a source of finance without saying why it suits the case.
- Using the same word for source and method.
- Calling retained profit a bank-style loan.
- Saying shares are repaid like a loan.
- Confusing Ltd and plc.
- Stating liability rules in legal detail instead of general terms.
- Ignoring the control the owner wants to keep.
- Giving only advantages in a judgement question.
