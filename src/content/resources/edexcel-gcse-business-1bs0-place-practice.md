## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define the term retailer. **[2]**

**2.** A business sells clothes to consumers only through its website and has no shops. Which **one** of the following describes it? **[1]**

A: A wholesaler

B: An e-tailer

C: A franchisor

D: A department store

**3.** Which **two** of the following are drawbacks to a producer of selling through retailers? **[2]**

A: The retailer takes a share of the selling price

B: Customers can see the product before buying

C: The producer has less control over how the product is displayed

D: The producer gains access to the retailer's footfall

E: The retailer handles customer payments

**4.** Ivydene Candles sells a candle on an online marketplace for £18. The marketplace charges a fee of 15% of the selling price. Each candle costs £6 to make and £2.50 to post.

**(a)** Calculate the marketplace fee per candle. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the contribution per candle. **[2]**

**5.** Oakhurst Cheese sells its cheeses in two delicatessens in York and through its own website. State **one** method of distribution that uses e-commerce at Oakhurst Cheese. **[1]**

**6.** Through retailers, Fennick Jams earns £1.20 contribution per jar on 5,000 jars a year. Through its own website it earns £2.00 per jar on 2,800 jars a year. Calculate the difference in total contribution between the two routes. **[3]**

**7.** Explain **one** benefit to a business of selling its products through e-commerce. **[3]**

### Section B: longer questions

**8.** Dunmore Mattresses sells its mattresses through furniture shops rather than online. Outline **one** reason why selling through shops may suit Dunmore. **[2]**

**9.** Explain **one** disadvantage to a small producer of selling its products through a large supermarket chain. **[3]**

**10.** Westcott Tea blends loose-leaf teas and sells them only through twenty independent tea shops across the north of England. Sales have been flat for two years. The owner is considering also selling through a large online marketplace, which would charge a fee on every sale. Analyse the possible effects on Westcott Tea of selling through the online marketplace. **[6]**

**11.** Rowan Prints sells art prints. Each print costs £3 to produce. The owner is choosing one main route for next year.

Option 1: sell through ten gift shops, which each pay £12 per print. Forecast sales: 1,500 prints.

Option 2: sell through her own website at £25 per print. Packing and postage cost £4 per print and payment fees £0.50. Online advertising would cost £6,000 a year. Forecast sales: 900 prints.

Justify which option Rowan Prints should choose. **[9]**

**12.** Brookvale Toys has two toy shops in a market town. The owner is considering selling online as well. A website would cost £7,000 a year. She forecasts 2,000 online orders a year, each earning £9 contribution after delivery costs. She expects that 30% of online orders would come from customers who already buy in her shops.

Evaluate whether Brookvale Toys should start selling online. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** A business that sells goods to the final consumer [1], usually through shops [1]. **[2]**

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* Wholesalers sell to other businesses, not to consumers, and are not part of this content item.

**3.** A [1]. C [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** 15% × £18 = **£2.70** [1]

**(b)** £18 − £2.70 − £2.50 − £6 [1] = **£6.80** [1] **[3]**

**5.** Its own website [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Retailers: £1.20 × 5,000 = £6,000 [1]. Website: £2.00 × 2,800 = £5,600 [1]. Difference = **£400** more through retailers [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The website earns more per jar, but the retailer route earns more in total because of its volume. Always multiply by the volumes.

**7.** E-commerce lets a business sell to customers anywhere in the country or abroad [1], without the cost of opening shops in each area [1], so it can increase sales and reach new market segments while keeping fixed costs lower than a chain of stores would need [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Other valid starting points: customers can buy at any time, or the business keeps the full selling price on its own website.

**8.** Customers spending a large sum on a mattress want to lie on it and test its comfort before buying [1], so being in furniture shops lets them try Dunmore's mattresses, which makes a sale more likely and reduces costly returns of bulky items [1]. **[2]**

**9.** A large supermarket chain buys in large quantities and can demand a low price [1], so the producer may receive very little profit per unit [1]. The supermarket may also take a long time to pay, which can cause cash-flow problems for a small producer that has already paid for its materials [1]. **[3]**

**10.** The marketplace has millions of visitors, so Westcott could reach customers far beyond the twenty shops in the north of England [1], which could end two years of flat sales [1]. Westcott would keep more of the selling price than it does through shops if the fee is smaller than the shops' mark-up, but it would have to pay for packing and postage on every order [1]. On a marketplace Westcott's teas would appear beside many rival brands, often cheaper, so it may struggle to stand out without spending on online promotion [1]. The independent tea shops might be unhappy if the same teas are available online at a lower price, and some could stop stocking Westcott [1]. Overall the marketplace offers growth, but only if Westcott can price it so that both the fee and its relationships with the shops are protected [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* Analyse answers should use the case details: the twenty shops, the north of England and the flat sales each give a point context.

**11.** Option 1 contribution per print = £12 − £3 = £9 [1]; total = £9 × 1,500 = **£13,500** [1]. Option 2 contribution per print = £25 − £4 − £0.50 − £3 = £17.50 [1]; total = £17.50 × 900 = £15,750, minus £6,000 advertising = **£9,750** [1]. Option 1 earns £3,750 more [1]. The gift shops also display the prints so customers can see their quality, which matters for art [1]. Option 2 earns almost twice as much per print and gives Rowan control over presentation and customer data [1]. However, Option 2 depends on online advertising bringing 900 buyers to a little-known website, which is uncertain [1]. **Judgement:** choose Option 1, because it earns more in total with less risk and lets customers see the prints; Rowan could add a small website later to sell to customers who discover her work in the shops [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* A recommendation based on per-unit contribution alone would choose the wrong option here. Totals and fixed costs such as advertising decide it.

**12.** New orders = 2,000 × 70% = 1,400, because 600 orders would be existing shop customers switching channel [1]. Extra contribution = 1,400 × £9 = £12,600 [1]. Net gain = £12,600 − £7,000 = **£5,600** a year [1]. For: a website reaches families beyond the market town and lets customers order at any time, which suits busy parents [1]. Customers who switch from the shops still buy from Brookvale rather than a rival online [1]. Against: toys are widely sold online by large e-tailers with lower prices and fast delivery, so the 2,000 forecast may be optimistic [1]. If orders were only 1,000, new contribution would be 700 × £9 = £6,300, less than the £7,000 website cost, a loss of £700 [1]. Packing orders takes staff time away from the shops, a cost not in the figures [1]. **Conclusion:** Brookvale should start selling online, because the forecast gain is positive and the website protects it against shoppers moving online anyway; but the room for error is small, so it should start with a simple, low-cost site and focus on products or advice that large e-tailers do not offer [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* Recognising that some online orders are not new sales is the step that lifts this answer above a simple calculation.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Comparing routes per unit and ignoring total volumes or fixed costs such as advertising.
- Forgetting to subtract postage, packing or fees from an online price.
- Treating every online order as a new sale.
- Calling a wholesaler a retailer.
- Recommending online selling for a product customers need to try, without discussing returns.
