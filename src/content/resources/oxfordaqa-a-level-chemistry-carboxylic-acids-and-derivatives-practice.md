This set covers section 3.3.9 (Carboxylic acids and derivatives, subsections 3.3.9.1 and 3.3.9.2, with required practical 10) of OxfordAQA's International AS and A-level Chemistry specification (9620), Version 5.3, for International AS and A-level exams from May/June 2020. The whole section is International A2 content, so it is **International A-level only**. Questions on it appear in Unit 4 (Organic 2 and Physical 2), and any section can also appear in Unit 5 (Practical and synoptic). Question 11 also uses the oxidation of alcohols from section 3.3.5.1.

Revise from the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-carboxylic-acids-and-derivatives/) and the [revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-carboxylic-acids-and-derivatives-revision-notes/) before attempting these. **Data.** Use Ar values Na = 23.0, O = 16.0, C = 12.0 and H = 1.0.

## Questions

**1.** **(a)** Name the esters HCOOCH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ and CH₃CH₂COOCH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃. **[2]**
**(b)** Which carboxylic acid and which alcohol would you use to make CH₃CH₂COOCH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃? **[1]**
**(c)** State two common uses of esters. **[1]**

**2.** Pentanoic acid, CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂COOH, is added to potassium carbonate.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[2]**
**(b)** Pentanoic acid reacts with carbonates, yet it is described as a weak acid. Explain why. **[1]**

**3.** State the family to which each compound belongs: (CH₃CH₂CO)₂O; CH₃CH₂CH₂COCl; CH₃CH₂CONHCH₃; (CH₃)₂CHCOOCH₃. **[4]**

**4.** Ethyl pentanoate, CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂COOCH₂CH₃, is hydrolysed.

**(a)** Give the products of heating it under reflux with (i) dilute sulfuric acid; (ii) aqueous sodium hydroxide. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the reaction with aqueous sodium hydroxide is not reversible. **[1]**
**(c)** State how pentanoic acid can be obtained from the mixture in (a)(ii). **[1]**

**5.** Glyceryl trioctanoate, (C₇H₁₅COO)₃C₃H₅ (Mr = 470.0), is a triester found in some vegetable oils.

**(a)** Give the structural formula of the alcohol from which all oils and fats are made. **[1]**
**(b)** Write an equation for the hydrolysis of glyceryl trioctanoate by sodium hydroxide. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the maximum mass of soap, sodium octanoate (Mr = 166.0), that 18.8 g of the triester can form. **[2]**
**(d)** State what biodiesel is, and give the reagent and condition used to make it from this oil. **[2]**

**6.** Write equations for the reactions of hexanoyl chloride, CH₃(CH₂)₄COCl, with:

**(a)** water **[1]**
**(b)** ethanol **[1]**
**(c)** excess ammonia **[1]**
**(d)** excess propylamine, CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂. **[1]**

**7.** Heptanoyl chloride, CH₃(CH₂)₅COCl, reacts with methanol.

**(a)** State the name of this type of mechanism. **[1]**
**(b)** Outline it, describing each curly arrow and the intermediate. **[3]**
**(c)** Name the organic product. **[1]**

**8.** Write equations for the reactions of pentanoic anhydride, (CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CO)₂O, with:

**(a)** water **[1]**
**(b)** propan-2-ol, (CH₃)₂CHOH **[1]**
**(c)** excess methylamine, CH₃NH₂. **[1]**

**9.** Aspirin is made industrially from 2-hydroxybenzoic acid (Mr = 138.0) and ethanoic anhydride (Mr = 102.0).

**(a)** Give an equation for this reaction. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the minimum mass of ethanoic anhydride needed to react with 8.28 g of 2-hydroxybenzoic acid. **[2]**
**(c)** Give three industrial advantages of ethanoic anhydride over ethanoyl chloride for this process. **[3]**

**10.** For required practical 10, a student makes a solid amide, Z, whose data-book melting point is 130 °C. The crude solid (3.10 g) melts over the range 119–125 °C. After one recrystallisation, 2.35 g of dry crystals melt over 129–130 °C.

**(a)** Explain why the student uses only the minimum volume of hot solvent to dissolve the crude solid. **[1]**
**(b)** State why the hot solution is filtered before it is cooled. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the crystals are washed with cold, not hot, solvent. **[1]**
**(d)** Use the melting point data to compare the purity of the two samples. **[2]**
**(e)** Suggest one reason, other than the removal of impurities, why the mass falls. **[1]**

**11.** Ester X, C₆H₁₂O₂, is heated under reflux with excess sodium hydroxide solution. The products are sodium propanoate and an alcohol, B. When B is warmed with acidified potassium dichromate(VI), the solution turns from orange to green. The organic product gives no silver mirror with Tollens' reagent.

**(a)** Identify B, explaining your reasoning. **[2]**
**(b)** Give the structural formula of X. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the mass of sodium propanoate formed from 4.64 g of X. **[2]**
**(d)** Name a reagent that converts B into X in a reaction that is not reversible, and write an equation for this reaction. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Butyl methanoate** [1]; **butyl propanoate** [1]
**(b)** Propanoic acid and butan-1-ol [1]
**(c)** Any two of: solvents, plasticisers, perfumes, food flavourings [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a), the alcohol word comes first; "propyl butanoate" for the second ester is a different compound and earns nothing.

**2. (a)** 2CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂COOH + K₂CO₃ → 2CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂COOK + CO₂ + H₂O: correct species [1]; balanced [1]
**(b)** It only **partially dissociates** into ions in water [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A weak acid is defined by the extent of dissociation, so answers about pH, fizzing rate or concentration do not explain "weak".

**3.** Acid anhydride [1]; acyl chloride [1]; amide (N-substituted) [1]; ester [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Read the atom after C=O: a second C=O through an O is an anhydride, Cl an acyl chloride, N an amide, an O then a carbon chain an ester.

**4. (a)** (i) Pentanoic acid and ethanol [1]; (ii) sodium pentanoate and ethanol [1]
**(b)** The acid forms as the pentanoate ion, which does not react with ethanol, so the reverse reaction cannot occur [1]
**(c)** Add a strong acid, such as dilute hydrochloric acid [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a)(ii), "pentanoic acid" is the product of the wrong conditions; name the salt.

**5. (a)** HOCH₂CH(OH)CH₂OH (propane-1,2,3-triol) [1]
**(b)** (C₇H₁₅COO)₃C₃H₅ + 3NaOH → 3C₇H₁₅COONa + C₃H₅(OH)₃: correct products [1]; 3 : 3 balancing [1]
**(c)** Amount of triester = 18.8 ÷ 470.0 = 0.0400 mol, so soap = 3 × 0.0400 = 0.120 mol [1]; mass = 0.120 × 166.0 = **19.9 g** [1]
**(d)** Methyl esters of long-chain carboxylic acids, as a mixture [1]; made with methanol in the presence of a catalyst [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* A 1 : 1 ratio in (c) gives 6.64 g; show the factor of 3 explicitly so an examiner can see your method.

**6. (a)** CH₃(CH₂)₄COCl + H₂O → CH₃(CH₂)₄COOH + HCl [1]
**(b)** CH₃(CH₂)₄COCl + CH₃CH₂OH → CH₃(CH₂)₄COOCH₂CH₃ + HCl [1]
**(c)** CH₃(CH₂)₄COCl + 2NH₃ → CH₃(CH₂)₄CONH₂ + NH₄Cl [1]
**(d)** CH₃(CH₂)₄COCl + 2CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂ → CH₃(CH₂)₄CONHCH₂CH₂CH₃ + CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₃Cl [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* With "excess" ammonia or amine, a balanced equation needs two molecules; HCl left beside a base is chemically inconsistent.

**7. (a)** Nucleophilic addition–elimination [1]
**(b)** Curly arrow from an O lone pair in CH₃OH to the δ+ C of C=O, plus a curly arrow from the C=O π bond onto O [1]; intermediate showing O⁻, Cl still attached, and O⁺ (bonded to H and CH₃) from the methanol [1]; C=O re-formed from the O⁻ lone pair, C–Cl electrons moving onto Cl as Cl⁻ leaves, and H⁺ lost from O⁺ [1]
**(c)** **Methyl heptanoate** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Begin every curly arrow at a bond or a lone pair; arrows that start on a charge or an atom symbol do not show the source of the electrons.

**8. (a)** (C₄H₉CO)₂O + H₂O → 2C₄H₉COOH [1]
**(b)** (C₄H₉CO)₂O + (CH₃)₂CHOH → C₄H₉COOCH(CH₃)₂ + C₄H₉COOH [1]
**(c)** (C₄H₉CO)₂O + 2CH₃NH₂ → C₄H₉CONHCH₃ + C₄H₉COO⁻ CH₃NH₃⁺ [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The anhydride by-product is the carboxylic acid (or its salt with excess amine), never HCl.

**9. (a)** HOC₆H₄COOH + (CH₃CO)₂O → CH₃COOC₆H₄COOH + CH₃COOH [1]
**(b)** Amount of 2-hydroxybenzoic acid = 8.28 ÷ 138.0 = 0.0600 mol [1]; 1 : 1 ratio, so mass = 0.0600 × 102.0 = **6.12 g** [1]
**(c)** Any three: cheaper [1]; less corrosive [1]; less readily hydrolysed by water or moist air [1]; reacts less vigorously, so easier to control; no corrosive, toxic hydrogen chloride gas formed **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Safer" alone is too vague to earn a mark; name the property, such as no HCl gas or slower hydrolysis.

**10. (a)** So the solution is saturated when hot and as much product as possible crystallises on cooling [1]
**(b)** To remove insoluble impurities [1]
**(c)** Hot solvent would dissolve some of the crystals; cold solvent rinses away impurities with little product loss [1]
**(d)** The crude solid melts below 130 °C and over a 6 °C range, so it is impure [1]; the recrystallised sample melts sharply at the data-book value, so it is much purer [1]
**(e)** Some Z stays dissolved in the cold solvent and is lost in the filtrate [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* For (d), use both features of the data, the temperature and the width of the range, for each sample.

**11. (a)** Sodium propanoate has three carbons, so B has 6 − 3 = 3 carbons [1]; B is oxidised to a product that is not an aldehyde, so B is a secondary alcohol: **propan-2-ol** [1]
**(b)** **CH₃CH₂COOCH(CH₃)₂** [1]
**(c)** Mr of X = 116.0, so amount = 4.64 ÷ 116.0 = 0.0400 mol [1]; Mr of CH₃CH₂COONa = 96.0, so mass = 0.0400 × 96.0 = **3.84 g** [1]
**(d)** Propanoyl chloride [1]; CH₃CH₂COCl + (CH₃)₂CHOH → CH₃CH₂COOCH(CH₃)₂ + HCl [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each observation to a conclusion in (a); a bare name for B cannot earn the reasoning mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming an ester with the acid word first.
- Writing pH or reaction speed, instead of partial dissociation, to explain "weak acid".
- Giving the free acid instead of the salt for alkaline hydrolysis.
- Missing the factor of 3 between a triglyceride and its soap or methyl ester.
- Writing HCl as a product of an acid anhydride.
- Using only one ammonia or amine molecule per acyl chloride.
- Leaving the charges off the addition–elimination intermediate.
- Writing "safer" or "better" without naming the property.
- Comparing melting points using only the temperature, without the range.
