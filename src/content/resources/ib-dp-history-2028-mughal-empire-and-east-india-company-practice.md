## Questions

**1.** Explain two reasons why Babur won the First Battle of Panipat (1526). **[4]**

**2.** Read the extract, which is *constructed for practice* and is not a real quotation.

> "The ranks were meant to bind the great men to the throne. Each man took his pay from a land grant and owed horsemen in return. But when the grants were poor, the horsemen were few and the grumbling loud." (A historian's summary, constructed for practice)

**(a)** Explain what the extract shows about the mansabdari system. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain one way in which the system helped Akbar to consolidate Mughal rule. **[3]**

**3.** Analyse the importance of the Deccan campaigns in the decline of the Mughal Empire. **[6]**

**4.** To what extent was the policy of conciliation the main reason for Mughal success under Akbar? **[15]**

**5.** Evaluate the reasons for the establishment of direct Crown rule in India in 1858. **[15]**

**6.** Compare and contrast the roles of Nur Jahan and one other woman in Mughal politics or culture. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two, each developed (1 mark for the reason, 1 for explanation). Babur's army used gunpowder weapons, matchlocks and field artillery, which the Sultan's army lacked; Babur used a flanking movement and a defensive layout that suited a smaller force; the Lodi army was divided because Afghan nobles distrusted Ibrahim Lodi; Babur was an experienced commander with a disciplined core. **[4]**

**2.** **(a)** The extract shows that mansabdars were paid through land grants and owed military service (1). It suggests the system bound nobles to the emperor (1) and that when the grants yielded little, service and loyalty weakened (1). **[3]**
**(b)** Award up to 3 for a developed point. For example: ranks fixed pay and duty in a single scale, which created a loyal and graded nobility drawn from Persians, Central Asians and Rajputs (1); rotating the jagirs prevented officials becoming local lords (1); this gave Akbar a dependable army and officials, which allowed him to hold new conquests (1). **[3]**

**3.** Marking points (up to 6): Aurangzeb's long absence from the north from 1681 to 1707 weakened central control (up to 2); the wars against Bijapur, Golconda and the Marathas cost large sums and drained the treasury and army (up to 2); the Marathas under Shivaji and later leaders were never crushed, and their guerrilla warfare created a rival power (up to 1); linking this to later decline, for instance that nobles competed for the limited supply of jagirs afterwards (up to 1). Credit a judgement on whether the Deccan was the main cause. **[6]**

**4.** Marking points (up to 15):
- Thesis and a definition of "success" (up to 2).
- Conciliation: marriage alliances with Rajput families, the abolition of the jizya in 1564, sulh-i kul, and the Ibadat Khana debates (up to 4).
- Other factors: military conquests such as Gujarat (1572 to 1573) and Bengal (1576); the mansabdari system; the revenue system (up to 4).
- Evaluation of perspectives, for example whether conciliation was principle or calculation, and historians who stress administration (up to 2).
- Judgement that is consistent with the evidence, for example that conciliation was necessary for stability, but the military and administrative base made it effective (up to 3). **[15]**

**5.** Marking points (up to 15):
- A thesis that ranks reasons (up to 2).
- The Rebellion of 1857 as the immediate trigger: Meerut, Delhi, Kanpur, Lucknow, Jhansi (up to 3).
- Failures of Company rule: revenue demands, annexations by Dalhousie, the doctrine of lapse, Awadh in 1856, reforms feared as attacks on religion (up to 4).
- The political question: whether the British Parliament and Crown wished to control a rule that had become costly and risky, with the Act of 1858 (up to 2).
- Perspectives: British accounts of "mutiny" and nationalist accounts of a rising (up to 2).
- A substantiated judgement (up to 2). **[15]**

**6.** Credit similarities and differences with named evidence (up to 3 for similarities, up to 3 for differences). Nur Jahan: ran court affairs with her father and brother, issued coins and orders in her name, and had a role in the succession struggle. A suitable comparison is Jahanara Begum, who held great wealth, advised Shah Jahan, and supported Dara Shikoh. Similarity: both were elite women with access to the emperor. Difference: Nur Jahan held real executive power through the junta, while Jahanara's influence was more as a counsellor and patron. **[6]**

## Frequently asked questions

**How should I use dates in an essay?**
Use them to show cause and sequence. Plassey (1757) and Buxar (1764) lead to the diwani (1765), and that is the point to make.

**Do I need to take a side in the 1857 debate?**
Yes, in a short and supported way. State whether you see 1857 as a mutiny, a rebellion or a war of independence, and justify it.
