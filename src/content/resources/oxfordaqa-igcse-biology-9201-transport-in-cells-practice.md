specification, Version 5.2, for exams in May/June 2018 onwards. The qualification is untiered. All data are
invented for practice. A calculator is useful for questions 6, 7 and 10.

## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by diffusion. **[2]**

**2.** State what is meant by osmosis. **[2]**

**3.**

**(a)** Name the process in which a root hair cell takes up ions against a concentration gradient. **[1]**
**(b)** State what the cell needs for this process, and which process in the cell provides it. **[1]**

**4.** A plant cell is placed in a solution that is more concentrated than its cell contents.

**(a)** Name the type of solution (hypotonic, isotonic or hypertonic). **[1]**
**(b)** Describe what happens to the cell, using the term plasmolysis. **[2]**

**5.** Name the process by which oxygen passes into a cell that is respiring. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** A student places cylinders of potato in sucrose solutions of different concentrations. Each cylinder starts at 3.00 g. The cylinders stay in the solutions for 40 minutes.

| Concentration (mol/dm³) | 0.0 | 0.1 | 0.2 | 0.3 | 0.4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Final mass (g) | 3.54 | 3.30 | 3.06 | 2.82 | 2.64 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in mass for the 0.1 mol/dm³ solution and for the 0.4 mol/dm³ solution. **[3]**
**(b)** Estimate the concentration at which the percentage change would be zero. The percentage changes at 0.2 and 0.3 mol/dm³ are +2.0% and −6.0%. Show your working. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain what a result of zero change tells you about the solution and the potato cells. **[2]**
**(d)** Give **two** variables that must be controlled. **[2]**

**7.** Two cubes of agar have sides of 3 cm and 6 cm.

**(a)** Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of each cube. **[3]**
**(b)** The cubes are placed in dye for the same time. Explain which cube would be coloured through to the centre first. **[2]**

**8.** Root hair cells in a flooded field take up fewer ions than those in well-drained soil. Explain why. **[3]**

**9.** Explain **three** ways in which the lungs are adapted to be an effective gas exchange surface. **[3]**

**10.** A cylinder of beetroot weighs 2.50 g before being placed in a very concentrated salt solution and 1.20 g afterwards. Calculate the percentage change in mass. **[2]**

**11.** An animal cell is placed in pure water. Explain what happens to the cell and why a plant cell placed in pure water behaves differently. **[3]**

**12.** Compare diffusion and active transport. Include a named example of each in your answer. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** The spreading of the particles of a gas or a substance in solution [1]; resulting in a net movement from an area of higher concentration to an area of lower concentration [1]

**2.** The diffusion of water [1]; from a dilute to a more concentrated solution through a partially permeable membrane [1]

**3. (a)** Active transport [1]
**(b)** Energy [1] from respiration (both needed for the mark)

**4. (a)** Hypertonic [1]
**(b)** Water leaves the cell by osmosis [1]; the cell membrane pulls away from the cell wall: plasmolysis [1]

**5.** Diffusion [1]

**6. (a)** 0.1 mol/dm³: (3.30 − 3.00) ÷ 3.00 × 100 = **+10.0%** [1]; 0.4 mol/dm³: (2.64 − 3.00) ÷ 3.00 × 100 [1] = **−12.0%** [1]
**(b)** Between 0.2 (+2.0) and 0.3 (−6.0), so 0.2 + 0.1 × (2.0 ÷ 8.0) [1] = **0.225 mol/dm³** (accept 0.22 to 0.23) [1]
**(c)** There is no net movement of water [1]; the solution has the same concentration as the potato cell contents (isotonic) [1]
**(d)** Any two of the following, 1 mark each [2]: temperature; time in solution; volume of solution; length and diameter (or surface area) of the cylinders; same potato; method of drying

**7. (a)** 3 cm: surface area = 6 × 3² = 54 cm²; volume = 3³ = 27 cm³; ratio = 54 ÷ 27 = **2.0** [1]; 6 cm: surface area = 6 × 6² = 216 cm²; volume = 6³ = 216 cm³; ratio = 216 ÷ 216 = **1.0** [1]; working shown for surface area and volume [1]
**(b)** The 3 cm cube [1]; it has the larger surface area to volume ratio and a shorter distance for the dye to diffuse to the centre [1]

**8.** Active transport needs energy from respiration [1]; waterlogged soil has little oxygen, so aerobic respiration in the root cells is reduced [1]; so less energy is available and fewer ions are absorbed [1]

**9.** Any three of the following, 1 mark each [3]: many alveoli give a large surface area; thin walls give a short diffusion path; a rich (efficient) blood supply keeps a steep concentration gradient; ventilation by breathing keeps a high oxygen concentration in the alveoli

**10.** (1.20 − 2.50) ÷ 2.50 × 100 [1] = **−52%** [1]

**11.** Water enters the cell by osmosis because the water is more dilute than the cell contents [1]; the cell swells and may burst because it has no cell wall to resist the swelling [1]; a plant cell has a cellulose cell wall, so it becomes firm (turgid) but does not burst [1]

**12.** Marking points, 1 mark each [6]:
- both involve the movement of particles
- diffusion is net movement from higher to lower concentration, down the gradient
- active transport is against the concentration gradient (lower to higher)
- diffusion needs no energy from respiration
- active transport needs energy from respiration
- example of diffusion: oxygen into respiring cells; example of active transport: ions into root hair cells (or sugar absorbed in the intestine or kidney tubules)

## Where marks are usually lost

- Missing the words "partially permeable membrane" in the definition of osmosis.
- Not stating "against a concentration gradient" and "energy from respiration" for active transport.
- Mixing up hypotonic and hypertonic.
- Giving the percentage change without the sign.
- Dividing by the final mass instead of the initial mass.
- Giving "large surface area" for an exchange surface with no mention of being thin or having a blood supply.
