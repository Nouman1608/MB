Condensed recall for section 3.6.1 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Business (9225). For full explanations and worked examples, use the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-9225-sources-of-finance/). Figures are invented.

## What the specification asks

- The main **internal and external** sources: **family and friends, retained profit, new share issue, loan or mortgage, selling unwanted assets, overdrafts, trade credit, crowdfunding, government and charitable grants or loans**.
- **Analyse** the advantages and disadvantages of each for a given situation.
- **Evaluate** the suitability of sources for **new and established** businesses.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Internal finance | Money from within the business or its owners |
| External finance | Money from outside the business |
| Short-term finance | Needed for less than about a year |
| Long-term finance | Needed for several years |
| Working capital | Money for day-to-day costs |
| Retained profit | Profit kept in the business |
| Dividend | Share of profit paid to shareholders |
| Security (collateral) | An asset a lender can claim if a loan is not repaid |
| Mortgage | Long-term loan to buy property, secured on the property |
| Overdraft | Borrowing by spending more than the bank balance, up to a limit |
| Trade credit | Buying now and paying the supplier later |
| Crowdfunding | Raising small sums from many people, usually online |

## The sources at a glance

| Source | Term | Best for | Main advantage | Main disadvantage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Family and friends | Either | New business | Quick, cheap, flexible | Limited sum; relationship strain |
| Retained profit | Long | Established, profitable business | No interest or repayment | Not available to a new business; less for owners |
| Selling assets | One-off | Business with spare assets | Quick, no interest | Only once; may sell cheaply |
| New share issue | Long | Limited company expanding | Large sums; no repayment | Loses some control; shares profit |
| Loan or mortgage | Long | Equipment or property | Large, fixed repayments | Interest; security needed |
| Overdraft | Short | Cash flow gaps | Flexible | High interest; can be withdrawn |
| Trade credit | Short | Buying stock | Free if paid on time | Lose discounts; relationship risk |
| Crowdfunding | Either | New, appealing products | No bank needed; publicity | May miss target; effort |
| Grants or loans | Either | Start-ups, special aims | May not be repaid | Strict conditions; slow |

## Matching source to need

| Need | Better source |
|---|---|
| Pay wages while waiting for customers to pay | Overdraft or trade credit |
| Buy a new machine | Loan, retained profit |
| Buy a building | Mortgage |
| Start a small business | Savings, family and friends, crowdfunding, grants |
| Large expansion by a company | Share issue or long-term loan |
| Quick cash with no borrowing | Sell unwanted assets |

**Rules:**
- Short-term needs, short-term finance; long-term needs, long-term finance.
- A sole trader or partnership cannot issue shares.
- A new business has no retained profit and no track record, so a bank may refuse a loan.

## Calculations

- Simple interest = amount x rate x years. $30,000 x 9% x 3 = $8,100.
- Total repaid = loan + interest. $30,000 + $8,100 = $38,100.
- Monthly repayment = total / number of months. $38,100 / 36 = $1,058.33.
- Overdraft interest = amount x rate x months/12. $4,000 x 15% x 2/12 = $100.
- Retained profit = percentage kept x profit.
- Ownership after a share issue = old shares / total shares x 100. 100,000 / 140,000 = 71.4%.

## Factors in choosing

1. **Amount** and **time** needed.
2. **Cost**: interest and fees.
3. **Risk**: must it be repaid if sales are poor?
4. **Control**: do owners lose any?
5. **Speed.**
6. **Type** of business.
7. **Security** available.

## Exam traps

- Overdrafts are for the **short term**, not buying buildings.
- Loans must be repaid with interest even if sales are poor.
- Sole traders cannot sell shares.
- Retained profit is not "free": owners receive less.
- A new share issue **dilutes** control.
- Always **justify** the recommendation with the amount, time and risk.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish between internal and external finance.
2. State two sources of finance suitable for a new business.
3. Give one advantage of retained profit.
4. Give one disadvantage of a new share issue.
5. Why is an overdraft unsuitable for buying a building?
6. Explain one advantage of trade credit.
7. A business borrows $12,000 at 10% simple interest for 2 years. Calculate total interest.
8. Calculate the total amount to repay in Question 7.
9. A company with 80,000 shares sells 20,000 new shares. Calculate the original owners' percentage afterwards.
10. A crowdfunding target is $18,000, and backers give an average of $40. How many backers are needed?
11. Give one reason why a bank might refuse a loan to a new business.
12. Recommend a source of finance for a large company wishing to expand, with a reason.

### Answers

1. Internal comes from within the business or its owners; external comes from outside.
2. Any two: owner's savings; family and friends; crowdfunding; grants; trade credit.
3. No interest or repayment (or no loss of control).
4. For example, the original owners' control is diluted, or the new shareholders share the profit.
5. It is short-term finance with high interest and can be withdrawn at short notice, so is unsuitable for a long-term purchase.
6. The business gets goods now and pays later, which helps cash flow and is free if paid on time.
7. 10% x $12,000 x 2 = **$2,400**.
8. $12,000 + $2,400 = **$14,400**.
9. 80,000 / 100,000 x 100 = **80%**.
10. $18,000 / $40 = **450** backers.
11. A new business has no track record and little security, so the bank sees it as high risk.
12. A new share issue or a long-term loan, because they provide large sums for the long term.
