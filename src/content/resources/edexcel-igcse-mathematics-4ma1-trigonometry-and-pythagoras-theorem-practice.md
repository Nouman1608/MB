## Section A

**1.** The two shorter sides of a right-angled triangle are 9 cm and 12 cm. Work out the length of the hypotenuse. **[2]**

**2.** A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 25 cm and one other side 7 cm. Work out the length of the third side. **[2]**

**3.** Work out the distance between the points (1, −2) and (6, 10). **[2]**

**4.** In a right-angled triangle, an angle of 28° has an adjacent side of 14 cm. Work out the length x of the side opposite the 28° angle. **[2]**

**5.** In a right-angled triangle, the side opposite angle θ is 6.5 cm and the hypotenuse is 11 cm. Work out θ. **[2]**

**6.** In a right-angled triangle, the side adjacent to an angle of 52° is 9 cm. Work out the length of the hypotenuse. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A ship sails 20 km from a port on a bearing of 065°.

**(a)** Work out how far east of the port the ship is. **[2]**

**(a)** Work out the length of a diagonal. **[2]**

**11.** **(Higher tier only)** In triangle PQR, PQ = 9 cm, PR = 12 cm and angle QPR = 115°.

**(a)** Work out the length of QR. **[3]**

**(b)** Work out the area of the triangle. **[2]**

**12.** **(Higher tier only)** A cuboid ABCDEFGH has a base ABCD with AB = 10 cm and BC = 4 cm. The vertical edges are 7 cm long, and G is vertically above C.

**(a)** Work out the length AC. **[2]**

**(b)** Work out the length AG. **[1]**

**(c)** Work out the angle between AG and the base ABCD. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** 9² + 12² = 81 + 144 = 225 [1]; √225 = **15 cm** [1].

**2.** 25² − 7² = 625 − 49 = 576 [1]; √576 = **24 cm** [1].

**3.** Across 5, up 12; √(5² + 12²) [1] = **13 units** [1].

**4.** tan 28° = x/14 [1]; x = 14 tan 28° = **7.44 cm** [1].

**5.** sin θ = 6.5/11 [1]; θ = sin⁻¹(0.5909...) = **36.2°** [1].

**6.** cos 52° = 9/h [1]; h = 9 ÷ cos 52° = **14.6 cm** [1].

**7. (a)** The angle between north and the ship's path is 65°, so east = 20 sin 65° [1] = **18.1 km** [1].

**(b)** North = 20 cos 65° [1] = **8.45 km** [1].

**8. (a)** √(9² + 4²) = √97 [1] = **9.85 cm** [1].

**(b)** tan θ = 4/9 [1]; θ = **24.0°** [1].

**9. (a)** tan 41° = h/35 [1]; h = 35 tan 41° = **30.4 m** [1].

**(b)** tan θ = 30.425.../50 [1] (using the unrounded height); θ = **31.3°** [1].

**10.** AC is opposite angle B and BC is opposite angle A, so AC/sin 71° = 8/sin 52° [1]. AC = 8 sin 71° ÷ sin 52° [1] = **9.60 cm** [1].

**11. (a)** QR² = 9² + 12² − 2 × 9 × 12 × cos 115° [1] = 225 + 91.28... = 316.28... [1]; QR = **17.8 cm** [1].

**(b)** Area = ½ × 9 × 12 × sin 115° [1] = **48.9 cm²** [1].

**12. (a)** AC = √(10² + 4²) [1] = √116 = **10.8 cm** [1].

**(b)** AG = √(116 + 7²) = √165 = **12.8 cm** [1].

**(c)** The projection of AG on the base is AC, so tan θ = 7/√116 [1]; θ = **33.0°** [1].

**13.** sin⁻¹(0.6) = **36.9°** [1]; the other angle is 180° − 36.9° = **143.1°** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 2: adding the squares (giving 25.96) when the hypotenuse is already known.
- Question 6: writing h = 9 cos 52°. The unknown is on the bottom, so divide.
- Question 7: swapping sin and cos. The east distance is opposite the 65° angle at the port, so it uses sine.
- Question 9(b): using the rounded height 30.4 m, which can change the last figure.
- Question 11(a): forgetting that cos 115° is negative, so subtracting 2bc cos A makes the total larger.
- Question 12(c): using the vertical edge and AB instead of AC.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I know whether to use the sine rule or the cosine rule?**
Use the sine rule when you know a side and the angle opposite it. Use the cosine rule when you know two sides and the angle between them, or all three sides.

**Why can sin⁻¹ give a wrong angle?**
A calculator only returns the acute angle, but sin θ = sin(180° − θ). If the angle could be obtuse, check 180° minus the calculator value.
