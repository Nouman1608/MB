These notes cover the "Output the presentation" content row of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 Topic 19: displaying a presentation as a looped on-screen carousel or a presenter-controlled show, and printing it as full page slides, presenter notes or handouts. The [Output the presentation study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-output-the-presentation-t19/) explains the set-up steps and calculations in full. Practise with the [Output the presentation practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-output-the-presentation-t19-practice/), and see the [Presentations revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-presentations-revision-notes/) for the rest of the topic.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Looped on-screen carousel | A show that advances automatically using slide timings and restarts after the last slide |
| Presenter-controlled show | A show in which the speaker advances each slide manually |
| Slide timing | The time a slide stays on screen before advancing automatically |
| Kiosk mode | A set-up option in many packages that loops automatically and stops viewers interrupting the show |
| Presenter view | A screen for the speaker showing the current slide, next slide and notes |
| Full page slides | Printout with one slide filling each page |
| Presenter notes (notes pages) | Printout with one slide per page and its notes underneath, for the speaker |
| Handouts | Printout with several slides per page, for the audience |

## Display: carousel vs presenter controlled

| | Looped carousel | Presenter controlled |
|---|---|---|
| Who moves the slides | The software, using timings | The speaker (click, key, remote) |
| Ends | Never; loops until Esc | When the speaker finishes |
| Typical place | Reception, shop window, exhibition, corridor screen | Lesson, talk, meeting, pitch |
| Settings | Timings on every slide; loop until Esc (or kiosk); use timings | Presented by a speaker; manual advance |
| Advantage | Runs unattended all day | Speaker sets pace, can pause, go back, jump with links |
| Watch out | Sound repeating; text too long to read in the time | Timings left on, so slides change mid-sentence |

## Printing: three layouts

| Layout | Slides per page | Who it is for | Typical orientation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full page slides | 1, full size | Checking work, display, evidence | Landscape |
| Presenter notes | 1, with notes beneath | The speaker | Portrait |
| Handouts | Several (commonly 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 or 9) | The audience | Portrait |

- Three per page handouts often include **lines for the audience's notes**.
- More slides per page saves paper but makes each slide smaller.
- **Notes and handouts have their own header and footer settings**, separate from the slides.
- Hidden slides may be included or excluded by a print option: check which the task needs.

## Matching output to purpose

| Purpose (invented examples) | Display | Printout |
|---|---|---|
| Shop window screen advertising this week's offers | Looped carousel | None needed |
| Head teacher's assembly talk | Presenter controlled | Presenter notes for the head teacher |
| Training course where trainees keep notes | Presenter controlled | Three per page handouts for trainees |
| Checking a design before it goes on a public screen | Either | Full page slides, so detail is visible |

When a question names a person, decide **who** uses the output: the **speaker** (presenter notes), the **audience** (handouts) or the **designer or checker** (full page slides).

## Calculations

**Running time of one loop** = slides shown × seconds per slide (hidden slides are not shown).

**Loops per hour** = 3600 ÷ seconds per loop.

**Handout pages** = slides printed ÷ slides per page, **rounded up**.

Examples (invented):

- 18 slides, 2 hidden, 10 seconds each: 16 × 10 = 160 seconds = 2 minutes 40 seconds per loop.
- 25 slides as handouts, 6 per page: 25 ÷ 6 = 4.17, so 5 pages.
- 25 slides as handouts, 9 per page: 25 ÷ 9 = 2.78, so 3 pages.
- 25 slides as presenter notes: 25 pages.

## Exam traps

- Timings set but the loop option not set: the show stops at the end.
- Loop set but slides left on mouse click: the carousel never advances.
- "Handouts" and "presenter notes" confused: handouts are for the audience, notes are for the speaker.
- Page counts rounded down.
- Candidate details added to slide footers when the task asked for them on the handouts.
- In theory answers, writing only "it repeats" for a carousel: also mention that it advances **automatically** using **timings**, with **no presenter**.

## Self-test

1. Describe a looped on-screen carousel.
2. Give **two** settings needed to make a carousel run.
3. Give **one** situation where a presenter-controlled show is better than a carousel, and why.
4. Who are presenter notes printed for, and what do they show?
5. What is the main purpose of handouts?
6. Why might a three-per-page handout be chosen?
7. A presentation has 14 slides, each shown for 15 seconds, with none hidden. How long does one loop take?
8. How many complete loops of the presentation in question 7 run in one hour?
9. 22 slides are printed as handouts with 4 per page. How many pages are needed?
10. Give **one** disadvantage of printing 9 slides per page.
11. A carousel has 12 slides; 2 are hidden. Each slide is shown for 9 seconds. How long is one loop?
12. Where should a candidate's name go if the task asks for it on the printed handouts?

### Answers

1. A show that moves from slide to slide automatically using slide timings, with no presenter, and starts again after the last slide.
2. Timings on every slide; loop continuously until Esc (or kiosk mode); use timings rather than manual advance.
3. For example a lesson: the teacher can control the pace, pause for questions or go back a slide.
4. The speaker; each slide with its presenter notes underneath, one slide per page.
5. To give the audience a copy of the slides to take away and refer to.
6. It usually includes lines beside each slide for the audience to write notes.
7. 14 × 15 = 210 seconds = 3 minutes 30 seconds.
8. 3600 ÷ 210 = 17.1, so 17 complete loops.
9. 22 ÷ 4 = 5.5, rounded up to 6 pages.
10. Each slide is printed very small and may be hard to read.
11. 10 × 9 = 90 seconds = 1 minute 30 seconds.
12. In the header or footer settings for notes and handouts.
