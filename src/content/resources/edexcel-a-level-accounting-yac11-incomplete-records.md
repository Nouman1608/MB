This guide covers outcome **1.3.8 Statements of profit or loss (and other comprehensive income) and statements of financial position from incomplete records and information**. It sits in Topic 1.3, Financial statements of organisations, in Unit 1 (The Accounting System and Costing) of the Pearson Edexcel International Advanced Subsidiary/Advanced Level in Accounting (YAC11), Specification Issue 2, September 2018. This is International AS content. All amounts are in dollars and every business named is invented.

Use it with the [financial statements of organisations revision notes](/resources/edexcel-ial-accounting-financial-statements-of-organisations-revision-notes/) and the [incomplete records practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-accounting-yac11-incomplete-records-practice/). The finished statements follow the layout in the [sole trader guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-accounting-yac11-financial-statements-of-sole-traders/), and the adjustments are those in the [year-end adjustments guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-accounting-yac11-year-end-adjustments/).

## What the specification asks for

| Outcome | Wording | What you must be able to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1.3.8 | Statements of profit or loss (and other comprehensive income) and statements of financial position from incomplete records and information | Rebuild missing figures from the information available and prepare both statements |

Many small traders do not keep a full double-entry system. They have bank statements, invoices, a till roll and a list of what they own and owe. The task is to rebuild the missing figures using the logic of double entry: every account must balance, so one unknown figure can be found as the **balancing figure**.

## The method in five steps

1. **Opening capital.** Use the assets and liabilities at the start of the year. Opening capital = assets − liabilities. This is the statement of affairs.
2. **Summarise the bank and cash.** Total the receipts and payments to find the closing balance, or one missing item.
3. **Total accounts.** Reconstruct trade receivables and trade payables accounts to find credit sales and credit purchases.
4. **Adjust.** Add accruals, prepayments, depreciation, irrecoverable debts and goods taken for personal use.
5. **Prepare** the statement of profit or loss and the statement of financial position. Closing capital must equal net assets.

## Worked example: Tariq Fabrics

At 1 January 2026 Tariq Fabrics owned equipment 18,000, inventory 9,200, trade receivables 6,400, bank 3,100 and cash 250. It owed trade payables of 4,800.

## The net assets (statement of affairs) method

Sometimes there is too little information to rebuild revenue and purchases. Profit can then be found from the change in capital.

Profit = closing capital − opening capital + drawings (− any capital introduced).

For Tariq Fabrics: 31,380 − 32,150 + 17,800 = **17,030**, the same answer. The method gives profit only, not gross profit or the breakdown of expenses, so it is used when the question gives no ledger information.

## Margin and mark-up

When revenue, purchases or cost of sales is missing, use the gross profit relationship.

- **Mark-up** is gross profit as a percentage of cost of sales. A 25% mark-up on cost of sales of 48,000 means gross profit of 12,000 and revenue of 60,000.
- **Margin** is gross profit as a percentage of revenue. Here 12,000 ÷ 60,000 = 20%.
- Converting: margin = mark-up ÷ (100 + mark-up), so 25 ÷ 125 = 20%. Mark-up = margin ÷ (100 − margin), so 20 ÷ 80 = 25%.

**Example: inventory lost.** A business has opening inventory 8,000, purchases 54,000 and revenue 80,000 at a 30% margin. Gross profit is 24,000 and cost of sales is 56,000. Goods available were 8,000 + 54,000 = 62,000, so the expected closing inventory is 62,000 − 56,000 = 6,000. If inventory worth 1,500 was saved after a fire, the loss is 6,000 − 1,500 = **4,500**. The loss is treated as an expense, or as a claim against insurers if cover exists.

## Common mistakes

- Using receipts from customers as if they were sales. Sales are found from the total receivables account.
- Forgetting the opening receivables or payables in the total account.
- Putting discount allowed on the wrong side of the receivables account (it reduces what customers owe, so it is credited).
- Treating goods taken for personal use as an expense instead of drawings.
- Mixing up margin and mark-up. Margin uses revenue as its base. Mark-up uses cost.
- Failing to add cash sales to credit sales when finding total revenue.
- Leaving out drawings paid from the till.
- Producing a statement of financial position where net assets do not equal capital, and not looking for the missing item.

## Exam technique

- **Prepare** questions score for each reconstructed figure, so show every account as a table, even when the answer is a single balancing number.
- Write "balancing figure" beside each unknown. It tells the examiner your method.
- Work in the order above. Opening capital and the bank summary usually come first.
- Check by comparing net assets with closing capital. If they differ, find the error before moving on.
- State assumptions when information is missing, for example "Assumed the loan was repaid on the last day of the year".
