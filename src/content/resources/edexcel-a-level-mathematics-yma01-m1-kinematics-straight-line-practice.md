These questions cover section 3 of Unit M1: Mechanics 1 in Pearson Edexcel International A Level Mathematics (YMA01): motion in a straight line with constant acceleration, including displacement–time, velocity–time, speed–time and acceleration–time graphs. Take g = 9.8 m s⁻² and give answers that use g to 3 significant figures.

Related: [M1 kinematics study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-mathematics-yma01-m1-kinematics-straight-line/) and [M1 kinematics revision notes](/resources/edexcel-a-level-mathematics-yma01-m1-kinematics-straight-line-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** A lorry slows down uniformly from 22 m s⁻¹ to 8 m s⁻¹ in 7 s. Find its deceleration and the distance it travels in this time. **[3]**

**2.** A particle moves with constant acceleration 2 m s⁻². At the end of a 6 s interval its speed is 20 m s⁻¹. Find the distance it travels in the interval, without first finding its initial speed. **[2]**

**3.** A ball is projected vertically upwards at 14.7 m s⁻¹. Find the time it takes to return to the point of projection. **[2]**

**4.** The displacement–time graph of a particle is a straight line from (0, 5) to (4, −7), where displacement is in metres and time in seconds. State the velocity and the speed of the particle. **[2]**

**5.** A particle starts from rest. Its acceleration–time graph shows an acceleration of 2 m s⁻² for 5 s, followed by an acceleration of −1 m s⁻² for 4 s. Find its velocity at t = 9 s and the distance it travels in the 9 s. **[3]**

**6.** A particle has initial speed 3 m s⁻¹ and travels 40 m in 4 s with constant acceleration. Find its acceleration and its final speed. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** A particle moves along a straight line with constant acceleration. It passes points A, B and C in that order. It takes 3 s to travel the 15 m from A to B, and a further 3 s to travel the 33 m from B to C.

**(a)** Find the acceleration of the particle and its speed at A. **[4]**

**(b)** Find its speed at C. **[2]**

**8.** A train starts from rest and accelerates uniformly for 30 s to a speed V m s⁻¹. It travels at this speed for T s, then decelerates uniformly at 0.5 m s⁻² until it stops. The journey takes 200 s and covers 4000 m.

**(a)** Sketch a velocity–time graph and show that T = 170 − 2V. **[3]**

**(b)** Show that V² − 185V + 4000 = 0. **[3]**

**(c)** Find V, explaining why one solution must be rejected. **[3]**

**(d)** State the values on the acceleration–time graph for the three stages. **[2]**

**9.** A ball A is released from rest at the top of a tower 40 m high. At the same instant, a ball B is projected vertically upwards at 20 m s⁻¹ from the ground directly below A. The balls move in the same vertical line.

**(a)** Find the time at which the balls collide. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the height above the ground at which they collide. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the velocity of B at the instant of collision, and state its direction. **[2]**

**10.** A stone is thrown vertically upwards at 4.9 m s⁻¹ from a point 29.4 m above the ground. It lands on the ground.

**(a)** Find the time from projection to landing. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the speed with which the stone hits the ground. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the total distance travelled by the stone. **[2]**

**(d)** Describe the speed–time graph of the motion, giving the key values. **[2]**

**11.** A particle P passes a point O on a straight line with velocity 12 m s⁻¹. It moves with constant deceleration 4 m s⁻² throughout.

**(a)** Find the time at which P returns to O. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the total distance travelled by P in the first 6 s. **[3]**

**(c)** Find the times at which P is 10 m from O, on the side it first moves towards. **[2]**

**12.** A lift rises from rest with acceleration 1.2 m s⁻² for 2 s. It then moves at constant speed for T s, and then decelerates uniformly to rest in 3 s. The total distance risen is 36 m.

**(a)** Find the greatest speed of the lift. **[1]**

**(b)** Find T. **[3]**

**(c)** State the acceleration in each stage of the motion. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** a = (8 − 22)/7 = −2, so the deceleration is **2 m s⁻²** [1].

s = ½(22 + 8)(7) [1] = **105 m** [1].

**2.** s = vt − ½at² = 20(6) − ½(2)(36) [1] = **84 m** [1].

**3.** Upwards positive, s = 0: 0 = 14.7t − 4.9t² [1], so t = 14.7/4.9 = **3 s** [1].

**4.** Velocity = gradient = (−7 − 5)/4 = **−3 m s⁻¹** [1]. Speed = **3 m s⁻¹** [1].

**5.** v at t = 5: 2 × 5 = 10. v at t = 9: 10 − 4 = **6 m s⁻¹** [1].

Distance = ½(5)(10) + ½(10 + 6)(4) [1] = 25 + 32 = **57 m** [1].

**6.** 40 = 3(4) + ½a(16) [1], so 8a = 28 and **a = 3.5 m s⁻²** [1].

v = 3 + 3.5(4) = **17 m s⁻¹** [1].

**7. (a)** Let u be the speed at A. A to B: 15 = 3u + 4.5a [1]. A to C: 48 = 6u + 18a [1].

Doubling the first: 30 = 6u + 9a. Subtracting: 18 = 9a, so **a = 2 m s⁻²** [1].

Then 3u = 15 − 9, so **u = 2 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** v = u + at = 2 + 2(6) [1] = **14 m s⁻¹** [1].

**8. (a)** Graph: a straight line from (0, 0) to (30, V), horizontal to (30 + T, V), then a straight line down to (200, 0) [1].

Deceleration time = V/0.5 = 2V [1]. 30 + T + 2V = 200, so **T = 170 − 2V** [1].

**(b)** Area: ½(30)V + VT + ½(2V)V = 4000 [1].

15V + V(170 − 2V) + V² = 4000 [1].

185V − V² = 4000, so **V² − 185V + 4000 = 0** [1].

**(c)** (V − 25)(V − 160) = 0, so V = 25 or V = 160 [1].

V = 160 gives T = 170 − 320 < 0, which is impossible [1], so **V = 25** (and T = 120) [1].

**(d)** **25/30 = 5/6 m s⁻²**, then **0**, then **−0.5 m s⁻²** [2] (one mark for two correct).

**9. (a)** Upwards positive. Height of A: 40 − 4.9t². Height of B: 20t − 4.9t² [1].

Equal: 40 − 4.9t² = 20t − 4.9t² [1], so **t = 2 s** [1].

**(b)** Height = 20(2) − 4.9(4) [1] = **20.4 m** [1].

**(c)** v = 20 − 9.8(2) [1] = 0.4, so B is moving **upwards at 0.4 m s⁻¹** [1].

**10. (a)** Upwards positive, s = −29.4: −29.4 = 4.9t − 4.9t² [1].

t² − t − 6 = 0, so (t − 3)(t + 2) = 0 [1]. **t = 3 s** (t = −2 rejected) [1].

**(b)** v = 4.9 − 9.8(3) = −24.5 [1], so the speed is **24.5 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(c)** Rise to the top: 4.9²/19.6 = 1.225 m. Fall from the top to the ground: 29.4 + 1.225 = 30.625 m [1].

Total = **31.85 m**, or **31.9 m** to 3 s.f. [1].

**(d)** A straight line falling from 4.9 at t = 0 to 0 at t = 0.5 [1], then a straight line rising from 0 to 24.5 at t = 3 [1].

**11. (a)** s = 12t − 2t² = 0 [1], so **t = 6 s** [1].

**(b)** At rest when 12 − 4t = 0, so at t = 3, when s = 36 − 18 = 18 m [1].

It then returns 18 m to O by t = 6 [1]. Total distance = **36 m** [1].

**(c)** 12t − 2t² = 10, so t² − 6t + 5 = 0 [1], giving **t = 1 s and t = 5 s** [1].

**12. (a)** v = 1.2 × 2 = **2.4 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** Distance accelerating: ½(2)(2.4) = 2.4 m. Distance decelerating: ½(3)(2.4) = 3.6 m [1].

2.4T = 36 − 2.4 − 3.6 = 30 [1], so **T = 12.5** [1].

**(c)** **1.2 m s⁻²**, then **0**, then **−0.8 m s⁻²** [2] (one mark for two correct).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using a different positive direction in different lines of the same answer.
- Writing the second equation in a two-interval problem from B to C with the speed at A.
- Rejecting a root without saying why, or keeping one that gives a negative time.
- Adding areas above and below the axis when displacement is wanted.
- Drawing acceleration–time graphs with sloping lines.
- Finding the time for an upward throw from a height without setting s negative.
