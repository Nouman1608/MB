## Section A

**1.** Identify **three** factors in the guide that explain the breakdown of the grand alliance between 1943 and 1949. **[3]**

**2.** State **two** aims of the Marshall Plan. **[2]**

**3.** Define "détente" and give **one** example of it. **[2]**

**4.** Outline **two** causes of the Sino-Soviet split. **[2]**

**5.** Identify **three** events between 1985 and 1991 that marked the end of the Cold War. **[3]**

**6.** Outline **two** ways in which the Cold War affected Korea. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** **(a)** Explain why the Berlin Blockade began in 1948. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain **two** consequences of the Berlin Blockade. **[4]**

**8.** **(a)** Explain why the Soviet Union placed missiles in Cuba in 1962. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain how the Cuban Missile Crisis was resolved. **[3]**

**9.** Explain why the USA and China moved towards better relations between 1969 and 1972. **[4]**

**10.** Evaluate the impact of Khrushchev on the course of the Cold War. **[6]**

**11.** Compare and contrast the impact of Cold War tensions on **two** countries, each chosen from a different region (excluding the USA and the USSR). **[6]**

**12.** "Economic problems were the main reason for the end of the Cold War." To what extent do you agree with this statement? **[15]**

## Answers

**1.** Any three, 1 mark each: ideology (communism against capitalism and liberal democracy); fear and aggression, including Soviet security fears and Western fears of Soviet expansion in Eastern Europe; economic interests, such as US desire for open markets and Soviet demands for reparations; disputes over Poland and Germany at Yalta and Potsdam; the US atomic monopoly from 1945 to 1949. **[3]**

**2.** Any two, 1 mark each: to aid the economic recovery of war-damaged Europe; to reduce the appeal of communism by reducing poverty; to create markets for American goods; to bind Western Europe to the USA politically. **[2]**

**3.** 1 mark: an easing of tension between the superpowers through negotiation and agreements, especially in the 1970s. 1 mark for an example: SALT I (1972), Nixon's visit to China (1972) or the Helsinki Accords (1975). **[2]**

**4.** Any two, 1 mark each: ideological disagreement after Khrushchev's de-Stalinization and policy of peaceful co-existence, which Mao condemned as "revisionism"; Soviet withdrawal of technical experts in 1960; Soviet refusal to share nuclear weapons technology; border disputes, leading to clashes in 1969; rivalry for leadership of the communist world. **[2]**

**5.** Any three, 1 mark each: Gorbachev's reforms of *glasnost* and *perestroika* (from 1985-86); the INF Treaty (1987); Soviet withdrawal from Afghanistan (completed 1989); the revolutions in Eastern Europe and the opening of the Berlin Wall (9 November 1989); German reunification (October 1990); the dissolution of the USSR (December 1991). **[3]**

**6.** Any two, 1 mark each: division at the 38th parallel into a Soviet-backed North and a US-backed South; the Korean War (1950-53), with intervention by UN forces led by the USA and by China; permanent division after the 1953 armistice; heavy civilian casualties and destruction. **[2]**

**7.** **(a)** Up to 4 marks: the Western powers merged their zones and planned a West German state (1); they introduced a new currency, the Deutschmark, in June 1948, which threatened the Soviet zone's economy (1); Stalin opposed a revived, Western-aligned Germany for security reasons and wanted reparations (1); he hoped to force the West out of Berlin, which lay inside the Soviet zone (1). **[4]**
**(b)** 2 marks for each developed consequence: the success of the airlift was a propaganda victory for the West and showed its commitment to Berlin; Germany was formally divided into two states in 1949; the crisis encouraged the formation of NATO (April 1949), committing the USA to the defence of Western Europe. **[4]**

**8.** **(a)** Up to 4 marks: to reduce the US lead in long-range nuclear missiles (1); to balance US Jupiter missiles in Turkey (1); to protect Castro's regime from a US invasion after the Bay of Pigs (1961) (1); to strengthen Khrushchev's position in the communist world and in disputes over Berlin (1). **[4]**
**(b)** 1 mark each: Kennedy announced a naval "quarantine" of Cuba rather than an air strike; after secret negotiations, Khrushchev agreed to withdraw the missiles in return for a public US pledge not to invade Cuba; the USA privately undertook to remove its missiles from Turkey. **[3]**

**9.** Up to 4 marks: the Sino-Soviet border clashes of 1969 made China fear the USSR more than the USA (1); Nixon and Kissinger wanted to use China as leverage against the USSR (1); the USA wanted help in ending the Vietnam War on acceptable terms (1); steps such as "ping-pong diplomacy" (1971), the transfer of China's UN seat to Beijing (1971) and Nixon's visit (February 1972) built the relationship (1). **[4]**

**10.** Up to 6 marks: 2 for identifying his policies, 2 for evaluating their impact, 2 for a supported judgement. Policies: peaceful co-existence (1956) and summitry; the Austrian State Treaty (1955); crushing the Hungarian uprising (1956); Berlin ultimatum (1958) and the Berlin Wall (1961); placing missiles in Cuba (1962). Impact: he reduced tension in some respects and, after Cuba, accepted the hotline and Partial Test Ban Treaty (1963); but his brinkmanship produced the most dangerous crisis of the Cold War, and his de-Stalinization contributed to the Sino-Soviet split. Judgement: contradictory impact, combining moves towards co-existence with dangerous risk-taking. **[6]**

**11.** Up to 6 marks; both similarities and differences are needed. *Germany (Europe) and Vietnam (Asia and Oceania), for example.* Similarities: both were divided as a result of superpower rivalry (Germany in 1949; Vietnam at Geneva in 1954); both became symbols and flashpoints of the Cold War. Differences: Germany's division was stabilized without war after the Berlin Wall, whereas Vietnam suffered prolonged war with direct US involvement; Vietnam was reunified by communist victory (1975-76), Germany by the collapse of East German communism (1990); Vietnam's conflict began as an independence struggle, while Germany's division came from the occupation after 1945. The top mark needs a judgement on why the impact differed. **[6]**

**12.** Marked holistically using the 15-mark markbands; the indicative content is a guide, not a checklist. For: Soviet economic stagnation from the 1970s, low productivity, falling oil prices in the 1980s, the cost of the arms race and of the war in Afghanistan, which pushed Gorbachev to seek reform and arms control. Against: the arms race and Reagan's pressure, including SDI; ideological challenges and dissent, such as Solidarity in Poland and nationalist movements within the USSR; Gorbachev's own choices, especially abandoning the Brezhnev Doctrine and refusing to use force in 1989; the failure of the August 1991 coup. A strong answer shows how the factors interacted (economic weakness made reform necessary; reform released dissent) and judges, for example, that economic problems were the underlying cause, but Gorbachev's decisions explain why the Cold War ended peacefully and when it did. **[15]**

## Frequently asked questions

**Does every Cold War essay need two regions?**
Only those that ask for leaders, countries or crises "each chosen from a different region". Questions on origins or the end of the Cold War may not.

**Can I use China as one of "two countries"?**
Yes. Only the USA and the USSR are excluded.
