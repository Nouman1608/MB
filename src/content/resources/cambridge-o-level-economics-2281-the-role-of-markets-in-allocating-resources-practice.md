These questions cover section 2.2, *The role of markets in allocating resources*, of the Cambridge O Level Economics 2281 syllabus for examination in 2026: how a market system works, equilibrium and disequilibrium, the questions of what, how and for whom, and the price mechanism. All markets, firms and figures are invented. ## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** In a market, the price is below the equilibrium price. What will exist? **[1]**

A: Excess supply

B: Excess demand

C: Equilibrium

D: A surplus of the good

**2.** A firm decides whether to use more machines or more workers. Which key question is it answering? **[1]**

A: What to produce

B: How to produce

C: For whom to produce

D: How much to charge

**3.** A rise in the price of a scarce good means that only those willing and able to pay can buy it. Which function of price is this? **[1]**

A: Signalling

B: Incentive

C: Rationing

D: Taxation

**4.** Define *market equilibrium*. **[2]**

**5.** State the three key questions that every economy must answer. **[3]**

**6.** Explain why a surplus in a free market is likely to be temporary. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** The table shows the market for phone cases in a town.

| Price (cu) | Quantity demanded | Quantity supplied |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 500 | 100 |
| 5 | 420 | 220 |
| 6 | 340 | 340 |
| 7 | 260 | 460 |
| 8 | 180 | 580 |

**(a)** State the equilibrium price and quantity. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the excess supply at a price of 8 cu. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain how the market would move from a price of 8 cu to equilibrium. **[3]**

**8.** Demand for printed newspapers falls as more people read news online. Explain how the price mechanism would reallocate resources in response. **[4]**

**9.** A textile firm can make the same output using method P (12 workers and 50 cu of equipment a day) or method Q (4 workers and 250 cu of equipment a day).

**(a)** Calculate the daily cost of each method when the wage is 20 cu a day. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the wage at which the two methods cost the same. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain what the firm would do if the wage rose to 30 cu a day. **[1]**

**10.** A country has a shortage of nurses. Explain how the price mechanism in the labour market could reduce the shortage over time. **[3]**

**11.** Explain how a market system answers the question "for whom to produce", and identify **one** problem with this answer. **[3]**

**12.** Discuss whether the price mechanism is a good way to answer the three key questions of what, how and for whom to produce. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**

**3.** C [1]. **[1]**

**4.** The situation where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied [1], so there is no pressure for the price to change [1]. **[2]**

**5.** What to produce [1]; how to produce [1]; for whom to produce [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Sellers with unsold stock cut their prices [1]; as the price falls, quantity demanded extends and quantity supplied contracts until the surplus disappears [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Price 6 cu [1]; quantity 340 [1].

**(b)** 580 − 180 = **400** cases [1].

**(c)** At 8 cu, 400 cases are unsold, so sellers cut the price [1]. As the price falls, quantity demanded extends [1] and quantity supplied contracts, until they are equal at 6 cu and 340 cases [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* use the language of movement along the curves: quantity demanded "extends", quantity supplied "contracts". Saying "demand rises" suggests a shift.

**8.** The fall in demand reduces the price of printed newspapers (signal) [1]. Profit from printing falls, so firms cut output and some leave the industry (incentive) [1]. Workers, printing presses and paper are released from newspaper printing [1] and move to industries where demand and profit are rising, such as online news, where firms hire more staff [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** P: 12 × 20 + 50 = **290 cu** [1]. Q: 4 × 20 + 250 = **330 cu** [1].

**(b)** 12w + 50 = 4w + 250, so 8w = 200 [1] and w = **25 cu** [1].

**(c)** At 30 cu, method Q is cheaper (370 cu against 410 cu), so the firm would switch to the more capital-intensive method [1]. **[5]**

**10.** The shortage means employers compete for nurses, so wages for nurses rise [1]. Higher wages signal and give an incentive for more people to train as nurses, and for nurses in other countries or other jobs to move into nursing [1]. Over time the supply of nurses increases and the shortage is reduced, although training takes several years [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Goods go to people who are willing and able to pay the market price [1], so the answer depends on income: those with higher incomes can buy more [1]. Problem: people on low incomes may be unable to afford essentials such as healthcare or housing [1]. **[3]**

**12.** For: prices signal what consumers want, so resources move into goods that are in demand without a central planner [1]. Profit encourages firms to use the lowest-cost method, so "how" is answered efficiently [1]. The system responds quickly to changes in tastes or costs [1]. Against: "for whom" depends on income, so the outcome may be unfair and people on low incomes may lack essentials [1]. Some goods may be under-provided or over-provided because prices do not reflect all costs and benefits [1]. Conclusion: the price mechanism answers "what" and "how" well, but it may need government action to improve the answer to "for whom" and to correct market failure [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* "discuss" needs both sides and a reasoned conclusion. A list of advantages alone cannot reach full marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Placing a shortage above the equilibrium price.
- Saying "demand rises" for a movement along the curve.
- Explaining the price mechanism without saying how firms respond and where resources move.
- Forgetting that factor markets work through the price mechanism too, with wages as the price.
- One-sided "discuss" answers.
