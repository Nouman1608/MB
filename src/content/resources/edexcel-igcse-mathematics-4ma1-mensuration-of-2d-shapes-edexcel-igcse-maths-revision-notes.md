Condensed recall for section 4.9 (Mensuration of 2D shapes) of the 4MA1 specification. Unit conversion, perimeters, triangles, parallelograms, trapezia, circles and semicircles are Foundation Tier content for both tiers. Perimeters and areas of sectors are **Higher tier only**, and radian measure is excluded. Worked examples are in the [Mensuration of 2D shapes study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-mensuration-of-2d-shapes-edexcel-igcse-maths/), and exam-style questions are in the [Mensuration of 2D shapes practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-mensuration-of-2d-shapes-edexcel-igcse-maths-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Perimeter | Total distance around the outside edge of a 2D shape |
| Area | Amount of surface covered, in square units |
| Perpendicular height | Distance at right angles to the base, not the slanting side |
| Parallelogram | Quadrilateral with two pairs of parallel sides |
| Trapezium | Quadrilateral with one pair of parallel sides |
| Circumference | Perimeter of a circle |
| Semicircle | Half of a circle, cut along a diameter |
| Sector | Region between two radii and an arc (Higher tier only for calculations) |
| Arc | Part of the circumference |

## Metric unit conversions (A)

| Length | Area |
|---|---|
| 1 cm = 10 mm | 1 cm² = 100 mm² |
| 1 m = 100 cm | 1 m² = 10 000 cm² |
| 1 km = 1000 m | 1 km² = 1 000 000 m² |

**Rule:** square the length factor for area. Large unit to small unit: multiply. Small unit to large unit: divide.

- 3.6 m² = 36 000 cm²
- 45 000 mm² = 450 cm²
- 0.0075 km² = 7500 m²

## Perimeter (B)

- Add every outside edge.
- Work out missing sides by subtraction from opposite sides, or by Pythagoras.
- A notch cut from the **corner** of a rectangle leaves the perimeter unchanged.
- When two parts join, the joining edge is inside the shape, so leave it out.

## Area formulae (C and D)

| Shape | Area | On the formulae sheet? |
|---|---|---|
| Rectangle | length × width | No |
| Triangle | ½ × base × perpendicular height | No |
| Parallelogram | base × perpendicular height | No |
| Trapezium | ½ × (a + b) × h | Yes, both tiers |

In the trapezium formula a and b are the parallel sides and h is the distance between them.

**Finding a missing length:** write the formula, substitute, solve. Trapezium of area 63 cm² with parallel sides 7 cm and 11 cm: 63 = ½ × 18 × h = 9h, so h = 7 cm.

## Circles and semicircles (E)

| Quantity | Formula | Example, r = 6.5 cm |
|---|---|---|
| Circumference | C = 2πr = πd | 40.8 cm |
| Area | A = πr² | 133 cm² |
| Semicircle area | ½πr² | 66.4 cm² |
| Semicircle perimeter | πr + 2r | 20.4 + 13 = 33.4 cm |

None of these is on the formulae sheet, so learn them.

- Use the **radius** in πr². Halve a given diameter first.
- "In terms of π" means leave π in the answer, such as 18π.
- Working backwards: r = C ÷ 2π. If C = 50 m then r = 7.96 m.

## Compound shapes

1. Split the shape into rectangles, triangles, semicircles and so on.
2. Area: add parts that join, subtract holes.
3. Perimeter: include outside edges only (the curved edge of a semicircle counts, its diameter does not when it is joined to another part).

Example: a 10 cm by 6 cm rectangle with a semicircle of diameter 6 cm on one short side. Area = 60 + 14.1 = 74.1 cm². Perimeter = 10 + 10 + 6 + π × 3 = 35.4 cm.

## Sectors (Higher tier only)

Fraction of the circle = θ/360.

| Quantity | Formula | Example, r = 9 cm, θ = 140° |
|---|---|---|
| Arc length | θ/360 × 2πr | 22.0 cm |
| Sector area | θ/360 × πr² | 99.0 cm² |
| Sector perimeter | arc length + 2r | 40.0 cm |

Finding the angle from an arc: θ = arc ÷ (2πr) × 360. Arc 20 cm, r = 8 cm gives θ = 143.2°.

## Quick chains

- Rectangle 15 by 8 → area 120, perimeter 46.
- Triangle base 12, height 7 → area 42.
- Parallelogram base 9, height 5, slant 6 → area 45, perimeter 30.
- Trapezium 6, 10, height 4 → area 32.
- Circle d = 10 → C = 31.4, A = 78.5.
- Square metre to square centimetres → × 10 000.

## Exam traps

- Using 100 for cm² to m². The factor is 10 000.
- Using the slanting side as the height.
- Using πd² or π × diameter × diameter for the area.
- Missing the diameter off a semicircle or sector perimeter.
- Forgetting ½ for a triangle or trapezium.
- Rounding in the middle of a calculation.
- Writing cm instead of cm² for an area.

## Self-test

1. Convert 2.4 m² to cm².
2. Convert 8500 mm² to cm².
3. A rectangle is 14 cm by 6.5 cm. Find its perimeter and area.
4. Find the area of a triangle with base 9 cm and perpendicular height 12 cm.
5. A parallelogram has base 13 cm and perpendicular height 4.5 cm. Find its area.
6. A trapezium has parallel sides 5 cm and 9 cm, 4 cm apart. Find its area.
7. Find the circumference of a circle of diameter 20 cm, to 3 significant figures.
8. Find the area of a circle of radius 4 cm, in terms of π.
9. Find the perimeter of a semicircle of diameter 10 cm, to 3 significant figures.
10. A trapezium of area 48 cm² has parallel sides 5 cm and 7 cm. Find the distance between them.
11. (Higher) Find the arc length of a sector with radius 12 cm and angle 60°, in terms of π.
12. (Higher) Find the area of a sector with radius 10 cm and angle 72°, in terms of π.

### Answers

1. 2.4 × 10 000 = 24 000 cm².
2. 8500 ÷ 100 = 85 cm².
3. Perimeter = 2 × (14 + 6.5) = 41 cm. Area = 14 × 6.5 = 91 cm².
4. ½ × 9 × 12 = 54 cm².
5. 13 × 4.5 = 58.5 cm².
6. ½ × (5 + 9) × 4 = 28 cm².
7. π × 20 = 62.8 cm.
8. π × 16 = 16π cm².
9. π × 5 + 10 = 15.7 + 10 = 25.7 cm.
10. 48 = ½ × 12 × h = 6h, so h = 8 cm.
11. 60/360 × 2π × 12 = 4π cm.
12. 72/360 × π × 100 = 20π cm².

## Frequently asked questions

**Which mensuration formulae are on the 4MA1 formulae sheet?**
Only the area of a trapezium, ½(a + b)h, appears among the area formulae. The circumference and area of a circle, the area of a triangle and parallelogram and the sector formulae must be learned.

**Do I need radians for sectors in 4MA1?**
No. The specification excludes radian measure, so sector work uses degrees.

**What is the difference between Foundation and Higher on this section?**
Both tiers cover statements A to E. Only the Higher Tier covers perimeters and areas of sectors.
