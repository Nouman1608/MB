## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Gravitational field | Region where a mass feels an attractive force from another mass | |
| Gravitational field strength, g | Force per kilogram in the field. 10 N/kg at the Earth's surface | N/kg |
| Mass, m | Amount of matter. Same everywhere | kg |
| Weight, W | Force on a mass due to the gravitational field it is in | N |
| Free fall | Motion with weight as the only force. Acceleration about 10 m/s² near Earth | m/s² |
| Pressure, p | Force at right angles to a surface per unit area | Pa |
| Fluid | A gas or a liquid | |

## Formulae

| Statement | Equation | Symbols and units | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| P2.3h | W = m g | W in N, m in kg, g in N/kg | Both |
| P2.3j | m = W / g and g = W / m | rearrangements of the above | Higher Tier only |
| P2.3o | p = F / A | p in Pa, F in N, A in m² | Higher Tier only |
| P2.3o | F = p A and A = F / p | rearrangements | Higher Tier only |
| P2.3o | F₁ / A₁ = F₂ / A₂ | pressure equal at both pistons | Higher Tier only |

## P2.3g: gravitational fields

- All matter has a gravitational field.
- The force on another mass is always **attraction**.
- Field strength is much greater for massive objects, so only planets and stars give noticeable forces.
- The pull acts on both objects: Earth pulls you and you pull Earth.

## P2.3h: weight and the newtonmeter

Weight is a **force**. Mass is not.

Method to find weight:

1. Write W = m g.
2. Check the mass is in kg (change grams by dividing by 1000).
3. Use g = 10 N/kg for Earth unless the question gives another value.
4. Substitute and give the answer in N.

Measuring: hang the object on a **newtonmeter**. The spring stretches until it balances the weight and the pointer shows the force in newtons.

Small reminder: a 2.4 kg mass weighs 24 N on Earth. On the Moon, with g = 1.6 N/kg, it weighs 2.4 × 1.6 = 3.84 N. Its mass is still 2.4 kg.

### Must-know distinction: mass versus weight

| | Mass | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Quantity type | Amount of matter | A force |
| Unit | kg | N |
| Changes with planet? | No | Yes |
| Measured with | Balance | Newtonmeter |
| Direction | None | Down, towards the centre of the planet |

## P2.3i: free fall

- Recall: acceleration in free fall is about 10 m/s² (9.8 m/s² more precisely) at the Earth's surface.
- All masses accelerate equally when air resistance is absent.
- Speed gained per second is about 10 m/s, so v ≈ 10 t from rest.
- In air, air resistance grows with speed and reduces the acceleration.
- N/kg and m/s² are equivalent units for g.

## P2.3j: applying the formulae (Higher Tier only)

| If this changes | Then |
|---|---|
| Mass doubles, same g | Weight doubles |
| g halves, same mass | Weight halves |
| Weight fixed, g smaller | Mass is larger |
| Graph of W against m | Straight line through origin, gradient = g |

Method for a multi-step problem:

1. List what is given with units.
2. Find mass first, since it does not change: m = W / g.
3. Use the new g to find the new weight.
4. If the weight is not balanced, find resultant force, then use F = m a.

Worked reminder: an object weighs 7.4 N where g = 3.7 N/kg. Its mass is 7.4 / 3.7 = 2.0 kg, so on Earth it weighs 2.0 × 10 = 20 N. The mass is found first because it is the quantity that stays the same.

## P2.3n: pressure force in fluids (Higher Tier only)

- Fluid particles collide with surfaces and each collision pushes on the surface.
- The net force from fluid pressure acts **at right angles** to the surface.
- On a curved surface the force on each patch points straight out from that patch.
- When fluid is on both sides, the net force depends on the **difference** in pressure.

## P2.3o: p = F / A and hydraulics (Higher Tier only)

Method:

1. Convert area to m² (1 m² = 10 000 cm²).
2. Pick the arrangement: p = F / A, F = p A or A = F / p.
3. Substitute, calculate and give the unit.

Small reminder: 500 N on 0.020 m² gives p = 500 / 0.020 = 25 000 Pa. A pressure of 3.0 × 10⁴ Pa on 0.50 m² gives F = 3.0 × 10⁴ × 0.50 = 15 000 N.

Idea behind the practical suggestion: one drawing pin concentrates the force on a tiny area, giving a high pressure that bursts a balloon. Many pins share the force over a bigger total area, so the pressure is lower.

### Hydraulic systems

- Enclosed liquid cannot be compressed, so it passes pressure on equally.
- Same pressure at both pistons: F₁ / A₁ = F₂ / A₂.
- Force multiplier = A₂ / A₁.
- Distance moved by the large piston is smaller by the same factor.
- Work done is equal in the ideal case: F₁ d₁ = F₂ d₂.

Worked reminder: pistons of area 3.0 × 10⁻⁴ m² and 9.0 × 10⁻³ m², with 50 N on the small one. The area ratio is 9.0 × 10⁻³ / 3.0 × 10⁻⁴ = 30, so the force on the large piston is 50 × 30 = 1500 N. If the small piston moves 0.12 m, the large one moves 0.12 / 30 = 0.0040 m.

## Quick self-test

Questions 1 to 4 are both tiers. Questions 5 to 12 are Higher Tier only.

1. What does a newtonmeter measure, and in what unit?
2. Find the weight of a 0.75 kg object at the Earth's surface.
3. A 6.0 kg rock is taken to the Moon, where g = 1.6 N/kg. State its mass and weight there.
4. Recall the acceleration in free fall near the Earth's surface.
5. An object weighs 90 N on Earth. Find its mass.
6. A sample weighs 21 N where g = 3.5 N/kg. Find its mass and its weight on Earth.
7. A graph of weight against mass is a straight line through the origin. A 3.0 kg mass has a weight of 24 N. Find the gradient and say what it represents.
8. State the direction of the force a gas exerts on the wall of its container.
9. A force of 250 N acts at right angles on 0.050 m². Find the pressure.
10. A force of 600 N is produced by a pressure of 2.4 × 10⁵ Pa. Find the area.
11. Convert 400 cm² to m².
12. A hydraulic system has small piston area 5.0 × 10⁻⁴ m² and large piston area 2.0 × 10⁻² m². A force of 40 N acts on the small piston. Find the force on the large piston.

### Answers

1. Weight (a force), in newtons (N).
2. W = 0.75 × 10 = **7.5 N**.
3. Mass **6.0 kg**; weight = 6.0 × 1.6 = **9.6 N**.
4. About **10 m/s²** (9.8 m/s² more precisely).
5. m = 90 / 10 = **9.0 kg**.
6. m = 21 / 3.5 = **6.0 kg**; Earth weight = 6.0 × 10 = **60 N**.
7. Gradient = 24 / 3.0 = **8.0 N/kg**, the gravitational field strength.
8. **At right angles** to the wall.
9. p = 250 / 0.050 = **5000 Pa**.
10. A = F / p = 600 / (2.4 × 10⁵) = **0.0025 m²**.
11. 400 / 10 000 = **0.04 m²**.
12. Ratio = 2.0 × 10⁻² / 5.0 × 10⁻⁴ = 40, so F = 40 × 40 = **1600 N**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving weight in kg or mass in N.
- Saying "gravity" is 10 without the unit N/kg.
- Forgetting to find the mass first when moving between planets.
- Using area in cm² or mm² without converting to m².
- Saying pressure force acts "along" or "parallel to" the surface.
- Ignoring the pressure outside when a surface has fluid on both sides.
- Dividing instead of multiplying when finding force on the large hydraulic piston.
- Claiming a heavier object falls faster when there is no air resistance.
- Giving a long unrounded decimal instead of a sensible number of significant figures.
