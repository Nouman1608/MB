These questions cover section **4.3.1 Chemical measurements, conservation of mass and the quantitative
interpretation of chemical equations** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462) specification, for teaching from
September 2016 onwards (version 1.2). The content is examined on Paper 1 at Foundation and Higher Tier, and no
question here tests Higher-tier-only content. Relative atomic masses: H 1, C 12, N 14, O 16, Na 23, Mg 24, Al 27,
S 32, Cl 35.5, Ca 40, Fe 56. Show your working.

Learn the content first in the [study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-chemical-measurements-conservation-of-mass/)
and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-chemical-measurements-conservation-of-mass-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** State the law of conservation of mass. **[1]**

**2.** Balance the equation: Fe + Cl₂ → FeCl₃. **[2]**

**3.** Calculate the relative formula mass (Mr) of Mg(OH)₂. **[2]**

**4.** Calculate the percentage by mass of calcium in calcium carbonate, CaCO₃. **[2]**

## Section B

**5.** A student burns 4.8 g of magnesium in an open crucible. The magnesium oxide formed has a mass of 8.0 g.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of oxygen that reacted. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the mass of solid increased. **[2]**

**6.** A student heats 10.0 g of calcium carbonate strongly in an open crucible. The solid left has a mass of
5.6 g. The equation is CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂

**(a)** Calculate the mass of gas that escaped. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain, using the particle model, why the mass of the crucible contents decreased. **[2]**
**(c)** State what would happen to the total mass if the reaction were done in a sealed container. **[1]**

**7.** Show that the relative formula masses are balanced in the equation:
Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂ **[4]**

**8.** Two fertilisers contain nitrogen. Ammonium nitrate is NH₄NO₃ and ammonium sulfate is (NH₄)₂SO₄.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage by mass of nitrogen in each. **[4]**
**(b)** State which fertiliser has the higher percentage of nitrogen. **[1]**

**9.** A student heats a sample four times and records the mass lost each time as 4.38 g, 4.46 g, 4.41 g
and 4.43 g.

**(a)** Calculate the mean mass lost. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the range. **[1]**
**(c)** State the result in the form mean ± uncertainty, using half the range as the uncertainty. **[2]**

**10.** A student reacts sodium carbonate with hydrochloric acid in an open conical flask on a balance. The mass
at the start is 150.0 g and the mass at the end is 148.9 g.

**(a)** Calculate the mass lost. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the substance lost and explain why the mass decreased. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why this does not break the law of conservation of mass. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **No atoms are lost or made** in a chemical reaction, so the mass of the products equals the mass of the
reactants [1]

**2.** 2Fe + 3Cl₂ → 2FeCl₃: **2FeCl₃** [1]; **2Fe** and **3Cl₂** [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Mr = 24 + 2 × (16 + 1) [1] = **58** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Mr CaCO₃ = 40 + 12 + 48 = 100 [1]; 40 ÷ 100 × 100 = **40%** [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** 8.0 - 4.8 = **3.2 g** [1]
**(b)** **Oxygen** from the air [1] combined with the magnesium, so the product is heavier than the metal [1].
**[2]**

**6. (a)** 10.0 - 5.6 = **4.4 g** [1]
**(b)** **Carbon dioxide** is a gas [1]; its particles spread out and escape from the crucible, so their mass is
no longer part of the contents [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The mass would **stay the same** [1]

**7.** Reactants: Mr Na₂CO₃ = 2 × 23 + 12 + 48 = 106 [1]; 2HCl = 2 × 36.5 = 73 [1]; total = 179. Products:
2NaCl = 2 × 58.5 = 117; H₂O = 18; CO₂ = 44, total = 179 [1]. The totals are equal, so the Mr values balance [1].
**[4]**

**8. (a)** NH₄NO₃: Mr = 14 + 4 + 14 + 48 = 80 [1]; N = 28 ÷ 80 × 100 = **35%** [1]. (NH₄)₂SO₄: Mr = 132 [1]; N =
28 ÷ 132 × 100 = **21.2%** [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** **Ammonium nitrate** [1]

**9. (a)** (4.38 + 4.46 + 4.41 + 4.43) ÷ 4 = 17.68 ÷ 4 = **4.42 g** [1]
**(b)** 4.46 - 4.38 = **0.08 g** [1]
**(c)** Uncertainty = 0.08 ÷ 2 = 0.04 [1]; result = **4.42 ± 0.04 g** [1]. **[2]**

**10. (a)** 150.0 - 148.9 = **1.1 g** [1]
**(b)** **Carbon dioxide** gas escaped from the open flask [1]; its mass is no longer on the balance [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** No atoms were lost: the carbon dioxide atoms are still conserved, but they are in the air [1]; in a
closed system the total mass would not change [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Changing a formula to balance an equation.
- Not multiplying the atoms in brackets, for example in Mg(OH)₂ and (NH₄)₂SO₄.
- Dividing by the wrong Mr in a percentage calculation.
- Saying "mass was lost" without naming the gas.
- Giving the mean without the unit, or forgetting to halve the range for the uncertainty.
