## Section A

**1.** State **two** differences between a receipts and payments account and an income and expenditure account. **[2]**

**2.** State the name given to the net assets of a club that has no owner, and the name given to a profit made in a year. **[2]**

**3.** At the start of the year a club had subscriptions in arrears of 120 and subscriptions paid in advance of 80. During the year 5,400 was received. At the year end, 200 was in arrears and 150 had been paid in advance. Calculate the subscription income for the year. **[3]**

**4.** Five members each paid 400 for life membership during the year. The club spreads life membership over 8 years. Calculate the income for the year and the liability at the year end. **[3]**

**5.** A club bar had opening inventory of 3,000, purchases of 14,000 and sales of 18,000, earning a gross profit margin of 30%. A count at the year end showed inventory of 3,700. Calculate the inventory lost. **[4]**

**6.** Explain why equipment purchased is not shown in the income and expenditure account. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Millbrook Chess Club prepares accounts to 31 December 2026. At 1 January 2026 it had equipment 6,500, refreshment inventory 400, subscriptions in arrears 150 and bank 2,950. It owed refreshment suppliers 320, and members had paid subscriptions of 60 in advance.

The receipts and payments for the year were:

|---|---:|---|---:|
| Subscriptions | 7,800 | Refreshment purchases | 2,400 |
| Refreshment sales | 3,900 | Hall hire | 2,600 |
| Tournament entry fees | 1,200 | Tournament prizes | 900 |
| Donations | 600 | Equipment | 1,500 |
| | | Insurance | 480 |
| | | Secretary's expenses | 350 |

Other information at 31 December 2026:

- subscriptions in arrears 210; subscriptions in advance 90
- refreshment suppliers owed 380; refreshment inventory 520
- hall hire owing 200; insurance paid in advance 120
- equipment is depreciated at 20% of its year-end cost

**(a)** Calculate the accumulated fund at 1 January 2026. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the closing bank balance and the subscription income for the year. **[4]**

**(c)** Prepare the refreshments trading account for the year. **[4]**

**(d)** Prepare the income and expenditure account for the year ended 31 December 2026. **[9]**

**(e)** Prepare the statement of financial position at 31 December 2026. **[8]**

**8.** Explain **two** reasons why members of a club may find an income and expenditure account more useful than a receipts and payments account. **[4]**

**9.** A club treasurer finds cash of 150 in the till at the end of the day. Cash at the start was 400, receipts recorded were 2,100 and payments recorded were 2,300. Calculate the cash missing and explain how it should be treated in the accounts. **[3]**

**10.** Explain why life membership fees are not all credited to income in the year they are received. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two differences: the receipts and payments account is a summary of cash and bank, but the income and expenditure account uses the accruals concept [1]; the first includes capital items, the second does not [1]; the first shows the closing cash balance, the second shows a surplus or deficit [1]; the second adjusts for accruals, prepayments and depreciation, the first does not [1].

**2.** The net assets are the **accumulated fund** [1]; a profit in a year is called a **surplus** [1].

**3.** 5,400 + 200 + 80 − 120 − 150 [2] = **5,410** [1].

**4.** Total fees 5 × 400 = 2,000 [1]; annual income 2,000 ÷ 8 = **250** [1]; liability 2,000 − 250 = **1,750** [1].

**5.** Gross profit 18,000 × 30% = 5,400 and cost of sales 12,600 [1]; goods available 3,000 + 14,000 = 17,000 [1]; expected closing inventory 17,000 − 12,600 = 4,400 [1]; loss 4,400 − 3,700 = **700** [1].

**6.** It is capital expenditure, which gives benefit over several years [1]; only the depreciation for the year is charged to the income and expenditure account [1].

**7. (a)** Assets 6,500 + 400 + 150 + 2,950 = 10,000 [1]; liabilities 320 + 60 = 380 [1]; accumulated fund **9,620** [1].

**(b)** Receipts 7,800 + 3,900 + 1,200 + 600 = 13,500; payments 8,230; closing bank 2,950 + 13,500 − 8,230 = **8,220** [2]. Subscriptions: 7,800 + 210 + 60 − 150 − 90 [1] = **7,830** [1].

**(c)** Refreshments trading account

Purchases 2,460 [2]; cost of sales 2,340 [1]; profit **1,560** [1].

**8.** Any two reasons with a development: it shows whether the club made a surplus or deficit [1], so members can judge whether subscriptions cover the running costs [1]; it includes accruals and prepayments [1], so costs and income are matched to the year [1]; it excludes capital items and includes depreciation [1], so the effect of using assets is shown [1].

**9.** Expected cash 400 + 2,100 − 2,300 = 200 [1]; missing cash 200 − 150 = **50** [1]; it is shown as an expense (loss of cash) in the income and expenditure account, unless it can be recovered [1].

**10.** The fee pays for services provided over many years [1]; under the accruals concept income is matched to the period in which the service is given, so the fee is spread over the expected years [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using subscriptions received as the income for the year (Questions 3 and 7).
- Treating arrears as a liability or advance payments as an asset.
- Charging the equipment purchase to the income and expenditure account (Question 7).
- Forgetting to adjust purchases for opening and closing payables.
- Calling the result "profit" or "capital" instead of surplus and accumulated fund.

## Approaching these questions

Begin with the accumulated fund, then build the subscription account and the activity accounts. Use the receipts and payments information as the starting point for each adjustment. Write the income and expenditure account line by line, and check that net assets equal the accumulated fund at the end.
