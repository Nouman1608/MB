## Questions

### Section A: short answer

**1.** Identify two ways a business can make its production more sustainable. **[2]**

**2.** Define the term **recycling**. **[2]**

**3.** Outline the difference between reusing and recycling. **[2]**

**4.** State two advantages to a business of becoming more sustainable. **[2]**

**5.** A brewery changes to returnable glass bottles, which cost $0.08 more per bottle to handle than single-use bottles. It sells 25,000 bottles a year. Calculate the extra yearly cost. **[2]**

**6.** Identify the method of sustainable production used in each example.

**(a)** A laundry installs solar panels to power its washing machines. **[1]**

**(b)** A café gives a discount to customers who bring their own cup. **[1]**

**(c)** A phone maker designs a phone whose battery can be replaced so the phone lasts longer. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Ridley Bakery has two proposals to make its production more sustainable.

| Proposal | Description | Cost | Yearly saving |
|---|---|---|---|
| X | Equipment that captures heat from the ovens to warm water | $18,000 | $4,500 |
| Y | Ordering software that predicts demand, so less unsold bread is thrown away | $2,400 | $3,200 |

**(a)** Calculate how many years it takes for the yearly savings to equal the cost of each proposal. **[2]**

**(b)** Ridley Bakery can afford only one proposal this year. Consider the advantages and disadvantages of each proposal. Justify which one it should choose. **[6]**

**8.** Explain two ways a bus company could make its service more sustainable. **[4]**

**9.** Lowenna Cosmetics plans to sell its face creams in glass jars that customers bring back to be refilled, instead of plastic tubs.

**(a)** Explain one advantage to Lowenna Cosmetics of this change. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain one disadvantage to Lowenna Cosmetics of this change. **[2]**

**10.** Tamsin Tea packs its tea in plastic tins costing $0.30 each. It plans to use simple card tubes made from recycled card, costing $0.22 each. It sells 150,000 packs a year.

**(a)** Calculate the yearly change in packaging cost. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why this example shows that becoming more sustainable does not always increase costs. **[2]**

**11.** Explain why the type of customers a business has affects whether becoming more sustainable increases its profit. **[3]**

**12.** Fallow Furniture is a small firm that sells low-price tables and chairs. It is thinking of using only timber from managed forests, which costs 15% more than its current timber. Its main competitors have not made this change.

Consider whether Fallow Furniture should make the change. Justify your answer. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two from: using renewable energy; using fewer resources or less waste; reusing; recycling; developing environmentally friendly products; using environmentally friendly packaging. One mark for each of two different methods [1] [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Processing waste materials [1] so they can be made into new materials or products [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Reusing means using an item again in its existing form, such as refilling a bottle [1], whereas recycling breaks the item down into material to make something new [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Lower running costs from using less energy or material [1]; a better reputation that may attract customers [1]. Also accept: easier to meet laws; more motivated employees; possible higher prices. **[2]**

**5.** 0.08 x 25,000 [1] = **$2,000** a year [1]. **[2]**

**6.** **(a)** Using renewable energy [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** Reusing [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** Developing environmentally friendly products [1]. **[1]**

**7.** **(a)** X: 18,000 / 4,500 = **4 years** [1]. Y: 2,400 / 3,200 = **0.75 years** (9 months) [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Proposal X: it uses heat that is currently wasted, which cuts energy use for as long as the ovens run, and the saving ($4,500) is the larger of the two [1]; but it costs $18,000, which may stretch a bakery's finances, and takes four years to cover its cost [1]. Proposal Y: it costs only $2,400 and covers its cost in nine months, and less bread thrown away means fewer ingredients wasted [1]; but its saving depends on the software predicting demand accurately, and unexpected events such as bad weather may still leave bread unsold [1]. Judgement: Ridley should choose Y this year [1], because it is cheap, low-risk and quickly pays for itself, and the savings it produces could help fund Proposal X later [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* A justified choice of X can also score full marks if it argues from the larger long-term saving and the bakery's ability to afford it.

**8.** Use buses powered by electricity from renewable sources [1], which cuts the use of fossil fuels and reduces the company's fuel costs over time [1]. Plan routes and timetables to avoid running nearly empty buses [1], which uses fewer resources (fuel and drivers' time) for the same number of passengers [1]. Also accept other suitable methods, such as recycling worn tyres, with explanation. **[4]**

**9.** **(a)** Reusing the jars means Lowenna buys fewer containers [1], which can lower its packaging costs over time and may attract customers who want to avoid plastic waste [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Glass jars cost more and must be collected, cleaned and checked before refilling [1], which adds handling costs and depends on customers remembering to bring jars back [1]. **[2]**

**10.** **(a)** 0.30 - 0.22 = $0.08 saving per pack [1]; 0.08 x 150,000 = **$12,000 saving** a year [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Using less packaging, or simpler recycled packaging, can be cheaper than the original [1]; here Tamsin both reduces plastic and lowers its costs by $12,000 a year [1]. **[2]**

**11.** If customers care about the environment and can see the change, they may buy more or pay a higher price [1], so the extra cost of sustainable methods is covered and profit may rise [1]. If customers buy mainly on price, any price rise may lose sales to cheaper rivals, so profit may fall instead [1]. **[3]**

**12.** For the change: Fallow could promote its furniture as made from responsibly grown timber, attracting customers who care about the environment and giving it a difference from competitors [1]; it may also avoid future problems if rules on timber sourcing tighten [1]. Against the change: a 15% rise in timber cost is large for a low-price firm and would raise the cost of every table and chair [1]; its customers buy mainly on price and competitors have not changed, so if Fallow raises prices it may lose sales [1]. Judgement: Fallow should not switch all its timber at once [1]; it could start with one range promoted as sustainable and see whether customers will pay more, before changing everything [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling a refill or return scheme "recycling".
- Giving factory examples for a service business.
- Claiming sustainability always cuts costs, or always raises them.
- Dividing the saving by the cost instead of the cost by the saving.
- Ignoring the case: budget, competitors and the type of customers decide the answer.
