Quick recall for section 3.4 Financial documents of Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Business (4BS1), Issue 2, February 2026, points 3.4.1 and 3.4.2. Worked readings of both statements are in the [financial documents study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-financial-documents/); questions are in the [financial documents practice set](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-financial-documents-practice/). Figures below are invented, in dollars.

## 1. The specification list

- **3.4.1 Statements of comprehensive income:** main features (sales, cost of sales, gross profit, expenses, operating profit); use in decision making; the nature of profit and its importance. You will not construct one.
- **3.4.2 Statements of financial position:** main features (current and non-current assets, current and non-current liabilities, capital employed); interpret one. You will not construct one.

## 2. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Statement of comprehensive income | Shows revenue, costs and profit over a period |
| Statement of financial position | Shows assets, liabilities and how the business is financed on one date |
| Revenue (sales) | Value of goods or services sold in the period |
| Cost of sales | Direct cost of the goods or services sold |
| Gross profit | Revenue − cost of sales |
| Other operating expenses | Running costs not in cost of sales, such as rent and administration wages |
| Operating profit | Gross profit − other operating expenses |
| Non-current assets | Assets kept for more than a year |
| Current assets | Assets expected to become cash within a year |
| Inventory | Stock of materials, work in progress and finished goods |
| Trade receivables | Money owed to the business by customers |
| Trade payables | Money the business owes to suppliers |
| Current liabilities | Debts due within a year |
| Non-current liabilities | Debts due after more than a year |
| Equity | The owners' investment in the business, including retained profit |
| Capital employed | Long-term finance used: equity + non-current liabilities |

The exam uses IAS terms only (the specification's glossary): inventory not stock, trade receivables not debtors, trade payables not creditors, non-current assets not fixed assets.

## 3. Statement of comprehensive income: the shape

```
Sales (revenue)
− Cost of sales
= Gross profit
− Other operating expenses
= Operating profit
```

**Missing-line reminder.** Sales 185,000 and cost of sales 111,000 give gross profit = **74,000**. If expenses are 52,000, operating profit = 74,000 − 52,000 = **22,000**. If you are given gross profit and operating profit, expenses = gross profit − operating profit.

## 4. Statement of financial position: the shape

```
Non-current assets
+ Current assets
− Current liabilities
= Capital employed  (= equity + non-current liabilities)
```

**Capital employed reminder.** Non-current assets 210,000, current assets 64,000, current liabilities 38,000: capital employed = 210,000 + 64,000 − 38,000 = **236,000**. If non-current liabilities are 90,000, equity = 236,000 − 90,000 = **146,000**.

## 5. Classifying items quickly

| Item | Classification |
|---|---|
| Delivery van | Non-current asset |
| Factory or shop property | Non-current asset |
| Inventory | Current asset |
| Trade receivables | Current asset |
| Cash | Current asset |
| Trade payables | Current liability |
| Bank overdraft | Current liability |
| Loan repayable within 12 months | Current liability |
| Loan repayable after 12 months | Non-current liability |

The 12-month rule decides it: within a year means current.

## 6. What each document is used for

| User | Statement of comprehensive income | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|
| Owners and managers | Is profit growing? Which costs are rising? Is pricing right? | What do we own? How much is borrowed? |
| Lenders | Can profit cover interest? | Are there assets to act as security? How much is already borrowed? |
| Investors | Is the return worth the risk? | How is the business financed? |
| Suppliers | Is the business trading profitably? | Can it pay what it owes soon? |

## 7. Why profit matters (and its limits)

- Reward for owners' risk.
- Source of retained profit for growth.
- Signal of success to lenders and investors.
- Measure of performance over time.
- Limit: profit is not cash, and the statement is historical.

## 8. Exam traps

- Constructing a full statement when the question only asks you to interpret one.
- Calling the statement of financial position a summary of a year: it is a single date.
- Putting an overdraft or trade payables under non-current liabilities.
- Treating inventory as cash. It must be sold first.
- Saying "profit went up" when only sales went up.
- Using older terms (stock, debtors) instead of the IAS terms in the paper.
- Saying a large figure for non-current assets means the business has lots of cash.

## 9. Answer frames

- **Explain** one use of a statement of comprehensive income: managers compare expenses with last year → find which costs have risen → decide where to cut.
- **Analyse** a statement of financial position: pick a feature (low cash, high loan) → what it means → the risk or decision it leads to.
- **Evaluate** using the documents: what they show, what they leave out (future demand, cash timing, non-financial factors), judgement.

## Quick self-test

1. State the purpose of a statement of comprehensive income.
2. Sales are 96,000 and cost of sales is 57,600. Calculate gross profit.
3. Gross profit is 41,000 and operating profit is 9,500. Calculate other operating expenses.
4. Give the IAS term for "debtors".
5. Classify a bank loan repayable in five years.
6. Give two examples of current assets.
7. Non-current assets are 150,000, current assets 40,000 and current liabilities 25,000. Calculate capital employed.
8. Why is a statement of financial position called a snapshot?
9. Give one reason why profit is important to a business.
10. Give one use of the statement of financial position to a lender.
11. Sales rose but operating profit fell. Give two possible reasons.
12. Give one limitation of using financial documents to make decisions.

### Answers

1. To show the revenue, costs and profit of a business over a period.
2. 96,000 − 57,600 = 38,400.
3. 41,000 − 9,500 = 31,500.
4. Trade receivables.
5. Non-current liability.
6. Any two of: inventory, trade receivables, cash.
7. 150,000 + 40,000 − 25,000 = 165,000.
8. It shows assets and liabilities on one particular date, not over a period.
9. Any one of: rewards owners; provides retained profit for growth; attracts investors and lenders; measures performance.
10. To see what assets could be used as security, or how much the business already owes.
11. Cost of sales rose faster than sales; other operating expenses rose.
12. They show past results, not future conditions, and do not show non-financial factors such as staff morale or customer satisfaction.
