Condensed notes on section **3.1.2 Forms of business and stakeholders** of AQA AS Business (7137), specification Version 1.1 (November 2025), for first teaching from September 2026. Full explanations and worked examples are in the [forms of business and stakeholders study guide](/resources/aqa-as-level-business-7137-forms-of-business-and-stakeholders-aqa-aslevel-business/); exam-style questions are in the [forms of business and stakeholders practice set](/resources/aqa-as-level-business-7137-forms-of-business-and-stakeholders-aqa-aslevel-business-practice/).

## What the specification lists

- **Forms:** sole trader; Ltd; plc; co-operative (worker, consumer, producer); social enterprise.
- **Comparison issues:** control; objectives; sources of finance; distribution of profits; unlimited and limited liability; reasons to buy shares; share price influences and the significance of changes.
- **Calculations:** market capitalisation; dividends per share; dividend yield.
- **Stakeholders:** internal (employees, owners/shareholders); external (customers, suppliers, other creditors, community, government); impact of business activity; ethical responsibilities.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sole trader | One owner, unlimited liability |
| Private limited company (Ltd) | Shares not offered to the general public; limited liability |
| Public limited company (plc) | Shares can be offered to the public and traded; limited liability |
| Worker co-operative | Owned and run by its employees |
| Consumer co-operative | Owned by its customer members |
| Producer co-operative | Producers combine to buy, process or market together |
| Social enterprise | Trades mainly to achieve a social or environmental aim, reinvesting most surplus |
| Limited liability | Owners can lose only what they invested |
| Unlimited liability | Owner personally responsible for all business debts |
| Stakeholder | Individual or group with an interest in, or affected by, a business |
| Internal stakeholder | Within the business: employees, owners/shareholders |
| External stakeholder | Outside the business: customers, suppliers, other creditors, community, government |

## Comparison grid (learn by issue)

| Issue | Sole trader | Ltd | plc | Co-operative | Social enterprise |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Control | Owner | Small group of shareholders | Many shareholders; directors | Members, one vote each | Guided by social aim |
| Objectives | Owner's choice | Growth, control | Profit, dividends, share price | Members' benefit | Social aim |
| Finance | Savings, loans | Private shares, loans | Public shares, loans | Members, loans | Trading, grants, social investors |
| Profits go to | Owner | Shareholders | Shareholders | Members, often by use or work | Mostly reinvested |
| Liability | Unlimited | Limited | Limited | Usually limited if registered | Usually limited if incorporated |

## Co-operatives in one line each

- **Worker:** staff own it, share decisions and surplus; motivation high, decisions slower.
- **Consumer:** customers own it; surplus returned to members or used to cut prices.
- **Producer:** independent producers gain bargaining power and economies of scale.
- One member one vote: protects control, deters outside investors.

## Shares

**Reasons to buy:** dividends; capital growth; voting say; personal or ethical reasons.

**Influences on price:** results and forecasts; dividends; takeover rumours; industry news; economy and interest rates; confidence.

**Significance of changes:** shareholder wealth; ease of raising new share capital; takeover risk; pressure on directors. Not a direct change in the company's cash.

## Formulae (specification annex)

```
Market capitalisation = number of issued shares x current share price
Dividend per share    = total dividend paid / number of ordinary shares in issue
Dividend yield (%)    = dividend per share (pence) x 100 / share price (pence)
```

Interpretation: if the price falls and the dividend is unchanged, the yield rises. A very high yield can mean investors doubt the dividend will last.

## Stakeholder analysis method

1. Identify the decision.
2. Pick the two or three stakeholders most affected.
3. Explain the impact on each (gain or loss, and why).
4. Identify the conflict between them.
5. Suggest how the business might reduce harm, or which group it is likely to prioritise and why.

**Ethical responsibilities:** fair treatment of staff; honesty with customers; paying suppliers and creditors on time; limiting harm to the community; paying taxes properly.

## Exam traps

- Owners/shareholders are **internal** stakeholders in this specification.
- Use pence for both parts of the dividend yield.
- Market capitalisation uses the **current** price.
- Co-operatives come in three types: name the right one.
- A higher yield is not automatically better.

## Self-test

1. Name the three types of co-operative and say who owns each.
2. Why might outside investors avoid buying into a co-operative?
3. How are a social enterprise's profits mainly used?
4. A plc has 6 million shares priced at 450p. Calculate its market capitalisation.
5. It pays total dividends of £540,000. Calculate the dividend per share.
6. Calculate the dividend yield.
7. The share price falls to 360p, with the same dividend. Calculate the new yield.
8. Give two reasons why someone buys shares.
9. Classify as internal or external: employees, suppliers, shareholders, the local council.
10. Give one ethical responsibility a business has to its suppliers.
11. A business closes a factory. Identify two stakeholders likely to be harmed and one likely to gain.
12. Why does a share price fall not directly affect the company's cash?

### Answers

1. Worker co-operative: its employees. Consumer co-operative: its customers. Producer co-operative: the independent producers who join it.
2. Each member usually has one vote, so buying more shares does not buy more control, and returns may be shared by use rather than capital.
3. Mainly reinvested in its social or environmental aim.
4. 6,000,000 × £4.50 = **£27 million**.
5. £540,000 ÷ 6,000,000 = **9p**.
6. 9 × 100 ÷ 450 = **2%**.
7. 9 × 100 ÷ 360 = **2.5%**.
8. Any two: dividends; capital growth; voting influence; personal or ethical reasons.
9. Employees: internal. Suppliers: external. Shareholders: internal. Local council: external (government).
10. Paying them on time (or dealing honestly, paying a fair price).
11. Harmed: employees, local community (or suppliers to that factory). Gain: shareholders, if costs fall.
12. Shares traded on the stock market pass between investors, so the money does not go to or from the company.
