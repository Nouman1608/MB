## Section A

**1.** State **one** property of a liquid that is the same as a solid and **one** property of a liquid that is the same as a gas. **[2]**

**2.** Name the change of state in each case.

**(a)** Water vapour forms droplets on a cold window. **[1]**

**(b)** Molten candle wax turns solid. **[1]**

**(c)** A puddle disappears on a warm day without reaching 100 °C. **[1]**

**3.** Describe the separation and motion of the particles in a gas. **[2]**

**4.** The table gives melting and boiling points.

| Substance | Melting point / °C | Boiling point / °C |
|---|---|---|
| P | −101 | −34 |
| R | 44 | 280 |
| T | −95 | 69 |

**(a)** State which substance is a gas at 20 °C. **[1]**

**(b)** State which substance is a solid at 20 °C. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the temperature range over which T is a liquid. **[1]**

**5.** A student writes: "When ice melts, the water particles get bigger." Correct the student's statement. **[2]**

**6.** State what happens to the volume of a fixed mass of gas when the pressure on it is increased at constant temperature. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Explain, in terms of particles, why a solid has a fixed shape but a liquid does not. **[3]**

**8.** A solid is heated at a steady rate from 20 °C until it has all boiled. Its heating curve has two flat sections, at 80 °C and at 218 °C.

**(a)** State the melting point of the substance. **[1]**

**(b)** State the physical state, or states, present at 80 °C while the temperature is constant. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the temperature does not rise during the flat section at 218 °C. **[2]**

**(d)** Suggest why the flat section at 218 °C lasts longer than the flat section at 80 °C. **[2]**

**9.** A sample of a molten substance is allowed to cool. Its temperature is recorded every minute.

| Time / min | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature / °C | 95 | 84 | 75 | 68 | 68 | 68 | 61 | 55 |

**(a)** State the freezing point of the substance. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe the movement of the particles at 7 minutes compared with 0 minutes. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the temperature stays constant between 3 and 5 minutes, even though the substance is still losing heat to the surroundings. **[2]**

**10.** Compare boiling with evaporation. Give **three** differences. **[3]**

**11.** A sealed gas syringe contains 40 cm³ of nitrogen at room temperature. The syringe is placed in iced water and the plunger moves in. Explain this observation in terms of particles. **[3]**

**12.** Describe and explain, in terms of kinetic particle theory, what happens to the particles of a substance as it is cooled from a gas at 150 °C to a solid at −10 °C. The substance condenses at 100 °C and freezes at 0 °C. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** Same as a solid: fixed volume / cannot easily be compressed / particles close together [1]. Same as a gas: can flow / takes the shape of its container / particles can move from place to place [1]. **[2]**

**2. (a)** Condensing (condensation) [1]

**(b)** Freezing [1]

**(c)** Evaporating (evaporation) [1]

**[3]**

**3.** Separation: particles are **far apart** compared with their size [1]. Motion: particles move **quickly and randomly** in all directions [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** P (20 °C is above its boiling point of −34 °C) [1]

**(b)** R (20 °C is below its melting point of 44 °C) [1]

**(c)** 69 − (−95) = **164 °C** [1]

**[3]**

*Examiner insight:* in (c), subtracting a negative number is where most errors occur; 69 − 95 = −26 is a common wrong answer.
**5.** The particles stay the **same size** [1]. They gain energy, vibrate more and leave their fixed positions, moving past each other (the arrangement changes, not the particles) [1]. **[2]**

**6.** The volume **decreases** [1]. **[1]**

**7.** In a solid the particles are held in **fixed positions** by strong forces [1] and can only **vibrate** [1]. In a liquid the particles can **move past each other**, so the liquid flows and takes the shape of its container [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** 80 °C [1]

**(b)** Solid and liquid [1]

**(c)** The energy supplied is used to **overcome the forces between particles** [1] rather than to increase the **kinetic energy** of the particles [1].

**(d)** Boiling separates the particles **completely**, which needs **more energy** than melting [1]; at a steady heating rate, supplying more energy takes **longer** [1].

**[6]**

**9. (a)** 68 °C [1]

**(b)** At 7 minutes the particles only **vibrate** [1] about **fixed positions** (a solid), whereas at 0 minutes they **moved past each other** as a liquid, and faster [1].

**(c)** As the particles move into fixed positions, forces of attraction form and **energy is released** [1]. This release **balances the heat lost** to the surroundings until all of the substance has frozen [1].

**[5]**

**10.** Any three [1] [1] [1]:

- boiling happens at a **fixed temperature** (the boiling point); evaporation happens at **any temperature** below it
- boiling happens **throughout** the liquid; evaporation happens only at the **surface**
- boiling produces **bubbles** in the liquid; evaporation does not
- boiling is rapid; evaporation is slow

**[3]**

**11.** The nitrogen particles **lose kinetic energy** and move more slowly [1]. They collide with the plunger **less often and less hard** [1], so the pressure inside drops and atmospheric pressure pushes the plunger in until the pressures balance; the **volume decreases** [1]. **[3]**

**12.** One mark per point, up to 5:

- from 150 °C to 100 °C (gas): particles far apart and moving randomly; they **slow down** as they lose energy [1]
- at 100 °C the gas **condenses**: particles are pulled close together by forces of attraction; temperature stays constant as energy is given out [1]
- from 100 °C to 0 °C (liquid): particles close together, **sliding past each other**, moving more slowly as the temperature falls [1]
- at 0 °C the liquid **freezes**: particles settle into **fixed positions** in a **regular arrangement** and energy is given out [1]
- below 0 °C (solid): particles only **vibrate**, and vibrate less as the temperature falls [1]

**[5]**

*Examiner insight:* answers that list the states without mentioning energy, forces or motion are only describing, and cannot reach full marks on an "explain" question.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying the particles themselves expand, shrink or melt.
- Drawing or describing liquid particles as far apart.
- Writing that temperature keeps rising during a change of state.
- Giving the freezing point as a temperature below the plateau.
- In gas questions, not saying which variable is held constant, or not linking speed to collisions with the walls.
