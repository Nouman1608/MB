## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Direct cost | Traced to the goods made: direct material and direct labour |
| Indirect cost | Factory cost not traced to units; a factory overhead |
| Direct material | Cost of raw materials consumed |
| Direct labour | Wages of production workers |
| Prime cost | Direct material + direct labour |
| Factory overheads | Indirect wages, factory rent, power, insurance, depreciation of factory machinery |
| Work in progress (WIP) | Part-finished goods at the year end |
| Factory cost of production | Prime cost + factory overheads + opening WIP - closing WIP |

## Formulas

- Cost of raw materials consumed = opening raw materials + purchases + carriage inwards - returns outwards - closing raw materials
- Prime cost = cost of raw materials consumed + direct labour
- Factory cost of production = prime cost + factory overheads + opening WIP - closing WIP
- Cost of sales (manufacturer) = opening finished goods + factory cost of production - closing finished goods
- Gross profit = net revenue - cost of sales

## Method in steps

1. Sort every cost into direct material, direct labour, factory overhead, or not a factory cost.
2. Adjust each factory expense for accruals, prepayments and depreciation before listing it.
3. Calculate raw materials consumed, then prime cost.
4. Add factory overheads. Add opening WIP, deduct closing WIP. This gives factory cost of production.
5. Carry that figure into cost of sales in the statement of profit or loss. Add office and selling expenses below gross profit.
6. In the statement of financial position, list raw materials, WIP and finished goods separately in current assets.

## Statement layout to remember

```
Manufacturing account for the year ended ...
Opening raw materials + Purchases + Carriage inwards - Returns outwards
  - Closing raw materials = Cost of raw materials consumed
+ Direct labour                          = PRIME COST
+ Factory overheads (adjusted)
+ Opening WIP - Closing WIP              = FACTORY COST OF PRODUCTION

Statement of profit or loss
Net revenue
- (Opening finished goods + Factory cost of production - Closing finished goods)
= Gross profit
+ Other income (adjusted) - Office and selling expenses = Profit for the year
```

## Small worked reminder

Oakhaven Preserves, a sole trader making jam, year ended 30 September 2028. Raw materials 5,400 opening and 6,100 closing; purchases 31,800; carriage inwards 600; direct wages 24,000; indirect wages 7,500; factory heating 2,600 paid and 300 owing; factory insurance 1,800 paid, 450 of it prepaid; factory equipment cost 40,000, depreciated 10% on cost; hand tools revalued from 1,500 to 1,300 after 400 of purchases; WIP 2,200 opening and 1,700 closing; finished goods 3,900 opening and 4,600 closing; revenue 96,000.

| Step | Working | $ |
|---|---|---|
| Raw materials consumed | 5,400 + 31,800 + 600 - 6,100 | 31,700 |
| Prime cost | 31,700 + 24,000 | 55,700 |
| Heating | 2,600 + 300 | 2,900 |
| Insurance | 1,800 - 450 | 1,350 |
| Equipment depreciation | 10% x 40,000 | 4,000 |
| Hand tools depreciation | 1,500 + 400 - 1,300 | 600 |
| Factory overheads | 7,500 + 2,900 + 1,350 + 4,000 + 600 | 16,350 |
| Factory cost of production | 55,700 + 16,350 + 2,200 - 1,700 | 72,550 |
| Cost of sales | 3,900 + 72,550 - 4,600 | 71,850 |
| Gross profit | 96,000 - 71,850 | 24,150 |

Inventories in the statement of financial position: 6,100 + 1,700 + 4,600 = 12,400.

## Adjustments from 5.1

| Adjustment | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Straight line | (Cost - residual value) / useful life |
| Reducing balance | Fixed % of net book value each year |
| Revaluation | Opening value + additions - closing value |
| Factory expense accrued or prepaid | Adjust in the manufacturing account |
| Office expense accrued or prepaid | Adjust in the statement of profit or loss |
| Income accrued or in advance | Adjust other income; current asset or current liability |
| Irrecoverable debts; allowance | Statement of profit or loss; receivables net of allowance |
| Goods taken by owner | Reduce cost of sales; increase drawings |
| Private expense paid by business | Remove from expenses; increase drawings |

Depreciation of factory machinery is a factory overhead. Depreciation of office equipment or vehicles used for delivery belongs in the statement of profit or loss.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Prime cost or factory cost of production?** Prime cost stops before overheads and WIP. Factory cost of production includes both.
- **Purchases or materials consumed?** Prime cost uses materials consumed, after inventory adjustment.
- **Opening or closing WIP?** Opening is added, closing is deducted.
- **Raw materials, WIP or finished goods?** Each is a separate inventory. Raw materials and WIP are used in the manufacturing account; finished goods in cost of sales.
- **Indirect labour or direct labour?** Supervisors and cleaners are indirect.
- **Factory or office?** Only factory costs enter the manufacturing account.

## Quick self-test

1. Classify as direct material, direct labour, factory overhead or not a factory cost: (a) leather for shoes; (b) wages of a storekeeper in the factory; (c) salary of an office clerk.
2. Opening raw materials 3,000, purchases 20,000, returns outwards 500, carriage inwards 400, closing raw materials 2,900. Find raw materials consumed.
3. Raw materials consumed are 20,000 and direct labour is 15,500. Find prime cost.
4. Prime cost 35,500, factory overheads 9,800, opening WIP 1,200, closing WIP 1,700. Find factory cost of production.
5. Closing WIP was understated by 800. State the effect on factory cost of production and gross profit.
6. Factory power paid is 6,400 and 700 is owing at the year end. State the charge for the year.
7. A machine has a net book value of 48,000 and is depreciated at 25% on reducing balance. Find the charge and the new net book value.
8. Factory tools were valued at 2,000, then 600 more were bought, and they are now valued at 1,700. Find the depreciation.
9. In which statement is depreciation of a delivery van charged?
10. Name the three inventories shown in the statement of financial position of a manufacturer.
11. Opening finished goods 8,200, factory cost of production 44,800, closing finished goods 9,000. Find cost of sales.
12. The owner takes finished goods costing 900 for home use. State the effect on gross profit and drawings.

### Answers

1. (a) Direct material. (b) Factory overhead (indirect labour). (c) Not a factory cost.
2. 3,000 + 20,000 - 500 + 400 - 2,900 = **20,000**.
3. 20,000 + 15,500 = **35,500**.
4. 35,500 + 9,800 + 1,200 - 1,700 = **44,800**.
5. Factory cost of production is overstated by 800, so cost of sales is too high and gross profit is understated by **800**.
6. 6,400 + 700 = **7,100**.
7. 25% x 48,000 = **12,000**; new net book value 48,000 - 12,000 = **36,000**.
8. 2,000 + 600 - 1,700 = **900**.
9. The statement of profit or loss.
10. Raw materials, work in progress and finished goods.
11. 8,200 + 44,800 - 9,000 = **44,000**.
12. Cost of sales falls by 900, so gross profit rises by 900; drawings rise by 900.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using purchases of raw materials in place of cost of raw materials consumed.
- Reversing the WIP adjustment.
- Treating all wages as direct labour.
- Charging factory machinery depreciation in the statement of profit or loss.
- Not adjusting the factory expense for the accrual or prepayment before totalling.
- Starting cost of sales with purchases when the business makes its goods.
- Giving one inventory figure in the statement of financial position.
- Writing a total with no label, so a marker cannot see which line it is.
- Omitting the $ sign or the year-end date from a heading.
