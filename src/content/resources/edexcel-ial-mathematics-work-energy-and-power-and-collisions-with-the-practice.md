## Questions

**1.** A sledge of mass 25 kg starts from rest on horizontal ground. It is pulled 12 m by a rope that makes an angle of 30° with the horizontal, with constant tension 80 N. The total resistance to motion is a constant 30 N. Find the speed of the sledge after 12 m. **[4]**

**2.** A cyclist and bicycle have total mass 80 kg. The cyclist works at a constant rate of 250 W. The resistance to motion is a constant 20 N.

**(a)** Find the steady speed on level ground. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the steady speed up a road inclined at α to the horizontal, where sin α = 1/25. **[3]**

**3.** A particle of mass 0.6 kg is launched at 7 m s⁻¹ up a plane that is inclined at 30° to the horizontal, moving along the steepest direction.

**(a)** The plane is smooth. How far up the plane does it travel before it is instantaneously at rest? **[3]**
**(b)** The plane is now rough, with a constant frictional resistance of 2 N. Find the new distance it travels up the plane. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the speed of the particle when it returns to the point of projection in the situation in part (b). **[2]**

**4.** Particle A of mass 3 kg moves at 7 m s⁻¹ along a straight line on a smooth horizontal surface. Particle B of mass 2 kg moves along the same line at 2 m s⁻¹ in the opposite direction. They collide directly. The coefficient of restitution is 0.5.

**(a)** Find the velocities of A and B after the collision, stating their directions. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the magnitude of the impulse exerted on B. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the kinetic energy lost. **[2]**

**5.** Particle P of mass m, moving at speed u on a smooth horizontal surface, collides directly with particle Q of mass 3m at rest. After the collision P moves in the opposite direction with speed u/4.

**(a)** Show that the coefficient of restitution is 2/3. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the fraction of the initial kinetic energy lost in the collision. **[3]**

**6.** A ball is dropped from rest at a height of 3.6 m above a horizontal floor. The coefficient of restitution between ball and floor is 0.7.

**(a)** Find the speed of the ball just before it first hits the floor. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the greatest height reached after the first bounce. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the greatest height reached after the second bounce. **[2]**

**7.** Particles A, B and C, of masses 3 kg, 1 kg and 2 kg, lie in that order on a smooth horizontal line. B and C are at rest. A moves towards B at 4 m s⁻¹. The coefficient of restitution is 0.5 for every collision.

**(a)** Find the speeds of A and B after the first collision. **[3]**
**(b)** B then collides with C. Find the speeds of B and C after this collision. **[3]**
**(c)** Show that A and B collide again, and find their speeds afterwards. **[4]**
**(d)** Explain whether B and C collide again. **[1]**

**8.** Particle A of mass m moves at 10 m s⁻¹ on a smooth horizontal surface and hits particle B of mass 4m, at rest between A and a fixed vertical wall. The coefficient of restitution between A and B is 0.5. The coefficient of restitution between B and the wall is e.

**(a)** Find the velocities of A and B after their first collision. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the range of values of e for which A and B collide a second time. **[4]**

**9.** A van of mass 1500 kg climbs a hill whose steepest direction makes an angle α with the horizontal, where sin α = 1/12. It moves at constant speed 18 m s⁻¹ with the engine working at 36 kW.

**(a)** Find the constant resistance to the motion of the van. **[4]**
**(b)** The engine is switched off. Assuming the resistance is unchanged, find the further distance the van travels up the road before stopping. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the work done by the engine in the 2 minutes before it was switched off. **[1]**

**10.** Particle A of mass 0.3 kg has velocity (2i − 4j) m s⁻¹ and particle B of mass 0.5 kg has velocity (−3i + j) m s⁻¹. They collide, and immediately afterwards A has velocity (−i + 2j) m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Find the velocity of B after the collision. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the magnitude of the impulse exerted by B on A. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Work done by the tension = 80 cos 30° × 12 = 831.4 J [1]. Work done against resistance = 30 × 12 = 360 J [1]. Work-energy: 831.4 − 360 = ½ × 25 × v² [1], so v² = 2 × 471.4 / 25 and **v = 6.14 m s⁻¹** [1].
*Examiner insight:* A force at an angle does work through its component along the motion, so a bare 80 × 12 is likely to lose credit.

**2. (a)** Driving force = resistance = 20 N [1]. v = 250 / 20 = **12.5 m s⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** Driving force = 20 + 80 × 9.8 × (1/25) [1] = 51.36 N [1]. v = 250 / 51.36 = **4.87 m s⁻¹** [1].
*Examiner insight:* On a hill the driving force must also overcome the weight component along the slope; finding it from power alone and then forgetting that term gives the level-ground speed.

**3. (a)** KE at start = ½ × 0.6 × 49 = 14.7 J [1]. Gain in PE = 0.6 × 9.8 × d sin 30° = 2.94d [1]. 14.7 = 2.94d, so **d = 5 m** [1].
**(b)** 14.7 = 2d + 2.94d [1], so **d = 2.98 m** [1].
**(c)** Down the slope: ½ × 0.6 × v² = 2.94d − 2d = 0.94 × 2.976 [1], so **v = 3.05 m s⁻¹** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Carry the unrounded distance from part (b) into part (c), since a rounded value can change the third significant figure.

**4. (a)** A's direction positive. Momentum: 3vA + 2vB = 21 − 4 = 17 [1]. Restitution: vB − vA = 0.5 × 9 = 4.5 [1]. So 5vA + 9 = 17 [1]. **vA = 1.6 m s⁻¹ and vB = 6.1 m s⁻¹, both in A's original direction** [1].
**(b)** Impulse on B = 2[6.1 − (−2)] [1] = **16.2 N s** [1].
**(c)** KE before = ½(3)(49) + ½(2)(4) = 77.5 J, KE after = ½(3)(2.56) + ½(2)(37.21) = 41.05 J [1]. Loss = **36.45 J** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Opposite directions mean the speed of approach is the sum of the speeds; a sign diagram makes this clear.

**5. (a)** Take P's original direction as positive. Momentum: mu = −m(u/4) + 3mvQ [1]. So vQ = 5u/12 [1]. Restitution: vQ − (−u/4) = eu [1]. So e = 5/12 + 1/4 = 8/12 = **2/3** [1].
**(b)** KE after = ½m(u/4)² + ½(3m)(5u/12)² [1] = mu²/32 + 25mu²/96 = 7mu²/24 [1]. Initial KE = mu²/2 = 12mu²/24, so the fraction lost is 5/12 [1].
*Examiner insight:* A "show that" needs both equations written in full, with exact fractions rather than decimals.

**6. (a)** v² = 2 × 9.8 × 3.6 = 70.56 [1], so **v = 8.4 m s⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** Rebound speed = 0.7 × 8.4 = 5.88 m s⁻¹ [1]. Height = 5.88² / (2 × 9.8) = **1.76 m** (1.764 m) [1].
**(c)** Speed after the second impact = 0.7 × 5.88 × 0.7 = 0.49 × 8.4 = 4.116 m s⁻¹ [1]. Height = 4.116² / 19.6 = **0.864 m** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Noting that each bounce multiplies the height by e² saves time.

**7. (a)** Momentum: 3(4) = 3vA + vB [1]. Restitution: vB − vA = 0.5 × 4 = 2 [1]. So 4vA + 2 = 12: **vA = 2.5 m s⁻¹ and vB = 4.5 m s⁻¹**, both towards C [1].
**(b)** Momentum: 1(4.5) = vB′ + 2vC [1]. Restitution: vC − vB′ = 0.5 × 4.5 = 2.25 [1]. So 3vC = 6.75: **vC = 2.25 m s⁻¹ and B is brought to rest** [1].
**(c)** B is at rest and A approaches at 2.5 m s⁻¹, so they collide again [1]. Momentum: 3(2.5) = 3a + b [1]. Restitution: b − a = 0.5 × 2.5 = 1.25 [1]. So **a = 1.5625 ≈ 1.56 m s⁻¹ and b = 2.8125 ≈ 2.81 m s⁻¹**, both towards C [1].
**(d)** B (2.81 m s⁻¹) is behind and faster than C (2.25 m s⁻¹), so they collide again [1].
*Examiner insight:* List all three velocities after each collision, and give a reason in words for any claim about a further collision.

**8. (a)** Take towards the wall as positive. Momentum: 10m = ma + 4mb [1]. Restitution: b − a = 0.5 × 10 = 5 [1]. Then 5a + 20 = 10: **a = −2 m s⁻¹ (A rebounds) and b = 3 m s⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** B returns from the wall at 3e m s⁻¹ [1], A moves away at 2 m s⁻¹ [1]. B must be faster: 3e > 2 [1]. So **2/3 < e ≤ 1** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Equal speeds mean no further meeting, so the inequality is strict; the upper bound comes from e ≤ 1.

**9. (a)** Driving force F = P/v = 36 000 / 18 = 2000 N [1]. Weight component down the slope = 1500 × 9.8 / 12 = 1225 N [1]. Constant speed: 2000 = R + 1225 [1]. **R = 775 N** [1].
**(b)** KE = ½ × 1500 × 18² = 243 000 J [1]. Work against resistance and weight component: (775 + 1225)d = 243 000 [1], so **d = 121.5 m** [1].
**(c)** Work = Pt = 36 000 × 120 = **4.32 × 10⁶ J** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Convert kilowatts and minutes to watts and seconds before substituting, and give units with final answers.

**10. (a)** Momentum before = 0.3(2i − 4j) + 0.5(−3i + j) = −0.9i − 0.7j [1]. After: 0.3(−i + 2j) + 0.5vB = −0.9i − 0.7j [1]. So **vB = (−1.2i − 2.6j) m s⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** I = 0.3[(−i + 2j) − (2i − 4j)] [1] = −0.9i + 1.8j [1]. |I| = √(0.81 + 3.24) = √4.05 = **2.01 N s** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Keep the i and j equations separate and write each vector in full, so that a slip in one component is easy to see.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting the length of the slope into mgh in place of the vertical rise.
- Leaving the weight component out of the driving-force equation on an inclined road.
- Writing the restitution equation with approach and separation the wrong way round.
- Using the momentum equation for a collision with a wall.
- Not checking each particle's direction after a collision before deciding on another.
- Rounding too early or omitting units.
