Money figures are in dollars. All businesses and people are invented, and any legal minimum wage is made up for practice.

## Questions

**1.** Define the term person specification. **[2]**

**2.** Identify two methods a business could use to select a new employee. **[2]**

**3.** **(a)** State two contents of an employment contract. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one benefit of an employment contract to an employer. **[2]**

**4.** Ardent Lane Bakery needs a new manager on an annual salary of 30,000 dollars. An employment agency would charge a fee of 12% of the first year's salary. Advertising online would cost 450 dollars.
**(a)** Calculate the agency fee. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how much more the agency would cost than online advertising. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain one reason why the bakery might still choose the agency. **[2]**

**5.** Outline two advantages of internal recruitment. **[4]**

**6.** Oriel Park Dental pays a receptionist 7.80 dollars an hour for a 35-hour week. The legal minimum wage is 8.20 dollars an hour.
**(a)** Calculate the weekly shortfall in the receptionist's pay. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the annual shortfall, assuming 52 weeks. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how a legal minimum wage may affect the employer. **[3]**

**7.** Explain one effect of health and safety controls on employers and one effect on employees. **[4]**

**8.** **(a)** Explain why training is important to a business. **[3]**
**(b)** Identify one advantage and one disadvantage of off-the-job training. **[2]**

**9.** Marden Toys hires 10 new packers. Option A is off-the-job training: a course costing 220 dollars per person, with each packer away for 2 days. A packer's output is worth 95 dollars a day. Option B is on-the-job training: an experienced packer supervises each new packer for 3 days, and the lost output from the supervisor costs 60 dollars per trainee per day, and damaged stock during this training is expected to cost 400 dollars. Option A is expected to cause no damaged stock.
**(a)** Calculate the total cost of each option, including lost output. **[5]**
**(b)** Justify which option Marden Toys should choose. **[4]**

**10.** Yarrow Bay Hotel needs a head receptionist. The person specification asks for three years of hotel front-desk experience, two foreign languages and experience of leading a small team. Bashir is an internal candidate. He has worked as a night receptionist at the hotel for five years, speaks one foreign language and has never led a team. Imogen applied from outside. She has four years at a larger hotel, speaks three languages and led a team of five. Imogen wants a salary 2,000 dollars a year higher than Bashir would be paid, and she would need induction training. Recommend and justify who the hotel should employ. **[8]**

**11.** Kiran was dismissed from Marden Toys after three years with no reason given and no warning. Explain how legal controls over unfair dismissal affect:
**(a)** employers **[3]**
**(b)** employees. **[3]**

**12.** Explain one way in which controls over discrimination affect how a business recruits and selects employees. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** A document that lists the qualifications, skills, experience and personal qualities [1] wanted in the ideal person for a particular job [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition is stronger when it separates the person specification from the job description. Saying only that it "describes the job" describes the wrong document.

**2.** Any two from: curriculum vitae (CV) or resumé [1]; application form, references, testing or assessment centre, interview [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* An identify question asks only for the names. Adding explanation takes time and gains nothing extra here.

**3.** **(a)** Any two from: duties and responsibilities [1]; working hours [1]; holiday entitlement; sick pay. **[2]**
**(b)** The duties and working hours are agreed in writing [1], so there are fewer disputes about what the employee must do [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A benefit explanation reads best as a short chain of reasoning. A bare statement such as "it is legal" does not say why it helps the employer.

**4.** **(a)** 12% × 30,000 [1] = 3,600 dollars [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 3,600 − 450 = **3,150 dollars** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** The agency already knows the labour market and may find suitable candidates quickly [1], which saves the owner's time for running the bakery [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the percentage step in a calculation, then state the units. A reason in part (c) should link to the situation, not merely say "it is better".

**5.** Cheaper than external recruitment, because there is no agency fee or large advertisement [1] and the money saved can be used elsewhere in the business [1]. The person already knows the business [1], so they need less induction and can start work faster [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* For outline questions, each advantage needs a small development. A list of four one-word advantages shows knowledge but has little else to credit.

**6.** **(a)** Pay actually received = 35 × 7.80 = 273 dollars [1]. Pay required = 35 × 8.20 = 287 dollars [1]. Shortfall = 287 − 273 = **14 dollars a week** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** 14 × 52 = **728 dollars** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** The business must pay higher wages to low-paid staff [1], so its wage costs rise (here by 14 dollars a week for this one receptionist) [1]. It may have to raise prices, cut hours or accept lower profit [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show both the actual and the required pay before subtracting. In part (c), tie the effect to the employer, not to the receptionist.

**7.** Employers: must spend money on safety equipment, training or inspections, which raises costs [1], but accidents and absence may fall [1]. Employees: work in safer conditions [1], so there is less risk of injury or illness [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The question names two groups, so each needs its own effect. Putting both in one sentence about "everyone" leaves one group unanswered.

**8.** **(a)** Training improves workers' skills [1], so productivity and quality rise [1], which can lower costs per unit or increase customer satisfaction [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Advantage: expert teachers or a recognised qualification [1]. Disadvantage: costly, or workers are away from their jobs [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* An "explain why" answer is strongest as a chain from training to skills to a business result. Stopping at "workers learn more" shows only the first link.

**9.** **(a)** Option A: course cost = 10 × 220 = 2,200 dollars [1]. Lost output = 10 × 2 × 95 = 1,900 dollars [1]. Total = **4,100 dollars** [1]. Option B: supervisor cost = 10 × 3 × 60 = 1,800 dollars [1]. Add damaged stock: 1,800 + 400 = **2,200 dollars** [1]. **[5]**
**(b)** Option B is cheaper by 4,100 − 2,200 = 1,900 dollars [1], and packing methods are specific to Marden Toys, so the training is directly relevant [1]. However, supervisors lose output and trainees may make mistakes or pick up bad habits [1]. Overall I would choose Option B for these packers, because it saves 1,900 dollars and teaches the firm's own methods, though off-the-job training could suit a skill that needs an outside expert [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A justify answer should use the figures from part (a), not just repeat them. A choice stated without a weighed alternative leaves the answer one-sided.

**10.** Recommend Imogen [1]. She meets the leadership requirement, having led five people, which Bashir has not [1]. She speaks three languages against his one, so she exceeds the language requirement and he falls short [1]. Both have enough front-desk experience, so that point does not separate them [1]. The drawbacks are the extra 2,000 dollars a year in salary and the cost of induction [1]. Bashir knows the hotel and would be motivated by promotion, and rejecting him may disappoint him and other staff [1]. The hotel could reduce this by explaining the decision and offering Bashir leadership training for a future post [1]. Overall, Imogen is the better fit for the specification, and her skills are likely to be worth the extra cost [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A recommend and justify answer is built from evidence matched to the specification. A choice supported by "she has more experience" does not use the data in the question.

**11.** **(a)** The employer must have a fair reason and follow a proper process before dismissing someone [1]. Failing to do so may lead to compensation claims or legal costs [1], so the employer needs to keep records and train managers [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Employees have more job security [1], because they cannot be sacked without a fair reason [1]. This may make them feel more confident and more motivated [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Describe the control in general terms and apply it to the case. Quoting a named law adds nothing, and a wrong detail may cost credibility.

**12.** The business must treat applicants fairly regardless of, for example, sex, race or age [1], so it should base selection on the person specification and not on personal characteristics [1]. This widens the pool of talent it can choose from, and breaking the rule risks legal action and damage to its reputation [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A strong answer links the control to a stage of recruitment, such as the advertisement or the interview, and says what the employer does differently.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining a person specification as a list of duties, which is the job description.
- Giving a list in an outline question with no development of each point.
- Forgetting to show both the actual and required pay in a minimum wage calculation.
- Leaving out units in cost calculations, or leaving out lost output from training costs.
- Choosing an option in a justify question without using the figures or weighing the alternative.
- Writing one-sided effects of legal controls, covering only employers or only employees.
- Recommending a candidate without matching each point to the person specification.
- Quoting named laws and details from memory instead of describing the control in general terms.
