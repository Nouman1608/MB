Revision notes for the focused study **Climate and innovation** in IB DP History (first assessment 2028), for SL and HL. Two examples, both to be studied: **Norse exploration (about 982 to 1020)** and **the Aztec Empire (about 1428 to 1469)**. See the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-climate-and-innovation/) for detail and the [practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-climate-and-innovation-practice/) for a paper 1 style set.

## Paper 1 in one box

| Question | Task | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| First | Explain how the **content** of sources A and B can be used to answer the inquiry question | 6 |
| Second | Analyse how the **context** of source C influences how it can be used | 6 |
| Third | Examine how the **perspectives** in all the sources can be used | 12 |

The paper is 1 hour 15 minutes and 24 marks. You are not asked to recall the grounding content directly. You use it to read the sources with understanding.

## The three inquiry questions

1. What factors prompted innovation?
2. How did climate conditions shape innovation?
3. What innovations took place?

## Norse exploration (about 982 to 1020)

**Factors prompting innovation**

- Population pressure and shortage of good farmland in Iceland, with feuds over land.
- Shipbuilding: clinker-built longships and broad cargo ships (knarrs) able to cross the open Atlantic.
- Erik the Red: outlawed from Iceland, explored Greenland from about 982, led settlers there in about 985 to 986.
- Leif Erikson: Erik's son, who sailed west in about 1000 and reached Vinland, according to the sagas.

**Climate**

- Medieval Warm Period: generally warmer North Atlantic conditions, roughly the tenth to thirteenth centuries.
- New sea routes: Iceland to Greenland, then on to Baffin Island, Labrador and Newfoundland.
- Greenland: sheltered south-western fjords with meadows for cattle and sheep; marginal farming.
- Vinland: timber and pasture described in the sagas.

**Innovations**

- Settlements in Greenland (Eastern and Western Settlements) and Vinland, including L'Anse aux Meadows in Newfoundland (found by Helge and Anne Stine Ingstad in 1960).
- Food production: farming combined with hunting seals and other animals.
- Relations with the Skrælingjar (the Norse term for the Indigenous peoples): trade and violence.

**Source notes.** The sagas were written in Iceland in the thirteenth century, long after the events. Archaeology can support or challenge them. Indigenous voices are mostly missing from the written sources.

## The Aztec Empire (about 1428 to 1469)

**Factors prompting innovation**

- The Triple Alliance of Tenochtitlan, Texcoco and Tlacopan (1428) after the defeat of Azcapotzalco.
- Imperial reforms, including the Flower Wars, ritual wars to capture prisoners and show power.
- Moctezuma I (about 1440 to 1469): expansion and consolidation.

**Climate and water**

- The Valley of Mexico: a high basin of shallow lakes with Tenochtitlan on an island in Lake Texcoco.
- Water management: dikes, causeways and an aqueduct from Chapultepec.
- Drought and frost leading to the famine of One Rabbit (1454), followed by a state response and more conquest.

**Innovations**

- Urban planning in Tenochtitlan: causeways, canals, temples, markets.
- Food production: chinampas, raised garden plots in lake beds that gave several harvests a year.
- Expansion and annexation of Totonacapan on the Gulf coast, giving access to cotton, cacao and other goods.

**Source notes.** The Codex Mendoza was produced in the 1540s under Spanish rule. Spanish accounts were written by conquerors after 1521.

## Comparing the two examples

| Point | Norse | Aztec |
|---|---|---|
| Main pressure | Land shortage, status, feuds | Imperial rivalry, population, water and food |
| Role of climate | Warm conditions opened sea routes | Water, drought and famine shaped state policy |
| Key innovation | New settlements and long sea voyages | Chinampas, city planning, tribute system |
| Outcome | Greenland and Vinland settlements did not last | Empire grew until the Spanish arrival in 1519 |
| Source problem | Late sagas, thin archaeology | Colonial-era codices and conquerors' accounts |

## Skills checklist

**Question 1 (content).** Pick two or three specific points from each source. For each, say how it helps answer the inquiry question. Use both sources. If you use only one, the maximum is 3 marks.

**Question 2 (context).** Use the origin, date, author, audience and purpose of source C. Link each to how far and in what way the source can be used. Do not simply list context.

**Question 3 (perspectives).** For each source, state the perspective. Compare similarities and differences. Show how together they give a fuller answer. Reference all three sources for the top band. Only two sources caps the mark at 9.

## Worked example: a question 2 paragraph

Inquiry question: How did climate conditions shape innovation?

Source C (constructed for practice) is a fourteenth-century Icelandic annal entry, written by a church clerk, noting that "the sea ice lay long that year and ships could not reach the western settlement".

"The context shapes the use of this source in two ways. First, it was written by a church clerk in Iceland, some distance from Greenland, so it is a report of what he had heard rather than an eyewitness account of conditions there. This limits how far it shows what the environment was like in Greenland itself. Second, it dates from the fourteenth century, when the climate in the North Atlantic was cooling, so it can be used to show how sea ice affected the routes that earlier voyagers had used, and so how climate could limit as well as enable exploration."

This links two items of context (author's position and date) to use, which is what the markband rewards.

## Linking to concepts

- **Cause and consequence:** distinguish conditions (warm climate, land shortage) from actors (Erik, Moctezuma I).
- **Continuity and change:** new settlements and farming methods, but continuing traditional beliefs and practices.
- **Perspectives:** Norse, Indigenous, Mexica and Spanish viewpoints.
- **Significance:** why L'Anse aux Meadows and the chinampas matter to historians.

## Common mistakes

- Writing the history of the voyages or the empire instead of using sources.
- Describing context without saying how it affects use.
- Treating climate as the only cause.
- Using only one source for question 1, or only two for question 3.
- Taking sagas or Spanish accounts as neutral.

## Self-test

1. Name the two examples in this focused study.
2. What was the Triple Alliance?
3. Why is Erik the Red said to have gone to Greenland?
4. What are chinampas?
5. What was the famine of One Rabbit?
6. Who found L'Anse aux Meadows, and when?
7. Why must the sagas be used with care?

**Answers.**

1. Norse exploration (about 982 to 1020) and the Aztec Empire (about 1428 to 1469).
2. The 1428 alliance of Tenochtitlan, Texcoco and Tlacopan that formed the core of the Aztec Empire.
3. He was outlawed from Iceland, and explored Greenland from about 982; he then recruited settlers.
4. Raised garden plots built in shallow lake beds, giving several harvests a year.
5. A famine in 1454 following drought and frost in the Valley of Mexico.
6. Helge and Anne Stine Ingstad, in 1960.
7. They were written down in the thirteenth century, long after the events, and shaped as stories.
