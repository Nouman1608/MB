## The specification points

1. Explain what is meant by 'promotion'.
2. Evaluate promotion strategies: above the line, below the line, branding, personal selling, internet, social media, drip marketing and viral marketing.
3. Explain what is meant by advertising elasticity of demand.
4. Calculate and interpret advertising elasticity of demand.
5. Evaluate the usefulness of advertising elasticity calculations to a business.
6. Evaluate the impact and importance of promotion to a business and its stakeholders.
7. Recommend and justify a promotional mix as part of a marketing strategy.

## Core recall

### Promotion and the promotional mix

Promotion communicates with customers to inform, persuade and remind. The promotional mix is the combination of methods used.

### Methods

| Method | Key point | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Above the line | Paid mass media (TV, radio, press) | Wide reach; brand image | Costly; hard to measure |
| Below the line | Discounts, coupons, samples, displays, loyalty cards | Targeted; quick; measurable | Short-lived; cuts margin |
| Branding | Identity through name, logo, packaging | Loyalty; premium price | Slow, costly |
| Personal selling | Staff talk to buyers | Complex, high-value sales | Costly per contact |
| Internet | Website, search, email, display | Targeted; measurable | Crowded; ignored or blocked |
| Social media | Posts, influencers | Low cost per reach; dialogue | Unpredictable; negative comments spread |
| Drip marketing | A series of regular messages | Keeps brand in mind; cheap | Irritation if too frequent |
| Viral marketing | Content shared by viewers | Cheap, trusted reach | Cannot be controlled or guaranteed |

### Cost per sale

- Sales = people reached × purchase rate.
- Cost per sale = cost ÷ sales.
- But also weigh the objective: awareness and brand value are not captured by cost per sale.

### Advertising elasticity of demand (AED)

AED = % change in quantity demanded ÷ % change in advertising spend.

| AED | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Above 1 | Advertising elastic: sales rise by a larger percentage than spend |
| Between 0 and 1 | Sales respond, but less than proportionately |
| About 0 | Advertising has little effect |
| Negative | Sales fell as spending rose |

Diminishing returns: AED usually falls as spending rises. Stop spending when extra contribution is less than extra cost.

Usefulness: guides budgets and comparison of media. Limits: other factors change sales (price, season, rivals); effects have a lag; one campaign's figure may not apply to the next.

### Viral growth

If each viewer shares with more than 1 person on average, each round grows; below 1, it fades. Cannot be guaranteed.

### Importance and stakeholders

- Supports sales, brand image and loyalty; launches or revives products.
- Customers (information, risk of misleading claims), employees (sales), shareholders (revenue against cost), agencies and media (income), regulators (control claims).

### Choosing a mix

1. Objective. 2. Target market. 3. Budget and cost per sale. 4. Product type. 5. Methods that support each other. 6. Risk and measurement.

## Quick worked reminder

Sunbeam Juice (invented): TV £70,000, 2,000,000 reached, 0.4% buy, 8,000 sales, £8.75 each. Social media £30,000, 400,000 reached, 1.5%, 6,000 sales, £5.00 each. Coupons £20,000, 25,000 shoppers, 12%, 3,000 sales, £6.67 each. Email £6,000, 50,000, 2.4%, 1,200 sales, £5.00 each.

AED: spend £40,000 to £50,000 (+25%); sales 80,000 to 88,000 (+10%); AED 0.40. Contribution £6 a carton: extra £48,000 less £10,000 gives +£38,000. Next step AED 0.17 and +£8,000; then AED 0.07 and −£4,000, so stop.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Promotion | Communication with customers to inform, persuade and remind |
| Above the line | Paid-for mass media advertising |
| Below the line | Promotions directly controlled by the business, such as coupons |
| Drip marketing | A planned series of regular messages to customers |
| Viral marketing | Content spread by customers sharing it |
| Advertising elasticity of demand | Responsiveness of sales to a change in advertising spend |
| Diminishing returns | Each extra unit of spending adds less than the one before |
| Promotional mix | The combination of promotion methods used |

## Exam traps

- Use percentage changes in AED.
- Cost per sale is not the only test; link to the objective.
- Below the line is not the same as digital.
- Say how the effect of advertising is separated from other causes.
- Viral marketing is not free or guaranteed.

## Command words

- **Explain:** define the method and say what it does for the business.
- **Calculate:** show the percentage change in spend and in sales before dividing for AED.
- **Interpret:** say whether sales respond strongly or weakly and whether extra spending pays.
- **Evaluate:** weigh strength against weakness for the product and audience, then judge.
- **Recommend and justify:** name the mix, tie it to the objective and budget, and state a risk.

## Self-test

1. Define promotion.
2. Give two examples of above the line promotion.
3. Give two examples of below the line promotion.
4. What is drip marketing?
5. Spend rises 10% and sales rise 4%. Find the AED.
6. Is advertising elastic in question 5? Explain.
7. A campaign costs £8,000 and wins 1,600 sales. Find the cost per sale.
8. Each extra £5,000 spent adds 500 sales at £8 contribution. Is it worthwhile?
9. Give one limit of AED.
10. Why might viral marketing fail?
11. Give one stakeholder affected by misleading advertising.
12. Why combine television and coupons?

### Answers

1. Communicating with customers to inform, persuade and remind them to buy.
2. Any two: television; radio; national press; cinema; posters.
3. Any two: discounts; coupons; free samples; displays; loyalty cards; competitions.
4. A planned series of regular messages to the same customers over time.
5. 4 ÷ 10 = **0.4**.
6. No: it is below 1, so sales rise by a smaller percentage than spending.
7. 8,000 ÷ 1,600 = **£5**.
8. 500 × £8 = £4,000, which is less than £5,000, so **no** (net −£1,000).
9. Any one: other factors affect sales; lag; may not apply to other campaigns.
10. People may not share it, or share it with fewer than one person each on average.
11. Customers, who may be misled; also regulators or rivals.
12. Television builds awareness and coupons encourage trial, so they support each other.
