## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define the term *species*. **[2]**

**2.** The lion belongs to the genus *Panthera* and has the species name *leo*. Write the binomial correctly. **[1]**

**3.** Which of the following lists the taxa in the correct order, from largest to smallest?

A: domain, kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species

B: kingdom, domain, phylum, order, class, family, genus, species

C: domain, phylum, kingdom, class, family, order, species, genus

D: species, genus, family, order, class, phylum, kingdom, domain **[1]**

**4.** A mule is the offspring of a female horse and a male donkey. Mules are sterile. Explain why this supports the classification of horses and donkeys as different species. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**5.** The table shows the percentage of precipitate formed when antibodies against a protein from species S were mixed with the same protein from four other species. The precipitate formed with the protein of species S itself was 100 %.

| Species | Precipitate / % |
|---|---|
| T | 96 |
| U | 78 |
| V | 55 |
| W | 31 |

**(a)** Explain how the antibodies were produced. **[2]**

**(b)** Use the data to put the four species in order of their relationship to species S, starting with the closest. Explain your answer. **[3]**

**(c)** Suggest one limitation of using this single protein to classify the species. **[1]**

**6.** The same short stretch of a gene was sequenced in four species. The sequences are shown.

- Species A: TTAGGCATCGGATCCAAGCT
- Species B: TTAGGCATCGGATCGAAGCT
- Species C: TTAGCCATTGGATCGAAACT
- Species D: TAAGCCTTTGGTTCGAAACG

**(a)** Compare each of the species B, C and D with species A. Calculate the percentage of bases that are the same. **[3]**

**(b)** Use your answers to part (a) to suggest which species is most closely related to species A and which is least closely related. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why comparing DNA sequences is more reliable than comparing the appearance of organisms. **[3]**

**7.** The amino acid sequence of a protein of 100 amino acids was compared in three species with the sequence in humans. Species 1 differed at 2 positions, species 2 at 9 positions and species 3 at 24 positions.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of the amino acids that are identical to the human protein in each species. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why a smaller number of differences suggests a more recent common ancestor with humans. **[2]**

**8.** Two populations of a plant species grow on opposite sides of a mountain. They differ in flower colour. In a garden, plants from the two populations produce fertile seeds when crossed. In the wild, the populations do not interbreed because they are 40 km apart.

**(a)** Use the definition of a species to decide whether the two populations are one species or two. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why this example shows the difficulty of defining species. **[2]**

**9.** Give two reasons why the definition of a species based on interbreeding cannot be applied to all organisms. **[2]**

**10.** Explain the advantages of the binomial system over the use of common names. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The largest group of organisms [1] capable of interbreeding and producing fertile offspring [1]. **[2]**

**2.** *Panthera leo* (genus with a capital, species in lower case, italicised or underlined) [1]. **[1]**

**3.** **A** [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Horses and donkeys can mate and produce offspring [1], but the offspring are sterile, so they do not meet the definition of fertile offspring and the horse and donkey are separate species [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** The protein from species S is injected into an animal such as a rabbit [1], which makes antibodies against it [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** T, U, V, W [1]. The more precipitate that forms, the more similar the protein is to that of S [1], so a greater precipitate means a closer relationship [1]. **[3]**

**(c)** One protein is a small part of the genome, so the result might not reflect the relationship between the whole organisms [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** B differs from A at 1 position: 19 of 20 match, 95 % [1]. C differs at 4 positions: 16 of 20, 80 % [1]. D differs at 8 positions: 12 of 20, 60 % [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** B is the most closely related and D is the least closely related [1]. The more similar the sequence, the fewer mutations have accumulated, so the more recent the common ancestor [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** Similar appearance can result from living in similar environments, so unrelated organisms can look alike [1]. DNA sequences can be compared exactly and objectively with numbers [1]. A long DNA sequence provides many characters, so the conclusion is based on more data [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** Species 1: 98 % [1]. Species 2: 91 %. Species 3: 76 % [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** The sequences of two species change by mutation after the species diverge from a common ancestor [1]. Fewer differences means less time has passed, so the common ancestor is more recent [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** They are one species [1], because they can interbreed and produce fertile offspring in the garden [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** In the wild they do not interbreed, so the result depends on whether the test is done in captivity or in the wild [1]. The populations are separated by distance and differ in appearance, so they might be classed as two species if only appearance and natural behaviour were used [1]. **[2]**

**9.** Any two from: some organisms reproduce only asexually, so interbreeding does not apply [1]; fossils cannot be tested for interbreeding [1]; some different species produce fertile hybrids [1]; it is impractical to carry out breeding tests on most organisms [1]. **[2]**

**10.** The binomial is the same in every country, whatever the language, so scientists can communicate without confusion [1]. A common name can refer to different species in different places [1]. A species can have several common names, but only one binomial [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving "fertile" out of the definition of a species.
- Writing binomials without the capital or italics.
- Not calculating a percentage similarity when the data allow it.
- Relying on appearance when the question asks about molecular evidence.
- Giving the conclusion without saying why fewer differences means a closer relationship.
