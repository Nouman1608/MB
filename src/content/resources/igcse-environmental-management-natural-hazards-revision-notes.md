Topic 6 of Cambridge IGCSE Environmental Management (0680) covers four hazards: earthquakes and
volcanoes, tropical cyclones, flooding and drought. For each you must describe where and why it happens,
its impacts on people and the environment, and strategies for managing it before, during and after the
event, with their benefits and limitations. Volcanoes and floods also bring opportunities, which the
syllabus asks you to explain.

For full explanations see the
[natural hazards study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-environmental-management-natural-hazards-study-guide/);
then try the [natural hazards practice questions](/resources/igcse-environmental-management-natural-hazards-practice/).

## Earthquakes and volcanoes (6.1)

**Structure of the Earth**: crust (thin, solid outer layer) → mantle (hot, slowly moving rock) → core.

**Continents (7)**: Africa, Antarctica, Asia, Europe, North America, Oceania, South America.

**Distribution**: most earthquakes and volcanoes lie along plate boundaries, such as the ring around the
Pacific Ocean; some volcanoes form over **hotspots** away from boundaries, such as Hawaii.

**Convection currents** in the mantle move the plates:

| Boundary | Movement | Hazards |
|---|---|---|
| Divergent (constructive) | Plates move apart | Volcanic activity and earthquakes |
| Convergent (destructive) | One plate subducts beneath another | Earthquakes and a line of volcanoes |
| Conservative | Plates slide past each other | Earthquakes only |

**Earthquake features**: sudden release of energy; **focus** (point underground where it starts);
**epicentre** (point on the surface directly above); magnitude measured on the **Moment Magnitude Scale**.

**Eruption features**: magma rises and reaches the surface as lava; ash; gases (water vapour, carbon
dioxide, sulfur dioxide); acid rain; volcanic bombs; pyroclastic flows (fast, hot clouds of gas and rock).

**Impacts**: damage to buildings and infrastructure; loss of crops, livestock and habitats; evacuation;
fire; tsunamis; landslides; contaminated drinking water; water-related disease; financial losses; human
health.

**Opportunities from volcanoes**: fertile soils; mineral extraction; building materials; geothermal power;
tourism.

## Tropical cyclones (6.2)

- Called storms, hurricanes or typhoons depending on region.
- Form between **5° and 30°** north or south of the Equator, over oceans at least **27 °C** and at least
  **60 m** deep.
- Impacts: buildings and infrastructure damaged; crops, livestock and habitats lost; evacuation; flooding;
  landslides; contaminated water and water-related disease; financial losses; health.

## Flooding (6.3)

**Causes**: land relief (low-lying land); saturated, compacted or eroded soil; deforestation, agricultural
practices and urbanisation (less infiltration, more run-off); climate change (sea-level rise, more extreme
weather); storm surges and tsunamis.

**Opportunities**: nutrient-rich silt deposited on farmland; recharge of surface water and ground water
stores.

## Drought (6.4)

**Drought** = a period of dry weather that is longer or more severe than normal.
- Causes: lack of rain; climate change (higher global temperatures, more extreme weather).
- Impacts: water sources dry up; loss of crops, livestock and habitats; soil erosion; desertification;
  death of organisms; famine; more wildfires; poorer air quality; financial losses; human health.
- Strategies: monitoring and warning; emergency water supplies; water conservation; increasing supply
  (dams and reservoirs, aquifers and wells, desalination, rainwater harvesting); international aid.

## How are hazard impacts managed? (before, during, after)

| Stage | Strategy | Benefit | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before | Monitoring and warning (seismometers, satellites, rainfall gauges) | Time to evacuate | Earthquakes cannot be predicted precisely; warnings must reach remote areas |
| Before | Land use zoning (earthquakes, floods) | Keeps homes off the most dangerous land | Hard to enforce where land is scarce |
| Before | Building structure (cross-bracing, deep foundations, raised floors, cyclone-proof roofs) | Fewer collapses and deaths | Expensive to build or retrofit |
| Before | Disaster preparation (plans, drills, supplies, rescue teams) | People know what to do | Needs funding and regular practice |
| During | Evacuation and shelters | Saves lives | People may refuse to leave homes and livestock |
| After | Rebuilding damaged areas | Restores homes and services | Slow and costly; may rebuild in the same risk zone |
| After | International aid | Quick supplies, money and expertise | May not reach those most in need; can create dependency |

## Worked example: can a tropical cyclone form here?

A source gives data for three ocean areas. Test each against all three conditions.

| Area | Latitude | Sea temperature | Depth | Cyclone possible? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 15° N | 28 °C | 3 000 m | Yes - all three conditions met |
| B | 2° S | 29 °C | 4 000 m | No - too close to the Equator (below 5°) |
| C | 22° S | 24 °C | 2 500 m | No - water cooler than 27 °C |

Exam tip: say which condition fails, quoting the figure, rather than just "no".

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- Confusing focus and epicentre: the focus is underground; the epicentre is on the surface.
- Saying volcanoes occur at conservative boundaries.
- Forgetting the three cyclone thresholds: 5-30° latitude, 27 °C, 60 m depth.
- Giving "evacuation" as a "before" strategy for earthquakes, which give little or no warning.
- Listing impacts without saying whether they affect people or the environment when asked for both.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why are some volcanoes far from plate boundaries?**
They form over hotspots, where hot mantle material
rises beneath the middle of a plate.

**What makes a strong "discuss" answer for hazard management?**
Name the strategy and the stage
(before, during or after), give a benefit, give a limitation and finish with a judgement.

**Can floods be good for people?**
Yes. The syllabus names two opportunities: nutrient-rich silt on
farmland and recharge of surface and ground water stores.
