## Section A

**1.** Define the active layer. **[1]**

**2.** State the three states whose continental shelf submissions overlap on the Lomonosov Ridge. **[1]**

**3.** Outline what a coastal state must show to claim an extended continental shelf beyond 200 nautical miles. **[2]**

**4.** State two environmental issues associated with desalination. **[2]**

**5.** Outline why the Fosen wind farms in Norway became controversial. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A pipeline's supports were designed for an active layer of up to 1.5 m. The active layer is now 1.1 m and deepening by 2.5 cm a year (invented figures).

**(a)** Calculate how many years remain before the design limit is reached. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of years if the rate of deepening rises to 4 cm a year. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest two ways engineers could adapt to this change. **[2]**

**7.** Explain why the economic value of the Arctic seabed may increase in future. **[3]**

**8.** Explain two factors that make some desert cities more resilient than others to future water shortages. **[4]**

**9.** Explain why adaptive management may be more appropriate than fixed plans in extreme environments. **[3]**

**10.** Explain one advantage and one disadvantage of building large solar power plants in a hot desert. **[4]**

## Section C

**11.** Discuss two contrasting possible futures for a hot arid or cold environment you have studied. **[10]**

**12.** "Low-carbon development will bring more benefits than costs to extreme environments." To what extent do you agree? **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** The upper layer of ground above permafrost that thaws each summer and refreezes in winter [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Russia, Denmark (through Greenland) and Canada [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Scientific evidence, submitted to the UN Commission on the Limits of the Continental Shelf [1], that the seabed is a natural prolongation of its land territory [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two [1] each: high energy use, often from fossil fuels; discharge of concentrated brine that raises local seawater salinity; harm to marine life at intakes; chemicals used in treatment. **[2]**

**5.** The turbines were built on land used for reindeer herding by Sami communities [1], and in 2021 Norway's Supreme Court ruled the licences violated the herders' rights to practise their culture, yet the turbines kept operating, leading to protests [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Margin = 1.5 - 1.1 = 0.4 m = 40 cm [1]. Years = 40 / 2.5 = **16 years** [1].

**(b)** 40 / 4 = **10 years** [1].

**(c)** Any two [1] each: install thermosyphons to keep the ground frozen; use adjustable supports that can be re-levelled; drive deeper piles; insulate or shade the ground; monitor ground temperature and movement. **[5]**

**7.** Retreating sea ice makes the seabed more accessible to ships and drilling platforms for longer each year [1]. Demand for oil, gas and minerals, including metals used in batteries, may make previously uneconomic deposits worth exploiting [1]. Clear legal rights from extended continental shelf claims would give states and companies confidence to invest [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Diverse water sources, such as a mix of desalination, recycled wastewater, groundwater and imports [1], mean that failure of one source does not cause a crisis [1]. Effective demand management, such as pricing, metering and leak repair [1], slows the growth of demand, so supply lasts longer and shortages are less likely [1]. (Accept: wealth to invest; good governance.) **[4]**

**9.** Conditions such as active-layer depth, glacier size and rainfall are changing, so plans based on past averages may fail [1]. Adaptive management monitors conditions and adjusts actions as evidence improves [1], so it can respond to unexpected rates of change rather than being locked into a fixed design [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Advantage: strong, reliable sunshine and cheap open land give high electricity output [1], which can cut fossil fuel imports and emissions and may be exported to neighbouring regions [1]. Disadvantage: panels and mirrors need water for cleaning and, in some designs, cooling [1], which competes with scarce local supplies; large plants can also occupy land used by herders [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Indicative content (for example, the Arctic):

- **Drivers:** warming, sea-ice loss, permafrost thaw, demand for resources, geopolitics [1].
- **Cooperative scenario:** shipping and extraction under strict rules through the Arctic Council and UNCLOS [1]; indigenous participation and revenue sharing [1]; investment in spill response and adaptation [1].
- **Competitive scenario:** unresolved seabed claims and weaker cooperation [1]; poorly regulated extraction and pollution risks [1]; indigenous communities face disruption with little say [1].
- **Deciding factors:** global emissions, relations between states, strength of indigenous rights [1].
- **Environmental outcome under both:** physical change continues because warming is already locked in [1].
- **Conclusion:** a judgement on likelihood or desirability, with reasons [1]. **[10]**

**12.** Indicative content:

- **Benefits:** solar and wind resources in deserts and windy uplands bring investment and jobs [1]; minerals such as lithium and copper earn revenue for producing countries [1]; lower global emissions slow the climate change that threatens extreme environments themselves [1].
- **Costs:** mining uses scarce water and can damage salt flats and lagoons, as around the Salar de Atacama [1]; wind farms can disrupt traditional land use, as at Fosen [1]; infrastructure fragments fragile landscapes that recover slowly [1]; benefits may go to distant companies and consumers [1].
- **Depends on:** consultation, consent and benefit-sharing with local and indigenous communities [1]; environmental regulation and monitoring [1].
- **Conclusion:** a balanced judgement, for example that the global benefits are real but the local balance is positive only where communities have rights and share in the gains [1]. **[10]**

*Examiner insight:* "To what extent" needs a clear position early. Separate global benefits from local costs, then decide whether the local balance is positive.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Presenting one future as certain.
- Treating renewable energy projects as free of local costs.
- Forgetting to convert metres to centimetres before dividing.
- Naming states in Arctic disputes without explaining the legal basis of claims.
