## Questions

**1.** A learner writes: "Area = 6 x 4 = 24 + 5 = 29 cm2. The mass is 4.5E3 g." Identify and correct four errors of mathematical language or notation. **[4]**

**2.** A taxi charges £3.20 plus £1.80 for each kilometre travelled.

**(a)** Make a table of the cost C for journeys of 1, 2, 3 and 4 km. **[1]**
**(b)** Write a rule for C in terms of the distance k km. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe the graph of C against k, naming two features and what each means. **[1]**
**(d)** Use the rule to find the distance for a fare of £30.20. **[2]**

**3.** For each task choose the most suitable form of representation (table, graph, equation or diagram) and give a reason. **[3]**

**(i)** Showing a shop's sales for each month of the year so that a rise or fall is easy to see.
**(ii)** Giving a rule so that any term of a number pattern can be found.
**(iii)** Giving a customer the exact price for each of five sizes of a product.

**4.** A learner tests 3 + 5 = 8, 7 + 9 = 16 and 11 + 13 = 24 and writes: "The sum of two consecutive odd numbers is always a multiple of 4."

**(a)** Explain why this does not show the statement is always true. **[1]**
**(b)** Write a complete, coherent and concise argument that it is always true. **[3]**

**5.** A wall is 5.4 m wide and 2.5 m high. It is to be painted with two coats. One litre of paint covers 9 m². Paint is sold in 2.5 litre cans. The wall colour is grey.

**(a)** Identify one fact that is not relevant to the calculation. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the volume of paint needed. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the number of cans to buy and explain why you round the way you do. **[2]**

**6.** In question 5 the width and height are measured to the nearest 0.1 m.

**(a)** Find the smallest and largest volume of paint the measurements allow, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(b)** Justify that buying two cans is enough. **[2]**

**7.** Water leaks from a bucket. A model for the volume V litres after t minutes is V = 30 − 0.8t. Measurements: at 5, 15 and 30 minutes the volumes are 26.1, 18.3 and 7.1 litres.

**(a)** State the values of t for which the model is valid, with a reason. **[2]**
**(b)** Compare the model with the three measurements. **[2]**
**(c)** Justify whether the model is reliable for predicting the volume at 40 minutes. **[2]**

**8.** Use the Year 1, Year 3 and Year 5 criteria.

**(a)** Which strand of Criterion C is not demonstrated in Year 1? **[1]**
**(b)** What extra quality does Year 5 ask for in lines of reasoning, compared with Year 3? **[1]**
**(c)** What change in Criterion D strand iv separates Year 3 from Year 5? **[1]**

**9.** Two companies hire bikes. Company X charges £5 plus £3 for each hour. Company Y charges £11 plus £1.50 for each hour. Both charge for every hour started.

**(a)** Write an equation for the cost of each company for h hours. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the number of hours for which the costs are equal, and the cost. **[2]**
**(c)** Complete a table for h = 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 for both companies. **[2]**
**(d)** A customer rides for 3.5 hours. Explain which company to choose and why the charging rule matters. **[2]**

**10.** A savings account holds £800 and pays 4% interest, added once a year. A model for the balance after n years is B = 800 × 1.04ⁿ.

**(a)** Find the balance after 6 years to the nearest penny. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the first whole year in which the balance exceeds £1000. **[2]**
**(c)** Justify whether the model gives a reliable balance after 18 years. **[3]**

**11.** A learner solves 5(x − 2) = 3x + 8 and writes: "5x − 10 = 3x + 8 = 2x = 18 = 9". Rewrite the solution as a complete, well-organised piece of reasoning. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** The multiplication sign x should be × [1]. The chain of equals signs joins unequal values, so write 6 × 4 = 24 and then 24 + 5 = 29 as separate lines [1]. cm2 should be cm² [1]. 4.5E3 is calculator notation and should be 4.5 × 10³ g [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the exact symbol and its correction, rather than writing "bad notation", because a reader cannot credit a vague comment.

**2. (a)** The costs are £5.00, £6.80, £8.60 and £10.40 for 1, 2, 3 and 4 km [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** C = 3.2 + 1.8k [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** The graph is a straight line with vertical intercept 3.2, the fixed charge, and gradient 1.8, the cost per kilometre [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** 3.2 + 1.8k = 30.2 [1] gives 1.8k = 27, so k = **15 km** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A graph feature is more convincing when you say what it means in context, so write "3.2 is the fixed charge" and not just "the intercept is 3.2".

**3.** (i) A graph, because a line shows the trend across the months [1]. (ii) An equation, because it gives any term by substitution [1]. (iii) A table, because it lists the exact value for each size [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Tie each form to the job it does, since a choice with no reason shows less communication.

**4. (a)** Three cases do not cover every pair of consecutive odd numbers, so they cannot show the result is always true [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Let the odd numbers be 2n − 1 and 2n + 1, where n is an integer [1]. Their sum is (2n − 1) + (2n + 1) = 4n [1]. Since 4n is 4 times an integer, the sum is a multiple of 4 [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Define the variable and finish by linking the result back to the claim.

**5. (a)** The colour of the paint (grey) is not needed for the calculation [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Area = 5.4 × 2.5 = 13.5 m², and two coats give 27 m² [1]. Paint = 27 ÷ 9 = **3 litres** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** 3 ÷ 2.5 = 1.2, so round up to **2 cans** [1], because one can (2.5 litres) is too little and cans are sold whole [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In real-life problems the rounding direction should come from the situation, and you should say so.

**6. (a)** Smallest wall: 5.35 × 2.45 = 13.1075 m², so two coats cover 26.215 m² and the paint is 26.215 ÷ 9 = 2.91 litres [1]. Largest wall: 5.45 × 2.55 = 13.8975 m², so 27.795 m² and 27.795 ÷ 9 = 3.09 litres [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Two cans give 5 litres [1]. The largest amount the measurements allow is 3.09 litres, which is less than 5, so two cans are enough [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Justify" calls for evidence, so a range of values from the measurement limits is stronger than the statement "it is about right".

**7. (a)** V cannot be negative, so 30 − 0.8t ≥ 0 gives t ≤ 37.5 [1]. The model is valid for **0 ≤ t ≤ 37.5**, since time cannot be negative [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The model gives 26, 18 and 6 litres [1]. The measurements are higher by 0.1, 0.3 and 1.1 litres, so the gap grows with time [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** At t = 40 the model gives −2 litres, which is impossible, and 40 is outside the valid domain [1]. The growing gap also suggests the real leak slows down, so the model is not reliable for large t and the real volume need not match the line [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Cite specific numbers from the data rather than a general remark that models can be wrong.

**8. (a)** Strand iii, moving between forms of representation [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Year 5 reasoning should also be concise, in addition to being complete and coherent [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** In Year 3 you explain the degree of accuracy, and in Year 5 you justify it [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote the verbs the criteria use, because the difference between years is in the verbs.

**9. (a)** C_X = 5 + 3h [1] and C_Y = 11 + 1.5h [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 5 + 3h = 11 + 1.5h gives 1.5h = 6, so h = **4 hours** [1]. Cost = 5 + 12 = **£17** for both [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** For 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 hours, X costs £11, £14, £17, £20 and £23 [1], and Y costs £14, £15.50, £17, £18.50 and £20 [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** 3.5 hours is charged as 4 started hours, so both companies cost £17 [1]. If hours were charged exactly, X would cost £15.50 and Y £16.25, so the charging rule changes the advice and the customer should choose on other grounds [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* State the context rule you used (charged per started hour) so a reader can see why your numbers differ from a pro rata calculation.

**10. (a)** 800 × 1.04⁶ [1] = **£1012.26** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** After 5 years: 800 × 1.04⁵ = 973.32, below £1000 [1]. After 6 years: £1012.26, above £1000, so the first year is **year 6** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The model gives 800 × 1.04¹⁸ = £1620.65 [1]. The model assumes the rate stays at 4% and no money is added or taken out for 18 years [1]. Rates often change over long periods, so the figure is only an estimate [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the assumption that could fail and say what it does to the answer, not only that the answer is uncertain.

**11.** Solve 5(x − 2) = 3x + 8 [1]. Expand: 5x − 10 = 3x + 8 [1]. Subtract 3x and add 10: 2x = 18, so x = 9 [1]. Check: left side 5(9 − 2) = 35, right side 3(9) + 8 = 35 [1]. Conclusion: x = **9** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* One equation per line, with each line following from the one before, shows structure better than a long chain of equals signs.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the equals sign to join steps that are not equal.
- Leaving out units, or writing calculator notation such as E3.
- Describing a graph feature without saying what it means in context.
- Treating a few examples as a proof.
- Writing "it is approximate" with no cause or size of error.
- Rounding material amounts without checking the context.
- Not stating the domain of a model.
- Not defining the variable before using it.
