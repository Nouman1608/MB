## Questions

**1.** Write a differential equation for each statement. Define any constant you introduce.

**(a)** The number N of bacteria in a culture grows at a rate proportional to N. **[1]**
**(b)** The mass m of a chemical decreases at a rate proportional to m². **[1]**
**(c)** The depth h cm of water in a pond falls at a rate proportional to h, and rain adds 3 cm per day. **[2]**

**2.** Show that y = 2 + Ce^(−3x), where C is a constant, is a solution of dy/dx = 6 − 3y. **[3]**

**3.** Solve dy/dx = (2x + 1)y, given that y = 3 when x = 0. Give y in terms of x. **[4]**

**4.** For x > 0 and y > −2, solve x dy/dx = 3(y + 2), given y = 6 when x = 2. **[5]**

**5.** Given that y > 0, solve y dy/dx = x e^(−x²), where y = 1 when x = 0. Give y in terms of x. **[5]**

**6.** Solve dy/dx = y² sin x, given that y = 1/2 when x = 0. Give y in terms of x. **[5]**

**7.** The number P of bacteria in a colony grows at a rate proportional to P. At time t = 0 hours there are 2000 bacteria. At t = 5 there are 3500. Calculate how long it takes for the colony to grow to 10 000 bacteria, to 3 significant figures. **[6]**

**8.** A metal block at 150 °C is placed in a room at 25 °C. Its temperature T °C decreases at a rate proportional to (T − 25). After 8 minutes the block is at 90 °C. Find its temperature after a total of 20 minutes. **[6]**

**9.** Water drains from a tank. The depth h metres satisfies dh/dt = −k√h, where t is in minutes and k is a positive constant. Initially h = 16. After 5 minutes h = 9.

**(a)** Find k. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the time taken for the tank to empty. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the depth after 12 minutes. **[1]**

**10.** The volume of a spherical balloon increases at a constant 40 cm³ per second. The radius is r cm after t seconds. The volume of a sphere is V = (4/3)πr³.

**(a)** Show that dr/dt = 10/(πr²). **[3]**
**(b)** When t = 0, r = 2. Find the time taken for the radius to reach 5 cm. **[4]**

**11.** A tank holds 100 litres of pure water. A salt solution of 0.5 kg per litre flows in at 4 litres per minute. The mixture is stirred well and leaves at 4 litres per minute. Let x kg be the salt in the tank after t minutes.

**(a)** Show that dx/dt = 2 − 0.04x. **[3]**
**(b)** Solve the equation to find x in terms of t. **[4]**
**(c)** Find the time for the tank to hold 25 kg of salt. **[2]**

**12.** A population P is modelled by dP/dt = P(400 − P)/2000, with P = 50 when t = 0.

**(a)** Express 1/(P(400 − P)) in partial fractions. **[2]**
**(b)** By integrating, show that P/(400 − P) = Ae^(t/5) and find the value of A. **[5]**
**(c)** Find the time at which P = 200. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** dN/dt = kN, k > 0 [1] **[1]**
**(b)** dm/dt = −km², k > 0 [1] **[1]**
**(c)** Rate of change is a rise of 3 from the rain [1] minus a fall proportional to h: dh/dt = 3 − kh, k > 0 [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A constant must be defined and its sign stated. In (c) two separate contributions need to appear in the equation.

**2.** dy/dx = −3Ce^(−3x) [1]. 6 − 3y = 6 − 6 − 3Ce^(−3x) [1] = −3Ce^(−3x), which equals dy/dx [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A "show that" question needs both sides worked out and compared. Substitute y into the right side and state that the two sides match.

**3.** ∫(1/y) dy = ∫(2x + 1) dx [1]. ln y = x² + x + c [1]. y = 3, x = 0: c = ln 3 [1]. ln y = x² + x + ln 3, so **y = 3e^(x² + x)** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Find the constant straight after integrating, then exponentiate the whole of the right side. Writing y = x² + x + ln 3 is a common slip.

**4.** ∫1/(y + 2) dy = ∫3/x dx [1]. ln(y + 2) = 3 ln x + c [1]. y + 2 = Ax³ [1]. x = 2, y = 6: 8 = 8A, A = 1 [1]. **y = x³ − 2** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Combining logs via c = ln A keeps the working short. Check the answer: at x = 2, y = 8 − 2 = 6.

**5.** y dy = x e^(−x²) dx. ∫y dy = y²/2 [1]. ∫x e^(−x²) dx = −½e^(−x²) [1]. y²/2 = −½e^(−x²) + c. x = 0, y = 1: ½ = −½ + c, c = 1 [1]. y² = 2 − e^(−x²) [1]. **y = √(2 − e^(−x²))**, positive root as y > 0 [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A reverse-chain-rule integral hides in the x side. Differentiate your answer to check it. State the root chosen when the question gives y > 0.

**6.** ∫y⁻² dy = ∫sin x dx [1]. −1/y = −cos x + c [2]. x = 0, y = ½: −2 = −1 + c, c = −1 [1]. 1/y = cos x + 1, so **y = 1/(1 + cos x)** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Take care with the sign when integrating y⁻². Each side of the integration is a separate piece of working that can be credited.

**7.** dP/dt = kP, so ln P = kt + c [1]. t = 0, P = 2000: P = 2000e^(kt) [1]. 3500 = 2000e^(5k), k = (1/5) ln 1.75 [1] = 0.112 [1]. 10 000 = 2000e^(kt): e^(kt) = 5 [1]. t = ln 5/k = **14.4 hours** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Using the exact k (or ln 1.75 over 5) gives a more reliable final answer than using a rounded 0.112. Say what t means in the context.

**8.** dT/dt = −k(T − 25). ln(T − 25) = −kt + c [1]. T − 25 = Be^(−kt) [1]. t = 0: B = 125 [1]. t = 8: 65 = 125e^(−8k), k = (1/8) ln(125/65) = 0.0817 [1]. t = 20: T = 25 + 125e^(−20k) [1] = **49.4 °C** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Apply the starting condition to T − 25, not to T. A temperature below the room temperature signals an error in the setup.

**9. (a)** h^(−1/2) dh = −k dt [1]. 2√h = −kt + c [1]. t = 0, h = 16: c = 8 [1]. t = 5, h = 9: 6 = 8 − 5k, **k = 0.4** [1] **[4]**
**(b)** Empty at √h = 0: 8 − 0.4t = 0, **t = 20 minutes** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** √h = 8/2 − 0.2 × 12 = 1.6, so **h = 2.56 m** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the solution as √h in terms of t. Both later parts follow quickly from it, and a clear formula makes them easy to check.

**10. (a)** dV/dr = 4πr² [1]. dV/dt = (dV/dr)(dr/dt), so 40 = 4πr² dr/dt [1]. **dr/dt = 10/(πr²)** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** πr² dr = 10 dt [1]. πr³/3 = 10t + c [1]. t = 0, r = 2: c = 8π/3 [1]. r = 5: 125π/3 = 10t + 8π/3, so t = 39π/10 = **12.3 seconds** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep π as a symbol until the end. Show the chain rule line in (a), since the result is given.

**11. (a)** Salt in: 0.5 × 4 = 2 kg per minute [1]. Salt out: 4 litres per minute carrying x/100 kg per litre = 0.04x [1]. dx/dt = 2 − 0.04x [1] **[3]**
**(b)** dx/(2 − 0.04x) = dt [1]. −25 ln(2 − 0.04x) = t + c [1]. t = 0, x = 0: c = −25 ln 2. So 2 − 0.04x = 2e^(−t/25) [1]. **x = 50(1 − e^(−t/25))** [1] **[4]**
**(c)** 25 = 50(1 − e^(−t/25)), e^(−t/25) = ½ [1]. t = 25 ln 2 = **17.3 minutes** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Explain both the inflow and outflow terms in words as you build the equation. The tank never exceeds 50 kg, which is a useful check on part (b).

**12. (a)** 1/(P(400 − P)) = (1/400)(1/P + 1/(400 − P)) [2] **[2]**
**(b)** ∫2000/(P(400 − P)) dP = ∫dt, so 5∫(1/P + 1/(400 − P)) dP = ∫dt [1]. 5[ln P − ln(400 − P)] = t + c [2]. P/(400 − P) = Ae^(t/5) [1]. P = 50: A = 50/350 = **1/7** [1] **[5]**
**(c)** P = 200 gives 1 = (1/7)e^(t/5) [1]. t = 5 ln 7 = **9.73** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The integral of 1/(400 − P) has a minus sign from the chain rule. Check by differentiating, or by testing P = 50 at the end.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not separating: integrating the whole equation with respect to x only.
- Putting a constant on both sides, or leaving it out entirely.
- Using the initial condition on the wrong quantity (T instead of T − 25).
- Turning ln y = kt + c into y = kt + c.
- Rounding k early, then giving answers that drift from 3 significant figures.
- Wrong sign for k in decay, cooling or draining models.
- Missing the minus sign when integrating terms such as 1/(400 − P) or y⁻².
- Forgetting the chain rule when the rate of change is of V but the variable is r.
- Not saying what t, x or P stands for when giving a final answer in context.
