Condensed recall for subtopic 4.1 of Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070. For explanations and a worked model answer, use the [electrolysis study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-electrolysis/); to practise, try the [electrolysis practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-electrolysis-practice/).

## Definition to learn word for word

**Electrolysis** is the decomposition of an ionic compound, when molten or in aqueous solution, by the passage of an electric current.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Anode | Positive electrode; oxidation happens here |
| Cathode | Negative electrode; reduction happens here |
| Electrolyte | Molten or aqueous substance that undergoes electrolysis |
| Cation | Positive ion; moves to the cathode |
| Anion | Negative ion; moves to the anode |
| Inert electrode | Platinum or carbon (graphite); conducts without reacting |
| Half-equation | Equation showing electrons gained or lost at one electrode |
| Electroplating | Coating an object with a thin layer of metal by electrolysis |

Memory aid: **OIL RIG** (Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain of electrons) and **"anode, oxidation"** both start with a vowel.

## Charge transfer in three places

- Wires: **electrons** flow from anode to cathode via the power supply.
- Electrodes: cations **gain** electrons at the cathode; anions **lose** electrons at the anode.
- Electrolyte: **ions** move; electrons never travel through it.

## Product rules

- Metals or hydrogen at the cathode; non-metals (other than hydrogen) at the anode.
- Molten binary compound: metal at cathode, non-metal at anode.
- Aqueous, cathode: metal less reactive than hydrogen (copper, silver) is deposited; otherwise **hydrogen**.
- Aqueous, anode (inert): concentrated halide gives **halogen**; dilute halide or no halide gives **oxygen**.

## Routine for any electrolyte

1. **List every ion.** Molten: the salt's two ions only. Aqueous: the salt's ions plus H⁺ and OH⁻ from water.
2. **Cathode:** is the metal below hydrogen in reactivity? Yes: metal. No: hydrogen.
3. **Anode:** is a halide present, and is it concentrated? Yes: halogen. No: oxygen.
4. **Check the electrode material.** A copper anode in a copper salt dissolves instead of releasing a gas.
5. **Write the half-equations** and decide what is left in solution.

Applying it: aqueous silver nitrate gives silver and oxygen; concentrated aqueous potassium iodide gives hydrogen and iodine (brown solution near the anode); molten lithium bromide gives lithium and bromine.

## What is left in the solution

| Electrolyte | Ions removed | Solution left |
|---|---|---|
| Concentrated NaCl(aq) | H⁺, Cl⁻ | NaOH(aq), alkaline |
| CuSO₄(aq), carbon electrodes | Cu²⁺, OH⁻ | H₂SO₄(aq), acidic |
| Dilute H₂SO₄ | H⁺, OH⁻ (water) | More concentrated acid |

## The named electrolytes

| Electrolyte | Cathode | Anode | Remaining / note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Molten PbBr₂ | Lead (grey liquid) | Bromine (red-brown vapour) | Heat needed to keep it molten |
| Concentrated NaCl(aq) | Hydrogen | Chlorine | Sodium hydroxide left in solution |
| Dilute H₂SO₄ | Hydrogen | Oxygen | 2 : 1 volume ratio of H₂ to O₂ |
| CuSO₄(aq), carbon electrodes | Copper (pink-brown) | Oxygen | Blue fades; solution turns acidic |
| CuSO₄(aq), copper electrodes | Copper deposited | Copper anode dissolves | Blue colour unchanged; anode mass lost = cathode mass gained |

## Half-equations to know

```
Pb²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Pb          2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻
2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂           2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻
Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu          4OH⁻ → O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻
                         Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻
```

Check: atoms balance and charges balance on both sides.

## Electroplating checklist

- Object to be plated = **cathode**.
- Plating metal = **anode**.
- Electrolyte = solution of a salt of the **plating metal**.
- Purpose: better **appearance** and **resistance to corrosion**.

## Exam traps

- A solid ionic compound does not conduct: its ions cannot move.
- "Chlorine" is a product; "bubbles of pale yellow-green gas" is an observation. Questions often ask for both.
- Hydrogen, not sodium, forms at the cathode in any aqueous sodium compound.
- With copper electrodes in CuSO₄(aq), no gas is formed at the anode.
- The anode in electroplating is the plating metal, not the object.
- In half-equations, electrons are on the left at the cathode and on the right at the anode.
- Molten lead(II) bromide must be electrolysed in a fume cupboard because bromine is toxic.
- "Dilute" or "concentrated" in a halide question is a deliberate clue: it decides between halogen and oxygen at the anode.
- In the external circuit, electrons flow **from** the anode **to** the cathode, through the power supply.

## Self-test

1. Define electrolysis.
2. Which electrode is positive?
3. Why does solid sodium chloride not conduct electricity?
4. Name the products at each electrode when molten calcium bromide is electrolysed.
5. Name the products when concentrated aqueous potassium chloride is electrolysed with inert electrodes.
6. Why is hydrogen, not potassium, formed in question 5?
7. Name the gas formed at the anode when dilute aqueous sodium chloride is electrolysed.
8. What would you see at the cathode during electrolysis of aqueous copper(II) sulfate with carbon electrodes?
9. Write the half-equation at the anode when copper electrodes are used in copper(II) sulfate solution.
10. Balance: OH⁻ → O₂ + H₂O + e⁻.
11. In silver plating a spoon, which electrode is the spoon and what is the electrolyte?
12. Give two reasons for electroplating.

**Answers**

1. The decomposition of an ionic compound, when molten or in aqueous solution, by the passage of an electric current.
2. The anode.
3. Its ions are held in fixed positions and cannot move.
4. Calcium at the cathode; bromine at the anode.
5. Hydrogen at the cathode; chlorine at the anode.
6. Potassium is more reactive than hydrogen, so H⁺ ions are discharged instead.
7. Oxygen.
8. A pink-brown coating of copper.
9. Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻.
10. 4OH⁻ → O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻.
11. The spoon is the cathode; the electrolyte is a solution of a silver salt, such as silver nitrate.
12. To improve its appearance; to improve its resistance to corrosion.
