**These notes condense key question 2.3 of Cambridge O Level History 2147: how far Hitler's foreign policy was to blame
for the outbreak of war in Europe in 1939.** Learn the sequence of Hitler's steps in order, with the reaction to each,
and be ready to weigh Hitler against the other named factors. The full explanation is in the
[Hitler's foreign policy study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-world-history-2147-hitlers-foreign-policy/); exam
practice is in the [Hitler's foreign policy practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-world-history-2147-hitlers-foreign-policy-practice/).

## Hitler's aims in four words

- **Revise** Versailles: rearm, regain land, end reparations.
- **Unite** German speakers in one Reich.
- **Expand** eastwards for Lebensraum.
- **Destroy** communism.

## Condensed recall by focus point

**Long-term consequences of Versailles**
- Grievances (army limits, Rhineland, Saar, Anschluss ban, Polish Corridor) gave Hitler popular targets.
- Many in Britain thought the Treaty unfair, so early demands looked reasonable.
- Germany's economic potential was left intact.

**Consequences of the League's failures**
- Manchuria and Abyssinia showed collective security did not work.
- Abyssinia moved Mussolini towards Hitler and distracted attention when the Rhineland was remilitarised.
- The League was ignored in the crises of 1938–39.

**Was appeasement justified?**
- For: Britain unready; public fear of war and bombing; sympathy over Versailles; Dominions reluctant; fear of
  communism; time to rearm.
- Against: Germany grew stronger; Hitler was encouraged; Czechoslovakia and its defences and arms industry lost; USSR
  excluded from Munich.

**How important was the Nazi–Soviet Pact?**
- Removed the threat of a two-front war; secret division of Poland.
- Made an attack on Poland possible at once.
- Stalin's motives: distrust of the West after Munich, need for time, buffer territory.

**Why did Britain and France declare war?**
- March 1939 showed Hitler's aims went beyond German lands.
- Guarantee to Poland (31 March 1939); credibility at stake.
- Better rearmed by 1939; public opinion hardened.
- German invasion of Poland on 1 September; ultimatum ignored; war declared on 3 September.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Lebensraum | "Living space" for Germans in Eastern Europe |
| Rearmament | Building up armed forces, banned by Versailles |
| Remilitarisation | Sending troops back into the demilitarised Rhineland |
| Anschluss | Union of Germany and Austria |
| Sudetenland | German-speaking border region of Czechoslovakia |
| Appeasement | Avoiding war by meeting some of an aggressor's demands |
| Munich Agreement | September 1938 settlement giving Germany the Sudetenland |
| Rome–Berlin Axis | 1936 understanding between Hitler and Mussolini |
| Anti-Comintern Pact | 1936 agreement against international communism, Germany and Japan, joined by Italy in 1937 |
| Condor Legion | German air unit that fought for Franco in Spain |
| Non-Intervention Committee | Body meant to keep foreign powers out of the Spanish war |
| Nazi–Soviet Pact | August 1939 non-aggression pact with a secret protocol dividing Poland |

## Exam traps

- **Placing Munich after the occupation of Prague.** Munich was September 1938; Prague was March 1939.
- **Saying Britain declared war over Austria or Czechoslovakia.** It was the invasion of Poland.
- **Treating the Pact as an alliance.** It was a non-aggression pact with a secret protocol.
- **Answering "how far was Hitler to blame" with only Hitler.** Weigh at least two other factors.
- **Ignoring the Saar and Spain,** which are in the specified content.

## Self-test

1. Name **three** of Hitler's foreign policy aims.
2. Why did Hitler back down over Austria in 1934?
3. What was the result of the Saar plebiscite?
4. What did the Anglo-German Naval Agreement allow, and why did it damage the Stresa Front?
5. Why did Britain and France not act when the Rhineland was remilitarised?
6. Give **two** ways Germany gained from involvement in the Spanish Civil War.
7. What did the Munich Agreement decide?
8. Why was the occupation of Bohemia and Moravia a turning point?
9. What did the secret protocol of the Nazi–Soviet Pact contain?
10. Give **two** reasons why Stalin signed the Pact.
11. What did A. J. P. Taylor argue about Hitler?

**Answers:** 1. Any three: overturn Versailles; unite German speakers; Lebensraum in the east; destroy communism.
2. Mussolini moved Italian troops to the Brenner Pass to protect Austria. 3. About 90% voted to rejoin Germany. 4. A
German navy up to 35% of the size of Britain's; Britain had agreed to break Versailles without consulting France or
Italy. 5. Many thought Germany was only moving into its own territory; France was divided and unwilling to act without
Britain; attention was on Abyssinia. 6. Any two: testing aircraft and tactics; training pilots; drawing closer to Italy;
gaining a friendly regime. 7. The Sudetenland would be transferred to Germany. 8. It was the first non-German territory
Hitler took, proving his aims went beyond revising Versailles. 9. A division of Poland and the placing of the Baltic
states in the Soviet sphere. 10. Any two: distrust of Britain and France after Munich; need for time to rearm; gaining
territory as a buffer; Western negotiators lacked authority. 11. That Hitler was an opportunist who exploited others'
mistakes rather than following a fixed plan.
