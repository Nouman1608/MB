Cambridge IGCSE Commerce (0715) studies how goods and services move from
producers to consumers, and the businesses, documents, services and
global networks that make trade possible. The syllabus covered here is a
single-year edition for 2028, which is both its first and final
assessment year, and it is the only published edition of 0715 so far.
The course is assessed through two externally assessed papers: a
multiple-choice paper worth 30% and a written paper worth 70%.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus has six topics, which move from the basics of trade through
day-to-day commercial operations to the global and ethical dimensions of
commerce.

1. **Commerce and production:** trade and commerce, and production. This
   topic defines the scope of the subject and where commerce sits within
   wider economic activity.
2. **Commercial operations:** commercial enterprises, outsourcing,
   retailing, wholesaling, trading documents and communication. It is the
   largest topic by number of subtopics and covers how goods actually
   reach customers.
3. **Globalisation of trade:** international trade and global supply
   chain management.
4. **Logistics in commerce:** how goods are moved and handled within the
   commercial system.
5. **Aids to trade that support commerce:** the services that make trade
   possible.
6. **Sustainability and ethics:** the environmental and ethical questions
   raised by commercial activity.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paper 1 | Multiple Choice | 60 minutes | 40 | 30% |
| Paper 2 | Written | 120 minutes | 80 | 70% |

Both papers are compulsory and externally assessed, and both may involve
simple calculations. Paper 2 consists of structured questions of varying
length. The course is not tiered, and candidates are eligible for grades
A* to G. The assessment is linear, so both papers are taken in the same
series.

Because this edition is examined only in 2028, check that you are
following the syllabus for your own examination year, particularly if
you expect to sit the exam later than 2028.

## How the parts fit together

The six topics describe one connected system, so questions on either
paper can easily combine them. Production in Topic 1 creates the goods
that retailers and wholesalers in Topic 2 distribute. Trading documents
and communication in Topic 2 are needed again when goods cross borders in
international trade. Global supply chain management in Topic 3 depends
on logistics in Topic 4 and on aids to trade in Topic 5. Sustainability
and ethics in Topic 6 then asks whether each of these activities is
carried out responsibly.

The two papers test this knowledge differently. Paper 1 checks precise
understanding of terms and ideas across the whole syllabus, which rewards
broad coverage. Paper 2, worth more than twice as much, asks you to
explain, apply and discuss commercial ideas at greater length, and to
handle simple calculations along the way. Keeping both kinds of practice
going on every topic is more effective than separating them.

A useful way to build understanding is to follow a single product, such
as a packet of tea or a mobile phone, through the whole syllabus. Ask
who produces it, which retailers and wholesalers handle it, which
documents and forms of communication accompany each sale, how it crosses
borders, how it is transported and stored, which services make the trade
possible, and what environmental or ethical questions arise along the
way. Doing this for two or three different products gives you a ready
stock of examples to use in Paper 2 answers and helps you remember how
the topics connect.

## Planning your study

Following the topic order works well, since each topic builds on the
ones before it:

- **Start of the course:** Topic 1, then the first half of Topic 2
  (commercial enterprises, outsourcing and retailing).
- **First year continued:** the rest of Topic 2 (wholesaling, trading
  documents and communication), with practice reading and completing
  simple trading documents.
- **Second year:** Topics 3 to 5, treating international trade, logistics
  and aids to trade as one chain.
- **Final stage:** Topic 6, linking sustainability and ethics back to the
  earlier topics, then full revision.

Since this edition has not yet had a live sitting, there are no past
papers for it yet. Use topic-based practice questions as each topic is
finished, and turn to the exam preparation page for paper-by-paper advice
in the revision period.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is Cambridge IGCSE Commerce 0715 assessed?**
Through two compulsory papers: Paper 1, Multiple Choice (60 minutes, 40 marks, 30%) and Paper 2, Written (120 minutes, 80 marks, 70%). Both are externally assessed.

**Does IGCSE Commerce involve calculations?**
Yes, to a limited extent: both papers may involve simple calculations, so be ready to work with figures as well as ideas.

**Is IGCSE Commerce tiered?**
No. All candidates take the same two papers and are eligible for grades A* to G.

**Which paper matters more in IGCSE Commerce?**
Paper 2, Written, carries 70% of the qualification and 80 marks, compared with 30% and 40 marks for Paper 1, so practising structured written answers deserves most of your preparation time.

**Are there past papers for 0715?**
This edition has not yet had a live sitting, so topic-based practice questions are the main way to prepare until papers become available.

**When is this 0715 syllabus examined?**
This edition is a single-year syllabus for 2028, which is its first and final assessment year.

**What are the six topics in 0715?**
Commerce and production, Commercial operations, Globalisation of trade, Logistics in commerce, Aids to trade that support commerce, and Sustainability and ethics.
