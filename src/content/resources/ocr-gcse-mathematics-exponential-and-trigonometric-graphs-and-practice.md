## Questions

**1.** (Higher tier only) The table is for y = 4 × 1.5ˣ.

```
x | -2 | -1  | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3
y |    | 2.67| 4 |   | 9 |
```

**(a)** Work out the missing y-values. **[3]**
**(b)** Write down the y-intercept and explain why the graph never crosses the x-axis. **[2]**

**2.** (Higher tier only) The graph of y = kˣ, where k > 0, passes through (2, 1/9).

**(a)** Find k. **[2]**
**(b)** State whether the graph rises or falls from left to right, and give its y-intercept and asymptote. **[2]**

**3.** (Higher tier only)

**(a)** Sketch y = sin x for 0° ≤ x ≤ 360°, marking the coordinates of the maximum, the minimum and the points where it meets the x-axis. **[3]**
**(b)** How many solutions has sin x = −0.6 for 0° ≤ x ≤ 720°? **[2]**

**4.** (Higher tier only)

**(a)** Sketch y = tan x for 0° ≤ x ≤ 360°. Show the asymptotes and the points where the graph meets the x-axis. **[3]**
**(b)** State the period of y = tan x. **[1]**

**5.** (Higher tier only) Each description fits one of y = sin x, y = cos x or y = tan x. Name the graph. **[3]**

**(i)** The graph passes through (0, 1) and its values never exceed 1.
**(ii)** The graph has vertical asymptotes.
**(iii)** The graph passes through (0, 0), has a maximum at (90°, 1) and a minimum at (270°, −1).

**6.** (Higher tier only) The graph of y = x² is translated 3 units left and 5 units down.

**(a)** Write down the equation of the new graph. **[2]**
**(b)** Write down its turning point and its y-intercept. **[2]**

**7.** (Higher tier only) The graph of y = x² − 6x has its turning point at (3, −9). It is reflected (a) in the x-axis and (b) in the y-axis. For each, write down the equation of the new graph and its turning point.

**(a)** Reflection in the x-axis. **[2]**
**(b)** Reflection in the y-axis. **[2]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) The graph of y = sin x is translated 2 units down.

**(a)** Write down the new equation, its maximum and minimum y-values, and its y-intercept. **[4]**
**(b)** Describe the translation that maps y = cos x onto y = sin x. **[1]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) Start from the graph of y = 2ˣ.

**(a)** It is translated 3 units down. Give the equation, the y-intercept and the equation of the asymptote. **[3]**
**(b)** It is reflected in the x-axis. Give the equation and the y-intercept. **[2]**
**(c)** It is reflected in the y-axis. Give the equation of the new graph, and write it in the form y = kˣ. **[1]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) The curve y = 3(x − 1)² − 2 has its turning point at (1, −2) and crosses the y-axis at (0, 1).

**(a)** The curve is translated 4 units right and 3 units up. Find the new turning point, the new equation, and the new y-intercept. **[3]**
**(b)** The original curve is reflected in the x-axis. Find the new equation and turning point. **[2]**

**11.** (Higher tier only) A car's value, V pounds, after t years is modelled by V = 18 000 × 0.85ᵗ.

**(a)** Find V when t = 0 and when t = 3, to the nearest pound. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the shape of the graph of V against t, and explain why the model never gives a value of zero. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the first whole number of years after which the value is below £9000. **[3]**

**12.** (Higher tier only) The curve C has equation y = (x + 1)² − 9.

**(a)** Write down the turning point of C and its y-intercept. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the x-coordinates of the points where C crosses the x-axis. **[2]**
**(c)** C is reflected in the x-axis and the result is then translated 3 units right. Find the equation of the final curve, its turning point and its y-intercept. **[4]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 1.5⁻² = 1/2.25, so y = 4 × 0.444… = 1.78 at x = −2 [1]. At x = 1, y = 4 × 1.5 = 6 [1]. At x = 3, y = 4 × 3.375 = 13.5 [1] **[3]**
**(b)** y-intercept (0, 4) [1]. 1.5ˣ is always positive, so y is never zero [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the power you are evaluating (for example 1.5⁻²) before the decimal, so your working is clear and easy to check.

**2. (a)** k² = 1/9 [1], so **k = 1/3** (k is positive) [1] **[2]**
**(b)** 0 < k < 1, so the graph **falls** [1]. The y-intercept is **(0, 1)** and the asymptote is **y = 0** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* State the equation before square-rooting. Writing k = ±1/3 and then rejecting the negative value shows you used the condition k > 0.

**3. (a)** A smooth wave starting at (0, 0) [1], with maximum (90°, 1) and minimum (270°, −1) [1], meeting the x-axis at (0°, 0), (180°, 0) and (360°, 0) [1] **[3]**
**(b)** The line y = −0.6 lies between 0 and −1, so it cuts each wave twice [1]. Two waves give **4 solutions** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A sketch scores best as a smooth curve with the key coordinates written on it. Straight-line segments or an unlabelled wave leave the examiner nothing to check.

**4. (a)** Vertical asymptotes drawn at x = 90° and x = 270° [1]. The curve passes through (0, 0), (180°, 0) and (360°, 0) [1]. Each branch rises from the left to the asymptote on its right, and the middle branch is continuous through (180°, 0) [1] **[3]**
**(b)** **180°** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Draw asymptotes as dashed lines and keep the curve from touching them. A curve that crosses an asymptote suggests the graph is misunderstood.

**5.** (i) **cos x** [1]. (ii) **tan x** [1]. (iii) The point (0, 0) and the maximum at 90° make it **sin x** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Give just the name for each description. Where two features are given, check that both fit before choosing.

**6. (a)** Left 3 means x becomes x + 3 [1]; down 5 gives **y = (x + 3)² − 5** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Turning point **(−3, −5)** [1]. When x = 0, y = 9 − 5 = 4, so the y-intercept is **(0, 4)** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Test the direction of a horizontal move by finding where the bracket equals zero. Here x + 3 = 0 gives x = −3, which is to the left.

**7. (a)** Change the sign of the whole right-hand side: **y = −x² + 6x** [1]. Turning point **(3, 9)** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Replace x by −x: (−x)² − 6(−x) gives **y = x² + 6x** [1]. Turning point **(−3, −9)** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A reflection in the x-axis changes the sign of every term, not just the first. Check by testing the turning point.

**8. (a)** **y = sin x − 2** [1]. Maximum −1 [1], minimum −3 [1], y-intercept **(0, −2)** [1] **[4]**
**(b)** **Translation 90° to the right** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the type of transformation, the size and the direction. "Moves 90" alone would not describe it completely.

**9. (a)** **y = 2ˣ − 3** [1]. y-intercept 1 − 3 = **−2**, so (0, −2) [1]. Asymptote **y = −3** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** **y = −2ˣ** [1]. y-intercept **(0, −1)** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** **y = 2⁻ˣ**, which is y = (1/2)ˣ = **0.5ˣ** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* When a graph is translated vertically, move the asymptote as well as the intercept. Leaving the asymptote at y = 0 is a common slip.

**10. (a)** Turning point **(5, 1)** [1]. Replace x by x − 4 and add 3: **y = 3(x − 5)² + 1** [1]. When x = 0, y = 3 × 25 + 1 = **76**, so (0, 76) [1] **[3]**
**(b)** **y = −3(x − 1)² + 2** [1]. Turning point **(1, 2)** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the squared bracket together and apply the minus sign to the whole expression. A minus applied only to the 3 would not reflect the curve.

**11. (a)** t = 0: **£18 000** [1]. t = 3: 18 000 × 0.85³ = 11 054.25, so **£11 054** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** It falls steeply at first and then flattens, getting closer to the t-axis [1]. 0.85ᵗ is always positive, so V is never zero [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Half of 18 000 is 9000. At t = 4, V = 9396.11, still above 9000 [1]. At t = 5, V = 7986.70, below 9000 [1]. So **5 years** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the values either side of 9000. A bare answer of 5 gives no evidence that 4 was checked and rejected.

**12. (a)** Turning point **(−1, −9)** [1]. y-intercept: 1 − 9 = −8, so **(0, −8)** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** (x + 1)² = 9 [1], so x + 1 = 3 or −3, giving **x = 2 and x = −4** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Reflection in the x-axis: y = 9 − (x + 1)² [1]. Moving 3 right replaces x by x − 3: **y = 9 − (x − 2)²**, that is y = −x² + 4x + 5 [1]. Turning point **(2, 9)** [1]. y-intercept 9 − 4 = **5**, so (0, 5) [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Do the two transformations in the order stated, and check the final equation against one point: the turning point should end up at (2, 9).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Drawing an exponential graph that touches or crosses the x-axis.
- Forgetting that y = kˣ passes through (0, 1), whatever the value of k.
- Moving y = (x + 3)² − 5 to the right instead of the left.
- Not moving the asymptote when an exponential graph is translated.
- Reflecting in the x-axis by changing the sign of only part of the equation.
- Sketching sin x starting at 1 or cos x starting at 0.
- Missing the asymptotes of tan x, or drawing straight lines between points.
- Giving a loose description such as "moves 2" instead of naming the transformation, its size and direction.
- Rounding the answer to a modelling question without showing the values either side of the target.
