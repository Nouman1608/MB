## Section A

**1.** Find the equation of the tangent to y = x³ − 2x² at the point where x = 2. **[3]**

**2.** Find the equation of the normal to y = 6/x at the point (3, 2). Give your answer in the form
ax + by + d = 0, where a, b, d ∈ ℤ. **[4]**

**3.** Find the coordinates of the points on y = x³ − 12x + 1 where the tangent is horizontal, and
write down the equation of each of these tangents. **[4]**

**4.** Find the equation of the tangent to y = x² − 5x + 4 that is perpendicular to the line
x + 3y = 6. **[4]**

**5.** The tangent to y = x² + 3x at the point where x = 1 meets the x-axis at A and the y-axis at B.
Find the area of triangle OAB, where O is the origin. **[5]**

## Section B

**6.** A curve has equation y = x³ − 3x² + 2.

**(a)** Find the equation of the tangent to the curve at the point where x = 3. **[3]**

**(b)** Show that the x-coordinates of the points where this tangent meets the curve satisfy
x³ − 3x² − 9x + 27 = 0. **[1]**

**(c)** Hence find the coordinates of the other point where the tangent meets the curve. **[3]**

**7.** The normal to y = x² at the point (1, 1) meets the curve again at Q. Find the coordinates of Q.
**[5]**

**8.** The tangent to y = x² at the point (a, a²) passes through the point (1, −3).

**(a)** Show that a² − 2a − 3 = 0. **[3]**

**(b)** Hence find the equations of the two tangents to y = x² that pass through (1, −3). **[3]**

**9.** A curve has equation y = x⁴ − 3x + 1. Use your GDC where helpful.

**(a)** Find the equation of the tangent at x = 1.2, in the form y = mx + c. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the equation of the normal at x = 1.2, in the form y = mx + c. **[2]**

**10.** The curve y = kx² − 3x has a tangent with gradient 5 at the point where x = 2.

**(a)** Find k. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the equation of the normal at x = 2, in the form ax + by + d = 0, where a, b, d ∈ ℤ.
**[3]**

**11.** The tangents to y = x² − 4x + 3 at the points where x = 0 and x = 4 meet at Q.

**(a)** Find the equations of both tangents. **[4]**

**(b)** Find the coordinates of Q. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** y(2) = 8 − 8 = 0 [1]. dy/dx = 3x² − 4x, so m = 12 − 8 = 4 [1]. Tangent: y = 4(x − 2), so
y = 4x − 8 [1]. **[3]**

**2.** y = 6x⁻¹, dy/dx = −6x⁻² = −6/9 = −2/3 at x = 3 [1]. Normal gradient 3/2 [1].
y − 2 = (3/2)(x − 3) [1], so 2y − 4 = 3x − 9 and 3x − 2y − 5 = 0 [1]. **[4]**

**3.** dy/dx = 3x² − 12 = 0, so x = ±2 [1]. y(2) = −15 and y(−2) = 17 [1]. Points (2, −15) and (−2, 17);
tangents y = −15 [1] and y = 17 [1]. **[4]**

**4.** The line has gradient −1/3, so the tangent's gradient is 3 [1]. 2x − 5 = 3, x = 4 [1];
y(4) = 16 − 20 + 4 = 0 [1]. Tangent: y = 3(x − 4) = 3x − 12 [1]. **[4]**

**5.** y(1) = 4 and dy/dx = 2x + 3 = 5 [1]. Tangent y − 4 = 5(x − 1), so y = 5x − 1 [1].
A = (1/5, 0) and B = (0, −1) [1]. Area = (1/2)(1/5)(1) [1] = 1/10 [1]. **[5]**

**6. (a)** y(3) = 27 − 27 + 2 = 2 [1]; dy/dx = 3x² − 6x = 9 at x = 3 [1]; y − 2 = 9(x − 3), so
y = 9x − 25 [1].

**(b)** x³ − 3x² + 2 = 9x − 25 gives x³ − 3x² − 9x + 27 = 0 [1].

**(c)** x = 3 is a repeated root (the tangent touches there), so the cubic is (x − 3)²(x + 3) [1].
x = −3 [1] and y = 9(−3) − 25 = −52, giving (−3, −52) [1]. **[7]**

**7.** dy/dx = 2x = 2 at x = 1, so the normal gradient is −1/2 [1]. Normal: y − 1 = −(1/2)(x − 1),
y = −x/2 + 3/2 [1]. x² = −x/2 + 3/2 gives 2x² + x − 3 = 0 [1], so (2x + 3)(x − 1) = 0 [1].
x = −3/2 and Q = (−3/2, 9/4) [1]. **[5]**

**8. (a)** Gradient at (a, a²) is 2a [1]; tangent y − a² = 2a(x − a) [1]. Substituting (1, −3):
−3 − a² = 2a − 2a², so a² − 2a − 3 = 0 [1].

**(b)** (a − 3)(a + 1) = 0, a = 3 or a = −1 [1]. a = 3: y − 9 = 6(x − 3), y = 6x − 9 [1].
a = −1: y − 1 = −2(x + 1), y = −2x − 1 [1]. **[6]**

**9. (a)** y(1.2) = −0.5264 [1]; dy/dx = 4(1.2)³ − 3 = 3.912 [1]; y = 3.912x − 5.2208, so
y = 3.91x − 5.22 (3 s.f.) [1].

**(b)** Normal gradient −1/3.912 = −0.2556… [1]; y + 0.5264 = −0.2556(x − 1.2), so
y = −0.256x − 0.220 (3 s.f.) [1]. **[5]**

**10. (a)** dy/dx = 2kx − 3, so 4k − 3 = 5 [1] and k = 2 [1].

**(b)** y(2) = 8 − 6 = 2 [1]; normal gradient −1/5 [1]; y − 2 = −(1/5)(x − 2) gives
x + 5y − 12 = 0 [1]. **[5]**

**11. (a)** dy/dx = 2x − 4 [1]. At x = 0: point (0, 3), m = −4, y = −4x + 3 [1]. At x = 4: point
(4, 3), m = 4 [1], y = 4x − 13 [1].

**(b)** −4x + 3 = 4x − 13, so x = 2 [1] and y = −5: Q = (2, −5) [1]. **[6]**

## How this set builds

Questions 1 to 3 practise the standard method, including the horizontal special case. Questions 4,
10 and 11 add gradient conditions and unknowns. Questions 5, 6, 7 and 11 use the line once found:
intercepts, an area and intersections, where knowing one root helps factorise. Question 8 reverses
the usual process, and question 9 is the technology version.
