All data are invented. Calculators are allowed. Revise first with the [movement into and out of cells study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-movement-into-and-out-of-cells/) and the [movement into and out of cells revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-movement-into-and-out-of-cells-revision-notes/).

## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** Uptake of a substance by root cells stops when a respiratory inhibitor is added. By which process does the substance most likely enter? **[1]**

A: Simple diffusion

B: Facilitated diffusion

C: Osmosis

D: Active transport

**2.** Which solution has the highest water potential? **[1]**

A: −850 kPa

B: −420 kPa

C: −1 200 kPa

D: −600 kPa

**3.** Define osmosis. **[2]**

**4.** Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of a cuboid measuring 6 cm × 2 cm × 1 cm. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why the rate of facilitated diffusion stops increasing at high external concentrations. **[2]**

**6.** State two differences between endocytosis and exocytosis. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** Cells were placed in a solution containing 5 mmol dm⁻³ of an amino acid. The concentration inside the cells was measured with and without a respiratory inhibitor.

| Time / min | 0 | 10 | 20 | 30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Inside, no inhibitor / mmol dm⁻³ | 2 | 10 | 18 | 26 |
| Inside, with inhibitor / mmol dm⁻³ | 2 | 4 | 5 | 5 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of uptake without the inhibitor over 30 minutes. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain how the results show that the amino acid is taken up by active transport. **[3]**

**(c)** Suggest why the concentration with the inhibitor rose to 5 mmol dm⁻³ but no higher. **[2]**

**8.** A root hair can be modelled as a cylinder of radius 0.25 cm and length 6 cm (scaled-up model).

**(a)** Calculate its surface area, volume and SA:V ratio, to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**(b)** A cube of side 1 cm has SA:V of 6 : 1. Explain the advantage of the cylinder shape for uptake. **[2]**

**9.** Agar cubes containing an indicator were placed in acid, and the time for each to change colour completely was recorded.

| Side / cm | 0.5 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time / s | 60 | 235 | 940 |

**(a)** Calculate the rate (1 000 ÷ time) for each cube, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the time increases so steeply with cube size. **[3]**

**(c)** Suggest two ways to improve the reliability of these results. **[2]**

**10.** Strips of onion epidermis were placed in sucrose solutions and the percentage of plasmolysed cells counted.

| Sucrose / mol dm⁻³ | 0.20 | 0.30 | 0.40 | 0.50 | 0.60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cells plasmolysed / % | 0 | 5 | 30 | 70 | 100 |

**(a)** Estimate the sucrose concentration at incipient plasmolysis. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why this concentration can be used to estimate the water potential of the epidermal cells. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the student counted at least 20 cells in each strip. **[1]**

**11.** Three adjacent plant cells have water potentials of P = −350 kPa, Q = −550 kPa and R = −450 kPa.

**(a)** State the direction of net water movement between each pair of cells. **[2]**

**(b)** Red blood cells with a water potential of −700 kPa are placed in a solution of −1 000 kPa. Describe and explain what happens. **[3]**

**12.** Describe how a gland cell secretes an enzyme by exocytosis. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **D** [1]. Only active transport depends directly on ATP from respiration. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. −420 kPa is the least negative. **[1]**

**3.** The net movement of water from a region of higher water potential to a region of lower water potential [1] through a partially permeable membrane [1]. **[2]**

**4.** SA = 2(12 + 6 + 2) = 40 cm²; V = 12 cm³ [1]; SA:V = 40 ÷ 12 = **3.33 : 1** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Facilitated diffusion uses a limited number of channel or carrier proteins [1]; at high concentrations all are in use, so they work at full capacity and the rate cannot increase further [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any two: endocytosis brings material into the cell, exocytosis releases it [1]; in endocytosis the membrane folds inwards to form a vesicle, in exocytosis a vesicle fuses with the membrane [1]; endocytosis adds no membrane to the cell surface, exocytosis does. **[2]**

**7. (a)** (26 − 2) ÷ 30 = **0.8 mmol dm⁻³ min⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** Without the inhibitor, the concentration inside rose above 5 mmol dm⁻³, so the amino acid moved against its concentration gradient [1]. This requires energy [1]. When respiration was inhibited, so little ATP was made, uptake stopped at the external concentration, showing that ATP is needed [1].

**(c)** The amino acid can still enter by diffusion through carrier proteins down its gradient [1], which stops when concentrations inside and outside are equal, at 5 mmol dm⁻³ [1]. **[6]**

**8. (a)** SA = 2π(0.25)² + 2π(0.25)(6) = **9.82 cm²** [1]; V = π(0.25)²(6) = **1.18 cm³** [1]; SA:V = **8.33 : 1** [1].

**(b)** The long, thin cylinder has a larger surface area per unit volume than the cube [1], so more water and ions can be absorbed per unit volume of cell, and the distance to the centre is short [1]. **[5]**

**9. (a)** 0.5 cm: **16.7 s⁻¹**; 1 cm: **4.26 s⁻¹**; 2 cm: **1.06 s⁻¹** (2 marks for all three; 1 mark for two correct) [2].

**(b)** Doubling the side halves SA:V, so there is less surface for acid to enter per unit volume [1]. The distance from the surface to the centre also doubles [1], so acid takes much longer to reach the middle; the time rises about four-fold each time the side doubles [1].

**(c)** Any two: repeat each size and calculate a mean [1]; judge the end point against a white tile [1]; cut all cubes from the same agar mixture; keep the acid temperature constant with a water bath. **[7]**

**10. (a)** 50% lies halfway between 30% and 70% [1], so 0.40 + 0.5 × 0.10 = **0.45 mol dm⁻³** [1].

**(b)** At incipient plasmolysis the average cell has lost just enough water to begin pulling away from its wall, so there is no net movement of water [1]; the cells' water potential therefore equals the water potential of the solution [1].

**(c)** Cells vary, so a larger sample gives a more representative percentage [1]. **[5]**

**11. (a)** P to Q and P to R [1]; R to Q [1].

**(b)** The solution has a lower water potential than the cells [1], so water leaves the cells by osmosis through the partially permeable cell surface membrane [1]. The cells shrink and their membranes crinkle (crenation) [1]. **[5]**

**12.** The enzyme is packaged into vesicles (from the Golgi body) [1]. The vesicles move to the cell surface membrane and fuse with it [1], releasing the enzyme outside the cell, using energy from ATP [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* In question 7(b), the decisive evidence is the concentration rising above 5 mmol dm⁻³. Saying "uptake was faster without the inhibitor" does not by itself show active transport.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving water potential or the partially permeable membrane out of the definition of osmosis.
- Treating −1 000 kPa as higher than −700 kPa.
- Using diameter, or forgetting the two ends, in cylinder surface area.
- Explaining agar times using SA:V only, without distance.
- Taking 0% or 100% plasmolysis as the equilibrium point.
