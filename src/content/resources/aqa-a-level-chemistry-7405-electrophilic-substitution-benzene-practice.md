These questions cover section **3.3.10.2 Electrophilic substitution** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 2 and Paper 3. Learn the ideas first in the [Electrophilic substitution study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-electrophilic-substitution-benzene/) and the [Electrophilic substitution revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-electrophilic-substitution-benzene-revision-notes/). Use Aᵣ values H = 1.0, C = 12.0, N = 14.0, O = 16.0.

## Questions

**1.** State what is meant by an electrophile. **[1]**

**2.** Benzene is converted into nitrobenzene.

**(a)** State the reagents and the temperature used. **[2]**

**(b)** Write an equation for the formation of the electrophile. **[1]**

**(c)** Name the electrophile. **[1]**

**3.** Outline the mechanism for the nitration of benzene. Show the intermediate and the regeneration of the catalyst. **[5]**

**4.** Explain why the temperature is kept below 55 °C in the nitration of benzene. **[1]**

**5.** State two uses of the products of nitration reactions of benzene rings. **[2]**

**6.** Benzene reacts with propanoyl chloride, CH₃CH₂COCl, in the presence of anhydrous aluminium chloride.

**(a)** Write an equation for the formation of the electrophile. **[1]**

**(b)** Outline the mechanism for the reaction. **[4]**

**(c)** Name the organic product. **[1]**

**7.** Explain why anhydrous conditions are needed in the reaction in question 6. **[1]**

**8.** State the role of aluminium chloride in the reaction in question 6 and show how it is regenerated. **[2]**

**9.** 7.80 g of benzene (Mᵣ = 78.0) is nitrated and 8.61 g of nitrobenzene (Mᵣ = 123.0) is isolated. Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**

**10.** 15.6 g of benzene (Mᵣ = 78.0) reacts with excess ethanoyl chloride and anhydrous aluminium chloride. 18.0 g of phenylethanone (Mᵣ = 120.0) is formed. Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**

**11.** Explain why benzene reacts with the nitronium ion by substitution rather than addition. **[3]**

**12.** Explain why benzene requires a strong electrophile generated using a catalyst, while cyclohexene reacts with bromine without a catalyst. **[3]**

**13.** State the product formed when benzene reacts with butanoyl chloride, CH₃CH₂CH₂COCl, in the presence of anhydrous aluminium chloride, and name the type of reaction. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** A species that accepts a pair of electrons to form a covalent bond [1]. **[1]**

**2. (a)** Concentrated nitric acid and concentrated sulfuric acid [1]; heat at about 50 °C [1]

**(b)** HNO₃ + H₂SO₄ → NO₂⁺ + HSO₄⁻ + H₂O [1]

**(c)** The nitronium ion [1]. **[4]**

**3.** Equation for the formation of NO₂⁺ [1]; arrow from the delocalised ring to the N of NO₂⁺ [1]; intermediate with H and NO₂ on the same carbon and a positive charge shown within the ring [1]; arrow from the C–H bond into the ring, giving nitrobenzene and H⁺ [1]; H⁺ + HSO₄⁻ → H₂SO₄ [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* The first arrow starts on the ring, not on the electrophile.

**4.** To prevent more than one nitro group being added to the ring (further substitution) [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Any two from: explosives (such as TNT); reduction to aromatic amines such as phenylamine; making dyes [2]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** CH₃CH₂COCl + AlCl₃ → CH₃CH₂CO⁺ + AlCl₄⁻ [1]

**(b)** Arrow from the delocalised ring to the carbon of CH₃CH₂CO⁺ [1]; intermediate with H and COCH₂CH₃ on one carbon and a positive charge in the ring [1]; arrow from the C–H bond into the ring, giving the product and H⁺ [1]; H⁺ + AlCl₄⁻ → AlCl₃ + HCl [1]

**(c)** Phenylpropanone (1-phenylpropan-1-one) [1]. **[6]**

**7.** Aluminium chloride reacts with water, which would stop it acting as a catalyst [1]. **[1]**

**8.** It is a catalyst that helps generate the acylium ion [1]; it is regenerated in H⁺ + AlCl₄⁻ → AlCl₃ + HCl [1]. **[2]**

**9.** n(benzene) = 7.80 ÷ 78.0 = 0.100 mol [1]; theoretical mass = 0.100 × 123.0 = 12.3 g [1]; percentage yield = 8.61 ÷ 12.3 × 100 = **70.0 %** [1]. **[3]**

**10.** n(benzene) = 15.6 ÷ 78.0 = 0.200 mol [1]; theoretical mass = 0.200 × 120.0 = 24.0 g [1]; percentage yield = 18.0 ÷ 24.0 × 100 = **75.0 %** [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Addition would destroy the delocalised system of benzene [1]; this would lose the extra stability (about 152 kJ mol⁻¹) [1]; in substitution the H⁺ is lost in the second step and the delocalised ring is restored [1]. **[3]**

**12.** The electrons in benzene are delocalised, so the electron density is lower than at the localised C=C in cyclohexene [1]; bromine is not polarised enough by benzene to react [1]; a catalyst is used to generate a much stronger electrophile (such as NO₂⁺ or RCO⁺) [1]. **[3]**

**13.** The product is a ketone, 1-phenylbutan-1-one (phenyl propyl ketone) [1]; the reaction is Friedel-Crafts acylation, a type of electrophilic substitution [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Starting the first arrow at the electrophile.
- Drawing the positive charge on one carbon outside the ring.
- Forgetting to regenerate the catalyst.
- Giving dilute acids for nitration or aqueous aluminium chloride for acylation.
- Naming NO₂ or HNO₃ as the electrophile.
- Missing the "anhydrous" condition.
