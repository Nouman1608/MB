Cambridge O Level Urdu is not one course but two closely related
syllabuses that sit under the same subject. First Language Urdu (3247)
is written for candidates whose mother tongue is Urdu, while Second
Language Urdu (3248) is for learners who use Urdu as an additional
language. Both are assessed through two written papers worth 50% each,
every answer is written in Urdu, and both are graded A* to E. The
first thing any student needs to settle, therefore, is which of the two
they are being entered for, because the papers, timings and skills
differ in ways that matter from the first week of study.

## How the course is organised

Each syllabus is built around its two papers rather than around a long
list of separate topics, so the course is best understood paper by
paper.

**First Language Urdu (3247)**

- **Paper 1 Reading and Writing** develops close reading of Urdu prose
  and the ability to write accurately and expressively in response.
- **Paper 2 Texts** moves into literary study, covering an unseen
  passage, poetry and prose. This is where 3247 most clearly goes
  beyond language skills into the appreciation of Urdu writing.

**Second Language Urdu (3248)**

- **Paper 1 Reading and Writing** tests understanding of written Urdu
  and the ability to produce clear written responses at a second
  language standard.
- **Paper 2 Grammar, Writing and Translation** is the more technical
  paper, checking control of grammar, continuous writing and the skill
  of moving meaning between languages.

The two syllabuses share a paper title, Reading and Writing, but they
are not interchangeable. The first-language route assumes a native
speaker's range of vocabulary and idiom and asks for literary
response; the second-language route builds secure, accurate use of the
language and replaces literature with grammar and translation.

## How it's assessed

Both syllabuses are linear: the two papers are taken together at the
end of the course. The figures below are taken from the Cambridge
syllabuses for exams from 2027.

| Syllabus | Paper | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3247 First Language | Paper 1 Reading and Writing | 1 hour 30 minutes | 50 | 50% |
| 3247 First Language | Paper 2 Texts | 2 hours | 50 | 50% |
| 3248 Second Language | Paper 1 Reading and Writing | 1 hour 45 minutes | 50 | 50% |
| 3248 Second Language | Paper 2 Grammar, Writing and Translation | 1 hour 30 minutes | 50 | 50% |

A few practical points follow from the syllabus notes:

- **3247** is offered in the June series only. The 2027 syllabus states
  that its papers are the same as those in the syllabus for 2025 and
  2026, so material written against the earlier version still fits.
- **3248** has moved to a 2027-2029 syllabus with Paper 1 and Paper 2
  confirmed unchanged in duration and marks. The 2024-2026 version runs
  to its final series in November 2026. Dictionaries are not permitted
  in the 3248 papers.
- Both syllabuses award grades A* to E, rather than extending lower.

Notice the different balance of time. In 3247 the longer paper is
Paper 2, reflecting the extended literary writing it asks for. In 3248
the longer paper is Paper 1, where reading comprehension and written
response take the most time.

## How the parts fit together

Although each syllabus divides neatly into two papers, the skills do
not stay in separate compartments.

For **First Language** candidates, reading closely in Paper 1 is the
same habit that drives good analysis of an unseen passage in Paper 2.
Noticing how a writer builds an argument, chooses a word or shifts tone
is useful in both, and the accuracy of written Urdu is rewarded across
the whole qualification. Paper 2 adds a literary vocabulary for talking
about poetry and prose, but a candidate who reads widely for Paper 1 is
already preparing for it.

For **Second Language** candidates, grammar is the thread that joins
everything. Secure control of verb forms, agreement and sentence
structure is tested directly in Paper 2, but it also decides how
clearly a student can answer comprehension questions and write in
Paper 1. Translation practice sharpens both: it forces attention to
exact meaning, which improves reading, and to correct form, which
improves writing.

In both routes, writing is assessed on every paper. Building a habit of
planning, drafting and checking written Urdu therefore pays off twice.

## Planning your study

A sensible plan starts with the paper that underpins the other.

- **First Language (3247):** spend the early part of the course on
  Paper 1 reading and writing skills, reading a broad range of Urdu
  prose alongside. Introduce poetry and prose study for Paper 2 once
  close-reading habits are in place, then return to the unseen passage
  regularly, since it cannot be learned in advance and rewards steady
  practice.
- **Second Language (3248):** build grammar and core vocabulary first,
  then use them in short written tasks and short translations. Extend
  to longer reading passages and continuous writing as accuracy
  improves, keeping a running list of grammar points that cause
  repeated errors.

Across a two-year course, a reasonable pattern is to cover the content
and skills for both papers in the first year, consolidate and widen
reading in the second, and move to timed practice in the final months.
Start working through full papers once you have met every question
type, and use the exam-preparation page for the paper-by-paper
approach rather than leaving technique until the last term.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between Cambridge O Level Urdu 3247 and 3248?**
3247 is First Language Urdu, for candidates whose mother tongue is Urdu, and its second paper is Texts. 3248 is Second Language Urdu, for learners with Urdu as an additional language, and its second paper is Grammar, Writing and Translation.

**How long are the Cambridge O Level Urdu papers?**
For 3247, Paper 1 is 1 hour 30 minutes and Paper 2 is 2 hours. For 3248, Paper 1 is 1 hour 45 minutes and Paper 2 is 1 hour 30 minutes. Every paper is out of 50 marks and worth 50%.

**Can I use a dictionary in O Level Second Language Urdu?**
No. The 3248 syllabus states that dictionaries are not permitted, and all answers are given in Urdu.

**When can I sit O Level First Language Urdu?**
3247 is available in the June series only. Check with your school which series you are entered for.

**What grades are available in O Level Urdu?**
Both 3247 and 3248 are graded A* to E.
