These notes condense **section 10.1 Financial statements** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Business 9609 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Worked examples are in the [financial statements study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-business-9609-financial-statements-cambridge-alevel-business/); questions are in the [financial statements practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-business-9609-financial-statements-cambridge-alevel-business-practice/). Ratio analysis follows in the [financial statements and analysis of published accounts notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-business-financial-statements-and-analysis-of-published-accounts-revision-notes/).

All businesses are invented. Figures are in $000 unless stated.

## Purpose of each statement

| | Statement of profit or loss | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|
| Shows | Profit or loss over a period | Assets, liabilities and equity on one date |
| Main use | Judging performance; dividends | Judging liquidity, debt and owners' stake |

Profit ≠ cash: late-paying customers, inventory build-up and capital spending can leave a profitable business short of cash.

## Statement of profit or loss: order of lines

```
Revenue
- Cost of sales
= Gross profit
- Expenses
= Profit from operations (operating profit)
- Taxation
= Profit for the year
- Dividends
= Retained earnings
```

**Amending:** change the line, then recalculate every line below it. Lines above do not change.

**Quick checks (base: revenue 5,000; cost of sales 3,000; expenses 1,400; tax 20%; dividends 200):**

- Base: gross profit 2,000; profit from operations 600; tax 120; profit for the year 480; retained 280.
- Expenses +100: profit from operations 500; tax 100; profit for the year 400; retained 200.
- Dividends +100: profit for the year still 480; retained 180.

## Statement of financial position: layout

```
Non-current assets
+ Current assets (inventory, trade receivables, cash)
- Current liabilities
  (Net current assets = current assets - current liabilities)
- Non-current liabilities
= Net assets
= Equity (issued shares + reserves)
```

**Every transaction has two effects.**

| Transaction | Effects | Net assets |
|---|---|---|
| Asset bought for cash | Non-current assets up; cash down | Same |
| Asset bought with a loan | Non-current assets up; non-current liabilities up | Same |
| Inventory bought on credit | Inventory up; current liabilities up | Same |
| Customer pays | Cash up; receivables down | Same |
| Shares issued for cash | Cash up; issued shares up | Up |
| Loan repaid | Cash down; non-current liabilities down | Same |
| Profit retained | Assets up (usually cash); reserves up | Up |

## Links between the statements

- Retained earnings → reserves: closing reserves = opening reserves + retained earnings.
- Closing inventory: a current asset and part of cost of sales.
- Credit sales → trade receivables; credit purchases → trade payables.
- Tax charged → current liability until paid.
- Depreciation → expense; lowers net book value of non-current assets.
- Interest on loans → expense; loans in non-current liabilities.

## Inventory valuation

**Difficulties:** damage, obsolescence, fashion change, price changes, counting large or varied stock, valuing part-finished goods.

**NRV** = selling price − costs to complete and sell. Value at the **lower of cost and NRV**, item by item.

**Effect of a write-down:** cost of sales up → gross profit down by the same amount; current assets down by the same amount.

Example: 50 jackets, cost $40 each, sell for $45 but need $10 of repair each. NRV = $35; value 50 × 35 = $1,750 instead of $2,000; write-down $250.

## Depreciation

- Role: spreads an asset's cost over its useful life; matches cost to the revenue earned.
- Not a cash payment; not a replacement fund.
- Straight-line: (cost − residual value) ÷ useful life.
- Statement of profit or loss: annual charge is an expense, lowering profit.
- Statement of financial position: net book value = cost − accumulated depreciation.
- Longer estimated life or higher residual value → smaller charge → higher profit.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Revenue | Value of sales in the period |
| Cost of sales | Direct cost of the goods sold |
| Profit from operations | Gross profit minus expenses |
| Retained earnings | Profit for the year kept after dividends |
| Net current assets | Current assets minus current liabilities |
| Net assets | Total assets minus total liabilities; equals equity |
| Reserves | Accumulated retained earnings and other owners' funds beyond issued shares |
| Net realisable value | Selling price less costs to complete and sell |
| Net book value | Cost minus accumulated depreciation |

## Exam traps

- Not recalculating every line below a change.
- Treating dividends as an expense before profit for the year.
- Recording one side of a transaction only.
- Comparing cost and NRV for total stock instead of each item.
- Saying depreciation is cash put aside.
- Mixing $000 and dollars in one answer.

## Self-test

1. State the purpose of the statement of financial position.
2. Write the lines of the statement of profit or loss from revenue to retained earnings.
3. Revenue 2,400; cost of sales 1,500; expenses 600; tax 25%. Calculate profit for the year.
4. In question 3, dividends are 100. Calculate retained earnings.
5. A business buys a machine with a bank loan. State the two effects on the statement of financial position.
6. Which transaction in the table above increases net assets without any profit?
7. Opening reserves 900; profit for the year 300; dividends 120. Calculate closing reserves.
8. Give two difficulties of valuing inventory.
9. Goods cost $20 each and sell for $22 after $5 of packaging each. Calculate NRV per unit and the value used.
10. A vehicle cost $36,000, residual value $6,000, life 5 years. Calculate annual depreciation and net book value after 2 years.
11. What happens to profit if the estimated life of an asset is increased?
12. Why can a profitable business run short of cash?

### Answers

1. To show what the business owns and owes, and the owners' stake, on one date.
2. Revenue, cost of sales, gross profit, expenses, profit from operations, taxation, profit for the year, dividends, retained earnings.
3. Gross profit 900; profit from operations 300; tax 75; profit for the year **225**.
4. 225 − 100 = **125**.
5. Non-current assets rise; non-current liabilities rise.
6. Issuing shares for cash.
7. 900 + (300 − 120) = **1,080**.
8. Any two: damage, obsolescence, changing prices, counting large stock, valuing part-finished goods.
9. NRV = 22 − 5 = $17; lower than cost, so value **$17** per unit.
10. (36,000 − 6,000) ÷ 5 = **$6,000** a year; NBV = 36,000 − 12,000 = **$24,000**.
11. The annual charge falls, so profit rises.
12. Customers may pay late, cash may be tied up in inventory, or it may have spent on non-current assets.
