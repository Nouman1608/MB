## Questions

**1.** A root tip cell is 42 mm long in a photomicrograph. Its actual length is 70 µm. Calculate the magnification of the photomicrograph. **[3]**

**2.** Describe how to make and view a temporary slide of onion epidermis. **[4]**

**3.** State three features of a good labelled scientific drawing of cells. **[3]**

**4.** A food sample is tested. Iodine solution stays orange-brown, Benedict's reagent stays blue after heating, biuret reagent turns purple and the ethanol test gives a clear liquid.

**(a)** Identify the biological molecule present and give the evidence. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe how to carry out the Benedict's test safely. **[3]**

**5.** A student investigates amylase digesting starch using continuous sampling.

**(a)** Describe how the end point is found. **[3]**
**(b)** The end point is reached at 270 s. Calculate the rate as 1 ÷ time, to 2 significant figures. **[1]**

**6.** A lawn of area 80 m² is sampled with a 0.25 m² quadrat for daisies.

**(a)** Describe how to place the quadrats randomly. **[2]**
**(b)** Counts in eight quadrats were 2, 7, 4, 0, 5, 3, 6 and 1. Estimate the number of daisies on the lawn. **[3]**

**7.** In a pond, 30 beetles are caught, marked and released. A second sample has 40 beetles, of which 8 are marked.

**(a)** Estimate the population. **[2]**
**(b)** State two assumptions made. **[2]**

**8.** A transect runs from a hedge across a field. At 2 m intervals from 0 m to 10 m, light intensity was 300, 900, 1800, 2900, 3800 and 4100 lux, and moss plants per quadrat were 14, 11, 7, 4, 2 and 1.

**(a)** Describe how moss numbers change with distance from the hedge. **[1]**
**(b)** State the relationship between light intensity and moss numbers. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the data do not prove that light intensity affects moss. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest one other abiotic factor to measure and how. **[1]**
**(e)** Suggest one improvement to make the results more representative. **[1]**

**9.** Catalase is added to hydrogen peroxide. Oxygen volumes were 0, 14, 25, 33, 38, 40 and 40 cm³ at 0, 20, 40, 60, 80, 100 and 120 s.

**(a)** Calculate the initial rate over the first 20 s, with units. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the rate between 60 s and 80 s. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the rate decreases and reaches zero. **[2]**
**(d)** Name apparatus that measures the gas volume more precisely than a measuring cylinder in a trough. **[1]**

**10.** Times for iodine to stop turning blue-black with amylase at five temperatures (three repeats each): 20 °C: 124, 118, 121 s; 30 °C: 66, 64, 68 s; 40 °C: 35, 150, 33 s; 50 °C: 52, 50, 54 s; 60 °C: no change in 300 s.

**(a)** Pick out the odd result and find the mean time at 40 °C without it. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate 1 ÷ mean time at 30 °C, to 2 significant figures. **[1]**
**(c)** State the best estimate of the optimum temperature and how to improve it. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain the result at 60 °C. **[2]**

**11.** A potometer bubble moves 36 mm in 5 minutes along a capillary of radius 0.6 mm.

**(a)** Calculate the volume of water taken up, in mm³ to 3 significant figures. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the rate in mm³ per minute, to 2 significant figures. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the shoot is cut under water. **[2]**

**12.** Plan an investigation of how pH affects amylase breaking down starch, using a colour change to follow the reaction. Include variables, apparatus, safety and how you would present results. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** 42 mm = 42 000 µm [1]. magnification = image size ÷ actual size = 42 000 ÷ 70 [1]. **×600** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Writing the formula and converting to one unit before dividing is what keeps a magnification answer from being off by a factor of 1000.

**2.** Lay a thin piece of epidermis flat on a drop of water or stain on a slide [1]. Lower a cover slip slowly from one edge so no air is trapped [1]. Begin on the shortest objective and use the large then the small focus wheel [1]. Centre the specimen before changing to a longer objective [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A four-mark description needs four distinct actions in order; "put it under the microscope" alone adds little.

**3.** Any three of: sharp pencil, single clear lines and no shading [1]; ruled labels that end on the structure and never intersect [1]; title with magnification or a scale bar [1]; large and in proportion. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Vague words such as "neat" are less useful than a specific, checkable feature.

**4. (a)** **Protein** [1], because biuret gave purple while the others were negative [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Place the sample and Benedict's reagent in a boiling tube [1]. Heat in a hot water bath, not directly in a flame, wearing eye protection and using a holder [1]. Look for a colour change from blue and compare it with a colour chart [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Give both the result and the reason; a bare "protein" answer leaves out the evidence the question asks for.

**5. (a)** Put drops of iodine in a spotting tile [1]. Every 30 s move a drop of the reaction mixture into a fresh well [1]. The end point is the time when the iodine stays orange-brown, showing no starch [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** 1 ÷ 270 = **0.0037 s⁻¹** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Say what the colour shows, not just "it changes colour", and give the unit with a rate.

**6. (a)** Mark a grid with two tape measures [1]. Use random numbers as the coordinates for each quadrat [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Total = 28, mean = 28 ÷ 8 = 3.5 per quadrat [1]. 3.5 ÷ 0.25 = 14 per m² [1]. 14 × 80 = **1120 daisies** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each stage of the scaling up; showing each stage lets a marker see where a slip happened.

**7. (a)** 30 × 40 = 1200 [1]. 1200 ÷ 8 = **150 beetles** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Any two of: marks do not harm or wash off [1]; marked beetles mix back with the population [1]; no births, deaths or migration in the period. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Assumptions must be about the method; "the count was accurate" is too general.

**8. (a)** Moss numbers fall as distance from the hedge increases [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** As light intensity increases, moss numbers decrease (a negative correlation) [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Other factors, such as soil moisture, also change along the transect, so correlation does not show cause [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** Soil moisture, using a moisture meter at each quadrat (or soil pH with a probe) [1]. **[1]**
**(e)** Repeat the transect or add more quadrats at each distance [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote the data, with direction and trend, rather than saying "it changes".

**9. (a)** 14 ÷ 20 [1] = **0.70 cm³/s** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** (38 − 33) ÷ 20 = **0.25 cm³/s** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Substrate is used up, so its concentration falls [1]; fewer collisions with active sites, and the rate is zero when no substrate remains [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** **Gas syringe** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the rate change to substrate concentration; saying the enzyme "gets tired" does not explain it.

**10. (a)** 150 s does not fit the pattern [1]. Mean = (35 + 33) ÷ 2 = **34 s** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Mean at 30 °C = 66 s; 1 ÷ 66 = **0.015 s⁻¹** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** About 40 °C, the shortest time [1]. Test more temperatures between 30 °C and 50 °C, such as 35 °C and 45 °C [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** High temperature breaks bonds and changes the shape of the active site [1]. The substrate no longer fits, so the enzyme is denatured and starch is not digested [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Exclude the anomaly before averaging, and say that you have done so.

**11. (a)** Area = π × 0.6² = 1.131 mm² [1]. Volume = area × length = 1.131 × 36 [1]. **40.7 mm³** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** 40.7 ÷ 5 = **8.1 mm³ per minute** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Cutting under water stops air entering the xylem [1]. Air would break the water column and stop uptake, so the bubble would not reflect water uptake [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the unrounded volume in the next step and round only at the end.

**12.** Any nine of: independent variable is pH, using buffer solutions at several pH values [1]; dependent variable is time for starch to disappear, found with iodine [1]; use continuous sampling every 30 s on a spotting tile [1]; keep temperature constant in a water bath [1]; same volume and concentration of amylase and starch [1]; repeat each pH and calculate a mean, leaving out anomalies [1]; safety: eye protection, avoid iodine on skin and take care with hot water [1]; table with pH in the first column and units in headings [1]; graph with pH on the x-axis and rate (1 ÷ time) on the y-axis [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Cover variables, method, safety and presentation; long answers that leave out controls are less complete.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not converting mm to µm before dividing in magnification questions.
- Naming a test result without the starting colour or the heating step.
- Heating Benedict's directly in a flame in a description.
- Writing "random" for quadrat placement without saying how.
- Forgetting to multiply the mean per m² by the total area.
- Treating a correlation as proof of cause.
- Leaving out units from rates, such as cm³/s.
- Including an anomalous result in a mean.
- Not saying how variables were controlled.
