These notes condense section 12.4, The management of a degraded environment, of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Geography (9696) syllabus for 2025 and 2026 (Paper 4, Topic 12 option). The full case study is in the [12.4 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-geography-9696-the-management-of-a-degraded-environment/); test yourself afterwards with the [12.4 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-geography-9696-the-management-of-a-degraded-environment-practice/).

## What the syllabus demands

One degraded environment, showing:

1. the **causes** of its degradation;
2. the **problems faced**;
3. **issues in attempts to improve** it;
4. an **evaluation** of the attempted solutions.

Problems are consequences of degradation. Issues are difficulties in fixing it. Keep them apart.

## Case-study template

| Heading | Notes to prepare |
|---|---|
| Location and nature | Named place, scale, why it matters |
| Causes | Ranked human and physical causes |
| Problems | Effects on health, livelihoods, ecosystems |
| Attempted solutions | Named programmes, dates, who led them |
| Issues | Cost, governance, conflict, physical limits, maintenance |
| Evaluation | Criteria-based judgement by place, group and time |

## River Ganga in brief

**Nature:** about 2 500 km from the Himalayas to the Bay of Bengal; basin home to several hundred million people; cities include Kanpur, Prayagraj, Varanasi, Patna, Kolkata; sacred river; declared national river in 2008.

**Causes (ranked):**

1. Untreated sewage from fast-growing towns and cities (largest source).
2. Industrial effluent, especially tanneries around Kanpur; also sugar mills, distilleries, paper mills.
3. Agricultural run-off from the Gangetic plain (diffuse).
4. Reduced dry-season flow from dams, barrages and canals, so less dilution.
5. Solid waste, offerings and riverside cremation in places.

**Problems:** faecal bacteria above bathing standards in many stretches; waterborne disease; low dissolved oxygen below cities; harm to fisheries; threats to the Ganges river dolphin (national aquatic animal since 2009); chromium and other metals.

**Attempted solutions:**

- Ganga Action Plan (from the mid-1980s): treatment plants, drain interception.
- National Ganga River Basin Authority (2009): basin-wide management.
- Namami Gange (from 2014): treatment plants and sewers, ghats and riverfronts, improved crematoria, industrial monitoring, riverside tree planting.
- Pollution control boards and courts: orders to install effluent treatment or close.

**Issues:** treatment capacity behind sewage growth; plants under-used (power cuts, missing sewer connections, running costs); responsibility split across central government, five main states and many cities; industry resistance because of jobs and exports; low flows; cultural sensitivity.

**Evaluation:** capacity and coverage up; some stretches cleaner; many stretches below large cities still polluted; building plants easier than running them; partial success.

## Comparison: Aral Sea and Ganga

- Aral: quantity problem; narrow cause (irrigation); single-country dike worked for the North Aral.
- Ganga: quality problem; many diffuse and point sources; shared authority slows progress.
- Lesson: narrow causes and single authorities make success easier.

## Evaluation criteria

| Criterion | Short test |
|---|---|
| Effectiveness | Did indicators improve? |
| Durability | Did gains last? |
| Cost | Affordable, and who paid? |
| Equity | Who gained, who lost? |
| Scale | Right scale for the causes? |
| Root causes | Causes or only symptoms? |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Degraded environment | A place whose environmental quality has seriously declined |
| Sewage treatment plant | Works that remove solids, organic matter and pathogens from waste water |
| Drain interception | Diverting drains to treatment instead of the river |
| Effluent | Liquid waste discharged from industry or treatment works |
| Faecal coliforms | Bacteria indicating sewage contamination |
| Dilution | Reduction of pollutant concentration by river flow |
| Abstraction | Taking water from a river or aquifer |
| Basin management | Managing a river as one system across administrative borders |
| Remediation | Restoring contaminated land or water |
| Root cause | The underlying driver, not the visible symptom |
| Stakeholder | Group affected by or influencing management |
| Operation and maintenance | Ongoing running and repair of infrastructure |

## Exam traps

- Choosing too large a "place"; name one river, lake or district.
- Mixing up problems and issues.
- Describing programmes without judging them.
- One verdict for the whole river: vary by stretch.
- Forgetting physical causes such as low dry-season flow.
- Precise figures you are unsure of: leave them out.

## Self-test

1. What four things must a 12.4 case study show?
2. How do "problems" differ from "issues"?
3. What is the largest source of pollution in the Ganga?
4. Which city is known for tannery pollution?
5. Why does reduced flow worsen pollution?
6. Name two problems caused by the river's degradation.
7. When did the Ganga Action Plan begin?
8. What did the 2009 authority aim to do differently?
9. Name three types of work funded by Namami Gange.
10. Give two reasons some treatment plants have worked below capacity.
11. Why is coordination difficult on the Ganga?
12. Give one lesson from comparing the Ganga and the Aral Sea.

### Answers

1. Causes, problems faced, issues in attempts to improve, evaluation of solutions.
2. Problems are effects of degradation; issues are difficulties in improving it.
3. Untreated sewage.
4. Kanpur.
5. Less water to dilute pollutants, so concentrations rise.
6. Any two: unsafe bathing and drinking water, waterborne disease, low oxygen, fish loss, threat to river dolphins, toxic metals.
7. In the mid-1980s.
8. Manage the whole basin rather than separate towns.
9. Any three: treatment plants, sewers, ghats and riverfronts, crematoria, industrial monitoring, tree planting.
10. Any two: power cuts, missing sewer connections, lack of running funds.
11. Responsibility is shared by central government, five main states and many cities.
12. Narrow causes and a single authority make success easier; diffuse causes and shared authority make it harder.
