This page is a guide to AQA GCSE Psychology (8182) past papers for the June 2026, June 2025 and June 2024 series. It gives the grade thresholds copied from AQA's own grade-boundary documents, and a short account of what examiners reported, written by us from AQA's Reports on the Examination. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on AQA's own site, linked below.

## Where to get the official papers

- [AQA GCSE Psychology (8182) specification](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/psychology/gcse/psychology-8182/specification)
- [AQA GCSE Psychology (8182) assessment resources](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/psychology/gcse/psychology-8182/assessment-resources)
- [AQA grade boundaries](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries) and the [grade boundaries archive](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries/archive)

The assessment resources page lists question papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination for each series. Use the three documents together. Sit a paper first, under timed conditions. Mark it strictly with the mark scheme, which shows what each mark needs. Then read the report for that paper: it explains why answers that looked reasonable still lost marks. The notes for each series below are a quick way into that report.

Check which specification a paper was set on. 8182 is a linear, untiered qualification, first taught from September 2017 and first examined in June 2019. It has two written papers, each 1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks and 50% of the GCSE: Paper 1 (8182/1) Cognition and behaviour, and Paper 2 (8182/2) Social context and behaviour. There is no coursework. The June 2024 and June 2025 reports describe themselves as the sixth and seventh series of the current specification, so every paper covered here was set on the same specification.

## June 2025

The reports describe a well-prepared entry. On Paper 1, multiple-choice and maths questions went well, describing named studies was often detailed, and applying knowledge to a scenario had improved on the year before. On Paper 2, short factual questions and descriptions of studies were secure. The common weakness in both papers was exam technique: misreading the question, ignoring the command word, and leaning on generic points instead of the study or scenario in front of the candidate. The synoptic question was among the weakest on each paper.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1, memory: retrieval defined as recalling from the brain instead of from a memory store; the long evaluation of a named memory study padded with points that could apply to any study, and a long description crowding out the evaluation; short answers where a point was made but not developed.
- Paper 1, perception and development: the application part of the Gibson question left as a restatement of the stem; monocular depth cues confused with size constancy; self-efficacy defined loosely, and mindset theory written up as open versus closed instead of growth versus fixed.
- Paper 1, research methods: no creditworthy definition of a case study; scatter diagram titles that did not name the relationship between the two variables; uncertainty about what correlations can and cannot show, including claims that they show cause and effect.
- Paper 1, synoptic question: laboratory or field experiments described, rather than both, and a published experiment named but never used in the discussion.
- Paper 2, research methods: the experimental design confused with the research method, null hypotheses rarely written correctly, and a dependent variable that was not measurable (for example "better" or "the level of conformity"); field experiments wrongly described as cheaper.
- Paper 2, language, thought and communication: neonate defined by describing research rather than saying what a neonate is; postural echo muddled with open and closed posture; Darwin's theory of non-verbal communication linked only loosely to survival and adaptation; any answer in words treated as qualitative data.
- Paper 2, brain and neuropsychology: the lobes question missing either a structure or a function; the brain scan comparison stopping at differences; the neuron diagram completed only in part; a design-a-study answer built on a simple repeated memory task instead of learning a new skill.
- Paper 2, psychological problems: social effects of depression and anxiety replaced by individual symptoms; aversion therapy described rather than evaluated, with ethics argued from harm instead of informed consent; twin results stated as rates of the behaviour instead of rates of matching.
- Paper 2, synoptic question: little understanding of how synaptic transmission works, and a discussion of neurotransmitters in depression drifting into antidepressant treatment.

## June 2024

Examiners found Paper 1 comparable with the year before. Small maths questions, graph work and describing a study were handled well. The recurring complaint was application: answers that did not use the details of the scenario, and evaluation that was generic. Paper 2 showed strong factual knowledge in definition questions and a gradual improvement in answering the synoptic question, which combined social influence with research methods.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1, memory and perception: a primacy effect explained without mentioning later items in the list; evaluation of a reconstructive memory study made of general points with no link to the study; objects said to "move" rather than appear to move when explaining a depth cue; a binocular depth cue not identified; a perception experiment evaluated with generic claims such as "it proves hunger changes perception".
- Paper 1, development: Dweck's theory described when the question asked for an evaluation; the synoptic question on early brain development answered with material on pregnancy risks or Piaget, and a discussion of Penfield's method that ignored the age range in the question.
- Paper 1, research methods: the term "alternative hypothesis" confused with aims, null hypotheses or ideas for future studies; a finding and a conclusion treated as the same thing; graph titles that described an effect instead of the correlation; interview types not named, or job interviews described.
- Paper 2, social influence and research methods: a two-word term defined with only one of its words explained; validity and reliability only half covered; evaluation of Milgram's study offered where the question asked about his theory.
- Paper 2, language, thought and communication and brain and neuropsychology: studies criticised for not testing every possible factor, which is not a sound evaluation point; correlation read as cause and effect, or data picked from a table instead of reasoning about a third factor; neurological damage explained without saying how it affects behaviour or movement.
- Paper 2, psychological problems: a rise in recorded mental health problems confused with a fall in discrimination; self-management programmes assumed to mean managing an addiction alone; a six-mark answer with description and evaluation but no application, which capped the mark.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find AQA GCSE Psychology 8182 past papers?**
AQA lists question papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination on the 8182 assessment resources page. Grade boundaries are on its grade boundaries page and archive.

**Do both papers count equally?**
Yes. Each paper is 100 marks and 50% of the GCSE, and the grade is decided on the total out of 200. The paper-by-paper boundaries AQA publishes are notional and only a rough guide.

**What do examiners say candidates should do about generic evaluation?**
Across 2024 and 2025 the reports say to tie every evaluation point to the named study or scenario. A general strength of laboratory experiments earns little when the question is about one particular study.

**Why do hypotheses and variables lose so many marks?**
The reports say a dependent variable must be measurable, and that "alternative" and "null" hypotheses are different things. Practise writing both in operational terms.

**Can grade thresholds tell me what mark I need next time?**
No. Thresholds change every series and should only be used as a rough guide for the series they belong to, never to predict future ones.
