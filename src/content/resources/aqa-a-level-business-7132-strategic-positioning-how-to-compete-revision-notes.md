Condensed notes on section **3.8.2 Strategic positioning: choosing how to compete** of AQA A-level Business (7132, for first teaching from September 2023). Section 3.8 is A-level only. The footwear comparison is in the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-strategic-positioning-how-to-compete/); questions are in the [practice set](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-strategic-positioning-how-to-compete-practice/).

## Specification content

- How to compete in terms of benefits and price; strategic positioning to include Porter's low cost, differentiation and focus strategies.
- Influences on the choice of a positioning strategy.
- The value of different strategic positioning strategies.
- The benefits of having a competitive advantage.
- The difficulties of maintaining a competitive advantage.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Strategic positioning | Choosing how to win customers: on cost, uniqueness or by serving a niche |
| Cost leadership (low cost) | Being the lowest-cost producer in the industry |
| Differentiation | Offering something customers value as unique, supporting a premium price |
| Focus | Concentrating on a narrow segment, on cost or differentiation |
| Stuck in the middle | Neither lowest cost nor clearly different, with poor returns |
| Competitive advantage | A lasting edge over rivals in winning customers or profit |
| Premium price | A price above the market average, justified by extra benefits |

## Porter's generic strategies

| | Lower cost | Uniqueness |
|---|---|---|
| Broad market | Cost leadership | Differentiation |
| Narrow segment | Cost focus | Differentiation focus |

## The three strategies

| Strategy | How | Benefits | Risks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low cost | Scale, standardisation, tight control, efficient technology, bulk buying | Survives price wars; barriers to entry; strong against buyers and suppliers | Rival with lower cost; quality neglected; easily copied |
| Differentiation | Quality, design, brand, service, innovation | Higher margin; loyalty; less price sensitivity | Premium may not cover costs; imitation; weak in recession |
| Focus | Serve a niche better | Deep customer knowledge; less rivalry with large firms | Small or shrinking niche; larger rivals may follow |

## Margins in context

| Firm type | Typical profit model |
|---|---|
| Low cost | Low margin, high volume |
| Differentiation | High margin, lower volume |
| Stuck in the middle | Low margin and low volume |

Example: price £40, cost £34, 100,000 units gives £600,000 (margin 15%). Price £70, cost £55, 40,000 units gives £600,000 (margin 21.4%).

## Influences on the choice

Customer priorities; market structure; resources and competences; competitors' positions; industry economics (fixed costs, scale); technology; owners' objectives; the economic climate.

## Benefits of competitive advantage

Higher margins or share; customer loyalty; ability to attract finance and staff; stronger against buyers, suppliers and entrants; freedom to invest.

## Difficulties of maintaining it

Imitation; technological change; changing customer tastes; complacency; new entrants and global competition; cost of keeping ahead; loss of staff and know-how.

**Lasting advantages** are hard to copy: patents, brands built over time, core competences, economies of scale.

## Model analysis paragraph

"Aldric & Rowe follows a differentiation strategy. It charges £70 against a cost of £55 and earns a margin of 21.4%, compared with 15.0% for the low-cost firm. Its strength is that customers pay for the brand and design, which protects it from price wars. The risk is that costs rise or customers trade down in a recession; a 10% rise in cost cuts annual profit from £600,000 to £380,000. The strategy is sound only while customers still value the difference enough to pay the premium."

## Choosing a strategy: questions to ask

1. What do customers value most: price, quality or fit to their needs?
2. Does the firm have the scale and processes to be the cheapest?
3. Does it have the brand, design or service to command a premium?
4. Is there a niche that large rivals ignore?
5. How easily can rivals copy the advantage?

## Command words

- **Analyse:** identify the strategy from evidence, explain the effect on profit and risk.
- **Evaluate:** weigh strengths against risks, link to the firm's resources, judge sustainability.

## Exam traps

- Low cost is not the same as low price.
- Differentiation does not guarantee a high price; customers must value the difference.
- Focus is about a narrow target, not about being a small firm.
- Say how the advantage might be lost, not just how it is won.
- Link the strategy to the business's resources and customers.

## Self-test

1. Name Porter's three generic strategies.
2. State two ways a business can reduce its costs to become a cost leader.
3. State two ways a business can differentiate its product.
4. What is meant by "stuck in the middle"?
5. A firm sells 50,000 units at £30 with a unit cost of £24. Calculate its profit and margin.
6. Why might a differentiation strategy be hard to maintain in a recession?
7. Give one risk of a focus strategy.
8. Give two benefits of competitive advantage.
9. Give two difficulties in keeping a competitive advantage.
10. Why are patents a strong source of lasting advantage?
11. Why might a low-cost strategy fail if a rival moves production overseas?
12. Give two influences on the choice of strategic positioning.

### Answers

1. Low cost (cost leadership), differentiation, focus.
2. Any two: economies of scale; standardised products; tight cost control; efficient technology; bulk buying; low-cost locations.
3. Any two: quality; design; brand image; innovation; service; reliability.
4. A business with neither the lowest cost nor a distinct difference, which cannot win on price or on value.
5. Profit = (30 − 24) × 50,000 = **£300,000**; margin = 6 ÷ 30 × 100 = **20%**.
6. Customers cut spending and trade down to cheaper products, so they may no longer pay the premium.
7. Any one: niche too small; niche shrinks; large rivals enter; tastes change.
8. Any two: higher margin; loyalty; ability to attract finance and staff; stronger position against buyers, suppliers and entrants.
9. Any two: imitation; technological change; changing tastes; complacency; new entrants.
10. They legally prevent others from copying a product for a period.
11. The rival's costs might fall below the firm's, letting it undercut on price and win the cost advantage.
12. Any two: customer priorities; the firm's resources; competitors' positions; market structure; technology.
