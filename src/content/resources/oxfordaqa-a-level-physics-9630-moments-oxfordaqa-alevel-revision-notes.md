These notes condense **3.2.2 Moments** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630) specification. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 1. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [moments study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-moments-oxfordaqa-alevel/); for exam-style questions, use the [moments practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-moments-oxfordaqa-alevel-practice/).

## The specification in one box

- Moment of a force about a point = force × perpendicular distance from the point to the line of action of the force.
- Couple: a pair of equal and opposite coplanar forces.
- Moment of a couple = force × perpendicular distance between the lines of action.
- Principle of moments.
- Centre of mass; for a uniform regular solid it is at the centre.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Moment | Turning effect of a force about a point: F × perpendicular distance |
| Line of action | The line along which a force acts, extended both ways |
| Pivot | The point about which moments are taken |
| Couple | Two equal, opposite, parallel forces not along the same line |
| Principle of moments | In equilibrium, total clockwise moment = total anticlockwise moment about any point |
| Centre of mass | Point at which the whole mass can be taken to act |
| Uniform | Mass spread evenly, so the centre of mass is at the geometric centre |

## Formulae

| Quantity | Equation | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Moment | F × d⊥ | N m |
| Moment with angle θ between force and lever of length L | F L sin θ | N m |
| Couple | F × d (separation of lines of action) | N m |

## Method: solving a balance problem

1. Draw every force, including the object's own weight at its centre of mass.
2. Choose a pivot where an unknown force acts, so it has no moment.
3. List clockwise and anticlockwise moments, each with its perpendicular distance.
4. Equate them and solve.
5. Use "upward forces = downward forces" (and horizontal balance) for any remaining unknown.
6. Sense-check: is each answer positive and the right size?

## Equilibrium of an extended object

| Condition | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Resultant force = 0 | No acceleration of the centre of mass |
| Resultant moment = 0 | No change in rotation |

A couple has zero resultant force but a non-zero moment, so it turns an object without moving it bodily.

## Centre of mass facts

- Uniform rod: at its midpoint. Uniform cuboid, sphere or cylinder: at its centre.
- Freely suspended: comes to rest with the centre of mass directly below the suspension point (the weight then has no moment about it).
- Finding it: hang the object from two points in turn and mark the vertical with a plumb line; the lines cross at the centre of mass.
- Toppling: happens when the line of action of the weight passes outside the base.
- A low centre of mass and a wide base make an object harder to topple.

## Worked reminders

- **Lever at an angle.** 45 N at 30° to a 0.30 m spanner: 45 × 0.30 × sin 30° = 6.75 N m.
- **Seesaw.** A 300 N child sits 1.5 m from the pivot; a 450 N child balances at 300 × 1.5 ÷ 450 = 1.0 m on the other side.
- **Metre rule.** A uniform 1.2 N rule pivoted at the 30 cm mark: its weight acts at 50 cm, 0.20 m from the pivot. A weight W at the 0 cm mark balances it when W × 0.30 = 1.2 × 0.20, so W = 0.80 N.
- **Couple.** Two 12 N forces with lines of action 0.25 m apart: 12 × 0.25 = 3.0 N m.

## Exam traps

- Distance along the lever used instead of perpendicular distance.
- Moment written in J.
- Couple calculated as 2 × F × d.
- Beam weight placed at the end, or left out.
- Principle of moments stated without "in equilibrium" or "about any point".
- "Force × distance" offered as the definition of moment.

## Quick self-test

1. Define the moment of a force.
2. State the SI unit of moment.
3. A 45 N force acts at right angles to a lever, 0.30 m from the pivot. Find its moment.
4. The same force acts at 30° to the same lever. Find its moment.
5. Find the moment of a couple of two 12 N forces whose lines of action are 0.25 m apart.
6. State the principle of moments.
7. State the two conditions for an extended object to be in equilibrium.
8. A 300 N child sits 1.5 m from a seesaw pivot. Where must a 450 N child sit to balance?
9. A uniform rule of weight 1.2 N is pivoted at the 30 cm mark. What weight at the 0 cm mark balances it?
10. Where is the centre of mass of a uniform cuboid?
11. Why does a freely hanging object come to rest with its centre of mass below the pivot?
12. When does a box on a tilted surface topple?

### Answers

1. Force × perpendicular distance from the point to the line of action of the force.
2. **N m**.
3. 45 × 0.30 = **13.5 N m**.
4. 45 × 0.30 × sin 30° = **6.75 N m**.
5. 12 × 0.25 = **3.0 N m**.
6. For an object in equilibrium, the sum of clockwise moments about any point equals the sum of anticlockwise moments about that point.
7. **Resultant force zero** and **resultant moment zero**.
8. 300 × 1.5 ÷ 450 = **1.0 m** on the other side.
9. **0.80 N**.
10. At its **geometric centre**.
11. Only then does the line of action of its weight pass through the pivot, so the weight has **no moment** to turn it.
12. When the **line of action of its weight passes outside the base**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not stating which point moments are taken about.
- Mixing clockwise and anticlockwise terms on one side of the equation.
- Leaving out the horizontal component of a hinge force when a cable pulls at an angle.
