Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620 is assessed in two tiers plus a practical component. Core candidates take Paper 1 Multiple Choice (Core) and Paper 3 Theory (Core); Extended candidates take Paper 2 Multiple Choice (Extended) and Paper 4 Theory (Extended). Candidates of either tier then sit one of Paper 5 Practical Test or Paper 6 Alternative to Practical. The multiple-choice papers are 45 minutes and 40 marks (30%), the theory papers 1 hour 15 minutes and 80 marks (50%), and the practical options each carry 40 marks and 20%. Pages such as [Atomic Structure](/resources/atomic-structure/), [Acids, Bases and Salts](/resources/acids-bases-and-salts/) and [Electrolysis and Fuel Cells](/resources/electrolysis-and-fuel-cells/) cover content areas that all of these papers draw on.

## Which papers are yours?

First confirm your tier. The paper numbers differ between tiers but the structure is parallel.

| Route | Multiple choice | Theory | Practical (one of) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core | Paper 1: 45 min, 40 marks, 30% | Paper 3: 75 min, 80 marks, 50% | Paper 5 or Paper 6 |
| Extended | Paper 2: 45 min, 40 marks, 30% | Paper 4: 75 min, 80 marks, 50% | Paper 5 or Paper 6 |

The syllabus makes a practical component compulsory, but you sit only one of the two options, and only that one counts towards your total. Ask your teacher well before the exam which of Paper 5 or Paper 6 you are entered for.

## Multiple choice (Paper 1 for Core, Paper 2 for Extended)

Each multiple-choice paper is 45 minutes for 40 marks, which is 45 / 40 = 1.125 minutes, about 67 seconds, per mark. That pace leaves little room for long calculations, so the skill is speed with accuracy. **Preparation priority**: practise short stoichiometry and reasoning questions under time pressure, eliminate options systematically, and flag any question that needs more than about a minute and a quarter. Returning to flagged questions at the end is better than losing time early.

## Theory (Paper 3 for Core, Paper 4 for Extended)

These are the heaviest papers, at 50% each. With 75 minutes for 80 marks, you have 75 / 80 = 0.9375 minutes per mark, just under a minute. That is a tight rate, so answers must be concise and aimed at the mark scheme: one correct, specific point per mark. **Preparation priority**: rehearse writing short, precise sentences that use correct terminology, and practise balanced equations with state symbols where the question asks for them. For the content, work through all twelve areas, from states of matter to experimental techniques and chemical analysis; the theory papers can use any of them.

## Practical (Paper 5 or Paper 6)

Paper 5 Practical Test is 75 minutes for 40 marks, a generous 75 / 40 = 1.875 minutes per mark, because you must carry out experiments, record observations and answer questions. Paper 6 Alternative to Practical is a written paper of 60 minutes for 40 marks, or 60 / 40 = 1.5 minutes per mark. Both are worth 20% of the total. **Preparation priority**: whichever you are entered for, learn the standard techniques and tests so that you can describe, interpret and evaluate them. Practise reading tables of results, drawing graphs, naming apparatus and suggesting improvements to a method.

## Totals and time

A Core candidate sitting Paper 5 spends 45 + 75 + 75 = 195 minutes in the exam room; with Paper 6 it is 45 + 75 + 60 = 180 minutes. The same totals apply to Extended candidates. In both cases the marks are 40 + 80 + 40 = 160.

## Command words and question styles

Typical command words in chemistry include state, name, describe, explain, calculate, deduce, suggest and predict. "Suggest" and "deduce" ask you to apply knowledge to an unfamiliar context, and these are the questions where a memorised answer does not help. **Preparation priority**: for each topic, practise one unfamiliar application, such as a new reaction that follows a pattern you know.

## Worked practice scenario: gas volume from a reaction

Question (original): "4.00 g of calcium carbonate, CaCO3, reacts completely with excess dilute hydrochloric acid. CaCO3 + 2HCl -> CaCl2 + H2O + CO2. Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide produced at room temperature and pressure. (Ar: Ca = 40, C = 12, O = 16; one mole of any gas occupies 24 dm3 at room temperature and pressure.)"

```
Step 1  Mr of CaCO3 = 40 + 12 + (3 x 16) = 100
Step 2  Moles of CaCO3 = 4.00 / 100 = 0.0400 mol
Step 3  Mole ratio CaCO3 : CO2 = 1 : 1, so moles CO2 = 0.0400 mol
Step 4  Volume = 0.0400 x 24 = 0.96 dm3  (960 cm3)
```

Show each step. Even if a slip occurs in Step 4, the earlier steps can earn method marks.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Confirm your tier (Core: Papers 1 and 3; Extended: Papers 2 and 4).
- Confirm whether you sit Paper 5 or Paper 6.
- Practise multiple-choice sets against the clock.
- Check which data and equations you must recall for your examination year.
- Write balanced equations and units every time.
- On theory papers, match the number of points to the marks.
- In practical papers, record readings to a consistent number of decimal places.
- Leave a couple of minutes to recheck calculations.

## Frequently asked questions

**What papers are there in Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620?**
Core candidates take Papers 1 and 3, Extended candidates take Papers 2 and 4, and every candidate also sits either Paper 5 or Paper 6.

**How are the papers weighted?**
The multiple-choice papers are 30% each, the theory papers 50% each and the practical component 20%.

**Do I sit both Paper 5 and Paper 6?**
No. A candidate of either tier sits one of them, and only that one counts towards the total.

**How long is the theory paper?**
Paper 3 and Paper 4 are each 1 hour 15 minutes and 80 marks.

**How long is the practical component?**
Paper 5 Practical Test is 1 hour 15 minutes and Paper 6 Alternative to Practical is 1 hour; both are 40 marks.
