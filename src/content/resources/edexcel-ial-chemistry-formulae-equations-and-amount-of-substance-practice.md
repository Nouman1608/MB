**Use these values:** H 1.0, C 12.0, O 16.0, Na 23.0, Al 27.0, S 32.1, Cl 35.5, Ca 40.1, Fe 55.8, Zn 65.4,
Ba 137.3. L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹. R = 8.31 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹. Molar volume at room temperature and pressure
= 24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹.

## Questions

**1.** State the empirical formula of hydrogen peroxide, H₂O₂, and explain why it is different from
the molecular formula. **[2]**

**2.** Write ionic equations, with state symbols, and give observations for these test-tube
reactions.

**(a)** Lead(II) nitrate solution is added to potassium iodide solution. Give one observation and
the ionic equation. **[2]**
**(b)** Iron filings are stirred into copper(II) sulfate solution. Give two observations and the
ionic equation. **[3]**
**(c)** Dilute nitric acid is added to sodium carbonate solution. Give the ionic equation only. **[1]**

**3.** Calculate the total number of ions in 5.684 g of sodium sulfate, Na₂SO₄. **[3]**

**4.** Parts per million.

**(a)** A 0.500 kg sample of tap water contains 0.60 mg of fluoride ions. Express this in ppm by
mass. **[1]**
**(b)** Air near a smelter contains 0.0150% sulfur dioxide by volume. Express this in ppm. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the volume, in dm³, of sulfur dioxide in 2.00 m³ of this air. **[2]**

**5.** 4.282 g of barium hydroxide, Ba(OH)₂, is dissolved in water and made up to 500 cm³. Calculate
the concentration in mol dm⁻³ and in g dm⁻³. **[3]**

**6.** Complete combustion of 0.580 g of a compound containing only carbon, hydrogen and oxygen gives
1.320 g of carbon dioxide and 0.540 g of water. The compound has Mr = 116. Determine its empirical and
molecular formulae. **[5]**

**7.** In the thermite reaction, Fe₂O₃(s) + 2Al(s) → Al₂O₃(s) + 2Fe(l). Calculate the mass of
aluminium needed to react with 8.00 g of iron(III) oxide, and the mass of iron formed. **[4]**

**8.** A 0.258 g sample of a volatile hydrocarbon is vaporised completely. The vapour occupies 94.9 cm³
at 90 °C and 100 kPa. The empirical formula is C₃H₅. Calculate the molar mass and deduce the molecular
formula. **[5]**

**9.** Yield and atom economy.

**(a)** Zinc sulfate crystals are made by the reaction ZnO + H₂SO₄ + 6H₂O → ZnSO₄·7H₂O. A student
starts with 3.00 g of zinc oxide and collects 7.85 g of dry crystals. Calculate the percentage
yield. **[4]**
**(b)** Electrolysis of brine is represented by 2NaCl + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + Cl₂ + H₂. Calculate the
percentage atom economy for chlorine. **[2]**
**(c)** The process in (a) has an atom economy of 100% but the yield is below 100%. Explain both
facts. **[2]**

**10.** In a molar volume experiment, 0.150 g of calcium carbonate is reacted with excess
hydrochloric acid in a flask connected to a gas syringe. The final volume of carbon dioxide is
34.0 cm³ at room temperature and pressure.

**(a)** Write the equation for the reaction, with state symbols. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the molar volume of carbon dioxide from these results, in dm³ mol⁻¹. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage difference between your answer and 24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹. **[1]**
**(d)** The mass was found by difference with a total uncertainty of ±0.001 g, and the syringe
reading has an uncertainty of ±0.5 cm³. Calculate both percentage uncertainties. **[2]**
**(e)** Use (c) and (d) to comment on the result, and suggest one improvement to the method. **[2]**

**11.** A student wants to confirm the equation for aluminium reacting with hydrochloric acid.
0.135 g of aluminium foil reacts completely with excess acid, giving 174 cm³ of hydrogen at room
temperature and pressure.

**(a)** Calculate the amounts in moles of aluminium and of hydrogen. **[2]**
**(b)** Use the ratio to deduce the balanced equation, with state symbols. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the volume of hydrogen expected, and suggest why less was collected. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Empirical formula **HO** [1]. The molecular formula gives the actual atoms in one molecule
(two of each); the empirical formula is only the simplest whole-number ratio, 1 : 1 [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Simplest ratio" on its own may not score the second mark; contrast it with the
actual number of atoms in a molecule.

**2. (a)** Yellow precipitate [1]. **Pb²⁺(aq) + 2I⁻(aq) → PbI₂(s)** [1]
**(b)** Pink-brown solid forms [1]; solution changes from blue to pale green [1].
**Fe(s) + Cu²⁺(aq) → Fe²⁺(aq) + Cu(s)** [1]
**(c)** **CO₃²⁻(aq) + 2H⁺(aq) → CO₂(g) + H₂O(l)** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Copper is formed" is a conclusion, not an observation; describe colour and state
as seen, and keep spectator ions out of the equation.

**3.** M(Na₂SO₄) = 142.1 g mol⁻¹; n = 5.684 / 142.1 = 0.0400 mol [1]. Each formula unit contains
2 Na⁺ and 1 SO₄²⁻, so ions = 0.120 mol [1]. Number = 0.120 × 6.02 × 10²³ = **7.22 × 10²² ions** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Stopping at 2.41 × 10²² counts formula units, not ions, so the answer is wrong;
read whether the question asks for ions, atoms or formula units.

**4. (a)** (0.60 × 10⁻³ g / 500 g) × 10⁶ = **1.2 ppm** [1]
**(b)** 0.0150 × 10⁴ = **150 ppm** [1]
**(c)** 2.00 m³ = 2000 dm³ [1]; 2000 × (150 / 10⁶) = **0.300 dm³** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), both masses must be in the same unit before dividing; mixing mg with kg
gives an answer wrong by a factor of 10⁶.

**5.** M(Ba(OH)₂) = 171.3 g mol⁻¹; n = 4.282 / 171.3 = 0.0250 mol [1].
c = 0.0250 / 0.500 = **0.0500 mol dm⁻³** [1]. Mass concentration = 4.282 / 0.500 = **8.56 g dm⁻³** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Dividing by 500 instead of 0.500 is the commonest slip; units are required on both
answers.

**6.** Mass of C = 1.320 × (12.0 / 44.0) = 0.360 g [1]. Mass of H = 0.540 × (2.0 / 18.0) = 0.0600 g [1].
Mass of O = 0.580 − 0.360 − 0.0600 = 0.160 g [1]. Moles C : H : O = 0.0300 : 0.0600 : 0.0100 =
3 : 6 : 1, so empirical formula **C₃H₆O** [1]. Empirical mass 58.0; 116 / 58.0 = 2, so molecular formula
**C₆H₁₂O₂** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Oxygen must be found by difference; using the oxygen in CO₂ and H₂O counts oxygen
that came from the air.

**7.** M(Fe₂O₃) = 159.6; n = 8.00 / 159.6 = 0.0501 mol [1]. n(Al) = 2 × 0.0501 = 0.100 mol [1].
Mass of Al = 0.100 × 27.0 = **2.71 g** [1]. n(Fe) = 0.100 mol; mass = 0.100 × 55.8 = **5.59 g** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Keep unrounded moles in the calculator; rounding 0.05013 to 0.05 gives 2.70 g and
5.58 g, which may not match the expected answer.

**8.** T = 363 K, p = 100 000 Pa, V = 94.9 × 10⁻⁶ m³ [1].
n = pV / RT = (100 000 × 94.9 × 10⁻⁶) / (8.31 × 363) = 3.146 × 10⁻³ mol [1].
M = 0.258 / 3.146 × 10⁻³ = **82.0 g mol⁻¹** [1]. Empirical formula mass of C₃H₅ = 41.0; 82.0 / 41.0 = 2 [1].
Molecular formula **C₆H₁₀** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each unconverted quantity (°C, kPa or cm³) gives a wrong molar mass; show the
converted values so an examiner can follow your method.

**9. (a)** n(ZnO) = 3.00 / 81.4 = 0.0369 mol [1]. M(ZnSO₄·7H₂O) = 287.5 g mol⁻¹ [1].
Theoretical mass = 0.0369 × 287.5 = 10.6 g [1]. Percentage yield = 7.85 / 10.6 × 100 = **74.1%** [1]
**(b)** Sum of product masses = (2 × 40.0) + 71.0 + 2.0 = 153.0 [1]. Atom economy = 71.0 / 153.0 × 100
= **46.4%** [1]
**(c)** There is only one product, so every reactant atom ends up in it [1]. Yield is lower because of
practical losses, such as crystals left in the solution or lost during filtration [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (b), the 2 in front of NaOH must be used; including only one NaOH gives 62.8%,
which is wrong.

**10. (a)** CaCO₃(s) + 2HCl(aq) → CaCl₂(aq) + CO₂(g) + H₂O(l): correct balanced species [1], all
state symbols correct [1].
**(b)** n(CaCO₃) = 0.150 / 100.1 = 1.4985 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; n(CO₂) = n(CaCO₃) [1].
Molar volume = 34.0 / 1.4985 × 10⁻³ = 22 690 cm³ mol⁻¹ = **22.7 dm³ mol⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** (24.0 − 22.69) / 24.0 × 100 = **5.5%** [1] (5.4% if the rounded 22.7 is used)
**(d)** Mass: 0.001 / 0.150 × 100 = **0.67%** [1]. Volume: 0.5 / 34.0 × 100 = **1.5%** [1]
**(e)** The total uncertainty, about 2.1%, is smaller than the 5.5% difference, so there is a
systematic error, such as gas escaping before the bung is fitted [1]. Improvement: put the solid in a
small tube inside the sealed flask and tip it over, or use a larger mass with a larger syringe [1].
*Examiner insight:* An improvement must match the error you identified; "repeat the experiment" does
not reduce a systematic error and is unlikely to gain credit.

**11. (a)** n(Al) = 0.135 / 27.0 = 0.00500 mol [1]; n(H₂) = 174 / 24 000 = 0.00725 mol [1]
**(b)** Ratio H₂ : Al = 0.00725 / 0.00500 = 1.45, close to 1.5, so 2 : 3 [1].
2Al(s) + 6HCl(aq) → 2AlCl₃(aq) + 3H₂(g): balanced [1], state symbols [1]
**(c)** Expected = 0.00750 × 24 000 = **180 cm³** [1]. Aluminium is coated with an oxide layer, so less
metal is present than weighed and the start of the reaction is delayed [1].
*Examiner insight:* Round the ratio to the nearest simple ratio and say so; a ratio of 1.45 left as
"about 1 : 1.45" does not confirm an equation.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Counting formula units instead of ions or atoms in Avogadro constant questions.
- Not converting cm³ to dm³ (concentration) or to m³ (pV = nRT).
- Finding oxygen in combustion analysis from the products instead of by difference.
- Using the wrong coefficient when converting between moles of reactant and product.
- Leaving the multiplier off a product in the atom economy denominator.
- Observations written as product names rather than what is seen.
- Suggesting "repeat it" or "be more careful" as an improvement in practical questions.
- State symbols left out where they are requested.
