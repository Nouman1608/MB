These notes condense subtopic **18.2 Genetic modification** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series: the definition, the production of human insulin by bacteria, the three examples of GM crops, and the advantages and risks. The full explanations and worked examples are in the [18.2 study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-genetic-modification/). Then try the [18.2 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-genetic-modification-practice/).

## Key facts

| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Genetic modification | Changing the genetic material of an organism by removing, changing or inserting individual genes |
| GM organism | An organism whose DNA has been altered in this way |
| Plasmid | A small ring of DNA in bacteria; used to carry a new gene |
| Why it works | A gene codes for a protein, and cells with a new gene make the new protein |

## Human insulin from bacteria

1. Cut the human insulin gene out of human DNA.
2. Cut open a bacterial plasmid.
3. Insert the gene into the plasmid.
4. Put the plasmid into bacteria.
5. Grow the bacteria in a fermenter; they reproduce rapidly.
6. Extract and purify the insulin.

**Benefits:** identical to human insulin, large quantities, cheap, no need for animal pancreases.

## GM crops: the three examples

| Gene inserted | Effect | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Herbicide resistance | Crop survives weedkiller | Weeds are killed; less competition; higher yield |
| Insect resistance | Crop makes a toxin harmful to pests | Less damage; less insecticide; higher yield |
| Extra vitamin | Crop makes an additional vitamin | Helps to prevent deficiency in people who rely on it |

## Advantages and risks

| Advantages | Risks |
|---|---|
| Higher yields | Harm to non-target organisms |
| Less pesticide use | Spread of genes to wild plants |
| More nutritious crops | Possible health effects (for example allergy) not yet known |
| Cheap production of useful proteins by bacteria | Pests may evolve resistance |
| Crops suited to poor conditions | Cost of GM seeds; reduced biodiversity; ethical objections |

## GM compared with selective breeding

| Feature | Selective breeding | GM |
|---|---|---|
| Genes involved | Many, together | One or a few individual genes |
| Source | Same species | Can be another species |
| Time | Many generations | Fewer generations |

## Why a gene from one species works in another

- All organisms use DNA with the same four bases (A, T, C, G).
- A gene is a sequence of bases that codes for a protein.
- Cells make proteins from the instructions in genes, so a bacterium given the human insulin gene can make the human protein.
- When the bacterium divides, the new gene is copied with the rest of the DNA, so all its descendants have it.

## Structure for a "describe how" answer

| Stage | Key words to include |
|---|---|
| 1. Cut | The gene is cut out of the donor DNA |
| 2. Carry | A plasmid (vector) is cut open and the gene is inserted |
| 3. Insert | The plasmid is put into the host cell (bacterium or plant cell) |
| 4. Grow | Bacteria are grown in a fermenter; plant cells are grown into plants |
| 5. Use | The product (insulin) is extracted, or the crop is grown |

## Discussion answer frame

1. **State the use** (for example, insect-resistant maize).
2. **Give an advantage with a reason** (less damage, so a higher yield and less insecticide).
3. **Give a risk with a reason** (the toxin could harm non-target insects, so biodiversity falls).
4. **Reach a balanced view** (benefits need to be weighed against risks, and monitored).

## Worked calculation

*A GM crop gives 8.4 tonnes per hectare. The non-GM crop gives 7.0 tonnes per hectare. Find the percentage increase.*

1. Increase = 8.4 - 7.0 = 1.4 tonnes per hectare.
2. Percentage = 1.4 divided by 7.0 x 100 = 20 per cent.

Divide by the non-GM value, which is the starting value.

## Short model answers

**Describe genetic modification.** Changing the genetic material of an organism by removing, changing or inserting individual genes.

**Describe how bacteria are made to produce human insulin.** The human insulin gene is cut out and inserted into a bacterial plasmid. The plasmid is put into bacteria, which are grown in a fermenter and make insulin.

**State one advantage of herbicide-resistant crops.** The weeds can be killed without harming the crop, which increases yield.

**State one risk of GM crops.** Genes could spread to wild plants, or the toxin could harm non-target insects.

**Explain why bacteria are suitable for making insulin.** They reproduce rapidly, have plasmids, and make complex molecules such as proteins.

## Exam traps

- GM changes **individual genes**; selective breeding does not.
- The plasmid carries the gene into the bacterium.
- Say "potential" risks and explain them.
- A discussion needs **both** advantages and risks.
- Insulin from bacteria **treats** Type 1 diabetes; it is not a cure.
- Insect-resistant crops make their **own** toxin.
- Percentage increase is divided by the **original** value.
- The syllabus limits discussion to crop plants and bacteria.

## Self-test

1. Define genetic modification.
2. What is a plasmid?
3. Name the organism that makes human insulin in industry.
4. State two benefits of making insulin this way.
5. State the three examples of genes inserted into crop plants.
6. How does a herbicide-resistant crop help a farmer?
7. How does an insect-resistant crop help a farmer?
8. Give one potential risk of GM crops.
9. Why is GM described as more precise than selective breeding?
10. Why is a fermenter used?
11. Why might GM seeds be a problem for poor farmers?
12. Give one advantage of a crop with extra vitamins.

### Answers

1. Changing the genetic material of an organism by removing, changing or inserting individual genes.
2. A small ring of DNA in a bacterium.
3. Bacteria.
4. Any two of: identical to human insulin; large quantities; cheap; no animals needed.
5. Herbicide resistance, insect pest resistance and additional vitamins.
6. Weeds can be sprayed and killed without harming the crop.
7. The crop is damaged less, so yield is higher and less insecticide is needed.
8. Any one: gene spread to wild plants; harm to non-target organisms; unknown health effects.
9. It changes one or a few chosen genes, not many genes together.
10. To grow large numbers of bacteria under controlled conditions.
11. The seeds can be expensive.
12. It can help to reduce vitamin deficiency.
