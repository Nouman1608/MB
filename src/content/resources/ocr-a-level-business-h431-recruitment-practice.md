## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Explain what is meant by recruitment. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between a job description and a person specification. **[3]**

**3.** Explain **one** reason why a business might use internal recruitment. **[2]**

**4.** Explain **one** reason why a business might use a recruitment agency. **[2]**

**5.** Which **one** of the following is most likely to be included in a job advertisement?

A: The results of other candidates' interviews.

B: The closing date and how to apply.

C: The salary of the current post-holder's manager.

D: The business's confidential accounts.

**[1]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**6.** Quillon Bakeries needs to recruit a production supervisor on a salary of £22,000. Managers' time is valued at £28 an hour.

Option A: advertise externally itself. The advertisement costs £950, managers spend 14 hours on the process, and induction costs £800.

Option B: use an agency, which charges 15% of the salary. Managers spend 6 hours on the process, and induction costs £800.

**(a)** Calculate the total cost of each option. **[5]**

**(b)** Calculate how much more expensive Option B is than Option A. **[1]**

**(c)** Evaluate which method Quillon Bakeries should use. **[8]**

**7.** Quillon Bakeries has a vacancy for a trainee baker. Write the key content that you would expect in the person specification for this post, and explain how this helps the business. **[6]**

**8.** Evaluate the importance of effective recruitment to a fast-growing restaurant chain and its stakeholders. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** Recruitment is the process of attracting suitable people to apply for a vacancy [1], ending with a pool of applicants from which one can be selected [1]. **[2]**

**2.** A job description sets out what the job involves, such as title, duties and responsibilities [1], whereas a person specification describes the qualifications, skills, experience and qualities that the ideal post-holder needs [1]. The job description is about the job; the person specification is about the person [1]. **[3]**

**3.** It is cheaper and quicker, as there is little or no advertising [1], and the candidate already knows the business, so induction is shorter and the business knows the candidate's strengths and weaknesses [1]. Accept: promotion motivates other staff. **[2]**

**4.** An agency has access to many candidates and specialist skills in finding them [1], which saves the business's managers time on advertising and sifting applications [1]. **[2]**

**5.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** Option A: advertisement £950 [1]; managers' time 14 × £28 = £392 [1]; induction £800; total = £950 + £392 + £800 = **£2,142** [1]. Option B: fee 15% × £22,000 = £3,300 [1]; managers' time 6 × £28 = £168; induction £800; total = £3,300 + £168 + £800 = **£4,268** [1]. **[5]**

**(b)** £4,268 − £2,142 = **£2,126** [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** Option A is £2,126 cheaper, and Quillon is a small business where money is limited [1]. The cost of Option A does not include the extra risk that the advert fails to attract a suitable person, which would cost more advertising and time [1].
The agency saves 8 hours of managers' time (14 − 6) [1], which is worth £224 at £28 an hour, a small amount compared with the £2,126 difference [1]. The agency might find a better-qualified candidate, since it searches its own database [1].
An internal promotion could be cheaper still, if a baker is ready to supervise, and it would motivate staff [1]. But it would create a vacancy elsewhere [1].
Judgement: Quillon should first offer the post to existing bakers, and if none is suitable advertise itself, since the extra £2,126 for an agency is hard to justify for a supervisor post which many candidates could fill; it should use an agency only if its own advertising fails to produce a suitable shortlist [1]. **[8]**

**7.** Essential criteria: the person specification should list qualifications or experience that every applicant must have, such as a food hygiene certificate [1] and the ability to work early shifts [1]. Desirable criteria: extra qualities such as experience in a bakery or a baking qualification [1]. Personal qualities: reliability, attention to detail and the ability to work in a team [1]. How it helps: it provides a checklist for shortlisting, so the business focuses on suitable applicants [1] and treats applicants fairly and consistently, which reduces the risk of discrimination claims [1]. **[6]**

**8.** Importance: a growing chain needs a steady supply of staff to open new sites on time, so recruitment affects growth directly [1]. Customer service depends on staff, so recruiting people with the right attitude affects reputation [1]. Hiring the wrong people brings extra costs from turnover and retraining, which is a particular problem in restaurants [1]. A good reputation as an employer attracts applicants and lowers advertising costs [1].
Stakeholders: customers receive better service from well-chosen staff [1]; existing staff have a lighter workload when posts are filled [1]; owners benefit from growth but bear recruitment costs, which are high when many staff are needed at once [1]; applicants need a fair and clear process.
Evaluation: the importance is high during expansion, but recruitment alone is not enough, since poor training or pay would still lose staff [1]. Judgement: recruitment is vital to the chain's growth plan, so it should invest in clear job descriptions and a recruitment brand, and measure the cost and quality of each hire [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing recruitment with selection.
- Mixing up the job description and the person specification.
- Describing a method without evaluating it for the post.
- Treating internal recruitment as free.
- Not using the figures in the case to compare costs.
