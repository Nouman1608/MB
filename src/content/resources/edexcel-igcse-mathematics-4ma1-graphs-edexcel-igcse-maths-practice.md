## Section A

**1.** P is the point (−2, 7) and Q is the point (6, −1).

**(a)** Find the coordinates of the midpoint of PQ. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the gradient of PQ. **[2]**

**2.** Write down the gradient and the coordinates of the y-intercept of the line y = 5 − 2x. **[2]**

**3.** Find the gradient and the y-intercept of the line 2y − 6x = 10. **[2]**

**4.** Find the coordinates of the points where the line 4x + 5y = 20 crosses the x-axis and the y-axis. **[2]**

**5.** **(a)** Write down the equation of the line parallel to y = 4x − 1 that passes through (0, −3). **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the equation of the horizontal line through (2, −6). **[1]**

**6.** A conversion graph changes pounds (£) into US dollars ($) at a rate of £1 = $1.25 (an invented rate).

**(a)** State the gradient of the graph, with pounds on the horizontal axis. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the graph passes through the origin. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** A cyclist rides 18 km from home to a lake in 45 minutes. She rests at the lake for 15 minutes, then rides home in 1 hour 30 minutes.

**(a)** Work out her speed, in km/h, on the way to the lake. **[2]**

**(b)** Work out her speed, in km/h, on the way home. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe the section of her distance–time graph that shows the rest. **[1]**

**8.** A train starts from rest and accelerates steadily to 24 m/s in 30 seconds. It travels at 24 m/s for 60 seconds, then decelerates steadily to rest in 20 seconds.

**(a)** Work out the acceleration in the first 30 seconds. **[1]**

**(b)** Work out the total distance travelled. **[3]**

**9.** y = x² − 3x − 1.

**(a)** Complete a table of values of y for x = −1, 0, 1, 2, 3 and 4. **[2]**

**(b)** Write down the equation of the line of symmetry of the graph. **[1]**

**(c)** The solutions of x² − 3x − 1 = 0 are where the graph crosses the x-axis. Between which pairs of consecutive integers do they lie? **[2]**

**10.** ABCD is a parallelogram. A is (−2, 1), B is (3, 2) and C is (6, 6). Find the coordinates of D. **[2]**

**11.** **(Higher tier only)**

**(a)** Find the equation of the line through (−1, 4) and (3, −8). **[3]**

**(b)** Find the equation of the line perpendicular to your line in (a) that passes through (6, 2). **[3]**

**12.** **(Higher tier only)** The graph of y = x² − 5x + 2 has been drawn.

**(a)** Find the equation of the straight line that should be drawn on the same axes to solve x² − 7x + 6 = 0. **[2]**

**(b)** Solve x² − 7x + 6 = 0 algebraically to check where the line meets the curve. **[1]**

**13.** **(Higher tier only)** The curve y = f(x) has a maximum point at (3, 5). Write down the coordinates of the maximum point of

**(a)** y = f(x) − 4 **[1]**

**(b)** y = f(x + 2) **[1]**

**(c)** y = 3f(x) **[1]**

**(d)** y = f(2x) **[1]**

**14.** **(Higher tier only)** A tangent is drawn to a distance–time curve at t = 4 seconds. The tangent passes through the points (2, 5) and (6, 21), where distance is in metres. Estimate the speed of the object at t = 4 seconds. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** ((−2 + 6)/2, (7 + (−1))/2) [1] = **(2, 3)** [1].

**(b)** (−1 − 7) ÷ (6 − (−2)) [1] = −8 ÷ 8 = **−1** [1].

**2.** Gradient **−2** [1]; y-intercept **(0, 5)** [1].

**3.** 2y = 6x + 10, so y = 3x + 5 [1]; gradient **3**, y-intercept **(0, 5)** [1].

**4.** y = 0 gives x = 5: **(5, 0)** [1]; x = 0 gives y = 4: **(0, 4)** [1].

**5. (a)** **y = 4x − 3** [1].

**(b)** **y = −6** [1].

**6. (a)** **1.25** (dollars per pound) [1].

**(b)** £0 is worth $0, so the point (0, 0) is on the graph [1].

**7. (a)** 45 minutes = 0.75 hours [1]; 18 ÷ 0.75 = **24 km/h** [1].

**(b)** 1 hour 30 minutes = 1.5 hours [1]; 18 ÷ 1.5 = **12 km/h** [1].

**(c)** A **horizontal** line segment, 15 minutes long, at a distance of 18 km [1].

**8. (a)** 24 ÷ 30 = **0.8 m/s²** [1].

**(b)** ½ × 30 × 24 = 360 and 60 × 24 = 1440 [1]; ½ × 20 × 24 = 240 [1]; total **2040 m** [1].

**9. (a)** x = −1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 give y = **3, −1, −3, −3, −1, 3** (2 marks for all six; 1 mark for four or five) [2].

**(b)** **x = 1.5** [1].

**(c)** y changes sign between x = −1 and x = 0 [1] and between x = 3 and x = 4 [1]. (The solutions are about −0.3 and 3.3.)

**10.** B to C is 3 across and 4 up, so D = (−2 + 3, 1 + 4) [1] = **(1, 5)** [1].

**11. (a)** m = (−8 − 4) ÷ (3 − (−1)) = −12 ÷ 4 = −3 [1]; 4 = −3(−1) + c, so c = 1 [1]; **y = −3x + 1** [1].

**(b)** Perpendicular gradient = 1/3 [1]; 2 = (1/3)(6) + c, so c = 0 [1]; **y = x/3** (or y = ⅓x) [1].

**12. (a)** x² − 5x + 2 = 2x − 4 rearranges to x² − 7x + 6 = 0 [1]; draw **y = 2x − 4** [1].

**(b)** (x − 1)(x − 6) = 0, so **x = 1 and x = 6** [1].

**13. (a)** **(3, 1)** [1].

**(b)** **(1, 5)** [1].

**(c)** **(3, 15)** [1].

**(d)** **(1.5, 5)** [1].

**14.** Gradient of tangent = (21 − 5) ÷ (6 − 2) [1] = **4 m/s** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 1(b): subtracting in different orders on the top and bottom, which gives +1 instead of −1.
- Question 3: giving the gradient as 6 and the intercept as 10 without dividing by 2.
- Question 4: swapping the intercepts, so the line is drawn through (4, 0) and (0, 5).
- Question 7: dividing 18 by 45 and getting 0.4, a speed in km per minute, then labelling it km/h.
- Question 8(b): using 24 × 110 for the whole journey, which treats the speed as constant throughout.
- Question 9(a): working out (−1)² − 3(−1) − 1 as −1 − 3 − 1 instead of 1 + 3 − 1.
- Question 10: using A + B − C or forgetting that opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal and parallel.
- Question 11(b): using a gradient of 3 or −1/3. The negative reciprocal of −3 is +1/3.
- Question 13(b): moving the point to (5, 5). f(x + 2) moves the graph 2 units left.
- Question 13(d): doubling the x-coordinate to (6, 5) instead of halving it.
