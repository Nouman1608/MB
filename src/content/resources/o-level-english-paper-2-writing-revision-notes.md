## Paper 2 at a glance

**50 marks, 2 hours, 50% of the final grade.** Weighted 80% Writing (AO2), 20% Reading (AO1) — the
mirror image of Paper 1.

## The five Writing objectives

| Code | Rewards |
|---|---|
| W1 | Articulating experience; expressing thought, feeling, imagination |
| W2 | Organising and structuring ideas for deliberate effect |
| W3 | Range of vocabulary and sentence structures for context |
| W4 | Register appropriate to context |
| W5 | Accurate spelling, punctuation, grammar |

**Section A tests all five** (15 of its 25 marks) plus Reading (R3, R5; 10 marks). **Section B tests
four** — W1, W2, W3, W5 — register (W4) is not separately assessed since there is no set audience.

## Section A: Directed Writing (25 marks)

**One compulsory question.** Source text(s) ~400-450 words, printed on the paper itself. Produce a
speech, email, report, letter or article, ~250-350 words.

- **15 marks**: your own voice, organisation, vocabulary, register, technical accuracy.
- **10 marks**: how well you analysed, evaluated and developed the source material's facts/ideas.

"**Directed**" is the operative word — respond to the specific angle, audience and purpose set out,
grounded in the source text(s). Draw at least one specific point from **each** source text, not just
one. The most common mark loss: writing a general opinion essay instead of grounding every point in
the given text(s).

## Section B: Composition (25 marks)

Choose **one of four**: two descriptive, two narrative. No source text — ~350-450 words, the longest
single piece of continuous writing in the qualification.

- **Descriptive**: person, place or situation — no plot required, the skill is controlled, vivid
  description.
- **Narrative**: a sequence of connected events with a beginning, development and resolution/turning
  point.

All 25 marks go to W1, W2, W3, W5 — no register (W4) since there's no set audience.

**Don't under-plan Section B just because it feels "creative"** — it carries the same 25 marks as
Section A and rewards structure just as much.

## Command words on this paper

- **Consider** — review and respond to given information.
- **Describe** — state points/give characteristics and main features.
- **Explain** — set out purposes or reasons, make relationships clear, support with evidence.

Misreading the command word is a common, avoidable source of lost marks.

## Timing across the two hours

Roughly **55-60 minutes per section**, including 8-10 minutes reading/annotating the source for
Section A, and 5 minutes planning for Section B. Leave a short buffer for a final proofread.

## Why the 80/20 split surprises candidates

That 20% of Reading marks on what looks like a "Writing" paper catches a lot of candidates off guard,
but it comes directly from Section A, where you must use information from a printed source text to
write your answer. You cannot access full marks in Section A by writing fluently in isolation from the
text — you have to demonstrate you have read, selected from, and evaluated it. This is worth
internalising early: a beautifully written Section A response that ignores the source material's
specific facts and ideas will lose the 10 Reading marks regardless of how strong its prose is.

## Treating the two sections as genuinely different skills

Section A rewards disciplined use of someone else's material for a stated purpose; Section B rewards
your own controlled, structured expression with no material to lean on. Practising both regularly, and
specifically checking your answers against the W1-W5 objectives rather than just "does this sound
good," is the most direct way to see where marks are actually being gained or lost. Past papers and
specimen mark schemes, freely available from Cambridge International, show real examples of how
examiners apply these objectives to full-length answers — use them alongside revision notes rather than
as a substitute for understanding the assessment structure first.

## Exam traps

- Writing a general opinion essay in Section A instead of grounding every point in the source text(s).
- Drawing on only one source text when two are given.
- Under-preparing Section B because it feels less structured than Section A.
- Skipping the final proofread — W5 (spelling, punctuation, grammar) is tested throughout and easy to
  lose marks on under time pressure.

## Self-test

1. What percentage split of Writing vs Reading does Paper 2 carry, and how does this differ from
   Paper 1?
2. Which two assessment objectives does Section A's Reading component test?
3. Why is W4 not separately assessed in Section B?
4. What is the expected word count for Section A vs Section B?
5. Name the three command words most likely to shape a Directed Writing response.

**Answers:** 1. 80% Writing, 20% Reading — the reverse of Paper 1 (80% Reading, 20% Writing). 2. R3
and R5. 3. Because there is no set audience or format being tested in Section B. 4. Section A:
250-350 words. Section B: 350-450 words. 5. Consider, describe, explain.
