## Questions

**1.** Brindle Bakes had revenue of 150,000, cost of sales of 97,500 and other operating expenses of 33,000.

**(a)** Work out the gross profit. **[1]**
**(b)** Work out the gross profit margin. **[2]**
**(c)** Work out the operating profit. **[1]**
**(d)** Work out the operating profit margin. **[2]**

**2.** Explain one reason why profit is important to a business. **[3]**

**3.** Ferrow Hardware buys a toolkit for 36 and sells it for 51.

**(a)** Calculate the markup. **[2]**
**(b)** Another item costs 48. The owner wants a markup of 75%. Calculate the selling price. **[2]**

**4.** Complete the table by classifying each item as a current asset, non-current asset, current liability or non-current liability. **[6]**

| Item | Classification |
|---|---|
| Delivery van | |
| Inventory | |
| Trade payables | |
| Bank loan repayable in six years | |
| Trade receivables | |
| Bank overdraft | |

**5.** Kestrel Signs has non-current assets of 140,000, current assets of 60,000, current liabilities of 35,000 and non-current liabilities of 45,000. Operating profit was 21,750.

**(a)** Work out the capital employed. **[2]**
**(b)** Work out ROCE. **[2]**
**(c)** Outline why the owner would find ROCE useful. **[2]**

**6.** Marwood Garden Centre had revenue of 320,000, a gross profit margin of 35% and an operating profit margin of 8%. Calculate gross profit, operating profit, cost of sales and other operating expenses. **[4]**

**7.** Tarnway Tools has these figures at its year end: non-current assets 95,000; inventory 18,000; trade receivables 9,000; cash 3,000; trade payables 21,000; bank overdraft 12,000; 40,000 owed on a bank loan that is repayable in five years.

**(a)** Calculate capital employed. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the current ratio. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the acid test ratio. **[2]**
**(d)** Analyse one problem these ratios could cause Tarnway Tools. **[3]**

**8.** Quillon Electrical is a small business. Its figures are:

| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 200,000 | 260,000 |
| Gross profit | 70,000 | 84,500 |
| Operating profit | 24,000 | 23,400 |
| Capital employed | 120,000 | 130,000 |
| Current assets | 54,000 | 62,000 |
| Inventory | 20,000 | 34,000 |
| Current liabilities | 36,000 | 50,000 |

**(a)** Calculate, for both years, the gross profit margin, operating profit margin, ROCE, current ratio and acid test ratio. **[5]**
**(b)** Analyse the change in performance between Year 1 and Year 2. **[4]**

**9.** Two wholesalers, Harlow and Dunmere, each have revenue of 500,000. A supplier must choose which one to offer 90 days of credit.

| | Harlow | Dunmere |
|---|---|---|
| Gross profit | 150,000 | 200,000 |
| Operating profit | 40,000 | 45,000 |
| Capital employed | 250,000 | 300,000 |
| Current assets | 90,000 | 80,000 |
| Inventory | 20,000 | 55,000 |
| Current liabilities | 60,000 | 70,000 |

Justify which wholesaler the supplier should choose. **[8]**

**10.** Sunbury Stores holds current assets of 48,000, including inventory of 24,000. Its current liabilities are 30,000.

**(a)** Find the current ratio and, separately, the acid test ratio. **[2]**
**(b)** The owner buys 12,000 of inventory on credit. Calculate both ratios again. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain one reason why liquidity matters to a business. **[3]**

**11.** Cresswell Cycles sells bicycles from one shop. The owner is thinking of borrowing 60,000 to open a second shop.

| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 180,000 | 230,000 |
| Gross profit margin | 42% | 42% |
| Operating profit margin | 14% | 11% |
| ROCE | 19% | 15% |
| Current ratio | 2.2 : 1 | 1.3 : 1 |
| Acid test ratio | 1.0 : 1 | 0.6 : 1 |

Evaluate whether the owner should go ahead with the loan. **[8]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 150,000 − 97,500 = **52,500** [1].
**(b)** 52,500 ÷ 150,000 [1] × 100 = **35%** [1].
**(c)** 52,500 − 33,000 = **19,500** [1].
**(d)** 19,500 ÷ 150,000 [1] × 100 = **13%** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Gross profit is needed again in part (c), so show each line of working.

**2.** Retained profit can fund new equipment [1]. The owner then has no need to borrow [1], so interest is avoided and the business can grow faster [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* An explain answer is one point plus two developments; a list with no development tends to earn less.

**3. (a)** Profit = 51 − 36 = 15 [1]. 15 ÷ 36 × 100 = **41.67%** [1].
**(b)** Profit = 48 × 0.75 = 36 [1]; price = 48 + 36 = **84** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Markup is measured against cost; dividing by 51 would give the margin, a different figure.

**4.** Delivery van: non-current asset [1]. Inventory: current asset [1]. Trade payables: current liability [1]. Bank loan repayable in six years: non-current liability [1]. Trade receivables: current asset [1]. Bank overdraft: current liability [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The deciding test is the one-year line: the loan is a liability but falls due after more than a year.

**5. (a)** 140,000 + 60,000 − 35,000 [1] = **165,000** [1]. Check: equity 120,000 + 45,000.
**(b)** 21,750 ÷ 165,000 [1] × 100 = **13.18%** [1].
**(c)** ROCE shows the return the business earns on the long-term funds invested [1]. The owner can compare it with last year or with what the money could earn elsewhere, to decide whether it is worth keeping the money in the business [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Capital employed must include the non-current liabilities, which is why the check against equity plus loans is worth doing.

**6.** Gross profit = 320,000 × 0.35 = **112,000** [1]. Operating profit = 320,000 × 0.08 = **25,600** [1]. Cost of sales = 320,000 − 112,000 = **208,000** [1]. Other operating expenses = 112,000 − 25,600 = **86,400** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Working back from a margin starts with revenue multiplied by the percentage; show that line before the subtractions.

**7. (a)** Current assets = 18,000 + 9,000 + 3,000 = 30,000; current liabilities = 21,000 + 12,000 = 33,000 [1]. Capital employed = 95,000 + 30,000 − 33,000 = **92,000** [1].
**(b)** 30,000 ÷ 33,000 [1] = **0.91 : 1** [1].
**(c)** (30,000 − 18,000) ÷ 33,000 [1] = **0.36 : 1** [1].
**(d)** Current liabilities are larger than current assets, so the business may struggle to pay on time [1]. The acid test of 0.36 : 1 means it has about 36 cents of liquid assets for every dollar owed [1]. Suppliers may stop credit or the bank may demand the overdraft back, which could force the business to close [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The overdraft belongs in current liabilities; leaving it out changes capital employed and both ratios.

**8. (a)** Gross profit margin: 35.0% and 32.5% [1]. Operating profit margin: 12.0% and 9.0% [1]. ROCE: 20.0% and 18.0% [1]. Current ratio: 1.50 : 1 and 1.24 : 1 [1]. Acid test: 0.94 : 1 and 0.56 : 1 [1].
**(b)** Revenue rose by 30%, but operating profit fell from 24,000 to 23,400, so growth did not raise profit [1]. Both margins fell, which suggests costs rose faster than sales [1]. ROCE slipped from 20.0% to 18.0%, so each dollar of capital employed earned less [1]. Liquidity weakened: inventory rose by 14,000 and the acid test dropped from 0.94 : 1 to 0.56 : 1, leaving less quick cover for its debts [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Analysis answers tend to gain credit when each ratio change is tied to a figure from the table and to a consequence for the business, not just described as "worse".

**9.** Calculations: Harlow gross margin 30%, operating margin 8%, ROCE 16%, current ratio 1.50 : 1, acid test 1.17 : 1. Dunmere gross margin 40%, operating margin 9%, ROCE 15%, current ratio 1.14 : 1, acid test 0.36 : 1 [2].
For Dunmere: its margins are higher, so it earns more on each sale [1], and its operating profit is larger at 45,000 [1].
For Harlow: its current ratio and acid test ratio are much stronger, so it is better placed to pay a supplier in 90 days [1], while Dunmere's acid test below 1 : 1 depends on selling its 55,000 of inventory first [1].
Judgement: the supplier needs to be paid, so liquidity matters most for a credit decision [1]; Harlow is the safer choice, although Dunmere is the more profitable business [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A justify answer recommends one option using the figures given; it is strongest when the measure chosen matches the decision (here, ability to pay) and the other option's strengths are weighed.

**10. (a)** 48,000 ÷ 30,000 = **1.60 : 1** [1]; (48,000 − 24,000) ÷ 30,000 = **0.80 : 1** [1].
**(b)** Current assets = 48,000 + 12,000 = 60,000 and current liabilities = 30,000 + 12,000 = 42,000 [1]. Current ratio = 60,000 ÷ 42,000 = **1.43 : 1** [1]. Acid test = (60,000 − 36,000) ÷ 42,000 = **0.57 : 1** [1].
**(c)** Liquidity means being able to find cash for bills due soon [1]. Suppliers or staff who are not paid on time may stop working with the business [1], so it can fail even though it is profitable [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Buying stock on credit raises both sides of the ratio, so state the new current assets and new current liabilities before dividing.

**11.** Points in favour: gross margin held at 42%, so selling prices cover the cost of bicycles [1]; revenue grew by about 28% (180,000 to 230,000) [1]; operating profit is positive and ROCE is 15%, so the business is still earning a return [1].
Points against: operating margin fell from 14% to 11% and ROCE from 19% to 15%, so costs are rising [1]; the current ratio fell from 2.2 : 1 to 1.3 : 1 and the acid test from 1.0 : 1 to 0.6 : 1, so the business already has less cover for its short-term debts [1]; a 60,000 loan would add repayments while liquidity is weak [1]; the figures are historical and give no evidence of demand for a second shop [1].
Judgement: the owner should delay the loan until expenses are under control and liquidity has recovered, because the trend is worsening on the measures that matter most for repaying debt [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Weigh the two sides, then decide; a decision that skips the weakest ratios is hard to defend.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing by revenue in ROCE, or by capital employed in a margin.
- Dividing markup by the selling price instead of the cost.
- Leaving the overdraft out of current liabilities.
- Deducting every current asset, rather than only inventory, in the acid test.
- Showing a liquidity ratio as a percentage, or without a unit.
- Writing "it went up" or "it got worse" with no figure and no consequence.
- Giving a justify or evaluate answer that lists points but never reaches a decision.
