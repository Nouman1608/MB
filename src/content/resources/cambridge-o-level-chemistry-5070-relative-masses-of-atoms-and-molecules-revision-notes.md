These notes condense section 3.2, Relative masses of atoms and molecules, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). The reasoning and worked examples are in the [relative masses of atoms and molecules study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-relative-masses-of-atoms-and-molecules/); exam-style questions are in the [relative masses of atoms and molecules practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-relative-masses-of-atoms-and-molecules-practice/).

## Definitions (learn the wording)

- **Relative atomic mass, Ar**: the average mass of the isotopes of an element compared to 1/12th of the mass of an atom of carbon-12.
- **Relative molecular mass, Mr**: the sum of the relative atomic masses of the atoms in a molecule.
- **Relative formula mass, Mr**: the same sum, used for ionic compounds (which have no molecules).

## Key facts

- The standard is **carbon-12 = exactly 12**.
- Relative masses are ratios: **no units**.
- Ar is an **average** over isotopes, so it need not be a whole number (Cl = 35.5).
- Ar values let you compare atoms: Ar 32 (S) is twice Ar 16 (O), so an S atom is on average twice as heavy as an O atom.
- Mr = Σ (number of atoms × Ar).

## Ar values used in these notes

H 1 · C 12 · N 14 · O 16 · Na 23 · Mg 24 · Al 27 · S 32 · Cl 35.5 · K 39 · Ca 40 · Fe 56 · Zn 65 · Br 80 · Ba 137 · Pb 207

Always use the values printed on the Periodic Table you are given, or those in the question.

## Worked Mr values

| Formula | Atom count | Mr |
|---|---|---|
| H₂O | H 2, O 1 | 18 |
| C₃H₈ | C 3, H 8 | 44 |
| CH₃COOH | C 2, H 4, O 2 | 60 |
| C₆H₁₂O₆ | C 6, H 12, O 6 | 180 |
| Mg(OH)₂ | Mg 1, O 2, H 2 | 58 |
| Fe₂O₃ | Fe 2, O 3 | 160 |
| (NH₄)₂SO₄ | N 2, H 8, S 1, O 4 | 132 |
| Pb(NO₃)₂ | Pb 1, N 2, O 6 | 331 |
| Na₂CO₃·10H₂O | Na₂CO₃ (106) + 10 × 18 | 286 |

## Bracket and dot rules

- A subscript after a bracket multiplies **every** atom inside: (NH₄)₂ = N 2, H 8.
- A subscript inside the bracket applies only to the atom before it: NO₃ has 3 O.
- In a hydrated salt, the number before H₂O multiplies **only the water**: ·5H₂O adds 5 × 18 = 90.
- Count H atoms in all parts of a formula: CH₃COOH has 4 H.

## Elements: atom or molecule?

| Element | Write | Value |
|---|---|---|
| oxygen gas | O₂ | Mr 32 |
| nitrogen gas | N₂ | Mr 28 |
| chlorine gas | Cl₂ | Mr 71 |
| a metal, e.g. iron | Fe | Ar 56 |
| carbon (diamond, graphite) | C | Ar 12 |

"Relative molecular mass of oxygen" = 32, not 16.

## Setting out a calculation

Write the counts, then the sum, then the answer: Ca(NO₃)₂ → Ca 1, N 2, O 6 → 40 + 28 + 96 → **164**. Showing the counts earns method credit even if the final addition slips.

## Working backwards

- Unknown element: Ar(X) = Mr − (mass of the known atoms). Example: X₂O with Mr 62 → 2X = 62 − 16 = 46 → X = 23, sodium.
- Unknown water of crystallisation: 18*n* = Mr(hydrated) − Mr(anhydrous). Example: FeSO₄·*n*H₂O, Mr 278; FeSO₄ = 152; 18*n* = 126; *n* = 7.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Relative atomic mass, Ar | average isotope mass compared with 1/12 of a carbon-12 atom |
| Relative molecular mass, Mr | sum of Ar values for a molecule |
| Relative formula mass, Mr | sum of Ar values for the formula of an ionic compound |
| Carbon-12 | the isotope used as the standard, mass exactly 12 |
| Isotope | atom of an element with a different number of neutrons |
| Water of crystallisation | water chemically combined in a hydrated salt, shown after a dot |

## Exam traps

- No units for Ar or Mr.
- "Average" and "carbon-12" are both needed in the Ar description.
- Multiply out brackets fully.
- Proton number is not Ar (oxygen: 8 protons, Ar 16).
- Do not round 35.5 for chlorine.
- In XCl₂-type problems, remember there are **two** chlorines (71), not one.

## Self-test

1. Describe relative atomic mass.
2. Why do relative masses have no units?
3. Why is the Ar of chlorine 35.5 and not a whole number?
4. Calculate the Mr of ammonium nitrate, NH₄NO₃.
5. Calculate the Mr of calcium hydroxide, Ca(OH)₂.
6. Calculate the Mr of potassium sulfate, K₂SO₄.
7. Calculate the Mr of barium hydroxide, Ba(OH)₂.
8. Calculate the Mr of hydrated iron(II) sulfate, FeSO₄·7H₂O.
9. Is the Mr of sodium chloride a relative molecular mass or a relative formula mass? Explain.
10. A compound YBr₂ has Mr 200. Find the Ar of Y and identify it.
11. How many times heavier, on average, is a bromine atom than a calcium atom?
12. MgCl₂·*n*H₂O has Mr 203. Find *n*.

## Answers

1. The average mass of the isotopes of an element compared to 1/12th of the mass of an atom of carbon-12.
2. They are ratios: masses compared with a standard, so the units cancel.
3. It is an average of the masses of chlorine's isotopes (35 and 37), weighted by their abundances.
4. 14 + 4 + 14 + 48 = 80.
5. 40 + 2 × (16 + 1) = 74.
6. 2 × 39 + 32 + 4 × 16 = 174.
7. 137 + 2 × 17 = 171.
8. 56 + 32 + 64 + 7 × 18 = 278.
9. Relative formula mass: sodium chloride is ionic, so it has no molecules.
10. 200 − (2 × 80) = 40; calcium.
11. 80 ÷ 40 = 2 times.
12. MgCl₂ = 24 + 71 = 95; 18*n* = 108; *n* = 6.
