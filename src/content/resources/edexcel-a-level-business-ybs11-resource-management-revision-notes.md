For explanations and worked examples, use the [Resource management study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-business-ybs11-resource-management/), then try the [practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-business-ybs11-resource-management-practice/). Figures are invented.

## Formula sheet

```
Labour productivity   = output / employees (or labour hours)
Average cost per unit = total cost / output
Capacity utilisation  = current output / maximum possible output x 100
Reorder level         = buffer inventory + (daily usage x lead time)
Order quantity        = maximum inventory - buffer inventory
```

Calculate questions carry 4 marks at Unit 2 tariff: formula, working and answer with units or a percentage.

## Methods of production

| Method | Key point | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Job | One-off item made to order | Bespoke, high-value goods |
| Batch | Group of identical items, then reset | Variety in moderate quantities |
| Flow | Continuous standard output | High, steady demand; lowest unit cost |
| Cell | Teams own a whole part of the product | Flexible, multi-skilled production |

- **Labour-intensive:** people do most of the work; flexible, but wages rise with output.
- **Capital-intensive:** machines do most of the work; high fixed cost, low variable cost per unit.

## Productivity and efficiency

- **Productivity** is physical: output per unit of input per time period.
- **Efficiency** is financial: producing at minimum average cost.
- Productivity rises if output grows faster than inputs. More output with proportionally more hours is not higher productivity.
- **Factors:** skills, motivation, machinery, layout, materials, management.
- **Ways to improve:** training, better equipment, motivation, better layouts, less waste.
- **Link to competitiveness:** higher productivity lowers unit labour cost, allowing lower prices or higher margins than rivals.
- **Short lead-in time:** reaching the market before rivals, with scope for a premium price.

## Capacity utilisation

| Situation | Effects | Responses |
|---|---|---|
| Under-utilised | High fixed cost per unit; wasted resources; room for extra orders | New markets or customers; price cuts; rationalise; subcontract work in |
| Over-utilised | Overtime cost; breakdowns; falling quality; late orders; no spare capacity | Extra shifts; new equipment or site; outsourcing; raise prices |

Fixed cost per unit falls as output rises towards capacity, which is why high utilisation lifts profit margins. Beyond about full capacity, extra output costs more per unit.

## Inventory control

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Maximum level | Most the business wants to hold |
| Buffer inventory | Minimum safety level |
| Reorder level | Level at which the order is placed |
| Lead time | Delay between order and delivery |

On the diagram the line falls at the usage rate, an order is placed at the reorder level, stock reaches the buffer as the delivery arrives, and it jumps back to the maximum. A steeper slope means faster usage.

**Poor inventory control:** stock-outs, lost sales, rush orders, storage costs, waste, cash tied up.

- **JIT:** materials arrive as needed. Gains: lower storage cost, less cash tied up, less waste. Risks: supplier failure, no bulk discounts, frequent delivery costs.
- **Waste minimisation:** remove overproduction, waiting, movement, defects and excess stock.
- **Lean production:** JIT plus waste minimisation plus continuous improvement; more output from fewer resources.

## Quality management

| Approach | One-line summary |
|---|---|
| Quality control | Inspect and reject faulty output; finds faults, does not prevent them |
| Quality assurance | Build quality into every stage so faults are prevented |
| Quality circles | Staff groups suggesting improvements |
| TQM | Everyone is responsible for quality; customer focus |
| Kaizen | Continuous small improvements by all staff |

Competitive advantage comes from fewer defects, lower waste, stronger reputation and loyal customers. Costs include training, time and the need for management commitment.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Productivity | Output per unit of input per time period |
| Efficiency | Production at minimum average cost |
| Capacity utilisation | Share of maximum output being produced |
| Buffer inventory | Minimum inventory held as a safety net |
| JIT | Receiving materials only as they are needed |
| Lean production | Using minimum resources and removing waste |
| TQM | Quality as everyone's responsibility |
| Kaizen | Continuous improvement |

## Exam traps

- Output up does not mean productivity up. Check the inputs.
- Efficiency needs a cost; productivity does not.
- JIT is not simply "no inventory": it needs reliable suppliers.
- Quality control is checking; quality assurance is preventing.
- Always say what a figure means for the business, for example "98% utilisation leaves no room for extra orders".
- For **discuss** and **assess**, give a judgement tied to the source (supplier reliability, demand pattern, skills of staff).

## Self-test

1. A firm makes 7,500 units using 250 labour hours. Calculate labour productivity.
2. A plant has maximum output 12,000 units and produces 9,000. Calculate capacity utilisation.
3. Total cost is $45,000 for 9,000 units. Calculate average cost.
4. Fixed costs are $36,000 a month. Calculate fixed cost per unit at 9,000 units and at 12,000 units.
5. Buffer inventory is 80 units, usage 30 units a day, lead time 3 days. Calculate the reorder level.
6. State one risk of JIT.
7. Distinguish quality control from quality assurance.
8. Name the method of production that suits a one-off bridge.
9. State one way to improve utilisation when capacity is over-used.
10. What does Kaizen mean?

### Answers

1. 7,500 / 250 = **30 units per hour**.
2. 9,000 / 12,000 x 100 = **75%**.
3. 45,000 / 9,000 = **$5.00** per unit.
4. 36,000 / 9,000 = **$4.00**; 36,000 / 12,000 = **$3.00**.
5. 80 + (30 x 3) = **170 units**.
6. Any one: late delivery stops production; no bulk discounts; higher ordering and delivery costs.
7. Control checks and rejects faulty output after the event; assurance sets standards at every stage to prevent faults.
8. Job production.
9. Any one: extra shifts, new equipment or premises, outsourcing, raising prices.
10. Continuous improvement through constant small changes by all employees.

## Answer frames

- **Calculate:** write the formula, substitute the figures, give the answer with units or a percentage.
- **Explain:** state one effect, then develop it with a detail from the source ("so unit cost falls because fixed costs are spread over more output").
- **Analyse:** build a chain of at least three linked steps, ending with the effect on profit, competitiveness or customers.
- **Discuss and assess:** give a case for and against, then a judgement that depends on a named feature of the business, such as reliability of suppliers or the steadiness of demand.
- **Useful link phrases:** "this means", "which leads to", "this is more likely to matter if", "the decision depends on".
