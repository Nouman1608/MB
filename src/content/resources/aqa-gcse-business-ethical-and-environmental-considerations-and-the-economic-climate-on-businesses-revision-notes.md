## Key terms

| Term | Meaning (as the specification uses it, or in general terms where stated) |
|---|---|
| Ethical behaviour | Acting in ways stakeholders consider both fair and honest (specification) |
| Trade off | Giving up some of one thing (such as profit) to gain another (such as reputation) |
| Environmental considerations | Traffic congestion, recycling, disposing of waste, noise and air pollution (specification list) |
| Sustainability | Global warming and using scarce resources (specification list); in general terms, meeting today's needs without harming the future |
| Interest rate | The cost of borrowing, and the reward for saving |
| Level of employment | How many people in the economy have jobs |
| Consumer spending | What households spend on goods and services |

## Ethics: the quick version

- Test: fair and honest, judged by stakeholders.
- Examples: fair prices to suppliers, honest advertising, fair pay and safe conditions, fair treatment of customers.
- Benefits: reputation, loyalty, easier recruitment, steadier supplier relations.
- Drawbacks: higher costs, higher prices, lower short-run profit.
- Trade off: ethics cost money, but may protect sales.

**Method for a trade-off answer**

1. Name the ethical action.
2. State the cost, using any figures.
3. State the benefit, and who gains.
4. Reach a judgement that depends on the business and its customers.

## Environmental considerations

| Area | One responsible action | One cost | One benefit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Traffic congestion | Off-peak deliveries | Less flexibility | Fewer complaints |
| Recycling | Sort and reuse waste | Bins and time | Lower disposal bills |
| Disposing of waste | Reduce packaging | Redesign cost | Lower waste charges |
| Noise pollution | Quieter machinery | Equipment cost | Better local relations |
| Air pollution | Cleaner vehicles | Purchase cost | Improved reputation |

- Businesses take responsibility by changing products, packaging, delivery and energy use.
- Consumers take responsibility by choosing low-waste products, recycling, repairing and sharing journeys.
- Link: if consumers shift, businesses that do not change risk losing sales.

## Sustainability

| Area | What a business can do | Short-term effect | Long-term effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Global warming | Cut energy use, change energy source, cut transport emissions | Cost of change | Lower bills, customer support |
| Using scarce resources | Use less, reuse, switch material | May cost more now | Protects against rising input costs |

Trade-off: sustainability can lower profit now and protect it later. Judge by how long the owners can wait and how much cash they have.

## Interest rates: cause-and-effect chains

| If the rate | Borrowers | Spending | Business effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rises | Pay more on loans and overdrafts | Households and firms spend and invest less | Profit and cash flow squeezed; credit-based sales fall |
| Falls | Pay less | Spending and investment easier | Profit and cash flow helped; more sales on credit |

Not required: why interest rates change.

**Calculating the effect of a rate change**

1. Interest = amount borrowed × rate.
2. Work out interest at the old rate and at the new rate.
3. Subtract to find the extra cost, and divide by 12 for a monthly figure.
4. Compare the extra cost with profit.

## Level of employment

| If employment | Household incomes | Demand | Recruitment and pay |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rises | Up | Up | Harder to recruit, pay may rise |
| Falls | Down | Down | Easier to recruit, less pay pressure |

Always give the how and the why.

## Consumer spending and income

- Income up: spending up, especially on non-essentials and expensive items.
- Income down: spending down, with basics falling least.
- Responses: change prices, range or promotion; adjust production and stock.

## Functional areas to link to

| Area | Example link |
|---|---|
| Business operations | Cleaner production, changed output levels |
| Human resources | Recruitment harder in high employment, ethical treatment of staff |
| Marketing | Reputation for ethics, price changes when incomes fall |
| Finance | Higher loan interest, cost of recycling schemes |

## Must-know distinctions

- Ethical is about fair and honest behaviour. Environmental and sustainability issues are separate lists in the specification.
- Environmental considerations are mostly local, immediate effects. Sustainability is global warming and scarce resources.
- Borrowers lose from a rate rise; savers gain.
- Employment affects incomes and demand; interest rates affect borrowing and spending.

## Command words and how to answer

| Command word | What to do | Shape of a good answer |
|---|---|---|
| State, identify | Give the point briefly | One short statement |
| Define | Say what the term means | Meaning plus, if useful, a short example |
| Calculate | Show working and units | Formula, substitution, answer with £ or % |
| Explain | Give a point and a reason | Point, because, so what |
| Analyse | Follow a chain of cause and effect | Action, effect, consequence for the business |
| Evaluate, justify | Weigh both sides and decide | Reasons for, reasons against, judgement with a reason |

For evaluate and justify questions, the judgement must come from the evidence in the case: the size of the cost, how long the business can wait for a benefit, what its customers care about and what its competitors do.

## Small worked reminders

**Ethics and cost.** A business buys 8,000 units a year. A fair-trade supplier charges 25p more per unit. Extra cost: 8,000 × 25p = £2,000 a year. If annual profit is £8,000, that is a quarter of it (2,000 ÷ 8,000 × 100 = 25%). The business then decides whether a better reputation, or a small price rise, can recover that £2,000.

**Environmental payback.** A recycling scheme costs £6,000 to set up and saves £2,000 a year after running costs. Payback is £6,000 ÷ £2,000 = 3 years. A short payback makes the scheme easier to justify. A long one makes it a bigger risk for a small business with little cash.

**Interest and demand together.** A rise in rates can hit a business twice: its own interest bill goes up, and customers who borrow buy less. Mention both when the question gives you a business that sells big-ticket items.

**Employment and spending together.** When employment falls, a business may lose sales and find recruiting easier at the same time. Say both effects, then say which matters more for the business in the question, for example a shop selling luxury goods or a firm hiring skilled staff.

## Judgement phrases that work

- "This is worthwhile if the business can afford the cost now, because the benefit builds over time."
- "The impact depends on how many of its customers care about this issue."
- "For a small business with limited cash, the higher cost carries more risk."
- "Overall, the most important factor is ..., because ..."

## Quick self-test

1. Define ethical behaviour.
2. State two environmental considerations named in the specification.
3. A business has a £50,000 loan. The rate rises from 3% to 5%. Calculate the extra interest a year.
4. A business has an £8,000 overdraft. The rate falls from 12% to 9%. Calculate the yearly saving.
5. A supplier raises the price of a part from 40p to 46p. Calculate the percentage increase, and the extra cost on 10,000 parts.
6. Give one benefit and one drawback of behaving ethically.
7. State the two sustainability areas named in the specification.
8. Explain one way a fall in the level of employment could affect a shop's sales.
9. Explain why a rise in interest rates could reduce a builder's sales.
10. A £200,000 loan carries 4% interest. Calculate the yearly interest, then the interest if the rate rises to 5.5%.

### Answers

1. Acting in ways stakeholders consider both fair and honest.
2. Any two of: traffic congestion, recycling, disposing of waste, noise and air pollution.
3. £50,000 × 3% = £1,500; £50,000 × 5% = £2,500; extra = £1,000.
4. £8,000 × 12% = £960; £8,000 × 9% = £720; saving = £240.
5. Increase 6p ÷ 40p × 100 = 15%. Extra cost 10,000 × 6p = £600.
6. Benefit: better reputation or loyalty. Drawback: higher costs or lower short-run profit.
7. Global warming and using scarce resources.
8. Fewer people have wages, so household incomes fall and customers buy fewer non-essential items, which lowers sales.
9. Borrowing for building work costs more, so households and firms delay projects, which lowers demand.
10. £200,000 × 4% = £8,000. At 5.5%, £11,000, so £3,000 more.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing an ethical or environmental action with only benefits, or only costs, so there is no trade off.
- Writing a judgement that repeats the question rather than using the business in the case.
- Confusing what interest does to borrowers and savers.
- Explaining why interest rates change, which is not required.
- Stating "unemployment is bad" without linking it to incomes, demand and sales.
- Giving a calculation answer without a working line or without the correct units (£ or %).
- Naming a stakeholder group without saying how it is affected.
- Treating recycling and sustainability as the same item.
- Missing the link to a functional area when the question names one.
