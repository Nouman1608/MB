These notes condense the full [study guide on The Media](/resources/aqa-a-level-sociology-7192-the-media/). Read that first if a term below is new to you.

They cover section 4.2.7 The Media (shown as 3.2.7 in the specification's at-a-glance list) of the AQA AS and A-level Sociology (7191, 7192) specification, Version 1.2 (14 October 2021). The Media is an A-level option in Paper 2: Topics in Sociology (2 hours, 80 marks, 33.3% of the A-level). The specification states: "Section A: one from option 1: 4.2.1, 4.2.2, 4.2.3 or 4.2.4" and "Section B: one from option 2: 4.2.5, 4.2.6, 4.2.7 or 4.2.8". So The Media is answered in Section B, "extended writing, 40 marks".

Keep track of what you have revised on the [AQA A-level Sociology hub](/boards/aqa/a-level/sociology/) and the [AQA A-level Sociology checklist](/checklists/aqa/a-level/sociology/). When you are ready, try the [practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-sociology-7192-the-media-practice/).

## The six content points

The specification lists these, and you need "both the evidence of and the sociological explanations for" each:

1. The new media and their significance for the role of the media in contemporary society.
2. The relationship between ownership and control.
3. The media, globalisation and popular culture.
4. Selection and presentation of the news.
5. Representations of age, social class, ethnicity, gender, sexuality and disability.
6. The relationship between the media, their content and presentation, and audiences.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| New media | Digital, interactive media such as social platforms, streaming and search |
| Convergence | Separate media merging on one device or platform |
| Digital divide | Unequal access to and skill with digital media |
| Concentration of ownership | Fewer companies owning a larger share of the media |
| Horizontal integration | Owning several firms in the same media sector |
| Vertical integration | Owning production, distribution and sale of content |
| Hegemony | Dominant ideas accepted as common sense |
| Cultural imperialism | One (usually Western) culture dominating others through media |
| Contra-flow | Media flowing from non-Western centres outward |
| News values | Criteria that make an event newsworthy |
| Gatekeeping | Deciding which stories are published |
| Agenda setting | Shaping which issues people think about |
| Hierarchy of credibility | Giving more weight to powerful sources |
| Symbolic annihilation | A group being absent, trivialised or condemned in media |
| Male gaze | Framing women as objects for a male viewer |
| Preferred reading | The meaning producers encode and hope audiences accept |

## Theories and studies at a glance

| Who | What | Finding or claim | One evaluation point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jenkins (2006) | *Convergence Culture* | Participatory culture: audiences create and share content | Ignores the digital divide |
| Keen (2007) | *The Cult of the Amateur* | Amateur content lowers quality | Elitist; amateurs have exposed real stories |
| Zuboff (2019) | Surveillance capitalism | Platforms profit from users' data | Users may still gain real benefits |
| Miliband (1969) | Instrumentalist Marxism | Owners use media to protect ruling-class interests | Journalists have some autonomy |
| Glasgow University Media Group (1976) | *Bad News* | Industrial disputes framed against workers | Critics question their coding of content |
| Herman and Chomsky (1988) | Propaganda model | Five filters shape news | Written about US media; may fit UK less well |
| Schiller (1969) | Cultural imperialism | Global media spread US values | Assumes passive audiences |
| Thussu (2007) | Contra-flow | Non-Western media flow outward | Flows remain unequal in size |
| Galtung and Ruge (1965) | News values | Negativity, elite persons and so on make news | Based on foreign news; updated by Harcup and O'Neill |
| McCombs and Shaw (1972) | Agenda setting | Voters' priorities matched media emphasis | Correlation, not proof of cause |
| Tuchman (1978) | Symbolic annihilation of women | Women absent or trivialised | Representations have diversified since |
| Gauntlett (2002) | Media and identity | More varied images give identity resources | May understate remaining stereotypes |
| Van Dijk (1991) | *Racism and the Press* | Minority groups linked to problems and conflict | Older press data |
| Barnes (1992) | Disabling imagery | Recurring disability stereotypes | More disabled presenters now |
| Hall (1973) | Encoding and decoding | Dominant, negotiated, oppositional readings | Hard to measure readings precisely |
| Philo (1990) | *Seeing and Believing* | News themes remembered and often believed | Experience can override media |

## Representations: one line per group

| Group | Evidence to use | Typical explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Age | Cohen (1972) on mods and rockers; Pearson (1983) on long-running fears about youth | Young people as a convenient "folk devil" for news values |
| Social class | Jones (2011) on "chavs"; Jensen (2014) on "poverty porn" | Marxists: ideology that blames the poor for inequality |
| Ethnicity | Van Dijk (1991): minorities linked to problems and conflict | News values and a mostly white newsroom culture |
| Gender | Tuchman (1978); Mulvey (1975); Ferguson (1983); Gauntlett (2002) | Feminists: patriarchy; postmodernists: more choice |
| Sexuality | Batchelor, Kitzinger and Burtney (2004): few positive images of lesbian and gay teenagers | Heteronormativity treated as the default |
| Disability | Barnes (1992): recurring stereotypes such as pitiable or sinister | Medical rather than social model of disability |

For each group, be ready to say whether representations are changing, and what evidence suggests so.

## Linking to the core themes

The specification asks you to connect topics to "socialisation, culture and identity" and "social differentiation, power and stratification".

- **Socialisation, culture and identity.** Representations and audience models show how the media may shape identity. Gauntlett and the postmodernists see media as a resource people use to build identity.
- **Social differentiation, power and stratification.** Ownership, news selection and stereotypes all raise questions about whose voice is heard. The digital divide shows unequal access to new media by class and age.

Using one of these themes in each extended answer helps show analysis, not just knowledge.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Instrumentalist vs hegemonic Marxism.** Owners' direct control versus a shared ideology among journalists.
- **Ownership vs control.** Owning a company is not the same as running daily editorial decisions.
- **News values vs ideology.** Pluralists say news follows audience interest; Marxists say it follows power.
- **Content vs effect.** A content analysis shows what is shown, not what audiences believe.
- **Passive vs active audience.** Hypodermic and cultivation models versus uses and gratifications and reception analysis.
- **Global vs hybrid culture.** Homogenisation versus mixing of global and local forms.
- **Optimists vs pessimists on new media.** Democratisation versus new forms of control.

## Mini essay plan

**Question type:** "Evaluate the view that the ownership of the media determines its content."

1. **Introduction.** Define ownership and content. Mention concentration and conglomerates.
2. **Instrumentalist Marxism (for).** Miliband; owners promoting their interests; apply to a cross-media owner.
3. **Hegemonic Marxism (for, but different).** Journalists' shared outlook; Glasgow group; content reflects ideology without orders.
4. **Pluralism (against).** Market demand, competition, Ofcom and BBC impartiality.
5. **Gatekeeping and news values (against).** Practical constraints shape content as much as owners do.
6. **New media (against and for).** Many voices online; but platforms are themselves concentrated.
7. **Audience link.** Even if owners shape content, reception analysis shows audiences may reject it.
8. **Judgement.** Ownership sets limits, but day-to-day content is shaped by many forces. Make a clear judgement and say why.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the section of the specification that covers The Media.
2. Which section of Paper 2 is The Media answered in?
3. Define convergence.
4. What is the difference between horizontal and vertical integration?
5. Name two of Herman and Chomsky's five filters.
6. Who introduced the term "male gaze"?
7. What did McCombs and Shaw study?
8. Give the three readings in Hall's encoding and decoding model.
9. Which writer described a "cult of femininity" in women's magazines?
10. What is a contra-flow?
11. What did Batchelor, Kitzinger and Burtney (2004) find about lesbian and gay teenagers in youth media?
12. Give one criticism of the hypodermic syringe model.

### Answers

1. Section 4.2.7.
2. Section B, which takes one from option 2.
3. Different media merging on one platform or device.
4. Horizontal: several firms in the same sector. Vertical: owning each stage from production to distribution.
5. Any two of ownership, advertising, sourcing, flak, anti-communism (later fear of an enemy).
6. Laura Mulvey (1975).
7. Agenda setting: whether voters' priorities matched the issues the media stressed.
8. Dominant (preferred), negotiated and oppositional.
9. Marjorie Ferguson, in *Forever Feminine* (1983).
10. Media flowing from non-Western centres to the rest of the world.
11. A lack of positive images of lesbian and gay teenagers.
12. It assumes a passive audience that reacts uniformly; reception studies show people interpret media differently.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing about "the media" in general instead of naming a type, platform or example.
- Covering one or two representation groups when the question asks about representations generally.
- Describing a theory without linking it to the wording of the question.
- Citing studies without showing what they found.
- Treating dated evidence as current without comment.
- Missing the methods angle: content analysis, experiments and focus groups each have limits.
- Listing views side by side without weighing them.
- Ignoring the core themes of identity and power, which the specification asks you to link to.
