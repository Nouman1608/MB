Condensed recall for **12.1 Experimental design**, Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620 (2026–2028). All of 12.1 is **Core**. Worked examples are in the [experimental design study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-experimental-design/); exam-style questions are in the [experimental design practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-experimental-design-practice/).

## The three outcomes

1. **Name apparatus** for time, temperature, mass and volume.
2. **Suggest advantages and disadvantages** of methods and apparatus.
3. **Describe** solvent, solute, solution, saturated solution, residue, filtrate.

## Apparatus

| Measure | Apparatus | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| time | stop-watch | measures to 1 s |
| temperature | thermometer | 1 °C divisions; read to 0.5 °C |
| mass | balance | precision of at least 0.1 g |
| exact fixed volume of liquid | volumetric pipette | one volume only, e.g. 25.0 cm³; use a pipette filler |
| accurate variable volume | burette | 50 cm³; reads top-down; volume = final − initial |
| approximate volume | measuring cylinder | quick; choose the smallest size that holds the volume |
| volume of gas | gas syringe | direct reading of gas produced |

## Choosing between volume apparatus

| If the question says... | Choose |
|---|---|
| "exactly 25.0 cm³ of alkali into a flask" | volumetric pipette |
| "add acid until the indicator changes colour" | burette |
| "about 50 cm³ of acid, in excess" | measuring cylinder |
| "measure the gas given off every 20 s" | gas syringe |

## Reading and recording

- Eye level with the scale (avoid **parallax**).
- Liquids: read the **bottom of the meniscus**.
- Read to the **nearest half-scale division** where required.
- Tables: **units in headings**, same number of decimal places down a column.
- Take **repeats** and calculate a **mean**; identify and leave out **anomalous** results.

## Variables

| Variable | Meaning | Example (temperature vs rate) |
|---|---|---|
| independent | the one you change | temperature of acid |
| dependent | the one you measure | time for reaction to finish |
| control | kept the same | volume and concentration of acid, mass of metal |

Choose an appropriate **number and range** of values for the independent variable – typically five or more, spread evenly.

## Judging methods: headings

- **Accuracy:** is the apparatus right for the size of the quantity?
- **Losses:** can gas escape, or solid be left behind?
- **Timing:** is the reading taken at the right moment?
- **Safety:** what is the hazard and how is it controlled?
- **Improvement:** state the change *and* why it helps.

Examples of good improvements:

- "Use a gas syringe instead of collecting over water, because carbon dioxide dissolves slightly in water."
- "Use a burette instead of a measuring cylinder, because it measures volume more accurately."
- "Insulate the beaker (for example a polystyrene cup), to reduce heat loss to the surroundings."

## The six terms (syllabus wording)

| Term | Description |
|---|---|
| Solvent | a substance that dissolves a solute |
| Solute | a substance that is dissolved in a solvent |
| Solution | a mixture of one or more solutes dissolved in a solvent |
| Saturated solution | a solution containing the maximum concentration of a solute dissolved in the solvent **at a specified temperature** |
| Residue | a substance that remains after evaporation, distillation, filtration or any similar process |
| Filtrate | a liquid or solution that has passed through a filter |

Picture filtration: **residue in the paper, filtrate in the flask**.

## Gas collection methods compared

| Method | Advantage | Disadvantage |
|---|---|---|
| gas syringe | measures volume directly; works for soluble gases | plunger can stick; limited maximum volume |
| over water in an upturned measuring cylinder | simple, cheap apparatus | unsuitable for gases that dissolve in water |
| loss of mass on a balance | continuous readings; no sealed apparatus | small mass changes for light gases |

## Exam traps

- "Pipette" alone may not score: say **volumetric pipette**.
- A **measuring cylinder** is not accurate enough for titration volumes.
- A saturated solution needs "**at a specified temperature**".
- Residue is not only from filtration: it is also what is left after **evaporation** or **distillation**.
- "Human error" and "more accurate" alone are not creditworthy evaluations.
- Burette readings increase as liquid runs out; do not subtract the wrong way round.

## Self-test

1. Name the apparatus used to measure the volume of a gas produced in a reaction.
2. Name the apparatus that delivers exactly 25.0 cm³ of a solution.
3. A thermometer has 1 °C divisions. To what precision should it be read?
4. Give one advantage of a measuring cylinder over a burette.
5. Give one disadvantage of a measuring cylinder compared with a burette.
6. Define a solute.
7. Define a saturated solution.
8. Sand and salt water are filtered. Name the residue and the filtrate.
9. In an experiment on the effect of concentration on rate, name the independent variable.
10. Why are repeat readings taken?
11. State one reason why a gas syringe may be preferred to collecting a gas over water.
12. A burette reads 1.20 cm³ at the start and 24.65 cm³ at the end. Calculate the volume added.

**Answers:**

1. Gas syringe.
2. Volumetric pipette.
3. To the nearest 0.5 °C.
4. It is quicker to use / can measure a large volume in one go.
5. It is less accurate.
6. A substance that is dissolved in a solvent.
7. A solution containing the maximum concentration of a solute dissolved in the solvent at a specified temperature.
8. Residue: sand. Filtrate: salt water (salt solution).
9. Concentration of the solution.
10. To identify anomalous results and calculate a reliable mean.
11. Some gases dissolve in water, so the volume measured over water would be too low.
12. 24.65 − 1.20 = 23.45 cm³.
