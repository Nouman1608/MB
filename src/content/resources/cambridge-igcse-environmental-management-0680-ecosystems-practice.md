## Section A

**1.** Define *community*. **[1]**

**2.** Distinguish between competition and predation. **[2]**

**3.** Write the word equation for aerobic respiration. **[2]**

**4.** Name **two** processes in the carbon cycle that release carbon dioxide into the atmosphere. **[2]**

**5.** State the role of chlorophyll in photosynthesis. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** Read the description of a rocky shore.

*Seaweeds grow on the rocks. Limpets and periwinkles graze on the seaweeds. Crabs eat the periwinkles. Gulls eat crabs, and nothing on the shore eats the gulls. There are about 400 limpets on one stretch of rock.*

(a) Identify a producer.
(b) Identify the apex predator.
(c) Identify the prey of the crabs.
(d) State what the "400 limpets" represents in ecological terms. **[4]**

**7.** In a meadow, grass captures 25,000 kJ per square metre per year. Grasshoppers receive 2,000 kJ and frogs that eat the grasshoppers receive 180 kJ.
(a) Calculate the percentage of energy passed from grass to grasshoppers.
(b) Calculate the percentage passed from grasshoppers to frogs.
(c) State **two** ways energy is lost between the grasshoppers and the frogs. **[5]**

**8.** A pyramid of numbers for a fruit tree shows 1 tree, 5,000 aphids, 50 ladybirds and 2 small birds. Explain why this pyramid is inverted at the base, and describe how a pyramid of energy for the same food chain would differ. **[3]**

**9.** A fast-growing water plant was introduced to a lake from another continent. Within ten years it covered most of the surface. Explain how this invasive species could reduce the populations of native plants and animals in the lake. **[3]**

**10.** Explain why a decline in insect pollinators near an orchard could reduce the farmer's fruit harvest. **[3]**

**11.** A tree dies in a forest. Describe **two** different routes by which the carbon in its wood could eventually return to the atmosphere as carbon dioxide. **[4]**

**12.** Over 20 years, the population of hares in a region rose and fell in cycles. The population of lynx, which eat hares, followed the same pattern a few years later. Explain this pattern. **[3]**

**13.** Explain how **two** named abiotic factors limit where organisms can live in an estuary, where a river meets the sea. **[4]**

---

## Answers

**1.** All the populations of different species living in an area at the same time [1].

**2.** Competition is a struggle between organisms for the same limited resource, such as food, light or space [1]; predation is when one animal hunts, kills and eats another [1].

**3.** glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water: correct reactants [1]; correct products [1].

**4.** Any two, 1 mark each: respiration [1]; combustion [1]. Also accept decomposition (by respiration of decomposers).

**5.** It captures light energy [1].

**6. (a)** Seaweeds [1]. **(b)** Gulls [1]. **(c)** Periwinkles [1]. **(d)** A population [1].

**7. (a)** 2,000 / 25,000 × 100 [1] = 8% [1].
**(b)** 180 / 2,000 × 100 = 9% [1].
**(c)** Any two, 1 mark each: respiration, with energy lost as heat [1]; movement [1]. Also accept: digestion, as undigested parts leave in faeces; excretion; parts of grasshoppers not eaten.

**8.** One large producer, the tree, provides enough food for thousands of small aphids [1], so the number of producers is smaller than the number of primary consumers [1]. A pyramid of energy would be a true pyramid with the widest bar at the bottom, because the tree contains the most energy and energy is lost at each trophic level [1].

**9.** The plant has no natural predators or diseases in the lake, so it spreads rapidly [1]; covering the surface blocks light, so native plants below cannot photosynthesise and die, and it outcompetes them for space and nutrients [1]; animals that depend on native plants for food or shelter decline, and when the dense plant dies, decomposition can reduce oxygen [1].

**10.** Insects transfer pollen from the anther of one flower to the stigma of another [1]; fewer pollinators means fewer flowers are pollinated [1]; pollination leads to fertilisation and then seed and fruit formation, so fewer fruits are produced [1].

**11.** Route 1: decomposers such as fungi and bacteria break down the wood [1] and release carbon dioxide through respiration [1]. Route 2: the wood is collected and burned as fuel [1], and combustion releases carbon dioxide [1]. Also accept: the wood is eaten by animals such as termites, which release carbon dioxide by respiration; or the wood is buried without decomposing and forms a fossil fuel over millions of years, which is later burned.

**12.** As the hare population rises, there is more food for lynx, so more lynx survive and breed [1]; more lynx eat more hares, so the hare population falls [1]; with less food, lynx numbers then fall, which lets the hare population rise again, so the lynx peaks follow the hare peaks [1].

**13.** Two factors, 2 marks each. Salinity [1]: it changes with the tide and distance from the sea, so only organisms that can tolerate changing salt levels survive [1]. Oxygen [1]: muddy sediment and decomposing material can have low oxygen, so only organisms that can survive low oxygen live buried in the mud [1]. Also accept: light, as cloudy water limits photosynthesis by plants and algae; temperature; pH; each with an explanation.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a habitat when asked for a niche, or a community when asked for a population.
- Dividing the wrong way in an efficiency calculation; always the higher level divided by the lower.
- Saying energy is "used up" without naming movement, respiration, digestion or excretion.
- Describing a predator-prey cycle without explaining why the predator peak comes later.
- Naming an abiotic factor without linking it to what the organism needs.
