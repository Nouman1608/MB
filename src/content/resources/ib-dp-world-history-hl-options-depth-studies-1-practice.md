## Section A

**1.** Identify **three** reasons for the European partition of Africa after 1880. **[3]**

**2.** State **two** reasons for Ethiopia's victory at Adwa in 1896. **[2]**

**3.** Identify **three** laws passed in the first five years of apartheid. **[3]**

**4.** Outline **two** consequences of the Sharpeville massacre. **[2]**

**5.** State **two** results of the Suez crisis of 1956 for Nasser. **[2]**

**6.** Identify **two** groups that opposed the Shah of Iran in 1978. **[2]**

---

## Section B

**7.** **(a)** Explain why most armed resistance to European conquest in Africa failed. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain **one** example of African resistance that delayed or changed colonial rule. **[2]**

**8.** **(a)** Explain why the National Party won the 1948 election in South Africa. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why the ANC turned to armed struggle in 1961. **[3]**

**9.** **(a)** Explain the aims of Nasser's domestic policies. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain **two** consequences of the Six-Day War of 1967 for the Arab world. **[4]**

**10.** Explain why the Arab-Israeli war of 1973 led to a peace process between Egypt and Israel. **[4]**

**11.** "Economic pressure was the main reason for the end of apartheid." To what extent do you agree with this statement? **[15]**

**12.** Evaluate the reasons for the success of the Iranian Revolution in 1979. **[15]**

---

## Answers

**1.** Any three, 1 mark each: search for raw materials and markets; investment and the protection of financial interests, as in Egypt; strategic concerns such as the route to India via the Suez Canal; national prestige and rivalry between European powers; new technology (quinine, steamships, machine guns) that reduced the cost of conquest; the rules agreed at the Berlin Conference (1884-85). **[3]**

**2.** Any two, 1 mark each: Menelik II's large army armed with modern rifles and artillery acquired from abroad; Italian underestimation of Ethiopian strength and poor planning; Menelik's success in uniting regional rulers behind him; knowledge of the terrain. **[2]**

**3.** Any three, 1 mark each: the Prohibition of Mixed Marriages Act (1949); the Population Registration Act (1950); the Group Areas Act (1950); the Suppression of Communism Act (1950); the Reservation of Separate Amenities Act (1953); the Bantu Education Act (1953). **[3]**

**4.** Any two, 1 mark each: the ANC and PAC were banned; a state of emergency and mass arrests followed; international condemnation grew; the ANC and PAC moved towards armed struggle; South Africa's isolation increased, leading to departure from the Commonwealth in 1961. **[2]**

**5.** Any two, 1 mark each: Nasser kept control of the canal; his prestige grew across the Arab world as the leader who had defied Britain and France; British and French influence in the region declined; Egypt drew closer to the Soviet Union. **[2]**

**6.** Any two, 1 mark each: the Shi'a clergy led by Khomeini; bazaar merchants; students and secular liberals such as the National Front; the left, including the Tudeh party and guerrilla groups; industrial and oil workers who went on strike. **[2]**

**7.** **(a)** Up to 4 marks: European firepower, especially breech-loading rifles and machine guns, overwhelmed African armies, as at Omdurman (1898) (1); Europeans exploited divisions between African states and recruited African troops and allies (1); Europeans could draw on greater resources and replace losses (1); disease and famine, sometimes caused by scorched-earth campaigns, weakened resistance, as after Maji Maji (1). **[4]**
**(b)** 2 marks for a developed example: Ethiopia's victory at Adwa preserved its independence until the Italian invasion of 1935; or Samori Touré's mobile resistance delayed French conquest of the upper Niger region for years; or Maji Maji led Germany to introduce reforms in its colonial administration of East Africa. **[2]**

**8.** **(a)** Up to 4 marks: Afrikaner nationalism, organized through the Broederbond and cultural bodies (1); white fears of African urbanization during the war and of the "black peril", which the National Party exploited with its apartheid programme (1); the electoral system weighted rural constituencies, so the party won more seats than the United Party despite fewer votes (1); Smuts's government was blamed for wartime problems and seen as too liberal by many white voters (1). **[4]**
**(b)** 1 mark each: the ANC was banned after Sharpeville, removing legal means of protest; non-violent campaigns such as the Defiance Campaign (1952) had been met with repression; leaders including Mandela concluded that only sabotage could pressure the state, forming Umkhonto we Sizwe (1961). **[3]**

**9.** **(a)** Up to 4 marks: land reform to break the power of large landowners and win peasant support (1); nationalization and state planning to modernize the economy and reduce foreign control (1); the Aswan High Dam to provide irrigation and electricity (1); expansion of education and welfare, and one-party rule to secure the regime (1). **[4]**
**(b)** 2 marks for each developed consequence: Israel occupied Sinai, Gaza, the West Bank, East Jerusalem and the Golan Heights, creating a new refugee crisis and a lasting territorial dispute; Nasser's prestige and pan-Arabism were badly damaged; Palestinian organizations such as the PLO grew more independent of Arab states; UN Resolution 242 set the "land for peace" principle. **[4]**

**10.** Up to 4 marks: Egypt's initial success in crossing the canal restored Arab pride, so Sadat could negotiate from strength (1); the oil embargo showed the war's global costs, increasing US pressure for a settlement (1); Kissinger's "shuttle diplomacy" produced disengagement agreements (1974-75) (1); Sadat's visit to Jerusalem (1977) led to the Camp David Accords (1978) and the peace treaty (1979), which returned Sinai to Egypt (1). **[4]**

**11.** Marked holistically using the 15-mark markbands; the indicative content is a guide, not a checklist. For: sanctions, the US Comprehensive Anti-Apartheid Act (1986), disinvestment by foreign banks and firms, the debt crisis of 1985, skills shortages caused by job reservation and Bantu education, and recession. Against: internal resistance (the UDF, COSATU, township uprisings from 1984) made the country ungovernable; the end of the Cold War removed the regime's claim to be a bulwark against communism and changed ANC funding; military costs in Angola and Namibia; leadership by de Klerk and Mandela in negotiations from 1990. A strong answer explains how economic pressure interacted with resistance, judges which factor was most important, and keeps within the section's dates. **[15]**

**12.** Marked holistically using the 15-mark markbands; the indicative content is a guide, not a checklist. Reasons: long-term grievances from the White Revolution's social effects and the Shah's secularism; repression by SAVAK and the closing of political space under the Rastakhiz system; economic crisis after the oil boom (inflation, austerity, campaigns against bazaar merchants); Khomeini's leadership and the clergy's mosque network; the breadth of the opposition coalition; strikes, including in the oil industry, that paralysed the economy; the Shah's illness, indecision and the loss of the army's will to suppress protest; the ambiguous position of the USA. A strong answer distinguishes long-term causes from the reasons the revolution succeeded in 1978-79 and reaches a judgement, for example that the alliance of very different groups behind Khomeini was decisive. **[15]**

## Frequently asked questions

**Paper 3 questions are only essays. Why include short questions?**
They test the precise knowledge each essay needs. Use them for quick recall, then spend most practice time on full essays.

**Can I write about the same country in two essays?**
Yes, if the questions are different and both fall within your sections.
