These notes cover the **gutter margin** statement in Topic 17 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417: "the purpose of setting gutter margins". ## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Gutter margin | Extra space added to the margin on the edge of the page that will be bound |
| Binding edge | The edge where pages are fastened together |
| Gutter position | The edge the gutter is added to: left or top |
| Mirror margins | Left and right margins become inside and outside, so facing pages mirror each other |
| Inside margin | The margin next to the spine on each page of a double-sided document |
| Outside margin | The margin at the outer edge, away from the spine |
| Text width | The width left for text after all margins and the gutter are subtracted |

## Purpose: what to write

- Adds space on the **binding edge**, on top of the normal margin.
- Stops text being **hidden** by staples, stitching, glue, combs, wire or punched holes.
- Stops text **disappearing into the fold**, because bound pages do not open flat.
- Keeps the **visible** margins balanced after binding.
- Lets the normal margins stay at the stated values while adding only the binding allowance.

## Where the gutter goes

| Situation | Gutter |
|---|---|
| Single-sided, bound on the left (report, ring binder) | Left |
| Single-sided, bound at the top (calendar, notepad) | Top |
| Double-sided, bound like a book (booklet, handbook) | Mirror margins: inside edge of every page |
| Not bound (a letter, a loose leaflet) | None needed |

In a double-sided bound document the binding is on the **left of right-hand (odd) pages** and the **right of left-hand (even) pages**. Mirror margins put the gutter on the inside each time.

## Calculations

- Text width = page width - left (inside) - right (outside) - gutter.
- Distance of text from the bound edge = margin on that edge + gutter.
- With a top gutter: text height = page height - top - bottom - gutter.

| Page | Margins | Gutter | Text width or height | Text starts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A4 portrait | L 2 cm, R 2 cm | 1 cm left | Width 16.0 cm | 3.0 cm from left edge |
| A4 portrait | L 3 cm, R 2 cm | 0.5 cm left | Width 15.5 cm | 3.5 cm from left edge |
| A4 portrait, mirrored | In 2.5 cm, Out 2 cm | 1 cm | Width 15.5 cm | 3.5 cm from the spine |
| A4 landscape | T 1.5 cm, B 1.5 cm | 2 cm top | Height 16.0 cm | 3.5 cm from top edge |

## Three-step reasoning

For any document in a question, answer three questions in order:

1. **Will it be bound, and how?** Stapled, stitched, glued, wire, comb, ring binder, or not at all.
2. **Which edge does the binding affect?** Left, top, or the inside edge that alternates on double-sided pages.
3. **So what setting?** Left gutter, top gutter, mirror margins with a gutter, or no gutter.

Example: a double-sided club magazine, stapled at the spine: bound, inside edge, so mirror margins with a gutter.

## Exam traps

- The gutter **margin** is not the space **between columns**.
- It is **added to** the margin, not a replacement for it.
- A **left** gutter on a **double-sided** document is wrong for every even page.
- Forgetting to subtract the gutter in a text-width calculation.
- "For binding" alone is thin; say what it **prevents** (text hidden or lost).
- A document that will not be bound does not need one.

## Self-test

1. Define a gutter margin.
2. Give two parts of the binding process that can hide text near the edge.
3. Why can text near the spine of a thick bound book be hard to read?
4. What are the two gutter position options?
5. Which documents might need a top gutter?
6. Where is the binding edge on an even-numbered page of a double-sided booklet?
7. What do mirror margins do?
8. A4 portrait, left 2.5 cm, right 2.5 cm, gutter 2 cm. What is the text width?
9. In question 8, how far from the left edge does the text start?
10. Why might a document gain a page after a gutter is set?
11. Why is setting a gutter better than just increasing the left margin of a double-sided document?
12. A student writes "the gutter is the gap between two columns of a newsletter". Correct the statement.

### Answers

1. Extra space added to the margin on the edge of the page that will be bound.
2. Any two: staples or stitching; glue; comb or wire binding; punched holes for a ring binder.
3. The pages curve into the fold near the spine and do not open flat, so text close to it is hidden.
4. Left and top.
5. Any one: a desk or wall calendar, a flip-over notepad, a report bound along the top.
6. On the right-hand edge of the page.
7. They turn left and right margins into inside and outside margins, so facing pages mirror each other and the gutter is added to the inside edge of every page.
8. 21.0 - 2.5 - 2.5 - 2 = 14.0 cm.
9. 2.5 + 2 = 4.5 cm.
10. The text area is narrower, so less text fits on each page and the text reflows.
11. With mirror margins, the gutter is placed on the inside edge of every page, which alternates sides; a bigger left margin would be on the wrong side for even pages.
12. In 0417 the gutter margin is extra space for binding on the bound edge of the page; the gap between columns is the spacing between columns.
