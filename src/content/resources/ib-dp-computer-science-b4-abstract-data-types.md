**B.4 Abstract data types** is the fourth and final sub-topic of Theme B in the IB Diploma Programme Computer Science course first assessed in 2027, and it is **HL only**. The IB subject brief gives it 23 recommended teaching hours at HL and none at SL, and says HL Paper 2 includes additional questions focused on OOP and abstract data types. SL students do not study B.4.

> **Scope of this page.** This overview is written from the IB's public *Diploma Programme Subject Brief, Sciences: Computer science, first assessment 2027* (© International Baccalaureate Organization 2024). The brief names B.4 and gives its hours and assessment but does **not** list which abstract data types are studied or the learning outcomes. Those are in the full DP Computer Science guide, available to IB schools through the Programme Resource Centre. Follow your school's copy of the full guide for the exact content and depth.

## What does the IB subject brief say about B.4?

| Item | Subject brief |
|---|---|
| Name | B.4 Abstract data types (HL only) |
| Theme | B, "Computational thinking and problem-solving" |
| Recommended teaching hours | 0 SL / 23 HL |
| Assessment | HL Paper 2: additional questions for HL focused on OOP and abstract data types |
| Paper 2 at HL | 2 hours, 40% of the final grade; some questions require programming, in Java or Python |

Together, B.3 (23 HL hours, against 7 at SL) and B.4 (23 HL hours, 0 at SL) account for most of the extra Theme B time at HL.

## Where does B.4 fit in the course?

Theme B "focuses on how we can use computing systems to solve real-world problems". B.4 follows B.1 Computational thinking, B.2 Programming and B.3 OOP, so it builds on programming and object oriented skills. The brief says the course can be studied in Python or Java, so B.4 work is done in the language your school uses.

## General background: what is an abstract data type?

*This section is general computer-science background, not the IB's list of B.4 outcomes.* An **abstract data type (ADT)** is defined by the values it can hold and the operations that can be carried out on it, independently of how it is implemented in a particular language. Separating "what it does" from "how it is stored" is an application of **abstraction**, one of the computational thinking abilities the brief lists. Which specific ADTs and operations B.4 requires is set out in the full guide.

## How should HL students prepare for B.4?

- Get the B.4 learning outcomes from the full guide and list every data type and operation named.
- For each one, practise implementing it in your course language and tracing operations on paper.
- Expect Paper 2 HL questions that combine B.4 with OOP (the brief groups the two in its description of the additional HL questions).
- Budget time: 23 hours is more than four times B.1's allocation and over half of B.2's.
- Use the B.1 process: specify the operations needed, decompose, abstract the data, then test and evaluate.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is B.4 on the SL exam?**
No. The brief labels B.4 "HL only" and gives it 0 SL hours. SL Paper 2 covers the three other Theme B topics.

**How many hours is B.4?**
23 recommended teaching hours at HL.

**Which abstract data types are in B.4?**
The public subject brief does not list them. They are in the full DP Computer Science guide, available through the IB Programme Resource Centre.

**Which paper assesses B.4?**
Paper 2, through additional HL questions focused on OOP and abstract data types.
