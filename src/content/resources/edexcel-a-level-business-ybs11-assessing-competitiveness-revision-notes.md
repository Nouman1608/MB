Condensed recall for section 3.3.5 of Unit 3. For explanations and worked examples, use the [Assessing competitiveness study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-business-ybs11-assessing-competitiveness/), then try the [practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-business-ybs11-assessing-competitiveness-practice/). The ratio formulas are not supplied in the exam, so learn them. Figures are invented.

## Formula sheet

```
Gross profit margin        = gross profit / revenue x 100
Profit for the year margin = profit for the year / revenue x 100
Current ratio              = current assets / current liabilities
Acid test ratio            = (current assets - inventory) / current liabilities
Capital employed           = non-current liabilities + total equity
Gearing                    = non-current liabilities / capital employed x 100
ROCE                       = operating profit / capital employed x 100
Labour productivity        = output (or revenue) / number of employees
Labour turnover            = leavers / average employees x 100
Retention rate             = staff who stayed / staff at the start x 100
Absenteeism                = days lost / total working days available x 100
```

## The statements

| Statement of comprehensive income | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|
| Revenue - cost of sales = gross profit | Non-current assets + current assets - current liabilities - non-current liabilities = net assets |
| Gross profit - other operating expenses = operating profit | Share capital + retained profits = total equity |
| Operating profit - interest = profit for the year | Net assets = total equity |
| Shows performance over a period | Shows position at one date |

**Stakeholder interest:** shareholders (returns), managers (margins and costs), lenders (profit to cover interest, gearing), suppliers (liquidity), employees (profit and security), government and competitors (tax and benchmarking).

## Reading the ratios

| Ratio | Rising means | Falling means | What to check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross profit margin | Better control of direct costs or higher prices | Dearer materials, discounting, inefficiency | Cost of sales against revenue |
| Profit for the year margin | Overall cost control | Overheads or interest rising faster than sales | Which cost line has grown |
| Current ratio | Stronger liquidity (or too much idle inventory) | Weaker liquidity | Overdraft, payables |
| Acid test ratio | Stronger liquidity | Cannot cover debts from liquid assets | Inventory build-up, cash |
| Gearing | More risk from borrowing | Less risk, lower interest burden | Interest cover, new loans |
| ROCE | More operating profit per dollar invested | Capital not earning enough | Compare with interest rates and rivals |

- An acid test below 1 is a warning, but some retailers operate below it safely because they sell for cash.
- Compare every ratio with the previous year, rivals and the industry.
- Then say what caused the change and what the business should do.

## Limitations of ratio analysis

- Based on past data; the future may differ.
- Different accounting policies reduce comparability.
- Ignores non-financial factors such as quality, brand and morale.
- One year may be unusual or seasonal.
- Inflation distorts comparisons across years.
- Year-end figures can be window-dressed.

## Human resource measures

| Measure | Use | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Labour productivity | Output per worker; compare over time and with rivals | Affected by machinery, price changes, mix of work |
| Labour turnover | Cost of recruitment and training; signals morale | Some turnover is healthy; averages hide departments |
| Retention | Share of staff kept; shows loyalty | Staying does not mean being productive |
| Absenteeism | Lost days and disruption | Some absence is legitimate; short-term spikes mislead |

| Strategy | Benefit | Drawback |
|---|---|---|
| Financial rewards | Direct incentive | Costly; short-term focus |
| Employee share ownership | Loyalty; shared success | Value depends on share price |
| Consultation | Better decisions; voice | Slow; expectations must be met |
| Empowerment | Motivation; speed | Needs training and trust |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Operating profit | Gross profit minus other operating expenses |
| Capital employed | Long-term finance: non-current liabilities plus equity |
| Gearing | Share of capital employed that is long-term debt |
| ROCE | Operating profit earned per $100 of capital employed |
| Labour turnover | Percentage of employees who leave in a period |
| Retention | Percentage of employees who stay |
| Empowerment | Giving staff authority over their own work |

## Exam traps

- ROCE uses operating profit, not profit for the year.
- Capital employed excludes current liabilities.
- Gearing uses non-current liabilities only.
- The acid test excludes inventory; the current ratio includes it.
- A single ratio is not a verdict. Use at least two, and compare with something.
- Turnover is not always bad; some staff leaving can be healthy.
- For **assess**, give a judgement that weighs profitability against liquidity or the short term against the long term.

## Answer frames

- **Ratio comparison:** quote both years, state the direction and size of change, give a cause from the source, and say what it means for competitiveness.
- **Decision from ratios:** say what the ratios show, what they miss, and what the business should do next.
- **HR strategy:** name the strategy, say how it changes behaviour (motivation, voice, control), give a cost, and judge if it suits the business.

## Self-test

1. Revenue $600,000; cost of sales $390,000; other operating expenses $120,000; interest $15,000. Calculate gross profit margin and profit for the year margin.
2. Current assets $210,000 (inventory $90,000); current liabilities $140,000. Calculate the current ratio and acid test ratio.
3. Non-current liabilities are $160,000 and total equity is $400,000. Calculate capital employed and gearing.
4. Operating profit is $90,000. Calculate ROCE using your answer to question 3.
5. Average employees are 400 and 36 leave. Calculate labour turnover.
6. Employees work 230 days each and 3,200 days are lost to absence among the 400 staff. Calculate absenteeism.
7. Revenue is $600,000 with 400 employees. Calculate revenue per employee.
8. Name one limitation of ratio analysis.
9. Give one reason for high labour turnover.
10. State one drawback of employee share ownership.

### Answers

1. Gross profit = 600 - 390 = 210 (in $000), so **35.0%**. Operating profit = 210 - 120 = 90; profit for the year = 90 - 15 = 75; margin = 75 / 600 x 100 = **12.5%**.
2. Current ratio = 210 / 140 = **1.5**; acid test = (210 - 90) / 140 = **0.86**.
3. Capital employed = 160 + 400 = **$560,000**; gearing = 160 / 560 x 100 = **28.6%**.
4. 90 / 560 x 100 = **16.1%**.
5. 36 / 400 x 100 = **9%**.
6. Total days = 400 x 230 = 92,000; 3,200 / 92,000 x 100 = **3.5%** (to one decimal place).
7. 600,000 / 400 = **$1,500**.
8. Any one: past data; accounting policies differ; ignores non-financial factors; one year may be unusual; inflation distorts.
9. Any one: low pay; poor management; lack of promotion; poor working conditions; better offers elsewhere.
10. Any one: the value depends on the share price; does little for low-paid or short-term staff; risk to employees' savings.
