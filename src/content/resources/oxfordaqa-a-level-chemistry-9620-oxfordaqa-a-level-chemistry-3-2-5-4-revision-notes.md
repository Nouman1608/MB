These notes condense **3.2.5.4 Formation of coloured ions** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International A2 content, examined in Unit 3 and able to reappear in Unit 5. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [formation of coloured ions study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-2-5-4/); then try the [formation of coloured ions practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-2-5-4-practice/).

## The subtopic in five lines

1. A coloured ion **absorbs** some visible wavelengths; the rest are **transmitted or reflected**, and that is the colour seen.
2. The energy absorbed promotes a **d electron** from the **ground state** to an **excited state**.
3. **ΔE = hν = hc/λ**: a bigger gap means higher frequency and shorter wavelength absorbed.
4. Changing the **oxidation state**, **co-ordination number** or **ligand** changes ΔE, so the colour changes.
5. A **colorimeter** with a calibration graph gives the concentration of a coloured ion.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Ground state | Lowest-energy arrangement of the d electrons |
| Excited state | Higher-energy arrangement after a d electron absorbs a photon |
| ΔE | Energy gap between ground and excited states |
| Frequency, ν | Number of waves per second (Hz, s⁻¹) |
| Wavelength, λ | Distance between successive peaks (m; often quoted in nm) |
| Planck constant, h | 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s |
| Transmitted light | Light that passes through a solution and reaches the eye |
| Complementary colour | Roughly the colour seen when a given colour is absorbed |
| Colorimeter | Instrument measuring absorbance of light of one colour by a solution |
| Calibration graph | Absorbance plotted against concentration for standard solutions |

## The equation and its units

```
ΔE = hν          ν in Hz (s⁻¹)
ΔE = hc/λ        λ in m; 1 nm = 1 × 10⁻⁹ m
c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹    h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s    L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹
```

**Unit route:** nm → m; J per ion × L → J mol⁻¹; ÷ 1000 → kJ mol⁻¹. Reverse each step for the opposite direction.

| λ absorbed | ΔE per ion | ΔE per mole |
|---|---|---|
| 450 nm | 4.42 × 10⁻¹⁹ J | 266 kJ mol⁻¹ |
| 590 nm | 3.37 × 10⁻¹⁹ J | 203 kJ mol⁻¹ |
| 800 nm | 2.49 × 10⁻¹⁹ J | 150 kJ mol⁻¹ |

Shorter wavelength, bigger ΔE.

## Absorbed and seen

| Absorbed | Seen |
|---|---|
| red | blue-green |
| orange | blue |
| yellow | violet |
| green | purple |
| blue | orange |
| violet | yellow |

## Colourless ions

No d–d transition is possible with an empty or a full 3d sub-level: Sc³⁺ and Ti⁴⁺ (3d⁰), Zn²⁺ (3d¹⁰). These ions are colourless in solution.

## Colour changes to quote

| What changes | Example | Colours |
|---|---|---|
| Ligand only | [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + excess NH₃ → [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ | pale blue → deep blue |
| Ligand and co-ordination number | [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ → [CuCl₄]²⁻ | blue → yellow (green mixture on the way) |
| Ligand and co-ordination number | [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ → [CoCl₄]²⁻ | pink → blue |
| Oxidation state | Fe²⁺(aq) → Fe³⁺(aq) | pale green → yellow-brown |
| Oxidation state | V³⁺(aq) → V²⁺(aq) | green → violet |

**Three-link explanation:** change in complex → change in ΔE → different wavelength absorbed, so a different colour is transmitted.

## Colorimetry in five steps

1. Filter of the colour **absorbed** (complementary to the colour seen).
2. Zero with a blank (solvent or reagents without the metal ion).
3. Measure absorbance of standards of known concentration.
4. Plot absorbance against concentration.
5. Measure the unknown (treated identically) and read off; scale for any dilution.

Intensify a pale colour by adding a ligand (for example excess NH₃ for Cu²⁺) to standards and unknown alike.

## Exam traps

- "Absorbs blue light so it looks blue": wrong. It transmits blue.
- "Electrons emit light": wrong. Colour here comes from absorption.
- λ must be in metres; 650 nm is 6.50 × 10⁻⁷ m.
- Per mole means × L; per ion means ÷ L. Do not do both.
- kJ mol⁻¹ answers need ÷ 1000 at the end.
- A colour-change answer that says only "different ligand" misses the ΔE and wavelength marks.
- Filter colour is the complementary colour, not the solution's colour.
- Diluted samples: multiply the concentration read from the graph by the dilution factor.

## Self-test

Answer without looking, then check below.

1. What happens to a d electron when visible light is absorbed?
2. Why does a solution of [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ look blue?
3. Write ΔE in terms of frequency and in terms of wavelength.
4. Calculate ΔE for one ion that absorbs light of wavelength 620 nm.
5. Convert your answer to question 4 into kJ mol⁻¹.
6. A transition needs 250 kJ mol⁻¹. Calculate the wavelength absorbed, in nm.
7. Why is Zn²⁺(aq) colourless?
8. Name the three changes the specification says alter ΔE.
9. Excess ammonia is added to aqueous copper(II) ions. State the colour change and the reason for it.
10. Why is a red filter a sensible choice for measuring a blue solution in a colorimeter?
11. What is the purpose of the blank in colorimetry?
12. A sample diluted from 10.0 cm³ to 50.0 cm³ reads 0.0180 mol dm⁻³ from the calibration graph. What was the original concentration?

### Answers

1. It is promoted from the ground state to an excited state (a higher-energy d orbital).
2. It absorbs light at the red–orange end of the spectrum and transmits the rest, which looks blue.
3. ΔE = hν and ΔE = hc/λ.
4. (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸) ÷ 6.20 × 10⁻⁷ = 3.21 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.
5. 3.208 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 6.02 × 10²³ ÷ 1000 = 193 kJ mol⁻¹.
6. 250 000 ÷ 6.02 × 10²³ = 4.15 × 10⁻¹⁹ J; λ = hc/ΔE = 4.79 × 10⁻⁷ m = 479 nm.
7. Its 3d sub-level is full (3d¹⁰), so no d electron can be promoted to a higher d orbital.
8. Oxidation state, co-ordination number, ligand.
9. Pale blue to deep blue; NH₃ ligands replace some H₂O ligands, ΔE changes, so a different wavelength is absorbed.
10. The blue solution absorbs red (and orange) light most strongly, so absorbance changes most with concentration at that colour.
11. To set zero absorbance so that only the coloured ion's absorption is measured.
12. 0.0180 × 5 = 0.0900 mol dm⁻³.
