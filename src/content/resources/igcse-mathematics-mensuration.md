This guide covers **Topic 5 Mensuration**, for Cambridge IGCSE
Mathematics 0580, 2025–2027 series. All five subtopics (C5.1–C5.5)
have both Core and Extended versions; the differences between the
two tiers are smaller and more specific here than in most other
topics, so this guide flags exactly where Core and Extended content
diverges rather than presenting two largely separate lists.

## Where this fits in 0580

Mensuration is the fifth of nine topics in 0580 and depends directly
on [Geometry](/resources/igcse-mathematics-geometry/) for its shape
vocabulary — you cannot calculate the area of a trapezium or the
volume of a prism without already knowing what those terms mean from
Topic 4. It also depends on Topic 2's algebraic substitution skills,
since most mensuration questions require substituting given
measurements into a formula and then rearranging or simplifying the
result.

## Syllabus coverage

**CAMBRIDGE IGCSE MATHEMATICS 0580 — TOPIC 5 MENSURATION**

- C5.1 Units of measure — using metric units of mass, length, area,
volume and capacity in practical situations, and converting
quantities into larger or smaller units, including between different
units of area (for example, cm² ↔ m²) and between units of volume
and capacity (for example, m³ ↔ litres)
- C5.2 Area and perimeter — carrying out calculations involving the
perimeter and area of a rectangle, triangle, parallelogram and
trapezium; except for the area of a triangle, formulas are not given
and must be known
- C5.3 Circles, arcs and sectors — carrying out calculations
involving the circumference and area of a circle, and involving arc
length and sector area as fractions of the circumference and area of
a circle, where the sector angle is a factor of 360°; answers may be
asked for in terms of π; formulas are given in the List of formulas
- C5.4 Surface area and volume — carrying out calculations and
solving problems involving the surface area and volume of a cuboid,
prism, cylinder, sphere, pyramid and cone; answers may be asked for
in terms of π; the curved surface area of a cylinder and cone, the
surface area of a sphere, and the volumes of a prism, pyramid,
cylinder, cone and sphere are all given in the List of formulas; the
term "prism" refers to any solid with a uniform cross-section, such
as a cylindrical sector
- C5.5 Compound shapes and parts of shapes — carrying out
calculations and solving problems involving perimeters and areas of
compound shapes and parts of shapes, and surface areas and volumes of
compound solids and parts of solids (for example, finding the volume
of half a sphere); answers may be asked for in terms of π

**Extended differences from Core**

- E5.3 removes the restriction that the sector angle must be a factor
of 360°, and explicitly includes minor and major sectors — Extended
candidates must be able to work with any sector angle, not only ones
that divide evenly into a full turn
- E5.5 extends to more complex compound solids, with the syllabus's
own example moving from "half a sphere" at Core to a frustum at
Extended — Extended candidates should be ready to calculate the
surface area and volume of a frustum (a cone or pyramid with its top
cut off), not just simpler compound shapes

## How to approach it

Because formulas for circles, arcs, sectors, and 3D surface area and
volume are given in the List of formulas, the exam skill being tested
is not memorising them but selecting the right one and substituting
correctly — practise identifying which given formula a question
requires before attempting to recall it from memory. By contrast, the
area formulas for a rectangle, triangle (only its own formula is
given), parallelogram and trapezium in C5.2/E5.2 are not provided, so
these four must be memorised precisely. Keep units disciplined
throughout a multi-step problem: converting between cm and m, or
between cm³ and litres, part-way through a calculation and forgetting
to convert back (or converting only one of several measurements) is
one of the most common ways marks are lost in this topic, especially
in compound-shape questions that mix units from different parts of
the question. For compound shapes and solids, always identify the
simple shapes a compound figure is built from before calculating —
sketching a quick decomposition (a compound shape as a rectangle plus
a triangle, for example, or a compound solid as a cylinder plus a
cone) makes the arithmetic that follows far less error-prone than
trying to compute the whole figure at once.

## Worked example: sector area with a non-factor angle (Extended)

Find the area of a sector with radius 8 cm and sector angle 110°.

```
Sector area = (angle / 360) x pi x r^2
            = (110 / 360) x pi x 8^2
            = (110 / 360) x pi x 64
            ~= 61.5 cm^2 (3 s.f.)
```

At Core tier, this exact question would not appear, since 110° is
not a factor of 360° — Core sector questions are restricted to
angles such as 90°, 60°, 45° or 120° that divide evenly into a full
turn. Recognising which tier a given sector angle belongs to is a
quick, reliable way to check whether a practice question matches your
own exam entry.

## Common mistakes

Using a formula from memory when the correct version is already
given in the List of formulas, introducing an avoidable error.
Forgetting the final unit conversion in a multi-step problem that
mixes units (for example, calculating in cm but being asked for an
answer in m³). Applying a sector-area or arc-length calculation as if
the angle were a factor of 360° when working at Extended tier, where
this restriction does not apply. Treating a compound shape as a
single unfamiliar figure instead of decomposing it into simple shapes
first. Leaving an answer that should be "in terms of π" as a rounded
decimal instead of retaining π in the final expression.

## Quick revision checklist

- Memorise the area formulas for a rectangle, triangle, parallelogram
  and trapezium, since these are not given.
- Practise locating and correctly substituting into the given
  formulas for circles, arcs, sectors, and 3D surface area and
  volume.
- Convert units confidently between mm/cm/m/km (and their squared and
  cubed forms) and between volume and capacity units.
- Practise decomposing compound shapes and solids into simple
  components before calculating.
- At Extended tier, practise sector and arc questions with angles
  that are not factors of 360°, and compound solids involving a
  frustum.
