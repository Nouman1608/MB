This guide covers outcomes **1.3.1 Statements of profit or loss (and other comprehensive income) showing the gross profit and profit for the year** and **1.3.2 Statements of financial position**. They open Topic 1.3, Financial statements of organisations, in Unit 1 (The Accounting System and Costing) of the Pearson Edexcel International Advanced Subsidiary/Advanced Level in Accounting (YAC11), Specification Issue 2, September 2018. This is International AS content. All amounts are in dollars and every business named is invented.

Use it with the [financial statements of organisations revision notes](/resources/edexcel-ial-accounting-financial-statements-of-organisations-revision-notes/) and the [sole trader practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-accounting-yac11-financial-statements-of-sole-traders-practice/). Year-end adjustments, which change the figures used here, are in the [year-end adjustments guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-accounting-yac11-year-end-adjustments/).

## What the specification asks for

| Outcome | Wording | What you must be able to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1.3.1 | Statements of profit or loss (and other comprehensive income) showing the gross profit and profit for the year | Prepare the statement from a trial balance, with cost of sales, gross profit, other income, expenses and profit for the year |
| 1.3.2 | Statements of financial position | Prepare the statement with non-current assets, current assets, current liabilities, working capital and the capital section |

Questions use the command word **prepare**: arrange the information into the standard format, with a correct heading, subtotals and totals. Marks are given for each correct figure and for the format.

## The two statements and how they link

The **statement of profit or loss** covers a period of time ("for the year ended") and measures performance. The **statement of financial position** is a snapshot on one date ("at") and shows what the business owns and owes. They connect through the capital section: profit for the year is added to the owner's capital and drawings are deducted.

Headings matter. Write the business name, the statement title and the date line on every statement. A missing or wrong date line loses a presentation mark.

## Step 1: the trial balance is the starting point

Every question gives a trial balance (or a list of balances) and some notes. Before writing anything, sort each balance into one of these groups.

| Group | Examples | Goes to |
|---|---|---|
| Income | Revenue, discount received, commission received | Statement of profit or loss |
| Cost of sales items | Opening inventory, purchases, carriage inwards, returns | Cost of sales section |
| Expenses | Wages, rent, carriage outwards, discount allowed | Statement of profit or loss |
| Assets | Premises, equipment, receivables, bank, cash | Statement of financial position |
| Liabilities | Trade payables, loans, overdraft | Statement of financial position |
| Owner's interest | Capital, drawings | Capital section |

Closing inventory is not in the trial balance. It arrives as a note and is used **twice**: as a deduction in cost of sales and as a current asset.

## Layout choices the examiner accepts

- The cost of sales section may be shown in a column layout, as above, or as a single line if the question gives the figure.
- Other income can be added after gross profit, as above, or shown as a separate line within income. Expenses are always deducted after it.
- Net current assets and working capital mean the same thing. Use whichever the question uses.
- Non-current liabilities, such as a bank loan repayable in five years, are deducted below working capital before net assets.

## Other comprehensive income

The heading of 1.3.1 includes "other comprehensive income". It records gains and losses that bypass profit for the year, the main example being a **revaluation gain on non-current assets**. If Kessler Tools revalued its premises from $90,000 to $105,000, the $15,000 gain would not be in profit for the year. It would be shown as other comprehensive income, and the premises would appear at $105,000 on the statement of financial position, with the gain added to the owner's interest. Total comprehensive income is profit for the year plus other comprehensive income.

## Interpreting what the statements show

The statements also feed analysis. From Kessler Tools, gross profit as a percentage of net revenue is 74,150 ÷ 183,200 × 100 = 40.5%, and profit for the year as a percentage of net revenue is 32,640 ÷ 183,200 × 100 = 17.8%. The gap between the two, about 22.7 percentage points, is the share of revenue taken by net expenses. Working capital of $27,250 means the business can cover its trade payables more than three times from current assets (39,050 ÷ 11,800 = 3.3). The [ratios guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-accounting-yac11-ratios/) develops this.

## Common mistakes

- Deducting closing inventory in the cost of sales but leaving it out of current assets.
- Putting carriage inwards among expenses, or carriage outwards in cost of sales.
- Treating drawings as an expense in the statement of profit or loss. They are a reduction in capital, not a cost of running the business.
- Showing discount allowed as income and discount received as an expense. It is the other way round.
- Using the opening capital in the statement of financial position without adding profit and deducting drawings.
- Omitting the date line, or writing "for the year ended" on the statement of financial position.
- Netting provision for depreciation against the wrong asset class, or forgetting that premises may carry no depreciation.
- Forcing the statement to balance with an invented figure instead of finding the error.

## Exam technique

- **Prepare** means use the standard format. Show subtotals for net revenue, cost of sales, gross profit and profit for the year, and for non-current assets, current assets and working capital.
- Show workings beside the statement for any figure that is not a straight copy from the trial balance. Marks for method are often available when the final figure is wrong.
- Present the capital section in full: opening capital, profit, drawings, closing capital. Examiners look for each line.
- Read the notes under the trial balance before you start. Closing inventory, accruals, prepayments and depreciation instructions are always there.
- Spend a minute checking that net assets equal capital. It is the fastest self-mark you have.
