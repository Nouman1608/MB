Condensed notes for the coursework reading essay in Pearson Edexcel International GCSE English
Language A (4EA1). For the full method and a worked plan, use the
[Assignment A Poetry and Prose Texts study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-english-language-4ea1-assignment-a-poetry-and-prose-texts/).

## Requirements checklist

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Texts | Three from Anthology Part 2 |
| Mix | At least one poetry text and one prose text |
| Focus | Chosen by you or your teacher; for example setting, character(s), event(s), theme(s) |
| Length | About 1200 words (advisory; no penalty for exceeding it) |
| Marks | 30: AO1 12, AO2 18 |
| Assessment | Internally assessed, externally moderated |
| Feedback | General suggestions at draft stage only; you make the corrections |
| After marking | No rewriting |

## Testing a focus

- Central to **all three** texts?
- Presented **differently** by each writer?
- Shaped by **different methods**, giving AO2 range?

If the answer to any is no, change a text or narrow the focus.

## Level wording to aim for

| AO | Level | Key phrases from the grid |
|---|---|---|
| AO1 | 3 (7–9) | "Clear and relevant selection"; "relevant interpretation" |
| AO1 | 4 (10–12) | "Detailed and persuasive selection"; "discriminating use of supporting quotations"; "perceptive interpretation" |
| AO2 | 3 (7–10) | "Clear explanations of the effects of language and structure" |
| AO2 | 4 (11–14) | "Thorough and confident selection"; "detailed exploration" |
| AO2 | 5 (15–18) | "Subtle and discriminating selection"; "perceptive analysis" |

Marks are awarded on a **best fit** basis across the whole essay.

## Two essay structures

| Structure | How | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Integrated | Each paragraph on one aspect of the focus, discussing two or three texts | Trios with clear similarities and contrasts |
| Sequenced with links | A section per text, each opening with a link to the last | Trios with very different methods |

## Paragraph recipe

1. Point about the **focus** (not about plot).
2. **Textual reference**: a short quotation or a precise moment.
3. **Method**: language, form or structure.
4. **Effect** on the reader.
5. **Idea or perspective** the writer conveys.
6. **Link** to another text or back to the thesis.

## Lifting a sentence: Level 3 to Level 5

**Level 3 (clear explanation):** *"Frost uses personification when the saw 'snarled', which makes it
sound dangerous."*

**Level 4 (detailed exploration):** *"Frost personifies the saw as an animal that 'snarled', so it seems
to have a will of its own, and this prepares the reader for the moment it seems to leap at the boy."*

**Level 5 (perceptive analysis, connected):** *"Frost gives the saw an animal's voice while giving the
adults almost none, so the machine seems more alive than the people around the boy; Chopin reverses
this pattern, filling Louise's silent room with the voices of spring outside, so in both texts what is
not human speaks loudest at the moment of crisis."*

Each step adds a reason, then a connection to another text.

## Draft-stage points to check

- Thesis names all three texts and makes a claim.
- Each paragraph opens with a point about the focus.
- Every text has at least one close-reading moment.
- Structure (openings, turns, endings) is analysed, not just language.
- No paragraph is mostly plot.
- Quotations are short and embedded in your sentences.
- Conclusion makes a judgement, not a summary.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Focus | The aspect (theme, setting, character, event) the essay examines |
| Thesis | Your overall answer to the task, stated early |
| Textual reference | A quotation or a reference to a specific moment or event |
| Perspective | A writer's viewpoint or attitude |
| Best fit | Marking that balances strengths and weaknesses to choose a level |
| Moderation | External check of a centre's marking by Pearson |
| Authentication | Signed confirmation that the work is your own |

## Traps

- Three poems: invalid mix.
- A focus that fits only one text well.
- Three separate essays joined by "also".
- Retelling plots or paraphrasing poems.
- Long quotations with short comments.
- Leaving structure out of AO2.
- Assuming you can revise after marking.
- Using online essays or AI text: work must be your own, and sources acknowledged.

## Self-test

1. How many texts must Assignment A discuss, and what mix is required?
2. What is the advisory word count, and is going over penalised?
3. How are the 30 marks split between AOs?
4. Give two examples of a focus suggested by the specification.
5. Complete the suggested task format: "Discuss how the writers use language and structure to present..."
6. What counts as a textual reference?
7. What phrase describes AO2 at Level 5?
8. What may your teacher do at the draft stage?
9. What is forbidden after marking?
10. What is "best fit" marking?
11. Name two advantages of an integrated essay structure.
12. Which AO carries more marks, and what does that mean for each paragraph?

**Answers:** 1. Three; at least one poetry text and one prose text. 2. About 1200 words; no penalty for
exceeding it. 3. AO1 12, AO2 18. 4. Any two: setting, character(s), event(s), theme(s). 5. "...[focus] in
[text 1], [text 2] and [text 3]." 6. A direct quotation or a reference to a specific moment or event.
7. "Subtle and discriminating selection" and "perceptive analysis". 8. Suggest general improvements;
you must make any corrections. 9. Rewriting the work. 10. Choosing the level that best matches the
essay overall, balancing strengths and weaknesses. 11. Connections are visible throughout; methods
are compared directly; the essay builds one argument. 12. AO2 (18); most of each paragraph should
analyse language and structure.
