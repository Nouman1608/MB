These questions cover sub-topic 5(b), **Selective breeding** (specification points 5.10 and 5.11), of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Biology (4BI1) specification, Issue 3. The qualification is untiered and both points can be examined on either paper. The set applies to the June and November series examined under Issue 3. A calculator may be used.

Learn the content first with the [selective breeding study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-biology-4bi1-selective-breeding/) and the [revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-biology-4bi1-selective-breeding-revision-notes/). ## Questions

**1.** State what is meant by selective breeding. **[2]**

**2.** A plant breeder wants to produce a variety of tomato that has large fruit and resists a fungal disease.

**(a)** Describe how the breeder can develop this variety. **[4]**
**(b)** Suggest why the breeder keeps seeds only from the best plants. **[1]**

**3.** A wheat variety yields a mean of 6.0 tonnes per hectare. A selectively bred variety yields a mean of 7.8 tonnes per hectare.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in yield. **[2]**
**(b)** A farmer has a field of 250 hectares. Calculate the extra mass of wheat harvested if the new variety is grown on all the land. **[2]**

**4.** A farmer selectively breeds hens. The table shows the mean number of eggs laid per hen per year in a flock.

| Generation | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mean eggs per hen | 180 | 192 | 204 | 216 | 228 | 240 | 252 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase from generation 0 to generation 6. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mean increase per generation. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why selective breeding must be carried out for several generations. **[2]**

**5.** A cattle farmer wants animals that grow quickly and produce lean meat.

**(a)** Describe how selective breeding is used to develop such cattle. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why selective breeding is different from natural selection. **[2]**

**6.** A farm grows only one selectively bred variety of potato. The variety produces a high yield.

**(a)** Explain why this variety could be at risk if a new disease arrives. **[3]**
**(b)** Suggest what the farmer could do to reduce the risk. **[1]**

**7.** A breeder repeatedly breeds together closely related dogs to keep a breed's appearance.

**(a)** State the name for this type of breeding. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why it may lead to health problems in the offspring. **[2]**

**8.** Evaluate the use of selective breeding to increase the milk yield of dairy cattle. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Humans choose which organisms breed together [1]; so that useful characteristics become more common in the population over generations [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Breeding the best animals" scores one mark only. The second mark needs "over generations" or "useful characteristics are passed on".

**2.** **(a)** Choose plants with the largest fruit and plants that resist the disease [1]; cross-pollinate them (breed them together) [1]; grow the seeds, and select the offspring that have large fruit and resistance [1]; breed these together and repeat for many generations [1]
**(b)** So that the next generation inherits the genes for the useful characteristics [1]
*Examiner insight:* The sequence select, breed, select again and repeat must be visible. The repeat is the most commonly forgotten step.

**3.** **(a)** (7.8 − 6.0) ÷ 6.0 × 100 [1] = **30%** [1]
**(b)** 1.8 × 250 [1] = **450 tonnes** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), find the difference per hectare first and then multiply by the area. Check that the unit is tonnes.

**4.** **(a)** (252 − 180) ÷ 180 × 100 [1] = **40%** [1]
**(b)** 72 ÷ 6 = **12 eggs per generation** [1]
**(c)** Each generation increases the proportion of useful alleles only a little [1]; so many generations are needed before most hens lay many eggs (accept: the process of selecting and breeding is repeated) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Divide by 6 generations (the number of steps) and not by 7 (the number of columns).

**5.** **(a)** Choose the cattle that grow fastest and have the leanest meat [1]; breed them together [1]; select the best offspring from these matings [1]; repeat for many generations [1]
**(b)** In natural selection the environment decides which organisms survive and reproduce [1]; in selective breeding humans choose the parents for characteristics that are useful to humans [1]
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both sides named. Saying "humans choose" without a contrast loses the first mark.

**6.** **(a)** Selective breeding reduces genetic variation [1]; all plants are genetically similar, so none may be resistant to the new disease [1]; so the whole crop could be lost [1]
**(b)** Any one of: grow more than one variety, keep some other varieties as a source of resistance [1]
*Examiner insight:* The key link is from "little variation" to "no resistant plants". Writing "the disease spreads quickly" alone is not enough.

**7.** **(a)** Inbreeding [1]
**(b)** Close relatives are more likely to carry the same harmful recessive alleles [1]; so offspring are more likely to inherit two copies of the harmful allele and show the problem (accept: reduced genetic variation) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Use the word "recessive" if you can. It shows that you understand why harmful alleles appear.

**8.** Advantage: more milk from each cow, so more profit or food [1]; disadvantage: reduces genetic variation, so a disease could affect the whole herd [1]; also, selecting for very high milk yield may cause health problems in cows (accept an equivalent disadvantage) [1]; a conclusion that balances these, such as "the benefits are real but the farmer should keep some variation" [1]
*Examiner insight:* A mark is reserved for a judgement. A list of points with no conclusion cannot gain full marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out the repetition over many generations.
- Saying that individual animals "change" during their life.
- Dividing by the new value when finding a percentage increase.
- Giving no reason when asked why variation is important.
- Treating selective breeding as the same as genetic engineering.
