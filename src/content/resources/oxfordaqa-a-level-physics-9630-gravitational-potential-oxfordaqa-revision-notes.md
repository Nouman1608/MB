These notes condense section **3.7.3 Gravitational potential** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630) specification: the definition of gravitational potential "including zero potential at infinity and gravitational potential difference", ΔW = mΔV, V = −GM/r, equipotential surfaces, "graphical representations of the variations of g and V with r" and g = −ΔV/Δr. Section 3.7 is International A-level only.

For explanations and worked examples, read the [gravitational potential study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-gravitational-potential-oxfordaqa/). Test yourself with the [gravitational potential practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-gravitational-potential-oxfordaqa-practice/).

Data: G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻²; GM(Earth) = 3.98 × 10¹⁴ N m² kg⁻¹; Earth radius 6.37 × 10⁶ m; GM(Moon) = 4.90 × 10¹² N m² kg⁻¹; Moon radius 1.74 × 10⁶ m.

## Core recall

- Gravitational potential V: work done per unit mass in moving a small mass from infinity to the point.
- V = 0 at infinity and is negative everywhere else; it increases (towards zero) with distance.
- Potential is a scalar, unit J kg⁻¹.
- Potential difference ΔV: work done per unit mass moving between two points.
- ΔW = mΔV, valid for any change in distance (unlike mgh).
- Radial field: V = −GM/r.
- No work is done moving along an equipotential surface.
- g = −ΔV/Δr: the field is the negative potential gradient.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Gravitational potential | Work done per unit mass bringing a small mass from infinity to the point |
| Potential difference | Work done per unit mass moving a mass between two points |
| Equipotential surface | Surface on which every point has the same potential |
| Potential gradient | Rate of change of potential with distance, ΔV/Δr |
| Potential well | The region of negative potential around a mass; deeper nearer the mass |
| Gravitational potential energy | mV = −GMm/r for a mass m at distance r |

## Equations

| Equation | Use |
|---|---|
| V = −GM/r | Potential in a radial field |
| ΔW = mΔV | Work to move mass m through potential difference ΔV |
| E(p) = mV = −GMm/r | Gravitational potential energy |
| g = −ΔV/Δr | Field strength from potential gradient |
| ΔV = area under g-r graph | Potential difference from a field graph |
| Energy to infinity = m|V| | Removing a mass completely from a point |

## Graphs

| Graph | Shape outside the planet | Read off |
|---|---|---|
| V against r | Negative, curving up towards zero | Gradient of tangent = size of g |
| g against r | Positive, falling as 1/r² | Area between two radii = ΔV |

## Equipotentials

- Concentric spheres around a planet.
- Field lines cross them at 90°.
- Equal steps of ΔV are further apart further out, because the field is weaker.
- Near the surface (uniform field) they are horizontal planes, equally spaced.

## Method: work done between two radii

1. Find V at each radius with V = −GM/r, keeping the minus signs.
2. ΔV = V(final) − V(initial). Moving outwards, ΔV is positive.
3. ΔW = mΔV. A positive answer is work done against the field; a negative one is work done by the field.
4. Check the size: it should be less than mgh calculated with the surface value of g, because g falls with height.

## Quick reminders with numbers

- V at the Earth's surface: −6.25 × 10⁷ J kg⁻¹.
- V at the Moon's surface: −2.82 × 10⁶ J kg⁻¹.
- Equipotentials at −6.0, −5.0 and −4.0 × 10⁷ J kg⁻¹ around the Earth: radii 6.64, 7.96 and 9.95 × 10⁶ m.
- ΔV from R to 2R for the Earth: GM/2R = 3.13 × 10⁷ J kg⁻¹.
- At the Earth-Moon neutral point, g = 0 but V = −1.28 × 10⁶ J kg⁻¹.

## Exam traps

- Potential is never positive in a gravitational field.
- ΔV = V(final) − V(initial); moving outwards gives a positive ΔV and positive work.
- Potential is a scalar: add potentials from two bodies without directions.
- Zero field does not mean zero potential.
- Gradient of V-r gives g; area under g-r gives ΔV. Do not swap them.
- r is measured from the centre.
- mgh only works for small height changes near the surface.

## Self-test

1. Define gravitational potential.
2. Why is gravitational potential negative?
3. Find the potential at the surface of the Moon.
4. A 50 kg mass moves from V = −3.0 × 10⁷ J kg⁻¹ to V = −2.0 × 10⁷ J kg⁻¹. Find the work done.
5. How much work is done moving a satellite along an equipotential?
6. State the angle between field lines and equipotentials.
7. A V-r tangent has gradient 2.5 J kg⁻¹ m⁻¹. What is the field strength?
8. What does the area under a g-r graph represent?
9. Find the potential 1.0 × 10⁷ m from the Earth's centre.
10. At one point, the potential due to a planet is −4.0 × 10⁶ J kg⁻¹ and the potential due to its moon is −1.5 × 10⁶ J kg⁻¹. Find the total potential there.
11. Why are equipotentials at equal steps of V further apart further from a planet?
12. How much energy is needed to move 1.0 kg from the Earth's surface to infinity?

### Answers

1. The **work done per unit mass** in moving a small mass **from infinity** to that point.
2. The zero is at **infinity** and gravity is **attractive**, so work must be done to move a mass away to infinity; potentials closer in are lower than zero.
3. V = −4.90 × 10¹²/1.74 × 10⁶ = **−2.82 × 10⁶ J kg⁻¹**.
4. ΔW = 50 × 1.0 × 10⁷ = **5.0 × 10⁸ J**.
5. **Zero**.
6. **90°**.
7. **2.5 N kg⁻¹**, towards the body.
8. The **potential difference** between the two radii.
9. V = −3.98 × 10¹⁴/1.0 × 10⁷ = **−3.98 × 10⁷ J kg⁻¹**.
10. **−5.5 × 10⁶ J kg⁻¹** (scalars add).
11. The **field is weaker** further out, so a larger distance is needed for the same change in potential (g = −ΔV/Δr).
12. **6.25 × 10⁷ J** (ignoring air resistance and rotation).
