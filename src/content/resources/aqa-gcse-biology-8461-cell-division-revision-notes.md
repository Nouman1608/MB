## 4.1.2.1 Chromosomes -- three facts

| Fact | Write it like this |
|---|---|
| Where | The **nucleus** contains **chromosomes** |
| What they are made of | Chromosomes are made of **DNA molecules** |
| What they carry | Each chromosome carries a **large number of genes** |
| How they are arranged | In **body cells**, chromosomes are normally found **in pairs** |

Size order, largest first: **cell → nucleus → chromosome → gene**. A gene is a section of the DNA in a
chromosome.

Human body cells: **23 pairs = 46 chromosomes**. Mitosis keeps the number the same in each new cell.

## 4.1.2.2 The cell cycle -- three stages

AQA wants the **three overall stages**. Do not name the phases of mitosis -- they are not required.

```
STAGE 1  Growth and DNA replication
         - cell grows
         - number of sub-cellular structures increases
           (ribosomes, mitochondria)
         - DNA replicates -> two copies of each chromosome

STAGE 2  Mitosis
         - one set of chromosomes pulled to each end of the cell
         - nucleus divides

STAGE 3  Cytoplasm and cell membrane divide
         - two genetically identical cells
```

**Summary sentence to learn:** during the cell cycle, the genetic material is **doubled** and then
**divided** into two identical cells.

### Method: describing the cell cycle for 4-6 marks

1. Say the cell **grows** and makes more **ribosomes and mitochondria**.
2. Say the **DNA replicates** so there are **two copies of each chromosome**.
3. Say **one set of chromosomes is pulled to each end** of the cell.
4. Say the **nucleus divides**.
5. Say the **cytoplasm and cell membrane divide**.
6. Finish with **two genetically identical cells**.

Six separate, ordered points. Each one is a likely marking point.

### DNA content per cell through one cycle

| Point in cycle | DNA (relative) |
|---|---|
| Start of stage 1 | 1 |
| After replication | 2 |
| During mitosis | 2 (in two sets) |
| Each new cell after stage 3 | 1 |

On a graph: flat, **rise** (replication), flat, **sudden drop** (cell splits into two).

### Why mitosis matters

- **Growth and development** of multicellular organisms (a fertilised egg divides by mitosis to form an
  embryo, then cells differentiate).
- **Repair and replacement** of cells -- in mature animals, cell division is mainly restricted to this.
- **Asexual reproduction** -- only mitosis is involved, so offspring are clones.

### Mitosis or not? Quick decision

Ask: **is a eukaryotic cell making genetically identical copies of itself?**

| Yes -- mitosis | No -- not mitosis |
|---|---|
| Skin healing after a cut | Making sperm or egg cells (meiosis) |
| Root tip or shoot tip growing | A bacterium dividing (binary fission) |
| Bone marrow making blood cells | |
| An embryo increasing its cell number | |
| A strawberry runner forming a new plant | |

### Data reminders

- **Percentage of cells in mitosis** = (cells in mitosis ÷ total cells counted) × 100.
- **Time in mitosis** ≈ fraction of cells in mitosis × length of the whole cycle.
- **Cells after n cycles** from one cell = 2ⁿ (not 2 × n).

## 4.1.2.3 Stem cells

### Definition (AQA wording)

A **stem cell** is an **undifferentiated cell** of an organism which can **give rise to many more cells of the
same type**, and from which **certain other cells can arise by differentiation**.

### Three sources compared

| Source | Can become | Main job |
|---|---|---|
| **Human embryo** | **Most** types of human cell (can be cloned and made to differentiate) | Forms the tissues of the developing body |
| **Adult bone marrow** | **Many** types, **including blood cells** | Replaces cells |
| **Plant meristem** (tips of shoots and roots) | **Any** type of plant cell, **throughout the plant's life** | Growth |

Most / many / any: learn the three words in that order.

### Medical use

- Stem cell treatment **may be able to help** conditions such as **diabetes and paralysis**.
- **Therapeutic cloning:** an **embryo is produced with the same genes as the patient** → its stem cells are
  **not rejected** by the patient's body → they **may be used for medical treatment**.
- Stem cell **techniques are not required** at GCSE.

### Risks and objections

| Type | Point |
|---|---|
| Practical risk | **Transfer of viral infection** |
| Ethical / religious | Some people **object** to producing and using human embryos |

**Evaluate = both sides + a conclusion linked to them.**

### Plant cloning from meristems

Clones are produced **quickly and economically**. Two uses to quote:

1. **Rare species** cloned to protect them from **extinction**.
2. **Crop plants** with special features (e.g. **disease resistance**) cloned to give **large numbers of
   identical plants** for farmers.

## Small worked reminders

**Mitosis in context.** "A lizard regrows part of its tail. Explain the role of mitosis."

```
cells near the wound divide by mitosis          -> more cells
new cells are genetically identical to parent   -> same genes
the cells then differentiate                    -> tissues of the tail
```

Name the process, say what it produces (identical cells), then link to the context (replacing the lost tissue).

**Reading a DNA graph.** If DNA per cell goes 5 → 10 → 5 units, the rise is DNA replication in stage 1 and the
fall is the cell splitting in stage 3. The value never drops below the starting 5.

**Stem cell source choice.** "Why might a doctor prefer embryonic to bone marrow stem cells for a nerve
injury?" Embryonic stem cells can differentiate into most types of human cell; bone marrow stem cells form many
types, including blood cells, so their range is narrower.

**Evaluate skeleton (stem cells).**

```
benefit 1   may help conditions such as diabetes or paralysis
benefit 2   therapeutic cloning -> cells not rejected
risk 1      viral infection may be transferred
risk 2      ethical or religious objections to using embryos
conclusion  one side outweighs the other, because ... (use your points)
```

**Plant cloning in context.** "A rare orchid has only a few plants left. Suggest how meristems could help."
Take meristem tissue from a tip, grow many genetically identical clones quickly and cheaply, and so increase
the number of plants and protect the species from extinction.

## Must-know distinctions

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| Chromosome vs gene | A chromosome is a DNA molecule carrying many genes; a gene is one section of it |
| DNA replication vs mitosis | Replication copies the DNA (stage 1); mitosis separates the copies (stage 2) |
| Nucleus dividing vs cell dividing | The nucleus divides in mitosis; the cytoplasm and membrane divide afterwards |
| Embryonic vs adult stem cells | Most cell types vs many cell types (including blood cells) |
| Mitosis vs binary fission | Eukaryotic cells (nucleus) vs bacteria (no nucleus) |
| Therapeutic cloning vs plant cloning | Embryo with patient's genes, for treatment vs meristem cells, to copy whole plants |

## Quick self-test

1. What are chromosomes made of?
2. State where chromosomes are found in a cell.
3. A body cell of one animal species has 78 chromosomes. How many pairs is that?
4. Name **two** sub-cellular structures whose number increases before a cell divides.
5. In which stage of the cell cycle does the DNA replicate?
6. What divides during mitosis itself?
7. One cell goes through three complete cell cycles, and every new cell also divides. How many cells result?
8. In a 24-hour cell cycle, a cell spends 3 hours in mitosis. What percentage of the cycle is this?
9. A student counts 50 cells in a root tip and 4 are in mitosis. What percentage is this?
10. Give **one** situation where cells divide but **not** by mitosis.
11. Which can form more types of cell: adult bone marrow stem cells or meristem cells?
12. Why are cells from therapeutic cloning not rejected?

### Answers

1. DNA molecules.
2. In the nucleus.
3. 78 ÷ 2 = **39 pairs**.
4. Ribosomes and mitochondria.
5. Stage 1, before mitosis (growth and DNA replication).
6. The nucleus (one set of chromosomes is pulled to each end of the cell).
7. 2³ = **8 cells**.
8. 3 ÷ 24 × 100 = **12.5%**.
9. 4 ÷ 50 × 100 = **8%**.
10. Gamete (sperm or egg) production, by meiosis; or a bacterium dividing, by binary fission.
11. Meristem cells -- they can differentiate into any type of plant cell.
12. The embryo they come from has the same genes as the patient, so the patient's body does not reject them.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Placing DNA replication inside mitosis instead of in the first stage.
- Leaving out the growth step: the cell grows and increases its ribosomes and mitochondria first.
- Writing "the cell divides" for stage 2 when the mark needs "the nucleus divides".
- Naming phases of mitosis and running out of time, when three overall stages are all AQA asks for.
- Calling the two new cells "similar" -- the answer needs "genetically identical".
- Saying mitosis halves the chromosome number.
- Saying adult stem cells can become any cell, or that meristems only make root cells.
- Claiming stem cells "cure" diabetes or paralysis instead of "may be able to help".
- Stopping a therapeutic cloning answer at "same genes" without "not rejected".
- In an evaluate question, listing points with no conclusion.
