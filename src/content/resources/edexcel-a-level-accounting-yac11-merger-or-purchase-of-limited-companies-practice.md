## Section A

**1.** Define purchase consideration. **[2]**

**2.** Define goodwill and state how it is calculated when one company buys another. **[2]**

**3.** Explain why purchased goodwill is recognised in the statement of financial position but internally generated goodwill is not. **[2]**

**4.** The fair value of the net assets of a company is 156,000. The buyer pays 140,000. State what has arisen and how it is treated. **[2]**

**5.** A buyer's goodwill of 12,000 is reviewed at the year end and the recoverable amount is 7,000. Calculate the impairment and prepare the journal entry. **[3]**

## Section B

Hale Ltd buys the business of Mott Ltd. The statement of financial position of Mott Ltd, with agreed fair values, is:

| | Book value | Fair value |
|---|---:|---:|
| Land and buildings | 90,000 | 110,000 |
| Machinery | 45,000 | 40,000 |
| Inventory | 25,000 | 23,000 |
| Trade receivables | 20,000 | 18,000 |
| Bank | 5,000 | 5,000 |
| Trade payables | (22,000) | (22,000) |
| Debentures | (18,000) | (18,000) |
| **Net assets** | **145,000** | |

Mott Ltd's equity is share capital 80,000 and reserves 65,000. Hale Ltd pays by issuing 60,000 new $1 ordinary shares valued at $2.50 each, and 18,000 in cash. Hale takes over all assets and liabilities.

Before the purchase Hale Ltd had: land and buildings 200,000; machinery 100,000; inventory 50,000; trade receivables 40,000; bank 60,000; trade payables 45,000; ordinary share capital 250,000; share premium 20,000; retained earnings 135,000.

**6. (a)** Calculate the fair value of Mott Ltd's net assets. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the purchase consideration. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the goodwill. **[1]**

**7.** In the books of Mott Ltd, prepare the revaluation account, the realisation account and the sundry shareholders' account. **[10]**

**8.** In the books of Hale Ltd, prepare the acquisition account and the journal entry to record the payment. **[6]**

**9.** Prepare the statement of financial position of Hale Ltd immediately after the purchase. **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** The total value given by the buyer to the vendor's shareholders [1] for the business, in cash, shares or both, measured at fair value [1].

**2.** Goodwill is the amount paid for the business above the fair value of its identifiable net assets [1]; goodwill = purchase consideration − fair value of net assets acquired [1].

**3.** Purchased goodwill has been measured by an actual transaction, so there is reliable evidence of its value [1]; internally generated goodwill cannot be measured reliably and its value is uncertain [1].

**4.** A bargain purchase (negative goodwill) of 156,000 − 140,000 = **16,000** has arisen [1]; after checking the fair values, it is recognised as a gain in profit [1].

**5.** Impairment 12,000 − 7,000 = **5,000** [1]; Dr impairment loss (statement of comprehensive income) 5,000 [1]; Cr goodwill 5,000 [1].

**6. (a)** Assets at fair value 110,000 + 40,000 + 23,000 + 18,000 + 5,000 = 196,000 [2]; less liabilities 22,000 + 18,000 = 40,000, so net assets **156,000** [1].

**(b)** Shares 60,000 × 2.50 = 150,000 [1]; plus cash 18,000 = **168,000** [1].

**(c)** Goodwill 168,000 − 156,000 = **12,000** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using book values instead of fair values for goodwill (Question 6).
- Adding Mott Ltd's reserves to Hale Ltd's equity (Question 9).
- Forgetting the share premium on the new shares (Question 8).
- Omitting the cash paid from the bank balance (Question 9).
- Not transferring the gain and the realisation profit to the sundry shareholders' account (Question 7).
- Using the nominal value of shares instead of their agreed value in the consideration.

## Approaching these questions

Go step by step: fair values, net assets, consideration, goodwill. Then do the vendor's accounts and the buyer's accounts. For the combined statement, add the fair values to the buyer's figures, deduct cash paid, and add only the shares and premium issued. Check that net assets equal equity.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these Edexcel YAC11 questions from past papers?**
No. They are original questions on outcomes 2.1.12 to 2.1.15, written for revision. Use them alongside the official past papers.

**Is a merger different from a purchase?**
The double entry is similar. In a merger the owners of both companies usually continue as shareholders, and the consideration is mainly shares.
