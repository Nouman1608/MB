## Questions

**1.** Explain two reasons why the USA became involved in the Korean War. **[4]**

**2.** Read the extract, which is *constructed for practice* and is not a real quotation.

> "Our bombers can level a village, but they cannot make the villagers support the government. The enemy appears at night and is gone by morning, and the people will not name him." (A report by an officer, constructed for practice)

**(a)** Explain what the extract suggests about the difficulties faced by US forces in Vietnam. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain one limitation of an officer's report for a historian studying the war's impact on civilians. **[3]**

**3.** Analyse the impact of the Korean War on Japan or China. **[6]**

**4.** To what extent were the actions of the USA the main reason for the escalation of conflict in Vietnam between 1954 and 1975? **[15]**

**5.** Evaluate the reasons for the Soviet intervention in Afghanistan in 1979 and its impact on Afghanistan to 1992. **[15]**

**6.** Explain the importance of the Tet Offensive (1968). **[6]**

**7.** Explain two reasons for the rise of the Taliban in Afghanistan. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two, each developed (1 mark for the reason, 1 for explanation). President Truman saw the invasion as a test of containment, and feared that failing to respond would encourage further communist aggression; the communist victory in China in 1949 and the Soviet atomic bomb had increased fears of communist expansion; the UN Security Council, in the absence of the Soviet Union, gave a legal and international basis for action; the USA wished to protect Japan and its position in East Asia, which South Korea's fall would threaten. **[4]**

**2.** **(a)** The extract suggests that bombing damaged villages but did not win civilian loyalty (1), that the enemy relied on guerrilla tactics and surprise (1), and that civilians would not give information to the government or the US forces (1). **[3]**
**(b)** Award up to 3. An officer's report is written from the American military point of view and may stress the enemy's tactics more than the effects of US actions on villagers (1); it cannot show what civilians experienced or thought, since they are not heard (1); it may be written to explain failure or to ask for more resources, so it may exaggerate difficulties (1). **[3]**

**3.** Marking points (up to 6), for Japan: American spending on supplies in Japan boosted industry and ended post-war depression (up to 2); the Peace Treaty and the Security Treaty took effect in 1952 and Japan began to rearm in a limited way with a National Police Reserve (up to 2); the war reinforced Japan's role as a base for US forces (up to 1); a short judgement of overall significance (up to 1). For China: prestige from fighting the USA, heavy casualties, trade embargo and the Seventh Fleet's protection of Taiwan, closer ties and then tension with the Soviet Union. **[6]**

**4.** Marking points (up to 15):
- A thesis and criteria for "main reason" (up to 2).
- US actions: aid to Diệm, the domino theory, the Gulf of Tonkin Resolution, Rolling Thunder, the combat troops of 1965 (up to 4).
- North Vietnamese and Vietcong actions: infiltration along the Hồ Chí Minh Trail, the National Liberation Front, the Tet Offensive (up to 3).
- South Vietnamese politics: Diệm's repression, the instability after 1963 (up to 2).
- Soviet and Chinese support (up to 2).
- A judgement ranking the factors, with a view of historians such as Logevall (up to 2). **[15]**

**5.** Marking points (up to 15):
- A thesis addressing both reasons and impact (up to 2).
- Reasons: the PDPA's coup, divisions within it, the risk to a client state on the Soviet border, resistance in the countryside, Amin's unreliability, Cold War calculations (up to 4).
- Impact on Afghans: civilian deaths and refugees, destroyed villages and irrigation, landmines (up to 4).
- Soviet failure and withdrawal: the mujahideen, Pakistani and American help, Stinger missiles, the cost to the USSR (up to 2).
- Perspectives: historians who see defence against Western influence and those who see expansion (up to 1).
- A judgement (up to 2). **[15]**

**6.** Marking points (up to 6): in January 1968 the communists attacked cities and bases across South Vietnam (up to 1); it failed militarily, because the uprising did not materialise and the communist forces suffered heavy losses (up to 1); it shocked the American public, who had been told that victory was near, and increased opposition to the war (up to 2); it led Johnson to limit the bombing, not to seek re-election, and begin peace talks (up to 2). **[6]**

**7.** Any two, each developed (1 mark for the reason, 1 for explanation). After the Soviet withdrawal, mujahideen groups fought one another over Kabul, and people wanted order; the Taliban promised an end to warlord rule and corruption, and found support in Pashtun areas; Pakistan supported them with money, training and supplies; the movement drew recruits from religious schools and refugee camps where young men had known only war. **[4]**

## Frequently asked questions

**How can I use one case to prepare for another?**
Use the same frame for each: local causes, great-power roles, tactics, civilian impact and long-term effects.

**Are casualty figures needed?**
Use approximate figures with "about" or "perhaps", and show you know they are estimates.
