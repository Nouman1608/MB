These notes condense section 1.03, Combining arithmetic operations, of OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560, Version 2.1 (August 2026). The single statement, 1.03a Priority of operations, asks you to "know the conventional order for performing calculations involving brackets, four rules and powers, roots and reciprocals". It is content for **both tiers**, on calculator and non-calculator papers.

Worked examples and explanations are in the [1.03 study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-combining-arithmetic-operations-ocr-gcse-maths/); exam-style questions are in the [1.03 practice questions](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-combining-arithmetic-operations-ocr-gcse-maths-practice/). The combined Topic 1 summary is in the [integer arithmetic and number theory revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-integer-arithmetic-number-theory-priority-of-revision-notes/).

## The order

```
1. Brackets            innermost first; includes fraction bars and root signs
2. Powers, roots, reciprocals
3. × and ÷             equal priority, left to right
4. + and −             equal priority, left to right
```

Inside any bracket, the whole order starts again.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Priority of operations | The agreed order for carrying out operations | 2 + 3 × 4 = 14 |
| Bracket | Groups part of a calculation to be done first | (2 + 3) × 4 = 20 |
| Nested brackets | Brackets inside brackets | 3 × [20 − (2 + 1)³ ÷ 9] |
| Index (power) | Repeated multiplication of the number it is attached to | 4² = 16 |
| Root | The inverse of a power | √25 = 5, ∛64 = 4 |
| Reciprocal | 1 divided by the number | Reciprocal of 0.125 is 8 |
| Vinculum | The line of a fraction or the top line of a root sign; acts as a bracket | (5² − 1)/(2³ − 2) |
| Four rules | Addition, subtraction, multiplication, division | — |

## Hidden brackets

| Written as | Means | Calculator keying |
|---|---|---|
| A fraction with a sum on top | (top) ÷ (bottom) | Brackets round top and bottom |
| √ over a sum | √(the whole sum) | Close the bracket after the last term |
| A power on a bracket | The whole bracket raised to the power | Keep the bracket |
| A power on a single number | Only that number raised | No bracket needed |

## Signs and powers

```
−2⁴    = −16       power first, then the minus sign
(−2)⁴  = 16        even power of a negative: positive
(−2)³  = −8        odd power of a negative: negative
−(−2)³ = 8
```

When substituting a negative number, put it in brackets before applying a power.

## Left to right

```
40 − 12 − 6 + 3 = 25          not 40 − 12 − 9 = 19
60 ÷ 5 × 2      = 24          not 60 ÷ 10 = 6
```

Equal-priority operations are done in reading order.

## Reciprocals

- Reciprocal of a whole number n is 1/n. Reciprocal of a fraction a/b is b/a.
- Reciprocal of a decimal: convert or divide. 1 ÷ 0.125 = 8; 1 ÷ 0.4 = 2.5.
- A reciprocal is worked out at the powers-and-roots stage.
- The reciprocal of a sum is **not** the sum of the reciprocals: 1 ÷ (1/2 + 1/4) = 4/3, but 2 + 4 = 6.
- A number multiplied by its reciprocal gives 1.

## Question types and moves

| Question type | Move |
|---|---|
| Evaluate an expression | One operation per line; rewrite the expression after each step |
| Insert brackets to make a target | Decide which operation must happen first; bracket it; recheck the whole line |
| Write a calculation from words | "The result" or "the answer" means the earlier steps go in a bracket |
| Use a calculator | Write the numerator and denominator values first; estimate to check |
| Spot the error in someone's working | Find the first line that breaks the order and name the rule |

## Exam traps

- **Squaring the sign:** −2⁴ is −16.
- **BIDMAS read literally:** division is not always before multiplication, nor addition before subtraction.
- **Right-to-left subtraction:** 40 − 12 − 6 is 22, not 34.
- **Partial numerator:** in (4.2 + 1.8)/(0.5 × 0.3), keying 4.2 + 1.8 ÷ 0.5 × 0.3 divides only the 1.8.
- **Partial root:** √(13² − 12²) is 5, not 13 − 12 = 1 and not √169 − 144.
- **Reciprocal of a sum** treated as a sum of reciprocals.
- **Forgetting the order inside a bracket:** (2 + 1)³ ÷ 9 inside a bracket still needs the power before the division.

## Quick self-test

1. Work out 7 + 3 × 4².
2. Work out (7 + 3) × 4².
3. Work out 60 ÷ 5 × 2.
4. Work out 40 − 12 − 6 + 3.
5. Write down the values of −2⁴ and (−2)⁴.
6. Work out (5² − 1)/(2³ − 2).
7. Work out √(13² − 12²).
8. Work out 3 × [20 − (2 + 1)³ ÷ 9].
9. Work out 2 × (the reciprocal of 0.125) − 3.
10. Without brackets, 8 − 2 × 3 + 1 = 3. Insert one pair of brackets to make the answer 19.
11. "Add 6 to 14, divide by 4, then cube the answer." Write this as a single calculation and evaluate it.
12. Use a calculator to work out (4.2 + 1.8)/(0.5 × 0.3). Write down the values of the numerator and denominator first.

### Answers

1. 7 + 3 × 16 = 7 + 48 = **55**.
2. 10 × 16 = **160**.
3. 12 × 2 = **24**.
4. 28 − 6 + 3 = 22 + 3 = **25**.
5. **−16** and **16**.
6. 24/6 = **4**.
7. √(169 − 144) = √25 = **5**.
8. Inner bracket 3, cubed 27, 27 ÷ 9 = 3, 20 − 3 = 17, 3 × 17 = **51**.
9. 2 × 8 − 3 = **13**.
10. **(8 − 2) × 3 + 1** = 18 + 1 = 19.
11. **((14 + 6) ÷ 4)³** = 5³ = **125**.
12. Numerator 6, denominator 0.15, answer **40**.
