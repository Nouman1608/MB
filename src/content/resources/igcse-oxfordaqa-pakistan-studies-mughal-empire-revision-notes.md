**These notes condense optional topic 3.1.4 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Pakistan Studies 9236,
The Mughal Empire (c1526–c1850s), examined in Paper 1 Section C.** Learn the rulers in order with
their main achievements, the key features of Aurangzeb's reign, and a clear list of reasons for the
empire's decline that you can use in extended answers.

## Syllabus coverage

**9236 topic 3.1.4 (Version 1.9)**
- **3.1.4.1** Early 16th-century context; Babur; Humayun, Akbar, Jahangir, Shah Jahan; political, military, economic, social and cultural developments to 1658
- **3.1.4.2** Aurangzeb's rise, administration, reforms and expansion; the empire's condition in 1707
- **3.1.4.3** Successors' problems; Nadir Shah, Karnal and the sack of Delhi (1739); East India Company and Plassey (1757); Sikhs and Marathas; British expansion to the 1850s

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mansabdar | Noble or official holding a rank (*mansab*) that fixed pay and troop numbers |
| Jagir | Assignment of land revenue to a noble in place of salary |
| Zabt | Akbar's system of assessing land revenue on measured land and average yields |
| Jizya | Tax on non-Muslims, abolished by Akbar, reimposed by Aurangzeb (1679) |
| Fatawa-e-Alamgiri | Collection of Hanafi legal rulings compiled under Aurangzeb |
| Muhtasib | Official who supervised public morality and markets |
| Diwani | Right to collect revenue, granted to the East India Company in Bengal (1765) |

## Emperors at a glance

| Ruler | Reign | Remember |
|---|---|---|
| Babur | 1526–30 | Panipat 1526 (Ibrahim Lodi); Khanwa 1527 (Rana Sanga); *Baburnama* |
| Humayun | 1530–40, 1555–56 | Lost to Sher Shah Suri; exile in Persia; regained Delhi 1555 |
| Akbar | 1556–1605 | Panipat 1556; Rajput alliances; mansabdari; Todar Mal's revenue system; jizya abolished; *Ibadat Khana*; Fatehpur Sikri |
| Jahangir | 1605–27 | Nur Jahan; painting; Sir Thomas Roe and English trade |
| Shah Jahan | 1628–58 | Taj Mahal; Red Fort and Jama Masjid, Delhi; Shalimar Gardens, Lahore |
| Aurangzeb | 1658–1707 | Sharia-based rule; *Fatawa-e-Alamgiri*; jizya 1679; Badshahi Mosque; Bijapur 1686 and Golconda 1687; Deccan wars |
| Muhammad Shah | 1719–48 | Nadir Shah's invasion; Karnal and sack of Delhi 1739 |
| Bahadur Shah Zafar | 1837–57 | Last emperor; exiled by the British in 1858 |

## Achievements to 1658

- **Political and military:** artillery at Panipat; Akbar's conquests and Rajput alliances; centralised provinces (*subas*) under governors.
- **Economic:** stable silver rupee; land revenue reform; roads and secure routes; textile exports; European trading companies.
- **Social and cultural:** Persian literature and histories; Mughal miniature painting; music at court; Indo-Persian architecture; madrasas.

## Buildings and places to name

- **Fatehpur Sikri** (Akbar): planned city with the Buland Darwaza.
- **Lahore Fort** (rebuilt under Akbar, extended by later emperors).
- **Wazir Khan Mosque, Lahore** (built under Shah Jahan, completed 1641).
- **Taj Mahal, Agra** (Shah Jahan, tomb of Mumtaz Mahal).
- **Red Fort and Jama Masjid, Delhi** (Shah Jahan).
- **Shalimar Gardens, Lahore** (Shah Jahan).
- **Badshahi Mosque, Lahore** (Aurangzeb, 1673).

## Aurangzeb's empire in 1707

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|
| Greatest territorial extent | Treasury drained by Deccan wars |
| Functioning administration and army | Maratha resistance unbroken |
| Reputation for personal piety and justice | Rajput, Jat and Sikh revolts |
| Sharia-based legal system | Shortage of jagirs for nobles; no succession rule |

## Causes of decline after 1707

1. **Weak successors** and wars of succession; nobles such as the Sayyid brothers as king-makers.
2. **Financial crisis:** shrinking revenue and jagir shortage.
3. **Nadir Shah (1739):** Karnal, sack of Delhi, loss of treasure and prestige.
4. **Marathas** expanding across central and western India.
5. **Sikhs** taking Punjab; Ranjit Singh's kingdom at Lahore.
6. **East India Company:** Plassey (1757), Bengal revenue (1765).
7. **British annexations:** Sindh 1843, Punjab 1849, Awadh 1856; end of the dynasty after 1857.

## Worked example

**Question:** Explain why the Battle of Plassey was important. **[4]**

**Model answer:** Robert Clive's victory over Siraj-ud-Daula in 1757 put a Company-backed ruler, Mir
Jafar, in charge of Bengal [1]. This gave the East India Company control of India's richest province
and its trade [1]. Bengal's wealth paid for the Company's army and further wars [1]. In 1765 the
Company gained the right to collect Bengal's revenue, the first step towards British rule over the
subcontinent [1].

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- **Muddling the two battles of Panipat.** 1526 = Babur against Ibrahim Lodi; 1556 = Akbar's forces against Hemu. (The third, 1761, belongs to the Abdali–Maratha story.)
- **Attributing the Badshahi Mosque to Shah Jahan.** Aurangzeb built it; Shah Jahan built the Shalimar Gardens.
- **Blaming decline on one cause.** Extended answers need several linked factors and a judgement.
- **Forgetting the dates for Karnal (1739) and Plassey (1757).**

## How is this topic examined?

This topic is examined in OxfordAQA 9236 Paper 1, Section C, worth 21 of the paper's 77 marks; each paper lasts 1 hour 30 minutes. The section mixes short and extended response questions on this optional topic. Short items reward precise named facts, so learn the dates, places and people in the tables above; the extended responses reward a clear line of argument, two or three developed points with evidence, and a judgement that answers the question.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why did Akbar build alliances with the Rajputs?** To secure the loyalty of powerful warrior
kingdoms, add their soldiers to his army and stabilise north India.

**How did Nadir Shah weaken the Mughals?** His 1739 victory and sack of Delhi emptied the treasury,
showed the empire could be defeated, and encouraged provincial governors to act independently.

**Why did the Marathas weaken the Mughals?** Their long resistance in the Deccan drained Aurangzeb's
treasury, and after 1707 they expanded north, collecting revenue from former Mughal lands.
