## Section A

**1.** Define the term computer model. **[2]**

**2.** Describe what is meant by what-if analysis. **[2]**

**3.** State **two** reasons why a computer model of a nuclear reactor is used instead of experiments on a real reactor. **[2]**

**4.** Describe what goal seek does. **[2]**

**5.** Give **two** uses of what-if analysis. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A school uses a spreadsheet model to plan a charity concert. Each ticket costs 5 pounds to produce and is sold for 12 pounds. The fixed cost of the hall is 600 pounds. The profit is calculated as units sold × (price − cost per ticket) − fixed cost.

**(a)** Calculate the profit when 200 tickets are sold. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the profit when 150 tickets are sold, and when 250 are sold. **[2]**

**(c)** The school wants a profit of 1000 pounds from 200 tickets. Calculate the ticket price needed. **[3]**

**(d)** State which feature of spreadsheet software would find this price automatically, and name the cells it uses. **[2]**

**(e)** Calculate the smallest number of tickets that must be sold at 12 pounds to avoid a loss. **[2]**

**7.** Explain why the fixed cost, ticket price and cost per ticket in the model of Question 6 should be placed in separate labelled cells rather than typed into the profit formula. **[3]**

**8.** A supermarket models queues at its tills. On average 120 customers arrive each hour. Each till serves 30 customers an hour.

**(a)** Calculate the number of tills needed so that the tills can serve all arriving customers. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe how what-if analysis could help the manager plan for a busy Saturday. **[3]**

**9.** Describe **three** characteristics of modelling software. **[3]**

**10.** A flight school wants to train pilots to cope with engine failure. Explain **two** advantages of using a flight simulator and **one** disadvantage. **[5]**

**11.** An analyst builds a spreadsheet model to forecast the population of a town. Evaluate how effective a spreadsheet is for this model. **[6]**

**12.** The council uses a simulation to plan the response to a flood. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of using the simulation rather than relying on past experience. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** A simplified representation of a real situation or system [1] that uses variables, rules and relationships in a computer to predict or imitate its behaviour [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Changing the input values of a model [1] and observing the effect on the results, to answer questions such as "what would happen if...?" [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Any two from: it is too dangerous to experiment on a real reactor [1]; real experiments are expensive [1]; accidents would have serious consequences [1]; the model can be run many times with the same conditions [1]. **[2]**

**4.** The user selects a target result [1] and one input cell, and the software changes that input until the result is reached (it works backwards) [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Any two from: financial forecasting [1]; population growth [1]; climate change [1]; weather systems [1]; queue management [1]; traffic flow [1]; construction [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** 200 × (12 − 5) − 600 = 200 × 7 − 600 = 1400 − 600 = 800 pounds [2] (one mark for the method, one for the result).

**(b)** 150 tickets: 150 × 7 − 600 = 450 pounds [1]. 250 tickets: 250 × 7 − 600 = 1150 pounds [1].

**(c)** 200 × (price − 5) − 600 = 1000, so 200 × (price − 5) = 1600 [1]; price − 5 = 8 [1]; price = 13 pounds [1].

**(d)** Goal seek [1]. The profit cell is the target (set to 1000) and the price cell is the cell that changes [1].

**(e)** The profit is zero when units × 7 = 600, so units = 600 / 7 = 85.7 [1]. At least 86 tickets must be sold [1]. **[11]**

**7.** If the values are in separate labelled cells, a user can change one value and the profit updates immediately [1], which makes what-if analysis quick [1]. Typing numbers into the formula hides them and risks errors when they change, because the formula must be edited each time [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** 120 / 30 = 4 tills [2] (one mark for the method).

**(b)** The manager changes the number of arriving customers to the expected Saturday figure [1] and the number of tills [1], and observes the effect on the waiting time, to decide how many staff are needed [1]. **[5]**

**9.** Any three from: allows variables and rules to be set and changed [1]; recalculates automatically when inputs change [1]; stores and processes large amounts of data quickly [1]; shows results as tables, graphs and charts [1]; saves and compares scenarios [1]; tools such as goal seek [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Advantage 1: pilots can practise emergencies safely, without risk to people or aircraft [1], which could not be done with a real engine failure [1]. Advantage 2: it is cheaper than using a real aircraft and fuel [1]. Disadvantage: the simulation is not exactly the same as a real flight, so trainees may react differently when in real danger [1], and a simulator is expensive to build [1]. Award the marks for two advantages (one developed) and one disadvantage (developed) to a maximum of five. **[5]**

**11.** Effective: familiar and cheap; formulas recalculate when inputs such as birth rate change [1]; charts show the future growth clearly [1]; what-if analysis allows different scenarios to be compared [1]. Limitations: population depends on many things that are hard to predict, such as migration and government policy, so results are estimates [1]; errors in formulas or assumptions are hard to see [1]; a large or complex model may be slow or need specialist software [1]. Conclusion: suitable for a simple forecast for one town if assumptions are clear and the results are treated as estimates [1]. Any six marks. **[6]**

**12.** Advantages: different floods can be tested safely, without a real disaster [1]; plans can be improved before an emergency happens [1]; the simulation can be repeated with different weather, river levels or times [1]. Disadvantages: the model cannot include every factor, so it may be wrong [1]; it relies on accurate data about rivers and buildings [1]; building and running it is expensive [1]. Conclusion: it should be used together with experience and expert judgement [1]. Any six marks. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not showing the working in a profit or break-even calculation.
- Describing goal seek as "finding the best answer" instead of finding the input for a chosen result.
- Naming a use of modelling without saying what is modelled.
- Saying a simulation is exactly the same as the real situation.
- Missing the conclusion in an evaluate or discuss question.
