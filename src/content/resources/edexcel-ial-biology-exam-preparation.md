Edexcel International A Level Biology (YBI11) is modular: three International Advanced Subsidiary
units followed by three International A2 units, each externally assessed. Unit 1 (Molecules, Diet,
Transport and Health) and Unit 2 (Cells, Development, Biodiversity and Conservation) are each 1 hour
30 minutes, 80 marks and 20%. Unit 4 (Energy, Environment, Microbiology and Immunity) and Unit 5
(Respiration, Internal Environment, Coordination and Gene Technology) are each 1 hour 45 minutes, 90
marks and 20%. Units 3 and 6 -- **Practical Skills in Biology I and II** -- are each 1 hour 20 minutes,
50 marks and 10%, and they are **written papers, not hands-on laboratory assessments**. These notes
complement the site's guides to
[Molecules, Transport and Health](/resources/a-level-edexcel-biology-molecules-transport-and-health/)
and
[Gas Exchange Surfaces, Membranes and Osmosis](/resources/a-level-edexcel-biology-membranes-gas-exchange-and-osmosis/).

## Units 3 and 6 are written papers about practical work -- and they are 20% together

This is the fact most often misunderstood about this specification. Practical skills are examined by
two written papers, which test experimental design, data handling, error analysis and evaluation
rather than manipulative technique. **Exam-preparation priority**: revise them as examinable content
in their own right -- variables and controls, precision versus accuracy, sources of systematic and
random error, how to improve a method, how to process and present data. Having done the practicals is
necessary but not sufficient; being able to write about them under exam conditions is what is
assessed.

## The modular structure lets you fix a weak unit -- if you plan for it

Because units are sat in separate series, a disappointing unit result can often be addressed without
retaking the whole qualification. **Exam-preparation priority**: treat each unit's result as
information, and plan any retake early rather than deciding late in the A2 year. Equally, do not bank
on a retake to justify an underprepared first sitting -- resit opportunities cost time that the A2
units need.

## The A2 units are longer papers with more marks -- and they assume the AS content

Units 4 and 5 run 105 minutes for 90 marks, against 90 minutes for 80 in Units 1 and 2. They also
build on the AS material rather than replacing it. **Exam-preparation priority**: revisit Unit 1 and
Unit 2 content during A2 preparation. Biochemistry, transport and cell structure recur throughout
respiration, immunity and gene technology, and a candidate whose AS knowledge has faded finds the A2
papers considerably harder than they need to be.

## Every written paper carries a stated minimum of mathematics-targeted marks

The specification states a minimum number of marks on each paper targeting Level 2 mathematics or
above -- for example, at least 8 marks on Unit 1. **Exam-preparation priority**: practise the specific
mathematical skills biology uses: percentage change, magnification and scale calculations, rates from
gradients, standard form, and statistical reasoning about significance. These are guaranteed marks
that are lost through unfamiliarity rather than through biological misunderstanding.

## Data-handling questions want inference, not description

Across all six units, questions supply unfamiliar data and ask what can be concluded from it.
**Exam-preparation priority**: practise the discipline of saying what the data does *and does not*
show. Recognising that a correlation in supplied data does not establish a mechanism, and saying so
explicitly, is regularly worth marks.

## Worked routine: evaluating an experimental method

The routine below is an original model written for this resource, not a reproduction of any official
past paper or mark scheme.

```
Step 1 - identify the independent, dependent and control variables:
Name them explicitly. Most evaluation marks depend on this being
right.

Step 2 - name ONE specific weakness, not "human error":
"The colour change endpoint was judged by eye, so the recorded
time varies between repeats" is creditable; "human error" is not.

Step 3 - say what effect that weakness has on the RESULT:
Does it make values systematically high or low, or just scatter
them? Systematic and random errors are treated differently.

Step 4 - give a specific improvement that addresses THAT weakness:
A colorimeter for the endpoint judgement, not a general "repeat
more times".

Step 5 - state what the data can and cannot support:
"These results are consistent with X, but do not establish it,
because Y was not controlled."
```

Step 3 is the step that distinguishes a genuine evaluation from a list of criticisms: naming a
weakness without saying how it affects the result leaves the argument incomplete.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exams**: revise Units 3 and 6 as examinable written content -- variables, error types,
  precision, improvement, data processing -- rather than as a record of practicals done; revisit AS
  content during A2 preparation; drill the specific mathematics each paper guarantees will appear.
- **Across the modular route**: use each unit result as information and plan any retake early.
- **During A2 papers**: allow for the longer paper and higher mark total, and expect AS content to
  reappear.
- **On evaluation questions**: name variables, name one specific weakness, state its effect on the
  result, give a targeted improvement, and say what the data cannot establish.

## Self-test

1. What are Units 3 and 6, and what is the common misconception about them?
2. What is the weighting of each unit type?
3. Why does AS content need revisiting during A2 preparation?
4. Why is "human error" not a creditable weakness?

**Answers:** 1. Practical Skills in Biology I and II -- they are written papers examining experimental
design, data handling and evaluation, not hands-on laboratory assessments, and together they are 20%
of the qualification. 2. Units 1, 2, 4 and 5 are 20% each; Units 3 and 6 are 10% each. 3. Because the
A2 units build on rather than replace AS material -- biochemistry, transport and cell structure recur
throughout respiration, immunity and gene technology. 4. Because it identifies nothing specific: a
creditable weakness names the particular step that introduces uncertainty and can be paired with a
targeted improvement.
