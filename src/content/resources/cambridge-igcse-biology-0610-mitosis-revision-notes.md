These notes condense sub-topic **17.2 Mitosis** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. The whole sub-topic is Supplement content: **Extended only**.

For explanations and worked examples, read the [mitosis study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-mitosis/). Then try the [mitosis practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-mitosis-practice/).

## The five statements to learn

1. Mitosis is **nuclear division** giving rise to **genetically identical cells**. Stages are **not required**.
2. Roles: **growth**, **repair of damaged tissues**, **replacement of cells**, **asexual reproduction**.
3. **Exact replication** of chromosomes happens **before** mitosis.
4. During mitosis the **copies of chromosomes separate**, **maintaining the chromosome number** in each daughter cell.
5. **Stem cells** are **unspecialised** cells that **divide by mitosis** to produce daughter cells that **can become specialised** for specific functions.

## Why daughter cells are identical

**exact copy of every chromosome (before) → copies separate, one to each new nucleus (during) → each daughter cell gets the same chromosomes as the parent**

## Numbers through one division

| | Chromosome number | DNA quantity |
|---|---|---|
| Parent cell before replication | n (e.g. 46) | x |
| After replication | n (each chromosome now two joined copies) | 2x |
| Each daughter cell | n | x |

- DNA **doubles** before mitosis and **halves** when the cell divides.
- Chromosome number **never changes** in mitosis.

**Cells from repeated division of one cell:** 2, 4, 8, 16 ... After k rounds: 2ᵏ cells.

**Worked recall: cells against time.** If a cell divides every 6 hours, after 1 day there have been 24 ÷ 6 = 4 rounds, so one cell gives 2⁴ = **16** identical cells. Convert all times to the same unit before dividing.

**Worked recall: reading a DNA graph.** A flat line, then a rise to double the value, shows exact replication before mitosis. A sudden drop back to the starting value shows the copies separating into two daughter nuclei. The chromosome number in each daughter nucleus is the same as at the start.

## The four roles, with examples

| Role | Example |
|---|---|
| Growth | zygote → embryo; dividing cells at root and shoot tips |
| Repair of damaged tissues | closing a cut in the skin; mending a broken bone |
| Replacement of cells | skin surface cells; gut lining; red blood cells from bone marrow |
| Asexual reproduction | yeast budding; potato tubers; strawberry runners |

## Stem cells

- **Unspecialised**; divide by **mitosis**; daughter cells **can become specialised**.
- Found in **early embryos** and in some adult tissues such as **bone marrow** (blood cells).
- Specialised cells made from stem cells have the **same genes**; they **express different genes** (17.1).

## Mitosis compared with meiosis

| | Mitosis | Meiosis |
|---|---|---|
| Daughter cells | genetically identical | genetically different |
| Chromosome number | maintained | halved (diploid → haploid) |
| Used for | growth, repair, replacement, asexual reproduction | producing gametes |

**Quick decision:** new cells for growth, repair, replacement or asexual reproduction → **mitosis**. Gametes → **meiosis**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mitosis | nuclear division giving rise to genetically identical cells |
| Nuclear division | division of the nucleus, followed by division of the cell |
| Replication | exact copying of chromosomes before mitosis |
| Daughter cells | the cells produced by a division |
| Genetically identical | containing exactly the same genes and alleles |
| Stem cell | an unspecialised cell that divides by mitosis to give cells that can become specialised |
| Specialised cell | a cell with features suited to a particular function |

## Exam traps

- Replication is **before** mitosis, not during it.
- Chromosome number is **maintained**, not halved.
- Give all **four** roles when asked for "the roles".
- Stem cells are **unspecialised**.
- Specialised cells have **not lost genes**.
- Daughter cells have half the DNA **of the replicated cell**, the same as the original cell.
- Stages of mitosis are **not** required; do not waste time on them.

## Answer frames

- **Why daughter cells are genetically identical:** chromosomes replicated exactly before mitosis → copies separate → each daughter cell receives one copy of every chromosome.
- **Why stem cells can repair tissues:** unspecialised → divide by mitosis → identical daughter cells → become specialised as the cell type needed.
- **Why plants grown from cuttings look alike:** cells made by mitosis → genetically identical to the parent → same alleles for features such as flower colour.

## Self-test

**1.** Describe mitosis.

**2.** State the four roles of mitosis.

**3.** When does replication of chromosomes happen?

**4.** What happens to the copies of chromosomes during mitosis?

**5.** A cell with 20 chromosomes divides by mitosis. How many chromosomes does each daughter cell have?

**6.** The DNA content of a cell is 8 units before replication. What is it just before division, and in each daughter cell?

**7.** How many cells are produced from one cell after four rounds of mitosis?

**8.** Describe a stem cell.

**9.** Where are stem cells found in an adult human?

**10.** Why are a nerve cell and a muscle cell from one person different, though both came from the same zygote by mitosis?

**11.** Name the type of division used when a strawberry plant produces runners.

**12.** Name the type of division that produces gametes.

### Answers

**1.** Nuclear division giving rise to genetically identical cells.

**2.** Growth, repair of damaged tissues, replacement of cells, asexual reproduction.

**3.** Before mitosis.

**4.** They separate, one copy going to each daughter cell.

**5.** 20.

**6.** 16 units just before division; 8 units in each daughter cell.

**7.** 2⁴ = 16.

**8.** An unspecialised cell that divides by mitosis to produce daughter cells that can become specialised for specific functions.

**9.** For example, in bone marrow.

**10.** They have the same genes, but different genes are expressed in each.

**11.** Mitosis.

**12.** Meiosis.
