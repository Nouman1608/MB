## 3.15 at a glance

| Specification content | Key skills |
|---|---|
| Prepare and comment on partnership financial statements | Income statement, appropriation account, statement of financial position; interest on capital and drawings, salaries, loan interest, profit/loss shares, revaluation, goodwill |
| Prepare capital and current accounts | Fixed or fluctuating capitals; Partnership Act 1890 where there is no agreement |
| Account for changes in partnership | Admission of a new partner; retirement of a partner |

Dissolution of partnerships will not be examined (specification note).

## Key terms

- **Partnership agreement** -- the partners' own terms for sharing profit, interest and salaries.
- **Appropriation account** -- shows how profit for the year is divided between partners. (Older name: profit and loss appropriation account.)
- **Interest on capital** -- a share of profit rewarding capital invested. Not an expense.
- **Interest on drawings** -- a charge on partners for withdrawing resources during the year. Added back in the appropriation account.
- **Partnership salary** -- a share of profit for work done. Not wages.
- **Residual profit (or loss)** -- what remains after interest and salaries, shared in the profit-sharing ratio.
- **Profit-sharing ratio** -- the agreed ratio for sharing residual profit or loss; equal under the Partnership Act 1890 if there is no agreement.
- **Fixed capital account** -- holds agreed capital only; everything else goes to the **current account**.
- **Fluctuating capital account** -- no current account; all appropriations and drawings pass through capital.
- **Revaluation account** -- records changes in asset and liability values when the partnership changes.
- **Goodwill** -- the excess of the value of the business as a whole over the net value of its separate assets.

## Partnership Act 1890: default terms

Apply only where there is no agreement (or the agreement is silent):

| Item | Default |
|---|---|
| Profit and loss sharing | Equally |
| Interest on capital | None |
| Interest on drawings | None |
| Partnership salaries | None |
| Interest on a partner's loan | Allowed |

## Where each item goes

| Item | Statement | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Interest on a partner's loan | Income statement (expense) | Reduces profit for the year |
| Partner's loan | Statement of financial position (liability) | Not capital |
| Interest on drawings | Appropriation account | Added; debited to current account |
| Interest on capital | Appropriation account | Deducted; credited to current account |
| Partnership salary | Appropriation account | Deducted; credited to current account |
| Residual profit share | Appropriation account | Credited (a loss is debited) |
| Drawings | Current account (debit) | Not in the appropriation account |

## Method in steps: appropriation account

```
Profit for the year (after loan interest)
+ Interest on drawings (each partner)
- Interest on capital (each partner)
- Partnership salaries
= Residual profit (or loss)
  shared in the profit-sharing ratio
```

Check: interest on capital + salaries + residual = profit for the year + interest on drawings.

## Method in steps: current account (fixed capitals)

```
Credit side:  opening credit balance, interest on capital,
              salary, share of residual profit
Debit side:   opening debit balance, interest on drawings,
              drawings, share of residual loss
Balance:      carried down; a debit balance means drawings
              exceeded what the partner was credited
```

Present partners side by side in columnar form: it is quicker and each column must balance.

## Changes in partnership: the entries

| Event | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Asset revalued upward | Asset | Revaluation |
| Asset revalued downward / provision increased | Revaluation | Asset / provision |
| Revaluation profit | Revaluation | Old partners' capitals (old ratio) |
| Revaluation loss | Old partners' capitals (old ratio) | Revaluation |
| Goodwill created | Goodwill | Old partners' capitals (old ratio) |
| Goodwill written off (not kept) | Continuing partners' capitals (new ratio) | Goodwill |
| New partner brings cash | Bank | New partner's capital |
| Retiring partner's current account (credit balance) | Current account | Capital account |
| Retiring partner paid | Capital account | Bank and/or loan account |

**Rule of thumb:** anything that arose under the old partnership (revaluation, goodwill created) is shared in the **old** ratio. Writing goodwill off is in the **new** ratio.

## Small worked reminders

**Goodwill net effect.** A and B share equally. C joins; the new ratio is A 2 : B 2 : C 1. Goodwill of £18,000 is not kept.

- Created 1 : 1: A £9,000, B £9,000.
- Written off 2 : 2 : 1: A £7,200, B £7,200, C £3,600.
- Net: A +£1,800, B +£1,800, C -£3,600.

The new partner pays for the share of goodwill gained, and the old partners who gave up profit share are credited.

**Residual loss.** If interest and salaries exceed the profit available, they are still credited in full and the shortfall is shared as a loss in the profit-sharing ratio.

**Retiring partner.** Amount due = capital + current account credit balance (or minus a debit balance) + revaluation share + goodwill share. Anything not paid in cash becomes a loan, shown as a liability.

## Commenting on partnership statements

- Compare each partner's total share (salary + interest + residual) with the capital and work they contribute.
- A falling or debit current account means drawings exceed the partner's share, draining cash from the firm.
- A large loan from a partner adds interest before profit for the year and must be repaid.
- On admission, judge whether the new partner's capital and goodwill payment are fair to the existing partners.
- Under the Partnership Act 1890, equal sharing may be unfair where capital or effort differs.

## Must-know distinctions

| Easily confused | The difference |
|---|---|
| Interest on capital vs interest on a partner's loan | Capital: appropriation of profit. Loan: expense before profit. |
| Partnership salary vs wages | Salary to a partner is an appropriation; wages to employees are an expense. |
| Fixed vs fluctuating capitals | Fixed: capital stays, current account moves. Fluctuating: one account moves. |
| Old ratio vs new ratio | Old for revaluation and creating goodwill; new for writing goodwill off. |
| Goodwill kept vs not kept | Kept: shown as a non-current asset. Not kept: created then written off, closing to nil. |
| Admission vs retirement | Admission: new partner brings capital. Retirement: retiring partner is paid in cash or left as a loan. |

## Quick self-test

1. Where is interest on a partner's loan shown?
2. Profit for the year is £40,000. Interest on drawings is £600, interest on capital £5,000 and salaries £12,000. Calculate the residual profit.
3. Share the residual profit from question 2 in the ratio 3 : 1.
4. Under fixed capitals, which account receives a partner's share of profit?
5. A partner draws £9,000 on 1 May. The year ends 31 December and interest on drawings is 6% a year. Calculate the interest.
6. On admission, a building rises £20,000, inventory falls £1,500 and the provision for doubtful debts rises £500. Old partners share 2 : 1. Calculate each partner's share of the revaluation.
7. Using the goodwill reminder above, what single amount does the new partner's capital account lose?
8. Two partners have capitals of £30,000 and £10,000 and no agreement. Profit is £25,000. How much is each partner credited?
9. A retiring partner has capital £20,000, a current account **debit** balance of £1,500, a revaluation share of £2,000 and a goodwill share of £5,000. Calculate the amount due.
10. Is a partnership salary an expense in the income statement?
11. State what a debit balance on a current account shows.

### Answers

1. Income statement, as an expense.
2. 40,000 + 600 - 5,000 - 12,000 = **£23,600**.
3. **£17,700** and **£5,900**.
4. The current account (credit side).
5. 9,000 x 6% x 8/12 = **£360**.
6. Profit 20,000 - 1,500 - 500 = £18,000; **£12,000** and **£6,000**.
7. **£3,600**.
8. **£12,500** each (shared equally).
9. 20,000 - 1,500 + 2,000 + 5,000 = **£25,500**.
10. No. It is an appropriation of profit.
11. The partner has drawn more than the opening balance plus everything credited for the year.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting loan interest below profit for the year, in the appropriation account.
- Subtracting interest on drawings in the appropriation account.
- Forgetting the time factor in interest on drawings (months from the drawing date to the year end).
- Applying the agreement's salary or interest when the question says there is no agreement.
- Sharing revaluation profit or goodwill created in the new ratio.
- Omitting the goodwill write-off, so the goodwill account does not close.
- Missing the retiring partner's current account transfer, or adding a debit balance instead of deducting it.
- Showing a debit current account balance without brackets in the equity section.
- Placing a retiring partner's loan in capital rather than liabilities.
