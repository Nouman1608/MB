**These notes condense two linked elements of Option 2B:** "the connection of the smaller world to
the larger world" and "the focus on human organisation: domestically, in the work place, in local
and national governments". The
[smaller world and larger world study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-english-literature-7717-smaller-world-and-larger-world/)
has full analysis and a model paragraph; the

## The four levels of organisation

1. **Domestic**: home, marriage, family.
2. **Workplace**: factory, mill, bank, farm.
3. **Local government**: town, parish, village, landowner.
4. **National government**: court, state, regime, war.

Trace a line through at least two levels in every answer.

## Six methods that connect scales

| Method | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Microcosm | A small world that stands for a larger one | The Commander's household as Gilead in miniature |
| Rehearsal or parody | A small world imitates a larger one | The tavern's mock court in *Henry IV Part I* |
| Synecdoche | A part stands for the whole | Blake's single sweep for a society's cruelty |
| Juxtaposition | Scenes at different scales side by side | Court, rebels and tavern alternating |
| Chain of cause | A decision followed to its effects | Gradgrind's schoolroom to factory to Parliament |
| Frame | An outer narrative places the story in history | The Historical Notes, set in 2195 |

## Text grid

| Text | Smaller world | Larger world | Link |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Henry IV Part I* | Eastcheap tavern | Court and rebellion | Hal and Falstaff play king and prince |
| *Hard Times* | Schoolroom, Gradgrind home | Coketown, Parliament | One philosophy of facts governs all |
| *The Handmaid's Tale* | Offred's room, the household | Gilead; a future symposium | The Ceremony; the Historical Notes |
| *A Doll's House* | The Helmers' living room | The bank and respectable society | Torvald's power over Krogstad's job |
| *The Kite Runner* | Baba's house in Kabul | Afghan history from the monarchy to the Taliban | Amir and Hassan mirror Pashtun and Hazara |
| Blake's *Songs* | A child, a voice | London's institutions | "Mind-forg'd manacles" in every voice |

| *Harvest* | One village | Economic change | Enclosure of common land for sheep |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Microcosm | A small world representing a larger one |
| Macrocosm | The larger world a microcosm reflects |
| Synecdoche | A part used to represent the whole |
| Frame narrative | An outer story that contains and contextualises an inner one |
| Domestic sphere | The private world of home and family |
| Public sphere | The world of work, politics and institutions |
| Juxtaposition | Placing contrasting scenes or ideas side by side |
| Allegory | A story whose elements systematically stand for something else |
| Body politic | The state imagined as a body, with the ruler as head |
| Utilitarianism | A philosophy judging actions by usefulness, satirised in *Hard Times* |

## Unseen checklist

- What is the smallest unit shown: a person, a room, a family?
- What larger structure is present or implied: a company, a law, a war?
- Which details connect them: a uniform, a notice, a wage, a broadcast, a name?
- Does the extract move between scales, or stay small and let the reader infer?

## Paragraph frame

1. Name the smaller world and its organisation.
2. Name the larger world it connects to.
3. Analyse the method of connection (microcosm, chain, frame…).
4. Explain what the connection reveals about power.
5. Weigh a limit: where the connection breaks or the larger world wins.

## Exam traps

- Writing about the text's "message" without showing how scales are linked.
- Ignoring domestic or workplace power, which AQA names explicitly.
- Describing settings without analysing what connects them.
- Context as a list of dates rather than as the larger world reaching a scene.
- Treating the frame of *The Handmaid's Tale* as an afterthought.

## Self-test

1. Quote the two AQA elements covered here.
2. Name the four levels of human organisation.
3. What is a microcosm? Give an example from a set text.
4. How does the tavern rehearse the court in *Henry IV Part I*?
5. Trace the chain from small to large in *Hard Times*.
6. How does the Ceremony connect household and state in Gilead?
7. What does the frame of the Historical Notes add?
8. How does the bank link the domestic and the economic in *A Doll's House*?
9. How do national events reshape the small world of *The Kite Runner*?
10. Define synecdoche and give a Blake example.
11. What larger event lies behind Harrison's "V"?
12. Name one method suited to an unseen extract that stays at a small scale.

## Answers

1. "The connection of the smaller world to the larger world" and "the focus on human organisation:
   domestically, in the work place, in local and national governments".
2. Domestic; workplace; local government; national government.
3. A small world that stands for a larger one, such as the Commander's household as Gilead in
   miniature.
4. Falstaff and Hal take turns playing the king and the prince in Act 2 Scene 4, staging a mock
   court.
5. Gradgrind's schoolroom of facts shapes the minds that run Coketown's factories, and Gradgrind
   enters Parliament; the same philosophy damages Louisa's marriage at home.
6. It makes the state's control of reproduction a domestic ritual inside one household.
7. It places Offred's account in a larger history and questions how scholarship treats individual
   suffering.
8. Torvald's promotion to bank manager gives him power over Krogstad's job, so Krogstad's threat to
   Nora is tied to the workplace.
9. The end of the monarchy, the Soviet invasion and Taliban rule break up the household and change
   Amir's and Hassan's lives.
10. A part representing the whole; for example, the single sweep standing for exploited children, or
    the voices of "London" carrying "mind-forg'd manacles".
11. The miners' strike of the mid-1980s.
12. Synecdoche: a single detail, such as a uniform or a notice, implying the larger system.
