These questions cover section 1.1, Types of business organisation, in Topic 1 of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Accounting (4AC1) specification, Issue 1, October 2016. All businesses and figures are invented. Amounts are in dollars, written without a currency sign. Teaching is in the [types of business organisation study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-accounting-4ac1-types-of-business-organisation/) and the [types of business organisation revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-accounting-4ac1-types-of-business-organisation-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** State whether each organisation is in the public sector or the private sector: (a) a city council's bus service; (b) a hair salon owned by one person; (c) a state secondary school; (d) a dental practice owned by two dentists. **[4]**

**2.** State **two** characteristics of a sole trader. **[2]**

**3.** Explain how a public sector organisation is mainly financed. **[2]**

**4.** A partnership makes a profit of 63,000, shared between Ayla and Ben in the ratio 4 : 3. Calculate each partner's share. **[2]**

**5.** Hartwell & Rowe, a partnership, has debts of 95,000. Its assets are sold for 71,000. Calculate the shortfall and explain who must pay it. **[3]**

**6.** Identify **two** internal stakeholders of a partnership. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Sanjay Mehta is a sole trader. His profit last year was 40,000. Lina Haddad offers to join him as a partner, bringing capital of 30,000 and experience of online selling. They forecast profit of 64,000 a year, shared 5 : 3 in Sanjay's favour.

**(a)** Calculate each partner's share of the forecast profit. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate Sanjay's share if profit is only 52,000. **[1]**
**(c)** Evaluate whether Sanjay should form the partnership. **[6]**

**8.** Extracts from the financial statements of Pinecrest Supplies, a sole trader, for the year just ended:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Revenue | 210,000 |
| Profit for the year | 33,600 |
| Bank | 2,100 |
| Trade receivables | 8,400 |
| Trade payables | 15,900 |
| Bank loan (repayments of 5,000 a year) | 25,000 |

**(a)** A supplier is deciding whether to give Pinecrest more credit. Use the figures to advise the supplier. **[3]**
**(b)** The bank is reviewing the loan. Calculate how many times profit covers the yearly repayment, and comment. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how an employee of Pinecrest might use the financial statements. **[2]**

**9.** Explain **two** differences between a public sector organisation and a private sector organisation. **[4]**

**10.** Explain **two** advantages and **one** disadvantage of operating as a sole trader. **[6]**

**11.** The partners of Dunmore Electrical plan to take drawings equal to almost all of this year's profit. Explain why the firm's bank and its employees might each object. **[4]**

**12.** Explain why two people starting a partnership should draw up a partnership agreement. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) Public [1]. (b) Private [1]. (c) Public [1]. (d) Private [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A business serving the public is still private if individuals own it.

**2.** Any two: one owner [1]; unlimited liability; owner keeps all the profit; decisions made by one person; limited capital [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Mainly through taxation collected by national or local government [1], so it can provide services free or at low cost rather than needing to make a profit [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Ayla 63,000 × 4/7 = **36,000** [1]. Ben 63,000 × 3/7 = **27,000** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Shortfall 95,000 − 71,000 = **24,000** [1]. The partners must pay it [1] from their personal assets, because they are personally responsible for the firm's debts [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Any two: the partners; managers; employees [1] [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Sanjay 64,000 × 5/8 = **40,000** [1]. Lina 64,000 × 3/8 = **24,000** [1].
**(b)** 52,000 × 5/8 = **32,500** [1].
**(c)** For: Sanjay's income stays at 40,000 if the forecast is met [1]; the 30,000 capital and Lina's online selling skills could help the business grow [1]; workload and risk are shared [1]. Against: if profit is only 52,000, Sanjay earns 7,500 less than now [1]; he must share decisions and becomes responsible for debts from Lina's decisions [1]. Judgement: form the partnership only if the forecast is realistic and a written agreement sets out profit shares and drawings [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the 32,500 from (b) in the evaluation; it is the strongest point against.

**8. (a)** Bank and receivables total 2,100 + 8,400 = 10,500 [1], which is less than trade payables of 15,900 [1], so Pinecrest may struggle to pay suppliers on time; the supplier should limit extra credit or ask for prompt payment [1].
**(b)** 33,600 ÷ 5,000 = **6.72 times** [1]; profit easily covers the repayment, so the loan looks safe, although the low bank balance means cash must be watched [1].
**(c)** The employee would look at profit for the year in the statement of profit or loss [1] to judge whether the job is secure and whether a pay rise is affordable [1]. **[7]**

**9.** Ownership: public sector organisations are owned by government, whereas private sector organisations are owned by individuals [1], so they are accountable to government and taxpayers rather than to owners [1]. Aim: the public sector mainly aims to provide a service [1], whereas the private sector usually aims to make a profit for its owners [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Advantage: the owner keeps all the profit [1], so there is a strong incentive to work hard [1]. Advantage: decisions are made quickly by one person [1], so the business can respond fast to customers [1]. Disadvantage: unlimited liability [1], so the owner's personal assets, such as a house, are at risk if the business fails [1]. **[6]**

**11.** Bank: drawings reduce the capital and the cash kept in the business [1], so less is available to repay the loan, increasing the risk to the bank [1]. Employees: profit taken out cannot be used for pay rises or investment [1], which may reduce job security if the firm cannot grow or meet difficulties [1]. **[4]**

**12.** It records the agreed profit-sharing ratio, capital contributions and drawings rules [1], so each partner knows their rights [1], which reduces the chance of disputes later, for example when a partner wants to leave [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling a sole trader or partnership "limited".
- Labelling an organisation without giving a reason when "explain" is used.
- Naming a stakeholder but not the figure or decision.
- Giving an evaluation with no judgement.
- Ignoring the case figures in an advice question.
