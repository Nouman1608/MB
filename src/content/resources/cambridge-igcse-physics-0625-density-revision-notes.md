Full explanations and worked examples are in the [study guide on density](/resources/cambridge-igcse-physics-0625-density/); the [Motion, forces and energy revision notes](/resources/igcse-physics-motion-forces-and-energy-revision-notes/) cover the whole of Topic 1.

## Definition and equation

- **Density:** mass per unit volume.
- **ρ = m / V**; rearranged, m = ρV and V = m/ρ.

| Mass in | Volume in | Density in |
|---|---|---|
| kg | m³ | kg/m³ |
| g | cm³ | g/cm³ |

**Conversions**

- 1 g/cm³ = **1000 kg/m³** (so 2.7 g/cm³ = 2700 kg/m³; 850 kg/m³ = 0.85 g/cm³).
- 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³; 1 litre = 1000 cm³ = 0.001 m³.
- 1 cm³ = 1 ml.
- Water ≈ 1.0 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³.

**Density is a property of the material:** it does not change when the object is cut into pieces.

## Experiments in steps

### Regular solid

1. Mass on a balance.
2. Measure length, width and height with a ruler.
3. V = l × w × h (cylinder: πr² × length).
4. ρ = m/V.

  <img src="https://img.marlbridge.com/cambridge-igcse-physics-0625-density-cambridge-igcse-physics--04-regular-solid-and-liquid.webp" width="900" height="700" alt="Left: a brass cuboid 5.0 cm by 3.0 cm by 2.0 cm on a balance reading 255 g; V = 30 cm³ and ρ = 255/30 = 8.5 g/cm³ = 8500 kg/m³. Right: an empty measuring cylinder reads 86.4 g and 126.4 g with 50.0 cm³ of liquid, so the liquid has mass 40.0 g and density 0.800 g/cm³." loading="lazy">
  <figcaption>Volume from a ruler or a measuring cylinder; mass from a balance; then ρ = m/V.</figcaption>
</figure>

### Liquid

1. Mass of the empty measuring cylinder (or tare the balance with it on).
2. Add liquid; read the volume (bottom of meniscus, eye level).
3. Mass of cylinder + liquid.
4. Liquid mass = difference; ρ = m/V.

### Irregular solid that sinks

1. Mass on a balance first (dry).
2. Part-fill a measuring cylinder with water; read V₁.
3. Lower the object in on a thread until fully covered; read V₂.
4. Volume = V₂ − V₁; ρ = m/(V₂ − V₁).

Alternative: a **displacement (overflow) can** filled to the spout; collect the water the object pushes out in a measuring cylinder.

Precautions: no splashing; object fully covered; no trapped air bubbles; read the bottom of the meniscus.

### Which method?

| Object | Find its volume by | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| cuboid or cylinder | measuring its dimensions | regular shape |
| small pebble | rise in a measuring cylinder | fits in the cylinder and sinks |
| large rock | overflow can | too wide for a cylinder |
| cork | not by simple displacement | floats, so it is not fully covered |

Small dimensions give large percentage errors: a 1 mm error in a 1.0 cm side is 10%, so measure each side in several places and average, or use a larger sample.

## Floating

- ρ(object) < ρ(liquid) → **floats**.
- ρ(object) > ρ(liquid) → **sinks**.
- Given mass and volume? Work out the density first.

### Liquids that do not mix (Extended only)

- Layers form with the **densest liquid at the bottom**.
- A less dense liquid floats on a denser one.
- An object settles at the boundary between two layers if its density lies between theirs.
- Liquids that mix form no layers.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Density | Mass per unit volume |
| Displacement | Water pushed aside by an object; its volume equals the volume of the submerged object |
| Displacement (overflow) can | Can with a spout; water that overflows when an object is added is collected and measured |
| Meniscus | Curved liquid surface; read the bottom for water |
| Tare | Set a balance to zero with a container on it |
| Immiscible liquids (Extended) | Liquids that do not mix |

## Exam traps

- **ρ = V/m** written by mistake: check that a metal gives a density bigger than 1 g/cm³.
- **Mixed units:** never combine g with m³ or kg with cm³.
- **Factor of 1000:** g/cm³ × 1000 = kg/m³.
- **Final level as volume:** the object's volume is the rise in level.
- **Container mass included:** subtract the empty cylinder's mass.
- **Floating objects by displacement:** the syllabus method is for solids that sink.
- **"Heavy things sink":** compare densities, not masses.
- *(Extended)* **Liquid order by mass or volume:** order is by density only.

## Quick self-test

1. Define density.
2. Convert 7.9 g/cm³ to kg/m³.
3. Convert 920 kg/m³ to g/cm³.
4. A metal cube of side 2.0 cm has a mass of 72 g. Find its density.
5. A liquid has a density of 1.2 g/cm³. Find the mass of 250 cm³ of it.
6. A stone is lowered into a measuring cylinder; the level rises from 35 cm³ to 47 cm³. The stone's mass is 30 g. Find its density.
7. An empty measuring cylinder has a mass of 92.0 g. With 40.0 cm³ of oil in it, the mass is 128.0 g. Find the density of the oil.
8. A block has a mass of 600 g and a volume of 750 cm³. Does it float in water?
9. A gold ring and a gold bar are made of the same gold. Compare their densities.
10. Why should an object be weighed before it is put into the water?
11. *(Extended)* Liquids A (0.79 g/cm³) and B (1.6 g/cm³) do not mix. Which forms the top layer?
12. *(Extended)* A cork of density 0.24 g/cm³ is dropped into the jar in question 11. Where does it end up?

### Answers

1. **Mass per unit volume.**
2. 7.9 × 1000 = **7900 kg/m³**.
3. 920 ÷ 1000 = **0.92 g/cm³**.
4. V = 2.0³ = 8.0 cm³; ρ = 72 ÷ 8.0 = **9.0 g/cm³**.
5. m = 1.2 × 250 = **300 g**.
6. V = 12 cm³; ρ = 30 ÷ 12 = **2.5 g/cm³**.
7. m = 36.0 g; ρ = 36.0 ÷ 40.0 = **0.900 g/cm³**.
8. ρ = 600 ÷ 750 = 0.80 g/cm³, less than 1.0 g/cm³, so it **floats**.
9. **The same**: density depends on the material, not the size.
10. Water clinging to it would add to its measured mass.
11. **A** (it is less dense).
12. It **floats on top of liquid A** (0.24 < 0.79).
