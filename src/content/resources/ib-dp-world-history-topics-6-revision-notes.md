Causes and effects of Early Modern wars (1500-1750) is world history topic 6 for Paper 2. Revise at least two wars from different regions under causes, practices of war and their impact on the outcome, and effects.

Related: [Early Modern wars study guide](/resources/ib-dp-world-history-topics-6/) and [Early Modern wars practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-world-history-topics-6-practice/).

## War profiles

**Thirty Years' War (1618-1648): Europe**
- *Causes*: limits of Augsburg (1555); Protestant Union (1608) and Catholic League (1609); Habsburg ambitions; Bohemian revolt and the Defenestration of Prague (1618); election of Frederick V (1619).
- *Phases*: Bohemian (White Mountain, 1620); Danish (to 1629); Edict of Restitution (1629); Swedish (Breitenfeld 1631, Lützen 1632); Peace of Prague (1635); French entry (1635).
- *Practices*: mercenary armies; Wallenstein's contribution system; Gustavus Adolphus's tactics; plunder and sieges, including the sack of Magdeburg (1631).
- *Effects*: Westphalia (1648); Calvinism recognised; princes' right to make alliances; Sweden and France gain territory; Dutch and Swiss independence recognised; heavy population losses in parts of Germany.

**Imjin War (1592-1598): Asia and Oceania**
- *Causes*: Hideyoshi's unification (1590) and ambition to conquer Ming China; Korea's refusal of passage; need to reward warriors.
- *Practices*: Japanese arquebusiers and rapid advance to Seoul and Pyongyang (1592); Yi Sun-sin's navy and turtle ships (Hansan Island 1592, Myeongnyang 1597, Noryang 1598); Korean righteous armies; Ming intervention (from 1593); truce talks; second invasion (1597).
- *Effects*: Japanese withdrawal after Hideyoshi's death (1598); Korea devastated; Ming finances strained; Tokugawa Ieyasu's rise (Sekigahara 1600); Korean artisans taken to Japan.

**War of the Spanish Succession (1701-1714): Europe and the Americas**
- *Causes*: will of Charles II leaving Spain to Philip of Anjou; fear of French dominance; colonial trade; Louis XIV's recognition of the Stuart claimant (1701).
- *Practices*: coalition warfare; Marlborough and Eugene (Blenheim 1704, Ramillies 1706, Oudenaarde 1708, Malplaquet 1709); war finance through public debt.
- *Effects*: Utrecht (1713) and Rastatt (1714); Philip V keeps Spain, renounces France; Austria gains the Spanish Netherlands, Milan and Naples; Britain gains Gibraltar, Minorca, Newfoundland, Nova Scotia and the *asiento*.

**Great Northern War (1700-1721): Europe**
- *Causes*: Swedish dominance of the Baltic; Russia, Denmark and Saxony seek to exploit the accession of the young Charles XII.
- *Practices*: Swedish victory at Narva (1700); Russian army and navy rebuilt; Charles XII's invasion of Russia; Poltava (1709).
- *Effects*: Treaty of Nystad (1721); Russia gains the Baltic coast and becomes a European great power; Swedish decline.

## Types of war

| Type | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Religious war | War in which confessional difference is a central cause | The early phases of the Thirty Years' War |
| Dynastic war | War over succession to a throne | War of the Spanish Succession |
| Civil war | War between groups within one state | English Civil Wars (1642-1651) |
| War of independence | War to break free of a ruling power | Dutch Revolt against Spain |
| War of expansion | War to conquer territory | Hideyoshi's invasions of Korea |

## Practices: what changed

| Development | Description | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| *Trace italienne* | Fortifications with angled bastions and low, thick walls | Long, costly sieges |
| Volley fire and drill | Ranks firing in turn, developed under Maurice of Nassau | Sustained firepower from infantry |
| Field artillery | Lighter guns moved with the army, used by Gustavus Adolphus | Firepower on the battlefield |
| Standing armies | Permanent, paid forces | Higher taxes and larger bureaucracies |
| Public debt | State borrowing backed by taxation, as in Britain after 1688 | Ability to sustain long wars |
| Naval innovation | Armoured "turtle ships" and cannon-armed fleets | Control of supply lines in the Imjin War |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Defenestration of Prague | The 1618 act of throwing royal officials from a window, starting the Bohemian revolt |
| Edict of Restitution | 1629 imperial order to return church lands taken by Protestants since 1552 |
| Contribution system | Funding an army by levies on occupied territory |
| Peace of Westphalia | The 1648 treaties of Münster and Osnabrück ending the Thirty Years' War |
| Righteous armies | Korean volunteer militias that fought the Japanese |
| Turtle ship | Armoured Korean warship associated with Yi Sun-sin |
| *Asiento* | Contract to supply enslaved Africans to Spanish America |
| Balance of power | Principle that no state should dominate the others |
| Military revolution | Debate on whether changes in warfare between about 1560 and 1660 transformed states |

## Exam traps

- **"Religious war" without qualification.** Catholic France fought the Catholic Habsburgs; explain the shift from religion to power politics.
- **Narrative of phases.** Use the phases only as evidence for an argument.
- **Ignoring non-European wars.** Prepare one Asian, African or American example for regional questions.
- **Effects beyond the war.** Say whether an effect came from the war itself or from later developments.
- **Dates.** The Seven Years' War (1756-1763) lies outside the topic.

## Self-test

1. What did the Peace of Augsburg fail to recognise, which later fed conflict?
   *Calvinism.*
2. What event of May 1618 began the Bohemian revolt?
   *The Defenestration of Prague.*
3. Who was elected king of Bohemia in 1619?
   *Frederick V, the Elector Palatine.*
4. What did the Edict of Restitution (1629) order?
   *The return of church lands taken by Protestants since 1552.*
5. Which Swedish king won at Breitenfeld and was killed at Lützen?
   *Gustavus Adolphus.*
6. Why did France join the war in 1635?
   *To weaken the Habsburgs, despite being Catholic.*
7. Who ordered the invasion of Korea in 1592, and what was his wider aim?
   *Toyotomi Hideyoshi, aiming to conquer Ming China.*
8. Which Korean admiral's fleet cut Japanese supply lines?
   *Yi Sun-sin.*
9. How did the Imjin War affect politics in Japan?
   *It weakened Toyotomi power and opened the way for Tokugawa Ieyasu's victory at Sekigahara in 1600.*
10. Whose inheritance caused the War of the Spanish Succession?
    *Charles II of Spain, who left his empire to Philip of Anjou.*
11. Name two gains Britain made at Utrecht.
    *Any two of Gibraltar, Minorca, Newfoundland, Nova Scotia and the asiento.*
12. Which historian first argued for a "military revolution" between 1560 and 1660?
    *Michael Roberts.*
