These questions cover subtopic **2.4 Internal and external communication** of Cambridge O Level Business Studies 7115 (2026 series). All businesses and figures are invented. The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question.

Related: the [Internal and external communication study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-business-7115-internal-and-external-communication/) and the [Internal and external communication revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-business-7115-internal-and-external-communication-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define effective communication. **[2]**

**2.** Identify **two** methods a business could use to communicate with its customers. **[2]**

**3.** Explain **one** difference between one-way and two-way communication. **[2]**

**4.** State **two** barriers to communication. **[2]**

**5.** Outline **one** benefit and **one** limitation of using text messages to communicate with delivery drivers. **[4]**

**6.** Explain **one** reason why effective communication is important in a hospital. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** Orchard Mills employs 300 people at two factories 80 km apart, working on different shifts. The managing director wants to explain a new bonus scheme to all employees. Some details are complicated and employees are likely to have questions. Recommend the method or methods of communication the managing director should use. Justify your answer. **[6]**

**8.** A restaurant chain wants to tell its customers about a new menu.

**(a)** Identify **two** IT-based methods it could use.

**(b)** Explain **one** benefit and **one** limitation of using social media for this message. **[6]**

**9.** At Torvane Construction, many site workers speak different first languages. Supervisors give instructions using technical building terms. Instructions from the site manager pass through two foremen before reaching the workers. Mistakes have become common.

**(a)** Identify **two** communication barriers at Torvane.

**(b)** Explain **two** problems that ineffective communication could cause for Torvane.

**(c)** Recommend **one** way Torvane could reduce one of the barriers you identified. **[8]**

**10.** Aldwyn Insurance has branches in four cities. Its branch managers meet every week. Consider whether the weekly meeting should be held by video conference or face-to-face at head office. Justify your answer. **[6]**

**11.** **(a)** Explain why a business should communicate in writing with a bank when agreeing a loan.

**(b)** Explain **one** problem a long chain of command can cause for communication. **[4]**

**12.** Five managers each travel to a monthly meeting at a cost of $180 per person per meeting. A video conferencing system would cost $4,000 a year.

**(a)** Calculate the annual saving if the business uses video conferencing instead of travel.

**(b)** Explain **one** factor, other than cost, that the business should consider. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** Communication in which the message is received and understood by the receiver [1] and acted on as the sender intended [1].

**2.** Any two [1] [1]: website; email; social media; letters; advertisements; telephone; text messages.

**3.** One-way communication gives no response from the receiver, such as a notice [1]; two-way communication includes feedback, such as a meeting, so the sender can check understanding [1].

**4.** Any two [1] [1]: jargon; unclear message; wrong medium; long chain of command; noise; technical failure; language differences; receiver not listening; lack of feedback.

**5.** Benefit identified [1] and outlined [1]: messages arrive instantly wherever the driver is, so urgent changes such as a new address reach them quickly. Limitation identified [1] and outlined [1]: messages must be short, so detailed instructions may be unclear; or drivers should not read messages while driving, so they may be delayed.

**6.** One reason identified [1] and explained [1]: staff must pass accurate information about patients' treatment between shifts, or mistakes could harm patients; or departments must coordinate so operations, beds and staff are ready at the same time.

**7.** A meeting at each factory, held for each shift and led by a senior manager [1], allows two-way communication, so employees can ask questions about the complicated details [1] and managers can check understanding [1]. Because the factories are 80 km apart and shifts differ, several meetings are needed, which takes management time [1]. A written summary sent by letter or email to every employee gives a permanent record of the scheme's rules [1]. An email alone would be cheaper and faster but gives no chance to ask questions about a complex scheme, so meetings plus a written summary are the better choice [1].

**8.** **(a)** Any two [1] [1]: website; email to customers on a mailing list; social media posts; text messages; a mobile app notification.

**(b)** Benefit identified [1] and explained [1]: reaches many customers quickly at low cost, and posts can include photos of the new dishes. Limitation identified [1] and explained [1]: customers who do not follow the chain on social media will not see the message; or negative comments about the menu are visible to everyone.

**9.** **(a)** Any two [1] [1]: language differences; technical jargon; long chain of command (message passes through two foremen).

**(b)** Two problems, each identified [1] and explained [1]: mistakes in building work, which must be redone, wasting materials and time; safety instructions misunderstood, causing accidents; delays in finishing projects, so customers may be lost or penalties paid; workers frustrated, lowering morale.

**(c)** One recommendation identified [1] and justified [1]: use visual instructions such as diagrams and translated safety notices, so workers understand regardless of language; or have the site manager brief workers directly at the start of each day, removing the chain through two foremen; or train supervisors to use plain words instead of technical terms.

**10.** Video conference: saves travel time and cost every week for managers in four cities [1]; managers spend more time running their branches [1]; but technical failures may interrupt the meeting, and it is harder to build relationships or discuss sensitive matters [1]. Face-to-face: easier discussion and body language help with complex or sensitive decisions [1]; but weekly travel is costly and tiring [1]. Judgement: use video conferencing for the weekly meeting and meet face-to-face occasionally, because weekly travel costs outweigh the benefits for routine updates [1].

**11.** **(a)** A written document gives a permanent record of the amount, interest and repayment terms [1], which can be checked later if there is a dispute [1].

**(b)** The message passes through many levels, so it may be delayed or changed before it reaches the receiver [1], leading to mistakes or late action [1].

**12.** **(a)** Travel cost = 5 × 180 × 12 [1] = $10,800 a year [1]. Saving = 10,800 − 4,000 = **$6,800** [1].

**(b)** One factor identified [1] and explained [1]: the quality of discussion may fall without face-to-face contact; the reliability of the internet connection; staff need training to use the system; managers may lose the chance to visit other sites.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Recommending email in question 7 even though the message is complex and needs questions answered.
- Listing barriers in question 9 without linking them to the facts given.
- Forgetting to multiply by 12 meetings in question 12.
- Giving a recommendation without explaining why the alternative is weaker.
- Defining communication as just "sending a message".
