of Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh), examined in Paper 2 Section A.** A timeline fixes the order of events, a
people table records who did what, and short lists give the reasons, events and lessons that 4-,
6- and 12-mark questions ask for.

## Pre-Islamic Arabia (Jahiliya)

- **Religious:** idol worship at the Ka'ba; some Jews, Christians and hanifs.
- **Political:** tribes, no state; Quraysh controlled Makka.
- **Moral:** alcohol, gambling, interest, blood feuds.
- **Social:** female infanticide; few rights for women and slaves.

## Qualities of the Prophet (pbuh)

Honesty (al-Amin, al-Sadiq); courage; kindness to the poor and orphans; earning an honest living as a
shepherd and trader; fair dealing; forgiveness.

## Reasons for Makkan opposition

1. Threat to idol-based trade and pilgrimage income.
2. Threat to leaders' status; teaching of equality.
3. Rejection of ancestral religion.
4. Disbelief in resurrection.

**Forms of opposition:** mockery; torture of weaker Muslims; boycott of Banu Hashim; rejection at
Ta'if; plot to kill.

## Isra wal Mi'raj: importance

- Comfort after the Year of Sorrow.
- Five daily prayers ordained.
- Link between Makka and Jerusalem; the Prophet leads earlier prophets in prayer.
- Test of faith; Abu Bakr named al-Siddiq.

## Madinah: first steps

- Mosque at Quba, then the Prophet's Mosque.
- Brotherhood between Muhajirun and Ansar.
- Charter of Madinah: one community; mutual defence; religious freedom; the Prophet as arbiter.

## Hudaibiyah terms

- Ten-year truce.
- Muslims return the following year for umrah.
- Makkans joining the Muslims returned; not the reverse.
- Tribes free to ally with either side.

## Farewell Sermon: key teachings

- Sanctity of life, property and honour.
- Equality: no Arab superior to a non-Arab except by piety.
- Rights and kind treatment of women.
- Interest abolished; blood feuds ended.
- Hold fast to the Qur'an.

## Worked example

**Question:** Explain two reasons why the Treaty of Hudaibiyah was important. **[4]**

**Model answer:** The truce ended fighting, so Muslims could travel and preach freely, and many tribes
accepted Islam in the next two years [2]. It also meant the Quraysh formally recognised the Muslims
as an equal party, which raised their standing across Arabia, and the Qur'an called it a "clear
victory" (Sura 48) [2].

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- **Mixing up Aqaba and Hudaibiyah.** Aqaba = pledges before the Hijrah; Hudaibiyah = treaty in 628.
- **Uhud as a defeat only.** Explain its lesson about obedience.
- **Forgetting honorifics.** Use (pbuh) for the Prophet and (RA) or (AS) as the specification does.
- **No link to lessons today.** 12-mark answers should connect events to Muslim life now.

## How is this topic examined?

The life and teachings of Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh) is examined in OxfordAQA 9237 Paper 2, Section A (25 marks). Each section has short questions worth 1, 2, 4 and 6 marks, building to an extended response worth 12 marks that is linked to a set passage. Learn the Arabic terms and their meanings for the short items, and for the 12-mark answer plan two or three developed points that quote or refer to the passage and reach a judgement.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who was the first person to accept Islam?**
Hazrat Khadija (RA).

**Why is 622 CE important?**
The Hijrah marks the start of the Islamic calendar.
