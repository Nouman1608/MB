The European option of Cambridge 9489 Paper 4 is a depth study of three dictatorships: Mussolini's Italy (1919-41), Stalin's Russia (1924-41) and Hitler's Germany (1929-41). Each topic is built on the same four key questions -- how the dictator gained control, how effectively he governed, how successful his economic policies were and how far he transformed society -- and you answer two 30-mark essays using the command words **assess** or **evaluate**.

Related: [European option study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-world-history-depth-study-european-interwar-study-guide/) and
[practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-world-history-depth-study-european-interwar-practice/).

## 1 Mussolini's Italy, 1919-41: key facts

| Date | Event | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| March 1919 | Fasci di Combattimento founded in Milan | Radical early programme; no seats in the 1919 election |
| Sept 1919 | D'Annunzio seizes Fiume | Exposes liberal weakness over the "mutilated victory" |
| 1919-20 | Biennio Rosso: strikes, land and factory occupations | Elites turn to squadrismo as protection against the left |
| Oct 1922 | March on Rome; Victor Emmanuel III refuses martial law | Appointment, not seizure: the "dual policy" of violence and legality |
| 1923 | Fascist Grand Council and MVSN militia; Acerbo Law (25% of the vote gives two-thirds of seats) | Party structures inside the state |
| June 1924 | Murder of Matteotti; Aventine Secession | The regime's greatest crisis |
| Jan 1925 | Mussolini accepts "responsibility" in a speech to the Chamber | Start of open dictatorship |
| Dec 1925-1926 | Law on the powers of the Head of Government; parties banned; Special Tribunal; OVRA | Dictatorship established |
| 1929 | Lateran Treaties with the Papacy | Major source of consent |
| 1935-36 | Invasion of Abyssinia | Peak popularity; League sanctions push Italy to autarky and Germany |
| 1938 | Racial laws | Imitation of Germany; strain with the Church |

**Economy (1.3):** De Stefani's orthodox policies (1922-25); Battle for the Lira and "Quota 90" (1926-27) overvalued the currency and hurt exports; Battle for Grain (1925) raised wheat output at the cost of other crops; Battle for the Marshes (Pontine Marshes); IMI (1931) and IRI (1933) gave the state a large industrial holding; Corporate State built through the Vidoni Pact (1925), Rocco Law (1926), Ministry of Corporations (1926), Charter of Labour (1927) and National Council of Corporations (1930). Real wages for many workers fell.

**Society (1.4):** Battle for Births (from 1927) with a bachelor tax and incentives, but the birth rate kept falling; Gentile reform (1923); ONB (1926) youth organisation; OND leisure organisation (1925); Reform of Customs (1938). Opposition included the underground Communist Party, Justice and Liberty (founded 1929) and assassination attempts in 1925-26.

## 2 Stalin's Russia, 1924-41: key facts

| Date | Event | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| April 1922 | Stalin becomes General Secretary | Control of appointments and party membership |
| Jan 1924 | Lenin dies; Testament suppressed by the Central Committee in May | Stalin survives Lenin's warning |
| 1924-25 | "Socialism in One Country" against Trotsky's "Permanent Revolution" | Patriotic, practical appeal to party members |
| 1925-27 | Trotsky, Zinoviev and Kamenev defeated; Trotsky expelled (1927), deported (1929) | Shifting alliances on the left |
| 1928-29 | Defeat of the Right (Bukharin, Rykov, Tomsky); "Great Turn" | End of NEP |
| 1928-32 | First Five Year Plan, Gosplan targets; collectivisation from late 1929 | Industrial growth; famine of 1932-33 |
| Dec 1934 | Kirov murdered | Pretext for terror |
| 1936-38 | Show trials (Zinoviev and Kamenev 1936; Bukharin 1938); Yezhovshchina; purge of the Red Army (Tukhachevsky, 1937) | Elimination of potential rivals; mass arrests |
| 1936 | Stalin Constitution; family law restricts abortion and divorce | Façade of democracy; "Great Retreat" |
| 1934-39 | League of Nations membership (1934); Franco-Soviet and Czech pacts (1935); aid to Spanish Republic | Collective security |
| Aug 1939 | Nazi-Soviet Pact | Security after collective security failed |

**Economy (2.3):** reasons for the Great Turn -- security, self-sufficiency, a fully socialist economy and Stalin's own control. Collectivisation: "Dizzy with Success" (March 1930) paused the drive; dekulakisation deported millions; livestock numbers collapsed. Industry: Magnitogorsk, the Dnieper dam, the White Sea Canal, the Moscow Metro; Stakhanov's record shift (August 1935) launched the Stakhanovite movement. Living standards: wage differentials, labour books (1938) and harsh discipline laws (1940); chronic housing shortage.

**Society (2.4):** the "Great Retreat" restored traditional family values and school discipline; women entered industry in large numbers but kept domestic burdens; Cultural Revolution (1928-31) gave way to Socialist Realism (1934 Writers' Congress); religion was repressed, though the 1937 census -- suppressed -- recorded a majority of respondents as believers.

## 3 Hitler's Germany, 1929-41: key facts

| Date | Event | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| March 1930 | Müller's coalition collapses; Brüning governs by Article 48 decree | Parliamentary government ends before Hitler |
| 1930-32 | Nazi Reichstag seats: 12 (1928), 107 (Sept 1930), 230 (July 1932), 196 (Nov 1932) | Peak in July 1932; decline by November |
| 30 Jan 1933 | Hitler appointed Chancellor by Hindenburg on Papen's advice | "Backstairs intrigue" by elites |
| 27-28 Feb 1933 | Reichstag Fire; decree suspending civil rights | Legal basis for terror |
| 23 March 1933 | Enabling Act | Legislation without the Reichstag |
| 30 June 1934 | Night of the Long Knives | SA crushed; army reassured |
| 2 Aug 1934 | Hindenburg dies; army oath to Hitler personally | Führer state complete |
| 1935-38 | Nuremberg Laws (1935); Rhineland (1936); Four Year Plan (1936); Anschluss and Kristallnacht (1938) | Racial state and expansion |
| 1939-41 | Prague (March 1939); Nazi-Soviet Pact; Poland; Barbarossa (22 June 1941) | War for Lebensraum |

**Economy (3.3):** Schacht, Mefo bills and the New Plan (1934); Law for the Reduction of Unemployment (1933); autobahn programme; Reich Labour Service; Reich Food Estate and Reich Entailed Farm Law (1933); Göring's Four Year Plan (1936) for autarky and war readiness; the "guns or butter" debate. Registered unemployment fell from about six million in early 1933 to under half a million by 1938, though hidden factors (women, conscription, RAD) flatter the figures. The DAF replaced trade unions in May 1933; KdF and Beauty of Labour offered leisure in place of wage rises.

**Society (3.4):** *Kinder, Küche, Kirche*; marriage loans (1933) and the Mother's Cross (1938); women recalled to work from 1937 as labour ran short. Hitler Youth and BDM, made compulsory by law in 1936 and 1939. Concordat (1933), Reich Church under Bishop Müller, German Faith Movement; Confessing Church resistance; Galen's 1941 sermon against euthanasia. Persecution: April 1933 boycott, Nuremberg Laws, Kristallnacht; sterilisation law (1933) and persecution of Roma, homosexuals, Jehovah's Witnesses and disabled people.

## Comparison grid for synoptic judgement

| Criterion | Italy | USSR | Germany |
|---|---|---|---|
| Route to power | Appointment after violence and elite support | Party bureaucracy and factional manoeuvre | Appointment after electoral rise and elite intrigue |
| Terror | Limited (OVRA, *confino*) | Mass (NKVD, Gulag, 1937-38) | Extensive and racial (SS, Gestapo, camps) |
| Economic model | Corporatism, then autarky | Command economy | Private industry directed for rearmament |
| Social transformation | Shallow; Church compromise | Deep upheaval, partial retreat after 1934 | Significant for youth and minorities; limited for women |

## Common mistakes / exam traps

- **Narrative without criteria.** The Paper 4 AO2 grid rewards answers that "establish valid and wide-ranging criteria"; define what "successful" or "effective" means before judging.
- **Treating propaganda claims as results.** Battle for Grain and Five Year Plan targets need real outcomes and costs.
- **Mixing regimes.** Answers are on one regime unless the question says otherwise; comparisons should sharpen the judgement, not replace it.
- **Ignoring the date range.** A 1929-41 question on Hitler does not reward long sections on 1923.
- **Assertion in the conclusion.** A judgement must be argued throughout, not added at the end.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many questions do I answer on Paper 4?**
Two 30-mark essays from your one chosen option, in 1 hour 45 minutes. Each is marked out of 15 for AO1 knowledge and 15 for AO2 analysis.

**Do I need all three dictatorships?**
The syllabus prescribes no rotation for Paper 4, so revise all three topics to guarantee a choice.

**Which dates matter most?**
Those that mark turning points: 1922, 1924-26 and 1929 for Italy; 1924, 1928-29 and 1934-38 for the USSR; 1930, 1933 and 1934 for Germany.

## Self-test

1. What did the Acerbo Law do? *Gave two-thirds of seats to the party with the most votes, if it had at least 25%.*
2. Why was the post of General Secretary important to Stalin? *It controlled party appointments and membership.*
3. What was the purpose of Mefo bills? *To fund rearmament secretly without visible state debt.*
4. Name two parts of the Corporate State's creation. *Rocco Law (1926), Charter of Labour (1927).*
5. What was the "Great Retreat"? *The mid-1930s return to traditional values in family and education policy in the USSR.*
