## 4.12 Air pollution: sulfur dioxide and carbon monoxide

### Sulfur dioxide

**Source:** burning fossil fuels that contain sulfur, such as coal in power stations.

**Biological consequences:**

1. Sulfur dioxide dissolves in rain water to form an acidic solution, called **acid rain**.
2. Acid rain damages the leaves of trees, which reduces photosynthesis, and it harms the roots, so the trees grow poorly or die.
3. When acid rain reaches lakes and rivers, the water becomes more acidic. Many fish and other aquatic organisms cannot survive in low pH water, so the **biodiversity** of the lake falls.
4. Sulfur dioxide gas irritates the lungs of animals and humans and can make breathing problems such as asthma worse.

### Carbon monoxide

**Source:** incomplete combustion of carbon-containing fuels, for example petrol in car engines when the oxygen supply is poor, and faulty gas heaters.

**Biological consequences:**

1. Carbon monoxide is a poisonous gas that combines with **haemoglobin** in red blood cells.
2. This combination stops haemoglobin carrying as much **oxygen**, so less oxygen reaches the tissues.
3. Cells cannot respire aerobically at the normal rate, causing headaches, tiredness and, at high concentrations, unconsciousness and death.

## 4.13 and 4.14 Greenhouse gases and human activity

The five greenhouse gases named in the specification are:

| Gas | Main human sources |
|---|---|
| **Water vapour** | Evaporation from oceans, with more in a warmer atmosphere |
| **Carbon dioxide** | Burning fossil fuels, and deforestation (burning or decay of cleared trees) |
| **Nitrous oxide** | Fertilisers and some industrial processes |
| **Methane** | Cattle and rice paddies (from microorganisms), landfill waste |
| **CFCs** | Old refrigerators, aerosols and foam, now banned or limited in many countries |

Human activities that increase greenhouse gases include burning fossil fuels, clearing forests, intensive cattle farming, rice growing, using fertilisers and landfill. Population growth increases the demand for each of these.

## 4.15 The enhanced greenhouse effect and global warming

### The natural greenhouse effect

The Sun's radiation passes through the atmosphere and warms the Earth's surface. The surface gives out heat as infrared radiation. Greenhouse gases in the atmosphere **absorb** some of this heat and re-radiate it, so some returns to the surface. This keeps the Earth warm enough for life. Without it, the average temperature would be far lower.

### The enhanced greenhouse effect

Human activities have increased the concentrations of greenhouse gases. More heat is trapped, so the average temperature of the Earth rises. This is **global warming**.

### Possible consequences

- Polar ice and glaciers melt, and the oceans expand as they warm, so **sea level rises** and low-lying coastal land floods.
- Climate patterns change, with more extreme weather, droughts and floods in some regions.
- Changes in temperature and rainfall alter **habitats**, so the distribution of species changes. Species that cannot move or adapt may become extinct.
- Crop yields may fall in some regions and rise in others.

### Worked example 1: percentage change

*The carbon dioxide concentration in the atmosphere rose from 280 ppm before industrial activity to 420 ppm today. Calculate the percentage increase.*

```
increase = 420 − 280 = 140 ppm
percentage increase = 140 ÷ 280 × 100 = 50%
```

The concentration is 50% higher than before. Divide by the original value (280), not the new value.

### Worked example 2: link activity to gas

*Explain why intensive cattle farming increases the greenhouse effect.*

1. Cattle produce **methane** in their digestive systems.
2. Forests may be cleared for grazing, which removes trees that take up carbon dioxide and adds carbon dioxide when the wood is burned or decays.
3. Methane and carbon dioxide are greenhouse gases, so more of them in the air trap more heat.

## 4.16 Water pollution by sewage

Untreated sewage that enters a river contains organic matter, nutrients such as nitrates and phosphates, and microorganisms.

### The sequence of events

1. Bacteria (decomposers) feed on the organic matter in the sewage and **multiply** quickly.
2. The bacteria use large amounts of **oxygen** in aerobic respiration.
3. The oxygen concentration in the water **falls**.
4. Organisms that need a lot of oxygen, such as fish and many invertebrates, **die** or move away.
5. Organisms that tolerate low oxygen (such as rat-tailed maggots and sludge worms) increase.
6. Sewage may also carry **pathogens**, so it can spread diseases such as cholera and typhoid to people who drink the water.

The overall result is a fall in biodiversity.

### Worked example 3: reading oxygen data

*A river's dissolved oxygen is 9.0 mg per dm³ upstream of a sewage outlet and 2.0 mg per dm³ 500 m downstream. Describe and explain the change.*

```
fall = 9.0 − 2.0 = 7.0 mg per dm³
percentage fall = 7.0 ÷ 9.0 × 100 = 77.8%
```

The oxygen concentration falls by about 78%. Bacteria feeding on the sewage use up the oxygen, so fish and other animals that need oxygen cannot survive downstream.

## 4.17 Eutrophication

**Eutrophication** happens when excess nutrients enter water bodies. Fertilisers contain nitrates and phosphates. Rain washes the minerals out of the soil, called **leaching**, into streams and then into lakes.

### The chain of events

1. Extra nitrates and phosphates make algae grow rapidly, forming an **algal bloom** on the surface.
2. The bloom blocks **light** from reaching plants below, so submerged plants cannot photosynthesise and die.
3. The algae also die as their nutrients run out.
4. **Decomposers** (bacteria) feed on the dead plants and algae and multiply.
5. The bacteria use up the **oxygen** in the water by aerobic respiration.
6. Fish and other organisms die from lack of oxygen, and biodiversity falls.

Do not say the algae "use up the oxygen" as the main cause. The key point is that the **decomposers feeding on the dead material** use up the oxygen.

### Preventing eutrophication

- Use less fertiliser, or apply it at the right time (not before heavy rain).
- Leave strips of uncultivated land beside streams to absorb minerals.
- Treat sewage before it is released into water.

## 4.18B Deforestation (Paper 2 only)

**Deforestation** is the large-scale removal of trees. Reasons include timber, farmland, grazing and building.

| Effect | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Leaching** | Tree roots hold minerals. Without trees, rain washes the minerals out of the soil, so the soil becomes less fertile |
| **Soil erosion** | Roots hold soil together and leaves reduce the force of rain. Without them, soil is washed into rivers, which can cause flooding and blocked rivers |
| **Disturbance of evapotranspiration** | Trees take up water and release water vapour through their leaves. With fewer trees, less water vapour goes into the air, so there is less cloud and rainfall, and the climate becomes drier |
| **Disturbance of the carbon cycle** | Fewer trees means less photosynthesis and less carbon dioxide removed. Burning or decay of the removed trees also adds carbon dioxide |
| **Balance of atmospheric gases** | Carbon dioxide concentration rises and oxygen release falls, adding to global warming |

Deforestation also reduces biodiversity because the habitat of many species is destroyed.

### Worked example 4

*A hillside is cleared of forest and planted with a short-term crop. After heavy rain, a nearby river becomes brown and a village downstream floods. Explain.*

1. With the trees gone, there are no roots to hold the soil.
2. Rain washes the loose soil down the hill (**soil erosion**).
3. The soil enters the river, making it brown and raising the river bed, so the river overflows more easily and causes flooding.

## Common mistakes

- Saying carbon monoxide "blocks the lungs". It combines with haemoglobin and reduces oxygen transport.
- Saying acid rain is caused by carbon dioxide. The cause is sulfur dioxide.
- Mixing up the greenhouse effect with the ozone layer. CFCs are on the greenhouse list, but ozone damage is not required by this specification.
- Saying the greenhouse effect is bad in itself. The natural effect keeps the Earth warm. The problem is the enhanced effect.
- Saying eutrophication is caused directly by algae using oxygen. The oxygen is used by decomposers.
- Leaving out "leaching" in a fertiliser answer, or confusing it with erosion.
- Giving "global warming" as the cause of the hole in the ozone layer.
- Using "pollution" without saying which substance and what it does to living things.

## Self-check

1. Name the gas that causes acid rain.
2. Explain how carbon monoxide affects the blood.
3. Name two greenhouse gases that come from farming.
4. In eutrophication, why do the submerged plants die?
5. (Paper 2 only) Give two effects of deforestation on the soil.

**Answers:** (1) Sulfur dioxide. (2) It combines with haemoglobin, so less oxygen is carried. (3) Methane and nitrous oxide (carbon dioxide from deforestation is also accepted). (4) The algal bloom blocks light, so they cannot photosynthesise. (5) Leaching of minerals and soil erosion.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between the greenhouse effect and global warming?**
The greenhouse effect is the natural trapping of heat that keeps the Earth warm. Global warming is the rise in temperature caused by the enhanced greenhouse effect.

**Why does sewage reduce the oxygen in a river?**
Bacteria feeding on the sewage multiply and use up the dissolved oxygen in respiration.

**Is deforestation examined on both papers?**
No. Point 4.18B is bold with a B reference, so it is Paper 2 only.
