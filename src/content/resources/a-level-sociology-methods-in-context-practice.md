## Section A

**1.** Using material from the item below and your knowledge of research methods, evaluate the strengths and limitations of using **structured interviews** to investigate **teachers' expectations of different pupils**.

*Item: Researchers investigating teacher expectations must consider that teachers are busy professionals with limited time, that classroom observation may not always be possible, and that teachers may be reluctant to admit to differential treatment of pupils.* **[20]**

---

## Answer (worked, showing the methods-in-context approach)

A strong response weaves method and context together throughout, rather than treating them as
two separate lists. It should draw on points such as:

**Practical fit (method + context):** structured interviews are relatively quick to administer,
which suits teachers' limited free time referenced in the item — but a fixed set of questions may
not capture the nuance of how expectations actually form in the classroom, which is a limitation
specific to researching a subtle, situational attitude like teacher expectation. **[practical, applied]**

**Access and status:** researchers need to gain access via school gatekeepers, and teachers —
being professionals used to being observed and evaluated — may give socially desirable answers
in a structured interview rather than admitting to differential treatment, directly reflecting the
item's point about reluctance to admit bias. **[ethical/theoretical, applied]**

**Validity:** structured interviews' fixed questions may not allow a teacher to explain the
complex, often unconscious reasoning behind differing expectations, reducing validity for this
specific topic — an unstructured or semi-structured approach might build more rapport and
elicit more honest reflection, but at a cost of comparability and time (both practical issues
raised by the item). **[theoretical, applied]**

**Reliability:** the standardised nature of structured interviews does support reliability
(another researcher could repeat the same questions with the same teachers), which is a genuine
strength specific to this method, even though it doesn't resolve the validity concern above.

A response scoring in the top band explains **at least three or four** such combined points in
depth, rather than covering many points shallowly, and reaches an overall **judgement** about
whether structured interviews are, on balance, well suited to this specific topic.

---

## Where marks are usually lost

- Discussing structured interviews' strengths and weaknesses in general, without reference to teachers or expectations specifically.
- Discussing the sensitivity of the topic without linking it to a specific feature of the named method.
- Ignoring the item — the item is there to be used, and answers that don't engage with its specific points miss marks for application.
- No overall judgement at the end weighing whether the method suits the topic on balance.
