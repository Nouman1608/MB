## Section B

**6.** A student investigated whether light is needed for photosynthesis. A destarched potted plant had one leaf partly covered with black paper. The plant was left in bright light for 24 hours.

**(a)** State why the plant was destarched before the experiment. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe how the leaf should be tested for starch. **[4]**

**(c)** State the result expected for the covered part and the uncovered part. **[2]**

**(d)** State what the uncovered part acts as in this experiment. **[1]**

**7.** The table shows the rate of photosynthesis, in arbitrary units, of a water plant at two concentrations of carbon dioxide at different light intensities. The temperature was kept constant.

| Light intensity (units) | Rate at 0.04% carbon dioxide | Rate at 0.10% carbon dioxide |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 1 | 8 | 8 |
| 2 | 16 | 16 |
| 3 | 22 | 24 |
| 4 | 24 | 31 |
| 5 | 24 | 36 |

**(a)** State the limiting factor for the 0.04% curve between light intensity 0 and 2. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the rate at 0.04% carbon dioxide does not increase between light intensity 4 and 5. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the percentage increase in the rate at light intensity 5 when the carbon dioxide is raised from 0.04% to 0.10%. **[2]**

**(d)** Suggest **one** other factor, apart from carbon dioxide and light, that could limit the rate at light intensity 5. **[1]**

**8.** In an experiment to measure the rate of photosynthesis, a student counted bubbles from *Elodea* in one minute. Three counts were 41, 44 and 38 bubbles.

**(a)** Calculate the mean number of bubbles per minute. **[1]**

**(b)** State **two** variables that must be kept constant if the student then changes the distance of the lamp. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the rate falls when the lamp is moved further away. **[2]**

**9.** Hydrogencarbonate indicator solution is orange at atmospheric carbon dioxide levels. A tube containing a water plant and indicator is placed in bright light and another is wrapped in foil.

**(a)** State the colour of the indicator after some hours in the bright light, and explain it. **[3]**

**(b)** State the colour of the indicator in the foil-wrapped tube, and explain it. **[3]**

**10.** Balance the equation: CO2 + H2O → C6H12O6 + O2. **[2]**

**11.** Suggest why growers who raise tomatoes in a greenhouse may burn fuel to release extra carbon dioxide inside it. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** D [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Carbon dioxide + water [1] gives glucose + oxygen, in light and with chlorophyll [1]. **[2]**

**4.** It absorbs light energy [1] and transfers it into chemical energy for making glucose and other carbohydrates [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Glucose is used in respiration to release energy [1]. Starch is made as an energy store [1]. Cellulose is made to build cell walls [1]. Sucrose is made for transport through the plant [1]. **[4]**

**6.**

**(a)** So that any starch found at the end was made during the experiment, not before [1].

**(b)** Boil the leaf in water to kill it and soften it [1]. Heat it in hot ethanol, in a water bath, to remove chlorophyll [1]. Rinse in water [1]. Add iodine solution [1].

**(c)** Covered part: orange-brown (no starch) [1]. Uncovered part: blue-black (starch present) [1].

**(d)** The control [1]. **[8]**

**7.**

**(a)** Light intensity [1].

**(b)** Light is no longer limiting [1]. Another factor, in this case carbon dioxide concentration (because a higher concentration raised the rate), is limiting [1].

**(c)** Increase = 36 - 24 = 12 [1]. Percentage = 12 divided by 24 x 100 = 50 per cent [1].

**(d)** Temperature [1]. **[6]**

**8.**

**(a)** (41 + 44 + 38) divided by 3 = 123 divided by 3 = 41 bubbles per minute [1].

**(b)** Any two of: temperature; carbon dioxide concentration (amount of hydrogencarbonate); the same plant or the same length of plant; time of counting [1 each].

**(c)** Light intensity decreases as the distance increases [1], so less light energy is absorbed by chlorophyll and less glucose is made, giving a lower rate [1]. **[5]**

**9.**

**(a)** Purple [1]. The plant photosynthesises faster than it respires [1], so it takes in carbon dioxide and the level falls [1].

**(b)** Yellow [1]. In the dark there is no photosynthesis [1], and respiration releases carbon dioxide, so the level rises [1]. **[6]**

**10.** 6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2. One mark for 6CO2 and 6H2O [1], one mark for 6O2 [1]. **[2]**

**11.** Carbon dioxide can be the limiting factor for photosynthesis [1], so increasing its concentration raises the rate, giving more glucose for growth and a higher crop yield [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "food" in place of glucose or carbohydrate.
- Missing out the removal of chlorophyll with ethanol, or heating ethanol with a flame.
- Calling the covered part of the leaf the control.
- Saying that light is limiting on a plateau.
- Using the end value, not the start value, as the divisor in a percentage change.
