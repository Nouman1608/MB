Cambridge O Level World History (2147) is not tiered and is assessed by two compulsory, externally assessed papers, with no coursework option. Paper 1 Structured Questions lasts 2 hours, carries 60 marks and is worth 55%. Paper 2 Document Questions lasts 1 hour 45 minutes, carries 40 marks and is worth 45%. The split is deliberately uneven, so Paper 1 deserves a little more revision time. Content pages that support this plan include [the First World War depth study](/resources/o-level-world-history-depth-study-first-world-war/), [the depth studies overview](/resources/o-level-world-history-depth-studies/) and [overseas empires in the nineteenth century](/resources/cambridge-world-history-overseas-empires-nineteenth-century/).

## How the syllabus content is organised

The syllabus has Core content with two options, Option A (the nineteenth century: the development of modern nation states, 1848 to 1914) and Option B (the twentieth century: international relations since 1919), together with Depth Studies. Check with your teacher which option and which depth study your class is following, then concentrate your preparation on those. Do not assume that content from the option you are not studying will be examined.

## Paper 1: Structured Questions

According to the verified record, Paper 1 has two Core-content questions plus one Depth-study question. It lasts 2 hours for 60 marks.

Structured questions usually have parts of increasing demand: a short narrative or description, an explanation, and a longer judgement. To prepare, build a one-page summary for each key development in your option and for each depth study, covering causes, key events, consequences and significance. For explanation parts, practise the pattern of point, evidence, link to the question. For judgement parts, plan a short argument with a clear line of reasoning, balanced by at least one counter-argument, and end with a conclusion that actually answers the question. Dates and names matter, but their use in an argument matters more than recital.

The [US Civil War and Reconstruction](/resources/cambridge-world-history-us-civil-war-1820-1877/) and [US containment of communism](/resources/cambridge-world-history-us-containment-korea-cuba-vietnam/) pages are examples of the kind of detailed topic revision that supports these questions, provided they match the content your class studies.

## Paper 2: Document Questions

Paper 2 is 1 hour 45 minutes for 40 marks and carries 45%. It is built around documents, meaning written and visual sources. The record gives the title, the duration and the marks, so check the specification for the exact format of the question types.

General source skills apply. For each source, note its provenance: who produced it, when, for whom and for what purpose. Distinguish what the source says from what you can infer from it. When comparing sources, look for both similarities and differences, and test them against your own knowledge. When judging usefulness or reliability, remember that a biased source can still be useful if you explain what it reveals about attitudes or the situation at the time. Always support a claim with a short quotation or a precise detail from the source.

## Timing and marks per minute

- Paper 1: 120 / 60 = 2.0 minutes per mark. A 12-mark part therefore deserves about 24 minutes.
- Paper 2: 105 / 40 = 2.625 minutes per mark.

The weightings 55% + 45% = 100%. Although Paper 1 carries more weight, Paper 2 is the more generous in time per mark because source analysis requires reading, thinking and writing. Use the extra time to read each source slowly, not to write longer answers with repeated points.

## Command words and question styles

Describe asks for features or events. Explain why asks for causes with reasons, not a list. How far or to what extent asks for a judgement that weighs factors. What can you learn from asks for inference. How reliable or how useful asks for evaluation of the source in context. Compare asks for similarities and differences. Make sure your answer uses the verb in the question, because a narrative where an explanation is wanted will be capped.

## Worked practice scenario

Question (original): Explain why trench warfare produced stalemate on the Western Front.

**Plan.** Choose two or three causes, give evidence for each and link back to stalemate.

**Model answer.**

One reason was that defensive weapons were stronger than offensive ones. Machine guns could cut down infantry crossing open ground, and barbed wire slowed attackers so that they were exposed for longer. This meant that even a heavily supported advance often failed to break the enemy line.

A second reason was the depth and organisation of the defences. Trenches were built in lines, with support and reserve trenches behind the front, so that if the first line was captured, a further line stood behind it. This meant that an attacker who gained a few hundred metres had to start again against fresh defenders.

A third reason was that defenders could move reinforcements more quickly than attackers could advance. Railways brought men and supplies to the threatened area, whereas an attacking army had to cross shell-torn ground, which made supplying advanced troops slow and difficult.

Overall, stalemate resulted because the defence combined strong weapons, layered positions and faster reinforcement, which together outweighed the artillery bombardments and infantry assaults used against it.

Notice that every paragraph states a cause, supplies detail and explains the link to stalemate in its last sentence.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Confirm which Core option and which depth study you are studying.
- Build summaries of causes, events, consequences and significance for each unit.
- Practise one timed Paper 1 and one timed Paper 2 style task each week.
- Learn source-evaluation phrases: provenance, purpose, tone, reliability and usefulness.
- In Paper 1, read all questions before starting and plan each long answer.
- In Paper 2, annotate each source before answering.
- Use quotations and precise details to support claims.
- Finish each long answer with a conclusion that answers the question.
