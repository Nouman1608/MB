Multicellular organisms keep their internal conditions within narrow limits by detecting changes and coordinating responses, using chemical and electrical signals. For OCR A Level Biology A (H420) section 5.1.1 you must explain why communication systems are needed, describe signalling between adjacent and distant cells, explain the principles of homeostasis (receptors and effectors, negative and positive feedback), and describe the physiological and behavioural responses used to control body temperature in endotherms and ectotherms.

## Why communication systems are needed (a)

In a multicellular organism cells are specialised and most are far from the surface, so they cannot sense the outside world or supply all their own needs. The organism must:

- **respond to changes in the external environment**, such as a fall in air temperature, the approach of a predator, or, for a plant, the direction of light;
- **respond to changes in the internal environment**, such as a rise in blood glucose after a meal or a build-up of carbon dioxide during exercise, because enzymes and cells work only within narrow ranges of temperature, pH and water potential;
- **coordinate the activities of different organs**, so that, for example, breathing rate, heart rate and blood flow to muscles all rise together during exercise.

Plants need communication too: they respond to light, gravity and water availability, and coordinate growth in different parts.

## Cell signalling (b)

Cell signalling is the process by which one cell releases a chemical that is detected by another cell, which responds. The responding cell must have a **receptor** with a shape complementary to the signal molecule, so only target cells respond.

| | Between adjacent cells | Between distant cells |
|---|---|---|
| Example | A neurone releases neurotransmitter across a synapse to the next neurone or a muscle cell | An endocrine gland secretes a hormone into the blood, which carries it round the body |
| Distance | Very short (across a narrow gap) | Throughout the body |
| Speed | Very fast | Slower, limited by blood flow |
| Range of cells affected | Only the cell on the other side of the synapse | All cells with the right receptor |

Nervous signals travel along neurones electrically and cross synapses chemically; hormones are entirely chemical. Both rely on specific receptors on the target cell.

## Principles of homeostasis (c)

**Homeostasis** is the maintenance of a constant internal environment, within narrow limits, despite changes inside or outside the body. Each controlled factor has an optimum value, the **set point**.

A control system has:

1. **receptors**, which detect a change (a stimulus) from the set point;
2. a **communication system** (nerves or hormones) that carries information to a coordinator, such as the hypothalamus, and on to effectors;
3. **effectors**, muscles or glands, which bring about a response.

### Negative and positive feedback

| | Negative feedback | Positive feedback |
|---|---|---|
| Effect of the response | Reverses the change, returning the factor towards the set point | Increases the change, moving the factor further from its starting value |
| Result | Stability; the factor oscillates within narrow limits | A rapid, large change, which must be switched off afterwards |
| Examples | Control of body temperature; control of blood glucose | Platelets releasing chemicals that attract more platelets in blood clotting; oxytocin increasing uterine contractions during birth |

Negative feedback is the basis of homeostasis. Having separate mechanisms that raise and that lower a factor gives more precise control than one mechanism alone. Positive feedback is useful only when a fast, one-off change is needed; if it happens by accident, for example when a very low body temperature slows metabolism and reduces heat production further, it can be dangerous.

## Temperature control (d)

**Endotherms** (mammals and birds) use heat from metabolism to keep a fairly constant core temperature. **Ectotherms** (such as reptiles, amphibians and most fish) rely largely on heat from their surroundings, so their body temperature varies with the environment.

### Endotherms

- **Peripheral temperature receptors** in the skin detect changes in skin temperature, giving early warning of external changes.
- **Temperature receptors in the hypothalamus** detect changes in the temperature of the blood, which shows the core temperature.
- The **hypothalamus** coordinates the response, sending nerve impulses to effectors in the skin and muscles.

| Effector | Too hot | Too cold |
|---|---|---|
| Arterioles supplying skin capillaries | Smooth muscle relaxes: vasodilation, more blood near the surface, more heat lost by radiation | Smooth muscle contracts: vasoconstriction, less blood near the surface, less heat lost |
| Sweat glands | Secrete more sweat; evaporation transfers heat away from the skin | Secrete less sweat |
| Hair erector muscles | Relax; hairs lie flat; less insulating air trapped | Contract; hairs stand up, trapping a layer of insulating air |
| Skeletal muscles | No shivering | Shivering: rapid contractions release heat from respiration |
| Metabolic rate | Reduced | Increased, releasing more heat |

**Behavioural responses** in endotherms add to these: moving into shade, lying still or spreading out in the heat; huddling, curling up, moving into the sun, or seeking shelter in the cold. Humans add or remove clothing.

### Ectotherms

The specification asks for behavioural responses in ectotherms. Examples:

- **basking** in the sun, and orienting the body so that a large surface faces the sun's rays to warm up, or only a small surface to cool down;
- pressing the body against warm rocks or ground (conduction);
- retreating into shade, burrows or water when too hot;
- being active at cooler or warmer times of day as needed.

Ectotherms need much less food than endotherms of similar size, because little of their energy is used to generate heat, but they are less active when it is cold.

### Annotated model answer

**Question.** Describe how a mammal responds to a fall in the temperature of its surroundings. [6]

> Peripheral temperature receptors in the skin detect the fall **(receptor named)**, and impulses pass to the hypothalamus **(coordinator)**. The hypothalamus sends impulses to effectors **(communication)**. Arterioles leading to skin capillaries constrict, so less blood flows near the surface and less heat is lost by radiation **(effector, response and effect)**. Skeletal muscles shiver, releasing heat from respiration; hair erector muscles contract, trapping a layer of insulating air **(two further effectors)**. The animal may also curl up or move to shelter **(behavioural response)**. These responses reverse the change, so this is negative feedback **(principle named)**.

Each bracket is a separate marking point. Notice that the answer says arterioles **constrict**; it does not say capillaries move away from the skin.

## Practical link (PAG11)

Responses can be measured, for example by recording skin temperature with a digital thermometer or data logger before and after a hand is placed briefly in cool water, or after exercise. Volunteers should give consent and be able to stop at any time, and conditions such as room temperature should be controlled.

## Common mistakes

- Saying blood vessels or capillaries "move" nearer to or further from the skin.
- Saying sweat "cools the body" without the evaporation of water removing heat.
- Calling the hypothalamus a receptor only; it contains receptors and also coordinates the response.
- Confusing positive feedback with "a good response".
- Saying ectotherms cannot control their temperature at all; they control it by behaviour.
- Defining homeostasis as "keeping conditions constant" without "within narrow limits".

## Exam technique

- **Describe** a feedback loop in order: stimulus, receptor, coordinator, effector, response, return to set point.
- **Explain** a response: link the effector to heat gain or loss (radiation, evaporation, insulation, respiration).
- **Compare** endotherms and ectotherms: give the source of heat and the type of response for each.
- **Distinguish** negative and positive feedback: state the direction of change and give one example of each.
