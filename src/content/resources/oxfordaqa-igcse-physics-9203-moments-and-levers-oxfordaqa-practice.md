## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State what is meant by the moment of a force. **[1]**

**2.** A mechanic applies a force of 85 N at right angles to a wrench, 0.32 m from the bolt. Calculate the moment. **[2]**

**3.** A moment of 18 N m is needed to open a valve. The handle is 0.24 m long and the force is applied at right angles at its end. Calculate the force needed. **[2]**

**4.** A force of 40 N gives a moment of 14 N m about a hinge. Calculate the perpendicular distance from the hinge to the line of action of the force. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why door handles are placed on the opposite side of the door from the hinges. **[2]**

**6.** State the condition for a beam not to turn, in terms of moments. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A seesaw is pivoted at its centre. Child X (350 N) sits 1.6 m to the left of the pivot. Child Y (250 N) sits 0.80 m to the left. Child Z sits 2.0 m to the right.

**(a)** Calculate the total anticlockwise moment. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the weight of child Z if the seesaw balances. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the upward force on the seesaw from the pivot. Ignore the weight of the seesaw. **[1]**

**8.** A uniform metre rule weighs 1.5 N. It is pivoted at the 30 cm mark. A weight W is hung at the 5 cm mark so that the rule balances.

**(a)** State the distance of the rule's centre of mass from the pivot. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate W. **[3]**

**9.** A uniform plank 5.0 m long, of weight 200 N, rests on a single support 2.0 m from its left end. A painter of weight 700 N stands on the plank to the left of the support.

**(a)** Calculate the moment of the plank's weight about the support, and state whether it is clockwise or anticlockwise. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate how far from the support the painter must stand for the plank to balance. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain what happens if the painter walks further to the left. **[2]**

**10.** A crowbar is used to lift the edge of a heavy stone. The pivot is 0.10 m from the stone end. The worker pushes down 1.4 m from the pivot with a force of 150 N.

**(a)** Calculate the largest force the crowbar can exert on the stone. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the crowbar is called a force multiplier. **[2]**

**(c)** The stone end rises by 2.0 cm. Calculate how far the worker's hand moves down. **[2]**

**11.** A delivery lorry carries a tall load.

**(a)** Explain, in terms of moments, why the lorry may topple when going round a sharp bend. **[3]**

**(b)** Suggest **two** ways of reducing the risk. **[2]**

**12.** A tall bookcase weighs 900 N and is 0.50 m deep, with its centre of mass at its middle. A child pulls horizontally on the top shelf, 1.8 m above the floor, towards the front.

**(a)** State the pivot about which the bookcase would tip. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the moment of the bookcase's weight about this pivot. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the smallest pull that would start to tip the bookcase. **[2]**

**(d)** Suggest why bookcases are often fixed to the wall. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** The turning effect of a force about a pivot [1]. **[1]**

**2.** M = F × d = 85 × 0.32 [1] = **27.2 N m** [1]. **[2]**

**3.** F = M ÷ d = 18 ÷ 0.24 [1] = **75 N** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** d = M ÷ F = 14 ÷ 40 [1] = **0.35 m** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** The handle is as far from the hinges as possible [1], so a given force produces a larger moment, and a smaller force is needed to open the door [1]. **[2]**

**6.** The total clockwise moment equals the total anticlockwise moment about the pivot [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** 350 × 1.6 + 250 × 0.80 [1] = 560 + 200 = **760 N m** [1].

**(b)** Z × 2.0 = 760 [1]; Z = **380 N** [1].

**(c)** 350 + 250 + 380 = **980 N** [1]. **[5]**

**8. (a)** 50 − 30 = **20 cm** (0.20 m) [1].

**(b)** Clockwise moment of the rule = 1.5 × 0.20 = 0.30 N m [1]; anticlockwise moment of W = W × 0.25 [1]; W = 0.30 ÷ 0.25 = **1.2 N** [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** The plank's centre of mass is 2.5 m from the left end, so 0.50 m to the right of the support [1]; moment = 200 × 0.50 = **100 N m clockwise** [1].

**(b)** 700 × d = 100 [1]; d = **0.14 m** (0.143 m) to the left of the support [1].

**(c)** The painter's anticlockwise moment becomes larger than the plank's clockwise moment [1], so there is a resultant moment and the plank tips down on the left [1]. **[6]**

**10. (a)** 150 × 1.4 = F × 0.10 [1]; F = 210 ÷ 0.10 = **2100 N** [1].

**(b)** The effort (150 N) is much smaller than the force on the stone (2100 N) [1] because the effort is applied much further from the pivot than the load [1].

**(c)** The hand is 14 times as far from the pivot [1], so it moves 14 × 2.0 = **28 cm** [1]. **[6]**

**11. (a)** The tall load raises the lorry's centre of mass [1]. On the bend the lorry tilts, and the line of action of its weight can move outside its wheels (its base) [1]. The weight then has a resultant moment about the outer wheels that turns the lorry over [1].

**(b)** Any two [2]: carry the load lower; spread the load lower or more evenly; use a vehicle with a wider wheelbase; drive more slowly round bends. **[5]**

**12. (a)** The front bottom edge of the bookcase [1].

**(b)** The weight acts 0.25 m behind the front edge [1]; moment = 900 × 0.25 = **225 N m** [1].

**(c)** F × 1.8 = 225 [1]; F = **125 N** [1].

**(d)** Fixing provides an extra force (and moment) from the wall that stops the bookcase tipping forward when pulled [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* in (b) many candidates use the full depth of 0.50 m; the weight acts at the centre of mass, halfway back.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Measuring distances from the end of the beam rather than from the pivot.
- Forgetting the beam's own weight when the pivot is not at its centre.
- Using N or N/m as the unit of moment.
- Toppling answers that never mention the line of action of the weight.
