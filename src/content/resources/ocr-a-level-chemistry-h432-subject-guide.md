OCR A Level Chemistry A (H432) covers the core of A Level chemistry across physical,
inorganic and organic chemistry, with analytical techniques and practical skills
running through all of it. The qualification is linear, first assessed in 2017, and is
examined by three written papers at the end of the course. Paper 1 (Periodic table,
elements and physical chemistry) and Paper 2 (Synthesis and analytical techniques) each
carry 100 marks over 2 hours 15 minutes and are worth 37%. Paper 3 (Unified chemistry)
carries 70 marks over 1 hour 30 minutes and is worth 26%. The 270 marks across the
three papers decide the A* to E grade, while the Practical Endorsement is reported
separately.

## How the course is organised

H432 is set out in six modules.

**Module 1: Development of practical skills in chemistry.** Planning, implementing,
analysis and evaluation, together with practical skills and the use of apparatus and
techniques. These skills are developed throughout the course rather than taught as a
standalone unit.

**Module 2: Foundations in chemistry.** Atomic structure and isotopes; compounds,
formulae and equations; amount of substance; acids; redox; electron structure; and
bonding and structure. Much of the quantitative work in the rest of the course depends
on this module, especially amount of substance.

**Module 3: Periodic table and energy.** Periodicity, Group 2, the halogens and
qualitative analysis, then enthalpy changes, reaction rates and chemical equilibrium.

**Module 4: Core organic chemistry.** Basic concepts of organic chemistry, alkanes,
alkenes, alcohols, haloalkanes, organic synthesis and analytical techniques.

**Module 5: Physical chemistry and transition elements.** Rates ("How fast?") and
equilibrium ("How far?") in more depth, acids, bases and buffers, lattice enthalpy,
enthalpy and entropy, redox and electrode potentials, transition elements and further
qualitative analysis.

**Module 6: Organic chemistry and analysis.** Aromatic compounds, carbonyl compounds,
carboxylic acids and esters, amines, amino acids, amides and chirality, polyesters and
polyamides, carbon–carbon bond formation, organic synthesis, chromatography and
qualitative analysis, and spectroscopy.

Modules 5 and 6 deliberately revisit themes from Modules 3 and 4 at greater depth:
Module 5 extends the energy, rates and equilibrium work, and Module 6 extends the
organic chemistry and analysis.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Time | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H432/01 | Paper 1: Periodic table, elements and physical chemistry | 2 hours 15 minutes | 100 | 37% |
| H432/02 | Paper 2: Synthesis and analytical techniques | 2 hours 15 minutes | 100 | 37% |
| H432/03 | Paper 3: Unified chemistry | 1 hour 30 minutes | 70 | 26% |

All three papers are taken at the end of the course and there are no tiers. The paper
titles show the emphasis of the first two: Paper 1 leans towards the periodic table,
elements and physical chemistry, and Paper 2 towards synthesis and analytical
techniques. Paper 3 is synoptic and covers all the modules. For a module-by-module
mapping of the papers, see the exam preparation page.

The Practical Endorsement is a non-exam element, internally assessed and based on a
minimum of 12 practical activities. It is reported as Pass or Not Classified and does
not contribute to the 270-mark total.

## How the parts fit together

Chemistry at this level is cumulative. The mole and amount-of-substance calculations
from Module 2 appear in almost every later topic, from enthalpy changes and equilibrium
constants to titrations in acids, bases and buffers. Electron structure and bonding
explain the trends in periodicity and the behaviour of transition elements. Organic
synthesis appears in both Module 4 and Module 6, and qualitative analysis appears in
Modules 3, 5 and 6, so these threads are revisited and extended rather than learned once.

Practical work links the modules in another way. Module 1 skills, such as planning
experiments, recording data, processing results and evaluating uncertainty, are tested
in the written papers as well as in the endorsement. Analytical techniques and
spectroscopy bring practical and theoretical chemistry together, and moving easily between the two
is good preparation for a synoptic paper such as Paper 3.

## Planning your study

The module order is a sound teaching order:

- **First year:** Modules 2, 3 and 4, with Module 1 skills built in through regular
  practical work. Spend extra time on amount of substance until calculations feel
  routine.
- **Second year:** Modules 5 and 6, which extend first-year content, alongside the
  remaining practical activities for the endorsement.
- **Final months:** revision across all six modules and full timed papers.

When you meet a Module 5 or 6 topic, go back to the Module 3 or 4 work it extends and
check that the foundations are secure. Organise organic chemistry around reaction
types and a growing synthesis map, adding reactions to it as each module is taught.
Begin past paper questions by topic during the first year, then move to whole papers,
and use the exam preparation page for how to approach each paper.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many exam papers are there in OCR A Level Chemistry A?**
Three. Papers 1 and 2 are each 2 hours 15 minutes, 100 marks and 37%; Paper 3, Unified chemistry, is 1 hour 30 minutes, 70 marks and 26%.

**What is the total mark for H432?**
The three papers total 270 marks, and this total determines the A* to E grade.

**What does Paper 3 Unified chemistry cover?**
It is a synoptic paper covering all the modules, so a single question can draw on several areas of the course.

**Does the Practical Endorsement affect the H432 grade?**
No. It is internally assessed, based on a minimum of 12 practical activities, and reported separately as Pass or Not Classified.

**Is OCR A Level Chemistry A tiered?**
No. Every candidate sits the same three papers at the end of the course.
