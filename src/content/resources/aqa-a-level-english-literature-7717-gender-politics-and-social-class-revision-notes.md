**These notes condense one element of Option 2B:** "gender politics and issues of social class". The
[gender politics and social class study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-english-literature-7717-gender-politics-and-social-class/)
has full analysis and a model paragraph; the
[protest writing revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-english-literature-7712-7717-option-2b-elements-of-political-and-social-protest-writing-7717-revision-notes/)
cover the whole option.

## Four questions for any system of power

1. **Who benefits**, and how is it justified (nature, law, God, money, tradition)?
2. **How is it maintained**: violence, law, language, or the cooperation of the powerless?
3. **What does resistance look like**, and what does it cost?
4. **Where is the reader placed**: inside, outside, or moving between?

"Gender politics" means relations of power between genders, in public and private spaces.

## Gender politics by text

| Text | How gender power works | Key evidence |
|---|---|---|
| *A Doll's House* | Law and marriage deny a wife financial agency | The secret loan and forged signature; pet names; the tarantella; the final exit |
| *The Handmaid's Tale* | Function decides status; reading forbidden | Colour-coded ranks; "Offred" as possession |
| *Henry IV Part I* | Women excluded from the politics of war | Lady Percy shut out of Hotspur's plans; Lady Mortimer's Welsh |
| *The Kite Runner* | Double standards in the émigré community | Soraya's past judged as men's are not |

## Social class by text

| Text | How class power works | Key evidence |
|---|---|---|
| *Hard Times* | Masters and "Hands"; one law for the rich | Stephen's marriage trap; Bounderby's false self-made myth |
| Blake's *Songs* | Institutions profit from the suffering poor | "London": sweep, soldier, harlot; the paired "Chimney Sweeper" poems |
| Harrison's *Selected Poems* | Accent and education as class markers | "Them and [uz]"; "Marked with D"; "V" and the skinhead |
| *Harvest* | Ownership decides who belongs | Enclosure of common land for sheep, seen by Walter Thirsk |
| *The Kite Runner* | Class bound to ethnicity | Amir, wealthy Pashtun; Hassan, Hazara servant |

## Intersections

- **Louisa and Stephen** (*Hard Times*): marriage as a trap from above and below.
- **Wives and Econowives** (*The Handmaid's Tale*): women divided by class.
- **Mrs Linde and the nursemaid** (*A Doll's House*): choices shaped by money as well as law.
- **The "youthful Harlot"** ("London"): exploited through gender and poverty at once.

## Resistance and its cost

- **Leaving**: Nora's exit, which costs her home and children.
- **Speaking in one's own voice**: Harrison's claim to write in his accent.
- **Telling the story**: Offred's recorded account, later handled by male academics.
- **Silent endurance**: Stephen Blackpool's refusal to join the union or betray his word, which leaves
  him isolated from both sides.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Patriarchy | A social system in which power is held mainly by men |
| Gender politics | Relations of power between genders |
| Class consciousness | Awareness of one's position in a class system |
| Hegemony | Dominance maintained through consent and ideas as well as force |
| Commodification | Treating people or labour as things to be bought and sold |
| Enclosure | Turning common land into private property |
| Received Pronunciation | The accent traditionally associated with education and authority in England |
| Intersectionality | The way systems such as gender and class combine |
| Paternalism | Control presented as care |
| Agency | The capacity to act and choose |

## Context to link to scenes

- **Nineteenth-century marriage law** behind Nora's secret debt.
- **Industrial working conditions** and divorce costs behind Stephen Blackpool's trap.
- **Late twentieth-century debates** about women's rights behind Atwood's speculative regime.
- **The miners' strike** of the mid-1980s behind Harrison's "V".
- **Enclosure** of common land behind *Harvest*.

## Exam traps

- Reducing a text to a message instead of analysing how power is built.
- Treating gender or class as setting rather than as a system.
- Forgetting how men are controlled by gender systems, and the middle class by class systems.
- Context in general terms, not tied to a scene.
- Missing the intersection of gender and class.
- Long quotations from the open book instead of short, exact ones.

## Self-test

1. Quote the AQA element covered here.
2. What does "politics" mean in "gender politics"?
3. Why does Nora forge a signature?
4. How does Gilead mark women's ranks?
5. How are women excluded from power in *Henry IV Part I*? Give two examples.
6. What does the word "Hands" suggest about workers in *Hard Times*?
7. How is Bounderby's class myth exposed?
8. What class issue does "Them and [uz]" dramatise?
9. Which historical process lies behind *Harvest*?
10. How do class and ethnicity combine in *The Kite Runner*?
11. Give one example of gender and class intersecting in a set text.
12. Name two ways a system of power can be maintained.

## Answers

1. "Gender politics and issues of social class".
2. Relations of power, in private as well as public spaces.
3. To borrow money to save her husband's life, because the law did not let a wife borrow without her
   husband's consent.
4. By colour-coded clothing: Wives in blue, Handmaids in red, Marthas in green, Aunts in brown,
   Econowives in stripes.
5. Lady Percy is shut out of Hotspur's plans; Lady Mortimer speaks only Welsh and cannot join the
   English conversation.
6. That workers are reduced to body parts useful for labour.
7. His mother, Mrs Pegler, appears and reveals that he was not abandoned and self-made as he claimed.
8. The policing of a working-class northern accent as unfit for poetry, and the poet's claim to his
   own voice.
9. Enclosure of common land.
10. Amir is a wealthy Pashtun and Hassan a Hazara servant, so the hierarchy of class is also a
    hierarchy of ethnicity.
11. Any one: Louisa and Stephen's marriages; Wives and Econowives; Mrs Linde or the nursemaid; the
    "youthful Harlot".
12. Any two: violence, law, language, tradition, the cooperation of the powerless.
