These questions cover section **3.3.13.5 Action of anticancer drugs** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 2 and Paper 3. Learn the ideas first in the [Action of anticancer drugs study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-action-of-anticancer-drugs/) and the [Action of anticancer drugs revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-action-of-anticancer-drugs-revision-notes/). Use Aᵣ values H = 1.0, N = 14.0, Cl = 35.5, Pt = 195.1.

## Questions

**1.** Cisplatin is [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂].

**(a)** State the oxidation state of platinum in cisplatin. **[1]**

**(b)** State the shape of the complex. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain what is meant by the term cis in cisplatin. **[1]**

**2.** Draw the structure of cisplatin and of its trans isomer. **[2]**

**3.** Cisplatin reacts with DNA in a ligand replacement reaction.

**(a)** State which ligands are replaced. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the atom in guanine that bonds to platinum. **[1]**

**(c)** Name the type of bond formed between platinum and this atom, and explain how it forms. **[2]**

**(d)** Write an equation for the reaction of cisplatin with two guanine nitrogen atoms, representing each as N(guanine). **[2]**

**4.** Explain how cisplatin prevents the replication of DNA in cancer cells. **[3]**

**5.** Explain why the cis isomer, and not the trans isomer, can form a cross-link between two adjacent guanine bases on the same DNA strand. **[2]**

**6.** Cisplatin has adverse effects, such as hair loss and nausea. Explain why. **[3]**

**7.** A patient receives 75.0 mg of cisplatin (Mᵣ = 300.1).

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in mol, of cisplatin in the dose. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of platinum in the dose. **[2]**

**8.** Calculate the percentage by mass of platinum in cisplatin. **[2]**

**9.** Explain why the complex formed when cisplatin has replaced both its chloride ligands has an overall charge of 2+. **[2]**

**10.** A hospital committee is deciding whether to use cisplatin for a patient with an advanced cancer. Discuss the factors the committee should consider. **[5]**

**11.** Suggest two ways in which chemists might try to reduce the adverse effects of a drug such as cisplatin. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** +2 [1]

**(b)** Square planar [1]

**(c)** The two chloride ligands (and the two ammonia ligands) are next to each other, on the same side of the complex [1]. **[3]**

**2.** Cisplatin: square planar with the two Cl ligands adjacent (90° apart) and the two NH₃ ligands adjacent [1]; trans: the two Cl ligands on opposite sides of the Pt (180° apart) and the two NH₃ ligands on opposite sides [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** The chloride ligands [1]

**(b)** Nitrogen [1]

**(c)** A dative covalent (coordinate) bond [1]; both electrons in the bond come from the lone pair on the nitrogen atom [1]

**(d)** [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] + 2 N(guanine) [1] → [Pt(NH₃)₂(N-guanine)₂]²⁺ + 2Cl⁻ [1]. **[6]**

**4.** Cisplatin bonds to nitrogen atoms of guanine bases in DNA by ligand replacement [1]; this distorts the DNA and cross-links it, so the strands cannot unwind and separate [1]; DNA cannot be replicated, so the cell cannot divide [1]. **[3]**

**5.** In the cis isomer the two chloride ligands are next to each other [1]; so the platinum can replace both with two nitrogen atoms that are close together on the DNA, whereas in the trans isomer the leaving ligands are on opposite sides and cannot bond to the two adjacent sites [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Cisplatin is not selective [1]; it stops DNA replication in any cell, including healthy cells that divide quickly [1]; such as hair follicle cells and the lining of the gut, causing hair loss and nausea [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** n = 0.0750 ÷ 300.1 = **2.50 × 10⁻⁴ mol** [1]

**(b)** Mass fraction of Pt = 195.1 ÷ 300.1 = 0.650 [1]; mass = 75.0 × 0.650 = **48.8 mg** [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Mᵣ = 195.1 + 2(14.0 + 3.0) + 2(35.5) = 300.1 [1]; percentage = 195.1 ÷ 300.1 × 100 = **65.0 %** [1]. **[2]**

**9.** The original complex is neutral, so Pt²⁺ + 2Cl⁻ gives zero [1]; replacing two Cl⁻ with neutral nitrogen donors removes the 2− charge, leaving the 2+ charge of the Pt²⁺ ion on the complex [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Benefits: cisplatin can shrink tumours and extend life in some cancers [1]; the more serious the illness, the more likely the patient will accept the risks [1]. Harms: adverse effects such as nausea, hair loss, reduced blood cell production and kidney damage [1]; these affect quality of life and may need extra treatment [1]. A balanced conclusion that weighs the likely benefit for this patient against the adverse effects, and takes account of the patient's wishes [1]. **[5]**

**11.** Any two from: design drugs that are more selective for cancer cells; deliver the drug directly to the tumour; use a smaller dose in combination with other treatments; use computers to design new drug molecules [2]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* In "discuss" questions, mark schemes reward points on both sides and a conclusion that follows from them.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying the drug "kills cancer cells" without a mechanism.
- Bonding to the wrong part of DNA, such as phosphate or sugar.
- Calling the bond ionic or a hydrogen bond.
- Giving NH₃ as the ligand that is replaced.
- Omitting that the drug is not selective.
- Presenting only one side in an assessment question.
