## 3.1.2.1 Relative masses

**Relative atomic mass, Ar:** the weighted mean mass of an atom of an element (allowing for its isotopes) divided by one twelfth of the mass of an atom of carbon-12.

**Relative molecular mass, Mr:** the mean mass of a molecule divided by one twelfth of the mass of an atom of carbon-12. Add the Ar values of every atom in the formula.

**Relative formula mass:** the term used for ionic compounds, because they exist as lattices, not separate molecules.

Neither quantity has units, because each is a ratio.

Reminder: boron is 20% ¹⁰B and 80% ¹¹B, so Ar = (0.20 × 10) + (0.80 × 11) = 10.8.

## 3.1.2.2 The mole, Avogadro and solutions

A mole of any substance always holds an identical number of entities: the Avogadro constant, L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹. The entity can be an atom, molecule, ion, electron, formula unit or equation unit.

| Quantity | Relationship | Units |
|---|---|---|
| Amount from mass | n = m / Mr | mol, g, g mol⁻¹ |
| Number of particles | N = n × L | particles |
| Concentration | c = n / V | mol dm⁻³, mol, dm³ |
| Volume conversion | dm³ = cm³ / 1000 | |

Worked reminders:

- 5.30 g of Na₂CO₃ (Mr 106.0) is 0.0500 mol. It contains 0.100 mol of Na⁺ ions, which is 6.02 × 10²² ions.
- Dissolve that 0.0500 mol to make 250 cm³ of solution: c = 0.0500 / 0.250 = 0.200 mol dm⁻³.
- 0.0200 mol of Al₂(SO₄)₃ gives 0.0400 mol Al³⁺ and 0.0600 mol SO₄²⁻, 0.100 mol of ions altogether.
- Each H₂O molecule has 10 electrons, so 0.250 mol of water holds 2.50 mol of electrons, which is 1.51 × 10²⁴ electrons.

A formula is a statement about moles too: the formula Al₂(SO₄)₃ says 2 mol Al to 3 mol SO₄ in 1 mol of compound.

## 3.1.2.3 The ideal gas equation

pV = nRT

| Symbol | Meaning | SI unit |
|---|---|---|
| p | gas pressure | Pa |
| V | gas volume | m³ |
| n | moles of gas | mol |
| R | molar gas constant (8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ in these notes) | J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ |
| T | absolute temperature | K |

Conversions: kPa × 1000 = Pa; cm³ × 10⁻⁶ = m³; dm³ × 10⁻³ = m³; K = °C + 273.

Method in steps:

1. List p, V, n (or mass and Mr) and T.
2. Convert every value to SI units.
3. Rearrange first, then substitute.
4. Finally, express the answer in whatever unit is requested.

Reminder (finding p): 0.0800 mol of gas in 1.50 dm³ at 350 K. V = 1.50 × 10⁻³ m³, so p = nRT / V = (0.0800 × 8.31 × 350) / (1.50 × 10⁻³) = 155 000 Pa, or 155 kPa.

Reminder (finding Mr): 0.640 g of a gas occupies 0.500 dm³ at 100 kPa and 300 K. n = pV / RT = (100 000 × 0.500 × 10⁻³) / (8.31 × 300) = 0.0201 mol, so Mr = 0.640 / 0.0201 = 31.9.

## 3.1.2.4 Empirical and molecular formula

**Empirical formula:** the smallest whole-number atom ratio for a compound.
**Molecular formula:** the true count of each kind of atom in a single molecule. It is a whole-number multiple of the empirical formula.

Method in steps:

1. Write the mass (or percentage, treated as grams in 100 g) of each element.
2. Divide each by its Ar to get moles.
3. Divide all by the smallest number of moles.
4. If a ratio is not whole, multiply all by the smallest integer that makes them whole (×2 for .5, ×3 for .33 or .67).
5. For molecular formula, divide the given Mr by the empirical formula mass and multiply.

Reminder A: a sample holds 1.20 g C, 0.20 g H and 0.80 g O. Moles: 0.100, 0.20, 0.050. Divide by 0.050: 2 : 4 : 1. Empirical formula C₂H₄O (mass 44). If Mr = 88, then 88 / 44 = 2, so the molecular formula is C₄H₈O₂.

Reminder B: an oxide is 69.9% Fe and 30.1% O. Moles: 1.25 and 1.88. Ratio 1 : 1.50, so multiply by 2 to give 2 : 3. The empirical formula is Fe₂O₃. Do not round 1.50 down to 1.

## 3.1.2.5 Equations and calculations

**Full equation:** all species shown, balanced for atoms and charge.
**Ionic equation:** only the species that change; spectator ions are left out. For example, Pb²⁺(aq) + 2I⁻(aq) → PbI₂(s).

To balance an unfamiliar reaction when reactants and products are given: write the correct formulae first, balance one element at a time (leave elements appearing alone until last), and never change a subscript. Example: Ca(OH)₂ + NH₄Cl → CaCl₂ + NH₃ + H₂O balances as Ca(OH)₂ + 2NH₄Cl → CaCl₂ + 2NH₃ + 2H₂O.

The calculation chain: given quantity → moles → use equation ratio → moles of wanted substance → required quantity.

**Gas volume reminder:** 0.486 g Mg (Ar 24.3) is 0.0200 mol. Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂ gives 0.0200 mol H₂. At 298 K and 100 kPa, V = nRT / p = (0.0200 × 8.31 × 298) / 100 000 = 4.95 × 10⁻⁴ m³ = 495 cm³.

**Percentage yield** = (actual mass / theoretical mass) × 100.

**Percentage atom economy** = (molecular mass of desired product / sum of molecular masses of all reactants) × 100.

**Yield and atom economy reminder:** Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂. For 40.0 g of Fe₂O₃ (Mr 159.6): n = 0.251 mol, so the theoretical mass of Fe is 0.501 × 55.8 = 28.0 g. An actual 24.5 g gives a yield of 87.6%. The atom economy for making iron is 111.6 / 243.6 × 100 = 45.8%. Yield describes what happened in one experiment; atom economy is fixed by the equation.

**Solutions and titration reminder:** H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O. A titre of 22.40 cm³ of 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ acid neutralises 25.0 cm³ of alkali. n(acid) = 0.02240 × 0.0500 = 1.12 × 10⁻³ mol, so n(NaOH) = 2.24 × 10⁻³ mol and c = 2.24 × 10⁻³ / 0.0250 = 0.0896 mol dm⁻³.

**Why high atom economy matters:** economically, less raw material ends up as unwanted product, and waste disposal or separation costs fall. Ethically and environmentally, fewer natural resources are consumed and less waste is produced, which benefits society and industry alike. Be ready to give one point for each, not a general statement.

**Required practical 1:** make up a volumetric solution (weigh by difference, dissolve in a beaker, transfer with rinsings to a volumetric flask, fill to the graduation line, then invert to mix) and carry out a simple acid-base titration (pipette, burette, indicator, concordant titres).

## Must-know distinctions

- Ar (atoms, weighted for isotopes) against Mr (molecules) against relative formula mass (ionic compounds).
- Percentage yield (experimental) against percentage atom economy (set by the equation).
- Empirical formula (ratio) against molecular formula (actual numbers).
- Amount (mol) against concentration (mol dm⁻³) against mass (g).

## Quick self-test

1. Write the definition of Mr in terms of ¹²C.
2. How many oxygen atoms are in 0.300 mol of CO₂? (L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)
3. What is the concentration when 0.0400 mol is dissolved to make 500 cm³ of solution?
4. Convert 250 kPa and 40 cm³ into SI units.
5. A gas has n = 0.0500 mol at 400 K in a 2.00 dm³ vessel. Find p in kPa (R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹).
6. Find the empirical formula of a compound with 2.40 g C and 0.60 g H.
7. The empirical formula is CH₂O and Mr is 60.0. State the molecular formula.
8. Balance: Al + HCl → AlCl₃ + H₂.
9. Find the percentage yield when 3.42 g is obtained against a theoretical 4.65 g.
10. Why is the ionic equation for HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq) simply H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O?

### Answers

1. The mean mass of a molecule divided by one twelfth of the mass of a ¹²C atom.
2. 0.300 × 2 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 3.61 × 10²³ oxygen atoms.
3. 0.0400 / 0.500 = 0.0800 mol dm⁻³.
4. 250 000 Pa and 4.0 × 10⁻⁵ m³.
5. p = (0.0500 × 8.31 × 400) / (2.00 × 10⁻³) = 83 100 Pa = 83.1 kPa.
6. C: 0.200 mol; H: 0.60 mol; ratio 1 : 3; CH₃.
7. CH₂O has mass 30.0, so 60.0 / 30.0 = 2 and the molecular formula is C₂H₄O₂.
8. 2Al + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂.
9. (3.42 / 4.65) × 100 = 73.5%.
10. Na⁺ and Cl⁻ are spectator ions: they are present unchanged on both sides.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting volumes in cm³ or dm³, or pressure in kPa, into pV = nRT unconverted.
- Forgetting to convert °C to kelvin.
- Rounding a ratio such as 1.50 or 2.33 to a whole number instead of multiplying through.
- Using the Mr of the wrong species or ignoring the mole ratio in the equation.
- Writing a formula like Mg(OH)₂ with the wrong Mr because the bracket multiplier was dropped.
- Giving yields or atom economy above 100% without noticing something is wrong.
- Quoting a final answer with no unit, or with far more significant figures than the data allow.
- Writing spectator ions into an ionic equation, or leaving charges unbalanced.
