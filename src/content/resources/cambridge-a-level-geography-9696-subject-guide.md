Cambridge International AS & A Level Geography (9696) covers both sides of the subject: the physical processes that shape rivers, weather, rocks, coasts and climates, and the human patterns of population, migration, settlement, economic activity and development. The AS Level builds a broad core in each; the A Level adds more specialised option topics. The 2025-2026 syllabus is assessed through four written papers, each 90 minutes long and marked out of 60, and each worth 25% of the full A Level: Paper 1 Core Physical Geography, Paper 2 Core Human Geography, Paper 3 Advanced Physical Geography Options and Paper 4 Advanced Human Geography Options.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus contains fourteen topics: six at AS and eight at A Level. Their names and the paper titles suggest a clear split between physical and human geography at each stage.

**AS Level: core physical geography**

1. **Hydrology and fluvial geomorphology**: the drainage basin system, discharge relationships, river channel processes and landforms, and the human impact.
2. **Atmosphere and weather**: diurnal energy budgets, the global energy budget, weather processes and phenomena, and the human impact.
3. **Rocks and weathering**: plate tectonics, weathering, slope processes, and the human impact.

**AS Level: core human geography**

4. **Population**: natural increase, demographic transition, population–resource relationships and the management of natural increase.
5. **Migration**: migration as a component of population change, internal migration, international migration and its management.
6. **Settlement dynamics**: changes in rural settlements, urban trends and urbanisation, the changing structure of urban settlements, and urban management.

**A Level: advanced physical geography options**

7. **Tropical environments**
8. **Coastal environments**
9. **Hazardous environments**
10. **Hot arid and semi-arid environments**

Each of these looks at climate or processes, the landforms or ecosystems that result, and sustainable management. Coastal environments, for example, includes coastal processes, coastal landforms, coral reefs and the sustainable management of coasts.

**A Level: advanced human geography options**

11. **Production, location and change**: agricultural systems, food production and manufacturing, and the management of change in each.
12. **Environmental management**: sustainable energy supplies and their management, environmental degradation, and managing a degraded environment.
13. **Global interdependence**: trade flows, international debt and aid, and international tourism and its management.
14. **Economic transition**: national development, the globalisation of economic activity, and regional development and its management.

The A Level topics are options, so you will not necessarily study all of them. Confirm with your teacher which options your class is following.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting (full A Level) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Core Physical Geography | 90 minutes | 60 | 25% |
| Paper 2 | Core Human Geography | 90 minutes | 60 | 25% |
| Paper 3 | Advanced Physical Geography Options | 90 minutes | 60 | 25% |
| Paper 4 | Advanced Human Geography Options | 90 minutes | 60 | 25% |

9696 is a staged qualification:

- **AS Level.** Papers 1 and 2 can be taken as a standalone AS Level. On that route each paper is worth 50% of the AS grade.
- **Full A Level.** Candidates take Papers 1 and 2 for the AS Level in one series, then Papers 3 and 4 in a later series to complete the A Level.

All four papers are written examinations of equal length, marks and weighting, so physical and human geography carry equal weight at both stages.

This page describes the syllabus for examinations in 2025 and 2026, first assessed in 2025. Students whose examinations fall after 2026 should look up the edition that covers their own series.

## How the parts fit together

The AS core gives you the concepts the A Level options build on.

- **Physical links.** River processes, weathering and slope processes from the AS core return in the landforms of tropical, coastal and hot arid environments. Plate tectonics and slope processes lay the groundwork for the tectonic and mass-movement hazards in Hazardous environments, while weather processes support the study of atmospheric disturbances.
- **Human links.** Population and migration underpin questions of development in Economic transition and Global interdependence. Settlement dynamics connects with manufacturing, services and regional development in the human options.
- **Management as a common thread.** Almost every topic ends with "the human impact" at AS or with management and sustainability at A Level. Learning to evaluate management strategies in one topic is a skill you transfer to all the others.

Across the course you will also develop geographical skills: interpreting maps, graphs, photographs and data, using case studies at different scales, and constructing an argument that weighs evidence.

## Planning your study

**AS stage.** Many students find it helpful to alternate between physical and human topics rather than completing all of one before the other, since it keeps both papers in view. A typical sequence is Hydrology and fluvial geomorphology with Population, then Atmosphere and weather with Migration, then Rocks and weathering with Settlement dynamics. Build a case-study file as you go, with located examples for each topic.

**A Level stage.** Once your options are confirmed, start with the physical option that draws most directly on AS content, and review the relevant AS topic before you begin it. Keep updating your case-study file with examples at local, national and global scales.

**Practice.** Answer short data-based questions from early on, and move to essays as each topic is completed. Leave full timed papers until the content for that stage has been taught. The course's exam-preparation page explains how to prepare for the staged four-paper route.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge A Level Geography 9696?**
Four, each 90 minutes and 60 marks: Paper 1 Core Physical Geography, Paper 2 Core Human Geography, Paper 3 Advanced Physical Geography Options and Paper 4 Advanced Human Geography Options.

**How is the full A Level weighted?**
Each of the four papers is worth 25%. For the AS Level alone, Papers 1 and 2 are worth 50% each.

**Do I take all four papers at once?**
On the staged route, Papers 1 and 2 are taken for the AS Level in one series and Papers 3 and 4 in a later series to complete the A Level.

**Do I study every A Level topic?**
The A Level topics are options, so check with your teacher which ones your class is studying.

**Which years does this syllabus cover?**
This page describes the 2025-2026 syllabus, first assessed in 2025.
