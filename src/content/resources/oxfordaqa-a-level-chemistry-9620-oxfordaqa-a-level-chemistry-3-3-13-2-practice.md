This set practises **3.3.13.2 Proteins** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International A2 content, assessed in Unit 4 and in the synoptic Unit 5. If you get stuck, go back to the [proteins study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-13-2/) or the [proteins revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-13-2-revision-notes/). Side chains: glycine –H, alanine –CH₃, serine –CH₂OH, valine –CH(CH₃)₂, cysteine –CH₂SH. Mr: glycine 75.0, alanine 89.0, serine 105.0, valine 117.0, cysteine 121.1.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** How many molecules of water are released when three amino acids join to form a tripeptide?

A: 1

B: 2

C: 3

D: 4

**[1]**

**2.** Draw the structure of the dipeptide Ser–Gly, in which serine has the free amino group. **[1]**

**3.** How many different tripeptides can be made from glycine, alanine and serine if each is used exactly once? **[1]**

**4.** Name the level of protein structure shown by each description.

**(a)** A coiled chain with dashed lines linking C=O groups on one turn to N–H groups on the next. **[1]**

**(b)** The sequence Gly–Ala–Ser–Cys–Val written in a line. **[1]**

**5.** In TLC, explain why the standard amino acids should be run on the same plate as the unknown mixture. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** This question is about the tripeptide Ala–Ser–Gly.

**(a)** Draw its structure. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate its Mr. **[1]**

**(c)** The tripeptide is heated under reflux with excess aqueous sodium hydroxide. Draw the three organic ions formed. **[3]**

**7.** The dipeptide H₂N–CH(CH(CH₃)₂)–CO–NH–CH(CH₂SH)–COOH is heated under reflux with excess concentrated hydrochloric acid.

**(a)** Name the two amino acids in the dipeptide. **[1]**

**(b)** Draw the two organic species formed. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the Mr of the dipeptide. **[1]**

**8.** Explain how hydrogen bonds maintain the α-helix structure of a protein. **[3]**

**9.** This question is about S–S bonds in proteins.

**(a)** Name the amino acid whose side chains form S–S bonds, and write an equation, using [O], for the formation of an S–S bond between two of its side chains. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why a single S–S bond is more effective than a single hydrogen bond at holding a protein's tertiary structure in place. **[1]**

**10.** The amino acids from a hydrolysed peptide are separated by TLC. The solvent front is 7.5 cm from the baseline. Two spots are found at 1.5 cm and 4.5 cm. On the same plate, standards J, K and L give Rf values of 0.20, 0.45 and 0.60.

**(a)** Explain two precautions the student should take when setting up the plate. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the Rf value of each spot. **[2]**

**(c)** Identify the amino acids present. **[1]**

**11.** A diagram shows a single protein chain folded into a compact, roughly spherical shape. Two cysteine residues far apart in the sequence are joined by a line labelled S–S. Identify the level of structure and explain how the diagram shows it. **[2]**

**12.** A dipeptide gives only glycine and alanine when hydrolysed. A separate test shows that alanine carries the free –NH₂ group.

**(a)** Draw the dipeptide. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why TLC of the hydrolysis products alone could not show which dipeptide was present. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. Two peptide links form, each releasing one water molecule.

**2.** H₂N–CH(CH₂OH)–CO–NH–CH₂–COOH [1]

**3.** 6 [1] (3 × 2 × 1)

**4. (a)** Secondary structure (α-helix) [1]

**(b)** Primary structure [1]

**5.** Rf values depend on the solvent and the plate, so a fair comparison needs identical conditions [1]

**6. (a)** H₂N–CH(CH₃)–CO–NH–CH(CH₂OH)–CO–NH–CH₂–COOH. Correct sequence with free NH₂ on alanine [1]; two correct peptide links [1]

**(b)** 89.0 + 105.0 + 75.0 − 36.0 = **233.0** [1]

**(c)** H₂N–CH(CH₃)–COO⁻ [1]; H₂N–CH(CH₂OH)–COO⁻ [1]; H₂N–CH₂–COO⁻ [1]

*Examiner insight:* Ala–Ser–Gly has the same Mr as Gly–Ala–Ser but is a different compound; the order of residues is the primary structure.

**7. (a)** Valine and cysteine [1]

**(b)** ⁺H₃N–CH(CH(CH₃)₂)–COOH [1]; ⁺H₃N–CH(CH₂SH)–COOH [1]

**(c)** 117.0 + 121.1 − 18.0 = **220.1** [1]

*Examiner insight:* in acid hydrolysis the cysteine side chain keeps its –SH group; only the amino groups gain H⁺.

**8.** In each peptide link the N–H hydrogen is δ+ and the C=O oxygen is δ− with lone pairs [1]; the C=O of one peptide link forms a hydrogen bond with the N–H of a peptide link a few residues further along the same chain [1]; many such hydrogen bonds, roughly parallel to the axis of the coil, hold the helix in shape [1]

**9. (a)** Cysteine [1]; –CH₂–SH + HS–CH₂– + [O] → –CH₂–S–S–CH₂– + H₂O [1]

**(b)** An S–S bond is a covalent bond, which is much stronger than a hydrogen bond [1]

**10. (a)** Any two [2]: draw the baseline in pencil, which does not dissolve in the solvent; keep the solvent level below the baseline so spots do not dissolve into the solvent pool; handle the plate by the edges or wear gloves, because fingerprints contain amino acids; cover the tank so the atmosphere is saturated with solvent vapour.

**(b)** 1.5 ÷ 7.5 = **0.20** [1]; 4.5 ÷ 7.5 = **0.60** [1]

**(c)** J and L [1]

**11.** Tertiary structure [1]; it shows the overall three-dimensional folding of the whole chain, with an S–S bond linking distant parts of the sequence [1]

**12. (a)** H₂N–CH(CH₃)–CO–NH–CH₂–COOH (Ala–Gly) [1]

**(b)** Both Ala–Gly and Gly–Ala give the same two amino acids on hydrolysis, so the spots would be identical [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Linking C to C or N to O in a peptide.
- Subtracting the wrong number of waters.
- Drawing neutral amino acids after hydrolysis in acid or alkali.
- Assigning S–S bonds to secondary structure.
- Measuring TLC distances from the bottom edge.
