Use Ar values H = 1.0, C = 12.0, N = 14.0 and O = 16.0.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** Glycine is dissolved in an excess of aqueous sodium hydroxide. Which species is present?

A: ⁺H₃N–CH₂–COOH

B: ⁺H₃N–CH₂–COO⁻

C: H₂N–CH₂–COO⁻

D: H₂N–CH₂–COOH

**[1]**

**2.** Valine is H₂N–CH(CH(CH₃)₂)–COOH.

**(a)** State what is meant by a zwitterion. **[1]**

**(b)** Draw valine as a zwitterion. **[1]**

**3.** Give the overall charge on each species.

**(a)** ⁺H₃N–CH(CH₂COOH)–COO⁻ **[1]**

**(b)** ⁻OOC–CH₂CH₂–CH(NH₂)–COO⁻ **[1]**

**(c)** ⁺H₃N–(CH₂)₄–CH(NH₃⁺)–COO⁻ **[1]**

**4.** Explain why amino acids have much higher melting points than most organic compounds of similar Mr. **[2]**

**5.** Write an equation for the reaction of alanine, H₂N–CH(CH₃)–COOH, with hydrochloric acid. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** Threonine is H₂N–CH(CH(OH)CH₃)–COOH.

**(a)** Draw threonine as a zwitterion. **[1]**

**(b)** Draw the ion formed in an excess of acid. **[1]**

**(c)** Draw the ion formed in an excess of alkali. **[1]**

**7.** Lysine is H₂N–(CH₂)₄–CH(NH₂)–COOH (Mr = 146.0).

**(a)** Draw the ion formed when lysine is dissolved in an excess of hydrochloric acid, and give its charge. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the volume of 0.200 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid that reacts completely with 0.584 g of lysine. **[2]**

**8.** An amino acid, X, has the general formula H₂N–CH(R)–COOH, where R contains only carbon and hydrogen. A 0.445 g sample of X reacts exactly with 20.0 cm³ of 0.250 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide.

**(a)** Calculate the amount of NaOH used. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the Mr of X. **[1]**

**(c)** Deduce the R group and name X. **[2]**

**9.** Aspartic acid is HOOC–CH₂–CH(NH₂)–COOH (Mr = 133.0).

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction of aspartic acid with an excess of sodium hydroxide. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the volume of 0.120 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide needed to react completely with 0.399 g of aspartic acid. **[2]**

**10.** Using glycine as the example, explain with equations what is meant by saying that amino acids have both acidic and basic properties. **[4]**

**11.** An unbranched amino acid, Y, has Mr = 147.0, and its amino group and one carboxyl group are on carbon 2. One mole of Y reacts with two moles of NaOH and with one mole of HCl.

**(a)** Deduce the number of –COOH and –NH₂ groups in Y. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest the structure of Y and show that it fits the Mr. **[2]**

**12.** Glycine dissolves readily in water but hardly at all in hexane. Explain this difference. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. In excess alkali the –NH₃⁺ group loses H⁺, and the carboxyl group is present as –COO⁻.

**2. (a)** A species with both a positive and a negative charge (on different atoms) but no overall charge [1]

**(b)** ⁺H₃N–CH(CH(CH₃)₂)–COO⁻ [1]

**3. (a)** 0 [1]

**(b)** −2 [1]

**(c)** +1 [1]

**4.** Amino acids exist as zwitterions in the solid [1]; there are strong ionic attractions between the opposite charges on neighbouring zwitterions, which need a lot of energy to overcome [1]

*Examiner insight:* "hydrogen bonding" alone is not enough here; the mark depends on naming the zwitterion and the ionic attraction between zwitterions.

**5.** H₂N–CH(CH₃)–COOH + HCl → ⁺H₃N–CH(CH₃)–COOH Cl⁻ [1]

**6. (a)** ⁺H₃N–CH(CH(OH)CH₃)–COO⁻ [1]

**(b)** ⁺H₃N–CH(CH(OH)CH₃)–COOH [1]

**(c)** H₂N–CH(CH(OH)CH₃)–COO⁻ [1]

*Examiner insight:* the side-chain –OH stays as –OH in every form; drawing –O⁻ in alkali loses the mark.

**7. (a)** ⁺H₃N–(CH₂)₄–CH(NH₃⁺)–COOH [1]; charge +2 [1]

**(b)** n(lysine) = 0.584 ÷ 146.0 = 0.00400 mol, so n(HCl) = 2 × 0.00400 = 0.00800 mol [1]; volume = 0.00800 ÷ 0.200 = 0.0400 dm³ = **40.0 cm³** [1]

**8. (a)** 0.250 × 20.0 ÷ 1000 = **0.00500 mol** [1]

**(b)** One –COOH reacts with one NaOH, so n(X) = 0.00500 mol and Mr = 0.445 ÷ 0.00500 = **89.0** [1]

**(c)** H₂N–CH–COOH accounts for 74.0, so R = 15.0, which is –CH₃ [1]; X is alanine (2-aminopropanoic acid) [1]

**9. (a)** HOOC–CH₂–CH(NH₂)–COOH + 2NaOH → NaOOC–CH₂–CH(NH₂)–COONa + 2H₂O [1]

**(b)** n(aspartic acid) = 0.399 ÷ 133.0 = 0.00300 mol; n(NaOH) = 0.00600 mol [1]; volume = 0.00600 ÷ 0.120 = 0.0500 dm³ = **50.0 cm³** [1]

*Examiner insight:* a 1 : 1 ratio gives 25.0 cm³; check the side chain for a second –COOH before using the ratio.

**10.** Acidic: the –COOH group donates H⁺, so glycine reacts with a base [1]; H₂N–CH₂–COOH + NaOH → H₂N–CH₂–COO⁻Na⁺ + H₂O [1]; basic: the lone pair on N of –NH₂ accepts H⁺, so glycine reacts with an acid [1]; H₂N–CH₂–COOH + HCl → ⁺H₃N–CH₂–COOH Cl⁻ [1]

**11. (a)** Two –COOH groups and one –NH₂ group [1]

**(b)** HOOC–CH₂CH₂–CH(NH₂)–COOH [1]; C₅H₉NO₄: 60.0 + 9.0 + 14.0 + 64.0 = 147.0 [1]

*Examiner insight:* in (b) many candidates draw the correct structure but do not show it fits the Mr; the arithmetic is the second mark.

**12.** Glycine exists as zwitterions, which are strongly attracted to polar water molecules (ion–dipole attractions and hydrogen bonds) [1]; hexane is non-polar and cannot form strong attractions to the ionic groups, so it cannot overcome the attractions between zwitterions [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- –NH₃⁺ in alkali or –COO⁻ in acid.
- Ignoring a second –COOH or –NH₂ in the side chain.
- Changing a side-chain –OH.
- A 1 : 1 ratio used for an amino acid with two acidic or two basic groups.
- Charges drawn on carbon atoms.
