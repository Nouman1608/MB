Section 8.1 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 covers four physical safety issues, their causes and strategies for preventing them. These notes are for fast recall. The [physical safety study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-physical-safety/) explains why each cause leads to harm and works through a room inspection, and the [physical safety practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-physical-safety-practice/) test you in context.

## Syllabus coverage

| Syllabus wording | Recall |
|---|---|
| Electrocution | From spilling drinks near electrical equipment and touching live cables |
| Fire | From sockets being overloaded or equipment overheating |
| Tripping | Over trailing cables |
| Heavy equipment falling | And injuring people |
| Causes and prevention | For each issue |

## Hazard → cause → prevention chains

### Electrocution

- **Cause 1**: drink spilled into a computer, keyboard or socket → liquid conducts electricity.
- **Cause 2**: touching a live cable → insulation damaged, worn or cut; case opened while plugged in.
- **Prevent**: no drinks near computers; check and replace damaged cables; do not open cases while connected; use a residual current breaker (RCB); keep cables away from water.

### Fire

- **Cause 1**: overloaded socket → too many devices or extension leads plugged into each other → wires overheat.
- **Cause 2**: equipment overheating → blocked vents, dust, failed fans, poor room ventilation.
- **Prevent**: do not overload sockets; never "daisy-chain" extension leads; keep vents clear and dust-free; ventilation or air conditioning; switch off when not in use; CO2 fire extinguisher; smoke detectors.

### Tripping

- **Cause**: cables trailing across floors and walkways.
- **Prevent**: cable ducts or covers; route cables along walls or under desks; tie cables together; use wireless connections; put desks near sockets.

### Heavy equipment falling

- **Cause**: weak or unstable desks; equipment overhanging the edge; stacking; equipment pulled off by a trip over its cable.
- **Prevent**: strong, sturdy desks; equipment well back from the edge; desks large enough; no stacking; secure brackets for mounted equipment.

## Why CO2, not water?

Water conducts electricity, so using it on an electrical fire risks electrocution. A **CO2 extinguisher** smothers the fire without conducting electricity and leaves no residue on the equipment. Switch off the power at the mains first if it is safe to do so.

## Safety versus health

| Physical safety (8.1) | Health (5.2) |
|---|---|
| Sudden accidents | Long-term effects of use |
| Electrocution, fire, tripping, falling equipment | Back and neck problems, repetitive strain injury, eye strain, headaches |
| Prevented by safe layout and rules | Prevented by ergonomic furniture, breaks, posture |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Electrocution | Injury or death caused by electric current passing through the body |
| Live cable | A cable carrying electric current |
| Insulation | The plastic covering that stops current reaching anyone who touches a cable |
| Overloaded socket | A socket supplying more current than it is designed for |
| Overheating | Equipment becoming hotter than it is designed to run |
| Ventilation | Air flow that carries heat away from equipment |
| Cable duct | A cover or channel that encloses cables |
| Residual current breaker (RCB) | A device that cuts the power quickly if current leaks to earth |
| CO2 extinguisher | Fire extinguisher using carbon dioxide, suitable for electrical fires |

## Exam traps

- **Pair every hazard with its cause and a matching prevention.** "Fire: use cable ducts" does not match.
- **"Be careful" is never a strategy.** Name the action.
- **Health is not safety.** Eye strain and back pain are not 8.1 answers.
- **Water on an electrical fire** is wrong; say CO2.
- **Do not repeat one strategy** for two different hazards when the question asks for different ones.
- **Tripping and falling equipment can be linked**: a trip can pull a monitor off a desk. Mention the link only if asked about one of them.

## Self-test

1. Name the four safety issues listed in the syllabus.
2. Explain why spilling a drink near a computer is dangerous.
3. Give two ways a cable can become live to the touch.
4. State two prevention strategies for electrocution.
5. Explain how an overloaded socket can cause a fire.
6. Give two causes of equipment overheating.
7. Which type of fire extinguisher should be used on an electrical fire, and why?
8. Give two ways to prevent people tripping over cables.
9. How can wireless technology improve physical safety?
10. Give two causes of heavy equipment falling.
11. Give two strategies to stop heavy equipment falling.
12. Is eye strain a physical safety issue in this section? Explain.

## Self-test answers

1. Electrocution; fire; tripping over trailing cables; heavy equipment falling.
2. Liquid conducts electricity, so it can create a path for current through the equipment and the person.
3. Any two of: insulation damaged or worn; cable cut or trapped; case opened while plugged in.
4. Any two of: no drinks near computers; check and replace damaged cables; do not open cases while connected; use an RCB.
5. Too many devices draw more current than the socket and wiring are designed for, so the wires overheat and can set fire to insulation or nearby materials.
6. Any two of: blocked vents; dust in fans or vents; failed fan; poor room ventilation.
7. CO2, because water conducts electricity.
8. Any two of: cable ducts; route cables along walls; tie cables together; use wireless connections.
9. It reduces the number of cables, so fewer can trail across the floor.
10. Any two of: weak or unstable desks; overhanging the edge; stacking; being pulled off by a trailing cable.
11. Any two of: sturdy desks; place equipment away from the edge; large enough desks; secure mounting.
12. No. It is a health problem from prolonged use, covered in section 5.2, not an accident.
