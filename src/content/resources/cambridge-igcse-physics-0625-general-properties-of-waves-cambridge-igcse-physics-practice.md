These questions cover section 3.1, General properties of waves, of the Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Take the speed of electromagnetic waves as 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s.

> **Tier note:** questions 1 to 5 and 7 to 10 use Core content only (3.1 Core 1–8), and every candidate should do them. Questions 6, 11 and 12, marked *(Extended)*, use Supplement content (3.1 Supplement 9–10), examined on Papers 2 and 4 only. The marks shown are indicative only.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** A cork floats on a pond as ripples pass.

**(a)** State what the ripples transfer across the pond. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the motion of the cork, and explain how it shows that the water is not carried along by the wave. **[1]**

**2.** Define:

**(a)** the amplitude of a wave **[1]**
**(b)** the frequency of a wave. **[1]**

**3.** A wave on a rope has a frequency of 2.5 Hz and a wavelength of 1.2 m. Calculate its speed. **[2]**

**4.** A radio station broadcasts at a frequency of 90 MHz. Calculate the wavelength of its radio waves. **[3]**

**5.** State whether each of these waves is modelled as transverse or longitudinal: sound, light, seismic S-waves, seismic P-waves, water waves. **[3]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Straight water waves of wavelength 2.0 cm reach a barrier with a gap. Describe the waves beyond the gap when the gap is (i) 2.0 cm wide and (ii) 15 cm wide. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A student uses a ripple tank.

**(a)** Describe how straight waves are produced and how the student can see the wavefronts. **[2]**
**(b)** On the screen, 8 neighbouring wavefronts span 21 cm. Calculate the wavelength. **[2]**
**(c)** The frequency is 8.0 Hz. Calculate the wave speed in m/s. **[2]**

**8.** A long spring (slinky) lies on a bench.

**(a)** Describe how to send (i) a transverse wave and (ii) a longitudinal wave along the spring. **[2]**
**(b)** For each type, state the direction of vibration compared with the direction of propagation. **[2]**
**(c)** An earthquake produces P-waves and S-waves. State which type of wave each is. **[2]**

**9.** Water waves of frequency 10 Hz travel at 0.30 m/s in deep water. They pass into shallow water, where their speed is 0.20 m/s.

**(a)** Calculate the wavelength in the deep water. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the wavelength in the shallow water. **[2]**
**(c)** State the quantity that does not change when the waves enter the shallow water. **[1]**
**(d)** The boundary between the deep and shallow water is at an angle to the wavefronts. State what else happens to the waves. **[1]**

**10.** This question is about reflection of water waves.

**(a)** Describe how a ripple tank can be used to show the reflection of waves at a plane surface. **[2]**
**(b)** State **two** properties of the waves that are unchanged by reflection. **[2]**

**11.** *(Extended)* A harbour wall has an entrance 20 m wide.

**(a)** State whether sea waves of wavelength 20 m or of wavelength 2.0 m spread out more inside the harbour, and explain why. **[2]**
**(b)** State how the spacing of the wavefronts inside the harbour compares with that outside. **[1]**
**(c)** A house in a valley behind a hill receives long-wavelength radio signals well but short-wavelength signals poorly. Explain why. **[2]**

**12.** *(Extended)* A student writes: "When water waves go through a narrow gap they slow down and their wavelength decreases."

**(a)** Correct the student's statement. **[2]**
**(b)** A sound of frequency 256 Hz travels in air at 340 m/s. Calculate its wavelength. **[2]**
**(c)** Use your answer to (b) to explain why this sound spreads out well through a doorway 0.90 m wide. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Energy [1]
**(b)** The cork moves up and down but stays in the same place, so the water is not moving along with the wave [1].

**2. (a)** The maximum displacement from the rest (undisturbed) position [1]
**(b)** The number of waves passing a point per second [1]

**3.** v = fλ = 2.5 × 1.2 [1] = **3.0 m/s** [1]

**4.** 90 MHz = 9.0 × 10⁷ Hz [1]; λ = v/f = 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 9.0 × 10⁷ [1] = **3.3 m** [1]

**5.** Transverse: light, S-waves, water waves; longitudinal: sound, P-waves [3 for all five correct; 2 for four; 1 for three].

**6.** (i) The waves spread out a lot (nearly semicircular wavefronts) [1]; (ii) the waves pass through mostly straight, with only slight spreading at the edges [1].

**7. (a)** A vibrating straight bar (dipper) touches the water surface [1]; a lamp above the tank projects the wavefronts onto a screen or the floor below [1].
**(b)** 8 wavefronts have 7 gaps [1]; λ = 21 ÷ 7 = **3.0 cm** [1]
**(c)** v = 8.0 × 0.030 [1] = **0.24 m/s** [1]
*Examiner insight:* 21 ÷ 8 = 2.6 cm is the common error in (b).

**8. (a)** (i) Move the end from side to side, at right angles to the spring [1]; (ii) push and pull the end backwards and forwards along the spring [1].
**(b)** Transverse: at right angles to propagation [1]; longitudinal: parallel to propagation [1].
**(c)** P-waves: longitudinal [1]; S-waves: transverse [1].

**9. (a)** λ = 0.30 ÷ 10 = **0.030 m** (3.0 cm) [1]
**(b)** λ = v/f = 0.20 ÷ 10 [1] = **0.020 m** (2.0 cm) [1]
**(c)** Frequency [1]
**(d)** They change direction (refract) [1].

**10. (a)** Place a straight (plane) barrier in the water [1]; send straight waves towards it and observe the reflected wavefronts on the screen [1].
**(b)** Any two: speed; wavelength; frequency [1 each]

**11. (a)** The 20 m waves [1]; their wavelength is the same as the gap width, so they diffract much more than the 2.0 m waves [1].
**(b)** The same (wavelength unchanged) [1].
**(c)** Waves diffract round the edge of the hill [1]; longer wavelengths diffract more at an edge, so they bend down into the valley [1].

**12. (a)** The waves do not slow down, and their wavelength stays the same [1]; they spread out (diffract) after the gap [1].
**(b)** λ = v/f = 340 ÷ 256 [1] = **1.3 m** [1]
**(c)** The wavelength (about 1.3 m) is similar to the width of the doorway [1]; so the sound diffracts strongly and spreads out into the room [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Measuring amplitude from crest to trough.
- Dividing by the number of wavefronts rather than the number of gaps.
- Leaving MHz or cm unconverted in v = fλ.
- Saying frequency changes in refraction.
- *(Extended)* Saying a wider gap gives more spreading.
