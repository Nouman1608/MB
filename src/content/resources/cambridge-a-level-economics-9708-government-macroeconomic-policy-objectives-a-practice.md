## Section A

**1.** Explain why governments aim for stability of the current account rather than a zero balance. **[2]**

**2.** Which statement best describes sustainability as a macroeconomic objective?

A: increasing real GDP every year

B: meeting present needs without reducing the ability of future generations to meet theirs

C: keeping government spending equal to tax revenue

D: keeping inflation at zero **[1]**

**3.** Real GDP grows by 3.5% and population grows by 1.8%. Calculate the growth of real GDP per head. **[2]**

**4.** Explain one reason why a government may aim for a low positive rate of inflation rather than zero inflation. **[2]**

**5.** Which indicator would best show a government's progress towards redistribution of income?

A: the growth rate of real GDP

B: the Gini coefficient

C: the current account balance

D: the consumer price index **[1]**

**6.** Distinguish between economic growth and economic development. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** The table shows data for two countries.

| Indicator | Country R | Country S |
|---|---|---|
| Inflation (target 2%) | 1.2% | 11.0% |
| Unemployment (natural rate 5%) | 9.0% | 3.5% |
| Real GDP growth (capacity growth in brackets) | 0.4% (2.5%) | 7.0% (4.0%) |
| Current account balance | +1% of GDP | −8% of GDP |
| Gini coefficient | 0.31 | 0.52 |

**(a)** Identify the most pressing macroeconomic objective in each country, using the data to explain your choice. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why the same change in aggregate demand would not suit both countries. **[2]**

**8.** Country T's real GDP grows by 5% in a year, and carbon emissions per unit of GDP fall by 2%.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in total emissions. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the fall in emissions per unit of GDP that would keep total emissions unchanged. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the objective of sustainability may conflict with the objective of economic growth. **[2]**

**9.** Explain how achieving low unemployment may help a government's objective of redistributing income. **[3]**

**10.** Explain why a low-income country may give development a higher priority than current account stability. **[3]**

**11.** Evaluate whether economic growth should be a government's most important macroeconomic objective. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Small or temporary imbalances are normal as economies move through booms and slowdowns, and a deficit used to import capital goods can raise future output [1]; the problem is a large, persistent imbalance relative to GDP, such as a deficit that is hard to finance [1].

**2.** **B** [1]. A describes growth, C a balanced budget and D an unrealistic inflation target.

**3.** 1.035 ÷ 1.018 = 1.0167 [1], so real GDP per head grows by about **1.7%** [1].

**4.** For example, zero measured inflation risks deflation if demand falls [1]; with falling prices, consumers may delay spending and the real burden of debt rises, reducing AD and output [1]. (Measurement bias in price indices, or flexibility for real wages, is also accepted.)

**5.** **B** [1]. The Gini coefficient measures inequality in the distribution of income.

**6.** Economic growth is an increase in real GDP, or in the economy's productive capacity [1]; economic development is a wider improvement in living standards, including health, education, freedom of choice and reduced poverty [1].

**7.** **(a)** Country R: low unemployment (and growth) is most pressing [1], because unemployment is 4 points above the natural rate and growth is well below capacity growth, showing weak demand and spare capacity [1]. Country S: price stability is most pressing [1], because inflation is more than five times the target, with growth above capacity and unemployment below the natural rate, showing an overheating economy; the large deficit supports this [1].

**(b)** Country R needs AD to rise to use its spare capacity and reduce unemployment, while Country S needs AD to grow more slowly to reduce inflation and its deficit [1]; a policy that raised AD would worsen inflation in S, and one that reduced AD would raise unemployment further in R [1].

**8.** **(a)** 1.05 × 0.98 − 1 = 0.029, a **rise of 2.9%** [1].

**(b)** 1 − 1 ÷ 1.05 = 0.0476, a **fall of about 4.8%** a year [1].

**(c)** More output usually needs more energy and raw materials, so emissions and resource use rise unless production methods change quickly [1]; policies to protect the environment, such as limits on pollution, can raise firms' costs and slow growth, at least in the short run [1].

**9.** Low unemployment means more people earn wages rather than relying on low benefits [1], so the incomes of the poorest households rise [1]. Tight labour markets also raise wages for low-skilled workers, narrowing the gap between high and low incomes and reducing the need for transfers [1].

**10.** In a low-income country, many people lack basic health care, education and adequate incomes, so improvements in these raise welfare greatly [1]. A moderate current account deficit may be the result of importing machinery and infrastructure that support development [1], and it can be acceptable as long as it is financed and does not grow too large, so development takes priority [1].

**11.** For growth as the most important: rising real output raises incomes and living standards [1]; it creates jobs, helping the unemployment objective [1]; it raises tax revenue, which can fund health, education and redistribution [1].

Against: growth above capacity causes inflation and a worse current account [1]; growth can damage the environment, so it may not be sustainable [1]; the gains may be unevenly shared, raising inequality, so growth does not guarantee development [1]; in a country with very high inflation, price stability must come first, because high inflation itself harms investment and growth [1].

Judgement: sustained, inclusive growth close to capacity is a strong candidate for the most important long-run objective, but its importance depends on the country's circumstances, and in the short run another objective, such as price stability, may need priority [1].
