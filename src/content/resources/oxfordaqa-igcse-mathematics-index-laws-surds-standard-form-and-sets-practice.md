## Questions

**1.** Simplify each expression, giving positive indices in your answers.

**(a)** 7c³ × 3c⁻⁵ **[2]**
**(b)** 20d⁶ ÷ 8d² **[2]**

**2.** **(a)** Work out the value of 2⁻⁴. **[1]**
**(b)** Work out the value of (3/2)⁻³ as a fraction. **[2]**
**(c)** Solve 3^(x+2) = 1/81. **[2]**

**3.** (Extension only) Work out the value of each, giving fractions where needed.

**(a)** 125^(2/3) **[2]**
**(b)** 16^(−3/4) **[2]**
**(c)** Simplify (49a⁶)^(3/2). **[2]**

**4.** Work out each, with the answer as a simplified fraction. Do not use decimals.

**(a)** 3⅛ − 1⅚ **[3]**
**(b)** (3/4 + 5/9) ÷ 1⅙ **[3]**

**5.** (Extension only)

**(a)** Show √288 can be written as a√2 and state the whole number a. **[1]**
**(b)** Simplify 5√18 − 2√50. **[3]**
**(c)** Expand and simplify (5 − √6)². **[2]**

**6.** (Extension only)

**(a)** Rationalise the denominator of 14/√7. **[2]**
**(b)** Write 12/(5 − √13) in the form p + √q, where p and q are integers. **[3]**
**(c)** Show that (2 + √5)/(√5 − 1) = (7 + 3√5)/4. **[4]**

**7.** (Extension only) A right-angled triangle has perpendicular sides of length 2√6 cm and 3√2 cm.

**(a)** Show that its area is 6√3 cm². **[3]**
**(b)** Find the exact length of the hypotenuse. **[2]**

**8.** **(a)** Write 0.000 0407 in standard form. **[1]**
**(b)** Write 3.6 × 10⁷ as an ordinary number. **[1]**
**(c)** Work out (2.5 × 10⁻³) × (6.4 × 10⁶), giving your answer in standard form. **[2]**
**(d)** Work out (9.6 × 10⁵) + (4.4 × 10⁶), giving your answer in standard form. **[2]**

**9.** A server holds 2.4 × 10¹⁴ bytes of data. Each stored file is 6 × 10⁵ bytes.

**(a)** How many files can the server hold? Give your answer in standard form. **[2]**
**(b)** A cell has a width of 1.2 × 10⁻⁷ m. A line of 5 × 10⁴ of these cells is laid end to end. Find the length of the line in metres, in standard form. **[2]**
**(c)** A calculator shows 3.15E-4 for another result. Write this in standard form and as an ordinary decimal. **[2]**

**10.** ξ = {integers from 1 to 30}. A = {factors of 30}. B = {multiples of 5}.

**(a)** List the elements of A ∩ B. **[2]**
**(b)** Find n(A ∪ B). **[2]**
**(c)** Find n(B′). **[1]**
**(d)** Find n((A ∪ B)′). **[1]**

**11.** Of 70 people at a gym, 30 use the treadmill (T), 28 use the rowing machine (R) and 34 use the weights (W). 10 use T and R, 12 use R and W, 11 use T and W, and 5 use all three.

**(a)** Draw a Venn diagram and write the number in each of the eight regions. **[4]**
**(b)** How many people use exactly one of the three? **[1]**
**(c)** How many people use the weights but not the rowing machine? **[2]**

**12.** In a class of 36 pupils, 20 study French (F), 15 study Spanish (S) and 5 study neither. x pupils study both.

**(a)** Form and solve an equation to find x. **[3]**
**(b)** How many pupils study French but not Spanish? **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Numbers: 7 × 3 = 21 [1]. Indices: 3 + (−5) = −2, so **21/c²** [1]
**(b)** 20 ÷ 8 = 5/2 [1]. 6 − 2 = 4, so **5d⁴/2** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the coefficient work and the index work as two separate lines, so a slip in one does not hide that the other was right.

**2. (a)** **1/16** [1]
**(b)** Flip the fraction: (2/3)³ [1] = **8/27** [1]
**(c)** 1/81 = 3⁻⁴ [1]. So x + 2 = −4 and **x = −6** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (c), writing the right-hand side as a power of 3 is the key line to show. A trial-and-error answer with no working is a risk on a two-mark question.

**3. (a)** 125^(1/3) = 5 [1]. 5² = **25** [1]
**(b)** 16^(1/4) = 2 [1]. Negative power: 2⁻³ = **1/8** [1]
**(c)** 49^(3/2) = 7³ = 343 [1]. (a⁶)^(3/2) = a⁹, so **343a⁹** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Taking the root first keeps the numbers small. Remember to apply the power to the letter as well as the number.

**4. (a)** 25/8 − 11/6 [1] = 75/24 − 44/24 [1] = **31/24** (or 1⁷⁄₂₄) [1]
**(b)** 3/4 + 5/9 = 27/36 + 20/36 = 47/36 [1]. 1⅙ = 7/6, so 47/36 × 6/7 [1] = 282/252 = **47/42** (or 1⁵⁄₄₂) [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* When the question says "fraction" and "no decimals", a rounded decimal gives away the exact answer. Converting the mixed numbers first is the line that shows your method.

**5. (a)** √288 = √(144 × 2) = **12√2** [1]
**(b)** 5√18 = 5 × 3√2 = 15√2 [1]. 2√50 = 2 × 5√2 = 10√2 [1]. Difference: **5√2** [1]
**(c)** 25 − 10√6 + 6 [1] = **31 − 10√6** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A common slip is squaring only the two terms and writing 25 + 6. Write all four products before collecting terms.

**6. (a)** Multiplying both parts by √7 gives 14√7/7 [1] = **2√7** [1]
**(b)** Use the conjugate: multiply numerator and denominator by (5 + √13) [1]. Denominator: 25 − 13 = 12 [1]. 12(5 + √13)/12 = **5 + √13** [1]
**(c)** Multiply numerator and denominator by (√5 + 1) [1]. Numerator: 2√5 + 2 + 5 + √5 [1]. Denominator: 5 − 1 = 4 [1]. Numerator = 7 + 3√5, so the fraction is **(7 + 3√5)/4** as required [1] **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* On a "show that" question, every line must be visible, including the expanded numerator and the denominator calculation. Stating the result without these lines is unlikely to be enough.

**7. (a)** Area = ½ × 2√6 × 3√2 [1] = 3√12 [1] = 3 × 2√3 = **6√3 cm²** [1]
**(b)** h² = (2√6)² + (3√2)² = 24 + 18 [1]. h = **√42 cm** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the answer exact. A decimal such as 6.48 does not show the surd the question asks for, and rounding early can change the final digits.

**8. (a)** **4.07 × 10⁻⁵** [1]
**(b)** **36 000 000** [1]
**(c)** 2.5 × 6.4 = 16 and 10⁻³ × 10⁶ = 10³ [1]. 16 × 10³ = **1.6 × 10⁴** [1]
**(d)** 9.6 × 10⁵ = 0.96 × 10⁶ [1]. 0.96 × 10⁶ + 4.4 × 10⁶ = **5.36 × 10⁶** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A result like 16 × 10³ is not yet in standard form. Check 1 ≤ A < 10 before you write the final answer.

**9. (a)** (2.4 ÷ 6) × 10¹⁴⁻⁵ = 0.4 × 10⁹ [1] = **4 × 10⁸ files** [1]
**(b)** 1.2 × 5 = 6 and 10⁻⁷ × 10⁴ = 10⁻³ [1]. Length = **6 × 10⁻³ m** [1]
**(c)** **3.15 × 10⁻⁴** [1], which is **0.000 315** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Include the unit in context questions. Copying "3.15E-4" as your final answer shows the display and not the number.

**10. (a)** A = {1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15, 30} and B = {5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30} [1]. A ∩ B = **{5, 10, 15, 30}** [1]
**(b)** n(A) = 8, n(B) = 6, n(A ∩ B) = 4 [1]. 8 + 6 − 4 = **10** [1]
**(c)** 30 − 6 = **24** [1]
**(d)** 30 − 10 = **20** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Listing the elements of both sets first makes later parts quick and checkable. A′ and B′ ask for what is outside, so subtract from the total.

**11. (a)** All three: 5 [1]. Pairs only: T and R = 5, R and W = 7, T and W = 6 [1]. Single sets: T only = 14, R only = 11, W only = 16 [1]. Outside: 70 − 64 = **6** [1]
**(b)** 14 + 11 + 16 = **41** [1]
**(c)** W only plus T and W only: 16 + 6 [1] = **22** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Begin in the centre and work outwards. Check that the eight regions add to 70 before answering parts (b) and (c).

**12. (a)** French only = 20 − x, Spanish only = 15 − x [1]. (20 − x) + x + (15 − x) + 5 = 36 [1]. 40 − x = 36, so **x = 4** [1]
**(b)** 20 − 4 = **16** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Label the regions in terms of x before forming the equation. Writing "20 + 15 + 5 = 36" without subtracting the overlap is the usual route to a wrong answer.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reading a negative index as a negative value, for example 2⁻⁴ = −16.
- Multiplying indices instead of adding them when multiplying powers of the same base.
- Applying a fractional power to the number but not to the letter.
- Rounding in the middle of a fraction or surd calculation when the question asks for an exact answer.
- Not simplifying a surd fully, or not rationalising the denominator when told to.
- Dropping the middle terms when squaring a bracket with a surd.
- Leaving a standard-form answer as 16 × 10³, or adding standard-form numbers without matching the powers of 10.
- Copying a calculator display (3.15E-4) instead of writing the full standard form.
- Counting the overlap twice in a set problem, or filling the single-set regions first.
