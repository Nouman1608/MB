These questions cover section 21.1 of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028: why bacteria are useful in biotechnology and genetic modification. Questions without a label test Core content. Questions labelled **(Extended)** test Supplement content, examined only on Papers 2 and 4. All numerical data are invented for practice.

Before you start, you may want the [section 21.1 study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-biotechnology-and-genetic-modification-cambridge-igcse-biology/) or the [section 21.1 revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-biotechnology-and-genetic-modification-cambridge-igcse-biology-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which feature of bacteria means that a large amount of product can be made in a short time?

A: they have a cell wall

B: they have a rapid reproduction rate

C: they have no nucleus

D: they respire anaerobically

**[1]**

**2.** State two reasons why bacteria are useful in biotechnology and genetic modification. **[2]**

**3.** Name the structure in a bacterial cell where proteins, such as enzymes, are made. **[1]**

**4.** **(Extended)** Which statement about plasmids is correct?

A: they are found inside the nucleus of a bacterium

B: they are the main circular DNA of a bacterium

C: they are small circular loops of DNA, separate from the main DNA

D: they are made of protein and carry no genes

**[1]**

**5.** A student wrote: "Bacteria are useful because they make penicillin and mycoprotein." Explain what is wrong with this statement. **[2]**

**6.** A culture of 400 bacteria doubles in number every 20 minutes. Calculate the number of bacteria after 2 hours, assuming no cells die. Show your working. **[2]**

**7.** **(Extended)** Give two reasons why growing and modifying bacteria raises few ethical concerns. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**8.** Bacteria were grown in a flask of nutrient broth at a constant temperature. The table shows the number of bacteria at intervals.

| Time / hours | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Number of bacteria | 1 000 | 4 000 | 16 000 | 64 000 | 128 000 |

**(a)** Calculate the doubling time of the bacteria between 0 and 3 hours. Show your working. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe how the pattern of growth between 3 and 4 hours differs from the pattern between 0 and 3 hours. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest two reasons for the change you described in (b). **[2]**

**9.** Explain why the rapid reproduction of bacteria means that all the cells in a fermenter can make the same product, if the culture was started from a single genetically modified cell. **[3]**

**10.** **(Extended)** Explain how the presence of plasmids makes bacteria useful in genetic modification. **[4]**

**11.** **(Extended)** A company wants to produce a human protein in large quantities. It considers two options: genetically modified bacteria or genetically modified goats that secrete the protein in their milk. Suggest why the company might choose the bacteria. **[4]**

**12.** A diagram of a bacterial cell shows a cell wall, cell membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes, a large loop of DNA and two small loops of DNA.

**(a)** Name the small loops of DNA. **[1]**

**(b)** State one structure found in an animal cell that is **not** found in this bacterial cell. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why a bacterium can make a human protein once the human gene has been inserted. **[2]**

**13.** **(Extended)** "Bacteria are useful in genetic modification only because they reproduce quickly." Discuss this statement. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Option D is true of some organisms used in biotechnology, such as yeast, but it is not the reason for producing a large amount of product quickly.

**2.** Rapid reproduction rate [1]; ability to make complex molecules [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* These are the two Core statements; Extended candidates may also give plasmids or few ethical concerns, but Core answers should use these two.

**3.** Ribosome(s) [1]. **[1]**

**4.** C [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Bacteria have no nucleus, so A is impossible; B describes the main circular DNA, not a plasmid.

**5.** Penicillin and mycoprotein are made by fungi, not bacteria [1]; bacteria are useful because of their rapid reproduction rate or ability to make complex molecules (accept a correct bacterial example, such as GM bacteria making insulin) [1]. **[2]**

**6.** 2 hours = 120 minutes, so 120 ÷ 20 = 6 doublings [1]; 400 × 2⁶ = 400 × 64 = **25 600** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Multiplying 400 by 6 (giving 2400) treats doubling as multiplying once by the number of intervals, which loses both marks.

**7.** Bacteria are single cells with no nervous system, so they do not feel pain or suffer [1]; their manipulation and growth raise far fewer objections than using animals, as they are not kept like farm or laboratory animals [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** The number multiplies by 4 each hour, which is two doublings an hour [1]; doubling time = **30 minutes** (0.5 hours) [1].

**(b)** Between 3 and 4 hours the number only doubles (it increases by a smaller factor), so growth has slowed [1].

**(c)** Any two: nutrients are being used up [1]; waste products are building up, which may be toxic or change the pH [1]; (also accept oxygen running short, or overcrowding/lack of space). **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a), 1000 to 64 000 is 64 times, which is 2⁶, so six doublings in 3 hours confirms 30 minutes. Showing either method earns the working mark.

**9.** Bacteria reproduce asexually [1]; so the offspring are genetically identical to the parent cell [1]; so every cell carries the inserted gene and can make the same product [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The key link is genetically identical offspring; "they reproduce quickly" alone explains the quantity, not why every cell makes the same product.

**10.** A plasmid can be cut open (with restriction enzymes) [1]; a gene, such as a human gene, can be inserted into it [1]; the recombinant plasmid can be put into a bacterium [1]; the plasmid is copied when the bacterium divides, so all offspring have the gene and make the protein [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming plasmids earns little on its own; each mark needs a step that shows what plasmids allow.

**11.** Bacteria reproduce much more rapidly than goats [1]; so a large quantity of protein can be made in a shorter time [1]; bacteria contain plasmids, which make it straightforward to insert the human gene [1]; modifying and growing bacteria raises fewer ethical concerns than modifying and keeping goats [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Suggest" rewards applying the four features to the comparison; each feature should be compared with the goats, not just stated.

**12. (a)** Plasmids [1].

**(b)** Nucleus [1] (accept mitochondria).

**(c)** A gene is a length of DNA that codes for a protein [1]; the bacterium's ribosomes use the gene's base sequence to build the protein (so it makes the human protein) [1]. **[4]**

**13.** Agree: rapid reproduction is important, because a large population and a large amount of product are produced in a short time [1]. Disagree, because other features also matter: bacteria can make complex molecules such as proteins [1]; they contain plasmids, into which a gene can be inserted [1]; the plasmid is copied when bacteria divide, so the gene passes to all offspring [1]; there are few ethical concerns over their manipulation and growth [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The word "only" invites a balanced answer. Accept a conclusion either way, but full marks need at least one reason the statement is partly true and the other features explained.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating doubling as steady addition, or forgetting to convert hours to minutes.
- Naming fungal products (penicillin, mycoprotein) as bacterial ones.
- Placing plasmids or inserted genes in a nucleus.
- Listing "plasmids" or "ethics" with no reason attached.
- Writing "no ethical concerns" instead of "few".
- Forgetting the link from asexual reproduction to genetically identical offspring.
