## Specification in brief

- Electrophilic addition of **HBr, H₂SO₄ and Br₂**; outline the mechanisms.
- **Bromine** as the test for unsaturation.
- **Major and minor products** from unsymmetrical alkenes, explained by **primary, secondary and tertiary carbocation** stability.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Electrophilic addition | Electrophile attacks C=C; two new single bonds form; one product (for symmetrical cases) |
| Carbocation | Ion with a positively charged carbon atom; the intermediate in these mechanisms |
| Primary / secondary / tertiary carbocation | C⁺ bonded to one / two / three other carbon atoms |
| Inductive (electron-releasing) effect | Alkyl groups push electron density towards C⁺, spreading the charge |
| Unsymmetrical alkene | Alkene whose two C=C carbons carry different groups |
| Major product | Product from the more stable carbocation; formed in greater amount |
| Alkyl hydrogensulfate | Product of adding H₂SO₄: R–OSO₂OH |
| Induced dipole | Polarity created in Br₂ by the electron density of C=C |

## Recall: the general mechanism

**Step 1:** arrow C=C → δ+ atom of electrophile; arrow from the electrophile's internal bond → δ− atom. Carbocation + anion form.

**Step 2:** arrow from an anion lone pair → C⁺.

| Reagent | Conditions | Example equation | Product |
|---|---|---|---|
| HBr | room temperature | CH₂=CH₂ + HBr → CH₃CH₂Br | bromoethane |
| H₂SO₄ | cold, concentrated | CH₂=CH₂ + H₂SO₄ → CH₃CH₂OSO₂OH | ethyl hydrogensulfate |
| Br₂ | room temperature | CH₂=CH₂ + Br₂ → CH₂BrCH₂Br | 1,2-dibromoethane |

## Recall: major and minor products

1. Draw the two carbocations (H on either C=C carbon).
2. Classify each by counting carbons on C⁺.
3. **Tertiary > secondary > primary** in stability.
4. More stable carbocation → forms more readily → **major** product.

| Alkene + reagent | Major product | Minor product |
|---|---|---|
| propene + HBr | 2-bromopropane (secondary C⁺) | 1-bromopropane (primary C⁺) |
| but-1-ene + HBr | 2-bromobutane | 1-bromobutane |
| 2-methylpropene + HBr | 2-bromo-2-methylpropane (tertiary C⁺) | 1-bromo-2-methylpropane (primary C⁺) |
| 2-methylbut-2-ene + H₂SO₄ | (CH₃)₂C(OSO₂OH)CH₂CH₃ (tertiary C⁺) | (CH₃)₂CHCH(OSO₂OH)CH₃ (secondary C⁺) |
| pent-2-ene + HBr | 2-bromopentane and 3-bromopentane in similar amounts (both secondary C⁺) | — |
| any alkene + Br₂ | one product (both added atoms are Br) | — |

### Worked reminder: 2-methylbut-1-ene and HBr

- Alkene: CH₂=C(CH₃)CH₂CH₃.
- H adds to C1 → C⁺ on C2, bonded to three carbons → **tertiary** → major product **2-bromo-2-methylbutane**, (CH₃)₂CBrCH₂CH₃.
- H adds to C2 → C⁺ on C1, bonded to one carbon → **primary** → minor product **1-bromo-2-methylbutane**, BrCH₂CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃.

### Mechanism checklist (tick each in your answer)

- Dipole on the reagent (permanent for HBr and H₂SO₄, induced for Br₂).
- Arrow from the C=C bond to the δ+ atom.
- Arrow from the reagent's own bond to its δ− atom.
- Carbocation drawn with + on the correct carbon.
- Arrow from a lone pair on the anion to C⁺.
- Final product with both added groups on adjacent carbons.

## Recall: the bromine test

- Reagent: bromine water (orange). Shake with the sample.
- C=C present: **orange → colourless**.
- Saturated: **stays orange**.
- 1 mol Br₂ reacts with 1 mol C=C, so the volume of a bromine solution used can measure the amount of alkene.

## Exam traps

- The first arrow starts on **the double bond**.
- Show **δ+/δ−** on HBr and an **induced** dipole on Br₂.
- H₂SO₄ bonds through **O**, never S.
- "More **stable**", not "more reactive", carbocation.
- When asked for the major product, still **explain** using both carbocations.
- Bromine water goes **colourless**, not clear.
- Primary carbocation: C⁺ bonded to **one** carbon, even if the molecule is large.

## Self-test

1. Name the electrophile in the reaction of HBr with an alkene.
2. Explain why Br₂ can act as an electrophile.
3. Where does the first curly arrow start in electrophilic addition?
4. Give the conditions for adding sulfuric acid to an alkene.
5. Name the product of cyclohexene and bromine.
6. Give the structure of the product of ethene and sulfuric acid.
7. Put primary, secondary and tertiary carbocations in order of increasing stability.
8. Why are tertiary carbocations more stable than primary ones?
9. Name the major product of 2-methylpropene with HBr.
10. Describe the test for unsaturation and the result for a saturated compound.
11. Why does propene give two products with HBr but only one with Br₂?
12. 25.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol dm⁻³ bromine solution reacts exactly with a sample of hex-1-ene. Calculate the mass of hex-1-ene (Mr 84.0).

### Answers

1. The **δ+ hydrogen** of HBr (or H⁺).
2. The C=C electron density **repels the electrons in Br–Br**, inducing a dipole; the nearer Br becomes δ+.
3. At the **C=C double bond**.
4. **Cold, concentrated** sulfuric acid.
5. **1,2-Dibromocyclohexane.**
6. **CH₃CH₂OSO₂OH** (ethyl hydrogensulfate).
7. **Primary < secondary < tertiary.**
8. A tertiary carbocation has **three electron-releasing alkyl groups**, which spread the positive charge more.
9. **2-Bromo-2-methylpropane.**
10. Shake with bromine water: C=C turns it **orange to colourless**; a saturated compound **stays orange**.
11. With HBr the two added atoms differ (H and Br), so two different carbocations lead to **two products**; with Br₂ both added atoms are Br, so **one product** forms either way.
12. n(Br₂) = 0.00500 mol = n(hex-1-ene); mass = 0.00500 × 84.0 = **0.420 g**.
