## Questions

**1.** Define the term "entrepreneur". **[2]**

**2.** Which one of the following is an example of adapting an existing product? **[1]**
A. A firm launches a device no one has sold before. B. A bakery adds a gluten-free version of its best-selling loaf. C. A shop closes for the summer. D. A firm reduces its staff.

**3.** Hollin Pottery sells 260 bowls a month at £18 each. Its total costs are £3,900 a month.
**(a)** Calculate monthly revenue. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate monthly profit. **[2]**

**4.** Greylag Video Hire has seen the number of DVD rentals fall each year.

| Year | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rentals | 4,800 | 4,100 | 3,500 | 3,000 |

**(a)** Identify the year with the largest fall in rentals compared with the year before. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in rentals from 2022 to 2025. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one reason why DVD hire may be becoming obsolete. **[2]**

**5.** Give two reasons why new business ideas come about. **[2]**

**6.** Explain one risk and one reward of starting a small business. **[4]**

**7.** Tarn and Teal is launching a range of reusable water bottles in a market with many similar products. Analyse how branding could add value for Tarn and Teal. **[6]**

**8.** A bakery buys £0.62 of ingredients for each loaf and sells it for £4.20.
**(a)** Calculate the difference between the selling price and the cost of ingredients. **[1]**
**(b)** Express this difference as a percentage of the selling price, to 1 decimal place. **[2]**
**(c)** Give one way, other than branding, that the bakery could add value. **[1]**

**9.** Tomasz rents a unit, hires two cooks and buys an oven. He chooses to add a vegan menu to his pizza takeaway and puts £15,000 of his own savings into the business. Analyse how Tomasz shows the three roles of an entrepreneur. **[6]**

**10.** Idris has two options and can pursue only one. Option A is an original idea, a sensor that tells owners when a houseplant needs water. Year 1 forecast: revenue £36,000, costs £44,000. Option B adapts an existing service by adding plant delivery to his flower shop. Year 1 forecast: revenue £30,000, costs £22,000. Justify which option Idris should choose. **[6]**

**11.** Maya earns a salary of £25,000 a year. She is thinking of leaving to run a coffee van. Her annual costs would be £34,000. If sales go well, revenue would be £48,000. If sales go badly, revenue would be £30,000. Evaluate whether Maya should leave her job to run the coffee van. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** An entrepreneur is a person who sets up and runs a business [1], organising resources, making business decisions and taking risks [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition gains from describing what the person does, rather than only saying they "own a business".

**2.** **B** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In a multiple-choice item, check each option against the term. Option A is an original idea, which is the contrast.

**3. (a)** Revenue = price × quantity [1] = 18 × 260 = **£4,680** [1]
**(b)** Profit = revenue − total costs [1] = 4,680 − 3,900 = **£780** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the formula and the substitution. Working lets the marker follow your method even if the arithmetic slips.

**4. (a)** **2023** (a fall of 700, compared with 600 in 2024 and 500 in 2025) [1]
**(b)** (3,000 − 4,800) ÷ 4,800 × 100 [1] = **−37.5%**, a fall of 37.5% [1]
**(c)** Streaming and downloads let customers watch films at home without visiting a shop [1], so fewer people want DVDs and demand for hire falls [1].
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the starting figure, and say whether the change is a rise or a fall. For (c), a reason linked to what customers now do is stronger than the word "technology" alone.

**5.** Changes in technology [1]; changes in what consumers want (or products and services becoming obsolete) [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Give" asks for recall, so no development is needed. Two different reasons, not one reason in two forms, are expected.

**6.** Risk: financial loss, because costs may exceed revenue [1], so the owner could lose the money invested [1]. Reward: independence, because the owner chooses how to work [1], so they have no boss to answer to and can shape the business [1].
*Examiner insight:* Each point is stronger with a short expansion. A bare label such as "profit" gives less to reward.

**7.** Branding means the name, logo and image by which customers know and trust a product [1]. In a crowded market, a well-known brand helps Tarn and Teal stand out from similar bottles [1]. Customers who trust the brand may be willing to pay a higher price for the same bottle [1], which raises revenue per sale [1]. A recognisable brand can also bring repeat purchases and recommendations [1], which helps a new firm grow even with little advertising money. This adds value because the product is worth more to the customer than the unbranded alternative [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Analyse" asks you to place the answer in the case. Refer to the reusable bottles and the crowded market, not to branding in general.

**8. (a)** 4.20 − 0.62 = **£3.58** [1]
**(b)** 3.58 ÷ 4.20 × 100 [1] = **85.2%** [1]
**(c)** Quality (for example better ingredients), convenience (delivery), design (distinctive shape) or a unique selling point [1] (any one).
*Examiner insight:* The difference is not profit, because wages and rent still need paying. Avoid calling it profit. Give the percentage to the accuracy requested.

**9.** Organising resources: renting the unit, hiring two cooks and buying the oven bring premises, labour and equipment together [1]. This is needed for the takeaway to produce pizza [1]. Making decisions: choosing to add a vegan menu is a decision about the product range [1], which may attract customers who want plant-based food [1]. Taking risks: using £15,000 of his own savings means that money could be lost if sales are weak [1], so he has no guarantee of getting it back [1].
*Examiner insight:* Pair each role with a detail from the case and add a consequence. Listing the three roles without the case detail leaves the answer undeveloped.

**10.** Option A forecasts a loss of 36,000 − 44,000 = £8,000 [1]. It is original, so there may be no rivals, but demand is untested and the loss shows the cost of developing it [1]. Option B forecasts a profit of 30,000 − 22,000 = £8,000 [1]. It adapts what the shop already does, so demand from existing customers is more likely [1]. I recommend Option B [1], because a forecast profit lowers the risk of financial loss and failure for a business that is already running, while A could still pay off later if the sensor sold well [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Justify" asks for a recommendation of one of the two options, so commit to one and use the figures. Mentioning what is risky about the other option strengthens it.

**11.** Best case: profit = 48,000 − 34,000 = £14,000 [1]. Poor case: 30,000 − 34,000 = a loss of £4,000 [1]. Reward: independence and the chance of building a successful business [1]. Even the best-case profit is £11,000 less than her current salary of £25,000 [1]. Risk: a poor year gives a financial loss and no guaranteed income, which is a lack of security [1]. Continued losses could lead to business failure [1]. Which outcome is likelier is not stated, so the forecasts should be tested, for example through market research [1]. Judgement: on these figures, Maya should not leave yet [1], because the best profit is below her salary and the downside is a loss. She could try the van part-time first, or leave if independence matters more to her than income and she can afford to lose £4,000 [1].
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation answer that weighs both sides and then decides, with a reason, tends to score better than a list. Using the figures from the case is important.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "profit" as a reward with no explanation of why it matters to the owner.
- Mixing up revenue and profit, or forgetting to subtract total costs.
- Using the new figure rather than the starting figure as the divisor in a percentage change.
- Describing an obsolete product as one that is broken or unpopular rather than out of date and replaced.
- Listing the three roles of an entrepreneur without a detail from the case.
- Treating value added as the same as profit.
- Describing branding in general terms without tying it to the firm named in the question.
- Stopping an "evaluate" or "justify" answer before reaching a decision.
