## Questions

**1.** State the three headings under which IAS7 classifies cash flows. **[3]**

**2.** Explain two differences between a rights issue and a bonus issue. **[4]**

**3.** Orrin Valves Ltd has 800,000 ordinary shares of £0.50 each. It makes a 1 for 5 rights issue at £1.10 per share, fully taken up. Calculate the number of new shares, the cash received and the amounts credited to share capital and share premium. **[3]**

**4.** Pentland Weave Ltd has 400,000 ordinary shares of £0.25 each and a share premium of £60,000. It makes a 1 for 4 bonus issue using the share premium.

**(a)** Calculate the amount transferred to share capital and the share premium remaining. **[2]**
**(b)** State the effect of the bonus issue on total equity and on cash. **[2]**

**5.** Kestrel Freight Ltd owns a building that cost £250,000. Accumulated depreciation is £40,000. The building is revalued to £300,000.

**(a)** Calculate the revaluation surplus. **[2]**
**(b)** Prepare the journal entries to record the revaluation (narratives not required). **[3]**
**(c)** The remaining useful life is 25 years. Calculate the annual depreciation from next year, using the straight-line method. **[1]**

**6.** Corran Ltd's tax charge for the year is £21,300. Tax payable was £17,800 at the start of the year and £21,300 at the end. Its 7% debentures of £90,000 were in issue all year. Accrued debenture interest was £1,575 at the start of the year and £3,150 at the end. Calculate the tax paid and the debenture interest paid. **[4]**

**7.** For each item, state where it appears in a statement of cash flows prepared using the indirect method, and whether it is added or deducted: depreciation; profit on sale of equipment; proceeds from sale of equipment; increase in trade receivables; repayment of debentures; a bonus issue. **[6]**

**8.** Brindle Cartons Ltd's delivery vehicles at 1 January 2025: cost £140,000; accumulated depreciation £56,000. During 2025 it sold a vehicle that cost £24,000 (accumulated depreciation £15,000) for £7,200, and bought a vehicle for £36,000. Depreciation is 20% a year on the cost of vehicles held at the year end.

**(a)** Prepare the schedule of non-current assets for delivery vehicles for the year ended 31 December 2025. **[7]**
**(b)** Calculate the profit or loss on disposal. **[2]**

**9.** Information for Saltmere Ltd for the year ended 30 June 2026:

| | £ |
|---|---:|
| Profit from operations | 74,800 |
| Depreciation charge | 31,600 |
| Profit on disposal of equipment | 1,400 |
| Proceeds from sale of equipment | 6,900 |
| Purchase of non-current assets | 88,000 |
| Debenture interest charge | 4,200 |
| Tax charge | 13,600 |
| Dividends paid | 35,000 |

| | 1 July 2025 £ | 30 June 2026 £ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 52,000 | 47,500 |
| Trade receivables | 38,200 | 45,700 |
| Trade payables | 29,400 | 24,900 |
| Accrued debenture interest | 0 | 1,050 |
| Tax payable | 11,900 | 13,600 |
| Cash and cash equivalents | 8,300 | 34,650 |

During the year Saltmere issued 40,000 ordinary shares of £1 at £1.50 each, made a bonus issue of £50,000 from share premium and revalued land upwards by £40,000.

Prepare the statement of cash flows for the year ended 30 June 2026 using the indirect method. **[12]**

**10.** Halberd Tools Ltd has asked a supplier for £40,000 of trade credit. The supplier plans to decide using only Halberd's latest published accounts. Evaluate this approach. **[6]**

**11.** Explain two reasons why international accounting standards are important to investors. **[4]**

**12.** State the purpose of each element of a company's published accounts: the auditor's report; the notes to the accounts; the statement of cash flows; the directors' report. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Operating activities [1]; investing activities [1]; financing activities [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the IAS7 headings exactly; "trading" or "capital" activities will not earn the mark.

**2.** A rights issue raises cash, because shareholders pay for the shares [1], so total equity increases [1]. A bonus issue raises no cash: shares are issued free [1] by converting reserves into share capital, so total equity is unchanged [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* For "explain", develop each difference by giving its effect (on cash or equity); a bare list of features is weaker.

**3.** New shares 800,000 / 5 = 160,000; cash 160,000 x £1.10 = **£176,000** [1]. Share capital 160,000 x £0.50 = **£80,000** [1]. Share premium 160,000 x £0.60 = **£96,000** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Check that capital plus premium equals the cash received; £80,000 + £96,000 = £176,000.

**4. (a)** New shares 400,000 / 4 = 100,000; transfer 100,000 x £0.25 = **£25,000** [1]. Share premium remaining 60,000 - 25,000 = **£35,000** [1].
**(b)** Total equity: **no change** [1]. Cash: **no effect** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The transfer is at nominal value, never at market price.

**5. (a)** Carrying amount 250,000 - 40,000 = £210,000 [1]; surplus 300,000 - 210,000 = **£90,000** [1].
**(b)** Dr Building £50,000 [1]; Dr Provision for depreciation £40,000 [1]; Cr Revaluation reserve £90,000 [1].
**(c)** 300,000 / 25 = **£12,000** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Crediting the surplus to the income statement or retained earnings is a common slip; it belongs in the revaluation reserve.

**6.** Tax paid = 17,800 + 21,300 - 21,300 [1] = **£17,800** [1]. Interest charge 7% x 90,000 = £6,300 [1]; interest paid 1,575 + 6,300 - 3,150 = **£4,725** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Write out opening + charge - closing, so your method is visible even if a figure is misread.

**7.** Depreciation: operating, added back [1]. Profit on sale: operating, deducted [1]. Proceeds: investing, inflow [1]. Increase in receivables: operating, deducted [1]. Repayment of debentures: financing, outflow [1]. Bonus issue: not shown, as no cash is involved [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In this answer each mark needs both the heading and the direction (added, deducted, inflow, outflow).

**8. (a)**

| Delivery vehicles | £ |
|---|---:|
| Cost at 1 January 2025 | 140,000 |
| Additions | 36,000 |
| Disposals | (24,000) |
| Cost at 31 December 2025 | 152,000 |
| Depreciation at 1 January 2025 | 56,000 |
| Charge for the year | 30,400 |
| On disposals | (15,000) |
| Depreciation at 31 December 2025 | 71,400 |
| Carrying amount at 1 January 2025 | 84,000 |
| Carrying amount at 31 December 2025 | 80,600 |

Additions and disposals at cost [1]; closing cost **£152,000** [1]; charge 20% x 152,000 = **£30,400** [1]; depreciation on disposal £15,000 deducted [1]; closing depreciation **£71,400** [1]; opening carrying amount £84,000 [1]; closing carrying amount **£80,600** [1].
**(b)** Carrying amount 24,000 - 15,000 = £9,000 [1]; loss = 9,000 - 7,200 = **£1,800** [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Base the charge on the closing cost, after the addition and the disposal; charging 20% on the opening £140,000 gives £28,000 and throws out every closing figure.

Depreciation added and profit on disposal deducted [1]; inventory +£4,500 [1]; receivables (£7,500) [1]; payables (£4,500) [1]; cash generated from operations **£97,500** [1]; interest paid £3,150 [1]; tax paid £11,900 [1]; net operating cash **£82,450** [1]; net investing **(£81,100)** [1]; share issue £60,000 [1]; dividends paid (£35,000), with bonus issue and revaluation excluded [1]; net increase **£26,350** reconciling £8,300 to **£34,650** [1]. **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* Including the £50,000 bonus issue or £40,000 revaluation loses marks and stops the closing balance from agreeing.

**10.** For: published accounts are audited, so they are more reliable [1]; the supplier can calculate liquidity, such as the current ratio and trade payable days, to judge whether Halberd pays on time [1]; the statement of cash flows shows whether operations generate cash [1]. Against: the figures are historical and may be months old, so liquidity may have changed [1]; the year-end position may be window dressed, and payment records with other suppliers are not shown [1]. Judgement: useful as a starting point, but the supplier should also seek trade references or a credit rating and start with a lower limit [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Points must relate to a supplier's concern (being paid); generic limitations with no link to the question score less.

**11.** Comparability: companies, including those in different countries, use the same rules [1], so investors can compare performance fairly when choosing where to invest [1]. Reliability: fewer choices of method limit creative accounting [1], so investors can have more confidence in reported profit and assets [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each reason needs a consequence for the investor to earn its second mark.

**12.** Auditor's report: an independent opinion on whether the accounts give a true and fair view [1]. Notes: explain and break down the figures, including accounting policies [1]. Statement of cash flows: shows where cash came from and how it was used [1]. Directors' report: the directors' account of the company's year [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Gives information" is too vague to earn a mark; say what the element tells the reader.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Proceeds from a sale added in operating activities instead of the profit or loss on disposal.
- Signs reversed on working-capital changes.
- Tax or interest charge used as the amount paid.
- Bonus issues and revaluations shown as cash flows.
- Bonus shares valued at market price instead of nominal value.
- Revaluation surplus taken to the income statement.
- Depreciation on disposals not removed in the schedule of non-current assets.
- Evaluation answers with no judgement, or with limitations not linked to the stakeholder named.
