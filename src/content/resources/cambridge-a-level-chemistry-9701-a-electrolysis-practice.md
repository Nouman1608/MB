These questions cover subtopic 24.1 (Electrolysis) of Cambridge International AS & A Level
Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027 series. Use F = 9.65 × 10⁴ C mol⁻¹, L = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹,
e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C and a molar gas volume of 24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹ at room conditions. Revise first

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State the relationship between the Faraday constant, the Avogadro constant and the charge
on the electron, and use it to calculate a value for the Faraday constant from the values
above. **[2]**

**2.** Dilute aqueous potassium bromide is electrolysed using graphite electrodes. What is formed
at the anode?

A: oxygen

B: bromine

C: hydrogen

D: potassium

**[1]**

**3.** What quantity of charge is needed to produce 0.500 mol of oxygen gas by electrolysis?

A: 4.83 × 10⁴ C

B: 9.65 × 10⁴ C

C: 1.93 × 10⁵ C

D: 3.86 × 10⁵ C

**[1]**

**4.** Explain why hydrogen, not magnesium, is formed at the cathode when aqueous magnesium
sulfate is electrolysed. Refer to electrode potentials in your answer. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why the main anode product is chlorine when concentrated NaCl(aq) is electrolysed,
but oxygen when very dilute NaCl(aq) is electrolysed. (E⦵: Cl₂/Cl⁻ +1.36 V;
O₂ + 4H⁺/2H₂O +1.23 V) **[3]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** Molten lead(II) bromide is electrolysed with inert electrodes. A current of 0.600 A flows
for 20.0 minutes. (A<sub>r</sub>: Pb = 207.2)

**(a)** Write the half-equations for the reactions at the cathode and at the anode. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of lead formed. **[3]**

**7.** Aqueous copper(II) sulfate is electrolysed with platinum electrodes. A current of 1.20 A
flows for 25.0 minutes. (A<sub>r</sub>: Cu = 63.5)

**(a)** Calculate the mass of copper deposited. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the volume of oxygen formed at room conditions. **[2]**

**(c)** State and explain how the pH of the solution changes. **[1]**

**8.** A metal M (A<sub>r</sub> = 52.0) is deposited from an aqueous solution of one of its salts.
A current of 0.300 A for 20.0 minutes deposits 0.0647 g of M. Deduce the charge on the metal
ion, showing your working. **[3]**

**9.** A student determines the Avogadro constant using copper electrodes in CuSO₄(aq). A
constant current of 0.500 A flows for 25.0 minutes, and the anode loses 0.240 g.
(A<sub>r</sub>: Cu = 63.5)

**(a)** Calculate a value for the Avogadro constant. **[3]**

**(b)** Suggest two sources of error in this experiment. **[2]**

**10.** Describe how the electrodes should be treated before and after the electrolysis in the
experiment in Question 9, and give a reason for each step. **[3]**

**11.** Two cells are connected in series. Cell 1 contains AgNO₃(aq) and cell 2 contains
CuSO₄(aq); both have inert electrodes. After some time, 0.540 g of silver is deposited in
cell 1. (A<sub>r</sub>: Ag = 107.9, Cu = 63.5)

**(a)** Calculate the mass of copper deposited in cell 2. **[2]**

**(b)** The current was 0.200 A. Calculate how long the current flowed. **[2]**

**12.** Predict the products at the cathode and at the anode for each electrolysis, using inert
electrodes.

**(a)** aqueous silver nitrate **[2]**

**(b)** aqueous potassium iodide **[2]**

**(c)** concentrated hydrochloric acid **[2]**

**(d)** molten magnesium chloride **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** F = Le [1]. F = 6.022 × 10²³ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = **9.64 × 10⁴ C mol⁻¹** [1].

**2.** **B** [1]. Br₂/Br⁻ (+1.07 V) is less positive than O₂/H₂O (+1.23 V), so bromide is
oxidised in preference to water even when dilute.

**3.** **C** [1]. Each O₂ needs 4 electrons, so 0.500 mol O₂ needs 2.00 mol of electrons:
2.00 × 96 500 = 1.93 × 10⁵ C.

**4.** Mg²⁺/Mg has a very negative E⦵ (much more negative than that for water/hydrogen) [1], so
water (or H⁺) is more easily reduced than Mg²⁺ and hydrogen is released [1].

**5.** The E⦵ values are close, so concentration decides which is oxidised [1]. In concentrated
solution the high [Cl⁻] makes the Cl₂/Cl⁻ potential less positive, so chloride is oxidised in
preference to water [1]. In dilute solution [Cl⁻] is too low, and water is oxidised to oxygen,
as the standard values predict [1].

**6. (a)** Cathode: Pb²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Pb [1]. Anode: 2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻ [1].

**(b)** Q = 0.600 × 1200 = 720 C [1]. mol e⁻ = 720 ÷ 96 500 = 7.46 × 10⁻³; mol Pb =
3.73 × 10⁻³ [1]. Mass = 3.73 × 10⁻³ × 207.2 = **0.773 g** [1].

**7. (a)** Q = 1.20 × 1500 = 1800 C [1]. mol e⁻ = 0.018 65; mol Cu = 0.009 33 [1].
Mass = 0.009 33 × 63.5 = **0.592 g** [1].

**(b)** mol O₂ = 0.018 65 ÷ 4 = 0.004 66 [1]. Volume = 0.004 66 × 24.0 = 0.112 dm³ =
**112 cm³** [1].

**(c)** pH falls, because H⁺ ions are produced at the anode (2H₂O → O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻) [1].

**8.** Q = 0.300 × 1200 = 360 C; mol e⁻ = 360 ÷ 96 500 = 3.73 × 10⁻³ [1]. mol M =
0.0647 ÷ 52.0 = 1.24 × 10⁻³ [1]. Ratio 3.73 × 10⁻³ ÷ 1.24 × 10⁻³ = 3, so the ion is
**M³⁺** [1].

**9. (a)** Q = 0.500 × 1500 = 750 C; electrons = 750 ÷ 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 4.69 × 10²¹ [1].
mol Cu = 0.240 ÷ 63.5 = 3.78 × 10⁻³; mol e⁻ = 7.56 × 10⁻³ [1].
L = 4.69 × 10²¹ ÷ 7.56 × 10⁻³ = **6.20 × 10²³ mol⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** Any two: the current was not perfectly constant; the anode was not completely dry when
reweighed; impurities in the copper anode dissolved or fell off as well as copper; the timing
was inaccurate [2].

**10.** Before: clean with emery paper [1], so that the surface is free of oxide and copper
reacts evenly. After: rinse with distilled water to remove the solution [1], then dry (for
example by rinsing with propanone and allowing it to evaporate) so that only copper is weighed
[1].

**11. (a)** mol Ag = 0.540 ÷ 107.9 = 5.00 × 10⁻³ = mol e⁻; mol Cu = 2.50 × 10⁻³ [1].
Mass Cu = 2.50 × 10⁻³ × 63.5 = **0.159 g** [1].

**(b)** Q = 5.00 × 10⁻³ × 96 500 = 483 C [1]. t = 483 ÷ 0.200 = **2410 s** (about 40 minutes)
[1].

**12. (a)** Cathode: silver [1]; anode: oxygen [1].

**(b)** Cathode: hydrogen [1]; anode: iodine [1].

**(c)** Cathode: hydrogen [1]; anode: chlorine [1].

**(d)** Cathode: magnesium [1]; anode: chlorine [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving time in minutes in Q = It.
- Forgetting that O₂ needs four electrons and Cu²⁺ two.
- Naming a reactive metal as the cathode product in aqueous solution.
- Treating all halides alike: chloride depends on concentration, bromide and iodide do not.
- Dividing by F instead of e when finding the number of electrons in the Avogadro calculation.
- Describing the Avogadro method without saying what is measured and how L is calculated.
