## Section A

**1.** Name the Five Pillars of Islam and state briefly what each requires. **[10]**

**2.** Name the Articles of Faith. **[3]**

## Section B

**3.** Explain the significance of salah in the daily life of a Muslim, giving **four** points. **[8]**

**4.** Explain the rules of zakat.

**(a)** State who must pay it, on what, and at what rate. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain **three** categories of people entitled to receive it. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain the social significance of zakat. **[4]**

**5.** Describe the main rites of Hajj in order, and explain the significance of **two** of them. **[10]**

**6.** Explain the purpose of fasting in Ramadan, giving **three** benefits Muslims are taught to derive from it. **[6]**

**7.** Name the five daily prayers in order, and describe **two** conditions that must be met before Salah is performed. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. Shahadah** — the declaration that there is no god but Allah and Muhammad is His Messenger [1] [1]. **Salah** — five daily prayers at prescribed times, facing the Kaaba [1] [1]. **Zakat** — the annual payment of a fixed portion of one's surplus wealth to the poor [1] [1]. **Sawm** — fasting from dawn to sunset throughout Ramadan [1] [1]. **Hajj** — the pilgrimage to Makkah, obligatory once in a lifetime for those able [1] [1].

**2.** Belief in **Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Day of Judgement, and divine decree (qadar)** [3 — 1 mark per two correct].

**3.** Any four, 2 marks each: it provides **regular, structured remembrance of God throughout the day**, so worship is not confined to one occasion but shapes the whole daily routine [1] [1]. It fosters **discipline and punctuality**, since prayer must be performed at set times regardless of other commitments [1] [1]. Congregational prayer creates **equality and community**, as worshippers stand in rows without distinction of wealth or rank [1] [1]. It offers **spiritual purification and a means of seeking forgiveness**, described as washing away minor sins like a river [1] [1]. It **reinforces accountability**, reminding the worshipper of the Day of Judgement and restraining wrongdoing [1] [1].

**4. (a)** It is payable by every **adult Muslim of sound mind whose wealth exceeds the nisab and has been held for a lunar year** [1] [1]; it is charged on **surplus wealth — savings, gold and silver, trade goods and livestock — not on the tools of one's trade or one's home** [1]; the standard rate is **2.5%** [1].
**(b)** Any three: the **poor**; the **needy**; **those employed to administer it**; those **whose hearts are to be reconciled**; **freeing captives**; **those in debt**; **in the cause of God**; the **stranded traveller** [1] [1] [1].
**(c)** It **redistributes wealth from the wealthy to the poor**, narrowing inequality within the community [1]. It **purifies the giver's wealth and heart from greed**, since it is an obligation rather than optional charity [1]. It **preserves the dignity of the recipient**, who receives by right rather than by favour [1]. It **binds the community together**, since those with means are directly responsible for those without, reducing resentment and social division [1].

**5.** On entering the miqat the pilgrim assumes **ihram** and makes the intention [1]. **Tawaf** — seven circuits of the Kaaba [1]. **Sa'y** — walking seven times between Safa and Marwah [1]. Standing at **Arafat** on the ninth of Dhul-Hijjah — the essential rite of Hajj [1]. Overnight at **Muzdalifah**, collecting pebbles [1]. **Stoning the jamarat at Mina**, followed by the **sacrifice** and the cutting of the hair [1]; then the **tawaf al-ifadah** and the farewell tawaf [1].
**Significance**, any two: **ihram** requires all pilgrims to wear the same simple garments, which **removes every distinction of wealth, nationality and status**, embodying the equality of all before God [1] [1]. **Standing at Arafat** is regarded as the climax of the Hajj, evoking the **Day of Judgement**, when all humanity will stand before God, and is the occasion of the Prophet's Farewell Sermon [1] [1]. **Sa'y** commemorates **Hajar's search for water for her son Ismail**, honouring trust in God in desperate circumstances [1] [1].

**6.** Fasting is commanded in the Qur'an so that Muslims may attain **taqwa — consciousness of God** [1] [1].
Benefits, any three: it develops **self-discipline and control of desires**, since the fast is broken only by one's own choice and God alone knows whether it is kept [1] [1]. It creates **empathy with the poor and hungry**, encouraging generosity, which is why charity increases during Ramadan [1] [1]. It **strengthens community bonds**, as families and neighbourhoods break the fast together and attend taraweeh prayers [1] [1]. It offers a period of **spiritual renewal** — increased Qur'an recitation, repentance, and the seeking of Laylat al-Qadr [1] [1].

**7.** **Fajr** (dawn), **Zuhr** (midday), **Asr** (afternoon), **Maghrib** (sunset), **Isha** (night) [1 for the set, in order].
Conditions, any two: **wudu** — ritual washing of the body, expressing purity of body and intention, must be performed first [1] [1]; the worshipper must face the **Qiblah**, the direction of the Kaaba in Makkah [1] [1]; prayer must fall within its **prescribed time** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Listing the Pillars without stating what each requires.
- Giving the zakat rate without the nisab and the one-year condition.
- Putting the Hajj rites in the wrong order.
- Describing fasting only as abstaining from food and drink.
