Cambridge International AS & A Level Information Technology (9626) combines the study of how IT systems work, and how they affect people and organisations, with hands-on use of software to solve problems. It is assessed by four externally marked papers, each worth 25% of the full A Level: Paper 1, Theory (1 hour 45 minutes, 70 marks), Paper 2, Practical (2 hours 30 minutes, 90 marks), Paper 3, Advanced Theory (1 hour 45 minutes, 70 marks) and Paper 4, Advanced Practical (2 hours 30 minutes, 90 marks). The first two papers form the AS Level; the second two complete the A Level. This overview explains how the 21 topics are divided, how the routes through the qualification work, and how to plan your study.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus content is split between an AS stage and an A Level stage.

**AS Level topics (1-11)**

The AS stage opens with foundations: Data processing and information, Hardware and software (including mainframe computers and supercomputers, system software, utility software, custom-written and off-the-shelf software, and user interfaces), and Monitoring and control (monitoring and measurement technologies, and control technologies). It continues with Algorithms and flowcharts, eSecurity, The digital divide and Expert systems. The remaining AS topics are more applied: Spreadsheets, Modelling, Database and file concepts, and Video and audio editing.

**A Level topics (12-21)**

The A Level stage widens the view to IT in society, New and emerging technologies, Communications technology, Project management and System life cycle. It then adds further applied topics: Data analysis and visualisation, Mail merge, Graphics creation, Animation and Programming for the web.

At each stage there is a mix of topics that are mainly about understanding concepts, systems and their impact, and topics that are mainly about using software to produce something. That mix mirrors the split between theory and practical papers.

## How it's assessed

All four papers are externally assessed. The weightings below are for the full A Level; when the AS Level is taken on its own, Paper 1 and Paper 2 are worth 50% each.

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | % of AS Level | % of A Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Theory | 1 hour 45 minutes | 70 | 50% | 25% |
| Paper 2 | Practical | 2 hours 30 minutes | 90 | 50% | 25% |
| Paper 3 | Advanced Theory | 1 hour 45 minutes | 70 | n/a | 25% |
| Paper 4 | Advanced Practical | 2 hours 30 minutes | 90 | n/a | 25% |

The assessment is staged, and there are three ways through it:

- take Papers 1 and 2 for a standalone Cambridge International AS Level;
- take Papers 1 and 2 first, then Papers 3 and 4 in a later series, carrying the AS result forward to the full A Level;
- take all four papers together in the same series for the full A Level.

Your school will decide which route you follow. The syllabus covers examinations from 2025 to 2027 and was first examined in 2025.

## How the parts fit together

The two theory papers and the two practical papers work as pairs, and within each pair the A Level paper builds on the AS paper.

On the theory side, the AS topics establish how data is processed, how hardware and software work together, how monitoring and control systems operate and how systems are kept secure. The A Level theory topics then ask bigger questions about IT in society, emerging technologies, communications and the way projects and systems are planned and managed. Knowing the AS ideas well makes the A Level discussion far easier, because many A Level questions assume you can explain the underlying technology before you evaluate its impact.

On the practical side, the AS applied topics such as Spreadsheets, Modelling and Database and file concepts give you core software skills, and the A Level applied topics extend them into data analysis and visualisation, mail merge, graphics, animation and web programming. Algorithms and flowcharts is a useful bridge: it supports logical thinking for both theory answers and practical problem-solving.

Across all four papers, two habits pay off: explaining technical ideas clearly in your own words, and working accurately and methodically when you build a solution.

## Planning your study

A sensible approach is to alternate theory and practical work rather than finishing one before starting the other. Practical skills improve with regular use, while theory topics benefit from being revisited.

In general terms, for a two-stage course:

- **AS stage:** begin with Data processing and information and Hardware and software, introduce Spreadsheets and Database and file concepts early so you have time to practise, and fit Algorithms and flowcharts, eSecurity, The digital divide, Expert systems, Modelling and Video and audio editing around them.
- **A Level stage:** pair the conceptual topics, from IT in society through to System life cycle, with steady practical work on Data analysis and visualisation, Mail merge, Graphics creation, Animation and Programming for the web.

Start timed practice on individual topics once each is taught, and move on to complete past papers for each paper in the final months before your examinations. For paper-by-paper exam advice, use the dedicated exam preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge A Level Information Technology 9626?**
There are four: Paper 1 Theory and Paper 2 Practical at AS Level, and Paper 3 Advanced Theory and Paper 4 Advanced Practical at A Level. Each is worth 25% of the full A Level.

**How long are the practical papers?**
Paper 2 Practical and Paper 4 Advanced Practical are each 2 hours 30 minutes and marked out of 90.

**Can I take AS Level Information Technology on its own?**
Yes. Papers 1 and 2 can be taken as a standalone AS Level, where each is worth 50%. They can also be the first half of a staged A Level, or all four papers can be taken in the same series.

**Which topics are A Level only?**
Topics 12 to 21: IT in society, New and emerging technologies, Communications technology, Project management, System life cycle, Data analysis and visualisation, Mail merge, Graphics creation, Animation and Programming for the web.

**Are any 9626 papers marked by my school?**
No. All four papers are externally assessed by Cambridge.
