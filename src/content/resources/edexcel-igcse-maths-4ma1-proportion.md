The specification notes limit the forms to those eight. You will not be asked about other powers, such as y ∝ x⁴ or a cube root.

Higher questions also assume the Foundation content. Foundation statement 1.7D asks you to "calculate an unknown quantity from quantities that vary in direct proportion" and find a missing value in a table. Section 2.5 extends that idea to powers, roots and inverse relationships, written as formulae.

## The language of proportion

The symbol **∝** means "is proportional to". Exam questions use several phrasings for the same idea:

- "y is directly proportional to x", "y varies directly as x" and "y varies as x" all mean **y ∝ x**.
- "y is inversely proportional to x" and "y varies inversely as x" mean **y ∝ 1/x**.
- "y is proportional to the square of x" means **y ∝ x²**.
- "y is inversely proportional to the square root of x" means **y ∝ 1/√x**.

Every proportion statement becomes an equation by replacing ∝ with "= k ×", where **k** is a constant called the **constant of proportionality**:

| Statement | Formula |
|---|---|
| y ∝ x | y = kx |
| y ∝ x² | y = kx² |
| y ∝ x³ | y = kx³ |
| y ∝ √x | y = k√x |
| y ∝ 1/x | y = k/x |
| y ∝ 1/x² | y = k/x² |
| y ∝ 1/x³ | y = k/x³ |
| y ∝ 1/√x | y = k/√x |

These formulae are not on the Higher tier formulae sheet, so learn the method rather than looking for them.

## The four-step method

Almost every proportion question follows the same four steps.

1. **Write the formula** with k, choosing the right power or root.
2. **Substitute** the pair of values you are given.
3. **Solve for k** and write the full formula with k replaced by its value.
4. **Use the formula** to find the unknown, either by substituting or by rearranging.

Step 3 matters. Many questions say "find a formula for y in terms of x", and the mark for that needs the formula with a number in place of k, not just the value of k on its own.

## Direct proportion

When y is directly proportional to a power or root of x, y increases as x increases (for positive k and x).

### Worked example 1: proportional to a square

E is directly proportional to the square of v. When v = 3, E = 36.

(a) Find a formula for E in terms of v.
(b) Find E when v = 5.
(c) Find v when E = 196, given v > 0.

```
(a)  E = kv²
     36 = k × 3²   so   36 = 9k   so   k = 4
     E = 4v²
(b)  E = 4 × 5² = 4 × 25 = 100
(c)  196 = 4v²   so   v² = 49   so   v = 7
```

In (c) you take a square root. v = −7 also satisfies v² = 49, but the question says v > 0. Always read the context: lengths, speeds and times are positive.

### Worked example 2: proportional to a square root

y is directly proportional to √x. When x = 25, y = 20.

(a) Find y when x = 64.
(b) Find x when y = 6.

```
     y = k√x
     20 = k × √25 = 5k   so   k = 4
     y = 4√x
(a)  y = 4 × √64 = 4 × 8 = 32
(b)  6 = 4√x   so   √x = 1.5   so   x = 1.5² = 2.25
```

To undo a square root, square both sides. To undo a cube, take the cube root.

## Inverse proportion

When y is inversely proportional to a power or root of x, y decreases as x increases. The product of y and the power of x stays constant: for y ∝ 1/x, the value of xy is always k.

### Worked example 3: inverse square

F is inversely proportional to the square of r. When r = 3, F = 20.

(a) Find a formula for F in terms of r.
(b) Find F when r = 6.
(c) Find r when F = 0.8, given r > 0.

```
(a)  F = k/r²
     20 = k/3² = k/9   so   k = 180
     F = 180/r²
(b)  F = 180/6² = 180/36 = 5
(c)  0.8 = 180/r²   so   r² = 180/0.8 = 225   so   r = 15
```

In (c), multiply both sides by r² first, then divide by 0.8. Doing it in one line on a calculator is fine, but write the rearranged equation so a method mark can be given.

### Worked example 4: inverse square root

a is inversely proportional to √b. When b = 16, a = 3.

(a) Find a when b = 9.
(b) Find b when a = 2.

```
     a = k/√b
     3 = k/√16 = k/4   so   k = 12
     a = 12/√b
(a)  a = 12/√9 = 12/3 = 4
(b)  2 = 12/√b   so   √b = 6   so   b = 36
```

### Worked example 5: inverse cube

y is inversely proportional to x³. When x = 2, y = 500. Find y when x = 5.

```
     y = k/x³
     500 = k/8   so   k = 4000
     y = 4000/5³ = 4000/125 = 32
```

## Scale factors: what happens when x changes

Some questions give no numbers for k at all. They ask what happens to y when x is multiplied by some factor. If y = kxⁿ and x is multiplied by c, then y is multiplied by cⁿ. For inverse forms, y is divided by cⁿ.

| Relationship | x multiplied by 2 | x multiplied by 3 |
|---|---|---|
| y ∝ x | y × 2 | y × 3 |
| y ∝ x² | y × 4 | y × 9 |
| y ∝ x³ | y × 8 | y × 27 |
| y ∝ 1/x | y ÷ 2 | y ÷ 3 |
| y ∝ 1/x² | y ÷ 4 | y ÷ 9 |

### Worked example 6: percentage change

y is directly proportional to x³. x is increased by 20%. Find the percentage increase in y.

```
x is multiplied by 1.2
y is multiplied by 1.2³ = 1.728
percentage increase in y = (1.728 − 1) × 100 = 72.8%
```

A common wrong answer is 60% (three lots of 20%). Powers multiply; they do not add percentages. This links to repeated percentage change in Higher statement 1.6A.

## Recognising proportion from a table

To test a table of values, work out the quantity that should stay constant.

- For y ∝ x, check y/x.
- For y ∝ x², check y/x².
- For y ∝ 1/x, check xy.
- For y ∝ 1/x², check x²y.

### Worked example 7: identify the relationship

| x | 2 | 4 | 5 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 40 | 20 | 16 | 10 |

As x doubles from 2 to 4, y halves, which suggests inverse proportion. Check xy: 2 × 40 = 80, 4 × 20 = 80, 5 × 16 = 80, 8 × 10 = 80. The product is constant, so y is inversely proportional to x and **y = 80/x**. When x = 32, y = 80/32 = 2.5.

## Graphs of proportion

The specification asks you to relate the algebra to the graphs. For positive k, and x ≥ 0 (or x > 0 for inverse forms), each graph has a recognisable shape.

| Relationship | Shape of the graph of y against x |
|---|---|
| y ∝ x | Straight line through the origin; the gradient is k |
| y ∝ x² | Curve through the origin, getting steeper (half of a parabola) |
| y ∝ x³ | Curve through the origin; with the same k, flatter than y = kx² between 0 and 1 and steeper after x = 1 |
| y ∝ √x | Curve through the origin, getting less steep |
| y ∝ 1/x, 1/x², 1/x³, 1/√x | Curve that falls as x increases, getting close to both axes but never touching them |

Two facts link graphs to the algebra:

- **Only direct proportion to x gives a straight line.** A straight line that does not pass through the origin is not proportion, because when x = 0, y = k × 0 = 0.
- **Every point on the graph satisfies the formula.** If you can read one point from the graph, substitute it to find k.

A further idea: if y = kx², then plotting y against x² (instead of against x) gives a straight line through the origin with gradient k. The same works for any of the eight forms, using the right power of x on the horizontal axis.

### Worked example 8: formula from a graph

A graph of y against x is a straight line through the origin and the point (4, 10). Find y when x = 7.

```
straight line through the origin, so y = kx
10 = k × 4   so   k = 2.5
y = 2.5x,  so when x = 7, y = 2.5 × 7 = 17.5
```

## Common errors

- Writing y = x² + k instead of y = kx². The constant multiplies; it is never added.
- Using the wrong power: "inversely proportional to the square of x" is y = k/x², not y = k/x or y = 1/(kx²).
- Giving k but not the formula when the question asks for "a formula for y in terms of x".
- Forgetting to square-root (or cube-root) at the end when finding x from y = kx² (or kx³).
- Forgetting to square at the end when solving y = k√x for x.
- Rounding k too early. If k is a long decimal, keep it as a fraction or in the calculator memory.
- Adding percentages for powers: a 20% rise in x gives a 44% rise in y when y ∝ x², not 40%.
- Assuming any rising curve is y ∝ x². Check where the graph passes through the origin and whether it gets steeper or less steep.
