These notes condense section **3.3.5.2 Oxidation of alcohols** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2 at AS and A-level. Explanations and worked examples are in the [Oxidation of alcohols study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-oxidation-of-alcohols/), and exam-style questions are in the [Oxidation of alcohols practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-oxidation-of-alcohols-practice/). The previous sub-topic is in the [Alcohol production revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-alcohol-production-revision-notes/).

## What you must be able to do

- Classify alcohols as **primary, secondary or tertiary**.
- Write **oxidation equations**, using [O].
- Explain how the **method** decides between **aldehyde** and **carboxylic acid**.
- Distinguish aldehydes from ketones with **Fehling's solution** and **Tollens' reagent**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Primary alcohol | C–OH carbon bonded to one other carbon (methanol: none) |
| Secondary alcohol | C–OH carbon bonded to two other carbons |
| Tertiary alcohol | C–OH carbon bonded to three other carbons |
| Aldehyde | C=O at the end of a chain, –CHO; name ends -al |
| Ketone | C=O between two carbons; name ends -one |
| Carboxylic acid | –COOH group; name ends -oic acid |
| [O] | Oxygen from the oxidising agent, acceptable in equations |
| Reflux | Continuous boiling with a vertical condenser returning vapour to the flask |

## Classification shortcut

Count the H atoms on the C–OH carbon: **2 = primary, 1 = secondary, 0 = tertiary**. Oxidation needs one of those H atoms.

| Examples | Class |
|---|---|
| Ethanol, 2-methylpropan-1-ol, 3-methylbutan-1-ol | Primary |
| Propan-2-ol, cyclohexanol, pentan-3-ol | Secondary |
| 2-methylpropan-2-ol, 2-methylbutan-2-ol, 3-methylpentan-3-ol | Tertiary |

## Reagent and colour

Acidified potassium dichromate(VI), K₂Cr₂O₇ / H₂SO₄.
**Orange (Cr₂O₇²⁻) → green (Cr³⁺)** if oxidation happens. Tertiary alcohols: stays orange.

## Equations with [O]

| Change | [O] used | H₂O formed |
|---|---|---|
| Primary alcohol → aldehyde | 1 | 1 |
| Aldehyde → carboxylic acid | 1 | 0 |
| Primary alcohol → carboxylic acid | 2 | 1 |
| Secondary alcohol → ketone | 1 | 1 |

Examples:

```
CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH + [O]  → CH₃CH₂CH₂CHO + H₂O        butanal
CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH + 2[O] → CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH + H₂O       butanoic acid
CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CH₂CH₃ + [O] → CH₃CH₂COCH₂CH₃ + H₂O   pentan-3-one
```

For a molecule with two –OH groups, add the counts for each group.

## Naming the products

Keep the carbon chain and its branches; change only the ending, and keep the position number for ketones.

| Alcohol | Aldehyde (distil) | Acid (reflux) | Ketone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ethanol | Ethanal | Ethanoic acid | — |
| 2-methylpropan-1-ol | 2-methylpropanal | 2-methylpropanoic acid | — |
| Butan-2-ol | — | — | Butanone |
| Cyclohexanol | — | — | Cyclohexanone |

Aldehydes and acids need no position number for the C=O: it is always on carbon 1.

## Aldehyde or acid: method decides

| | Aldehyde | Carboxylic acid |
|---|---|---|
| Amounts | Alcohol in excess; oxidant added slowly | Oxidant in excess |
| Apparatus | Distillation | Reflux, then distil |
| Why it works | Aldehyde distils off as it forms, before further oxidation | Vapour returns to flask until fully oxidised |
| Key reason | Aldehyde has the lowest b.p.: no hydrogen bonding between its molecules | — |

Typical boiling points: propanal about 48 °C, propan-1-ol about 97 °C, propanoic acid about 141 °C.

## Tests for aldehydes

| Test | Conditions | Aldehyde | Ketone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tollens' reagent | Warm in a water bath | Silver mirror (Ag⁺ → Ag) | No change |
| Fehling's solution | Warm in a water bath | Blue → red precipitate | Stays blue |

The aldehyde is oxidised: RCHO + [O] → RCOOH.

## Solving "identify the alcohol" problems

1. Green with dichromate? No → tertiary.
2. Yes → is the product an aldehyde (positive Tollens' or Fehling's)? Yes → primary. No → secondary.
3. Choose a structure that fits the molecular formula and the class.
4. Quote the evidence for each conclusion.

## Practical points

- Anti-bumping granules; heat with a water bath or electric heater.
- Thermometer bulb level with the condenser side arm when distilling.
- Water enters the condenser at the bottom.
- Reflux condenser top left open.
- Collect a volatile aldehyde in a cooled receiver.

## Exam traps

- Classifying by the number of carbons in the molecule.
- –COH for an aldehyde.
- H₂O written in the aldehyde → acid step.
- "Reflux" given for making an aldehyde.
- Aldehyde b.p. explained by size rather than lack of hydrogen bonding.
- Missing the ketone's "no change" observation.
- "Tertiary alcohol turns dichromate green."
- Fehling's precipitate described as brown or white.

## Self-test

1. Classify cyclohexanol.
2. Classify 3-methylpentan-3-ol.
3. State the colour change of acidified dichromate with a secondary alcohol.
4. Write an equation using [O] for the oxidation of ethanol to ethanal.
5. Name the product of refluxing propan-1-ol with excess acidified dichromate.
6. Name the product of oxidising pentan-3-ol.
7. Why is the alcohol used in excess when making an aldehyde?
8. Why does propanal boil at a lower temperature than propan-1-ol?
9. What is the purpose of reflux?
10. What is seen when propanal is warmed with Tollens' reagent?
11. How many [O] are needed to oxidise propane-1,3-diol fully to the dicarboxylic acid?
12. Why does refluxing propan-2-ol with excess oxidant give no carboxylic acid?

### Answers

1. Secondary.
2. Tertiary.
3. Orange to green.
4. CH₃CH₂OH + [O] → CH₃CHO + H₂O.
5. Propanoic acid.
6. Pentan-3-one.
7. So there is no excess oxidising agent to oxidise the aldehyde further.
8. Propanal has no O–H bond, so no hydrogen bonding between its molecules; its intermolecular forces are weaker.
9. To heat the mixture for a long time without losing volatile substances, so oxidation is complete.
10. A silver mirror forms.
11. Four (two per primary –OH group).
12. It forms a ketone, propanone, which is not easily oxidised further.
