These notes condense sub-topic 4(a) Introduction (points 4.1 to 4.6) of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) specification, Issue 3 (September 2024). None of the points has a C reference, so all can appear on Paper 1 and Paper 2. Worked examples are in the [organic chemistry introduction study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-organic-chemistry-introduction/); then try the [practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-organic-chemistry-introduction-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hydrocarbon | Compound of hydrogen and carbon **only** |
| Homologous series | Family with the same functional group and general formula, similar chemical properties and a trend in physical properties; members differ by CH₂ |
| Functional group | Atom or group of atoms that gives a compound its characteristic reactions |
| Isomers | Same molecular formula, different structural formula |
| Substitution | One atom or group is replaced by another |
| Addition | Atoms add across a double bond to give one product |
| Combustion | Burning in oxygen |

## Valency rules for drawing

| Atom | Bonds |
|---|---|
| Carbon | 4 |
| Hydrogen | 1 |
| Oxygen | 2 |
| Halogen (Cl, Br) | 1 |

Check every atom after drawing. A carbon with five bonds is wrong.

## The five types of formula (propene as example)

| Type | Propene |
|---|---|
| Empirical | CH₂ |
| Molecular | C₃H₆ |
| General | CₙH₂ₙ (alkenes) |
| Structural | CH₂=CHCH₃ |
| Displayed | Every atom and bond drawn |

- **Empirical:** simplest whole-number ratio. Divide by the highest common factor.
- **Molecular:** real numbers of atoms in one molecule.
- **From percentages:** divide each percentage by Ar, then divide by the smallest answer.
- **Molecular from empirical:** divide Mr by the empirical formula mass, then multiply the empirical formula by the result.

## Homologous series

| Series | Functional group | General formula | Ending |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alkanes | C-C single bonds only | CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ | -ane |
| Alkenes | C=C | CₙH₂ₙ | -ene |
| Alcohols (Paper 2 only) | -OH | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH | -ol |
| Carboxylic acids (Paper 2 only) | -COOH | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH | -oic acid |

## IUPAC naming

**Stems:** meth 1, eth 2, prop 3, but 4, pent 5, hex 6.

1. Find the longest chain that includes the functional group.
2. Add the ending for the series.
3. Number from the end that gives the lower number to the functional group or branch.
4. Branches go in front: methyl (1 C), ethyl (2 C). Halogens: chloro-, bromo-.
5. Use di- for two identical groups, with a number for each: 1,2-dibromoethane.

### Method in steps: drawing isomers from a molecular formula

1. Draw the longest straight chain.
2. Shorten the chain by one carbon and attach it as a branch to each different inner carbon.
3. Repeat until no new arrangement is possible.
4. Check that you have not drawn the same structure twice: a bent or flipped chain is not a new isomer.

**Isomer counts to remember:** C₄H₁₀ has 2 (butane, 2-methylpropane); C₅H₁₂ has 3 (pentane, 2-methylbutane, 2,2-dimethylpropane); C₄H₈ has 3 alkene isomers (but-1-ene, but-2-ene, 2-methylpropene).

## Reaction types

| Type | Key feature | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Substitution | Two products; an atom is replaced | CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl (UV light) |
| Addition | One product; across C=C | C₂H₄ + Br₂ → C₂H₄Br₂ |
| Combustion | Reacts with oxygen; releases heat | C₂H₆ + 3½O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O |

Reaction mechanisms are not required.

## Answer frames

- **Define a homologous series:** same functional group and general formula, similar chemical properties, trend in physical properties, differ by CH₂.
- **Isomers:** "same molecular formula but different structural formulae".
- **Name a compound:** longest chain, ending, then position numbers, then branches.
- **Classify a reaction:** count the products and ask whether atoms were swapped or added across a double bond.

## Exam traps

- Ethanol, bromoethane and chloromethane are not hydrocarbons.
- A longer chain hidden in a bent drawing: always find the longest chain.
- Numbering from the wrong end gives the wrong name.
- C₃H₈ is already an empirical formula; C₄H₁₀ simplifies to C₂H₅.
- The general formula of alkenes is CₙH₂ₙ, with no "+2".
- Addition gives one product; substitution gives two.
- Alcohols and carboxylic acids are Paper 2 only content in Topic 4.

## Naming practice

| Formula | Name | Series |
|---|---|---|
| CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃ | Butane | Alkane |
| CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₃ | 2-methylpropane | Alkane (isomer of butane) |
| CH₃CH=CH₂ | Propene | Alkene |
| CH₃CH₂CH=CH₂ | But-1-ene | Alkene |
| CH₃CH₂CH₂Cl | 1-chloropropane | Halogenoalkane |
| BrCH₂CH₂Br | 1,2-dibromoethane | Halogenoalkane |
| CH₃CH₂OH | Ethanol (Paper 2 only) | Alcohol |
| CH₃CH₂COOH | Propanoic acid (Paper 2 only) | Carboxylic acid |

## Two more worked examples

**Empirical formula from percentages.** A hydrocarbon is 75% carbon and 25% hydrogen by mass. Moles: C = 75 / 12 = 6.25; H = 25 / 1 = 25. Dividing by the smaller: C = 1, H = 4. The empirical formula is **CH₄**; with Mr = 16 the molecular formula is also CH₄ (methane).

**Counting isomers.** C₄H₁₀ has two isomers: butane (a straight chain of four carbons) and 2-methylpropane (a chain of three with a methyl group on the middle carbon). A "third" structure formed by bending the butane chain is the same molecule. Always test a new drawing by finding its longest chain: if the longest chain is four carbons with nothing attached, it is butane.

## Self-test

1. Define a hydrocarbon.
2. State the empirical formula of C₆H₁₂.
3. A compound contains 80% carbon and 20% hydrogen by mass. Calculate its empirical formula.
4. State the general formula of the alkanes.
5. Name the alkane with four carbon atoms.
6. Draw the structural formula of 2-methylpropane.
7. Give the name of CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃.
8. How many isomers does C₅H₁₂ have?
9. State what type of reaction is C₂H₄ + Br₂ → C₂H₄Br₂.
10. Name the two isomers of C₂H₄Br₂.

### Answers

1. A compound that contains hydrogen and carbon only.
2. CH₂.
3. Moles: C = 80 / 12 = 6.67; H = 20 / 1 = 20. Ratio 6.67 : 20 = 1 : 3, so the empirical formula is **CH₃**.
4. CₙH₂ₙ₊₂.
5. Butane.
6. CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₃ (a three-carbon chain with a methyl group on the middle carbon).
7. But-1-ene.
8. Three: pentane, 2-methylbutane, 2,2-dimethylpropane.
9. Addition.
10. 1,2-dibromoethane and 1,1-dibromoethane.
