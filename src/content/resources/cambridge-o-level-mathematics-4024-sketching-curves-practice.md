These questions cover section 2.11, Sketching curves, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Where a question says "describe the sketch", give the shape and every labelled point you would show. The methods are in the [sketching curves study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-sketching-curves/), and the [sketching curves revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-sketching-curves-revision-notes/) summarise the facts. Show your working.

## Section A

**1.** Find the coordinates of the points where the line 2x − 3y = 12 crosses the axes. **[2]**

**2.** Write x² + 6x + 1 in the form (x + p)² + q, and state the coordinates of the turning point of y = x² + 6x + 1. **[3]**

**3.** For y = −3/x + 2, write down the equations of both asymptotes and find the root. **[3]**

**4.** For y = 5(2)ˣ − 5, find the y-intercept and state the equation of the asymptote. **[2]**

**5.** Match each description to one of the equations y = 2/x − 1, y = x³ − 9x, y = 2(3)ˣ + 1 and y = −x² + 4.

**(a)** Passes through the origin and has three roots.

**(b)** Has a vertical asymptote x = 0 and a horizontal asymptote y = −1.

**(c)** Has a maximum point and a vertical line of symmetry.

**(d)** Has only a horizontal asymptote and crosses the y-axis at (0, 3). **[4]**

## Section B

**6.** The curve has equation y = 2x² − 12x + 13.

**(a)** Write 2x² − 12x + 13 in the form a(x + p)² + q.

**(b)** State the coordinates of the turning point, whether it is a maximum or a minimum, and the equation of the line of symmetry.

**(c)** Write down the y-intercept, and use symmetry to find another point on the curve with the same y-coordinate.

**(d)** Find the roots, correct to 2 decimal places. **[9]**

**7.** The curve has equation y = x³ + 2x² − 3x.

**(a)** Factorise the expression completely and find the roots.

**(b)** Describe the shape of the sketch, saying where its turning points lie.

**(c)** Show that y is positive when x = −1, and explain how this fits your answer to part (b). **[7]**

**8.** For y = −2x³ + 54, find the y-intercept and the root, and describe the shape of the curve, including whether it has any turning points. **[4]**

**9.** The curve has equation y = 4/x + 2.

**(a)** State the equations of the asymptotes.

**(b)** Find the root.

**(c)** State which regions, relative to the point where the asymptotes cross, contain the two branches.

**(d)** How many solutions does 4/x + 2 = k have when k = 2, and when k = 5? **[6]**

**10.** The temperature of an oven part, y °C, x minutes after switching off is modelled by y = 80(0.5)ˣ + 20. The model is invented.

**(a)** Find the temperature at x = 0 and at x = 2.

**(b)** State the equation of the horizontal asymptote and explain what it means in the context.

**(c)** Explain why the sketch must not cross the asymptote. **[5]**

**11.** The curve y = x² + bx + c has its minimum point at (2, −9).

**(a)** Find b and c.

**(b)** Find the roots of the curve. **[5]**

**12.** The curve y = (x + 1)² − 4 is sketched.

**(a)** How many times does the line y = k meet the curve when k = −5, k = −4 and k = 0?

**(b)** State the range of values of y on the curve. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** x = 0: −3y = 12, so **(0, −4)** [1]. y = 0: 2x = 12, so **(6, 0)** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Intercepts are quicker than rearranging into y = mx + c for a sketch.

**2.** x² + 6x = (x + 3)² − 9 [1], so x² + 6x + 1 = **(x + 3)² − 8** [1]. Turning point **(−3, −8)** [1].

*Examiner insight:* The x-coordinate has the opposite sign to the number in the bracket.

**3.** Asymptotes **x = 0** [1] and **y = 2** [1]. Root: 3/x = 2, so **x = 1.5** [1].

**4.** y-intercept 5 × 1 − 5 = **0** [1]. Asymptote **y = −5** [1].

*Examiner insight:* This curve passes through the origin, but it is still exponential: check the asymptote, not just the intercept.

**5. (a)** y = x³ − 9x [1]

**(b)** y = 2/x − 1 [1]

**(c)** y = −x² + 4 [1]

**(d)** y = 2(3)ˣ + 1 [1]

**6. (a)** 2(x² − 6x) + 13 [1] = 2[(x − 3)² − 9] + 13 [1] = **2(x − 3)² − 5** [1].

**(b)** **(3, −5)** [1], a **minimum**, with line of symmetry **x = 3** [1].

**(c)** y-intercept **13** [1]. (0, 13) is 3 left of x = 3, so **(6, 13)** is also on the curve [1].

**(d)** 2(x − 3)² = 5, so x − 3 = ±√2.5 [1], giving **x = 1.42 and x = 4.58** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Taking out the 2 before completing the square is the step most often missed, and it changes q from −5 to a wrong value.

**7. (a)** x(x² + 2x − 3) [1] = x(x + 3)(x − 1) [1], so roots **x = −3, 0, 1** [1].

**(b)** a = 1 > 0, so the curve rises from bottom left to top right [1], with a maximum between −3 and 0 and a minimum between 0 and 1 [1].

**(c)** y = −1 + 2 + 3 = **4** [1]. A positive value between −3 and 0 shows the curve is above the axis there, consistent with a maximum in that interval [1].

**8.** y-intercept **54** [1]. −2x³ + 54 = 0 gives x³ = 27, **x = 3** [1]. a < 0, so high on the left and low on the right [1]; this form has **no turning points**, flattening momentarily at (0, 54) [1].

**9. (a)** **x = 0 and y = 2** [2].

**(b)** 4/x = −2, so **x = −2** [1].

**(c)** a = 4 > 0, so **top right and bottom left** [1].

**(d)** k = 2: **no solutions**, as y = 2 is the asymptote [1]. k = 5: **one solution** (x = 4/3) [1].

**10. (a)** x = 0: 80 + 20 = **100 °C** [1]. x = 2: 80 × 0.25 + 20 = **40 °C** [1].

**(b)** **y = 20** [1]: the part cools towards 20 °C, the temperature it settles at [1].

**(c)** 80(0.5)ˣ is always positive, so y is always above 20 [1].

*Examiner insight:* A context answer should use the units and words of the question, not just "y approaches 20".

**11. (a)** The curve is (x − 2)² − 9 [1] = x² − 4x + 4 − 9 = x² − 4x − 5, so **b = −4** [1] and **c = −5** [1].

**(b)** (x − 5)(x + 1) = 0 [1], so **x = 5 and x = −1** [1].

*Examiner insight:* The roots are 3 either side of x = 2, as symmetry requires.

**12. (a)** k = −5: **0** [1]; k = −4: **1** (the line touches at the turning point) [1]; k = 0: **2** [1].

**(b)** **y ≥ −4** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Turning points read with the wrong sign from completed-square form.
- Not taking out the coefficient of x² before completing the square.
- Drawing branches of a reciprocal graph in the wrong regions.
- Exponential sketches that touch or cross the asymptote.
- Cubics drawn the wrong way round for negative a.
- Missing labels for intercepts, turning points or asymptotes.
