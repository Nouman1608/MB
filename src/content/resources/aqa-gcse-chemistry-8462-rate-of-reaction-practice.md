These questions cover section **4.6.1 Rate of reaction** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462) specification, for
teaching from September 2016. The content is assessed on Paper 1. Questions 8 and 9 test rates in mol/s and the
gradient of a tangent, which the specification marks (HT only), and are labelled **Higher tier only**.
Question 7 is based on required practical 5.

Learn the content first in the [rate of reaction study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-rate-of-reaction/)
and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-rate-of-reaction-revision-notes/).

Relative atomic mass: Mg = 24.

## Questions

**1.** A reaction produces 72 cm³ of hydrogen in 60 seconds. Calculate the mean rate of reaction in cm³/s. **[2]**

**2.** A flask containing calcium carbonate and acid loses 0.84 g in mass in 120 seconds as carbon dioxide escapes. Calculate the mean rate of reaction in g/s. **[2]**

**3.** A student collects a gas and records the total volume at intervals.

| Time / s | 0 | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Volume of gas / cm³ | 0 | 24 | 38 | 46 | 50 | 50 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate between 0 and 10 s. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the mean rate between 20 and 30 s. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the rate decreases as the reaction goes on. **[2]**
**(d)** State what the data show about the reaction at 40 to 50 s. **[1]**

**4.** Magnesium reacts with hydrochloric acid. Explain, in terms of particles, why the reaction is faster at 50 °C than at 20 °C. **[3]**

**5.** Two cubes of calcium carbonate are placed in separate beakers of excess acid. Cube A has sides of 1 cm. Cube B has sides of 2 cm.

**(a)** Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of each cube. **[3]**
**(b)** Predict which cube reacts faster and explain why. **[2]**

**6.** Hydrogen peroxide decomposes slowly: 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂. Adding manganese(IV) oxide makes it decompose much faster. The manganese(IV) oxide is unchanged at the end.

**(a)** Identify the catalyst and give **two** pieces of evidence that it is one. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how a catalyst increases the rate of reaction. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe how the reaction profile for the catalysed reaction differs from the uncatalysed one. **[2]**

**7.** A student investigates how the concentration of hydrochloric acid affects the rate of reaction with magnesium ribbon, by collecting the hydrogen in a gas syringe.

**(a)** Write a hypothesis the student could test. **[1]**
**(b)** State the independent variable and the dependent variable. **[2]**
**(c)** State **two** variables that must be kept the same. **[2]**
**(d)** Describe how the student could vary the concentration. **[1]**
**(e)** Suggest why the student should repeat the experiment. **[1]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) 0.48 g of magnesium is completely used up in 40 seconds in a reaction with excess acid. Calculate the mean rate of reaction in mol/s. **[3]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) A student plots the volume of gas against time. A tangent is drawn at 15 s. It passes through (0 s, 6 cm³) and (30 s, 36 cm³).

**(a)** Calculate the rate of reaction at 15 s. **[2]**
**(b)** The tangent at 60 s has a gradient of 0.25 cm³/s. Explain why this rate is lower than the rate at 15 s. **[2]**

**10.** A student doubles the concentration of an acid. Use collision theory to predict and explain the effect on the rate, assuming the same volume of acid. **[3]**

**11.** Two experiments use 5 g of marble chips with excess hydrochloric acid at the same temperature. Experiment 1 uses large lumps. Experiment 2 uses powder.

**(a)** Sketch (describe) how the graphs of volume of gas against time compare for the two experiments. **[3]**
**(b)** State the final volume of gas in experiment 2 compared with experiment 1 and explain your answer. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Mean rate = 72 ÷ 60 [1] = **1.2 cm³/s** [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Mean rate = 0.84 ÷ 120 [1] = **0.0070 g/s** [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** 24 ÷ 10 = **2.4 cm³/s** [1]
**(b)** (46 − 38) ÷ 10 = **0.8 cm³/s** [1]
**(c)** The **reactants are being used up** [1], so their **concentration falls** and collisions become less frequent [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** The volume is constant at 50 cm³, so the reaction has **stopped** (a reactant has been used up) [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Rate is a change in volume divided by the time interval, not the volume at the end.

**4.** At the higher temperature the particles move **faster** [1], so they collide **more frequently** [1], and a **greater proportion** of collisions have energy **at least equal to the activation energy** (collisions are more energetic) [1]. **[3]**

**5. (a)** Cube A: surface area = 6 × 1 × 1 = 6 cm²; volume = 1 cm³; ratio **6 : 1** [1]. Cube B: surface area = 6 × 2 × 2 = 24 cm²; volume = 8 cm³ [1]; ratio **3 : 1** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** **Cube A** reacts faster [1], because it has the **larger surface area to volume ratio**, so more particles are exposed to the acid and collisions are more frequent [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** **Manganese(IV) oxide** [1]; it **speeds up** the reaction [1]; it is **unchanged / not used up** at the end (and is not in the equation) [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** It provides an **alternative pathway** [1] with a **lower activation energy**, so a greater proportion of collisions are successful [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The catalysed profile has a **lower peak** (lower activation energy) [1]; the reactants and products are at the **same levels** (the overall energy change is the same) [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** For example: "The higher the concentration of acid, the faster the rate of reaction." [1]
**(b)** Independent: **concentration of acid** [1]; dependent: **volume of hydrogen (per unit time)** / rate [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Any two [1] each: volume of acid; mass or length of magnesium; temperature; surface area of magnesium. **[2]**
**(d)** Dilute a stock solution with measured volumes of **water** [1]. **[1]**
**(e)** To identify anomalies and calculate a **mean**, making the results more reliable [1]. **[1]**

**8.** Moles of Mg = 0.48 ÷ 24 = **0.020 mol** [1]. Rate = 0.020 ÷ 40 [1] = **5.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol/s** (0.00050 mol/s) [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** Gradient = (36 − 6) ÷ (30 − 0) [1] = **1.0 cm³/s** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** By 60 s the **reactants have been used up**, so their concentration is lower [1], and collisions are less frequent, so the rate is lower [1]. **[2]**

**10.** The rate **increases** (about doubles) [1]. There are **twice as many particles** in the same volume [1], so collisions are **more frequent** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Do not say the particles have more energy or move faster. Only temperature does that.

**11. (a)** Powder line is **steeper** at the start [1]; both lines are **curved** and become flat [1]; the powder line flattens **earlier** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** The **same final volume** [1], because the same mass of marble and the same excess acid produce the same amount of product [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Missing units, or using the wrong time interval.
- Saying "more collisions" with no mention of **frequency** or **energy** for temperature questions.
- Saying a catalyst changes the products or reactants, or is used up.
- Forgetting to convert mass to moles in mol/s calculations.
- Saying a faster reaction gives more product.
