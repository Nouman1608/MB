## Questions

**1.** Compare the arrangement and motion of the particles in a solid and in a gas. **[4]**

**2.** A rock sample of mass 0.84 kg is lowered into a measuring cylinder and displaces 310 cm³ of water. Calculate the density of the rock in kg m⁻³. **[3]**

**3.** The temperature of a gas rises from 290 K to 335 K.

**(a)** State the temperature change in degrees Celsius. **[1]**
**(b)** Convert 25 °C to kelvin, and 90 K to degrees Celsius. Use 273 for the conversion. **[2]**
**(c)** A student says that the average kinetic energy of the particles doubles when the temperature rises from 10 °C to 20 °C. Explain why this is incorrect. **[2]**

**4.** Argon atoms of mass 6.63 × 10⁻²⁶ kg are in a gas at 540 K. The Boltzmann constant is 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the average kinetic energy of an atom. **[2]**
**(b)** Determine the temperature at which the average kinetic energy is half this value. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the speed corresponding to the average kinetic energy at 540 K. **[2]**

**5.** Define the internal energy of a block of metal, and explain why a hot block at 80 °C of mass 0.05 kg and a large water bath at 45 °C can be in contact while energy flows from the block to the bath. **[4]**

**6.** An electric heater of power 150 W warms a 2.4 kg aluminium block from 18 °C to 46 °C. Take c for aluminium as 900 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹ and assume all the energy goes into the block. Calculate the time taken. **[4]**

**7.** A freezer removes energy from 0.75 kg of water that is already at 0 °C, at a steady rate of 420 W. The specific latent heat of fusion of water is 3.34 × 10⁵ J kg⁻¹. Calculate the time taken for all the water to freeze, in seconds. **[4]**

**8.** Distinguish between boiling and evaporation, and explain why evaporation cools the liquid that remains. **[4]**

**9.** A 0.50 kg sample of ice is heated by a steady 800 W heater. The table shows the readings.

| Time / s | 0 | 26 | 235 | 496 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature / °C | −20 | 0 | 0 | 100 |

The temperature is constant between 26 s and 235 s.

**(a)** Explain, using the molecular model, why the temperature is constant during this interval. **[2]**
**(b)** Determine the specific latent heat of fusion of ice from the data. **[3]**
**(c)** Show that the specific heat capacity of the liquid is about 4180 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹. **[2]**

**10.** An aluminium block of mass 0.35 kg is heated to 160 °C and dropped into 0.90 kg of water at 24 °C in a polystyrene cup. Take c as 900 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹ for aluminium and 4180 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹ for water. Calculate the final temperature, stating one assumption. **[5]**

**11.** Steam of mass 0.040 kg at 100 °C is passed into 0.30 kg of water at 20 °C in an insulated container. The steam condenses and mixes with the water. Use L_v = 2.26 × 10⁶ J kg⁻¹ and c = 4180 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹.

**(a)** Show that the water alone would need 1.0 × 10⁵ J to reach 100 °C, and deduce that the final temperature is below 100 °C. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the final temperature. **[4]**
**(c)** Suggest why steam at 100 °C causes a worse burn than the same mass of water at 100 °C. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** In a solid, particles are close together and fixed in position, vibrating about those positions [1]; in a gas they are far apart [1] and move rapidly and randomly in straight lines between collisions [1]. Forces between solid particles are strong and hold them in place, but in a gas they are negligible except during collisions [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Comparison answers earn credit when both states are described against the same feature, such as spacing or motion, rather than describing each state in a separate paragraph.

**2.** V = 310 cm³ = 3.10 × 10⁻⁴ m³ [1]; ρ = m/V = 0.84 / 3.10 × 10⁻⁴ [1]; ρ = **2.71 × 10³ kg m⁻³** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The unit conversion is where this kind of question is usually lost, so show the volume in m³ as a separate line.

**3. (a)** 335 − 290 = **45 °C** [1]
**(b)** 25 + 273 = **298 K** [1]; 90 − 273 = **−183 °C** [1]
**(c)** Average kinetic energy depends on the kelvin temperature in direct proportion [1]; 10 °C to 20 °C is 283 K to 293 K, a rise of about 4 percent, not a doubling [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show the kelvin values in the explanation, because a bare statement that Celsius is the wrong scale is less convincing than the numbers.

**4. (a)** Ek = 3/2 kB T = 1.5 × 1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 540 [1]; **1.12 × 10⁻²⁰ J** [1]
**(b)** Ek is proportional to T, so T = **270 K** [1]
**(c)** ½mv² = Ek, so v = √(2 × 1.118 × 10⁻²⁰ / 6.63 × 10⁻²⁶) [1]; v = **581 m s⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Keep the unrounded Ek for part (c); round once, at the end.

**5.** Internal energy is the sum of the intermolecular potential energy arising from forces between the molecules and the random kinetic energy of those molecules [1]. Energy flows because of the temperature difference, from higher to lower temperature [1]; the block is at 80 °C and the bath is at 45 °C so the net flow is from the block to the bath [1], even though the bath has more total internal energy because it is much larger [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A strong answer says that direction depends on temperature, not on the amount of internal energy, and uses the two temperatures given.

**6.** Q = mcΔT = 2.4 × 900 × 28 [1]; Q = 6.05 × 10⁴ J [1]; t = Q/P = 60 480 / 150 [1]; t = **403 s** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A change of 28 °C equals 28 K, so no conversion is needed, and stating that explicitly shows you understand the scales.

**7.** Q = mL = 0.75 × 3.34 × 10⁵ [1]; Q = 2.51 × 10⁵ J [1]; t = Q/P = 250 500 / 420 [1]; t = **596 s** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* No temperature change is involved, so Q = mcΔT is the wrong tool here, and the state of the water is the clue.

**8.** Boiling happens throughout the liquid at the boiling point, at a fixed temperature [1]; evaporation happens only at the surface and can occur at any temperature below the boiling point [1]. In evaporation it is the fastest molecules that leave through the surface [1]; the molecules that stay have a lower average kinetic energy, so the liquid cools [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the escape of the highest-energy molecules is what turns a description of cooling into an explanation.

**9. (a)** The supplied energy raises the potential energy as particles move apart [1], not their average kinetic energy, so the temperature does not change [1].
**(b)** Energy supplied = Pt = 800 × (235 − 26) = 1.672 × 10⁵ J [1]; L = Q/m = 167 200 / 0.50 [1]; L = **3.34 × 10⁵ J kg⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** Time to heat the liquid = 496 − 235 = 261 s, so Q = 800 × 261 = 2.088 × 10⁵ J [1]; c = Q/(mΔT) = 208 800 / (0.50 × 100) = **4176 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹, about 4180** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A show-that answer should state the unrounded value before the rounded one and must use the time interval for the liquid only, not the whole run.

**10.** Assume no energy is lost to the surroundings, and none of the water boils [1]. Energy lost by aluminium = energy gained by water [1]: 0.35 × 900 × (160 − T) = 0.90 × 4180 × (T − 24) [1]; 315(160 − T) = 3762(T − 24), so 50 400 + 90 288 = 4077T [1]; T = **34.5 °C** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A final temperature outside the range of the two starting values points to a sign error, so test your answer against that range before moving on.

**11. (a)** Q = mcΔT = 0.30 × 4180 × 80 = 1.00 × 10⁵ J [1]; condensing steam releases 0.040 × 2.26 × 10⁶ = 9.04 × 10⁴ J [1], which is less than 1.0 × 10⁵ J, so the final temperature is below 100 °C [1]
**(b)** Energy released = energy gained: 9.04 × 10⁴ + 0.040 × 4180 × (100 − T) = 0.30 × 4180 × (T − 20) [1]; 90 400 + 16 720 − 167.2T = 1254T − 25 080 [1]; 132 200 = 1421.2T [1]; T = **93.0 °C** [1]
**(c)** Condensing on the skin gives out the latent heat of vaporization as well [1], on top of what the water gives out when it cools, so more energy reaches the skin for the same mass [1].
*Examiner insight:* Test the limiting case in part (a) first, because the working in part (b) is only valid if all the steam condenses and the final temperature is below 100 °C.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Substituting cm³ or g without first converting to m³ or kg.
- Using Celsius temperatures in Ek = 3/2 kB T.
- Rounding part-way through, so the third significant figure of the answer drifts.
- Applying Q = mcΔT across a phase change, or forgetting the latent heat stage.
- Writing "heat rises" or "temperature rises" in a phase change explanation rather than describing potential energy.
- Describing the direction of transfer in terms of which body has more energy.
- Leaving out the assumption of no energy loss in a mixing question.
- Leaving units off the final answer.
