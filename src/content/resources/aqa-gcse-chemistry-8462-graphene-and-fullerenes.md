## Graphene

**Graphene** is a **single layer of graphite**. It is a sheet of carbon atoms, one atom thick, arranged in
hexagonal rings. Each carbon atom forms **three covalent bonds** with three other carbon atoms, and one
electron from each carbon atom is **delocalised**.

Because it has the same bonding as one layer of graphite, graphene's properties follow from the same ideas.

| Property | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Conducts electricity very well | The delocalised electrons are free to move across the sheet, so it is useful in electronics |
| Very strong | The sheet is a network of strong covalent bonds in a flat layer, so a very large force is needed to break it |
| Very thin and light | It is only one atom thick |
| High melting point | Strong covalent bonds must be broken |

### Why graphene is useful

- In **electronics**, it conducts electricity, so it can be used in devices such as touch screens and in tiny
  circuits.
- In **composites**, thin sheets of graphene can be mixed into another material to make it stronger without
  adding much mass.

A **composite** is a material made by combining two or more materials so that the result has better properties
than either on its own.

## Fullerenes

**Fullerenes** are **molecules of carbon atoms with hollow shapes**: spheres, tubes and related cages. The
structure of fullerenes is based on **hexagonal rings** of carbon atoms, like graphite, but they may also contain
rings of **five or seven** carbon atoms. These other rings are what allow the flat sheet to curve into a hollow
shape.

Each carbon atom is bonded to three others. Fullerenes are made of **molecules**, so they are not giant
structures. This makes them different from diamond, graphite and graphene.

### Buckminsterfullerene, C₆₀

The first fullerene to be discovered was **Buckminsterfullerene**. It has the formula **C₆₀**, so one molecule has
60 carbon atoms. It has a **spherical** shape.

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Formula | C₆₀ |
| Atoms per molecule | 60 |
| Relative formula mass | 60 × 12 = 720 |
| Shape | Hollow sphere |
| Bonds per atom | 3 |

### Carbon nanotubes

**Carbon nanotubes** are **cylindrical fullerenes** with very high **length to diameter ratios**: they are very
long compared with their width. Imagine a sheet of graphene rolled up into a tube. Their properties make them
useful for **nanotechnology**, **electronics** and **materials**.

| Property | Reason |
|---|---|
| Very strong | Strong covalent bonds throughout the structure |
| Conduct electricity | Delocalised electrons |
| Very high length to diameter ratio | They are long thin tubes |

### Uses of fullerenes

Examples that you may meet include:

- strengthening materials (for example in sports equipment), because nanotubes are very strong;
- electronics, because nanotubes can conduct electricity;
- delivering drugs to specific parts of the body, because the hollow shape can hold a molecule;
- catalysts and lubricants.

You should be able to give examples including carbon nanotubes. The hollow shape of a fullerene and the strength
of its bonding are the properties behind these uses.

## Comparing the forms of carbon

| Form | Structure | Bonds per atom | Conducts? | Particles |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Diamond | 3D network | 4 | No | Giant covalent |
| Graphite | Layers of hexagonal rings | 3 | Yes | Giant covalent |
| Graphene | One layer of hexagonal rings | 3 | Yes | Giant covalent sheet |
| C₆₀ fullerene | Hollow sphere | 3 | Not needed for the specification | Molecules |
| Carbon nanotube | Hollow cylinder | 3 | Yes | Molecules |

## Worked examples

### Worked example 1: recognising a form from a description

*A structure is a hollow sphere made of 60 carbon atoms. Each carbon atom is bonded to three others. Name the
structure and the type to which it belongs.*

```
Hollow sphere, 60 C atoms  -> Buckminsterfullerene, C60
It is a fullerene.
```

### Worked example 2: relative formula mass of C₆₀

*Calculate the relative formula mass of C₆₀. (Relative atomic mass of C = 12.)*

```
Mr = 60 x 12 = 720
```

### Worked example 3: bonds in C₆₀

*Each atom in C₆₀ is bonded to three others. How many bonds are there?*

```
Each atom has 3 bonds, and each bond joins 2 atoms.
Number of bonds = 60 x 3 / 2 = 90
```

### Worked example 4: explaining why graphene conducts

*Explain why graphene conducts electricity.*

```
Each carbon atom is bonded to three others, using three of its four outer electrons.
The fourth electron is delocalised and free to move across the sheet.
These delocalised electrons carry charge.
```

### Worked example 5: explaining why graphene is strong

*Explain why graphene is very strong although it is only one atom thick.*

All the atoms are joined by strong covalent bonds across the whole sheet. To tear it, many strong covalent bonds
must be broken, so a very large force is needed.

### Worked example 6: choosing a material for a purpose

*A company wants a very light, strong, electrically conducting material for a flexible screen. Suggest a
suitable carbon material and justify your choice.*

Graphene. It is a sheet one atom thick, so it is very thin. It is very strong because of its covalent bonds, and
it conducts electricity because of its delocalised electrons.

### Worked example 7: telling fullerenes from graphite in a diagram

*A diagram shows a flat layer of hexagonal rings that is repeated in layers. A second diagram shows rings of
five and six carbon atoms that curve to form a hollow ball.*

The first diagram is graphite (layered, hexagons only). The second is a fullerene, because it contains
five-membered rings, which cause the structure to curve into a hollow shape.

## Common mistakes

- Saying that graphene is "many layers". Graphene is a single layer.
- Calling C₆₀ a giant structure. It is a molecule with 60 atoms.
- Saying that fullerenes contain only hexagons. They may also contain rings of five or seven carbon atoms.
- Saying nanotubes are "flat". They are cylindrical.
- Using diamond-style bonding (four bonds) for these structures. In graphene and fullerenes each carbon forms
  three bonds.
- Forgetting the delocalised electron when explaining conduction.

## Exam technique

- **Name and describe:** say "single layer of graphite" for graphene and "hollow shape" for fullerenes.
- **Recognise from diagrams:** hexagons only and flat is graphene or graphite. A curved hollow structure is a
  fullerene. A long cylinder is a nanotube.
- **Explain properties:** link each property to covalent bonding or delocalised electrons.
- **Uses:** state the property, then the use that depends on it.
