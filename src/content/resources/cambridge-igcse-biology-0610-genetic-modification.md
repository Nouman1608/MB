This guide covers section 21.3, Genetic modification, of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Statements 21.3.1 and 21.3.2 are Core and examined on every paper. Statements 21.3.3 and 21.3.4 are Supplement, examined only on the Extended papers (Papers 2 and 4), and are labelled "Extended only". Genetic modification questions reward precise vocabulary: most marks are attached to named terms used in the right order.

Use it with the [section 21.3 revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-genetic-modification-revision-notes/) and the [section 21.3 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-genetic-modification-practice/). The [topic 21 study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-biotechnology-and-genetic-modification/) covers the whole topic more briefly.

## What the syllabus asks for

| Statement | You must be able to | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| 21.3.1 | Describe genetic modification as changing the genetic material of an organism by removing, changing or inserting individual genes | Core |
| 21.3.2 | Outline four examples: human genes into bacteria to produce human proteins; genes into crop plants for resistance to herbicides; for resistance to insect pests; to improve nutritional qualities | Core |
| 21.3.3 | Outline the process of GM using bacterial production of a human protein, limited to steps (a) to (f) | Extended only |
| 21.3.4 | Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of genetically modifying crops, including soya, maize and rice | Extended only |

## The definition (Core, 21.3.1)

**Genetic modification** is changing the genetic material of an organism by **removing, changing or inserting individual genes**.

Three parts of that sentence carry the marks: *genetic material* (DNA), *individual genes* (not whole chromosomes or whole genomes), and the three possible actions. "Changing an organism's DNA" is only half an answer.

How GM differs from selective breeding (topic 18) is a useful contrast. Selective breeding chooses parents with desirable features over many generations; GM moves or changes a specific gene directly, can take a gene from a completely different species, and works within one generation.

## Four examples (Core, 21.3.2)

For each example, an "outline" answer should say **which gene goes into which organism** and **what new feature results**.

| Example | Gene inserted into | Resulting feature | Benefit |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Human protein | Bacteria | Bacteria make a human protein, such as insulin | Large amounts of human insulin for people with diabetes |
| (b) Herbicide resistance | A crop plant, such as soya | The crop survives being sprayed with a herbicide | Weeds can be killed without killing the crop |
| (c) Insect pest resistance | A crop plant, such as maize | The plant makes a substance toxic to certain insect pests | Less crop damage, less insecticide sprayed |
| (d) Improved nutrition | A crop plant, such as rice | The plant makes a nutrient it would not normally contain, such as beta-carotene, used by the body to make vitamin A | Reduces vitamin A deficiency where rice is a main food |

Questions often give an unfamiliar crop and ask you to identify which of the four types of modification it shows. Look for the clue: surviving spraying is herbicide resistance; fewer insects eating leaves is pest resistance; a new vitamin or nutrient is nutritional improvement.

## The six-step process (Extended only, 21.3.3)

The syllabus limits you to six steps, using bacterial production of a human protein. Learn them with their key words.

1. **(a) Isolate the human gene.** The DNA making up the human gene is cut out using **restriction enzymes**, which cut DNA at specific points, leaving **sticky ends**: short single-stranded lengths of DNA.
2. **(b) Cut the plasmid.** Bacterial plasmid DNA is cut with **the same restriction enzymes**, forming **complementary sticky ends**.
3. **(c) Join them.** The human DNA is inserted into the plasmid DNA. The complementary sticky ends pair up, and **DNA ligase** joins the DNA, forming a **recombinant plasmid**.
4. **(d) Insert into bacteria.** Recombinant plasmids are inserted into bacteria. The syllabus says specific details are not required.
5. **(e) Multiply.** The bacteria containing recombinant plasmids multiply, for example in a fermenter.
6. **(f) Express.** The bacteria **express** the human gene: their ribosomes use it to make the human protein, which is collected and purified.

### Why "the same" restriction enzyme?

A restriction enzyme cuts at one particular base sequence and leaves a particular sticky end. Cutting the plasmid with the same enzyme leaves sticky ends whose bases are complementary to those on the human gene, so the two can pair up. A different enzyme would leave non-matching ends.

### Annotated model answer

**Question:** *Outline how bacteria can be genetically modified to produce a human protein.* [6]

> The human gene is cut out using restriction enzymes ✔, leaving sticky ends ✔. A plasmid is cut with the same restriction enzyme ✔ to give complementary sticky ends. The gene is inserted into the plasmid and joined by DNA ligase ✔, making a recombinant plasmid. The plasmid is put into a bacterium, which multiplies ✔. The bacteria express the gene and make the human protein ✔.

*Commentary:* each tick is a named idea from steps (a) to (f). Notice what the answer avoids: it never says ligase "cuts", never calls the plasmid a chromosome, and never puts the gene into a nucleus. An answer that said only "the gene is taken from a human and put into bacteria, which then make the protein" would earn one or two marks at most, because the named terms are missing.

## GM crops: advantages and disadvantages (Extended only, 21.3.4)

"Discuss" means set out both sides. The syllabus names soya, maize and rice, so tie points to those crops where you can.

**Advantages**

- **Herbicide-resistant soya:** a field can be sprayed to kill weeds while the crop survives, reducing competition for light, water and mineral ions, so yields rise.
- **Insect-resistant maize:** less crop is eaten, so yields rise; less insecticide is needed, saving money, reducing harm to other insects and reducing the farmer's exposure to chemicals.
- **Nutritionally improved rice:** can reduce deficiency diseases in places where people rely heavily on rice.
- In general, more food can be produced from the same area of land.

**Disadvantages**

- **Cost:** GM seed can be expensive, and farmers may have to buy new seed each season, which may be hard for poorer farmers.
- **Gene transfer:** pollen can carry the inserted gene to wild relatives, possibly producing herbicide-resistant weeds.
- **Biodiversity:** if weeds are removed efficiently, insects and other animals that depend on them lose food and habitat; insect-resistant crops may harm insects that are not pests.
- **Resistance:** insect pests exposed to the toxin over many generations may become resistant, through natural selection (topic 18).
- **Genetic variation:** large areas of a single variety reduce variation in the crop, so a new disease could affect a whole crop.
- **Health concerns:** some people are worried about possible effects such as allergies, although evidence of harm is limited. Present this as a concern, not a proven effect.

### Building a balanced answer

A strong six-mark discussion of, say, insect-resistant maize might run: advantage with reason (less insecticide, so fewer harmful chemicals and lower costs); second advantage (higher yields because less crop is lost); disadvantage with reason (pests may become resistant over time, so the benefit may not last); second disadvantage (seed is expensive and must be bought again); third point either way; then a brief conclusion that weighs them. A list of advantages alone, however long, cannot reach full marks.

## Common mistakes

- Saying ligase cuts DNA or restriction enzymes join it. Restriction enzymes cut; DNA ligase joins.
- Leaving out "same" when describing the cut plasmid.
- Calling the recombinant plasmid a "recombinant gene" or "recombinant bacterium".
- Writing that the gene is put into the bacterial nucleus. Bacteria have no nucleus.
- Mixing up herbicides (kill weeds) and insecticides (kill insects).
- Presenting health risks of GM food as certain.
- Answering "discuss" with only one side.

## Exam technique

- **Describe** (21.3.1): give the full definition with "individual genes".
- **Outline** (21.3.2 and 21.3.3): set out the main points in order; each step needs its key term, not a long explanation.
- **Explain**: give the reason, for example why sticky ends must be complementary.
- **Evaluate** or **discuss-style** questions on crops: both sides, linked to the named crop, with a conclusion.
- **Suggest**: apply the four example types to an unfamiliar organism or gene.
