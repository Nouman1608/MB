Cambridge IGCSE Statistics (0479) is a new syllabus, first examined in
2027, for students who want to learn how data are collected, summarised,
interpreted and used to make decisions. It suits anyone who enjoys
working with numbers in real contexts and pairs well with mathematics,
the sciences, geography, economics and business. The assessment is two
written papers of equal standing: each lasts 135 minutes, carries 100
marks and counts for 50% of the grade. This overview describes the
edition of the syllabus for examination in 2027; Cambridge has also
published a separate 2028-2030 edition, which is not covered here.

## How the course is organised

The content is set out in twelve topics. They group naturally into
four stages.

**Collecting and presenting data**

1. Data and its collection
2. Representation of data
3. Frequency distributions

These topics cover where data come from and how to display them
clearly, from tables and charts to grouped frequency distributions.

**Summarising data**

4. Measures of central tendency
5. Quartiles, percentiles and measures of dispersion
6. Transformations of data sets

Here you learn to describe a data set with a single representative
value and a measure of spread, and to see how those values change when
the data are transformed.

**Probability**

7. Probability
8. Probability distributions

These topics move from the likelihood of individual events to the
behaviour of a whole random variable, including expectation.

**Statistics in context**

9. Crude and standardised rates
10. Index numbers
11. Bivariate distributions
12. Time series

The final group applies statistics to comparing populations, measuring
change over time, examining relationships between two variables and
identifying patterns in data collected over time.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | 135 minutes | 100 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | 135 minutes | 100 | 50% |

The syllabus gives the papers no titles beyond "Paper 1" and "Paper 2".
Both contain questions of mixed length and style, drawn from any part
of the subject content, so neither paper is linked to a particular set
of topics. A calculator is required for both, although algebraic and
graphical calculators are not permitted, and essential working must be
shown. Both papers are externally assessed.

The course is not tiered and is graded A* to G. Assessment is linear,
with both papers sat at the end of the course. Only the June series is
available for this syllabus, and as of this guide's publication it has
not yet had its first live examination.

## How the parts fit together

Because either paper can test any topic, the course is best understood
as a single chain of ideas rather than two halves.

- **Collection comes first.** How a sample is chosen, and whether it is
  biased, affects every conclusion drawn later, including the
  interpretation of averages, correlation and trends.
- **Representation and summary go together.** A histogram or
  cumulative frequency graph from the early topics is often the
  starting point for estimating a median, quartiles or a percentile.
- **Summary measures reappear in context.** Means and measures of
  spread are used again in transformations, in comparing data sets and
  in the applied topics such as time series.
- **Probability links to distributions.** Work on events, tree diagrams
  and conditional probability leads directly to probability
  distributions and expectation.

Interpretation is the skill that ties the course together. Calculating
a value is rarely the end of a question; you also need to say what it
means, compare it with another value, or judge whether a method or
diagram is appropriate. Showing clear working matters too, since it is
a stated requirement of both papers.

## Planning your study

The topic order in the syllabus is a sensible order to learn it in. In
general terms:

- **Early in the course:** data collection, representation and
  frequency distributions, so that you have a secure foundation in
  handling raw data.
- **Next:** averages, quartiles, percentiles and dispersion, followed by
  transformations of data sets.
- **Then:** probability and probability distributions, which form a
  self-contained block.
- **Finally:** crude and standardised rates, index numbers, bivariate
  distributions and time series, which apply earlier skills to
  real-world contexts.

For each topic, keep a short set of fully worked examples showing every
step, along with a sentence explaining what the final answer means in
context. Writing out the interpretation each time builds the habit the
papers reward, and the worked examples become a ready-made revision
resource once you reach the mixed questions that draw on several topics
at once.

Use the same approved calculator throughout the course so that you are
fluent with it well before the exam. Because this is a new syllabus,
published past papers will be scarce at first, so topic practice
questions and any specimen material are especially valuable. Begin
mixed practice drawing on several topics once the first two stages are
complete, and leave paper-by-paper preparation for the period before
the June series.

## Frequently asked questions

**When is Cambridge IGCSE Statistics 0479 first examined?**
The syllabus is new for first examination in 2027, and only the June series is available.

**How many papers are there?**
Two: Paper 1 and Paper 2. Each lasts 135 minutes, carries 100 marks and is worth 50%.

**Do I need a calculator?**
Yes, a calculator is required for both papers, but algebraic and graphical calculators are not permitted.

**Is IGCSE Statistics tiered?**
No. All candidates take the same papers, and grades run from A* to G.

**Does each paper cover different topics?**
No. Both papers contain questions of mixed length and style drawn from any part of the subject content.
