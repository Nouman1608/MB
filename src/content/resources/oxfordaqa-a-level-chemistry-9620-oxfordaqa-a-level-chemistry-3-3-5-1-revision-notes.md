## Specification in brief

- Classify alcohols as **primary, secondary, tertiary**.
- Primary → **aldehyde** → **carboxylic acid**; secondary → **ketone**; tertiary **not easily oxidised**.
- Oxidising agent: **acidified potassium dichromate(VI)**.
- Write equations (**[O]** acceptable).
- Explain how the **method** decides aldehyde or acid.
- Distinguish aldehydes and ketones with **Fehling's solution** and **Tollens' reagent**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Primary alcohol | C–OH carbon bonded to one other carbon (methanol: none) |
| Secondary alcohol | C–OH carbon bonded to two other carbons |
| Tertiary alcohol | C–OH carbon bonded to three other carbons |
| Aldehyde | Contains –CHO; name ends -al |
| Ketone | Contains C=O bonded to two carbons; name ends -one |
| Carboxylic acid | Contains –COOH; name ends -oic acid |
| [O] | An oxygen atom from the oxidising agent |
| Distillation | Heating so that the most volatile substance boils off and is condensed elsewhere |
| Reflux | Heating with a vertical condenser so vapours condense and return to the flask |

## Recall: products and equations

| Alcohol | Class | Product (method) | Equation with [O] |
|---|---|---|---|
| propan-1-ol | primary | propanal (distil as formed) | CH₃CH₂CH₂OH + [O] → CH₃CH₂CHO + H₂O |
| propan-1-ol | primary | propanoic acid (reflux, excess oxidant) | CH₃CH₂CH₂OH + 2[O] → CH₃CH₂COOH + H₂O |
| 3-methylbutan-1-ol | primary | 3-methylbutanal | (CH₃)₂CHCH₂CH₂OH + [O] → (CH₃)₂CHCH₂CHO + H₂O |
| pentan-3-ol | secondary | pentan-3-one | CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CH₂CH₃ + [O] → CH₃CH₂COCH₂CH₃ + H₂O |
| propan-2-ol | secondary | propanone | CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ + [O] → CH₃COCH₃ + H₂O |
| 2-methylbutan-2-ol | tertiary | none: stays orange | — |

**Colour change when oxidation occurs:** orange (Cr₂O₇²⁻) → green (Cr³⁺).

### Full redox equations (synoptic)

- Reduction: Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6e⁻ → 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O
- Alcohol → aldehyde or ketone: loses 2H⁺ + 2e⁻. Ratio Cr₂O₇²⁻ : alcohol = **1 : 3**.
- Primary alcohol → acid: RCH₂OH + H₂O → RCOOH + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻. Ratio Cr₂O₇²⁻ : alcohol = **2 : 3**.
- Example: Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 8H⁺ + 3CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ → 2Cr³⁺ + 3CH₃COCH₃ + 7H₂O

## Recall: distil or reflux

| Wanted | Amounts | Apparatus | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aldehyde | alcohol in excess | distillation; collect as it forms | aldehyde has no O–H, no hydrogen bonding between molecules, lowest boiling point; leaves before further oxidation |
| Carboxylic acid | oxidising agent in excess | reflux, then distil | aldehyde vapour returns to the flask and is oxidised to the acid |

Boiling points (three-carbon series): propanal about 48 °C < propan-1-ol about 97 °C < propanoic acid about 141 °C.

## Recall: aldehyde or ketone?

| Reagent | Aldehyde | Ketone |
|---|---|---|
| Fehling's solution, warm | blue → red precipitate (Cu₂O) | stays blue |
| Tollens' reagent, warm | silver mirror | no silver mirror |

Warm in a **water bath**. Both reagents oxidise the aldehyde to a carboxylic acid (as its salt in the alkaline reagent).

### Planning order for an unknown

1. Fehling's or Tollens' first: positive means **aldehyde**.
2. Then acidified dichromate on the rest: green means **primary or secondary alcohol**; orange means **ketone or tertiary alcohol**.
3. To decide primary or secondary, oxidise with distillation and test the distillate: an aldehyde (positive Tollens') means the alcohol was **primary**; a ketone means **secondary**.

## Exam traps

- Count carbons on the **C–OH carbon** only.
- Aldehyde group is **–CHO**.
- Direct primary-to-acid equation uses **2[O]** and forms **one H₂O**.
- "No reaction" is not an observation; "stays orange", "stays blue" are.
- The boiling-point reason is **hydrogen bonding**, not bond strength or mass.
- Dichromate does **not** distinguish aldehydes from primary or secondary alcohols.
- Fehling's gives a **red** precipitate; Tollens' gives a **silver mirror**. Don't swap them.

## Self-test

1. Classify 3-methylbutan-2-ol.
2. Classify 2,2-dimethylpropan-1-ol.
3. Name the product when pentan-3-ol is oxidised.
4. Write an [O] equation for the oxidation of butan-1-ol to butanoic acid.
5. Write an [O] equation for the oxidation of butanal to butanoic acid.
6. Give the observation when 2-methylbutan-2-ol is warmed with acidified dichromate(VI).
7. Explain why propanal distils from a mixture containing propan-1-ol.
8. State two features of the method for converting propan-1-ol into propanoic acid.
9. What is seen when propanone is warmed with Tollens' reagent?
10. Write the half-equation for the reduction of dichromate(VI) ions in acid.
11. How many moles of Cr₂O₇²⁻ are needed to oxidise 0.0300 mol of propan-2-ol to propanone?
12. An alcohol C₃H₈O gives a product that turns Fehling's solution red. Name the alcohol and the product.

### Answers

1. **Secondary** (C–OH carbon bonded to two carbons).
2. **Primary** (C–OH carbon bonded to one carbon).
3. **Pentan-3-one.**
4. **CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH + 2[O] → CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH + H₂O**
5. **CH₃CH₂CH₂CHO + [O] → CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH**
6. The mixture **stays orange**.
7. Propanal has **no O–H group**, so no hydrogen bonds between its molecules; its **boiling point is lower** than that of propan-1-ol, so it boils off first.
8. **Excess oxidising agent**; **heat under reflux** (then distil off the acid).
9. **No silver mirror** forms.
10. **Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6e⁻ → 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O**
11. Ratio 1 : 3, so **0.0100 mol**.
12. **Propan-1-ol**; product **propanal** (an aldehyde).
