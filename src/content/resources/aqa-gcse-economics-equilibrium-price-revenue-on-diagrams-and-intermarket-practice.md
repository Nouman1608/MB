## Questions

**1.** Define equilibrium price. **[2]**

**2.** The table shows the weekly market for loaves in a village in Country A (illustrative).

| Price (£) | Quantity demanded | Quantity supplied |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 300 | 60 |
| 2 | 240 | 120 |
| 3 | 180 | 180 |
| 4 | 120 | 240 |
| 5 | 60 | 300 |

**(a)** Give the equilibrium price and the equilibrium quantity. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the excess demand or excess supply at £2, and say which it is. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the revenue of all sellers at the equilibrium. **[1]**

**3.** In a market for craft scarves the equilibrium is £6 and 50 scarves a week. A new trend raises demand, and the equilibrium becomes £8 and 60 scarves.
**(a)** Calculate the revenue before and after the change, and the change in revenue. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe how revenue is shown on a demand and supply diagram. **[2]**

**4.** A market has an equilibrium price of £5, but sellers charge £7. Explain how the price will return to £5. **[4]**

**5.** The price of margarine rises from £2.00 to £2.40.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage rise. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain the effect on the market for butter. **[4]**

**6.** Tennis rackets and tennis balls are complements. Explain the effect on the market for tennis balls of a fall in the price of rackets. **[4]**

**7.** A heatwave hits Town A. Describe, using a diagram, the effect on the market for ice lollies. **[4]**

**8.** A market for garden benches has demand of 900 minus 30 times the price and supply of 60 plus 30 times the price (price in £, quantities in benches a month, illustrative).
**(a)** Calculate the equilibrium price and quantity. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the excess demand at £10. **[2]**
**(c)** Higher timber costs cut supply by 60 benches at every price. Calculate the new equilibrium. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate revenue before and after the cost rise. **[2]**

**9.** A poor potato harvest raises the price of potatoes. Explain the effects on the markets for rice (a substitute) and for butter (a complement). **[6]**

**10.** A fall in the price of streaming subscriptions will harm cinemas. Evaluate this statement. **[9]**

**11.** A concert hall has 4,200 seats. Tickets sell out in minutes at the face value, and some are resold at higher prices. Explain this using supply and demand. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Equilibrium price is the price at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied [1]. At that price there is no excess demand or excess supply, so there is no pressure for the price to change [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Saying only that it is where the curves meet repeats the idea without defining it; stating that the two quantities are equal is the point to include.

**2. (a)** Quantities match at £3 [1], with 180 loaves [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** At £2, demanded 240 minus supplied 120 = 120 [1]. This is excess demand [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** 3 x 180 = **£540** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Giving the price without the quantity in part (a) leaves half the answer out, and giving a gap without naming its type in part (b) often loses the second point.

**3. (a)** Before: 6 x 50 = £300 [1]. After: 8 x 60 = £480 [1]. Change: +£180 [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Revenue is the area of the rectangle from the origin to the equilibrium point [1]. The rectangle is as tall as the price and as wide as the quantity, so area = price x quantity [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the multiplication out. A bare total cannot show whether the price or the quantity was misread, and the area under a curve is not revenue.

**4.** At £7, quantity supplied is greater than quantity demanded, so there is excess supply [1]. Stock is left unsold [1]. Sellers lower the price to clear their stock [1]. Each cut tempts more buyers and discourages some sellers, so the gap narrows and closes at £5 [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A chain of cause and effect gains more credit than a statement that the market corrects itself; make sure the surplus appears at the start of the chain.

**5. (a)** (0.40 / 2.00) x 100 = **20%** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Margarine and butter are substitutes [1]. The dearer margarine makes butter relatively cheaper, so some buyers switch [1]. Demand for butter shifts right [1]. The butter price and quantity both rise [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the relationship before giving the direction. A shift of demand is the correct term here; "quantity demanded" would describe a movement along the curve.

**6.** Rackets and balls are used together, so they are complements [1]. Cheaper rackets mean more rackets are bought [1]. More players need balls, so demand for tennis balls shifts right [1]. The price and quantity of balls both rise [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Linking the two goods through use ("people who buy rackets need balls") shows why the demand curve moves and is stronger than naming the term alone.

**7.** Hot weather increases demand for ice lollies, so the demand curve shifts right [1]. At the old price there is excess demand [1]. The price rises, moving up the supply curve [1]. The market settles at a dearer price with more lollies sold [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A diagram description should name both axes, both curves and both equilibrium points; a sketch alone, with no words, leaves the reader to guess your meaning.

**8. (a)** Set 900 - 30P = 60 + 30P, so 840 = 60P [1]. P = **£14**, Q = 900 - 420 = **480 benches** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** At £10, demand = 900 - 300 = 600 and supply = 60 + 300 = 360 [1]. Excess demand = **240 benches** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** New supply = 30P. Set 900 - 30P = 30P, so P = **£15** [1]. Q = 900 - 450 = **450 benches** [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Before: 14 x 480 = £6,720 [1]. After: 15 x 450 = **£6,750** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the line where the two expressions are set equal. Writing the method line makes your reasoning visible.

**9.** Fewer potatoes are supplied, so the price rises and quantity demanded falls [1]. Rice is a substitute, so buyers switch to it and demand for rice shifts right [1]. The price and quantity of rice rise [1]. Butter is used with potatoes, so it is a complement [1]. Fewer potatoes are bought, so demand for butter shifts left [1]. The price and quantity of butter fall [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Separate the two markets. Marks are lost when one paragraph mixes up which good's demand moved and in which direction.

**10.** Streaming and cinema are substitutes [1]. A cheaper subscription makes streaming relatively more attractive [1]. Some consumers switch, so demand for cinema tickets shifts left [1]. There is excess supply at the old price [1]. The price and quantity of tickets fall, and revenue falls [1]. However, the effect depends on how close the substitutes are: a cinema gives a big screen and a social outing, so many buyers will not switch [1]. Other factors, such as a popular film release, could shift demand the other way [1]. Cinemas can respond with offers or by cutting costs, which limits the harm [1]. Overall, harm is likely but modest, since the two goods are only partial substitutes [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation points are stronger when each is tied to cinemas and streaming and carries a reason. A list of "it depends" statements without explanation is weaker, and a final judgement should follow from the points made.

**11.** The number of seats is fixed at 4,200, so supply is a vertical line [1]. At the face value, demand is greater than 4,200, so there is excess demand [1]. The venue cannot supply more tickets [1]. Buyers who missed out are willing to pay more [1]. On resale, the price rises until demand equals 4,200 where the demand curve meets the vertical supply curve [1]. Quantity stays at 4,200, so the whole adjustment is in price [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* State that supply is fixed and explain why; it is the context that makes the price move without a change in quantity.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming the new price without the new quantity.
- Calling a movement along a curve a shift, or the reverse.
- Showing revenue as the area under a curve instead of a price x quantity rectangle.
- Describing a shift without saying what appears at the old price.
- Treating complements as substitutes, so that the direction of the shift is reversed.
- Writing about the market in general terms rather than the one in the question.
- Giving a percentage or a total without the working.
- Listing evaluation points with no reasons.
