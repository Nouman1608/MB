These questions cover Unit M2 section 5, statics of rigid bodies: the moment of a force, and equilibrium of rigid bodies under parallel and non-parallel coplanar forces, including rods and ladders against smooth or rough walls and ground. Take g = 9.8 m s⁻² unless told otherwise.

Related: [statics of rigid bodies revision notes](/resources/edexcel-a-level-mathematics-yma01-m2-statics-of-rigid-bodies-edexcel-alevel-maths-revision-notes/) and the [statics of rigid bodies study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-mathematics-yma01-m2-statics-of-rigid-bodies-edexcel-alevel-maths/).

## Section A

**1.** A force (3i + 4j) N acts at the point with position vector (2i + j) m. Find the moment of the force about the origin O, stating whether it is clockwise or anticlockwise. **[2]**

**2.** A uniform rod AB of length 3 m and weight 40 N is freely pivoted at A and rests at 30° to the horizontal. Find the moment of the weight of the rod about A. **[2]**

**3.** State the two conditions that must hold for a rigid body to remain in equilibrium under a set of coplanar forces. **[2]**

**4.** A ladder rests with its top against a smooth vertical wall. State what the word "smooth" tells you about the force on the ladder at the wall, and describe its direction. **[2]**

## Section B

**5.** A non-uniform rod AB of length 4 m and mass 12 kg rests horizontally on two supports, one at A and one at B. The reaction at A is twice the reaction at B.

**(a)** Find the distance of the centre of mass of the rod from A. **[3]**

**(b)** A particle of mass 6 kg is now placed on the rod at the point P. The two reactions become equal. Find the distance AP and the magnitude of each reaction. **[4]**

**6.** A uniform rod AB of length 2a and weight W is freely hinged at A to a point on a vertical wall. The rod is held in equilibrium at 60° to the horizontal, with B above the level of A, by a light horizontal string joining B to the wall.

**(a)** Find the tension in the string in terms of W. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the magnitude of the force exerted by the hinge on the rod, and the angle this force makes with the horizontal. **[4]**

**7.** A uniform ladder rests with its top against a smooth vertical wall and its foot on rough horizontal ground. The coefficient of friction between the ladder and the ground is 0.35.

**(a)** Show that the ladder can rest in equilibrium only if tan θ ≥ 1/0.7, where θ is the angle between the ladder and the ground, and hence find the least possible value of θ. **[5]**

**(b)** The ladder has length 5 m and mass 20 kg, and is placed at 65° to the ground. A child of mass 30 kg climbs to the very top of the ladder. Determine whether the ladder can remain in equilibrium. **[5]**

**8.** A uniform rod AB of length 3 m and weight 50 N rests with A on rough horizontal ground. The rod rests against a smooth peg at C, where AC = 2.5 m, and makes an angle of 40° with the horizontal. The rod is in limiting equilibrium.

**(a)** Find the magnitude of the reaction of the peg on the rod. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the coefficient of friction between the rod and the ground. **[4]**

**9.** A uniform rod AB of length 1.6 m and mass 5 kg is freely hinged at A to a vertical wall. A light string is attached to the rod at D, where AD = 1.2 m, and to the point C on the wall 0.9 m vertically above A. A particle of mass 2 kg hangs from B, and the rod rests horizontally.

**(a)** Find the tension in the string. **[4]**

**(b)** Find the horizontal and vertical components of the force exerted by the hinge on the rod. **[3]**

**(c)** The string breaks if its tension exceeds 300 N. Find the greatest mass that can hang from B. **[3]**

**10.** A uniform ladder rests in limiting equilibrium with its foot on rough horizontal ground and its top against a rough vertical wall. The coefficient of friction is 0.5 at the ground and 0.25 at the wall. Find the angle the ladder makes with the ground. **[7]**

**11.** A uniform plank AB of length 6 m and mass 30 kg rests horizontally on two supports, C and D, where AC = 1 m and DB = 2 m. A man of mass 75 kg walks along the plank from A towards B.

**(a)** Find how far from B the man is when the plank is about to tilt. **[4]**

**(b)** Find the reaction at D at that moment. **[2]**

**12.** A uniform rod AB of length 2a and weight W rests with A on rough horizontal ground. The rod is held at 30° to the horizontal by a light string attached at B and perpendicular to the rod. The string and the rod lie in a vertical plane.

**(a)** Find the tension in the string in terms of W. **[2]**

**(b)** Show that the coefficient of friction must be at least √3/5. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** Moment = xY − yX = 2(4) − 1(3) [1] = **5 N m anticlockwise** [1].

**2.** Perpendicular distance from A to the line of the weight = 1.5 cos 30° [1]; moment = 40 × 1.5 cos 30° = **52.0 N m** (3 s.f.) [1].

**3.** The resultant force on the body is zero [1], and the sum of the moments of the forces about any point is zero [1].

**4.** There is no friction at the wall [1], so the only force is a normal reaction, perpendicular to the wall, which is horizontal [1].

**5. (a)** R_A + R_B = 12g with R_A = 2R_B, so R_A = 8g and R_B = 4g [1]. Moments about A: 4g × 4 = 12g × d [1], so **d = 4/3 m (1.33 m)** [1].

**(b)** Equal reactions: R = 18g ÷ 2 = 9g each [1]. Moments about A: 9g × 4 = 12g × 4/3 + 6g × AP [1], so 36 = 16 + 6AP and **AP = 10/3 m (3.33 m)** [1]. Each reaction = 9g = **88.2 N** [1].

**6. (a)** Moments about A: T × 2a sin 60° [1] = W × a cos 60° [1], so **T = W/(2√3) = (√3/6)W ≈ 0.289W** [1].

**(b)** Horizontally, H = T = 0.289W, away from the wall; vertically, V = W [1]. Magnitude = √(W² + W²/12) [1] = **√(13/12) W ≈ 1.04W** [1]. Angle with horizontal: tan⁻¹(W ÷ T) = tan⁻¹(2√3) = **73.9°** [1].

**7. (a)** Weight W, length 2l. Vertically, R = W [1]. Horizontally, F = S [1]. Moments about the foot: S × 2l sin θ = W × l cos θ, so S = (W/2) cot θ [1]. Equilibrium needs F ≤ μR: (W/2) cot θ ≤ 0.35W, so tan θ ≥ 1/0.7 [1]. Least angle: θ = tan⁻¹(1.4286) = **55.0°** [1].

**(b)** Vertically, R = 50g = 490 N [1]. Moments about the foot: S × 5 sin 65° = 20g × 2.5 cos 65° + 30g × 5 cos 65° [1], so S = 40g cot 65° = 183 N (3 s.f.) [1]. Friction needed F = S = 183 N, but the maximum available is 0.35 × 490 = 171.5 N [1]. Since 183 > 171.5, **the ladder cannot remain in equilibrium; it slips** [1].

**8. (a)** Moments about A: N × 2.5 [1] = 50 × 1.5 cos 40° [1], so **N = 23.0 N** (3 s.f.) [1].

**(b)** N is perpendicular to the rod, so it acts at 40° to the vertical. Vertically: R + N cos 40° = 50, so R = 32.4 N [1]. Horizontally: F = N sin 40° = 14.8 N [1]. Limiting, so F = μR [1]: μ = 14.77 ÷ 32.40 = **0.456** (3 s.f.) [1].

**9. (a)** CD forms a 0.9, 1.2, 1.5 triangle with the wall and rod, so sin of the angle between string and rod = 0.6 [1]. Moments about A: T × 0.6 × 1.2 [1] = 5g × 0.8 + 2g × 1.6 [1], so 0.72T = 70.56 and **T = 98 N** [1].

**(b)** Horizontally: H = T × 0.8 = **78.4 N**, away from the wall [1]. Vertically: V + 0.6T = 7g [1], so **V = 68.6 − 58.8 = 9.8 N upwards** [1].

**(c)** With mass m at B and T = 300: 300 × 0.72 = 5g × 0.8 + mg × 1.6 [1], so 216 = 39.2 + 15.68m [1], giving **m = 11.3 kg** (3 s.f.) [1].

**10.** Let the weight be W, length 2l, ground reaction R, wall reaction S. Ground friction 0.5R acts towards the wall [1]; wall friction 0.25S acts upwards [1]. Horizontally: S = 0.5R [1]. Vertically: R + 0.25S = W, so R(1 + 0.125) = W and S = 0.5W ÷ 1.125 [1]. Moments about the foot: S × 2l sin θ + 0.25S × 2l cos θ = W × l cos θ [1], so S(tan θ + 0.25) = W/2, giving (0.5/1.125)(tan θ + 0.25) = 0.5 and tan θ = 0.875 [1]. **θ = 41.2°** (3 s.f.) [1].

**11. (a)** About to tilt about D, so the reaction at C is zero [1]. D is 4 m from A and the plank's weight acts 3 m from A, 1 m on the A side of D. With the man x m beyond D, moments about D: 75g × x = 30g × 1 [1], so x = 0.4 m [1]. Distance from B = 2 − 0.4 = **1.6 m** [1].

**(b)** Vertically: R_D = 105g [1] = **1029 N (1030 N to 3 s.f.)** [1].

**12. (a)** Moments about A: T × 2a [1] = W × a cos 30°, so **T = (√3/4)W** [1].

**(b)** T is perpendicular to the rod, so it makes 30° with the vertical [1]. Vertically: R + T cos 30° = W, so R = W − (3/8)W = (5/8)W [1]. Horizontally: F = T sin 30° = (√3/8)W [1]. Equilibrium needs F ≤ μR [1], so μ ≥ (√3/8) ÷ (5/8) = **√3/5** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the full length along an inclined rod as the moment arm of a vertical weight.
- Drawing the peg reaction or the string tension in Question 12 vertically instead of perpendicular to the rod.
- Assuming limiting friction in part (b) of Question 7, where you must instead compare the friction needed with the friction available.
- Leaving out a conclusion after a comparison.
- Rounding the tension before using it to find hinge components.
