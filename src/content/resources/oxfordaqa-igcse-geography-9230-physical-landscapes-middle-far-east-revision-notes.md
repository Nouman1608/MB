These notes cover section 3.1.3.1, Physical landscapes in Middle and Far East, of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Geography (9230) specification, Version 4.3. Key idea: "The Middle and Far East have a range of diverse landscapes." The content is "an overview of the location of major upland/lowland areas and river systems." For full explanations, read the [Middle and Far East study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-geography-9230-physical-landscapes-middle-far-east/). For the whole unit, see the [Living with the Physical Environment study guide](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-geography-living-with-the-physical-environment/).

## Specification checklist

- **Location of major upland areas.**
- **Location of major lowland areas.**
- **Location of major river systems.**
- Be able to describe them using an atlas or map.

## Region used in these notes

- **Middle East:** south-west Asia: Turkey, Iran, Iraq, the Levant, Arabian Peninsula.
- **Far East:** East Asia (China, Mongolia, Japan, Koreas) plus South-East Asia, and the Himalayan edge of South Asia.
- Check your teacher's definition. Always use an atlas to confirm names.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Upland | High ground: mountains, plateaus, hills |
| Lowland | Low, flat or gently sloping land |
| Plateau | Large, high, flat-topped area |
| Basin | Low area surrounded by higher land |
| Delta | Fan of land at a river mouth, built from deposited sediment |
| River system | Main river and tributaries, with the basin it drains |
| Source / mouth | Start / end of a river |
| Relief | Shape and height of the land |

## Why such variety

- **Plate collisions:** India meeting Eurasia raised the Himalayas and the Tibetan Plateau.
- **Alpine belt:** Zagros, Elburz, Taurus, Pontic mountains.
- **Pacific Ring of Fire:** Japan, Taiwan, Philippines have volcanoes and earthquakes.
- **Rivers fill basins and build plains and deltas.**
- **Climate varies** from desert to monsoon.

## Uplands (learn these)

| Area | Where | Fact (approximate) |
|---|---|---|
| Himalayas | North edge of South Asia | Everest about 8 849 m (Nepal and China) |
| Tibetan Plateau | South-west China | Highest and largest plateau; average over 4 000 m |
| Karakoram, Hindu Kush, Pamirs | North-west of the Himalayas | Part of the same high mountain arc |
| Kunlun, Tian Shan, Altai | Northern Tibet to Central Asia and Mongolia | Mountain ranges |
| Mongolian Plateau, Gobi | Mongolia and northern China | High, dry steppe and desert |
| Qinling | Central China | Divides the Yellow River and Yangtze basins |
| Japanese Alps, Mt Fuji | Japan | Fuji about 3 776 m; most of Japan is mountainous |

## Rivers (learn these)

| River | Rises | Ends | Fact (approximate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tigris | Eastern Turkey | Persian Gulf (joins Euphrates) | About 1 850 km |
| Euphrates | Eastern Turkey | Persian Gulf | About 2 800 km |
| Jordan | Mount Hermon area | Dead Sea | Short; scarce water |
| Yangtze | Tibetan Plateau | East China Sea at Shanghai | About 6 300 km; longest in Asia |
| Yellow River | Tibetan Plateau | Bohai Sea | About 5 460 km; silt from the Loess Plateau |
| Pearl River | Yunnan Plateau | South China Sea | Large industrial delta |
| Mekong | Tibetan Plateau | South China Sea (Vietnam) | About 4 350 km; six countries |
| Irrawaddy | Northern Myanmar | Andaman Sea | Delta for rice |

**Pattern:** many Asian rivers rise on the Tibetan Plateau and flow out in different directions. Middle East rivers rise in Turkey and Iran and flow through dry land.

## Describing a landscape (method)

1. **Location** with compass direction and a landmark.
2. **Relief:** high, low, flat or steep; name features.
3. **Drainage:** source, direction, mouth.
4. **Link** features (plateau to mountains to plain to delta).
5. **Evidence:** heights, names, scale.

Model: "The Yangtze rises on the Tibetan Plateau in the west of China, flows east through mountains and the Sichuan Basin and across lowlands, and reaches the East China Sea near Shanghai. It is about 6 300 km long."

## Exam traps

- Tigris and Euphrates are in the Middle East; Yangtze and Yellow River are in China.
- Himalayas lie between South Asia and Tibet.
- A plateau is flat-topped; do not call it a mountain range.
- Describe rivers with source, direction **and** mouth.
- Use the words "about" for measurements; check against the atlas.
- Link lowlands with farming and high populations.

## Self-test

1. Name the plateau where the Yangtze, Yellow River and Mekong rise.
2. Which mountains run along the western side of Iran?
3. Name the lowland between the Tigris and Euphrates.
4. Where does the Yellow River end?
5. Name the highest mountain in the world and the countries it lies between.
6. Which river reaches the sea near Shanghai?
7. Name **two** deltas in South-East Asia.
8. Which desert is found in the Tarim Basin?
9. Why are plains and deltas densely populated?
10. Describe the location of the Tibetan Plateau.

**Answers**

1. The Tibetan Plateau.
2. The Zagros Mountains.
3. Mesopotamia (the Tigris-Euphrates plain).
4. In the Bohai Sea (in north-east China).
5. Mount Everest, between Nepal and China (Tibet).
6. The Yangtze.
7. Any two: Mekong Delta; Red River Delta; Irrawaddy Delta; Chao Phraya delta or basin.
8. The Taklamakan Desert.
9. They are flat and fertile, with water for irrigation and transport, so they support farming and cities.
10. In south-west China, north of the Himalayas and south of the Kunlun mountains.
