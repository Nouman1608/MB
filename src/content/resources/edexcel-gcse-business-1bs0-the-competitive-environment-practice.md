These questions cover content item 1.2.4, The competitive environment, in Topic 1.2 Spotting a business opportunity of the Pearson Edexcel Level 1/Level 2 GCSE (9-1) in Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022. Calculators may be used. Market share is taken as the business's sales ÷ total market sales × 100 (a standard formula). Every business and figure is invented and the mark splits are the writer's own.

## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a factor named in the specification for judging the strengths and weaknesses of competitors? **[1]**

A: Product range

B: Interest rates

C: Number of shareholders

D: Business aims

**2.** A competitor receives many online complaints about late deliveries and unanswered emails. Which factor is this a weakness in? **[1]**

A: Price

B: Location

C: Customer service

D: Product range

**3.** Define the term competitor. **[1]**

**4.** Give **two** ways a small business could find out about the strengths and weaknesses of its competitors. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** way the arrival of a new competitor could affect an existing business's pricing decisions. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **one** reason why competing on quality may reduce a small business's profit. **[3]**

### Section B: case-study questions

**7.** *Hartwell Window Cleaning* cleans windows in a town where about 2,300 homes have their windows cleaned each month. Hartwell cleans 300 of them at £18 a home. The other 2,000 homes use three rivals.

| Rival | Homes a month | Price per home (£) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| SparkleCo | 760 | 15 | Cheapest; often misses booked days |
| ClearView | 540 | 22 | Also cleans gutters and conservatory roofs |
| Pane Free | 700 | 17 | Online booking and text reminders |

**(a)** Calculate Hartwell's market share. Give your answer to one decimal place. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate how much more, as a percentage, Hartwell charges than SparkleCo. **[2]**

**(c)** Identify **one** strength and **one** weakness of SparkleCo. **[2]**

**(d)** Analyse how competition from ClearView and Pane Free could affect Hartwell's decisions. **[6]**

**8.** The owner of Hartwell is considering two responses to competition.

Option 1: cut the price to £15 a home to match SparkleCo. He expects to clean 360 homes a month.

Option 2: keep the price at £18 but add gutter clearing and an online booking system costing £250 a month. He expects to clean 330 homes a month.

Justify which option Hartwell should choose. **[9]**

**9.** *Oakmere Books* is an independent bookshop. A large chain bookshop has opened two streets away, selling many titles at lower prices. Outline **one** way Oakmere Books could respond to this competition. **[2]**

**10.** Explain **one** reason why a business might choose to locate close to its competitors. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** A: Product range [1]. **[1]**

**2.** C: Customer service [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A business that sells similar products or services to the same customers [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Any two from: read online reviews of competitors [1]; visit or buy from competitors as a customer [1]; compare prices on their websites; read market reports; observe how many customers they attract. **[2]**

**5.** A new competitor may charge lower prices to win customers [1]. The existing business may then have to cut its own price to avoid losing sales [1], which reduces the revenue and profit it earns from each sale [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* An alternative chain is that the business keeps its price but must justify it with better quality or service.

**6.** Raising quality usually means better materials or more skilled, better-paid staff [1], which increases the business's costs [1]. If customers will not pay a higher price to cover these costs, the profit made on each sale falls [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** 300 ÷ 2,300 × 100 [1] = **13.0%** [1].

**(b)** £18 − £15 = £3 [1]. £3 ÷ £15 × 100 = **20%** more [1].

**(c)** Strength: the lowest price at £15 a home [1]. Weakness: it often misses booked days, which is poor customer service [1].

**(d)** ClearView offers a wider product range, cleaning gutters and conservatory roofs [1], so Hartwell may lose customers who want all these jobs done by one firm unless it adds similar services [1]. Pane Free offers online booking and text reminders, which is better customer service and more convenient [1], so Hartwell may need to invest in a booking system to keep up [1]. Both changes would raise Hartwell's costs [1], so it would need to win enough extra homes, or keep its price above SparkleCo's, to protect its profit [1]. **[12]**

**8.** Option 1: revenue now is 300 × £18 = £5,400 a month [1]. At £15 and 360 homes, revenue is 360 × £15 = £5,400 [1], so Hartwell would clean 60 more homes for no extra revenue, and the extra time and fuel would reduce profit [1]. SparkleCo may also cut its price again, starting a price war [1].

Option 2: revenue is 330 × £18 = £5,940 [1], and after the £250 cost the gain is £5,690, which is £290 more than now [1]. It copies ClearView's range and Pane Free's online booking, closing two weaknesses [1], and it avoids competing on price with the cheapest firm [1].

Judgement: Hartwell should choose Option 2, because it brings in more money for less extra work, provided the forecast of 330 homes is met [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Comparing the revenue of each option shows why a price cut can leave a business no better off, which supports the judgement.

**9.** Oakmere could offer services the chain does not, such as ordering rare books or holding author events [1], giving customers a reason to visit even though prices are higher, so it competes on customer service and range rather than price [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Customers often go to an area where several similar businesses are found so that they can compare them [1]. Locating there means many potential customers pass the business without it paying for extra promotion [1], so it can win sales if it is strong on another factor, such as price or customer service [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the rivals' total (2,000) instead of the whole market (2,300) for market share.
- Using £18 instead of £15 as the base when finding how much more Hartwell charges.
- Naming a strength without saying which factor it belongs to.
- Assuming a price cut must raise revenue.
- Writing about competition in general rather than the named rivals.
