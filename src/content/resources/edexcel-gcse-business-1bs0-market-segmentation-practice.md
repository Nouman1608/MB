## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a way of identifying market segments named in the specification? **[1]**

A: Market share

B: Lifestyle

C: Profit

D: Cash flow

**2.** A bookshop runs a club for readers aged 8 to 12. Which basis of segmentation is it using? **[1]**

A: Income

B: Location

C: Age

D: Lifestyle

**3.** Define the term market segmentation. **[1]**

**4.** State **two** things a market map can help a business to identify, as given in the specification. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** benefit to a small business of segmenting its market. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **one** limitation of using a market map to identify a gap in the market. **[3]**

### Section B: case-study questions

**7.** *Lighthouse Swim School* teaches swimming in a town. The owner, Bronwen, has estimated the following for three segments.

| Segment | People in the town | Share interested | Monthly spend each (£) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Children under 5 | 1,800 | 20% | 36 |
| Children aged 5 to 11 | 3,600 | 12% | 28 |
| Adults | 22,000 | 2% | 40 |

**(a)** Calculate the number of interested people in the "Children aged 5 to 11" segment. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the monthly value of each segment. **[3]**

**(c)** Identify the segment with the highest monthly value. **[1]**

**(d)** Outline **one** way Lighthouse Swim School could change its service to target the "Children under 5" segment. **[2]**

**8.** *Scoop Street* is planning an ice cream parlour in a seaside town. The owner rated four rivals from 1 (low) to 10 (high) on price and on how unusual their flavours are.

| Rival | Price | Unusual flavours |
|---|---|---|
| Frosty's | 2 | 2 |
| Seafront Cones | 3 | 3 |
| Harbour Gelato | 8 | 9 |
| Sundae Best | 4 | 2 |

**(a)** Identify where on the market map the competition is strongest. **[1]**

**(b)** Analyse how Scoop Street could use this market map to decide where to position itself. **[6]**

**9.** Bronwen from Lighthouse Swim School can afford to add classes for only one new segment. She is deciding between:

Option 1: adult beginner classes in the evenings.

Option 2: parent-and-baby classes for children under 5 on weekday mornings.

Use your answers to question 7 and the information above. Justify which option Bronwen should choose. **[9]**

**10.** Explain **one** disadvantage to a small business of targeting a very narrow market segment. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** B: Lifestyle [1]. **[1]**

**2.** C: Age [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Dividing a market into groups of customers who share similar characteristics, such as age or income [1]. **[1]**

**4.** A gap in the market [1]; the competition [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Segmenting lets a business identify the group whose needs it can meet best [1]. It can then design its product and promotion for that group instead of trying to appeal to everyone [1], so less money is wasted on promotion that reaches people who will not buy, and sales from the target group rise [1]. **[3]**

**6.** A gap on a market map only shows that no business currently offers that position [1]. It does not show whether customers want a product there [1], so a business that fills the gap without research could find there is too little demand to cover its costs [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Other valid limitations are that positions are opinions, or that the map shows only two features; each must be developed with a consequence.

**7. (a)** 3,600 × 12% = **432 people** [1].

**(b)** Under 5: 1,800 × 20% = 360 people; 360 × £36 = **£12,960** [1]. Aged 5 to 11: 432 × £28 = **£12,096** [1]. Adults: 22,000 × 2% = 440 people; 440 × £40 = **£17,600** [1].

**(c)** Adults [1].

**(d)** It could run parent-and-baby sessions in a warm, shallow pool [1], because very young children need a parent in the water with them and gentle introductions, which would make parents more willing to book [1]. **[7]**

**8. (a)** Low price and ordinary flavours (prices 2 to 4, flavours 2 to 3), where Frosty's, Seafront Cones and Sundae Best are placed [1].

**(b)** The map shows three rivals crowded at low prices with ordinary flavours [1], so if Scoop Street positioned itself there it would have to compete mainly on price, which could reduce its profit per cone [1]. Only Harbour Gelato offers unusual flavours, and it charges a high price [1]. There is a gap for unusual flavours at a mid price, around 5 to 6 [1], which could attract tourists who want something different but find Harbour Gelato expensive [1]. However, the scores are the owner's own opinions, so Scoop Street should test the gap with research to check that enough customers want this position [1]. **[7]**

**9.** Option 1: adults have the highest monthly value at £17,600 [1], which is £4,640 more than the under-5 segment [1]. However, only 2% of adults are interested, so Bronwen depends on a small share of a large group, and the estimate could easily be wrong [1]. Evening classes may also clash with existing children's lessons after school, limiting pool time [1].

Option 2: the under-5 segment is worth £12,960 a month [1], and 20% of families are interested, a much higher share, so the demand estimate is likely to be more dependable [1]. Weekday mornings are when school-age children are in school, so the pool would otherwise be quiet [1]. Children who start at under 5 may stay on into the 5 to 11 classes, bringing future sales [1].

Judgement: Bronwen should choose Option 2. Although adults have a higher estimated value, the parent-and-baby classes use spare pool time, rest on stronger interest and feed her existing children's classes, so the overall risk is lower [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* A recommendation of Option 1 can also earn full marks if it is supported by the higher value and addresses the weaker interest figure.

**10.** A narrow segment contains fewer potential customers [1], so even if the business wins many of them, total sales may be too low [1] to cover its fixed costs, putting its survival at risk if the segment's tastes change [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Multiplying by the percentage as a whole number (by 12 instead of 12%).
- Giving the number of interested people when the value in pounds was asked for.
- Choosing the biggest segment by population without checking interest or spending.
- Saying a market map gap guarantees sales.
- Analysing a map without naming the rivals or the scores.
