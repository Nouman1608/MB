## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** State **two** reasons why the balance in a cash book may differ from the balance on the bank statement on the same date. **[2]**

**2.** Explain the difference between a standing order and a direct debit. **[2]**

**3.** Explain why a favourable balance appears as a credit on a bank statement but as a debit in the cash book. **[2]**

**4.** State whether each item is used to update the cash book or is shown in the bank reconciliation statement.

**(a)** A cheque paid to a supplier that has not yet been presented **[1]**

**(b)** Interest charged by the bank on an overdraft **[1]**

**(c)** A payment by a customer directly into the business's bank account **[1]**

**(d)** A payment wrongly taken from the account by the bank **[1]**

**5.** A business's updated cash book shows an overdraft of $860. Cheques of $1,200 have not yet been presented. There are no other differences. Calculate the balance on the bank statement and state whether the account is in funds or overdrawn. **[2]**

**6.** Explain **one** purpose of preparing a bank reconciliation statement. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** Grace's cash book showed a debit balance of $2,145 at 31 May 2028. Her bank statement showed a credit balance of $2,412 on the same date. A comparison showed:

1. Bank charges of $38 and a direct debit of $96 for telephone appeared only on the bank statement.

2. A credit transfer of $725 from a customer and interest received of $9 appeared only on the bank statement.

3. A cheque for $180 from a customer, S Pillay, was dishonoured; this appeared only on the bank statement.

4. A cheque for $517 paid to a supplier had been entered in the cash book as $571.

5. A deposit of $890 made on 31 May did not appear on the bank statement.

6. Cheques for $415 and $268 did not appear on the bank statement.

**(a)** Prepare the cash book (bank columns) to show the updated balance at 31 May 2028. **[7]**

**(b)** Prepare a bank reconciliation statement at 31 May 2028, starting with the balance on the bank statement. **[5]**

**(c)** State the bank figure that will appear in Grace's statement of financial position at 31 May 2028. **[1]**

**8.** Hamza's updated cash book showed a credit balance (overdraft) of $2,340 at 31 January 2028. On that date:

- cheques for $1,150 and $620 had not been presented
- a deposit of $980 had not been credited by the bank
- the bank had credited a $300 deposit belonging to another customer to Hamza's account.

**(a)** Prepare a bank reconciliation statement at 31 January 2028, starting with the balance in the updated cash book. **[5]**

**(b)** State how the bank balance will be shown in Hamza's statement of financial position. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the bank statement shows a smaller overdraft than the cash book. **[2]**

**9.** Joseph's cash book showed a debit balance of $1,060 at 30 September 2027. Comparing it with the bank statement revealed:

1. A standing order of $250 for rent.

2. A credit transfer of $400 from a customer.

3. Bank charges of $22.

4. A payment of $90 for stationery had been entered on the debit side of the cash book.

**(a)** Calculate the updated cash book balance at 30 September 2027. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why item 4 changes the cash book balance by $180, not $90. **[2]**

**10.** Fatima runs a café. Most customers pay by card and she pays her suppliers by online transfer. She writes no cheques.

**(a)** Explain **two** ways in which her bank reconciliation differs from that of a business that pays and receives mainly by cheque. **[4]**

**(b)** Advise Fatima whether she should reconcile her bank account monthly or weekly. Justify your answer. **[2]**

**11.** In a small business, the owner's assistant writes up the cash book, makes the payments and also prepares the bank reconciliation statement.

**(a)** Explain **one** risk of this arrangement. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest **one** improvement and explain how it reduces the risk. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Unpresented cheques or uncredited deposits (items in the cash book not yet on the statement) [1]. Bank charges, interest, direct debits, standing orders or credit transfers not yet in the cash book [1]. Also accept: errors by the business or the bank. **[2]**

**2.** A standing order pays a fixed amount at regular intervals on the account holder's instruction [1]. A direct debit lets the receiving business collect amounts, which may vary, from the account [1]. **[2]**

**3.** The bank owes the money to the business, so the bank shows it as a liability (credit) in its own records [1]. To the business the money in the bank is an asset, so it is a debit in the cash book [1]. **[2]**

**4.** **(a)** Bank reconciliation statement [1]

**(b)** Update the cash book [1]

**(c)** Update the cash book [1]

**(d)** Bank reconciliation statement [1] **[4]**

**5.** −$860 + $1,200 = $340 [1]. The account is in funds: a credit balance of $340 on the bank statement [1]. **[2]**

**6.** It explains the difference between the two balances [1], so the owner can be sure the cash book figure used in the statement of financial position is correct [1]. Also accept a purpose with an explanation such as: it reveals errors by the bank or the business, so they can be corrected; it helps to detect fraud or unauthorised payments. **[2]**

**(b)** As a current liability (bank overdraft) of $2,340 [1].

**(c)** The bank has not yet paid cheques of $1,770, which is more than the $980 deposit it has not yet received [1], and it has wrongly added $300 to the account, so its overdraft is $1,090 smaller [1]. **[8]**

**9.** **(a)** $1,060 − $250 [1] + $400 [1] − $22 [1] − $180 = **$1,008** debit [1].

**(b)** The $90 was added to the balance when it should have been deducted [1], so $90 is needed to cancel the wrong entry and another $90 to record the payment correctly [1]. **[6]**

**10.** **(a)** Two ways, each identified and explained. There are no unpresented cheques [1], because online transfers to suppliers usually leave the account almost immediately [1]. More items appear on the bank statement before the cash book [1], such as card processing fees and direct debits, so updating the cash book is a bigger part of the task [1]. Also accept: few uncredited deposits, because card takings reach the bank quickly.

**(b)** Weekly [1], because a café has many card receipts each day and online banking shows them immediately, so errors, card fees or unauthorised payments are found while they are recent [1]. Also accept monthly, if justified by low volume of transactions or limited time. **[6]**

**11.** **(a)** The assistant could make an unauthorised payment to himself or herself [1] and hide it in the cash book and the reconciliation, because no one else checks the records [1].

**(b)** The owner, or another person, should prepare or check the bank reconciliation using the bank statement [1], so any payment not supported by documents is noticed because that person did not write up the cash book [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting bank charges, standing orders and credit transfers in the reconciliation statement.
- Treating a "Dr" bank statement balance as money in the account.
- Ending the statement on the original cash book balance.
- Correcting a wrong-side entry with the single amount.
- Giving general digital-banking points without applying them to the business in the question.
