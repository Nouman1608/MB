## 4.7.2.1 Electromagnetism (both tiers)

- Current in a wire → **magnetic field around the wire**: concentric circles centred on the wire.
- Larger current → stronger field. Further from wire → weaker field.
- Reverse the current → field direction reverses.
- **Solenoid**: fields of the turns add, so the field is **stronger**; inside it is **strong and uniform**; outside
  it is shaped like a **bar magnet's** field.
- **Iron core** → the core becomes an induced magnet → field strength increases.
- **Electromagnet** = solenoid + iron core. Can be switched off; soft iron loses magnetism quickly.
- More current or more turns per length → stronger electromagnet.

**Method: explaining a device from a diagram**

```
current flows in coil → core becomes an electromagnet → attracts iron armature
→ armature moves (closes/opens contacts, strikes, releases)
→ current stops → core loses magnetism → spring returns armature
```

Electric bell: armature movement **breaks** the circuit, so the cycle repeats and the hammer vibrates.
Relay: a small current in one circuit switches a larger current in another.

## 4.7.2.2 Motor effect (Higher tier only)

- Current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field → magnet and conductor **exert a force on each other**.
- **Fleming's left-hand rule**: First finger = Field (N → S); seCond finger = Current (+ → −); thuMb = Motion
  (force).
- Reverse current **or** field → force reverses. Reverse **both** → unchanged.
- Wire **parallel** to field → **no force**.
- Factors increasing the force: **flux density B**, **current I**, **length l** in the field.

### The equation (on the Physics equation sheet)

```
F = B I l        F in N, B in T, I in A, l in m
B = F / (I l)    I = F / (B l)    l = F / (B I)
```

Mini-examples:

```
B = 0.40 T, I = 2.5 A, l = 0.20 m  →  F = 0.40 × 2.5 × 0.20 = 0.20 N
F = 0.090 N, B = 0.30 T, l = 0.10 m →  I = 0.090 / (0.30 × 0.10) = 3.0 A
```

## 4.7.2.3 Electric motors (Higher tier only)

```
current opposite ways in the two sides of the coil
→ each side at right angles to field feels a force (motor effect)
→ forces in opposite directions (one up, one down)
→ coil rotates
```

- Faster: bigger current, stronger field, more turns.
- Reverse rotation: reverse current or reverse field.
- Split-ring commutator (practical d.c. motor): reverses the coil current every half turn so rotation continues in
  one direction.

## 4.7.2.4 Loudspeakers and headphones (Higher tier only)

```
alternating current in coil (in permanent magnet's field)
→ force on coil reverses as current reverses
→ coil + cone vibrate at the frequency of the current
→ pressure variations in air = sound wave
```

- Larger current variation → larger vibration → louder sound.
- Steady d.c. → steady force → no vibration → no sound.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Solenoid | A coil of wire; carrying a current, it has a field like a bar magnet |
| Electromagnet | A solenoid with an iron core |
| Armature | Iron part attracted by an electromagnet in a device |
| Motor effect | Force between a current-carrying conductor and a magnetic field |
| Magnetic flux density (B) | Measure of field strength, in tesla (T) |
| Fleming's left-hand rule | Gives the relative directions of field, current and force |
| Conventional current | Direction from positive to negative terminal |

## Exam traps

- Field around a wire drawn as straight lines or as one circle with no direction.
- Iron core said to "increase the current".
- Right hand used; first and second fingers swapped.
- Length left in cm (8.0 cm must be 0.080 m).
- F = BIl used when the wire is parallel to the field.
- Motor answers with no mention of currents in **opposite directions** in the two sides.
- Loudspeaker described with d.c. or with no link between current frequency and sound frequency.

## Quick self-test

1. Describe the shape of the field around a straight current-carrying wire.
2. Give two ways to make an electromagnet stronger.
3. Describe the field inside a solenoid.
4. Why does an electric bell keep ringing while the switch is pressed?
5. (Higher tier only) In Fleming's left-hand rule, what does the second finger represent?
6. (Higher tier only) The current and the field are both reversed. What happens to the force?
7. (Higher tier only) State the force on a wire lying parallel to a magnetic field.
8. (Higher tier only) Calculate F for B = 0.20 T, I = 5.0 A, l = 0.30 m.
9. (Higher tier only) A force of 0.12 N acts on 0.25 m of wire carrying 4.0 A. Calculate B.
10. (Higher tier only) Why does a coil carrying a current in a magnetic field rotate?
11. (Higher tier only) What happens to the force on a loudspeaker coil when the current reverses?
12. (Higher tier only) Why would a loudspeaker connected to a battery make no continuous sound?

### Answers

1. Concentric circles centred on the wire.
2. Add an iron core; increase the current (or add more turns).
3. Strong and uniform.
4. The moving armature breaks the circuit, the core loses magnetism, the spring returns the armature and remakes the
   circuit, so the cycle repeats.
5. The current.
6. It is unchanged.
7. Zero.
8. F = 0.20 × 5.0 × 0.30 = **0.30 N**.
9. B = 0.12 ÷ (4.0 × 0.25) = **0.12 T**.
10. The currents in the two sides are in opposite directions, so the forces on them are in opposite directions,
    which turns the coil.
11. It reverses.
12. The current is steady, so the force is steady and the cone does not vibrate.
