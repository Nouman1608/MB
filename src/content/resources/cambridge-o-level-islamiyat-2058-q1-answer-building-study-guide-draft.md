# Question 1: building a two-part answer

**Question 1 of Paper 1 is compulsory and worth 8 marks.** The syllabus says three passages are set from Appendix 1 and candidates may choose any two. For each passage you are asked to explore (1) the major theme or themes in it and (2) the importance of those themes in the lives of Muslims today. The Cambridge level descriptors give Question 1(a) a maximum of 4 marks for knowledge (AO1), and part (b) is marked for understanding (AO2) on levels worth 1 to 4 marks. Candidates must show knowledge of the original Arabic as well as the translation.

Related pages: [Major Themes of the Qur'an study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-islamiyat-major-themes-of-the-quran/) and [revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-islamiyat-major-themes-of-the-quran-revision-notes/).

## What the levels reward

| Part | Objective | Top level in the syllabus grid | What earns it |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) | AO1 knowledge | Level 4 = 4 marks on Question 1 | A well-structured, clear and comprehensive response with extensive, accurate knowledge, detailed points, and likely quotation of Qur'an verses or Hadiths to support the points |
| (b) | AO2 understanding | Level 2 = 3 to 4 marks | A clear understanding of the question, using your own opinions to engage with it and reasoning that shows understanding |

At the lower level in (b), the syllabus describes answers that give descriptive and factual references with limited discussion (1 to 2 marks). The difference between the levels is **reasoning**, not length.

## A four-step routine for each passage

1. **Place it.** Name the group it belongs to: Allah in himself (passages 1 to 5), Allah's relationship with the created world (6 to 10) or Allah's Messengers (11 to 15).
2. **State the theme in one sentence** in your own words, then point to a word or verse number that shows it.
3. **Add one more point** (a second theme, or a link to a related verse), so part (a) is developed and not a single line.
4. **Answer part (b) with an action**: what a Muslim does, believes or avoids today because of the theme, and why the passage gives that reason.

## Worked example 1: Sura 112 (Appendix 1, passage 5)

**Part (a), themes.** The passage is a complete statement of tawhid, the oneness of Allah. Its four verses state that Allah is one, that He is eternal and absolute, that He neither has offspring nor was born, and that nothing is like Him. Together these deny any partner, any family, any beginning and any comparison. (A strong answer names the Arabic title, al-Ikhlas, and notes that the syllabus lists it in the group "Allah in himself".)

**Part (b), importance today.** Muslims recite it often in prayer, so the statement of Allah's oneness is renewed daily. Because Allah has no partner, a Muslim directs worship, requests for help and trust to Him alone and does not treat wealth, status or any person as a rival to Him. *Why this scores:* the action (directing worship and trust to Allah alone) is explained by the theme, not just described.

## Worked example 2: Sura 99 (Appendix 1, passage 9)

**Part (a), themes.** The passage describes the earth shaken to its utmost and throwing out its burdens, and people being shown their deeds. Its closing verses say that anyone who has done an atom's weight of good will see it and anyone who has done an atom's weight of wrong will see it. The themes are accountability on the Last Day and the weight of small deeds. It belongs to the group "Allah's relationship with the created world".

**Part (b), importance today.** The reminder that even the smallest deed is recorded leads a Muslim to take small acts of kindness seriously and to avoid small wrongs such as a careless word, because all are seen on the Day of Judgement. It encourages honesty even when no one is watching.

## Common mistakes

- Retelling the passage verse by verse instead of naming its theme.
- Giving one theme and stopping, so part (a) cannot reach the top level.
- A part (b) that says "it is important" without saying what a Muslim does differently.
- Using the same application for both passages: each passage should have its own.
- Ignoring the Arabic: the syllabus requires knowledge of the original as well as the translation.
