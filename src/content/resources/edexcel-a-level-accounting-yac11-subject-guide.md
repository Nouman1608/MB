Pearson Edexcel International A Level Accounting (YAC11) is a modular
course for students who want a thorough grounding in financial and
management accounting, from double entry bookkeeping through to
budgeting, costing and project appraisal. Its structure is unusually
compact: just two units, each a 3-hour written paper out of 200 marks
and worth 50% of the full International A Level. Unit 1 on its own
leads to the International Advanced Subsidiary (XAC11); the full
International A Level requires both units. First teaching was
September 2015, and the specification in use is Issue 2.

## How the course is organised

The content is divided between the two units, with the first building
the bookkeeping and reporting foundations and the second applying them
to companies and to decision-making.

**Unit 1: The Accounting System and Costing (IAS)**

- **Principles of accounting and double entry bookkeeping** covers the
  role and purpose of accounting, the double entry system, accounting
  concepts and conventions, the distinction between capital and revenue
  expenditure, and depreciation of non-current assets.
- **Control procedures** deals with the trial balance, control accounts
  and the correction of errors.
- **Financial statements of organisations** develops the preparation of
  final accounts.
- **Introduction to costing** introduces how businesses measure and
  classify costs.
- **Analysis of accounting statements** turns from preparing figures to
  interpreting them.
- **Social and ethical accounting** looks at accounting's wider
  responsibilities.

**Unit 2: Corporate and Management Accounting (IA2)**

- **Limited companies**, **investment ratios** and the **statement of
  cash flows** extend financial accounting to corporate reporting.
- **Budgeting**, **standard costing**, **project appraisal**,
  **break-even analysis**, and **marginal costing and absorption
  costing** form the management accounting core, focused on planning
  and decision-making.
- **Information and communication technology (ICT) in accounting**
  considers how technology supports accounting work.

## How it's assessed

| Unit | Paper code | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unit 1 | WAC11/01 | The Accounting System and Costing | 3 hours | 200 | 50% |
| Unit 2 | WAC12/01 | Corporate and Management Accounting | 3 hours | 200 | 50% |

Both units are externally assessed and share the same shape:

- **Section A** contains two compulsory multi-part data-response
  questions, each worth 55 marks.
- **Section B** offers four multi-part questions, of which candidates
  answer three, each worth 30 marks.

Because the qualification is modular, the two units can be taken in
different series. Unit 1 can also stand alone as the International
Advanced Subsidiary, which makes the course flexible for students who
want to stop after the first stage or bank one unit before taking the
other.

## How the parts fit together

Unit 2 cannot be tackled properly without Unit 1. The double entry
system, accounting concepts and the trial balance are the language in
which every later topic is written. When you prepare a statement of
cash flows for a limited company in Unit 2, you are reworking the same
records and adjustments learned at IAS. Depreciation, for example,
appears first as a bookkeeping entry in Unit 1 and later as a non-cash
item in the cash flow statement.

Interpretation is another thread. The analysis of accounting statements
in Unit 1 introduces ratio-based thinking, and investment ratios in
Unit 2 extend it to the perspective of shareholders. In the same way,
the introduction to costing at IAS lays the ground for standard
costing, marginal and absorption costing, and break-even analysis.

The section structure of each paper rewards two distinct skills. The
compulsory data-response questions in Section A test accurate,
methodical preparation of accounts and calculations. The optional
questions in Section B still involve calculation, but the choice means
you need to recognise quickly which questions suit your strengths.
Written evaluation, such as advising on a decision or commenting on the
results of a calculation, appears across both units.

## Planning your study

A practical sequence is to follow the specification order:

1. **Start with double entry.** Spend time on the principles and
   bookkeeping until journal entries, ledgers and the trial balance feel
   routine. Almost every later error can be traced back here.
2. **Move to control procedures and financial statements.** Control
   accounts and the correction of errors make much more sense once you
   have prepared several sets of accounts yourself.
3. **Finish Unit 1 with costing, analysis and ethics,** linking
   interpretation back to the statements you can now prepare.
4. **In Unit 2, begin with limited companies and cash flows,** then work
   through the management accounting topics, which share many
   techniques with each other.

One natural pattern is to sit Unit 1 after the first year of study and
Unit 2 after the second, but the modular structure allows others, so
confirm your entry plan with your school. With papers of
3 hours, stamina matters: begin full timed papers once a unit's content
is complete, and use the exam-preparation page for advice on managing
the two sections.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many units are in Edexcel International A Level Accounting?**
Two. Unit 1, The Accounting System and Costing, and Unit 2, Corporate and Management Accounting, are each a 3-hour paper out of 200 marks and worth 50% of the full qualification.

**Can I get an AS-equivalent qualification from Unit 1 alone?**
Yes. The International Advanced Subsidiary in Accounting (XAC11) is awarded on Unit 1 alone. The full International A Level (YAC11) requires both units.

**What is the structure of each Accounting paper?**
Section A has two compulsory multi-part data-response questions worth 55 marks each. Section B has four multi-part questions worth 30 marks each, of which you answer three.

**Which topics are in Unit 2?**
Unit 2 covers limited companies, investment ratios, the statement of cash flows, budgeting, standard costing, project appraisal, break-even analysis, marginal and absorption costing, and ICT in accounting.

**Is IAL Accounting coursework-based?**
No. Both units are externally assessed written papers.
