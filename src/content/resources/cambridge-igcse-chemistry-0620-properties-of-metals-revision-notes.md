## Outcome 1: compare physical properties

| | Metals (general) | Non-metals (general) |
|---|---|---|
| Thermal conductivity | good | poor |
| Electrical conductivity | good, as solid and as liquid | poor (graphite is the exception) |
| Malleability | malleable: hammered into sheets | brittle when solid |
| Ductility | ductile: drawn into wires | not ductile |
| Melting and boiling points | usually high; most solid at room temperature | usually low; many are gases |

**Exceptions to name only when relevant:** mercury (liquid metal), sodium and potassium (soft metals that melt below 100 °C), graphite (non-metal that conducts electricity), diamond and graphite (non-metals with very high melting points).

**Best single test:** a solid that conducts electricity is a metal, unless it is graphite.

**Compare answers:** one property per sentence, metal side and non-metal side together, linked by "whereas".

## Outcome 2: describe chemical properties

Three reactions only. Learn the general equation and one example of each.

### Dilute acids

    metal + acid → salt + hydrogen
    Fe + 2HCl → FeCl2 + H2        (iron(II) chloride)
    Mg + H2SO4 → MgSO4 + H2       (magnesium sulfate)

- Observations: fizzing, metal dissolves, mixture warms.
- Hydrogen test: lighted splint gives a squeaky pop.
- Hydrochloric acid → chloride; sulfuric acid → sulfate.
- Copper, silver and gold: **no reaction**.

### Cold water and steam

    metal + cold water → metal hydroxide + hydrogen
    2Na + 2H2O → 2NaOH + H2
    metal + steam → metal oxide + hydrogen
    Mg + H2O → MgO + H2

- Cold water → **hydroxide**; steam → **oxide**.
- Solution after a cold-water reaction is **alkaline** (the hydroxide dissolves).
- Only the most reactive metals react with cold water; magnesium needs steam to react quickly.

### Oxygen

    metal + oxygen → metal oxide
    2Mg + O2 → 2MgO       bright white flame, white powder
    2Cu + O2 → 2CuO       no flame, surface turns black
    4Na + O2 → 2Na2O      tarnishes quickly in air

- Metal oxides are generally **basic** (they react with acids).
- Non-metal oxides such as CO₂ and SO₂ are **acidic**: a quick way to tell metals from non-metals in data questions.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Thermal conductivity | how well a material transfers heat |
| Electrical conductivity | how well a material allows an electric current to pass |
| Malleable | can be hammered or pressed into shape without breaking |
| Ductile | can be drawn out into a wire |
| Brittle | breaks or shatters when hit |
| Effervescence | bubbles of gas given off during a reaction |
| Basic oxide | oxide of a metal that reacts with an acid to form a salt and water |
| Tarnish | a dull layer of oxide forming on a fresh metal surface |

## Exam traps

- **Steam gives an oxide, not a hydroxide.** Mg + H₂O → MgO + H₂.
- **Name both properties in a pair.** "Malleable and ductile" are separate marks in many questions.
- **Hydrogen is not "seen".** You see bubbles; the squeaky pop test identifies hydrogen.
- **One clue is not enough.** In a data question, a high melting point alone does not prove an element is a metal. Look for conductivity, malleability and the type of oxide together.
- **Iron and dilute acid gives iron(II) chloride, FeCl₂**, not iron(III) chloride.
- **"Compare" needs both sides.** A list of metal properties alone does not compare anything.
- **Graphite conducts but is a non-metal.** Do not use conductivity alone if the element is brittle with an acidic oxide.

## Self-test

1. State two physical properties that are typical of metals and not of non-metals.
2. Explain the difference between malleable and ductile.
3. Name a non-metal that conducts electricity.
4. Name a metal that is a liquid at room temperature.
5. Write the general word equation for a metal reacting with a dilute acid.
6. Name the salt formed when zinc reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid.
7. Describe the test for the gas produced in question 5.
8. State the type of product formed when a metal reacts with (a) cold water and (b) steam.
9. Write a balanced equation for magnesium reacting with steam.
10. Describe what is seen when copper is heated strongly in air.
11. An element forms an oxide that dissolves in water to give a solution of pH 3. Is the element more likely to be a metal or a non-metal? Explain.
12. Explain why sodium is stored under oil.

**Answers:**

1. Any two: good conductor of heat; good conductor of electricity; malleable; ductile; usually high melting point.
2. Malleable: can be hammered into shape. Ductile: can be drawn into a wire.
3. Graphite (carbon).
4. Mercury.
5. metal + acid → salt + hydrogen.
6. Zinc chloride.
7. Hold a lighted splint at the mouth of the tube; hydrogen burns with a squeaky pop.
8. (a) A metal hydroxide and hydrogen. (b) A metal oxide and hydrogen.
9. Mg + H₂O → MgO + H₂.
10. It does not burn; the surface turns black as copper(II) oxide forms.
11. A non-metal: pH 3 is acidic, and acidic oxides are typical of non-metals; metal oxides are basic.
12. Sodium reacts quickly with oxygen (and with water vapour) in the air; the oil keeps air away.
