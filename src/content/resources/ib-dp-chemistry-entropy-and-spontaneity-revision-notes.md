## Key definitions

- **Entropy, S:** how dispersed the matter and energy of a system are. The more ways the energy can be shared out, the higher S. Unit: J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.
- **Standard entropy, S⦵:** the entropy value per mole for a substance under standard conditions. It is positive for every pure substance above 0 K, elements included.
- **Perfect crystal at 0 K:** only one possible arrangement, so its entropy is predicted to be zero.
- **Gibbs energy change, ΔG:** links ΔH, ΔS and the absolute temperature T; it relates to the energy a reaction can supply for work.
- **Spontaneous change:** at constant pressure, any change with a negative ΔG. It may still be slow.
- **Reaction quotient, Q:** the equilibrium-constant expression evaluated for any mixture, at equilibrium or not.

## Formulas

| Quantity | Equation | Units to use |
|---|---|---|
| Standard entropy change | ΔS⦵ = ΣS⦵(products) − ΣS⦵(reactants) | J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ |
| Gibbs energy | ΔG⦵ = ΔH⦵ − TΔS⦵ | ΔH, ΔG in kJ mol⁻¹; ΔS in J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ (divide by 1000); T in K |
| Threshold temperature | T = ΔH ÷ ΔS (set ΔG = 0) | ΔH and ΔS in matching units |
| Entropy change of surroundings | ΔS(surroundings) = −ΔH ÷ T | J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ if ΔH in J mol⁻¹ |
| ΔG for any mixture | ΔG = ΔG⦵ + RT lnQ | J mol⁻¹; R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ |
| At equilibrium | ΔG⦵ = −RT lnK | J mol⁻¹ |

The guide says the data booklet gives standard entropy values, thermodynamic data and the two ΔG equations used in 1.4.4.

## Reactivity 1.4.1 Predicting and calculating ΔS

Order under the same conditions: **gas > liquid > solid**.

ΔS of the system is **positive** when:

- the number of moles of gas increases (the strongest effect);
- a solid melts, a liquid boils or a solid sublimes;
- a solid dissolves to give aqueous ions, or two substances mix;
- a substance is heated.

ΔS is **negative** for the reverse of each.

**Method in steps: ΔS⦵ from data**

1. Write the balanced equation with state symbols.
2. Multiply each S⦵ by its coefficient.
3. Sum products; sum reactants; subtract (products − reactants).
4. Compare the sign with your gas-moles prediction.

**Worked reminder.** Titanium extraction, TiCl₄(l) + 2Mg(s) → Ti(s) + 2MgCl₂(s). S⦵ values: TiCl₄(l) 252.3, Mg(s) 32.7, Ti(s) 30.7, MgCl₂(s) 89.6 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.
Products: 30.7 + (2 × 89.6) = 209.9. Reactants: 252.3 + (2 × 32.7) = 317.7.
ΔS⦵ = 209.9 − 317.7 = **−107.8 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹**. No gas is involved, but a liquid is turned into solids, so a negative value is expected.

## Reactivity 1.4.2 Using ΔG⦵ = ΔH⦵ − TΔS⦵

**Method in steps**

1. Convert ΔS to kJ K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ (÷ 1000).
2. Put T in kelvin (add 273 to °C).
3. Substitute and keep the signs in brackets.
4. Rearrange for an unknown: ΔS = (ΔH − ΔG) ÷ T, or ΔH = ΔG + TΔS.

**Worked reminder.** A reaction has ΔH⦵ = −92.0 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔG⦵ = −33.0 kJ mol⁻¹ at 298 K.
ΔS⦵ = (−92.0 − (−33.0)) ÷ 298 = −0.198 kJ K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ = **−198 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹**. Bracket the negative ΔG before subtracting it; that step is where sign slips creep in.

## Reactivity 1.4.3 Spontaneity

| ΔH | ΔS | Spontaneous |
|---|---|---|
| − | + | at all temperatures |
| + | − | never |
| − | − | below T = ΔH/ΔS |
| + | + | above T = ΔH/ΔS |

**Worked reminder.** ΔH = +65.0 kJ mol⁻¹, ΔS = +160 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. T = 65.0 ÷ 0.160 = 406 K. With both signs positive, the change is spontaneous **above 406 K**.

**Direct and indirect entropy.** ΔG accounts for the system's own entropy change (direct) and the surroundings' change caused by heat transfer (indirect, −ΔH/T). Since ΔG = −TΔS(total), a negative ΔG means total entropy rises.

## Reactivity 1.4.4 Gibbs energy and equilibrium

- As a reaction moves towards equilibrium, ΔG becomes less negative and reaches **zero** at equilibrium.
- If Q < K, ΔG is negative and the forward reaction proceeds. If Q > K, ΔG is positive and the reverse reaction proceeds.
- ΔG⦵ negative gives K > 1 (products favoured); ΔG⦵ positive gives K < 1 (reactants favoured).

**Method in steps: ΔG for a given mixture**

1. Write the expression for Q exactly as you would for K, using the mixture's concentrations.
2. Convert ΔG⦵ to J mol⁻¹ and T to kelvin.
3. Calculate RT lnQ; it is negative when Q < 1 and positive when Q > 1.
4. Add it to ΔG⦵. The sign of ΔG gives the spontaneous direction for that mixture.

**Worked reminder.** At 310 K, ΔG⦵ = +3.00 kJ mol⁻¹ and Q = 0.0500.
RT lnQ = 8.31 × 310 × ln 0.0500 = −7717 J mol⁻¹. ΔG = 3000 − 7717 = **−4.72 kJ mol⁻¹**. The forward reaction is spontaneous for this mixture, even though ΔG⦵ is positive.

**Worked reminder.** ΔG⦵ = +12.0 kJ mol⁻¹ at 298 K.
lnK = −12 000 ÷ (8.31 × 298) = −4.85, so K = **7.86 × 10⁻³**. At equilibrium, reactants make up most of the mixture.

## Must-know distinctions

- **ΔG vs ΔG⦵:** ΔG⦵ refers to standard conditions and is fixed at a given temperature; ΔG depends on the actual mixture through Q.
- **Spontaneous vs fast:** ΔG tells you whether a change is thermodynamically favoured; the activation energy controls its rate.
- **System vs surroundings:** ΔS⦵ from data is the system only. The surroundings' change comes from −ΔH/T.
- **Entropy vs enthalpy of formation for elements:** an element in its standard state is given ΔfH⦵ = 0, but its S⦵ is always above zero.
- **Threshold temperature:** T = ΔH/ΔS is only meaningful when ΔH and ΔS have the same sign.

## Quick self-test

1. State the sign of ΔS for CH₄(g) + 2O₂(g) → CO₂(g) + 2H₂O(l), with a reason.
2. Explain why a perfect crystal is expected to have zero entropy at absolute zero.
3. A change has ΔH = +25.0 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔS = +95.0 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. Calculate ΔG at 298 K and say whether it is spontaneous.
4. ΔH = −120 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔS = −150 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. Find the temperature range in which the reaction is spontaneous.
5. Which combination of signs of ΔH and ΔS gives a reaction that is never spontaneous?
6. ΔG⦵ = −10.0 kJ mol⁻¹ at 298 K. Calculate K.
7. What is ΔG⦵ for a reaction with K = 1?
8. ΔG⦵ = +15.0 kJ mol⁻¹ at 298 K, and a mixture has Q = 1.0 × 10⁻⁴. Calculate ΔG and state which direction is spontaneous.
9. An exothermic reaction releases 50.0 kJ mol⁻¹ at 250 K. Calculate ΔS(surroundings).
10. At 500 K, K = 2.0 × 10⁻³ for a reaction. Calculate ΔG⦵.
11. State what happens to the value of ΔG as a reaction mixture approaches equilibrium.

### Answers

1. Negative: 3 mol of gas become 1 mol of gas (water is a liquid).
2. There is only one possible arrangement of the particles and their energy.
3. ΔG = 25.0 − (298 × 0.0950) = **−3.31 kJ mol⁻¹**; negative, so spontaneous.
4. T = −120 ÷ −0.150 = 800 K; spontaneous **below 800 K**.
5. ΔH positive and ΔS negative.
6. lnK = 10 000 ÷ (8.31 × 298) = 4.04; K = **56.7**.
7. **Zero**, because ln 1 = 0.
8. ΔG = 15 000 + (8.31 × 298 × ln 1.0 × 10⁻⁴) = 15 000 − 22 808 = **−7.81 kJ mol⁻¹**; the forward reaction is spontaneous.
9. ΔS(surroundings) = +50 000 ÷ 250 = **+200 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹**.
10. ΔG⦵ = −8.31 × 500 × ln(2.0 × 10⁻³) = **+25.8 kJ mol⁻¹**.
11. It becomes less negative and reaches zero at equilibrium.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Mixing J and kJ in ΔH − TΔS: ΔS must be divided by 1000 first.
- Leaving T in °C.
- Dropping coefficients when summing S⦵, especially for O₂ and H₂.
- Missing state symbols, so liquid water is counted as a gas in a ΔS prediction.
- Giving T = ΔH/ΔS without "above" or "below".
- Using kJ inside −RT lnK, so K comes out close to 1.
- Writing a sign-only answer for ΔS when the question asks for a reason; name the change in moles of gas.
- Treating ΔG = 0 as "no reaction" rather than as equilibrium.
- Stating that a spontaneous reaction must be fast.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is entropy in IB DP Chemistry?**
Entropy, S, describes how dispersed the matter and energy of a system are; the more ways the energy can be shared out, the higher the entropy. Under the same conditions, gases have higher entropy than liquids, which have higher entropy than solids.

**How do you predict the sign of ΔS for a reaction?**
ΔS is positive when the number of moles of gas increases, which is usually the strongest effect, or when a solid melts, a liquid boils, a solid dissolves or a substance is heated. ΔS is negative for the reverse of each.

**When is a reaction spontaneous at all temperatures?**
A reaction with a negative ΔH and a positive ΔS is spontaneous at all temperatures, and one with a positive ΔH and a negative ΔS is never spontaneous. When both have the same sign, spontaneity depends on temperature.

**What is the value of ΔG at equilibrium?**
At equilibrium ΔG is zero. As a reaction approaches equilibrium, ΔG becomes less negative; a negative ΔG⦵ gives K greater than 1 and a positive ΔG⦵ gives K less than 1.
