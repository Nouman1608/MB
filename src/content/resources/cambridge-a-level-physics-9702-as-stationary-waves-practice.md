Take the speed of sound in air as 340 m s⁻¹ and c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹. End corrections are negligible.

---

## Section A

**1.** Two waves meet at a point. At one instant, their displacements at that point are +2.5 mm and −4.0 mm. Name the principle you use to find the resultant displacement, and state the resultant. **[2]**

**2.** Define a node and an antinode in a stationary wave. **[2]**

**3.** A microwave transmitter is directed at a flat metal plate. Explain how a stationary wave is formed between the transmitter and the plate. **[2]**

**4.** Points P and Q lie between the same pair of adjacent nodes in a stationary wave. Point R lies in the next segment. State the phase difference between **(a)** P and Q and **(b)** P and R. **[2]**

**5.** Two progressive waves of equal amplitude and frequency travel in opposite directions and are exactly in step at time t = 0. Using the graphical method, explain why the resultant displacement is zero at every point at t = T/4, where T is the period. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A probe is moved along the line between a microwave transmitter and a metal reflector. Successive minima of the probe reading are found at 12.0 cm, 13.6 cm, 15.2 cm, 16.8 cm and 18.4 cm from a reference mark.

**(a)** Determine the wavelength of the microwaves. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate their frequency. **[1]**

**(c)** The readings at the minima are small but not zero. Suggest why. **[1]**

**7.** A string is stretched between a vibration generator and a pulley 1.50 m away. At a frequency of 40 Hz the string vibrates in four segments.

**(a)** Calculate the wavelength. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the speed of the waves on the string. **[1]**

**(c)** The tension is unchanged. Calculate the frequency at which the string vibrates in five segments. **[2]**

**(d)** Calculate the lowest frequency at which a stationary wave forms on the string. **[1]**

**8.** A tube of length 0.60 m is closed at one end and open at the other.

**(a)** Calculate the lowest frequency at which the air column resonates. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the next two resonant frequencies. **[2]**

**(c)** For the lower of the two resonances in **(b)**, state the distances from the closed end of the nodes and the antinodes in the tube. **[2]**

**9.** A tube closed at the bottom by water is held below a tuning fork of frequency 384 Hz. The water level is lowered, and loud resonances are heard when the air column is 0.220 m long and again when it is 0.665 m long.

**(a)** Determine the wavelength of the sound. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the speed of sound. **[1]**

**(c)** Predict the length of the air column at the next resonance. **[1]**

**10.** Two progressive waves on a string, each of amplitude 3.0 mm and wavelength 0.40 m, travel in opposite directions and form a stationary wave.

**(a)** State the amplitude at an antinode. **[1]**

**(b)** State the distance between adjacent antinodes. **[1]**

**(c)** State the amplitude at a node. **[1]**

**(d)** Describe the amplitude of a point 0.05 m from a node. **[1]**

**11.** An organ pipe 0.68 m long is open at both ends.

**(a)** Calculate the frequency of its lowest resonance. **[2]**

**(b)** For this resonance, state the distance from one end of the pipe to the nearest node. **[1]**

**12.** A loudspeaker faces a flat wall. A microphone moved along the line between them detects a maximum, and then an adjacent minimum 0.25 m further along.

**(a)** Determine the wavelength of the sound. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the frequency of the sound. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** The **principle of superposition**: the resultant displacement is the sum of the individual displacements [1]. Resultant = 2.5 + (−4.0) = **−1.5 mm** [1].

**2.** Node: a point where the displacement is **always zero** [1]. Antinode: a point where the amplitude is **maximum** [1].

**3.** The microwaves **reflect** from the plate and travel back towards the transmitter [1]. The incident and reflected waves have the **same frequency** and travel in **opposite directions**, so they **superpose** to form a stationary wave [1].

**4. (a)** **0** (in phase) [1]. **(b)** **180°** (π rad, antiphase) [1].

**5.** In a quarter of a period each wave moves a **quarter-wavelength**, in opposite directions, so their relative shift is λ/2 [1]. The waves are then in **antiphase** at every point, and with equal amplitudes their displacements cancel everywhere [1].

**6. (a)** Adjacent minima are 1.6 cm apart = λ/2 [1] (or 6.4 cm across four gaps), so λ = **3.2 cm** [1].

**(b)** f = c/λ = 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 0.032 = **9.4 × 10⁹ Hz** [1].

**(c)** The reflected wave has a **smaller amplitude** than the incident wave (some energy is not reflected back along the line, or spreads out), so the cancellation at nodes is incomplete [1].

**7. (a)** Four segments = 4 × λ/2 = 2λ = 1.50 m, so λ = **0.75 m** [1].

**(b)** v = fλ = 40 × 0.75 = **30 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(c)** Five segments: λ = 2 × 1.50 ÷ 5 = 0.60 m [1]; f = 30 ÷ 0.60 = **50 Hz** [1].

**(d)** One segment: λ = 3.0 m, f = 30 ÷ 3.0 = **10 Hz** [1].

**8. (a)** L = λ/4, so λ = 2.40 m [1]; f = 340 ÷ 2.40 = **140 Hz** (142 Hz) [1].

**(b)** Closed tube resonances are odd multiples of the lowest: 3 × 141.7 = **425 Hz** [1] and 5 × 141.7 = **708 Hz** [1].

**(c)** At 425 Hz, L = 3λ/4, so λ = 0.80 m. Nodes at **0 and 0.40 m** from the closed end [1]; antinodes at **0.20 m and 0.60 m** (the open end) [1].

**9. (a)** Successive resonances are λ/2 apart: λ/2 = 0.665 − 0.220 = 0.445 m [1]; λ = **0.89 m** [1].

**(b)** v = fλ = 384 × 0.89 = **342 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(c)** 0.665 + 0.445 = **1.11 m** [1].

**10. (a)** **6.0 mm** [1].

**(b)** λ/2 = **0.20 m** [1].

**(c)** **Zero** [1].

**(d)** 0.05 m is λ/8, midway between a node and an antinode, so the amplitude is **between 0 and 6.0 mm** (greater than zero but less than the antinode amplitude) [1].

**11. (a)** Open at both ends: L = λ/2, so λ = 1.36 m [1]; f = 340 ÷ 1.36 = **250 Hz** [1].

**(b)** Each open end is an antinode and the node is at the centre, so the distance is λ/4 = **0.34 m** [1].

**12. (a)** Maximum to adjacent minimum is antinode to node = λ/4 [1], so λ = 4 × 0.25 = **1.0 m** [1].

**(b)** f = 340 ÷ 1.0 = **340 Hz** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Taking the spacing of minima as λ instead of λ/2 (Questions 6 and 9).
- Forgetting that a closed tube has only odd multiples of the lowest frequency (Question 8).
- Placing a node at the open end of a pipe (Questions 8 and 11).
- Taking the distance from a maximum to a minimum as λ/2 instead of λ/4 (Question 12).
- Not stating that the two waves travel in opposite directions with the same frequency (Question 3).
- Writing that the amplitude at an antinode equals the amplitude of each component wave rather than twice it (Question 10).
