## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** Explain the meaning of depreciation. **[2]**

**2.** A computer costs $4,800. It is expected to last 3 years and then be sold for $600. Calculate the annual depreciation using the straight-line method. **[2]**

**3.** A lorry costs $40,000 and is depreciated at 25% per year using the reducing balance method. Calculate its net book value at the end of the third year. **[3]**

**4.** A garage's loose tools were valued at $3,200 at the start of the year. Tools costing $1,150 were bought during the year. At the end of the year the tools were valued at $2,980. Calculate the depreciation for the year. **[2]**

**5.** State the most suitable depreciation method for each asset.

**(a)** A fleet of delivery lorries **[1]**

**(b)** Shop fittings that give the same benefit each year **[1]**

**6.** Equipment that cost $12,500 and had accumulated depreciation of $7,800 was sold for $5,100. Calculate the profit or loss on disposal. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** Ellie depreciates office equipment at 10% per year on cost, using the straight-line method, on a monthly basis. Her year ends on 31 December. On 1 January 2027 the office equipment account had a balance of $46,000 and the provision for depreciation of office equipment account a balance of $18,400.

- On 31 March 2027 equipment bought on 1 January 2023 for $6,000 was sold for $1,800, received by bank transfer.
- On 1 September 2027 new equipment was bought for $9,000 by bank transfer.

**(a)** Calculate the total depreciation charge for the year ended 31 December 2027. **[3]**

**(b)** Prepare for 2027:

**(i)** the office equipment account **[3]**

**(ii)** the provision for depreciation of office equipment account **[4]**

**(iii)** the disposal of office equipment account. **[4]**

**(c)** State the net book value of office equipment in the statement of financial position at 31 December 2027. **[1]**

**8.** On 30 June 2028 Rafael sold a motor vehicle that had cost $22,000 and had accumulated depreciation of $13,700. The buyer, Lane Motors, agreed to pay $9,500 in July 2028.

Prepare the journal entries to record the disposal, including the transfer of any profit or loss on disposal. Narratives are not required. **[8]**

**9.** Kamala's delivery business buys a van for $32,000. She is choosing between:

- straight-line over 4 years with a residual value of $4,000
- reducing balance at 30% per year.

**(a)** Calculate the depreciation for each of the first two years under each method. **[4]**

**(b)** Advise Kamala which method to use. Justify your answer. **[3]**

**10.** Tom forgot to charge depreciation of $3,600 on his machinery for the year.

**(a)** State the effect of this omission on his profit for the year, his non-current assets and his capital. **[3]**

**(b)** Tom says: "Charging depreciation would put $3,600 aside to replace the machine." Explain why he is wrong. **[2]**

**11.** A trainee recorded the sale of a machine that cost $10,000 and had accumulated depreciation of $7,000. The proceeds were $4,000. The trainee recorded a loss on disposal of $6,000.

**(a)** Calculate the correct profit or loss on disposal. **[2]**

**(b)** State the effect of the trainee's error on the profit for the year. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Depreciation is the estimated part of the cost of a non-current asset used up in an accounting period [1], through causes such as wear and tear, the passage of time or obsolescence [1]. **[2]**

**2.** ($4,800 − $600) ÷ 3 [1] = **$1,400** per year [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Year 1: $10,000, net book value $30,000 [1]. Year 2: $7,500, net book value $22,500 [1]. Year 3: $5,625, net book value **$16,875** [1]. **[3]**

**4.** $3,200 + $1,150 − $2,980 [1] = **$1,370** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** **(a)** Reducing balance [1]

**(b)** Straight-line [1] **[2]**

**6.** Net book value $12,500 − $7,800 = $4,700 [1]. Proceeds $5,100, so a **profit of $400** [1]. **[2]**

**7.** **(a)** Equipment held all year: ($46,000 − $6,000) × 10% = $4,000 [1]. New equipment: $9,000 × 10% × 4/12 = $300 [1]. Equipment sold: $6,000 × 10% × 3/12 = $150. Total **$4,450** [1].

**(b)** **(i)**

                                   ======                                     ======

```

Bank $9,000 [1]; disposal $6,000 [1]; balance $49,000 carried and brought down [1].

**(ii)** Depreciation on the item sold: 4 full years (2023 to 2026) × $600 = $2,400, plus $150 for 2027, a total of $2,550.

```

Disposal $2,550 [1]; profit or loss $4,450 [1]; balance c/d $20,300 [1]; balance b/d [1].

**(iii)**

```

Equipment $6,000 [1]; provision $2,550 [1]; bank $1,800 [1]; loss $1,650 [1].

**(c)** $49,000 − $20,300 = **$28,700** [1]. **[15]**

**8.**

```
                                                       Debit $   Credit $
30 Jun  Disposal of motor vehicle                      22,000
          Motor vehicles                                           22,000
30 Jun  Provision for depreciation of motor vehicles   13,700
          Disposal of motor vehicle                                13,700
30 Jun  Lane Motors                                     9,500

Cost transferred to disposal [2]; accumulated depreciation transferred [2]; Lane Motors debited (an other receivable) with $9,500 [2]; profit on disposal $9,500 − ($22,000 − $13,700) = $1,200, debited to disposal and credited to the statement of profit or loss [2]. **[8]**

**9.** **(a)** Straight-line: ($32,000 − $4,000) ÷ 4 = $7,000 in year 1 [1] and $7,000 in year 2 [1]. Reducing balance: 30% × $32,000 = $9,600 in year 1 [1]; 30% × $22,400 = $6,720 in year 2 [1].

**(b)** Reducing balance [1]. A van loses most of its value in its early years, so a higher charge then gives a more realistic net book value [1]; repair costs rise as the van ages, so the falling depreciation charge helps keep the total yearly cost of using the van more even [1]. Also accept straight-line with a justified reason, such as even use over a known four-year life. **[7]**

**10.** **(a)** Profit for the year is overstated by $3,600 [1]. Non-current assets are overstated by $3,600 [1]. Capital is overstated by $3,600 [1].

**(b)** Depreciation is a book entry, debit statement of profit or loss and credit provision for depreciation, with no entry in the bank or cash account [1]. It allocates the cost already paid; no money is set aside unless the owner decides to do so separately [1]. **[5]**

**11.** **(a)** Net book value $10,000 − $7,000 = $3,000 [1]. Proceeds $4,000, so a **profit of $1,000** [1].

**(b)** The trainee recorded a loss of $6,000 instead of a profit of $1,000 [1], so profit for the year is understated by **$7,000** [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculating reducing balance on cost every year.
- Charging depreciation for the wrong number of months.
- Forgetting the part-year depreciation on an asset sold when the policy is monthly.
- Leaving the cost or accumulated depreciation of an asset sold in the ledger.
- Comparing sale proceeds with cost instead of net book value.
