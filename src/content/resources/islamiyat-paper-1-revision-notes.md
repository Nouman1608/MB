## The Qur'an — key facts

- Revealed over **23 years**, beginning in the Cave of Hira in 610 CE, through the angel **Jibril**.
- **114 surahs**, classified as **Makkan** (shorter, on tawhid, the afterlife, moral conduct) or **Madinan** (longer, on law, community and social order).
- Compiled under **Abu Bakr (RA)** after the Battle of Yamama, when many *huffaz* were killed; standardised under **Uthman (RA)**, who sent official copies to the main centres and had variants destroyed.

## Themes of the Qur'an

**Tawhid** (oneness of God) · prophethood · the afterlife and judgement · guidance for personal conduct · law and social justice · relations with earlier scriptures.

## The Prophet's (pbuh) life — sequence

```
570  Birth in Makkah
610  First revelation, Cave of Hira
613  Public preaching begins; persecution follows
615  First migration to Abyssinia
619  Year of Sorrow -- deaths of Khadijah and Abu Talib
622  HIJRA to Madinah -- start of the Islamic calendar
622  Constitution of Madinah
624  Battle of Badr      -- victory
625  Battle of Uhud      -- setback; archers left their post
627  Battle of the Trench -- siege withstood
628  Treaty of Hudaybiyyah
630  Conquest of Makkah  -- general amnesty granted
632  Farewell Sermon; death of the Prophet
```

## Key events in Madinah (part of the Prophet's (pbuh) life, 1.3)

- **Constitution of Madinah** — defined rights and duties of Muslims, Jews and other groups; established Madinah as a single community with the Prophet (pbuh) as arbiter.
- The **Farewell Sermon** set out equality regardless of race, the sanctity of life and property, rights of women, and the abolition of usury and blood feuds.

## The first Islamic community (1.4) — organised by people

The syllabus builds this sub-topic around **named groups of people**. Know the significant facts of
their lives and be ready to explain the importance of their actions and experiences.

- **The Prophet's (pbuh) wives** — for example **Khadijah**, his first wife and the first person to accept
  his message, whose support sustained him in Makkah, and **Aisha (RA)**, later an important narrator of
  Hadith.
- **His descendants** — his children (including **Fatimah**), his grandchildren (including **Hasan (RA)**
  and **Husayn (RA)**), and the line recognised among Shi'a Muslims as the **Imams**.
- **His leading Companions** — the **Ten Blessed Companions**, his **Scribes** (such as **Zayd ibn
  Thabit**, who wrote down revelation), and the other major figures of his ministry; for converts,
  know how and why they accepted Islam and what it cost them.
- **The Emigrants (Muhajirun) and Helpers (Ansar)** — the Muslims who left Makkah for Madinah and the
  Muslims of Madinah who received them; the Prophet (pbuh) paired them in brotherhood on arrival.
- **The four Rightly Guided Caliphs during his lifetime** — Abu Bakr (RA), Umar (RA), Uthman (RA) and Ali (RA), studied
  here for their lives and roles while the Prophet (pbuh) lived; their rule after his death is Paper 2.

## Battle of Badr — the detail examiners reward

A high-level answer names the specific facts, not just "the Muslims won":

```
CONTEXT   Muslims set out to intercept a Quraysh caravan led by Abu
          Sufyan; he took a different route and sent for help instead
NUMBERS   Roughly 1000 Quraysh met about 313 Muslims, 2 AH
TACTICS   The Prophet secured the wells, denying the enemy water
OPENING   Single combat, won by the Muslims, before the general battle
OUTCOME   Quraysh defeated -- around 70 killed (incl. Abu Jahl), a
          similar number taken prisoner; prisoners treated humanely
```

Lessons drawn: faith and trust in God outweigh numbers (a much smaller
force prevailed), but preparation still matters (securing the wells,
taking advice on position), and the humane treatment of prisoners set
a standard for the conduct of war.

## Yamama and the compilation of the Qur'an

Abu Bakr (RA), chosen as the first Caliph at **Saqifah** immediately after
the Prophet's (pbuh) death, faced revolts across Arabia known as the **Ridda
(apostasy) wars** — but his rule, his policies and the wars themselves
are Paper 2 content (2.3), not this paper. The one part of that story
that belongs here: heavy losses among Qur'an memorisers (*huffaz*) at
the **Battle of Yamama**, fought during those wars, directly prompted
Abu Bakr (RA) to order the Qur'an's collection into a single written text —
the event covered under "The Qur'an — key facts" above.

## Exam traps

- Don't retell a narrative when the question asks for **significance** — the second half carries the marks.
- Abu Bakr (RA) **collected**; Uthman (RA) **standardised**. Distinguish the two stages.
- Uhud was a **setback**, not a total defeat.
- The Hijra marks the start of the Islamic calendar, not the Prophet's (pbuh) birth.
- Always support a point with a specific event, date or Qur'anic reference.
- Answering a first-Islamic-community question (1.4) with events alone — the topic is built around named people, so name them and explain the importance of what they did.
- Writing about a Caliph's rule after the Prophet's (pbuh) death (for example the Ridda wars) in a Paper 1 answer — that is Paper 2 content; on Paper 1 the Caliphs are studied for their lives during the Prophet's (pbuh) lifetime.
- Giving only "the Muslims won at Badr" without the numbers, the securing of the wells, or the treatment of prisoners that examiners look for.

## Self-test

1. Who compiled the Qur'an, and who standardised it?
2. Distinguish Makkan from Madinan surahs.
3. Why was the Constitution of Madinah significant?
4. What went wrong at Uhud?
5. Name three principles from the Farewell Sermon.
6. Why did Hudhayfah warn Uthman (RA) about how the Qur'an was being recited?
7. Which groups of people does the syllabus list under the first Islamic community?

**Answers:** 1. Abu Bakr (RA) compiled it after Yamama; Uthman (RA) standardised it and distributed official copies. 2. Makkan are generally shorter and focus on tawhid, the afterlife and moral conduct; Madinan are longer and address law, community organisation and social order. 3. It bound Muslims, Jews and other groups into one community with defined rights and duties, and established the Prophet (pbuh) as arbiter — the first written constitution of the Islamic state. 4. Archers abandoned their position to collect spoils, allowing a counter-attack from the rear. 5. Any three: equality regardless of race, sanctity of life and property, rights of women, abolition of usury, ending of blood feuds. 6. Because Muslims in different regions had begun reciting the Qur'an in different dialects during the campaigns in Armenia and Azerbaijan, risking division over the text itself. 7. The Prophet's (pbuh) wives; his descendants (his children, grandchildren and the line recognised among Shi'a Muslims as Imams); and his leading Companions, including the Ten Blessed Companions, his Scribes, the major figures of his ministry, the Emigrants and Helpers, and the four Rightly Guided Caliphs during his lifetime.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is 0493 Paper 1 structured?**
Paper 1 lasts 1 hour 30 minutes and is worth 50 marks (50%). It has five questions: Questions 1 and 2 are compulsory and you choose two of Questions 3 to 5. Question 1 is worth 8 marks (4 + 4) and each other answered question 14 marks (10 + 4).

**When was the Qur'an revealed?**
Over about 23 years, from 610 CE in the Cave of Hira to 632 CE, through the angel Jibril.

**How many surahs are there?**
114, classified as Makkan or Madinan according to when they were revealed.
