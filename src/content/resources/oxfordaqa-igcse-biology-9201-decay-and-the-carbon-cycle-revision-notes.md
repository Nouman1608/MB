## The essentials

- Living organisms **remove materials** from the environment for growth and other processes.
- Materials are **returned** in waste materials or when organisms **die and decay**.
- Materials decay because **microorganisms break them down (digest them)**.
- Microorganisms are more active, and digest faster, in **warm, moist, aerobic** conditions.
- Decay **releases substances that plants need to grow**.
- In a stable community, processes that **remove** materials are **balanced** by those that **return** them: a
  constant cycle.

## Conditions for decay

| Condition | Effect on microorganisms |
|---|---|
| Warm | Faster enzyme action, faster growth and reproduction |
| Moist | Water needed for activity and for absorbing the soluble products |
| Aerobic | Oxygen for aerobic respiration, which supplies energy |

Remove one of these (cold, dry or waterlogged with little oxygen) and decay slows.

## The carbon cycle

| Step | Carbon moves from | to |
|---|---|---|
| Photosynthesis | Carbon dioxide in the air | Carbohydrates, fats and proteins in plants and algae |
| Respiration (plants and algae) | Plant and algal compounds | Carbon dioxide in the air |
| Feeding | Plants and algae | Fats and proteins in animals, then in the animals that eat them |
| Respiration (animals) | Animal compounds | Carbon dioxide in the air |
| Death and waste | Plants, algae and animals | Dead bodies and waste, fed on by animals and microorganisms |
| Respiration (microorganisms) | Dead bodies and waste | Carbon dioxide in the air |
| Combustion | Wood and fossil fuels | Carbon dioxide in the air |

Remember the **two forms**: carbon dioxide in the air; carbohydrates, fats and proteins in organisms.

By the time microorganisms and detritus feeders have broken down the waste and dead bodies, **all the energy
originally absorbed by green plants and algae has been transferred**.

## Calculations

```
mass lost = starting mass − final mass
percentage lost = mass lost ÷ starting mass × 100
rate = mass lost ÷ time
```

Worked reminder: leaf litter falls from 25.0 g to 10.0 g in 20 days.

```
mass lost = 25.0 − 10.0 = 15.0 g
percentage lost = 15.0 ÷ 25.0 × 100 = 60.0 %
rate = 15.0 ÷ 20 = 0.75 g per day
```

Carbon budget reminder: if 500 units of carbon are removed in a year and 480 units are returned, the net change is
480 − 500 = −20 units. More carbon is removed from the air than returned, so atmospheric carbon dioxide falls.

## Applying the principles

| Situation | Effect on carbon dioxide in the air |
|---|---|
| More photosynthesis (more plant growth) | Falls |
| More combustion of fossil fuels | Rises |
| More decay in warm, moist, aerobic soil | More respiration by microorganisms, so more carbon dioxide returned |
| A waterlogged bog with little oxygen | Decay is slow, so carbon stays in dead plant material |
| A sealed jar with a plant in the light | Photosynthesis and respiration may balance |

## Model answers to learn

- **Why do dead leaves decay faster in a warm, moist compost heap than in a frozen one? (2 marks):** "Microorganisms are more active in warm, moist conditions. They digest the leaves faster."
- **How does decay help plants grow? (1 mark):** "It releases substances that plants need to grow."
- **Describe how carbon in a dead rabbit returns to the air. (3 marks):** "Microorganisms and detritus feeders feed on the body. The microorganisms respire. This releases carbon dioxide into the atmosphere."

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Decay | Breakdown (digestion) of dead material and waste by microorganisms |
| Microorganism | Organism too small to see without a microscope, such as bacteria and fungi |
| Aerobic | With oxygen |
| Detritus feeder | Animal that feeds on dead material and waste |
| Stable community | One in which removal of materials is balanced by return |
| Carbon cycle | The constant cycling of carbon |
| Combustion | Burning, which releases carbon dioxide from wood and fossil fuels |
| Photosynthesis | Process in green plants and algae that removes carbon dioxide |

## Exam traps

- Microorganisms **digest** dead material; they do not simply "eat" it.
- Plants **respire** as well as photosynthesise, so they also release carbon dioxide.
- The energy is **transferred**, not destroyed.
- Name the **form of carbon** at each stage.
- Decay is slowed by cold, dryness or lack of oxygen, not only by cold.
- Combustion is a separate route from respiration. Name both.
- The cycle is balanced in a **stable** community only.

## Self-test

1. How are materials returned to the environment after organisms take them in?
2. Why do materials decay?
3. Give the three conditions in which microorganisms digest most quickly.
4. What does decay release that plants need?
5. Name the process that removes carbon dioxide from the air.
6. In what forms is carbon found in plants and animals?
7. Name three groups of organisms whose respiration releases carbon dioxide.
8. What is released by the burning of wood and fossil fuels?
9. Compost falls from 60.0 g to 45.0 g in 30 days. Calculate the percentage lost.
10. In a year 500 units of carbon are removed from the air and 480 units are returned. State what happens to the carbon dioxide in the air.

### Answers

1. In waste materials, or when living things die and decay.
2. Microorganisms break them down (digest them).
3. Warm, moist and aerobic.
4. Substances that plants need to grow.
5. Photosynthesis.
6. Carbohydrates, fats and proteins.
7. Any three of: plants, algae, animals, microorganisms.
8. Carbon dioxide.
9. (60.0 − 45.0) ÷ 60.0 × 100 = **25 %**.
10. 480 − 500 = −20, so more is removed than returned and the amount in the air **falls**.
