## Shells, sub-shells and orbitals

Each **shell** (principal energy level, n = 1, 2, 3, 4) is divided into **sub-shells** labelled s, p and d. Each sub-shell is made of **orbitals**, regions where an electron is likely to be found. An orbital holds **at most two electrons, with opposite spins**.

| Sub-shell | Orbitals | Maximum electrons | First appears in shell |
|---|---|---|---|
| s | 1 | 2 | n = 1 |
| p | 3 | 6 | n = 2 |
| d | 5 | 10 | n = 3 |

So shell 1 holds 2 electrons, shell 2 holds 8, shell 3 holds 18.

### Three rules for filling

1. Electrons enter the lowest-energy sub-shell available: 1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d 4p.
2. No orbital holds more than two electrons, and a pair has opposite spins.
3. Within one sub-shell, electrons occupy orbitals **singly before pairing**, because two electrons in the same orbital repel.

Rule 3 matters when you draw electrons-in-boxes. Nitrogen's 2p³ is three unpaired electrons in three boxes, not one pair and one single.

## Writing configurations of atoms

Count the electrons and fill in order. Textbooks write the 3d and 4s sub-shells either in shell order (3d then 4s, as in this guide) or in filling order (4s then 3d); choose one and use it consistently. The noble-gas shorthand replaces the inner electrons with the symbol in square brackets.

| Atom | Z | Configuration |
|---|---|---|
| P | 15 | 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p³ |
| Ca | 20 | [Ar] 4s² |
| V | 23 | [Ar] 3d³ 4s² |
| Ga | 31 | [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p¹ |
| Br | 35 | [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p⁵ |

Chromium ([Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹) and copper ([Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹) do not follow the simple pattern; learn them as the two exceptions in this range. The superscripts must add up to the number of electrons, which is a quick check worth doing every time.

**Blocks.** The sub-shell holding the highest-energy electron gives the block: s block (Groups 1 and 2), p block (Groups 3 to 0), d block (scandium to zinc in Period 4).

## Writing configurations of ions

**Main-group ions.** Add or remove electrons from the outer sub-shell. Many ions then have the configuration of a noble gas, and species with the same configuration are **isoelectronic**.

- Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺, F⁻, O²⁻ and N³⁻ are all 1s² 2s² 2p⁶.
- Se²⁻ (34 + 2 = 36 electrons) is [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p⁶, the same as krypton.

**Transition-metal ions.** The 4s electrons are removed before the 3d electrons. Remove 4s first, then 3d.

### Worked example 1: three d-block ions

```
Mn   [Ar] 3d5 4s2   →  Mn2+  [Ar] 3d5     (two 4s removed)
Fe   [Ar] 3d6 4s2   →  Fe3+  [Ar] 3d5     (two 4s, then one 3d)
Cu   [Ar] 3d10 4s1  →  Cu2+  [Ar] 3d9     (one 4s, then one 3d)
```

Mn²⁺ and Fe³⁺ are isoelectronic. A common wrong answer for Fe³⁺ is [Ar] 3d³ 4s², which removes 3d electrons while leaving 4s full.

## Ionisation energies

**First ionisation energy** is the energy needed to remove one mole of electrons from one mole of gaseous atoms to form one mole of gaseous 1+ ions. Each **successive** ionisation energy removes one more electron from the ion formed in the previous step.

### Writing the equations

Every species is gaseous, one electron is removed per step, and the charge goes up by one.

```
first:   Ca(g)   → Ca⁺(g)  + e⁻
second:  Ca⁺(g)  → Ca²⁺(g) + e⁻
third:   Ca²⁺(g) → Ca³⁺(g) + e⁻
```

The second ionisation equation does **not** start from the atom. Ca(g) → Ca²⁺(g) + 2e⁻ is the sum of two steps, not the second ionisation energy.

### What controls the size

- **Nuclear charge:** more protons attract the electron more strongly.
- **Distance from the nucleus:** an electron in a higher shell is further away and held less tightly.
- **Shielding:** inner electrons repel the outer electron, reducing the attraction it feels.

## Evidence from Group 2 (Be–Ba)

First ionisation energy decreases down Group 2 (kJ mol⁻¹): Be 900, Mg 738, Ca 590, Sr 550, Ba 503.

Each element down the group has its outer two electrons in a new, higher shell (2s, 3s, 4s, 5s, 6s). The outer electron is further from the nucleus and more shielded by extra inner shells. Although the nuclear charge increases, these effects outweigh it, so less energy is needed. This is evidence that electrons are arranged in **shells** at increasing distance from the nucleus.

## Evidence from Period 3 (Na–Ar)

First ionisation energies (kJ mol⁻¹):

| Na | Mg | Al | Si | P | S | Cl | Ar |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 496 | 738 | 578 | 787 | 1012 | 1000 | 1251 | 1521 |

- **General increase:** nuclear charge rises across the period while the electrons are added to the same shell, so shielding is similar and the outer electron is held more tightly.
- **Drop at Al:** the outer electron is in 3p, higher in energy than 3s (and partly shielded by the 3s electrons). This is evidence for **sub-shells**.
- **Drop at S:** S has 3p⁴, so two electrons share one 3p orbital; their repulsion makes one easier to remove. This is evidence for **orbitals holding pairs**.

## Evidence from successive ionisation energies

### Worked example 2: aluminium

The first four ionisation energies of aluminium (kJ mol⁻¹) are 578, 1817, 2745 and 11 577.

```
1 → 2   ×3.1   2nd electron from 3s, closer and lower in energy than 3p
2 → 3   ×1.5   same 3s sub-shell, ion more positive
3 → 4   ×4.2   huge jump: 4th electron comes from shell 2
```

The big jump after three electrons shows **three electrons in the outer shell** (Group 3). The larger rise from the first to the second value, compared with the second to the third, shows that the first electron came from a different, higher-energy sub-shell (3p) than the next two (3s). Successive ionisation energies therefore give evidence for both shells and sub-shells.

Why does every successive value rise, even within a shell? Each electron is removed from an increasingly positive ion, so the remaining electrons are attracted more strongly.

## Common mistakes

- Removing 3d electrons before 4s when forming transition-metal ions.
- Writing successive ionisation equations from the atom, or leaving out (g).
- Pairing electrons in a p sub-shell before every orbital has one.
- Explaining the Group 2 trend by nuclear charge alone; the increase in distance and shielding must be stated as outweighing it.
- Saying the S drop is due to "a full sub-shell". The cause is repulsion between paired 3p electrons.
- Superscripts that do not total the number of electrons.

## Exam technique for the command words

- **Write** a configuration: complete, in order, with superscripts; check the total.
- **Define**: include one mole, gaseous atoms and 1+ ions.
- **Explain** a trend: name the sub-shell or shell involved, the factor that changes and its effect on attraction.
- **Deduce** from data: point to the specific jump or drop, then state the conclusion (the group, or the sub-shell).

## Frequently asked questions

**Why are 4s electrons lost before 3d electrons?**
Once the 3d sub-shell is occupied, the 4s electrons are the outermost and are removed first. The configuration of a transition-metal ion therefore keeps its 3d electrons and loses 4s.

**Why does first ionisation energy decrease down Group 2 if the nuclear charge increases?**
The outer electron is in a higher shell, further from the nucleus and more shielded. These effects outweigh the increased nuclear charge.

**What is the difference between a shell and a sub-shell?**
A shell is a principal energy level, numbered 1, 2, 3 and so on. A sub-shell is a group of orbitals of the same type (s, p or d) within a shell.
