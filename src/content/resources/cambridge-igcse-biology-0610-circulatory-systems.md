This guide goes deeper into one sub-topic of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028: **9.1 Circulatory systems**, the first part of topic 9, Transport in animals. It has four learning outcomes. Outcome 1 is Core. Outcomes 2 to 4, on fish and mammal circulations, are Supplement content, so they are **Extended only** and appear on Papers 2 and 4.

The [transport in animals study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-transport-in-animals/) gives the whole topic in one place. ## What sub-topic 9.1 asks you to do

| Outcome | Syllabus wording (summarised) | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| 9.1.1 | Describe the circulatory system as a system of blood vessels with a pump and valves to ensure one-way flow of blood | Core |
| 9.1.2 | Describe the single circulation of a fish | Extended only |
| 9.1.3 | Describe the double circulation of a mammal | Extended only |
| 9.1.4 | Explain the advantages of a double circulation | Extended only |

Notice the command words. Outcomes 1 to 3 say **describe**: you state what happens, in order. Outcome 4 says **explain**: you must give a reason for each advantage.

## 9.1.1 The three parts of a circulatory system

The Core definition has three parts, and each one does a different job.

| Part | What it is in a mammal | Its job |
|---|---|---|
| Blood vessels | arteries, capillaries and veins | carry blood to and from every tissue |
| Pump | the heart | creates the pressure that moves blood through the vessels |
| Valves | in the heart and in veins | stop blood flowing backwards, so flow is one-way |

Why does one-way flow matter? Blood picks up oxygen in one place (the lungs or gills) and delivers it somewhere else (the respiring tissues). If blood could slosh backwards and forwards, it would not move steadily from the place where it collects substances to the place where it delivers them. Valves make each contraction of the heart push blood forwards only.

A good definition answer names all three parts and links the valves to direction: "a system of blood vessels, with a pump, and valves that ensure blood flows in one direction only". Writing "the heart pumps blood round the body" describes only one of the three parts.

## 9.1.2 Single circulation in a fish (Extended only)

In a fish, blood travels in **one loop**:

```
heart → gills → body organs → heart
```

The heart pumps blood to the gills, where it takes up oxygen and loses carbon dioxide. Blood then flows on from the gills **directly** to the rest of the body without returning to the heart first. After passing through the body organs it returns to the heart.

The key descriptive point is the count: **blood passes through the heart once for each complete circuit of the body**. That is what "single" means.

Two further points help you explain the comparison later:

- The fish heart only ever receives blood returning from the body, so the blood it pumps is **deoxygenated**.
- Blood passes through two capillary networks in one loop: first in the gills, then in the body organs. Pushing blood through the narrow gill capillaries makes it lose much of its pressure, so it reaches the body organs at **low pressure** and flows slowly.

## 9.1.3 Double circulation in a mammal (Extended only)

In a mammal, blood travels in **two loops**, and both start and finish at the heart.

```
Pulmonary circulation:  right side of heart → lungs → left side of heart
Systemic circulation:   left side of heart → body organs → right side of heart
```

Blood passes through the heart **twice for each complete circuit of the body**: once through the right side (on its way to the lungs) and once through the left side (on its way to the body).

### Worked example 1: tracing a red blood cell

A red blood cell is in a capillary in the big toe. Trace its route until it returns to the toe, and count the passes through the heart.

1. Toe capillary → veins of the leg → **vena cava**.
2. Vena cava → right atrium → right ventricle (**first pass through the heart**, right side).
3. Right ventricle → **pulmonary artery** → capillaries in the lungs. The cell picks up oxygen here.
4. Lungs → **pulmonary vein** → left atrium → left ventricle (**second pass**, left side).
5. Left ventricle → **aorta** → arteries of the leg → toe capillary. The cell gives up oxygen to respiring cells.

**Answer: two passes through the heart in one circuit.** In a fish, the same exercise gives one pass: body → heart → gills → body.

This kind of tracing appears often in structured questions. Keep the order exact and name the vessels where the question asks for them; the vessels themselves are learned in [blood vessels](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-blood-vessels/).

## 9.1.4 Advantages of a double circulation (Extended only)

Each advantage follows from the fact that blood **returns to the heart after the lungs**.

| Feature of the double circulation | Advantage | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Blood is pumped a second time after the lungs | blood reaches the body at **high pressure** | it flows faster, so oxygen and glucose reach respiring cells quickly and carbon dioxide is removed quickly |
| The two loops are pumped separately | the lungs can receive blood at **lower pressure** | delicate lung capillaries are not damaged, and blood moves slowly enough for gas exchange |
| The two sides of the heart are separate | oxygenated and deoxygenated blood **do not mix** | blood going to the body carries as much oxygen as possible |

Mammals are active and keep a constant body temperature, and both need a high rate of respiration. A fast supply of oxygen and glucose supports that rate. This is the "so what" that turns a statement into an explanation.

### Worked example 2: annotated model answer

**Question:** Explain the advantages of a double circulation in a mammal. [4]

**Answer:** After passing through the lungs, blood returns to the heart and is pumped again (1), so it reaches the body organs at high pressure and flows quickly (1). This means oxygen and glucose are delivered to respiring cells faster (1). Blood is sent to the lungs at a lower pressure, which avoids damaging the lung capillaries (1).

**Why it scores:** every advantage carries its consequence. A weaker answer, "it has higher pressure and keeps blood separate", names two features but explains neither and would score little.

### Worked example 3: interpreting a comparison

A question shows two circulation diagrams. Diagram X has one loop passing through gills; diagram Y has a loop to lungs and a loop to the body, both returning to the heart.

- X is a **single circulation** (fish): blood passes through the heart once per circuit.
- Y is a **double circulation** (mammal): blood passes through the heart twice per circuit.
- In X, blood reaches the body after two capillary networks, so at lower pressure than in Y, where the heart re-pressurises blood before it goes to the body.

## Fish and mammal compared

| | Fish (single) | Mammal (double) |
|---|---|---|
| Number of loops | one | two (pulmonary and systemic) |
| Passes through heart per circuit | one | two |
| Gas exchange organ | gills | lungs |
| Pressure of blood reaching body organs | low, after gill capillaries | high, re-pumped by the heart |
| Blood pumped by the heart | deoxygenated only | deoxygenated (right side) and oxygenated (left side), kept apart |

When a question says "compare", write matched pairs: "in a fish blood passes through the heart once per circuit, **whereas** in a mammal it passes through twice".

## Common mistakes

- Defining a circulatory system as "the heart and blood" and leaving out vessels or valves.
- Saying valves "pump blood". They only stop backflow.
- Describing double circulation as "two hearts" or "blood going round twice". It is one heart with two sides, and blood passes **through the heart** twice per circuit.
- Placing the lungs in the systemic circulation. The lungs are in the pulmonary circulation.
- Writing the fish route as heart → body → gills. The gills come straight after the heart.
- Stating "higher pressure" as an advantage with no consequence. Add "so blood flows faster and oxygen reaches cells more quickly".
- Saying lung capillaries need low pressure "because lungs are small". The reason is that the capillaries are delicate and could be damaged.

## Exam technique for 9.1 command words

- **Describe** (fish or mammal circulation): give the route in order and the number of passes through the heart. Two marks are usually route and count.
- **Explain** (advantages): use "so" or "which means" in every sentence. One linked idea per mark.
- **Compare**: use "whereas" or "but" and make every point about both animals.
- **State**: a short answer is enough, for example "pulmonary circulation".

## Frequently asked questions

**Is double circulation Core or Extended in Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610?**
Single and double circulation and the advantages of a double circulation are Supplement content, so they are Extended only. The Core outcome is the definition of a circulatory system.

**What does "blood passes through the heart twice" mean?**
In one complete circuit of the body, blood goes through the right side of the heart on its way to the lungs and through the left side on its way to the body.

**Why does blood flow slowly to the body in a fish?**
Blood loses much of its pressure passing through the narrow gill capillaries, and it is not pumped again before it reaches the body organs.
