## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pathogen | A microorganism that causes infectious disease: virus, bacterium, protist or fungus |
| Communicable disease | A disease that can be passed from one host to another |
| Toxin | A poison produced by bacteria that damages tissues |
| Vector | An organism that carries a pathogen between hosts, such as the mosquito for malaria |
| Phagocytosis | A white blood cell engulfing and digesting a pathogen |
| Antibody | A protein made by white blood cells that binds to a specific antigen on a pathogen |
| Antitoxin | A substance made by white blood cells that neutralises a toxin |
| Vaccine | Small quantities of dead or inactive pathogen that stimulate antibody production |
| Antibiotic | A medicine that kills infective bacteria inside the body |
| Placebo | A substance given in a trial that does not contain the drug |
| Double blind trial | Neither doctors nor patients know who has the drug or the placebo until the trial ends |
| Peer review | Checking of results by other scientists before publication |

## How pathogens cause illness

- Bacteria and viruses **reproduce rapidly** inside the body.
- **Bacteria** → produce **toxins** → damage tissues, make us feel ill.
- **Viruses** → live and reproduce **inside cells** → cell damage.
- Spread: **direct contact**, **water**, **air**.

## The seven named diseases in one line each

- **Measles** (virus): fever, red rash; droplets from sneezes and coughs; can be fatal; young children vaccinated.
- **HIV** (virus): flu-like illness first; attacks immune cells unless controlled by **antiretroviral drugs**;
  late stage HIV infection (AIDS) when the immune system cannot deal with other infections or cancers; sexual
  contact or body fluids such as blood from shared needles.
- **Tobacco mosaic virus** (virus, plants including tomatoes): mosaic discolouration of leaves → less
  photosynthesis → less growth.
- **Salmonella** (bacterium): fever, abdominal cramps, vomiting, diarrhoea from the bacteria and their toxins;
  in food or food prepared in unhygienic conditions; UK poultry vaccinated.
- **Gonorrhoea** (bacterium, STD): thick yellow or green discharge, pain on urinating; sexual contact; was treated
  with **penicillin** until resistant strains appeared; antibiotics and condoms control spread.
- **Rose black spot** (fungus): purple or black spots, leaves yellow and drop early → less photosynthesis;
  spread by water or wind; fungicides and/or remove and destroy affected leaves.
- **Malaria** (protist): life cycle includes the mosquito; recurrent episodes of fever; can be fatal; stop
  mosquitoes breeding and use mosquito nets.

## Defences in order

**Non-specific (keep pathogens out):**

| Defence | How it works |
|---|---|
| Skin | Barrier; antimicrobial secretions |
| Nose | Hairs and mucus trap particles |
| Trachea and bronchi | Mucus traps pathogens; cilia move mucus to the throat |
| Stomach | Hydrochloric acid kills pathogens |

**Immune system (destroy pathogens that get in):** white blood cells carry out **phagocytosis**, make
**antibodies** and make **antitoxins**.

## Vaccination chain

1. Dead or inactive pathogen injected.
2. White blood cells stimulated to make the correct antibodies.
3. Same pathogen enters later.
4. White blood cells respond **quickly** with the correct antibodies.
5. Pathogen destroyed before illness develops.

Population effect: a large proportion immune → pathogen spreads very little → unvaccinated people protected too.

## Medicines

| Medicine | Kills the pathogen? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Antibiotic (such as penicillin) | Yes, bacteria only | Specific antibiotics for specific bacteria; resistance a great concern |
| Painkiller | No | Treats symptoms only |
| Antiviral drugs | Hard to develop | Viruses are inside cells, so drugs may damage body tissue |

## Drug development

**Origins:** digitalis from foxgloves; aspirin from willow; penicillin discovered by Alexander Fleming from
Penicillium mould. Most new drugs are synthesised by chemists, sometimes starting from a plant chemical.

**Tested for:** toxicity, efficacy, dose.

| Stage | Who or what | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Preclinical | Cells, tissues, live animals in a laboratory | Check toxicity before human use |
| Clinical, first | Healthy volunteers, very low doses | Check safety in humans |
| Clinical, later | Patients | Find the optimum dose and check efficacy |
| Double blind with placebo | Patients randomly given drug or placebo | Remove bias from expectations |
| Peer review | Other scientists | Check results before publication |

## Exam traps

- "Antibiotics treat flu." No: flu is viral.
- "Mosquitoes cause malaria." The protist causes it; the mosquito is the vector.
- "Vaccines give you the disease." They contain dead or inactive pathogens and do not cause the illness.
- "Antitoxins kill bacteria." They neutralise toxins.
- Writing "germs" instead of naming the type of pathogen.
- Forgetting that plant diseases reduce growth **because photosynthesis is reduced**.
- Calculating a percentage change by dividing by the new value instead of the original.

## Self-test

1. Name the four types of pathogen.
2. How do bacteria and viruses each make us feel ill?
3. Give the cause and the method of spread of measles.
4. Why do people with late stage HIV infection die of infections a healthy person would survive?
5. Why does tobacco mosaic virus reduce plant growth?
6. Give two ways the spread of gonorrhoea can be controlled.
7. How are rose black spot spores spread?
8. Give two ways to control the spread of malaria.
9. Describe how the trachea and bronchi defend against pathogens.
10. Name three ways white blood cells defend the body.
11. Why are some people in a trial given a placebo?
12. Which plant does aspirin originate from?

### Answers

1. Viruses, bacteria, protists, fungi.
2. Bacteria produce toxins that damage tissues; viruses reproduce inside cells and damage them.
3. A virus; by inhalation of droplets from sneezes and coughs.
4. HIV has damaged the immune system so badly that it can no longer deal with other infections.
5. The leaves become discoloured, so less photosynthesis takes place and less glucose is made.
6. Treatment with antibiotics; using a barrier method of contraception such as a condom.
7. By water or wind.
8. Preventing mosquitoes from breeding; using mosquito nets to avoid being bitten.
9. Mucus traps pathogens and cilia move the mucus up to the throat.
10. Phagocytosis, antibody production, antitoxin production.
11. To compare with the drug group, so any improvement caused by the drug itself can be seen.
12. Willow.
