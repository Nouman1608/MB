## The seven books in the specification

Purchases day book, sales day book, purchases returns book, sales returns book, three-column cash book, petty cash book, the journal.

## Purpose in five points

1. First record of each transaction, in date order, from a document.
2. Groups similar items so totals are posted to the ledger.
3. Fewer ledger entries, so fewer errors.
4. Work can be divided among staff.
5. Gives a trail from ledger back to document; the journal adds a narrative.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Book of original entry | Where a transaction is first recorded before posting to the ledger |
| Day book | List of credit transactions of one type, at net amounts |
| Three-column cash book | Cash book with discount, cash and bank columns on each side |
| Discount allowed | Cash discount given to a customer; debit side of the cash book |
| Discount received | Cash discount from a supplier; credit side of the cash book |
| Contra entry | Movement between cash and bank, entered on both sides, marked C |
| Imprest system | Petty cash float restored to a fixed amount by reimbursing what was spent |
| Analysis columns | Columns in the petty cash book splitting payments by expense type |
| Journal | Book for transactions that fit no other book, with a narrative |
| Narrative | Short explanation under a journal entry |

## Which book? Decision table

| Transaction | Book |
|---|---|
| Credit sale of goods | Sales day book |
| Credit purchase of goods for resale | Purchases day book |
| Goods returned by a customer | Sales returns book |
| Goods returned to a supplier | Purchases returns book |
| Any cash or bank receipt or payment | Cash book |
| Small cash expense from the float | Petty cash book |
| Non-current asset on credit, opening entries, goods taken by owner, write-offs, corrections | Journal |

## Posting rules

| Book | Lines go to | Total goes to |
|---|---|---|
| Sales day book | Dr each customer | Cr sales |
| Sales returns book | Cr each customer | Dr sales returns |
| Purchases day book | Cr each supplier | Dr purchases |
| Purchases returns book | Dr each supplier | Cr purchases returns |
| Cash book discount allowed | Cr each customer | Dr discount allowed |
| Cash book discount received | Dr each supplier | Cr discount received |
| Petty cash analysis columns | | Dr each expense account |

## Cash book reminders

- Debit side: receipts. Credit side: payments.
- Balance cash and bank separately; never balance discount columns.
- A customer paying 588 in settlement of 600: bank 588, discount allowed 12, customer credited 600 in total.
- Cash banked: Dr bank, Cr cash. Cash withdrawn for office use: Dr cash, Cr bank.
- A credit balance in the bank column means an overdraft.

## Petty cash reminders

- Float in, vouchers out, totals cross-checked: analysis columns must add to the total column.
- Reimbursement equals amount spent.
- Balance carried down equals the float after reimbursement.

Mini-check: float 200.00, spent 163.45. Cash held before reimbursement 36.55; reimbursement 163.45.

## Journal layout

Date, account debited, account credited (indented), debit and credit amounts, narrative. Debits equal credits in every entry.

Typical entries:

- Opening: Dr each asset, Cr each liability, Cr capital (balancing figure).
- Non-current asset on credit: Dr asset, Cr supplier.
- Goods taken by owner: Dr drawings, Cr purchases.
- Debt written off: Dr irrecoverable debts, Cr customer.

## Exam traps

- A van, computer or shelving bought on credit goes in the journal, not the purchases day book.
- Day books record credit items only, net of trade discount.
- Discount allowed belongs on the debit side, beside the receipt it relates to.
- One-sided contra entries.
- In an opening entry, capital = assets − liabilities.
- Leaving out the journal narrative.
- Forgetting to bring balances down after balancing the cash book.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the seven books of original entry in 2.2 (b).
2. Which book records a credit note received from a supplier?
3. A computer for office use is bought on credit for 1,150. Which book?
4. A customer owing 450 pays 441 by cheque. Show the cash book entries.
5. Which side of the cash book records discount received?
6. Cash of 300 is paid into the bank. What is the entry?
7. Why are discount columns not balanced?
8. Float 120.00; vouchers total 97.65. What is the reimbursement and the cash held before it?
9. Give the journal entry for goods costing 95 taken by the owner.
10. Assets total 52,000 and a loan is 12,500. What is opening capital?
11. Which account is credited with the total of the purchases returns book?
12. Why is the cash book both a book of original entry and part of the ledger?

### Answers

1. Purchases day book, sales day book, purchases returns book, sales returns book, three-column cash book, petty cash book, the journal.
2. Purchases returns book.
3. The journal (debit office equipment, credit supplier).
4. Debit side: bank 441, discount 9, against the customer's name.
5. The credit side.
6. Debit bank column, credit cash column; both marked C.
7. They are memorandum columns; their totals are posted to the discount accounts.
8. Reimbursement 97.65; cash held 120.00 − 97.65 = 22.35.
9. Dr drawings 95, Cr purchases 95, narrative "goods taken for private use".
10. 52,000 − 12,500 = 39,500.
11. The purchases returns account.
12. Transactions are first entered in it, and its balances are the cash and bank accounts.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do day books record cash sales?**
No. Cash sales and purchases go straight into the cash book; day books hold credit transactions only.

**What makes the journal different from the other books?**
It handles any transaction with no other book, shows both the debit and credit account, and explains each entry with a narrative.
