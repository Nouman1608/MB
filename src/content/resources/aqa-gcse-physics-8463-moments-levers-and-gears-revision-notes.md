## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pivot | The fixed point about which an object rotates |
| Moment | The turning effect of a force: force × perpendicular distance from the pivot |
| Line of action | The line along which a force acts |
| Perpendicular distance | Shortest distance from the pivot to the line of action, at right angles to it |
| Principle of moments | For a balanced object, total clockwise moment = total anticlockwise moment |
| Lever | A rigid bar turning about a pivot, used to transmit a turning effect |
| Gear | A toothed wheel that transmits a turning effect to another gear |
| Driver gear | The gear that is turned first |
| Driven gear | The gear turned by the driver |

## The equation

```
M = F d          recall
F = M / d        d = M / F
M in N m,  F in N,  d in m (perpendicular to the line of action)
```

*Worked reminder.* 25 N on a door handle 0.80 m from the hinge, at right angles: M = 25 × 0.80 = **20 N m**.
The same moment pushing 0.20 m from the hinge needs F = 20 / 0.20 = **100 N**.

## Getting the distance right

- Measure from the **pivot**, not from the end of the object.
- Measure to the **line of action** of the force, at right angles.
- A force whose line of action passes through the pivot has **zero** moment.
- Convert cm to m if the answer must be in N m.

## Balanced objects

```
Step 1  label the pivot
Step 2  sort forces into clockwise and anticlockwise
Step 3  sum of (F × d) clockwise = sum of (F × d) anticlockwise
Step 4  solve for the unknown
Step 5  if asked: force at pivot = total downward force (forces balance too)
```

*Worked reminder.* A beam of negligible weight, pivoted at its centre, has a 2.0 N weight 30 cm to the left.
Where must a 1.5 N weight go on the right?
2.0 × 0.30 = 1.5 × d → d = 0.60 / 1.5 = **0.40 m** to the right.

### Uniform beams

- A uniform beam's weight acts at its **midpoint** (its centre of mass).
- If the pivot is not at the midpoint, include the beam's weight as a moment.

## Levers

| Lever | Pivot | Effort | Load |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crowbar | Edge it rests on | Hand at the long end | Object being lifted at the short end |
| Wheelbarrow | Wheel axle | Lift at the handles | Load in the barrow, between axle and handles |
| Scissors | Central screw | Fingers at the handles | Material between the blades |
| Bottle opener | Edge on top of the cap | Hand at the end of the handle | Edge of the cap |

**How a lever transmits a turning effect:**

1. A small effort far from the pivot gives a large moment.
2. That moment is transmitted along the rigid lever.
3. At a small distance from the pivot, the same moment means a large force on the load.

## Gears

| Driven gear compared with driver | Moment on driven gear | Speed of driven gear |
|---|---|---|
| Larger (more teeth) | Larger | Slower |
| Same size | Same | Same |
| Smaller (fewer teeth) | Smaller | Faster |

- At the teeth, the forces on the two gears are equal and opposite.
- Moment on each gear = force at the teeth × that gear's radius.
- Meshing gears turn in opposite directions.
- Ratio of moments (ignoring friction) = ratio of radii = ratio of numbers of teeth.

*Worked reminder.* A 10-tooth gear drives a 40-tooth gear. The driven gear receives **4 times** the moment
and turns at **¼** of the speed, in the opposite direction.

## Exam traps

- Using the slanting length instead of the perpendicular distance.
- Answers in N cm when N m is needed.
- Leaving out one of several forces on the same side.
- Ignoring the weight of a non-uniform or off-centre beam.
- Saying gears "increase the force and the speed" together. A larger moment comes with a lower speed.
- Writing "the moment is bigger because the force is bigger" when the force is the same and the distance is
  what changed.

## Quick self-test

1. State the equation for the moment of a force, with units.
2. Calculate the moment of a 40 N force acting 0.25 m from a pivot, at right angles.
3. Why is a door handle placed far from the hinges?
4. A force of 60 N gives a moment of 21 N m. Calculate the perpendicular distance.
5. State the principle of moments.
6. On a seesaw, a 450 N person sits 1.2 m left of the pivot. How far right must a 360 N person sit to
   balance?
7. A force acts along a line that passes through the pivot. What is its moment?
8. A uniform beam is pivoted at its midpoint. Does its own weight have a moment about the pivot? Why?
9. A crowbar has an effort of 200 N at 1.2 m from the pivot. The load is 0.15 m from the pivot. Calculate the
   force on the load.
10. A small gear drives a larger gear. State what happens to the moment and the speed.
11. A 15-tooth gear drives a 45-tooth gear. The driver has a moment of 2.0 N m. Calculate the moment on the
    driven gear, ignoring friction.
12. In which direction does a driven gear turn compared with its driver?

### Answers

1. M = F d; M in N m, F in N, d in m.
2. 40 × 0.25 = **10 N m**.
3. A larger perpendicular distance gives a larger moment for the same force, so less force is needed.
4. 21 ÷ 60 = **0.35 m**.
5. When an object is balanced, the total clockwise moment about a pivot equals the total anticlockwise
   moment about that pivot.
6. 450 × 1.2 = 540 N m; d = 540 ÷ 360 = **1.5 m**.
7. **Zero**: the perpendicular distance is zero.
8. No: its weight acts at the midpoint, which is the pivot, so the distance is zero.
9. 200 × 1.2 = 240 N m; 240 ÷ 0.15 = **1600 N**.
10. The moment on the larger gear is larger, and it turns more slowly.
11. Ratio 45 ÷ 15 = 3; moment = 3 × 2.0 = **6.0 N m**.
12. The opposite direction.
