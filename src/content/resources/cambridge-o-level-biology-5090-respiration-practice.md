## Section A

**1.** Which statement about respiration is correct?

A: it happens only in animal cells

B: it is another word for breathing

C: it happens in all living cells

D: it makes energy from glucose

**[1]**

**2.** Which is **not** a use of the energy released by respiration?

A: active transport

B: muscle contraction

C: protein synthesis

D: transpiration

**[1]**

**3.** Define respiration. **[2]**

**4.** State **two** uses of energy in a growing seedling. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why muscles need a continuous supply of glucose during exercise. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A student investigated the effect of temperature on respiration in yeast. The yeast was mixed with glucose solution and kept in water baths. The number of bubbles of gas produced per minute was counted.

| Temperature (degrees Celsius) | Bubbles per minute |
|---|---|
| 10 | 5 |
| 20 | 12 |
| 30 | 26 |
| 40 | 38 |
| 50 | 6 |

**(a)** Name the gas in the bubbles. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe the effect of temperature on the rate of respiration between 10 and 40 degrees Celsius. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the percentage increase in the rate between 20 and 30 degrees Celsius. Show your working. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain the results at 50 degrees Celsius. **[3]**

**(e)** State **two** variables that should be kept constant. **[2]**

**(f)** Suggest **two** ways of making the results more reliable or precise. **[2]**

**7.** A student says, "We breathe in oxygen so that we can respire." Another says, "Breathing and respiration are the same thing."

**(a)** State which of these statements is correct. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain the difference between breathing and respiration. **[3]**

**8.** Explain why a mammal that keeps its body temperature constant needs more food in cold weather than in warm weather. **[3]**

**9.** Name **three** uses of energy in the human body and give an example of each. **[3]**

**10.** A root hair cell takes up nitrate ions from soil water that has a lower concentration of nitrate than the cell. Explain why this process needs energy from respiration. **[3]**

**11.** A pile of damp, germinating seeds is kept in a closed flask with a thermometer. After two days the temperature of the pile is 6 degrees Celsius higher than the air in the room.

**(a)** Explain why the temperature rises. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest why the seeds need energy at this stage. **[2]**

**12.** Explain why plants also respire, even though they make their own glucose in photosynthesis. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** C [1]. **[1]**

**2.** D [1]. **[1]**

**3.** The chemical reactions in all living cells [1] that release energy from glucose [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two of: growth; cell division; protein synthesis; active transport [1 each]. **[2]**

**5.** Muscles contract to move the body, which needs energy [1]. Respiration of glucose releases energy, so the supply of glucose must be continuous [1]. **[2]**

**6.**

**(a)** Carbon dioxide [1].

**(b)** The rate increases [1] as the temperature rises, from 5 to 38 bubbles per minute [1].

**(c)** (26 - 12) = 14 [1]. 14 divided by 12 x 100 = 116.7 per cent (accept 117) [1].

**(d)** The rate falls to 6 bubbles per minute [1]. The enzymes are denatured at the high temperature [1], so their active sites change shape and the substrate no longer fits [1].

**(e)** Any two of: volume and concentration of glucose; mass of yeast; time over which bubbles are counted [1 each].

**(f)** Any two of: repeat and calculate a mean; use smaller temperature intervals; use a thermostatic water bath; collect and measure the volume of gas [1 each]. **[12]**

**7.**

**(a)** The first statement is correct [1].

**(b)** Respiration is the chemical reactions in cells that release energy from glucose [1]. Breathing is the movement of air into and out of the lungs [1]. Breathing supplies the oxygen that cells use in aerobic respiration [1]. **[4]**

**8.** In cold weather, more heat is lost to the surroundings [1]. The mammal must release more heat by respiration to keep its temperature constant [1], so it needs more glucose from food [1]. **[3]**

**9.** Any three correct uses with examples, such as: muscle contraction, for example running [1]; protein synthesis, for example making enzymes [1]; active transport, for example ion uptake [1]; cell division, for example growth of skin [1]; passage of nerve impulses [1]; growth [1]; maintaining body temperature [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Nitrate is moving from a lower to a higher concentration, against the concentration gradient [1]. This is active transport, which cannot happen by diffusion [1], so energy released by respiration is needed to move the ions [1]. **[3]**

**11.**

**(a)** The seeds respire [1], and some of the energy released from glucose is released as heat [1].

**(b)** Any two of: growth; cell division; protein synthesis; active transport [1 each]. **[4]**

**12.** Respiration is needed to release the energy from glucose for the cell's activities [1], such as growth, active transport and protein synthesis [1]. Photosynthesis makes glucose but does not release energy for these activities, and it does not happen in the dark or in roots [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that respiration "makes energy" or "produces energy".
- Using "breathing" in a definition of respiration.
- Giving the effect of temperature without mentioning enzymes.
- Saying that enzymes are "killed" by heat.
- Writing that temperature is the dependent variable.
