Prescribed subject 3 covers Japanese expansion in East Asia (1931-1941) and German and Italian expansion (1933-1940). For each, revise the causes of expansion, the events and the responses of other states and organisations.

Related: [Move to global war study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-world-history-dp-history-ib-dp-world-history-prescribed-subjects-3/) and [Move to global war practice questions](/resources/ib-ib-dp-world-history-dp-history-ib-dp-world-history-prescribed-subjects-3-practice/).

## Japan, 1931-1941: chain of events

1. **September 1931**: Mukden Incident; Kwantung Army occupies Manchuria without cabinet approval.
2. **1932**: Manchukuo proclaimed with Puyi, the last Qing emperor, as figurehead; Prime Minister Inukai assassinated (May).
3. **1932-1933**: Lytton Report; League Assembly adopts it (February 1933); Japan announces withdrawal (March 1933).
4. **1933**: Jehol taken; Tanggu Truce.
5. **1936**: February 26 Incident; Anti-Comintern Pact with Germany; Xi'an Incident leads to the Second United Front.
6. **1937**: Marco Polo Bridge (July); battle for Shanghai; fall of Nanjing and the Nanjing Massacre (December).
7. **1938**: Prime Minister Konoe proclaims a "New Order in East Asia".
8. **1939**: defeat by Soviet forces at Nomonhan (Khalkhin Gol); US announces the end of the commercial treaty.
9. **1940**: northern Indochina occupied; Tripartite Pact (September); US restricts scrap iron and steel exports.
10. **1941**: Neutrality Pact with the USSR (April); southern Indochina occupied (July); US freezes assets and halts oil; Pearl Harbor (7 December).

## Germany and Italy, 1933-1940: chain of events

1. **1933**: Germany leaves the Disarmament Conference and the League.
2. **1934**: Non-aggression pact with Poland; failed Nazi coup in Austria; Mussolini moves troops to the Brenner Pass.
3. **1935**: Saar returns to Germany by plebiscite; conscription announced; Stresa Front; Anglo-German Naval Agreement; Italy invades Abyssinia; Hoare-Laval plan.
4. **1936**: Rhineland remilitarised; Abyssinia annexed; Spanish Civil War begins; Rome-Berlin Axis.
5. **1937**: Italy joins the Anti-Comintern Pact and leaves the League.
6. **1938**: *Anschluss*; Sudeten crisis; Munich Agreement (September).
7. **1939**: Germany occupies Bohemia and Moravia (March); British guarantee to Poland; Italy invades Albania (April); Pact of Steel (May); Nazi-Soviet Pact (August); Poland invaded (1 September); Britain and France declare war (3 September).
8. **1940**: German offensives in the west; Italy declares war (10 June).

## Causes: factor grid

| Factor | Japan | Germany | Italy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ideology | Emperor-centred nationalism; army's pan-Asian mission | Reversal of Versailles; *Lebensraum*; racial ideology | Fascist glorification of war; new Roman empire |
| Economics | Depression and silk collapse; need for raw materials | Rearmament and the search for resources | Economic weakness; colonial prestige |
| Domestic politics | Army independence; assassinations; weak cabinets | Dictatorship from 1933; foreign success built popularity | Mussolini's need for prestige |
| Opportunity | Chinese disunity; Western powers distracted by the Depression | Divided Britain and France; distrust of the USSR | Anglo-French reluctance to lose Italy as an ally against Germany |

## Responses: what was done and why it failed

| Response | Target | Why it failed |
|---|---|---|
| Lytton Report | Japan | No sanctions; Japan simply left the League |
| Brussels Conference (1937) | Japan | Powers would not commit to action |
| League sanctions | Italy | Oil excluded; canal open; non-members still traded |
| Hoare-Laval plan | Italy | Exposed and abandoned, discrediting both Britain and the League |
| Stresa Front | Germany | Undermined by the Anglo-German Naval Agreement and Abyssinia |
| Appeasement and Munich | Germany | Encouraged further demands; broken in March 1939 |
| Guarantee to Poland | Germany | Could not be enforced directly; Nazi-Soviet Pact removed the Soviet threat to Germany |
| US embargoes | Japan | Pushed Japan towards seizing resources in the south |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Kwantung Army | Japanese army stationed in Manchuria, which acted independently in 1931 |
| Manchukuo | Japanese puppet state in Manchuria from 1932 |
| Second United Front | Nationalist-Communist alliance in China against Japan from 1937 |
| Collective security | Members of the League acting together against an aggressor |
| Appeasement | Meeting reasonable grievances by negotiation to avoid war |
| *Lebensraum* | "Living space" for Germans in eastern Europe |
| *Anschluss* | Union of Germany and Austria (March 1938) |
| Axis | The alignment of Germany and Italy, later joined by Japan |
| Tripartite Pact | 1940 agreement of Germany, Italy and Japan |

## Exam traps

- **Forgetting responses.** The case studies include how others reacted; a Question 4 on causes of war often needs them.
- **"The League did nothing."** It investigated Manchuria and applied sanctions to Italy; say why these were ineffective.
- **Hindsight.** Sources from 1938 cannot know about the Holocaust or 1945; judge provenance by what the author could know.
- **Mixing up pacts.** Anti-Comintern (1936), Pact of Steel (1939), Nazi-Soviet (1939), Tripartite (1940).
- **Historians as sources.** In Question 2 a historian's extract is evaluated like any other: consider date, purpose and focus.

## Self-test

1. What incident in September 1931 began the occupation of Manchuria?
   *The Mukden Incident, an explosion on the South Manchurian Railway staged by Kwantung Army officers.*
2. What did the Lytton Report conclude?
   *Japan's action was not self-defence and Manchuria should remain under Chinese sovereignty with autonomy.*
3. What was the Second United Front, and what led to it?
   *The Nationalist-Communist alliance against Japan, following the Xi'an Incident of December 1936.*
4. Where did full-scale war between Japan and China begin in July 1937?
   *The Marco Polo Bridge, near Beijing.*
5. Which American measure of 1941 most threatened Japan's war in China?
   *The freezing of Japanese assets and the halt to oil exports.*
6. Why did League sanctions against Italy fail to stop the conquest of Abyssinia?
   *They excluded oil, the Suez Canal stayed open and some states did not apply them.*
7. What was the Hoare-Laval plan?
   *A 1935 Anglo-French proposal to give Italy much of Abyssinia, abandoned when it leaked.*
8. Which German action of March 1936 broke both Versailles and Locarno?
   *The remilitarisation of the Rhineland.*
9. What did the Munich Agreement give Germany?
   *The Sudetenland.*
10. Which country did Italy invade in April 1939?
    *Albania.*
11. Why did the Nazi-Soviet Pact matter for the outbreak of war?
    *It removed the threat of a war on two fronts and allowed Germany to invade Poland.*
12. Summarise the difference between Taylor's and Trevor-Roper's views of Hitler.
    *Taylor saw an opportunist exploiting others' mistakes; Trevor-Roper saw a leader pursuing long-held aims.*
