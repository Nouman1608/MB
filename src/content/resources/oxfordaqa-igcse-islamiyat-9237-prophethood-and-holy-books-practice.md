## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. In the 4-mark questions, one mark for each point identified and one for developing it, up to two marks for each of two points (question 6: four marks for a developed explanation). The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks on that pattern plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general, accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The 12-mark question is marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

### Section A

**1.** The Tawrat [1].

**2.** Seal of the Prophets; the last prophet [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: they were human; they were chosen by Allah; they were truthful and trustworthy; they taught Tawhid; they delivered Allah's message; they were supported by miracles; they should all be respected.

**4.** Any two, [1] each: the Suhuf of Ibraheem (AS); the Tawrat; the Zabur; the Injil.

**5.** For each of two reasons: reason identified [1] and developed [1].

- **First human and prophet** [1]: he began the line of prophets who taught Tawhid [1].
- **Steward of the earth** [1]: Allah made him a *khalifah*, which shows humans are trusted with responsibility [1].
- **Repentance** [1]: he asked Allah to forgive him after eating from the tree, and Allah accepted, which shows that sincere repentance is accepted [1].
- **Human dignity** [1]: the angels were told to bow to him, showing the honour Allah gave humanity [1].

**6.** Up to four marks: the earlier books were revealed by Allah [1], such as the Tawrat, Zabur and Injil [1]; Muslims believe that parts of their texts were altered or lost over time [1]; the Qur'an confirms what was true in them and is preserved by Allah, so Muslims treat it as the final guide [1].

### Section B

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two lessons, the lesson identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Lessons (AO1):

- **Submission** [1]: he was willing to sacrifice his son at Allah's command, so Muslims learn to put Allah's command first [1].
- **Rejecting idols** [1]: he broke the idols and stood alone for Tawhid, so Muslims learn courage in worshipping Allah alone [1].
- **Trust in Allah** [1]: he was saved from the fire, so Muslims learn to rely on Allah in danger [1].
- **Building the Ka'aba** [1]: he and Isma'il built the house of worship, so Muslims learn to value shared work for Allah [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "O fire, be coolness and safety upon Abraham" (Qur'an 21:69) [2].
- "Our Lord, accept [this] from us" said while raising the foundations of the Ka'aba (2:127) [2].

**8.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Reasons (AO1):

- **The Qur'an is final** [1]: since no prophet follows, the Qur'an remains the preserved guide for all time [1].
- **Universal message** [1]: he was sent for all humanity, so Islam is for every place and age [1].
- **Completeness of religion** [1]: Muslims believe the religion was completed, so no new revelation is expected [1].
- **Model for life** [1]: his Sunnah provides the example of how to live the Qur'an [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Muhammad is ... the Messenger of Allah and last of the prophets" (Qur'an 33:40) [2].
- The Hadith that he is the last brick of a completed house (Bukhari and Muslim) [2].

**9.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- The passage describes his character: from among the people, compassionate, concerned and kind.
- Before revelation he was known as *al-Amin* (the trustworthy), which Muslims take as evidence of his sincerity.
- His character made him a model who lived the Qur'an.
- His trust in Allah when people turned away shows firmness.
- **Against the statement:** many Muslims say the strongest proof is the Qur'an itself, which they hold to be a miracle in its language and guidance; also, miracles and fulfilled signs are used as proof, and character alone is not unique to him.
- **Judgement:** character is a major proof for believers and for those who knew him, but it works together with the Qur'an and his message.

*Use of the passage*

- "from among yourselves" shows he was human and accessible.
- "Grievous to him is what you suffer" shows compassion.
- "concerned over you" shows concern for guidance.
- "to the believers is kind and merciful" shows mercy.
- "Sufficient for me is Allah ... On Him I have relied" shows complete trust in Allah.
