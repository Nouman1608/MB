The non-exam assessment (NEA) in OxfordAQA International GCSE English Language (9270) is a research
project taken **instead of Paper 2**. You research a topic using **at least five sources**, write a
centre-set task with a specified purpose and audience, and complete a **Candidate record form** with
details of your sources, an example of your note-making and a commentary on your writing. It is worth
**60 marks (30 reading, 30 writing) and 40%** of the qualification. For the full explanation, use the
[Non-exam assessment study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-english-language-non-exam-assessment-study-guide/).

## Route check

| Component | Weighting | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Paper 1: Literary non-fiction and composition | 60% | 2-hour exam, 80 marks; compulsory |
| **Either** Paper 2: Source-based reading and directed writing | 40% | 2-hour exam, 80 marks |
| **Or** Non-exam assessment project | 40% | 60 raw marks, scaled ×1.33 to 80 |

The NEA is teacher assessed and moderated by the board. The qualification is linear, and the specification
states that NEA marks can be carried forward for the lifetime of the specification.

## The three-stage process

1. **Task set.** Before research, your centre sets a written task with a specified **purpose and
   audience**. Tasks can vary between students and may relate to academic study, a sport or interest, a
   famous person or an organisation. A word limit may be set.
2. **Research.** Use **at least five different sources**: research texts (encyclopaedia, textbook),
   literary texts (novel, poem), non-fiction (review, autobiography), media (film, documentary) or other
   valid sources. Keep clear records of sources and processes (note-taking, highlighting).
3. **Write and reflect.** Write the assignment (diagrams and illustrations are allowed where relevant),
   then complete the Candidate record form.

## What goes on the Candidate record form

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Sources | Details of **all** sources, enough for a moderator to access them; at least five |
| Selection and process | How and why you chose sources, and how you gathered information (note-making, annotation, highlighting) |
| Note-making example | **One** example of your note-making method |
| Commentary | Why you included or rejected information and ideas; how and why you organised your writing; presentational and stylistic features used to achieve your purpose |

The form "is an important feature of the assignment and will be considered in the assessment".

## Assessment objectives assessed

| AO | Strands relevant to the NEA |
|---|---|
| AO1 Reading | R1 select and use material for purpose; R2 collate and synthesise from sources; R3 develop interpretations of writers' ideas and perspectives; R4 explain and evaluate writers' use of features |
| AO2 Writing | W1 communicate clearly and imaginatively, adapting form and vocabulary; W2 range of sentence structures; W3 organise for cohesion and coherence; W4 technical accuracy |

## Reading bands (30 marks)

| Band | Marks | Key words |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | 26–30 | "impressive" breadth of reading; "sophisticated and independent integration and adaptation" of sources; "impressive condensing and extrapolating" |
| 5 | 21–25 | "substantial" reading; "assured and independent integration" |
| 4 | 16–20 | "variety" of relevant information; "clear and effective use" of sources |
| 3 | 11–15 | "a range of relevant information"; "appropriate selection" |
| 2 | 6–10 | "some relevant information"; "some use" of sources |
| 1 | 1–5 | "little to no" information or evidence of sources |

Every band lists "five sources cited on Candidate record form".

## Writing bands (30 marks)

| Band | Marks | Key words |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | 26–30 | "convincing and/or compelling"; "sustained personal voice"; "clear and controlled manipulation of sentence structures" |
| 5 | 21–25 | "effectively most of the time"; "sophisticated vocabulary"; "beginning to manipulate the reader's response" |
| 4 | 16–20 | "clearly engages the audience"; "deliberate structure"; integrated discourse markers |
| 3 | 11–15 | "begins to engage"; logically sequenced; some conscious control of paragraphing |
| 2 | 6–10 | "at times clear"; "occasional awareness of purpose and audience" |
| 1 | 1–5 | "some relevant meaning"; some ideas in order |

Both grids include the commentary: Band 6 needs "explanation, example and commentary to show
sophisticated and independent command" of the reading and research processes (reading) and of the
writing process (writing).

## The specification's example tasks

- A **letter** to the organisers of an international conference on global warming, discussing causes and
  consequences for your generation and proposing solutions.
- A **magazine article** for a business centre's clients on designing, maintaining and developing an
  effective website, to inform, educate and persuade.
- The **opening speech** in a radio debate, for or against "Bill Gates is a true hero of our times",
  combining factual accuracy, logical consistency and emotional appeal.

## Worked example: synthesising, not copying

**Copying (Band 2–3):** "According to Source 3, sea levels rose by a certain amount. Source 4 says
glaciers are melting."

**Synthesising (Band 5–6):** "Rising seas and shrinking glaciers are two sides of the same warming:
the scientific sources I consulted describe the physical change, while a documentary I watched showed
what that change means for coastal families. For delegates, the point is that my generation will inherit
both the data and the displacement."

The second version condenses, combines sources, adapts them for the audience (conference organisers) and
keeps a personal voice, which is what "integration and adaptation" means. Note that it avoids inventing
figures: in your own work, every statistic must come from a cited source.

## Common mistakes / traps

- **Fewer than five sources**, or sources described too vaguely for a moderator to find.
- **No note-making example** on the Candidate record form.
- **A commentary that only describes** the writing instead of explaining choices.
- **Copying source wording**: integration and adaptation are rewarded, not reproduction.
- **Ignoring purpose and audience**: the task specifies both, and the writing grid rewards engaging the
  intended reader.
- **Weak accuracy**: W4 (grammar, punctuation, spelling) runs through every writing band.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many sources do I need for the OxfordAQA 9270 NEA?**
At least five, recorded on the Candidate record form with enough detail for a moderator to access them.

**Can the NEA task be on any topic?**
The task is set by your centre and may differ between students. The specification's examples include
academic study, a sport or interest, a famous person or an organisation.

**Is the commentary assessed?**
Yes. The Candidate record form, including the commentary, is considered in the assessment, and both mark
grids refer to explanation and commentary.

**Can I take both Paper 2 and the NEA?**
No. The NEA project is the alternative to Paper 2; each is worth 40%.

## Self-test

1. What does the NEA replace? (Paper 2.)
2. How many raw marks is it worth, and how are they split? (60: 30 reading, 30 writing.)
3. What is the minimum number of sources? (Five.)
4. Name the three parts of the Candidate record form. (Sources; processes with a note-making example; commentary.)
5. What scaling factor applies to the NEA? (1.33.)
