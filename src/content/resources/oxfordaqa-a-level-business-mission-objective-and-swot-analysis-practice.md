## Questions

**1.** State **two** purposes of a mission statement. **[2]**

**2.** Orchard Point Hotels plc has 125 million shares in issue. Its share price is £1.84. Calculate its market capitalisation. **[2]**

**3.** Orchard Point paid total dividends of £9.6 million last year on 80 million ordinary shares (before a later share issue). The share price at the time was £2.40. Calculate the dividend yield. **[4]**

**4.** A furniture maker sets an objective to cut its carbon emissions by 30% within four years. Explain how this objective could affect its operations function. **[4]**

**5.** Penrowan Print had an average of 56 employees last year, and 14 left. It completed 8,400 print jobs against a maximum potential output of 10,500. The industry averages are labour turnover 15% and capacity utilisation 85%.

**(a)** Calculate Penrowan's labour turnover. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate Penrowan's capacity utilisation. **[2]**
**(c)** State whether each result is a strength or a weakness, giving a reason. **[2]**

**6.** A market grew from £80 million to £92 million in a year. A firm's sales in that market rose from £10.4 million to £11.5 million.

**(a)** Calculate the market growth rate. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the change in the firm's market share, in percentage points. **[3]**

**7.** A firm has fixed costs of £90,000. It sells each unit for £45 and variable cost is £27 per unit. It sells 6,200 units. Calculate the break-even output and the margin of safety. **[4]**

**8.** A bakery chain finds that:

**(a)** when a rival raised its prices by 8%, the chain's quantity demanded rose by 6%. Calculate the cross-price elasticity and state what it shows. **[2]**
**(b)** when local incomes fell by 3%, the chain's quantity demanded rose by 1.5%. Calculate the income elasticity and state what it shows. **[2]**

**9.** An online retailer finds a correlation of −0.72 between average delivery time and the rate of repeat orders. Explain what this shows and **one** limitation of using it in a SWOT analysis. **[3]**

**10.** Brockhall Quarry Ltd wants to extend its quarry towards a village. Analyse how stakeholder mapping could help Brockhall manage this decision. **[6]**

**11.** Analyse why the objectives of shareholders and employees in a public limited company may both overlap and conflict. **[6]**

**12.** Tallowfield Tiles plc (£000): revenue 12,500; operating profit 975; non-current liabilities 4,200; total equity 3,300; current assets 2,100 including inventory 1,300; current liabilities 1,400. Industry averages: ROCE 10%, gearing 40%, acid test 0.8. Labour turnover is 9% against an industry 14%. The directors want a growth objective of opening a second factory.

**(a)** Calculate Tallowfield's ROCE and gearing. **[4]**
**(b)** Evaluate whether a SWOT analysis based on this data is enough for the directors to decide on the growth objective. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: to communicate the business's purpose and values to stakeholders [1]; to guide objectives and strategy, or to motivate staff and shape culture [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "State" questions reward brief, distinct points; two versions of the same idea tend to gain credit only once.

**2.** Market capitalisation = 125,000,000 × £1.84 [1] = **£230 million** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the formula with the figures substituted; a bare final number risks losing the method credit if it is wrong.

**3.** Dividend per share = 9,600,000 ÷ 80,000,000 [1] = £0.12 [1]. Dividend yield = 0.12 ÷ 2.40 × 100 [1] = **5%** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Find dividend per share first; going straight from total dividends to a percentage is the usual way the method is lost.

**4.** Operations may need new, lower-emission machinery or energy sources [1], which raises short-term costs and may disrupt production while it is installed [1]. It may switch to recycled or locally sourced timber [1], which can cut transport emissions but may limit supplier choice or raise unit costs [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each point needs a consequence for operations; naming "greener machines" alone earns less than explaining what changes in production.

**5. (a)** 14 ÷ 56 × 100 [1] = **25%** [1].
**(b)** 8,400 ÷ 10,500 × 100 [1] = **80%** [1].
**(c)** Labour turnover is a weakness: 25% is well above the 15% average, raising recruitment and training costs [1]. Capacity utilisation is a weakness: 80% is below 85%, so fixed costs are spread over fewer jobs [1].
*Examiner insight:* A strength/weakness label gains credit only when it is tied to the benchmark given in the question.

**6. (a)** (92 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100 [1] = **15%** [1].
**(b)** Share before = 10.4 ÷ 80 × 100 = 13% [1]; share after = 11.5 ÷ 92 × 100 = 12.5% [1]; change = **−0.5 percentage points** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Sales rose, yet share fell; say "percentage points", not "per cent", for a difference between two shares.

**7.** Contribution per unit = 45 − 27 = £18 [1]. Break-even output = 90,000 ÷ 18 = **5,000 units** [1]. Margin of safety = 6,200 − 5,000 [1] = **1,200 units** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give units with the answer; a margin of safety stated in pounds when the question works in units can lose credit.

**8. (a)** XED = +6 ÷ +8 = **+0.75**: the products are **substitutes** [1], with a fairly weak relationship (below 1) [1].
**(b)** YED = +1.5 ÷ −3 = **−0.5**: an **inferior** good [1], so demand rises in a downturn, a possible strength in recession [1].
*Examiner insight:* The sign carries the meaning; a correct number with the wrong interpretation of the sign gains little.

**9.** A strong negative correlation: longer delivery times go with fewer repeat orders [1], so slow delivery is a likely weakness [1]. Limitation: correlation does not prove causation; other factors such as price or product range may explain repeat orders [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Comment on both sign and strength; "negative" alone earns less than "strong negative".

**10.** Mapping places groups by power and interest so Brockhall can prioritise [1]. The planning authority has high power and high interest, so Brockhall should involve it early to avoid refusal and delay [1]. Villagers have high interest; their power rises if they organise or gain media coverage [1], so keeping them informed and offering dust and noise controls may prevent a campaign [1]. Lenders may have high power but lower interest, so they need to be kept satisfied about costs and returns [1]. However, the map is a judgement that can quickly change, so Brockhall must review it as opposition develops [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that apply the grid to named groups in the case score more highly than ones that describe the four quadrants in general.

**11.** Overlap: both groups gain when the company grows profitably [1], because higher profits fund dividends and job security or pay rises [1]. Productivity gains from well-trained staff can raise both wages and returns [1]. Conflict: shareholders may want higher dividends now, which leaves less for pay [1]. Cost-cutting through automation or relocation can lift returns while cutting jobs [1]. The degree of conflict depends on time horizon: over the long term the overlap is usually greater [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Analysis questions reward developed chains of reasoning; a list of stakeholder wants with no cause and effect gains limited credit.

**12. (a)** Capital employed = 4,200 + 3,300 = 7,500 [1]; ROCE = 975 ÷ 7,500 × 100 = **13%** [1]. Gearing = 4,200 ÷ 7,500 × 100 [1] = **56%** [1].
**(b)** SWOT gives a structured view of the internal position before a strategic decision [1]. ROCE of 13% beats the 10% average, a strength that suggests the new factory could earn a good return [1]. Labour turnover of 9% against 14% suggests a stable workforce that could train new staff [1]. However, gearing of 56% is well above 40% [1], so borrowing for a second factory would raise interest costs and risk [1]. The acid test is (2,100 − 1,300) ÷ 1,400 = 0.57, below 0.8, so liquidity is a weakness if building costs overrun [1]. The data covers strengths and weaknesses only; it says nothing about demand, competition or the economy [1], which are the opportunities and threats [1]. It is also a snapshot, and the factors are not weighted [1]. Judgement: the data supports growth on profitability but not on how it is financed [1]. The directors also need market research and a forecast of returns before deciding [1]. Overall, the SWOT is a useful starting point but not enough on its own [1].
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation answers that use the calculated figures and reach a supported judgement tend to gain more credit than balanced lists with no conclusion.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing total dividends by market capitalisation instead of finding dividend per share first.
- Leaving out non-current liabilities from capital employed.
- Calling a figure a strength or weakness without comparing it to the benchmark given.
- Writing "per cent" for a change in market share when the answer is in percentage points.
- Reversing the meaning of a negative income or cross-price elasticity.
- Describing the stakeholder grid in general instead of placing the groups in the case.
- Treating opportunities and threats as internal factors.
- Ending an evaluation without a clear judgement.
