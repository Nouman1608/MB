These notes condense heading 9 *Ownership and control of media* from Cambridge International
A Level Sociology (9699) Paper 4. The central debate is whether media content reflects audience
choice (pluralism) or the interests of a powerful few (Marxism and neo-Marxism), and whether the
new media have changed that balance. Expect 35-mark essays asking you to evaluate one theory or one
claim about the new media.

Full explanations: [media ownership and control study guide](/resources/a-level-cambridge-sociology-media-ownership-and-control/).

## Syllabus coverage

9699 (2024–2026) Paper 4 Section B, heading 9: **9.1** Traditional media and the new media;
**9.2** Theories of the media and influences on media content; **9.3** The impact of the new media.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Media conglomerate** | Large corporation owning many media (and often non-media) businesses |
| **Cross-media ownership** | Owning different media types (press, TV, online) |
| **Digitalisation** | Conversion of content into digital form |
| **Convergence** | Merging of media technologies and content on one platform (Jenkins, 2006) |
| **Gatekeeping** | Editors deciding what becomes news |
| **News values** | Criteria that make events newsworthy (Galtung and Ruge, 1965) |
| **Agenda setting** | Media influence on what issues people think about (McCombs and Shaw, 1972) |
| **Hegemony** | Dominance of ruling-class ideas through consent (Gramsci) |
| **Hyperreality** | Media images become more real than reality (Baudrillard) |
| **Censorship** | Restriction of content by state, law, owners or self-censorship |
| **Digital optimism / pessimism** | Positive / negative evaluations of the new media's impact |
| **Filter bubble** | Algorithmic personalisation limiting exposure to other views (Pariser, 2011) |

## 9.1 Trends and traditional versus new media

- Trends: cross-media ownership, conglomerates (Bagdikian, *The Media Monopoly*, 1983),
  digitalisation, social media.
- Traditional: one-to-many, professional, linear, national. New: interactive, user-generated,
  on demand, global.
- **Undermined?** Falling print and scheduled TV audiences and advertising shift **vs** continuing
  trust in established brands and new-media concentration in a few corporations.

## 9.2 Theories compared

| Theory | Who controls content? | Evidence | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pluralism** | Audiences, through market choice; independent journalists | Diversity of outlets; regulation; public-service broadcasting | Ignores concentration; audiences choose from limited menu |
| **Instrumental Marxism** | Owners, deliberately (mass manipulation) | Miliband (1969); Herman and Chomsky's five filters (1988) | Underplays journalists' autonomy and audience resistance |
| **Neo-Marxism / hegemonic** | Shared middle-class assumptions of journalists | Glasgow University Media Group, *Bad News* (1976); Hall | Hard to prove; may underplay owner influence |
| **Postmodernism** | No single source; fragmented audiences create meaning | Baudrillard (1981) hyperreality | Neglects economic power |

**Influences on news:** news values; gatekeepers; commercial pressure and advertisers; owners;
official and PR sources; deadlines and budgets; censorship and self-censorship.

**Political process:** agenda setting (McCombs and Shaw); opinion polls (bandwagon/underdog);
framing in news reporting; targeted social-media campaigns (Cambridge Analytica, 2018).

## 9.3 The new media

| Optimist claim | Pessimist reply |
|---|---|
| Speeds globalisation and connection (Castells, 1996) | Spreads Western corporate culture and misinformation |
| Challenges power: citizen journalism, Arab Spring (2010–2011) | States use it for surveillance (Morozov, 2011) |
| Participation and choice (Jenkins, 2006) | Amateurism and declining quality (Keen, 2007) |
| Diverse information | Filter bubbles (Pariser, 2011); surveillance capitalism (Zuboff, 2019) |
| Identity experimentation; maintaining ties (boyd, 2014) | Weaker intimacy (Turkle, 2011) |

## Worked essay plan

*Practice question:* "Media content reflects the interests of media owners." Evaluate this view.
[35]

1. Introduce trends towards conglomerates and cross-media ownership.
2. Instrumental Marxist support: Miliband; Herman and Chomsky's filters.
3. Neo-Marxist refinement: hegemony through journalists' shared assumptions (GUMG).
4. Pluralist challenge: audience choice, regulation, professional values.
5. New-media challenge: user-generated content and citizen journalism — but platforms are
   themselves conglomerates.
6. Postmodern angle: audiences interpret content in many ways.
7. Judgement: owners shape the boundaries of content; day-to-day content reflects several
   influences.

## Model evaluation paragraph

*On the pluralist view:* "Pluralists are right that audiences can now choose from more outlets than
ever, and the new media let ordinary users publish content. However, choice is exercised within a
market dominated by a small number of conglomerates, and Herman and Chomsky's filters suggest that
advertising and reliance on official sources narrow the range of views that reach mainstream news.
Neo-Marxists add that this narrowing happens without owners giving orders, through journalists'
shared assumptions. Pluralism therefore describes the *variety* of media products better than it
explains the *limits* on what they say."

The paragraph uses three theories, applies them to the new media and ends with a judgement — the
pattern examiners reward with AO3 marks.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- Confusing **instrumental** Marxism (owners direct) with **hegemonic** Marxism (shared ideology).
- Describing news values without linking them to who controls content.
- Treating new media as automatically democratic — the syllabus asks for optimism *versus*
  pessimism.
- Forgetting **censorship** and the **political process** bullets in 9.2.
- Using only one country's media; apply examples from more than one society where you can.

## Quick self-test

1. Name Herman and Chomsky's five filters.
2. What did McCombs and Shaw find?
3. Give one difference between traditional and new media.
4. Define hegemony.
5. Give one digital pessimist argument and its author.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which theory should my conclusion favour?** Any, if justified. A common, defensible conclusion is
that owners set limits while professional, commercial and audience factors shape daily content.

**Are news values a pluralist or Marxist idea?** They can support either: pluralists see them as
audience-driven, while critics say they systematically favour elites.

**Does 9.3 overlap with globalisation?** Yes — the syllabus explicitly asks how the new media
contribute to globalisation, so links to heading 7 are credited.
