These questions cover section **3.1.8.1 Born–Haber cycles** of the AQA A-level Chemistry specification (7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 1 (and Paper 3). Learn the methods in the [Born–Haber cycles study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-born-haber-cycles/) and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-born-haber-cycles-revision-notes/). All values are in kJ mol⁻¹.

## Questions

**1.** Define each term.

**(a)** Enthalpy of lattice formation **[2]**

**(b)** First electron affinity **[2]**

**(c)** Enthalpy of atomisation **[2]**

**2.** Explain why the second electron affinity of oxygen is positive although the first is negative. **[2]**

**3.** Calculate the enthalpy of lattice formation of potassium chloride using these data: ΔfH(KCl) = −437; ΔatH(K) = +89; first ionisation energy of K = +419; bond enthalpy of Cl–Cl = +244; first electron affinity of Cl = −349. Draw a labelled Born–Haber cycle first. **[5]**

**4.** Calculate the enthalpy of lattice formation of calcium chloride, CaCl₂, using these data: ΔfH = −796; ΔatH(Ca) = +178; first ionisation energy of Ca = +590; second ionisation energy of Ca = +1145; bond enthalpy of Cl–Cl = +244; first electron affinity of Cl = −349. **[4]**

**5.** The lattice enthalpy of formation of lithium fluoride is −1049. Use this and the data below to calculate the first ionisation energy of lithium: ΔfH(LiF) = −617; ΔatH(Li) = +159; bond enthalpy of F–F = +158; first electron affinity of F = −328. **[3]**

**6.** Explain why the lattice enthalpy of formation of magnesium oxide (−3845) is much more exothermic than that of sodium chloride (−787). **[3]**

**7.** The lattice enthalpy of formation of silver chloride calculated from a Born–Haber cycle is −905, but the value calculated using the perfect ionic model is −864.

**(a)** State what is assumed in the perfect ionic model. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain what the difference in the values suggests about the bonding. **[2]**

**8.** The experimental and theoretical lattice enthalpies of silver iodide differ by more than those for silver chloride. Suggest an explanation. **[2]**

**9.** The enthalpy of lattice dissociation of magnesium chloride is +2524. The enthalpies of hydration are −1920 for Mg²⁺ and −364 for Cl⁻.

**(a)** Define enthalpy of hydration. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the enthalpy of solution of MgCl₂. **[2]**

**10.** State the relationship between the enthalpy of lattice formation and the enthalpy of lattice dissociation of the same compound. **[1]**

**11.** A student calculates the lattice enthalpy of magnesium chloride and uses one electron affinity and one atomisation step for chlorine, instead of two of each. Using the data ΔfH = −641, ΔatH(Mg) = +148, IE₁ + IE₂ = +2189, Cl–Cl = +244 and EA₁(Cl) = −349, calculate the value the student obtains and say whether it is too exothermic or not exothermic enough. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The enthalpy change when one mole of an ionic solid [1] is formed from its gaseous ions [1]

**(b)** The enthalpy change when one mole of gaseous atoms gains one mole of electrons [1] to form one mole of gaseous 1− ions [1]

**(c)** The enthalpy change when one mole of gaseous atoms [1] is formed from the element in its standard state [1]. **[6]**

**2.** The first electron is added to a neutral atom and is attracted by the nucleus, so energy is released [1]; the second electron is added to an ion that is already negative, so it is repelled and energy must be supplied [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Cycle: elements K(s) + ½Cl₂(g) at the bottom left, KCl(s) at the bottom right with an arrow labelled ΔfH, and an upper route through K(g) + Cl(g), K⁺(g) + Cl⁻(g) with arrows labelled atomisation, ionisation energy, ½ bond enthalpy, electron affinity and lattice formation [2]. Sum of steps: 89 + 419 + ½(244) − 349 [1] = 89 + 419 + 122 − 349 = 281 [1]; −437 = 281 + x, so x = **−718 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[5]**

**4.** Steps: 178 + (590 + 1145) + 244 + 2(−349) [1] = 178 + 1735 + 244 − 698 = 1459 [1]; −796 = 1459 + x [1]; x = **−2255 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[4]**

**5.** −617 = 159 + IE + 79 + (−328) + (−1049) [1]; −617 = −1139 + IE [1]; IE = **+522 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ have charges of 2+ and 2−, compared with 1+ and 1− in NaCl [1]; the Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ ions are smaller, so the ions are closer together [1]; the electrostatic attraction is much stronger, so the lattice enthalpy is more exothermic [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** The ions are perfectly spherical [1]; with their charge evenly distributed, and the bonding is purely electrostatic [1]

**(b)** The Born–Haber value is more exothermic [1]; this suggests that the bonding has some covalent character, as well as ionic attraction [1]. **[4]**

**8.** The iodide ion is larger than the chloride ion, so it is more easily polarised by the Ag⁺ ion [1]; so there is more covalent character in silver iodide, and a bigger difference between the experimental and theoretical values [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** The enthalpy change when one mole of gaseous ions [1] is dissolved in water to form aqueous ions [1]

**(b)** ΔsolH = +2524 + (−1920) + 2(−364) [1] = **−124 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[4]**

**10.** They have the same magnitude and opposite signs [1]. **[1]**

**11.** The student's sum of steps = 148 + 2189 + 122 + (−349) = 2110 [1]; lattice formation = −641 − 2110 = **−2751 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]; this is too exothermic, because the correct value is −2524 (the student has left out a net −227 kJ mol⁻¹ from the second chlorine) [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using half the bond enthalpy per atom incorrectly, or not at all.
- Forgetting the second ionisation energy or second electron affinity.
- Not doubling the chlorine steps for MgCl₂ or CaCl₂.
- Getting the sign of the lattice enthalpy the wrong way round.
- Explaining covalent character without referring to polarisation.
