Condensed recall for two linked elements in the 9675 Unit 3 content: "punishment, justice,
retribution, the legal system" and "the sense that there will be a resolution and the criminal will be
punished". The unit overview is in the
[Unit 3 study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-english-literature-unit-3-elements-of-crime-and-mystery-study-guide/);
the full discussion and a model paragraph are in the
[punishment and justice study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-english-literature-9675-punishment-and-justice/).

## The idea in one box

- Ask **who punishes**, **whether the law appears** and **whether order is restored**.
- Four kinds of justice: **legal, divine or supernatural, private, internal**.
- Endings can be complete, partial or ironic; "relief" is one reader response the specification
  names, and texts may withhold it.
- Unit 3 is **closed book**, two essays, 25 marks each.

## Four kinds of justice

| Kind | Agent | Typical effect |
|---|---|---|
| Legal | Courts, police, magistrates | Order restored or the system exposed |
| Divine or supernatural | God, spirits, fate | Moral law beyond human institutions |
| Private | Avenger, detective, victim | Law absent, slow or unfit |
| Internal | Conscience | Guilt as the true sentence |

## Text by text

| Text | Main kind of justice | Point to argue |
|---|---|---|
| *Macbeth* | Private avenger and restored kingship | No court; retribution as public spectacle |
| *The Rime of the Ancient Mariner* | Supernatural; religious penance | Punishment disproportionate and never complete |
| Browning | Often none from humans; private revenge | The reader becomes judge |
| *Oliver Twist* | Legal, with satire of the law | The system attacked and relied on |
| Poe | Internal compulsion; an unjust court | Criminals betray themselves; the Inquisition as injustice |
| Stevenson | Internal and supernatural | The law fails to reach Hyde |
| *The Murder of Roger Ackroyd* | Private, arranged by the detective | Discretion over courts |
| *Death of a Red Heroine* | Legal, constrained by politics | Justice as negotiation with power |
| *When Will There Be Good News?* | Official and unofficial investigators | The limits of what punishment can repair |

## Moments to learn

- **Macbeth**: Cawdor's execution reported; the "even-handed justice" Macbeth fears; Macduff's
  victory; the head displayed.
- **Mariner**: the spirits' pursuit; the blessing of the water-snakes; the Hermit's shriving; the
  endless retelling.
- **Browning**: God's silence in "Porphyria's Lover"; the poison in "The Laboratory"; the walk to
  execution in "The Patriot"; the betrayed confession.
- **Oliver Twist**: Mr Fang's court; Bumble on the law; Fagin's condemned cell; Sikes's death.
- **Poe**: the shrieked confession; the betraying wall; the released innocent; the Inquisition.
- **Stevenson**: Hyde's death before arrest; Markheim's surrender; the horror in "The Body Snatcher".
- **Christie**: Poirot's offer to the murderer at the end.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning | Use it to say |
|---|---|---|
| Retribution | Punishment as repayment for a wrong | Whether the penalty fits the crime |
| Poetic justice | An outcome that rewards virtue and punishes vice | Whether the ending feels deserved |
| Providence | Divine ordering of events | How chance seems to punish the guilty |
| Penance | Suffering accepted to atone for sin | The Mariner's endless tale |
| Vigilante justice | Punishment by private individuals | Detectives or avengers acting outside the law |
| Miscarriage of justice | Wrong conviction or accusation of the innocent | What a text thinks of the law |
| Transportation | Victorian sentence of exile to a penal colony | The Artful Dodger's fate |
| Resolution | The ending's settling of the plot | Whether order is truly restored |

## Exam traps

- Assuming every text ends in arrest and trial.
- Ignoring satire of legal institutions.
- Missing wrongful accusations of the innocent.
- Treating the ending as a neutral summary rather than a shaped effect.
- Context about prisons or the church with no link to a moment in the text.

## Self-test

1. Quote the two Unit 3 elements these notes cover.
2. Name the four kinds of justice in these notes.
3. Define retribution.
4. How is Cawdor's execution used at the start of *Macbeth*?
5. Who delivers retribution to Macbeth, and how is it made public?
6. Why is the Mariner's punishment never complete?
7. What does the speaker of "Porphyria's Lover" observe about God, and why does it matter?
8. What does Mr Bumble say about the law, and in what situation?
9. Give two legal outcomes for members of Fagin's gang.
10. How are the narrators of "The Tell-Tale Heart" and "The Black Cat" caught?
11. What does Poirot do instead of handing the murderer to the police?
12. Why is justice difficult in *Death of a Red Heroine*?

## Answers

1. "Punishment, justice, retribution, the legal system" and "the sense that there will be a
   resolution and the criminal will be punished".
2. Legal, divine or supernatural, private, internal.
3. Punishment as repayment for a wrong.
4. It is reported, and the traitor's dignified death sets a standard against which Macbeth's own end
   is measured.
5. Macduff kills him; his head is displayed, so punishment becomes public spectacle.
6. He must keep telling his tale, so his penance continues after his release.
7. That God has not said a word; the silence leaves judgement to the reader.
8. That the law is "a ass", when told that the law assumes a wife acts under her husband's direction.
9. Any two: Fagin is tried and condemned; the Artful Dodger is transported; Sikes dies in flight.
10. Their own compulsion betrays them: one shrieks a confession, the other's rapping on the wall
    reveals the hidden body.
11. He gives the murderer the chance to end his own life and spare his sister a public trial.
12. The suspect has political protection, so the investigation must navigate Party power as well as
    evidence.
