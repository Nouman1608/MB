Recall notes for **4.3.1 Changes of state and the particle model** (4.3.1.1 Density of materials and 4.3.1.2
Changes of state) in AQA GCSE Physics (8463). Paper 1, both tiers; includes required practical 5.

Worked examples and the full practical method are in the [changes of state and the particle model study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-8463-changes-of-state-and-the-particle-model/).
Practise with the [changes of state and the particle model practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-8463-changes-of-state-and-the-particle-model-practice/).

## The equation to recall

| Equation | Units | Rearranged |
|---|---|---|
| ρ = m / V (density = mass ÷ volume) | kg/m³, kg, m³ | m = ρV; V = m / ρ |

Conversions: 1 cm³ = 1 × 10⁻⁶ m³; 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³; 1 ml = 1 cm³; 1 g = 0.001 kg.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Density | Mass per unit volume |
| Particle model | Matter made of tiny particles whose arrangement and motion explain the states |
| Displacement | Finding the volume of an irregular object from the volume of water it pushes aside |
| Eureka (displacement) can | Can with a spout; the overflow equals the object's volume |
| Meniscus | Curved liquid surface; read from the bottom at eye level |
| Physical change | Reversible change in which the material recovers its original properties |
| Sublimation | Change directly from solid to gas |

## States and density

| State | Arrangement | Density |
|---|---|---|
| Solid | Touching, regular, fixed positions | High |
| Liquid | Touching, irregular, moving past each other | High (usually a little lower than the solid) |
| Gas | Far apart, random | Very low |

- Density differs because of **particle spacing**, not particle size.
- Same particles, same mass in every state; a larger volume means a lower density.

## Required practical 5 in brief

- **Regular solid:** measure dimensions (ruler, Vernier callipers or micrometer) → volume → mass on a balance →
  ρ = m / V.
- **Irregular solid:** mass on a balance; volume by displacement in a measuring cylinder (new level − old level) or
  a eureka can.
- **Liquid:** zero the balance with the empty cylinder; add a measured volume; read the mass.
- Read volumes at eye level, from the bottom of the meniscus.
- Choose the measuring instrument to suit the size: micrometer for thin wire or sheet, Vernier callipers for small
  blocks.

## Changes of state

| Change | Direction |
|---|---|
| Melting | Solid → liquid |
| Freezing | Liquid → solid |
| Boiling / evaporating | Liquid → gas |
| Condensing | Gas → liquid |
| Sublimating | Solid → gas |

- **Mass is conserved** in every change of state: the number of particles does not change.
- Changes of state are **physical changes**: reverse the change and the material recovers its original properties.
- Chemical changes make new substances and are not undone simply by reversing the conditions.

## Worked reminders

- Block 2.0 cm × 3.0 cm × 5.0 cm, mass 237 g: V = 30 cm³, ρ = 7.9 g/cm³ = **7900 kg/m³**.
- Stone raises water from 40 cm³ to 52 cm³ and has mass 30 g: ρ = 30 ÷ 12 = 2.5 g/cm³ = **2500 kg/m³**.
- 25.0 cm³ of liquid with mass 20.0 g: ρ = 0.800 g/cm³ = **800 kg/m³**.
- 2.0 kg of a liquid of density 800 kg/m³: V = 2.0 ÷ 800 = **0.0025 m³**.

## Exam traps

- **Unit trap:** g ÷ cm³ gives g/cm³; multiply by 1000 for kg/m³. Dividing by 1000 instead is the classic error.
- **Displacement trap:** using the final water level as the volume instead of the rise in level.
- **Particle trap:** "gas particles are bigger" or "solid particles are heavier" scores zero.
- **Mass trap:** saying mass changes when ice melts or water boils in a closed container.
- **Method trap:** giving "measure the volume" without saying how (dimensions or displacement).
- **Splash trap:** dropping the object in loses water and gives too small a volume, so too large a density.
- **Diagram trap:** a liquid drawn with large gaps between particles looks like a gas.

## Self-test

1. Write the equation linking density, mass and volume, with units.
2. Convert 1.5 g/cm³ to kg/m³.
3. A cube of side 0.10 m has a mass of 2.7 kg. Calculate its density.
4. Why is a gas much less dense than the same substance as a liquid?
5. Which instrument would you use to measure the thickness of a coin?
6. Describe how to find the volume of an irregular stone.
7. A liquid has a mass of 63 g and a volume of 70 cm³. Calculate its density in kg/m³.
8. Name the change of state from gas to liquid.
9. Name the change of state from solid directly to gas.
10. 250 g of ice melts in a sealed bag. What is the mass of water afterwards?
11. Why is melting a physical change?
12. A metal has density 8900 kg/m³. Calculate the volume of 4.45 kg of it.

### Answers

1. ρ = m / V; ρ in kg/m³, m in kg, V in m³.
2. **1500 kg/m³**.
3. V = 0.001 m³; ρ = 2.7 ÷ 0.001 = **2700 kg/m³**.
4. The particles are much further apart, so the same mass occupies a much larger volume.
5. A micrometer (or Vernier callipers).
6. Lower it into a measuring cylinder of water and find the rise in water level, or collect the overflow from a
   full displacement can.
7. 63 ÷ 70 = 0.90 g/cm³ = **900 kg/m³**.
8. Condensing.
9. Sublimating (sublimation).
10. **250 g**: mass is conserved.
11. The process can be reversed and the material recovers its original properties.
12. V = 4.45 ÷ 8900 = **0.00050 m³** (5.0 × 10⁻⁴ m³).
