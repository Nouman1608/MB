The specification places Section P in **Paper 2**. A calculator is required in every 7357 paper, so every question here assumes you have one. Give answers to 3 significant figures where they are not exact, unless the question says otherwise.

## Questions

**1.** Write down the SI unit, with its symbol, of each of these quantities: acceleration, force, moment. **[3]**

**2.** A train travels at 108 km h⁻¹. Convert this speed to m s⁻¹. **[2]**

**3.** A parcel has mass 750 g. Taking g = 9.8 m s⁻², find the weight of the parcel. **[2]**

**4.** Express the newton in terms of the SI units of mass, length and time. Hence show that a mass in kilograms multiplied by g in m s⁻² gives a quantity measured in newtons. **[3]**

**5.** A cyclist's speed increases steadily from 18 km h⁻¹ to 27 km h⁻¹ in 4 seconds. Calculate the cyclist's acceleration in m s⁻². **[3]**

**6.** A force of 60 N is applied at right angles to a spanner, 25 cm from the centre of a nut.

**(a)** Calculate the moment of the force about the centre of the nut, stating its units. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the force, applied at right angles 40 cm from the centre, that has the same moment. **[2]**

**7.** A model for the air resistance R newtons on a ball moving at v m s⁻¹ is R = kv², where k is a constant.

**(a)** Find the SI units of k, in terms of kg, m and s. **[2]**
**(b)** When v = 30, R = 54. Find k and use the model to find R when v = 20. **[2]**

**8.** A student writes the formula v = u + as for motion with constant acceleration. Use units to show that this formula cannot be correct. **[3]**

**9.** A space probe has mass 120 kg.

**(a)** Find its weight where g = 9.8 m s⁻². **[1]**
**(b)** Find its weight at a location where g = 1.6 m s⁻². **[1]**
**(c)** State the mass of the probe at the second location, and explain your answer. **[2]**

**10.** A lorry of mass 7.5 tonnes (1 tonne = 1000 kg) speeds up with constant acceleration from 36 km h⁻¹ to 63 km h⁻¹ in 15 seconds.

**(a)** Convert both speeds to m s⁻¹. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the acceleration of the lorry. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the resultant force on the lorry, giving your answer in kN. **[2]**
**(d)** Taking g = 9.8 m s⁻², find the weight of the lorry in kN. **[2]**

**11.** A crate of mass 40 kg is raised vertically by a cable. The tension in the cable is 0.42 kN. Air resistance is ignored.

**(a)** Taking g = 9.8 m s⁻², find the weight of the crate. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the resultant force on the crate and hence its acceleration. **[3]**
**(c)** The crate starts from rest and the acceleration stays constant. Find its speed after 5 seconds, giving your answer in km h⁻¹. **[2]**
**(d)** Repeat part (b) using g = 9.81 m s⁻², and comment on the effect of the value of g on your answer. **[2]**

**12.** A sign of mass 2.5 kg hangs from the end of a light horizontal rod fixed to a vertical wall. The sign hangs 80 cm from the wall. Take g = 9.8 m s⁻².

**(a)** Find the weight of the sign. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the moment of the weight of the sign about the point where the rod meets the wall. **[2]**
**(c)** The fixing can withstand a moment of at most 30 N m. The sign is replaced by a heavier sign of mass m kg hanging at the same point. Find the greatest possible value of m. **[3]**
**(d)** Express the newton metre in terms of kg, m and s. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** acceleration: **metres per second per second, m s⁻²** [1]
force: **newton, N** [1]
moment: **newton metre, N m** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* in this answer the unit name or the symbol earns the mark, but a wrong power (m s⁻¹ for acceleration) or a missing distance unit for moment (N only) earns nothing for that item.

**2.** 108 × 1000 ÷ 3600, or 108 ÷ 3.6 [1]
= **30 m s⁻¹** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* the method mark needs a correct conversion factor; multiplying by 3.6 (388.8) loses both marks.

**3.** mass = 750 ÷ 1000 = 0.75 kg [1]
W = mg = 0.75 × 9.8 = **7.35 N** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* using m = 750 gives 7350, which earns nothing; the conversion to kilograms is where the method mark sits.

**4.** From F = ma, 1 N = 1 kg × 1 m s⁻² [1]
so **1 N = 1 kg m s⁻²** [1]
mg has units kg × m s⁻² = kg m s⁻², which is N, as required [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* in a "show that" you must write the final link (kg m s⁻² = N) explicitly; stopping at kg m s⁻² leaves the last mark unearned.

**5.** 18 km h⁻¹ = 5 m s⁻¹ and 27 km h⁻¹ = 7.5 m s⁻¹ [1]
a = (7.5 − 5) ÷ 4 [1]
= **0.625 m s⁻²** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* working in km h⁻¹ throughout (9 ÷ 4 = 2.25) gives the wrong units altogether; convert both speeds first.

**6. (a)** 25 cm = 0.25 m, moment = 60 × 0.25 [1]
= **15 N m** [1]
**(b)** F × 0.4 = 15 [1]
F = **37.5 N** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* in (b) this scheme allows follow-through from your (a), but the answer must be a force in N, not a moment.

**7. (a)** k = R ÷ v², units N ÷ (m s⁻¹)² = kg m s⁻² ÷ m² s⁻² [1]
= **kg m⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** k = 54 ÷ 30² = 0.06 [1]
R = 0.06 × 20² = **24 N** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* in (a) writing N in base units is the key step; an answer of "N m⁻² s²" is equivalent but must be simplified if the question asks for kg, m and s.

**8.** v has units m s⁻¹ and u has units m s⁻¹ [1]
as has units m s⁻² × m = m² s⁻² [1]
m² s⁻² is not m s⁻¹, so the terms cannot be added and the formula **cannot be correct** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* the units of each term must be shown; a bare statement that "the units don't match", with no working, does not earn the method marks.

**9. (a)** W = 120 × 9.8 = **1176 N** (1180 N to 3 s.f.) [1]
**(b)** W = 120 × 1.6 = **192 N** [1]
**(c)** **120 kg** [1]
Mass is the amount of matter in the probe and does not depend on g; only the weight changes [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* the explanation mark needs the idea that mass is independent of location or of g; "it is the same probe" alone is too vague.

**10. (a)** 36 ÷ 3.6 = **10 m s⁻¹** [1]
63 ÷ 3.6 = **17.5 m s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** a = (17.5 − 10) ÷ 15 [1]
= **0.5 m s⁻²** [1]
**(c)** F = ma = 7500 × 0.5 = 3750 N [1]
= **3.75 kN** [1]
**(d)** W = 7500 × 9.8 = 73 500 N [1]
= **73.5 kN** [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* using m = 7.5 gives 3.75 N in (c), a factor of 1000 out; follow-through applies to a wrong acceleration from (b), but not to an unconverted mass.

**11. (a)** W = 40 × 9.8 = **392 N** [1]
**(b)** tension = 0.42 kN = 420 N [1]
resultant = 420 − 392 = 28 N upwards [1]
a = 28 ÷ 40 = **0.7 m s⁻²** upwards [1]
**(c)** v = 0 + 0.7 × 5 = 3.5 m s⁻¹ [1]
3.5 × 3.6 = **12.6 km h⁻¹** [1]
**(d)** W = 392.4 N, resultant = 27.6 N, a = 27.6 ÷ 40 = **0.69 m s⁻²** [1]
The acceleration depends on the value of g used, so the answer is only as accurate as g; a small change in g makes a noticeable change here because the resultant is the difference of two close forces [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* in (b) the kN must be converted before subtracting; 0.42 − 392 is a sign of an unconverted unit and earns no accuracy marks.

**12. (a)** W = 2.5 × 9.8 = **24.5 N** [1]
**(b)** 80 cm = 0.8 m, moment = 24.5 × 0.8 [1]
= **19.6 N m** [1]
**(c)** m × 9.8 × 0.8 ≤ 30 [1]
m ≤ 30 ÷ 7.84 = 3.8265... [1]
greatest m = **3.83 kg** (3 s.f.) [1]
**(d)** N m = kg m s⁻² × m = **kg m² s⁻²** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* in (c) the method mark is for forming the inequality (or the equation at the limit) with the distance in metres; using 80 for the distance gives 0.0383 kg and loses the accuracy marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving grams, centimetres, kN or km h⁻¹ unconverted inside F = ma, W = mg or a moment.
- Multiplying by 3.6 instead of dividing when converting to m s⁻¹.
- Giving the weight in kg or the mass in N.
- Writing m s⁻¹ for acceleration, or N for a moment.
- Using a different value of g from the one the question gives.
- Rounding a converted value early and carrying it forward.
- Stopping a "show that" one line short of the stated result.
- Missing units, or the direction, on a final force or acceleration.
