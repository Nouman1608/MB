## 2.34 Respiration produces ATP

- **Respiration**: chemical reactions in living cells that break down glucose to **release energy**, which is used to make ATP.
- Happens in **every living cell**, in plants, animals and microorganisms, day and night.
- Controlled by **enzymes**, so affected by temperature and pH.
- Respiration ≠ breathing. Breathing moves air; respiration is a chemical process in cells.
- Energy is released, never "made". Some is lost as heat.

## 2.35 ATP provides energy for cells

- ATP = **adenosine triphosphate**.
- Glucose → (respiration) → energy stored in ATP → used by the cell.
- Uses of energy from ATP:
  - muscle contraction
  - active transport
  - building large molecules (e.g. proteins from amino acids)
  - cell division and growth
  - nerve impulses
  - keeping body temperature constant in mammals and birds (heat from respiration).
- High-energy cells (muscle, sperm) have **many mitochondria**.

## 2.37–2.38 The three equations

| Type | Word equation | Symbol equation needed? |
|---|---|---|
| Aerobic (all organisms) | glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water | Yes |
| Anaerobic in animals | glucose → lactic acid | No |
| Anaerobic in plants (and yeast) | glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide | No |

Aerobic symbol equation:

```
C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O
```

**Method in steps: checking it is balanced**

```
1. Count C, H and O on the left (include the 6 O inside glucose).
2. Count C, H and O on the right (multiply by the big number in front).
3. C: 6 = 6   H: 12 = 12   O: 18 = 18
```

## 2.36 Aerobic vs anaerobic

| | Aerobic | Anaerobic |
|---|---|---|
| Oxygen | Needed | Not needed |
| Glucose broken down | Completely | Partly |
| Energy / ATP per glucose | Much more | Much less |
| Products | CO₂ + water | Lactic acid (animals); ethanol + CO₂ (plants, yeast) |
| Site | Mitochondria | Cytoplasm |

**Must-know distinctions**

- **Lactic acid vs ethanol**: animals make lactic acid only; plants and yeast make ethanol **and** carbon dioxide.
- **Less energy vs no energy**: anaerobic releases less energy, not none. Energy is left in lactic acid or ethanol.
- **Respiration vs photosynthesis**: the aerobic equation looks like photosynthesis reversed, but respiration happens in all cells all the time, while photosynthesis needs light and chloroplasts.
- **When anaerobic respiration happens**: human muscle during vigorous exercise when oxygen cannot be supplied fast enough; plant roots in waterlogged soil; yeast in a sealed container.

**Worked reminder: a 3-mark "describe the differences" answer**

Each point names both types and uses a linking word such as "but" or "whereas":

1. Aerobic respiration needs oxygen, **but** anaerobic respiration does not.
2. Aerobic respiration releases much more energy from each glucose molecule **than** anaerobic respiration.
3. Aerobic respiration produces carbon dioxide and water, **whereas** anaerobic respiration in animals produces only lactic acid.

A list such as "uses oxygen, makes water, happens in mitochondria" describes aerobic respiration only and does not compare.

**Why anaerobic respiration releases less energy**

- Glucose is only partly broken down.
- Lactic acid and ethanol still contain a lot of chemical energy.
- Carbon dioxide and water, the products of aerobic respiration, contain very little.
- So less energy is transferred to ATP from each glucose molecule.

## 2.39 Practical: carbon dioxide and heat from respiring organisms

### Carbon dioxide

| Indicator | Starts | More CO₂ | Less CO₂ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hydrogen-carbonate | Red / orange | Yellow | Purple |
| Limewater | Clear | Milky / cloudy | -- |

**Method in steps**

```
1. Same volume of indicator in each tube.
2. Organisms on a gauze platform above the indicator.
3. Control: boiled (dead) seeds or glass beads of the same volume.
4. Seal with bungs; same temperature; dark for seeds.
5. Record colour (from ... to ...) or time to change colour.
```

### Heat

**Method in steps**

```
1. Soak peas so they germinate; boil a second batch to kill them (control).
2. Rinse both in disinfectant -- kills microbes that would respire and give heat.
3. Put each batch in a vacuum flask -- insulation keeps heat in.
4. Cotton wool plug -- lets oxygen in, keeps most heat in.
5. Read the thermometer daily for several days.
```

**Result**: germinating peas warm up; boiled peas stay at room temperature.

**Worked reminder (rate of gas release):** seeds release 2.4 cm³ of carbon dioxide in 8 minutes.

```
Rate = 2.4 ÷ 8 = 0.3 cm³ per minute
```

**Worked reminder (rate of temperature rise):**

```
Rate = (final temperature − starting temperature) ÷ time
```

**Worked reminder (explaining a result with a control):** a flask of germinating beans rises from 19.0 °C to 23.0 °C, while a flask of boiled beans stays at 19.0 °C.

- The germinating beans are alive and respire, releasing energy as heat.
- The vacuum flask stops this heat escaping, so the temperature rises by 4.0 °C.
- The boiled beans are dead, so they cannot respire and release no heat.
- The boiled beans show the rise is caused by respiration of living cells, not by the surroundings.

### Command words for this practical

| Command word | What earns the marks |
|---|---|
| Describe (a method) | Numbered, practical steps, with named equipment and quantities |
| Explain (a result) | What happened **and** why, linked to respiration |
| Suggest (a reason) | A sensible reason applied to the set-up in the question |
| Calculate | Working shown, final answer with a unit |

### Variables to name

- Independent: living vs dead seeds (or temperature, or seed type).
- Dependent: temperature rise, or time for indicator to change colour.
- Control: mass and type of seeds, volume of indicator, starting temperature, flask size.
- Repeat and take a mean for reliability.

## Quick self-test

1. What does ATP stand for?
2. Name the organelle where most aerobic respiration takes place.
3. Give the word equation for aerobic respiration.
4. Give the balanced symbol equation for aerobic respiration.
5. Give the word equation for anaerobic respiration in human muscle.
6. Give the word equation for anaerobic respiration in plant root cells.
7. State two ways anaerobic respiration differs from aerobic respiration.
8. State three uses of the energy from ATP in cells.
9. Hydrogen-carbonate indicator over respiring woodlice changes colour. From what colour, to what colour?
10. Why are seeds rinsed in disinfectant before the vacuum flask experiment?
11. Germinating seeds rise by 9.0 °C over 6 days. Calculate the mean rate of temperature rise.
12. The rate of carbon dioxide release from some seeds rises from 0.25 to 0.40 cm³ per minute when they are warmed. Calculate the percentage increase.

### Answers

1. Adenosine triphosphate.
2. Mitochondrion (mitochondria).
3. glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water.
4. C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O.
5. glucose → lactic acid.
6. glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide.
7. Any two: anaerobic needs no oxygen; releases less energy (less ATP) per glucose; glucose only partly broken down; different products (lactic acid, or ethanol and CO₂, instead of CO₂ and water); happens in the cytoplasm, not mainly in mitochondria.
8. Any three: muscle contraction; active transport; building proteins (large molecules); cell division/growth; nerve impulses; keeping body temperature constant.
9. From red (orange) to yellow.
10. To kill microorganisms on the seed surface, which would respire and release heat, so any temperature rise is due to the seeds alone.
11. 9.0 ÷ 6 = **1.5 °C per day**.
12. (0.40 − 0.25) ÷ 0.25 × 100 = **60%**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- "Respiration produces energy" instead of "releases energy" or "produces ATP".
- Putting carbon dioxide in the animal anaerobic equation, or leaving it out of the plant/yeast one.
- Writing "oxygen" as O, or "water" as H₂O₂, in the symbol equation.
- Stating that anaerobic respiration releases no energy.
- Confusing respiration with breathing or with gas exchange.
- Describing a colour change with no starting colour.
- Not saying the boiled seeds are dead and so cannot respire (the reason they are a control).
- Missing the reason for disinfectant (microbes respire too) or for the vacuum flask (insulation).
- A rate with no unit, such as "1.5" instead of "1.5 °C per day".
