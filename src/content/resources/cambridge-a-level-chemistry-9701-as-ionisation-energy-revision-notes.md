## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| First ionisation energy | Energy to remove one electron from each atom in one mole of gaseous atoms, forming one mole of gaseous 1+ ions |
| Second ionisation energy | Energy to remove one electron from each ion in one mole of gaseous 1+ ions, forming gaseous 2+ ions |
| Successive ionisation energies | The 1st, 2nd, 3rd and later energies for one element, in order |
| Nuclear charge | Number of protons |
| Shielding | Reduction in attraction for an outer electron caused by inner electrons |
| Spin-pair repulsion | Repulsion between two electrons sharing one orbital |

## Equations

    first:   X(g)  → X⁺(g)  + e⁻
    second:  X⁺(g) → X²⁺(g) + e⁻
    nth:     X⁽ⁿ⁻¹⁾⁺(g) → Xⁿ⁺(g) + e⁻

- One electron per equation; gaseous species; charge up by one.
- X(g) → X²⁺(g) + 2e⁻ equals first + second, not the second alone.

## The four factors

| Factor | Higher IE when… |
|---|---|
| Nuclear charge | more protons |
| Radius | electron closer to nucleus |
| Shielding | fewer inner electrons between nucleus and electron |
| Spin-pair repulsion | electron is unpaired |

## Period 3 first ionisation energies (kJ mol⁻¹, rounded)

| Na | Mg | Al | Si | P | S | Cl | Ar |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 496 | 738 | 578 | 787 | 1012 | 1000 | 1251 | 1521 |

- **Rise:** nuclear charge up; same shell; shielding roughly constant; radius smaller.
- **Mg → Al dip:** Al loses a 3p electron: higher energy, further out, shielded by 3s².
- **P → S dip:** S loses a paired 3p electron; spin-pair repulsion.
- **Down a group:** Li 520, Na 496, K 419; extra shell, more distance and shielding outweigh nuclear charge.

## Second ionisation energies across Period 3 (kJ mol⁻¹)

| Na | Mg | Al | Si | P | S | Cl | Ar |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4562 | 1451 | 1817 | 1577 | 1907 | 2252 | 2298 | 2666 |

- Pattern of first IEs shifted one element right.
- Na highest: Na⁺ loses a 2p electron (inner shell).
- Al > Si: Al⁺ loses 3s; Si⁺ loses 3p.
- Small rise S → Cl: Cl⁺ (3p⁴) loses a paired electron.

## Successive ionisation energies

- Always increase: each electron leaves an increasingly positive, smaller ion.
- Large jump: next electron is from an inner shell.
- Smaller step within a shell: change of sub-shell (e.g. 3p to 3s).

**Method: group from data**
1. Calculate ratios (or differences) of consecutive values.
2. Find the largest jump: after the nth value means n outer electrons.
3. s and p block: n outer electrons means Group n (1 or 2) or Group n + 10 (13 to 18).
4. Use the period (or number of electrons) to name the element.

**Method: configuration from a full set**
1. Mark every large jump.
2. Count the values between jumps, starting from the last value (innermost shell).
3. Those counts are the shell occupancies, e.g. 2, 8, 3 for aluminium.

| Element | Successive IEs (kJ mol⁻¹) | Big jump after | Group |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mg | 738, 1451, 7733 | 2nd | 2 |
| Al | 578, 1817, 2745, 11 577 | 3rd | 13 |
| P | 1012, 1907, 2914, 4964, 6274, 21 267 | 5th | 15 |

## Exam traps

- Second IE equation with 2e⁻.
- No state symbols, or (s) instead of (g).
- "More shielding across a period": shielding is roughly constant.
- Using spin-pair repulsion for Mg/Al: it explains P/S.
- Misplacing the jump: after the 2nd value means Group 2.
- Forgetting that a group number in the p block is the outer electron count plus 10.

## Self-test

**1.** Define first ionisation energy.

**2.** Write the equation for the third ionisation energy of aluminium.

**3.** Why is the second ionisation energy of any element larger than its first?

**4.** Explain why the first IE of argon is higher than that of chlorine.

**5.** Explain why the first IE of aluminium is lower than that of magnesium.

**6.** Explain why the first IE of sulfur is lower than that of phosphorus.

**7.** Explain why the first IE of potassium is lower than that of sodium.

**8.** Why does sodium have the highest second IE in Period 3?

**9.** An element has successive IEs of 800, 2427, 3660, 25 026 kJ mol⁻¹. Which group is it in?

**10.** The element in Question 9 is in Period 2. Name it and give its configuration.

**11.** What does a small step between the 3rd and 4th values of phosphorus show?

**12.** An element has 2, 8, 8, 2 electrons per shell. Between which values do the large jumps in its successive IEs occur?

## Self-test answers

**1.** The energy needed to remove one electron from each atom in one mole of gaseous atoms to form one mole of gaseous 1+ ions.

**2.** Al²⁺(g) → Al³⁺(g) + e⁻.

**3.** The electron is removed from a positive ion, so the remaining electrons are attracted more strongly (and the ion is smaller).

**4.** Argon has a greater nuclear charge, with the electron in the same shell and similar shielding, so it is held more strongly.

**5.** Aluminium's electron is in 3p, higher in energy than 3s and shielded by the 3s electrons.

**6.** Sulfur's electron is removed from a doubly occupied 3p orbital; spin-pair repulsion makes it easier to remove.

**7.** Potassium's outer electron is in the fourth shell, further from the nucleus and more shielded, which outweighs the greater nuclear charge.

**8.** Its second electron is removed from the 2p sub-shell of Na⁺, much closer to the nucleus and less shielded.

**9.** Group 13 (large jump after the 3rd value).

**10.** Boron, 1s²2s²2p¹.

**11.** The 4th electron comes from 3s, a lower-energy sub-shell than 3p, so the data show sub-shells within the third shell.

**12.** Between the 2nd and 3rd, the 10th and 11th, and the 18th and 19th values (calcium).
