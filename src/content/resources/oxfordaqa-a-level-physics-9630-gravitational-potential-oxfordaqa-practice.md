These questions cover section **3.7.3 Gravitational potential** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630) specification: the definition of potential and potential difference, ΔW = mΔV, V = −GM/r, equipotential surfaces, graphs of g and V against r, and g = −ΔV/Δr. Section 3.7 is International A-level only. Marks are shown for each part. The planet in Questions 9 and 10 is invented for practice.

Use G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻², GM(Earth) = 3.98 × 10¹⁴ N m² kg⁻¹, Earth radius 6.37 × 10⁶ m, GM(Moon) = 4.90 × 10¹² N m² kg⁻¹, Moon radius 1.74 × 10⁶ m and Earth-Moon distance 3.84 × 10⁸ m.

Revise first with the [gravitational potential study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-gravitational-potential-oxfordaqa/) and the [gravitational potential revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-gravitational-potential-oxfordaqa-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Define gravitational potential at a point. **[2]**

**2.** What is the unit of gravitational potential?

A: J

B: J kg⁻¹

C: N kg⁻¹

D: N m² kg⁻² **[1]**

**3.** Calculate the gravitational potential at the surface of the Moon. **[2]**

**4.** A 50 kg mass is moved from a point where V = −3.0 × 10⁷ J kg⁻¹ to a point where V = −2.0 × 10⁷ J kg⁻¹. Calculate the work done on it. **[2]**

**5.** A satellite moves along an equipotential surface. The work done by the gravitational field on it is

A: positive

B: negative

C: zero

D: equal to its kinetic energy **[1]**

**6.** Explain why no work is done when a mass moves along an equipotential surface. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A 1200 kg satellite is moved from a circular path of radius 7.0 × 10⁶ m to one of radius 2.1 × 10⁷ m about the Earth's centre.

**(a)** Calculate the gravitational potential at each radius. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the work that must be done on the satellite against gravity. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the work cannot be found using mgh with g = 9.81 N kg⁻¹. **[1]**

**8.**

**(a)** Calculate the radii of the equipotential surfaces at −3.0 × 10⁷ J kg⁻¹ and −2.0 × 10⁷ J kg⁻¹ around the Earth. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the work done in moving a 10 kg mass halfway round the −3.0 × 10⁷ J kg⁻¹ surface. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why equipotential surfaces drawn at equal steps of potential are further apart at greater distances from the Earth. **[2]**

**9.** The table gives the gravitational potential at three distances from the centre of a planet.

| r / 10⁷ m | 1.0 | 1.5 | 2.0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| V / 10⁷ J kg⁻¹ | −2.00 | −1.33 | −1.00 |

**(a)** Show that the data are consistent with V ∝ 1/r. **[2]**

**(b)** Use the values at 1.0 × 10⁷ m and 2.0 × 10⁷ m to estimate the field strength at 1.5 × 10⁷ m. **[2]**

**(c)** The exact value at 1.5 × 10⁷ m is 0.89 N kg⁻¹. Explain why your estimate is larger. **[2]**

**10.** On a graph of g against r for the planet in Question 9, the area under the curve between r = 1.0 × 10⁷ m and r = 2.0 × 10⁷ m is found by counting squares to be 1.0 × 10⁷ J kg⁻¹.

**(a)** State what this area represents. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the energy needed to move a 500 kg probe between these radii. **[2]**

**11.** Calculate the total gravitational potential at the point midway between the centres of the Earth and the Moon. **[3]**

**12.** Explain why gravitational potential is negative near a planet and why it increases with distance from the planet. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The work done per unit mass [1] in moving a small mass from infinity to that point [1].

**2.** **B** [1]

**3.** V = −GM/r = −4.90 × 10¹²/1.74 × 10⁶ [1] = **−2.82 × 10⁶ J kg⁻¹** [1]

**4.** ΔV = −2.0 × 10⁷ − (−3.0 × 10⁷) = +1.0 × 10⁷ J kg⁻¹ [1]; ΔW = mΔV = 50 × 1.0 × 10⁷ = **5.0 × 10⁸ J** [1]

**5.** **C** [1]

**6.** Along an equipotential the potential is the same everywhere, so ΔV = 0 [1]; therefore ΔW = mΔV = 0 (the gravitational force is at right angles to the surface, so it has no component along the motion) [1].

**7.**

**(a)** V₁ = −3.98 × 10¹⁴/7.0 × 10⁶ = **−5.69 × 10⁷ J kg⁻¹** [1]; V₂ = −3.98 × 10¹⁴/2.1 × 10⁷ = **−1.90 × 10⁷ J kg⁻¹** [1]

**(b)** ΔV = −1.90 × 10⁷ − (−5.69 × 10⁷) = 3.79 × 10⁷ J kg⁻¹ [1]; ΔW = 1200 × 3.79 × 10⁷ = **4.6 × 10¹⁰ J** [1]

**(c)** g decreases greatly over this change in distance, so it cannot be treated as constant at 9.81 N kg⁻¹ (mgh would give a value more than three times too large) [1].

**8.**

**(a)** r = GM/|V|: r = 3.98 × 10¹⁴/3.0 × 10⁷ = **1.33 × 10⁷ m** [1]; r = 3.98 × 10¹⁴/2.0 × 10⁷ = **1.99 × 10⁷ m** [1]

**(b)** **Zero** [1]

**(c)** g = −ΔV/Δr, so Δr = ΔV/g for a fixed step ΔV [1]; g is smaller further from the Earth, so the distance between surfaces is larger [1].

**9.**

**(a)** V × r = −2.00 × 10¹⁴ for the first row [1]; the other rows give −1.33 × 10⁷ × 1.5 × 10⁷ = −2.0 × 10¹⁴ and −1.00 × 10⁷ × 2.0 × 10⁷ = −2.0 × 10¹⁴, so Vr is constant and V ∝ 1/r [1].

**(b)** |g| ≈ ΔV/Δr = (−1.00 − (−2.00)) × 10⁷/(2.0 − 1.0) × 10⁷ [1] = **1.0 N kg⁻¹** [1]

**(c)** The estimate is the gradient of a chord, not the tangent at 1.5 × 10⁷ m [1]; the V-r curve is steeper at smaller r, so a chord stretching back to 1.0 × 10⁷ m includes steeper parts of the curve and overestimates the gradient [1].

**10.**

**(a)** The gravitational potential difference between the two radii [1].

**(b)** ΔW = mΔV [1] = 500 × 1.0 × 10⁷ = **5.0 × 10⁹ J** [1]

**11.** Distance to each centre = 1.92 × 10⁸ m [1]; V = −3.98 × 10¹⁴/1.92 × 10⁸ − 4.90 × 10¹²/1.92 × 10⁸ [1] = **−2.1 × 10⁶ J kg⁻¹** [1]

**12.** Potential is defined as zero at infinity [1]. Gravity is attractive, so work has to be done to move a mass away from the planet towards infinity, which means the potential at any finite distance is less than zero [1]. Moving outwards, less work per kilogram is still needed to reach infinity, so V becomes less negative, rising towards zero [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out "per unit mass" or "from infinity" in the definition (Question 1).
- Getting the sign of ΔV wrong by subtracting the wrong way round (Questions 4 and 7).
- Using mgh for a large change of radius (Question 7).
- Treating potentials from two bodies as vectors and subtracting them (Question 11).
- Confusing a chord gradient with a tangent gradient (Question 9).
- Saying the area under a g-r graph is the work done, without "per unit mass" (Question 10).
