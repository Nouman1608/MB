These questions cover section 1.6 (Multinational companies (MNCs)) of the IB *Business management guide*, first assessment 2024, for SL and HL: the impact of MNCs on the host countries. All companies, countries and figures are invented, and money is in dollars. Revise first with the [1.6 study guide](/resources/ib-dp-business-1-6/) and the [1.6 revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-business-1-6-revision-notes/).

## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** Define the term *multinational company (MNC)*. **[2]**

**2.** Explain one way an MNC may create jobs in a host country beyond its own workforce. **[2]**

**3.** Ravenna Phones, an MNC, makes a profit of 45m in its host country. The profit is taxed at 18%, and 70% of after-tax profit is sent to the home country.

**(a)** Calculate the amount sent to the home country. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the after-tax profit reinvested in the host country. **[1]**

**4.** Distinguish between an MNC's home country and its host country. **[2]**

**5.** Explain one way an MNC could harm local competitors in a host country. **[2]**

**6.** State two ways a host government could increase the benefits it receives from an MNC. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** Quill Coffee, an MNC, opens 15 cafés in the city of Lisoria, creating 180 jobs. Within two years, 20 independent cafés close, with the loss of 110 jobs. Quill buys 40% of its coffee beans from Lisorian farms.

**(a)** Calculate the net change in jobs. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain two impacts of Quill Coffee on stakeholders in Lisoria other than workers. **[4]**

**8.** Sorrel Textiles, an MNC, operates a factory in Bandaria. Each year it employs 3,500 workers at an average wage of 2,400, buys 6.0m of fabric from Bandarian suppliers, and makes a profit of 12m. The Bandarian government has given it a five-year tax holiday, so it pays no tax on profit. It sends 90% of its profit to its home country.

**(a)** Calculate the amount that stays in Bandaria each year during the tax holiday. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the amount sent abroad each year. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the impact on Bandaria might change after five years. **[3]**

**9.** Explain how an MNC's training of local staff could benefit the host country in the long run. **[3]**

**10.** Torrence Mining, an MNC, plans a lithium mine in the district of Ostvale in Valmora. The mine would employ 1,200 local people, mostly in unskilled roles, and pay the government a royalty of 8% of revenue, expected to be 200m a year. All machinery would be imported. The mine would use 40% of the district's water supply. The contract runs for 15 years. Evaluate the likely impact of the mine on Valmora. **[8]**

**11.** Explain two reasons why a small, low-income host country may gain less from an MNC than a large, high-income one. **[4]**

**12.** Explain how a STEEPLE analysis could help a host government assess a proposed MNC investment. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** A business that has operations or production facilities [1] in more than one country [1]. **[2]**

**2.** By buying inputs from local suppliers (supplier linkages) [1], the MNC increases those suppliers' sales, so they hire more staff [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** After-tax profit = 45m × (1 − 0.18) = 36.9m [1]. Sent home = 36.9m × 70% = **25.83m** [1]

**(b)** 36.9m − 25.83m = **11.07m** [1]

**4.** The home country is where the MNC is based and has its headquarters [1]; a host country is another country in which it operates [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Its lower costs from economies of scale let it charge lower prices [1], so local firms lose customers and may close (crowding out) [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any two, one mark each: require a share of inputs to be bought locally; set and enforce environmental standards; require training of local staff for skilled roles; tax profits; limit incentives to a fixed period [1][1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** 180 − 110 = **+70 jobs** [1]

**(b)** Consumers: they gain a consistent product and possibly lower prices [1], but have less variety now that 20 independent cafés have closed [1]. Local coffee farmers: Quill's purchases of 40% of its beans locally give them a large, steady customer [1], although they may depend on one buyer that could switch to imports [1]. (Former café owners or the government also earn credit.) **[4]**

**8. (a)** Wages = 3,500 × 2,400 = 8.4m [1]. Reinvested profit = 12m × 10% = 1.2m [1]. Staying in Bandaria = 8.4 + 6.0 + 1.2 = **15.6m** a year [1]

**(b)** 12m × 90% = **10.8m** [1]

**(c)** When the tax holiday ends, the government begins to receive tax on the 12m profit, so more money stays in Bandaria [1]. However, Sorrel may be footloose [1]: if its costs rise once tax is due, it may move to another country offering incentives, and the 3,500 jobs and 6.0m of local purchases would be lost [1]. **[3]**

**9.** Workers gain skills such as machine operation or management [1]. Some later move to local firms or start their own businesses [1], so the skills spread through the economy, raising productivity even if the MNC leaves [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Award up to 8 marks for a balanced evaluation that uses the case and reaches a judgement. Credit points such as:

- The royalty brings 8% × 200m = 16m a year to the government, or 240m over 15 years, which could fund public services [1].
- 1,200 jobs is a large gain for one district [1], but mostly unskilled roles limit the spread of skills [1].
- All machinery is imported, so supplier linkages and technology transfer are weak [1].
- Using 40% of the district's water could harm farming and households, a serious environmental and social cost [1].
- After 15 years the jobs may end, leaving the district dependent on a mine that has closed [1].
- The impact depends on the government's ability to enforce water and environmental rules and on how it spends the royalty [1].
- Judgement: financially positive for Valmora as a whole, but risky for Ostvale itself; the impact is positive only if water use is controlled and the royalty is invested in skills and local supply [1]. **[8]**

*Examiner insight:* top answers separate the national gain (royalty) from the local cost (water), use the figures and give a conditional judgement. A two-column list of benefits and costs without weighing stays in the lower bands.

**11.** Weaker bargaining power: a small country with few alternative investors may accept low tax and weak conditions to attract the MNC [1], so less of the MNC's profit stays in the country [1]. Fewer local suppliers with the right skills or quality [1], so the MNC imports more inputs and supplier linkages are weaker [1]. (Weaker enforcement of laws also earns credit.) **[4]**

**12.** STEEPLE lists the social, technological, economic, environmental, political, legal and ethical factors [1], so the government checks the investment's effects in every category, not only jobs and tax [1]. For example, it would flag environmental costs such as water use alongside economic gains, giving a more balanced decision [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculating repatriated profit from profit before tax.
- Counting jobs created without the jobs lost in local firms.
- Listing impacts without naming who gains and who loses.
- Ignoring how long the MNC is likely to stay.
- Writing about the home country when the question asks about the host country.
- Ending an "evaluate" answer without a judgement and a condition.
