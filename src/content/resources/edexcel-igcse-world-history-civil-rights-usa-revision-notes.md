## Protest and the law: what caused what?

| Protest | Federal response |
|---|---|
| NAACP legal campaign | Brown v Board (1954) |
| Montgomery Bus Boycott | Supreme Court bans bus segregation (1956) |
| Little Rock crisis | Federal troops; Civil Rights Act 1957 |
| Freedom Rides | Interstate travel desegregation enforced (1961) |
| Birmingham and the March on Washington | Civil Rights Act 1964 |
| Freedom Summer and Selma | Voting Rights Act 1965 |

## King and Malcolm X compared

| | Martin Luther King | Malcolm X |
|---|---|---|
| Aim | Integration and equal rights | Black pride; separation (until 1964) |
| Method | Non-violent direct action | Self-defence "by any means necessary" |
| Base | Southern churches, SCLC | Northern cities, Nation of Islam |
| Strength | Won national sympathy and laws | Spoke to urban anger; inspired Black Power |

## Worked example: two effects

*Explain two effects of the Birmingham Campaign (1963).*

1. **National outrage:** television pictures of police dogs and hoses used on children shocked
   Americans and the world, turning opinion further against segregation.
2. **Federal action:** Kennedy announced a civil rights bill in June 1963, which became the Civil
   Rights Act 1964 under Johnson.

## Essay plan in outline

*"Federal government action was the main reason for civil rights progress in the years 1954–65."
How far do you agree?*

- **Paragraph 1 (stated factor):** the Supreme Court in Brown (1954); Eisenhower's troops at Little
  Rock (1957); Kennedy's civil rights bill; Johnson's Civil Rights Act (1964) and Voting Rights Act
  (1965).
- **Paragraph 2 (protest):** Montgomery, sit-ins, Freedom Rides, Birmingham and Selma forced
  reluctant presidents to act.
- **Paragraph 3 (media and opponents):** television coverage of violence by Connor and others
  turned public opinion.
- **Conclusion:** federal action made change law; protest made federal action happen.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- **Treating 1954 as the end of segregation.** Brown set no deadline; resistance continued for years.
- **Confusing the 1957, 1964 and 1965 Acts.** 1957 = Commission, weak; 1964 = public places and
  employment; 1965 = voting.
- **Describing King only.** Questions often ask you to weigh King against others.
- **Ignoring the North.** Watts and the Black Panthers show problems outside the South.
- **Forgetting topic 5.** Watergate and its laws are examinable; learn the four Acts.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why did McCarthy fall?** Television exposed his bullying in the Army–McCarthy hearings, and the
Senate censured him in 1954.

**Why did Black Power grow after 1965?** Slow change in Northern cities, poverty, police violence, the
assassination of Malcolm X and frustration with non-violence.

**What did Watergate change?** It forced Nixon's resignation and led Congress to limit presidential
power and protect privacy.
