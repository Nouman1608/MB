These notes condense section **3.3.12.1 Condensation polymers** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 2 and Paper 3. Explanations and worked examples are in the [Condensation polymers study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-condensation-polymers/), and exam-style questions are in the [Condensation polymers practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-condensation-polymers-practice/). The next sub-topic is in the [Biodegradability and disposal of polymers revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-biodegradability-and-disposal-of-polymers-revision-notes/).

## What you must be able to do

- State the monomers for **polyesters and polyamides** (diacid + diol, diacid + diamine) and **amino acids**.
- Know the repeating units of **Terylene, nylon 6,6 and Kevlar** and the links.
- State **typical uses**.
- Draw the **repeating unit** from monomers and from a chain section, and the **monomers** from a chain section.
- Explain the **intermolecular forces** between chains.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Condensation polymerisation | Monomers join and a small molecule (usually water) is lost |
| Dicarboxylic acid | Molecule with a –COOH group at each end |
| Diol | Molecule with an –OH group at each end |
| Diamine | Molecule with an –NH₂ group at each end |
| Ester link | –CO–O– |
| Amide (peptide) link | –CO–NH– |
| Repeating unit | The section that repeats along the chain |

## Types of condensation polymer

| Polymer | Monomers | Link | Lost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Polyester (Terylene) | Benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid + ethane-1,2-diol | Ester | H₂O |
| Polyamide (nylon 6,6) | Hexanedioic acid + 1,6-diaminohexane | Amide | H₂O |
| Polyamide (Kevlar) | Benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid + benzene-1,4-diamine | Amide | H₂O |
| Polypeptide | Amino acids | Peptide (amide) | H₂O |

## Repeating units

```
Terylene:  –[CO–C₆H₄–CO–O–CH₂CH₂–O]ₙ–
Nylon 6,6: –[CO–(CH₂)₄–CO–NH–(CH₂)₆–NH]ₙ–
Kevlar:    –[CO–C₆H₄–CO–NH–C₆H₄–NH]ₙ–
Glycine:   –[NH–CH₂–CO]ₙ–
```

Each repeating unit of a two-monomer polymer contains **one unit of each monomer**.

## Drawing skills

| Task | Steps |
|---|---|
| Monomers to repeating unit | Remove –OH from the acid and H from the –OH or –NH₂; join with the link; bracket with n |
| Chain section to repeating unit | Find the pattern that holds one of each monomer; start and end at the same point |
| Chain section to monomers | Break each ester or amide link; add –OH to C=O to give –COOH; add H to O or N |

## Intermolecular forces

| Polymer | Main force between chains |
|---|---|
| Polyester | Permanent dipole–dipole (and London forces) |
| Polyamide | Hydrogen bonds between N–H and C=O (and London forces) |
| Kevlar | Hydrogen bonds; rigid straight chains pack closely |

## Uses

| Polymer | Uses |
|---|---|
| Terylene | Clothing fibres, plastic bottles |
| Nylon 6,6 | Ropes, fabrics, carpets, mouldings |
| Kevlar | Bulletproof vests, protective clothing, reinforcing tyres and ropes |

## Addition against condensation

| | Addition | Condensation |
|---|---|---|
| Monomer | Alkene | Two functional groups each |
| By-product | None | H₂O (usually) |
| Atom economy | 100 % | Below 100 % |
| Hydrolysis | Not hydrolysed | Hydrolysed (ester, amide links) |

## Worked examples to copy

**Example 1: monomers to repeating unit (polyester).** Monomers: HOOC–(CH₂)₂–COOH and HO–(CH₂)₂–OH.

1. The –COOH end of the acid reacts with the –OH end of the diol, losing H₂O.
2. The link formed is –CO–O–.
3. Repeating unit: –[CO–(CH₂)₂–CO–O–(CH₂)₂–O]ₙ–.

**Example 2: chain section to monomers (polyamide).** Chain: –NH–(CH₂)₆–NH–CO–(CH₂)₄–CO–NH–(CH₂)₆–NH–CO–.

1. The links are –CO–NH–, so this is a polyamide.
2. Break each link between C and N.
3. Add –OH to each C=O end and –H to each N end.
4. Monomers: H₂N–(CH₂)₆–NH₂ and HOOC–(CH₂)₄–COOH, which are those of nylon 6,6.

## Why the forces matter

| Force | Strength | Where found |
|---|---|---|
| London forces | Weakest | All polymers |
| Permanent dipole–dipole | Medium | Polyesters, polar polyalkenes such as PVC |
| Hydrogen bonding | Strongest of the three | Polyamides, proteins |

The stronger the forces between chains, the higher the melting point and the stronger and less flexible the fibre.

## Calculations

- n(water lost) = 2 × n(repeating units) for diacid + diol or diamine.
- Mᵣ of repeating unit = Mᵣ(diacid) + Mᵣ(diol or diamine) − 2 × 18.0.
- Atom economy = Mᵣ of repeating unit ÷ (sum of monomer Mᵣ) × 100.

Example: nylon 6,6 repeating unit Mᵣ = 146.0 + 116.0 − 36.0 = 226.0. A chain of Mᵣ 22 600 contains 22 600 ÷ 226.0 = 100 repeating units.

## Checklist for an exam answer

1. Name the link (ester or amide) and say that water is lost.
2. Draw the link in full, including C=O.
3. Check that each monomer has two functional groups.
4. For forces, name the groups responsible: N–H and C=O for hydrogen bonds.

## Command words

| Command word | What to write |
|---|---|
| Draw | The structure, with the link in full and extended bonds for a repeating unit |
| Name | The polymer or the monomers using the correct ending |
| Explain | The force between chains, naming the groups responsible |
| Deduce | The monomers from a chain section by breaking each link |

## Exam traps

- Half a link at the end of the bracket.
- Forgetting the C=O in the link.
- A monomer with only one functional group.
- Water not shown as the product.
- Nylon called a polyester.
- Hydrogen bonding claimed for polyesters.
- A C=C in the repeating unit.
- The monomers wrongly assigned after breaking the link (H to the wrong atom).

## Self-test

1. What is lost in a condensation polymerisation?
2. Name the monomers of nylon 6,6.
3. Name the link in a polyester.
4. Name the link in a polyamide.
5. What are the monomers of Terylene?
6. How many monomer types does a polypeptide from one amino acid need?
7. Name the main force between nylon chains.
8. Why does Kevlar form strong fibres?
9. State one use of Terylene.
10. Break this link into monomers: –CO–O–.
11. What is the Mᵣ of the repeating unit of nylon 6,6?
12. Why is the atom economy of condensation polymerisation below 100 %?

### Answers

1. A small molecule, usually water.
2. Hexanedioic acid and 1,6-diaminohexane.
3. Ester, –CO–O–.
4. Amide, –CO–NH–.
5. Benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid and ethane-1,2-diol.
6. One: the amino acid has both an –NH₂ and a –COOH group.
7. Hydrogen bonding between N–H and C=O groups.
8. Rigid straight chains pack closely with many hydrogen bonds between chains.
9. Clothing fibres or plastic bottles.
10. A carboxylic acid (–COOH) and an alcohol (–OH).
11. 226.0.
12. A second product, water, is formed.
