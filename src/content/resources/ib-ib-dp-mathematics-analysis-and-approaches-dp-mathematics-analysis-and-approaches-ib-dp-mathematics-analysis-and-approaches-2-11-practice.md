## Section A

**1.** The point A(3, −4) lies on the graph of y = f(x). Write down the coordinates of the image of A on each graph.

**(a)** y = f(x − 2) **[1]**
**(b)** y = f(x) − 2 **[1]**
**(c)** y = f(−x) **[1]**
**(d)** y = 4f(x) **[1]**

**2.** Describe fully the single transformation that maps the graph of y = f(x) onto each graph.

**(a)** y = f(x/3) **[1]**
**(b)** y = −f(x) **[1]**
**(c)** y = f(x + 1) − 4 **[2]**

**3.** Let f(x) = x² − 6x + 10.

**(a)** Write f(x) in the form (x − h)² + k. **[2]**
**(b)** Hence describe fully the single transformation that maps the graph of y = x² onto the graph of y = f(x). **[2]**

**4.** The function f has domain −1 ≤ x ≤ 5 and range −2 ≤ f(x) ≤ 7. Let g(x) = 3 − f(2x).

**(a)** Find the domain of g. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the range of g. **[2]**

**5.** The graph of y = f(x) has a vertical asymptote x = −2 and a horizontal asymptote y = 3. Write down the equations of both asymptotes of each graph.

**(a)** y = f(−x) **[2]**
**(b)** y = 5 − f(x) **[2]**
**(c)** y = 3f(x/2) **[2]**

**6.** Let f(x) = x² − x − 6.

**(a)** Find the points on the graph of y = f(x) that are invariant under the transformation to y = 2f(x). **[2]**
**(b)** Write down the point on the graph of y = f(x) that is invariant under the transformation to y = f(3x). **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Let f(x) = x² + 4x + 1. The graph of y = g(x) is obtained from the graph of y = f(x) by a translation by the vector (3, 0), then a vertical stretch with scale factor 2, then a reflection in the x-axis.

**(a)** Find g(x), giving your answer in the form ax² + bx + c. **[4]**
**(b)** The vertex of the graph of f is (−2, −3). Find the coordinates of the vertex of the graph of g. **[2]**
**(c)** Write down the range of g. **[1]**

**8.** The graph of y = x³ is transformed onto the graph of y = 2 − ½(x + 3)³.

**(a)** Describe a sequence of transformations that does this. **[4]**
**(b)** Write down the image of the point (0, 0). **[1]**
**(c)** Find the exact x-intercept of the transformed graph. **[2]**

**9.** The graph of y = f(x) passes through P(−1, 4), Q(0, 3) and R(2, 0), and has a horizontal asymptote y = 1. Let g(x) = 2f(−x) − 1.

**(a)** Find the images of P, Q and R on the graph of y = g(x). **[3]**
**(b)** Find the equation of the horizontal asymptote of the graph of g. **[1]**
**(c)** Show that the graph of g meets the y-axis at (0, 5). **[1]**

**10.** Two students transform the graph of y = x². Student A reflects it in the x-axis and then translates it 4 units up. Student B translates it 4 units up and then reflects it in the x-axis.

**(a)** Write down the equation of each student's final graph. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the x-intercepts of student A's graph, and explain why student B's graph has none. **[3]**

**11.** Let f(x) = x² − 2x. The graph of y = f(x) is mapped onto the graph of y = x² + 6x + 5 by a single translation.

**(a)** By completing the square on both expressions, find the vector of the translation. **[3]**
**(b)** Verify your answer by expanding the transformed function. **[2]**

**12.** The graph of y = 2ˣ is stretched horizontally with scale factor 2, then stretched vertically with scale factor 3, then translated 4 units down, to give the graph of y = h(x).

**(a)** Find h(x). **[3]**
**(b)** Write down the equation of the horizontal asymptote of the graph of h. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the exact x-intercept of the graph of h. **[2]**
**(d)** Find the y-intercept of the graph of h. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** (5, −4) [1]
**(b)** (3, −6) [1]
**(c)** (−3, −4) [1]
**(d)** (3, −16) [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a) the change is inside the bracket, so x increases by 2; only (b) and (d) change the y-coordinate.

**2. (a)** Horizontal stretch with scale factor 3 [1]
**(b)** Reflection in the x-axis [1]
**(c)** Translation [1] by the vector (−1, −4) [1]
*Examiner insight:* A horizontal shift and a vertical shift together are one translation; giving "1 left, then 4 down" as two transformations does not answer "single transformation".

**3. (a)** x² − 6x + 10 = (x − 3)² − 9 + 10 [1] = (x − 3)² + 1 [1]
**(b)** Translation [1] by the vector (3, 1) [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Hence" means read the vector straight from (a): h gives the horizontal component and k the vertical one.

**4. (a)** Inside f(2x) the x-values are halved [1], so the domain is −0.5 ≤ x ≤ 2.5 [1]
**(b)** g = 3 − f, so f = −2 gives 5 and f = 7 gives −4 [1]. Range −4 ≤ g(x) ≤ 5 [1]
*Examiner insight:* The minus sign reverses the order of the endpoints; writing 5 ≤ g(x) ≤ −4 or 1 ≤ g(x) ≤ 10 loses the accuracy mark.

**5. (a)** x = 2 [1]; y = 3 [1]
**(b)** x = −2 [1]; y = 5 − 3 = 2 [1]
**(c)** x = −4 (x-values doubled) [1]; y = 9 [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each asymptote responds only to transformations in its own direction: vertical asymptotes to horizontal changes, horizontal asymptotes to vertical changes.

**6. (a)** Invariant points of a vertical stretch lie on the x-axis: f(x) = (x − 3)(x + 2) = 0 [1], giving (−2, 0) and (3, 0) [1]
**(b)** The y-intercept is invariant under a horizontal stretch: (0, −6) [1]

**7. (a)** f(x − 3) = (x − 3)² + 4(x − 3) + 1 [1] = x² − 2x − 2 [1]. Stretch: 2x² − 4x − 4 [1]. Reflect: g(x) = −2x² + 4x + 4 [1]
**(b)** (−2, −3) → (1, −3) → (1, −6) [1] → (1, 6) [1]
**(c)** g(x) ≤ 6 [1]
*Examiner insight:* Check (b) against (a): g(1) = −2 + 4 + 4 = 6. A mismatch tells you to recheck the expansion.

**8. (a)** Translation by the vector (−3, 0) [1]; vertical stretch with scale factor ½ [1]; reflection in the x-axis [1]; translation by the vector (0, 2), applied after the stretch and reflection [1]
**(b)** (−3, 2) [1]
**(c)** ½(x + 3)³ = 2, so (x + 3)³ = 4 [1], giving x = ∛4 − 3 [1]
*Examiner insight:* The translation up must come last. Translating up 2 before the stretch and reflection gives −½((x + 3)³ + 2) = −½(x + 3)³ − 1, a different graph, so the order mark is lost.

**9. (a)** (x, y) → (−x, 2y − 1) [1]. P → (1, 7) and Q → (0, 5) [1]; R → (−2, −1) [1]
**(b)** y = 2(1) − 1, so y = 1 [1]
**(c)** g(0) = 2f(0) − 1 = 2(3) − 1 = 5 [1]

**10. (a)** A: y = 4 − x² [1]; B: y = −(x² + 4) = −x² − 4 [1]
**(b)** 4 − x² = 0 gives x = −2 and x = 2 [1], so the intercepts are (−2, 0) and (2, 0) [1]. For B, x² ≥ 0 so −x² − 4 ≤ −4 < 0, and the graph never reaches the x-axis [1]

**11. (a)** x² − 2x = (x − 1)² − 1, vertex (1, −1) [1]; x² + 6x + 5 = (x + 3)² − 4, vertex (−3, −4) [1]. Vector (−4, −3) [1]
**(b)** f(x + 4) − 3 = (x + 4)² − 2(x + 4) − 3 [1] = x² + 8x + 16 − 2x − 8 − 3 = x² + 6x + 5 [1]

**12. (a)** Horizontal stretch: 2^(x/2) [1]; vertical stretch: 3 × 2^(x/2) [1]; h(x) = 3 × 2^(x/2) − 4 [1]
**(b)** y = −4 [1]
**(c)** 2^(x/2) = 4/3 [1], so x = 2 log₂(4/3) [1]
**(d)** h(0) = 3 − 4 = −1, so (0, −1) [1]
*Examiner insight:* A horizontal stretch with scale factor 2 replaces x by x/2, not 2x; this is the most common error in part (a).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Moving inside changes the wrong way: f(x + 1) goes left, f(x/3) stretches by 3.
- Giving two translations where one "single transformation" is asked for.
- Reversing a range incorrectly after a reflection in the x-axis.
- Translating before stretching when the equation has the form p f(x) + b.
- Moving an asymptote in the wrong direction, or moving one that should stay fixed.
- Leaving the scale factor or vector out of a description.
