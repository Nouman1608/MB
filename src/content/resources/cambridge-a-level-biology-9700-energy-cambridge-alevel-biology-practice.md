These questions cover sub-topic **12.1 Energy** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027 (A Level, Paper 4 style, with Paper 5 planning and analysis in Questions 10 and 11). The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question. All data are invented for practice. Calculators are allowed.

Revise first with the [energy study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-energy-cambridge-alevel-biology/) and the [energy revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-energy-cambridge-alevel-biology-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Which is the typical RQ when lipid is the only respiratory substrate?

A: 0.7

B: 0.9

C: 1.0

D: 1.3 **[1]**

**2.** In which location is ATP made by chemiosmosis?

A: cytoplasm, during glycolysis

B: mitochondrial matrix, during the Krebs cycle

C: inner mitochondrial membrane

D: stroma of a chloroplast **[1]**

**3.** State the definition of the respiratory quotient. **[2]**

**4.** Outline why energy is needed for protein synthesis. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why ATP, rather than glucose, is used as the immediate source of energy in cells. **[3]**

## Section B

**6.** Describe the features of ATP that make it suitable as the universal energy currency. **[4]**

**7.** Compare substrate-linked phosphorylation with chemiosmosis. **[4]**

**8.** Explain why the energy value of lipids is about twice that of carbohydrates. **[4]**

**9.** The equation shows the aerobic respiration of a fatty acid.

C₁₄H₂₈O₂ + 20O₂ → 14CO₂ + 14H₂O

**(a)** Show that the equation is balanced for oxygen atoms.

**(b)** Calculate the RQ. Give your answer to 2 significant figures.

**(c)** Explain why this RQ is lower than that for glucose. **[4]**

**10.** A student used a respirometer to find the RQ of germinating seeds at 25 °C. The capillary bore had a radius of 0.50 mm.

| Contents of tube | Distance moved by liquid towards seeds in 20 min / mm |
|---|---|
| Seeds with potassium hydroxide | 38 |
| Seeds with water | 2 |

**(a)** Calculate the volume of oxygen taken in per minute. Give your answer in mm³ min⁻¹ to 3 significant figures.

**(b)** Calculate the RQ.

**(c)** Suggest the main respiratory substrate. Give a reason.

**(d)** Explain why the seeds and potassium hydroxide must not be in contact. **[7]**

**11.** Outline how you would use the respirometer in Question 10 to ensure the results are valid and reliable. **[5]**

**12.** Germinating seeds kept in a sealed container gave an RQ that rose from 0.95 to 1.6 over several hours.

**(a)** Suggest and explain why the RQ rose above 1.

**(b)** State **one** way to show that your explanation is correct. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **A** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**2.** **C** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**3.** The ratio of the number of molecules of carbon dioxide produced to the number of molecules of oxygen taken in [1]; as a result of respiration [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**4.** Energy (from ATP) is needed to attach amino acids to tRNA (activate them) [1]; and to join amino acids by peptide bonds, an anabolic reaction [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**5.** Hydrolysis of ATP is a single reaction, so energy is released immediately [1]; it releases small amounts, matched to each reaction, so little is wasted as heat [1]; respiring glucose releases a large amount of energy through many steps, too slowly for immediate needs [1]. Total: 3 marks.

**6.** Any four from: hydrolysis is a single step, so energy is released quickly; releases a small, manageable amount of energy; small and water-soluble, so moves easily within cells; easily regenerated from ADP and phosphate; used by all cells and organisms [4]. Total: 4 marks.

**7.** Both produce ATP from ADP and phosphate [1]; substrate-linked involves direct transfer of a phosphate from a substrate molecule, chemiosmosis uses a proton gradient driving ATP synthase [1]; substrate-linked occurs in the cytoplasm (glycolysis) and matrix (Krebs cycle), chemiosmosis on membranes (inner mitochondrial membrane, thylakoids) [1]; chemiosmosis produces most of the ATP in aerobic respiration [1]. Total: 4 marks.

**8.** Lipids (fatty acids) contain more hydrogen atoms per gram than carbohydrates [1]; carbohydrates contain more oxygen, so are already partly oxidised [1]; more hydrogen means more reduced NAD and FAD are produced [1]; so more ATP is made by oxidative phosphorylation (chemiosmosis) [1]. Total: 4 marks.

**9. (a)** Left: 2 + 40 = 42; right: 28 + 14 = 42 [1].

**(b)** 14 ÷ 20 [1] = **0.70** [1].

**(c)** The fatty acid contains much more hydrogen and less oxygen, so proportionally more oxygen is needed to oxidise it [1]. Total: 4 marks.

**10. (a)** π × 0.50² × 38 = 29.8 mm³ [1]; 29.8 ÷ 20 = **1.49 mm³ min⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** (38 − 2) ÷ 38 [1] = **0.95** [1].

**(c)** Carbohydrate (mainly), because the RQ is close to 1.0 [1].

**(d)** Potassium hydroxide is corrosive and would damage or kill the seeds [1]; seeds touching the solution would also take up liquid, so gas exchange measured would not be normal [1]. Total: 7 marks.

**11.** Any five from: keep the respirometer in a water bath at constant temperature; allow time to equilibrate with the system open before readings; use a control tube with glass beads of the same volume; use the same seeds (same mass and species) for both runs; use the same volume of potassium hydroxide and of water; repeat each run at least three times and calculate a mean; reset the liquid with a syringe between readings [5]. Total: 5 marks.

**12. (a)** Oxygen in the sealed container ran out, so some seeds respired anaerobically [1]; ethanol fermentation releases carbon dioxide without taking in oxygen, so CO₂ produced exceeds O₂ taken in [1].

**(b)** Any one: test the seeds for ethanol; measure oxygen concentration in the container; repeat in a container with a continuous air supply and show RQ stays near 1 [1]. Total: 3 marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining RQ the wrong way up.
- Treating the water-run reading as oxygen uptake.
- Explaining energy values without hydrogen and reduced coenzymes.
- Using the diameter in πr².
- Omitting the control tube and equilibration.
