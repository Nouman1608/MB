These notes condense section **5.4 Manufacturing accounts** of the Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus for 2027 to 2029. The section covers "direct and indirect costs", "direct material, direct labour, prime cost and factory overheads", adjustments for work in progress, "factory cost of production", and preparing manufacturing accounts, statements of profit or loss and statements of financial position with the 5.1 adjustments. The full worked example is in the [manufacturing accounts study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-manufacturing-accounts/); test yourself afterwards with the [manufacturing accounts practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-manufacturing-accounts-practice/). Figures are invented and in dollars.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Direct cost | A cost that can be traced to each unit produced |
| Indirect cost | A factory cost incurred for production as a whole, not traceable to one unit |
| Direct material | Raw materials that become part of the product, including carriage inwards on them |
| Direct labour | Wages of workers who make the product |
| Prime cost | Direct material + direct labour |
| Factory overheads | All indirect factory costs, such as supervisors' wages, factory power and factory depreciation |
| Work in progress | Partly completed goods at a date |
| Factory cost of production | Prime cost + factory overheads + opening work in progress - closing work in progress |
| Finished goods | Completed goods waiting to be sold |

## The layout in order

1. Opening raw materials + purchases of raw materials + carriage inwards - closing raw materials = **cost of raw materials consumed**.
2. Add direct labour = **prime cost**.
3. Add factory overheads.
4. Add opening work in progress, deduct closing work in progress = **factory cost of production**.
5. Statement of profit or loss: opening finished goods + factory cost of production - closing finished goods = **cost of sales**.
6. Revenue - cost of sales = gross profit; gross profit - office and selling expenses = profit for the year.

## Which account gets the cost

| Cost | Manufacturing account | Statement of profit or loss |
|---|---|---|
| Raw materials and carriage inwards | Yes (direct material) | |
| Production workers' wages | Yes (direct labour) | |
| Supervisors' and factory cleaners' wages | Yes (factory overheads) | |
| Factory power, factory rent, factory insurance | Yes (factory overheads) | |
| Depreciation of factory machinery | Yes (factory overheads) | |
| Office salaries, office rent, office depreciation | | Yes |
| Carriage outwards, advertising, sales staff | | Yes |
| Irrecoverable debts and allowance changes | | Yes |
| Finished goods inventory | | Yes (cost of sales) |

Shared costs, such as rent of one building, are split in the proportion the question gives, with each share going to its own account.

## Statement of financial position

Three inventories appear under current assets: raw materials, work in progress and finished goods, each at the closing value. Factory costs owing are other payables and factory costs paid in advance are other receivables, as in 5.1.

## Exam traps

- Closing work in progress is **deducted**; opening work in progress is **added**.
- Carriage inwards is part of the cost of raw materials. Carriage outwards is a selling expense.
- Finished goods never appear in the manufacturing account.
- Split a shared cost and use both parts. Missing the office share understates expenses.
- Adjust factory wages for accruals before working out prime cost.
- An overstated closing work in progress understates factory cost of production and overstates gross profit, profit for the year and current assets.
- Label each subtotal: cost of raw materials consumed, prime cost, factory overheads and factory cost of production.
- The syllabus asks for factory cost of production, so finished goods transfer at cost.

## Self-test

1. Define prime cost.
2. State whether each cost is direct or indirect: (a) wages of the factory cleaner; (b) fabric used in each sofa.
3. Opening raw materials $8,000; purchases of raw materials $52,000; carriage inwards $1,000; closing raw materials $9,500. Calculate the cost of raw materials consumed.
4. Direct material is $51,500 and direct labour is $64,000. Calculate prime cost.
5. Prime cost plus factory overheads is $180,000. Opening work in progress is $6,000 and closing work in progress is $7,500. Calculate the factory cost of production.
6. Opening finished goods $12,000; factory cost of production $178,500; closing finished goods $15,000. Calculate cost of sales.
7. Rent of $24,000 is shared five-sixths factory and one-sixth office. State each share.
8. In which statement does carriage outwards appear?
9. Closing work in progress is overstated by $1,000. State the effect on factory cost of production and on gross profit.
10. Name the three inventories shown in a manufacturer's statement of financial position.
11. Direct wages paid were $70,000 and $1,500 is owing at the year end. State the direct labour figure and where the $1,500 appears in the statement of financial position.
12. Explain why the depreciation of delivery vehicles is not a factory overhead.

### Answers

1. Direct material plus direct labour: the total of costs traced directly to the units produced.
2. (a) Indirect: the cleaner's work cannot be traced to any one unit. (b) Direct: the fabric becomes part of each sofa.
3. 8,000 + 52,000 + 1,000 - 9,500 = **$51,500**.
4. 51,500 + 64,000 = **$115,500**.
5. 180,000 + 6,000 - 7,500 = **$178,500**.
6. 12,000 + 178,500 - 15,000 = **$175,500**.
7. Factory: 24,000 x 5/6 = **$20,000**. Office: 24,000 x 1/6 = **$4,000**.
8. The statement of profit or loss, as a selling expense.
9. Factory cost of production is **$1,000 too low**, so cost of sales is $1,000 too low and gross profit is **$1,000 too high**.
10. Raw materials, work in progress and finished goods.
11. Direct labour = 70,000 + 1,500 = **$71,500**. The $1,500 is an other payable under current liabilities.
12. Delivery vehicles are used to send goods to customers, which is a selling activity, not making goods, so their depreciation is an expense in the statement of profit or loss.
