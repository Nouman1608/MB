This guide covers subtopic **28.5, Stability constants, Kstab**, from Topic 28, Chemistry of transition
elements, of Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027 series. It is A Level content. The
overview in [Transition Elements: Colour, Stereoisomerism and Stability Constants](/resources/a-transition-elements-colour-stereoisomerism-and-stability-constants/)
shows one copper–ammonia calculation; this page sets out every type of Kstab question you are likely to meet,
with a method for each. Afterwards, use the [Kstab revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-chemistry-9701-a-stability-constants-kstab-revision-notes/)
and the [Kstab practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-chemistry-9701-a-stability-constants-kstab-practice/).

All Kstab values on this page are **illustrative**, chosen to be realistic in size. In an examination, always
use the values given in the question.

## What the syllabus asks for

1. **define** the stability constant, Kstab, of a complex as the equilibrium constant for the formation of
   the complex ion in a solvent (from its constituent ions or molecules);
2. **write an expression** for a Kstab of a complex (**[H₂O] should not be included**);
3. **use Kstab expressions to perform calculations**;
4. **describe and explain ligand exchanges in terms of Kstab values**, and understand that a large Kstab is
   due to the formation of a stable complex ion.

## The definition, word by word

*"The equilibrium constant for the formation of the complex ion in a solvent from its constituent ions or
molecules."*

- **Equilibrium constant**: it is a Kc, so it is written products over reactants, with concentrations at
  equilibrium, and it changes only with temperature.
- **Formation**: the equation is always written with the complex on the **right**.
- **In a solvent**: for aqueous chemistry the solvent is water. Its concentration is effectively constant, so
  it is left out.
- **From its constituent ions or molecules**: the metal ion and the ligands.

## Writing expressions and units

Write the formation equation, then apply Kc rules, leaving out water.

**Example A: an ammine complex.**

    [Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺(aq) + 6NH₃(aq) ⇌ [Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺(aq) + 6H₂O(l)

    Kstab = [[Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺] / ( [[Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺] [NH₃]⁶ )

Units: (mol dm⁻³) / (mol dm⁻³)⁷ = **mol⁻⁶ dm¹⁸**.

**Example B: a bidentate ligand.**

    [Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺(aq) + 3en(aq) ⇌ [Ni(en)₃]²⁺(aq) + 6H₂O(l)

    Kstab = [[Ni(en)₃]²⁺] / ( [[Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺] [en]³ )

Units: **mol⁻³ dm⁹**. The power is the number of **ligand particles**, three, not the number of bonds, six.

**Example C: a hexadentate ligand.**

    [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺(aq) + EDTA⁴⁻(aq) ⇌ [Cu(EDTA)]²⁻(aq) + 6H₂O(l)

    Kstab = [[Cu(EDTA)]²⁻] / ( [[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺] [EDTA⁴⁻] )

Units: **mol⁻¹ dm³**.

**Rule for units:** if n ligand particles appear in the equation, the units are mol⁻ⁿ dm³ⁿ.

## lg Kstab

Kstab values are often enormous, so data are frequently given as **lg Kstab** (log₁₀ Kstab). A larger
lg Kstab means a larger Kstab. Convert with Kstab = 10^(lg Kstab). For example, lg Kstab = 13.1 means
Kstab = 10¹³·¹ ≈ 1.3 × 10¹³.

Because the units of Kstab depend on the number of ligands, compare Kstab values directly only for complexes
formed by the **same number of ligand particles**, or use the qualitative idea that the much larger value
corresponds to the much more stable complex, as the syllabus does.

## Calculation type 1: finding Kstab from equilibrium data

*An equilibrium mixture contains [[Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺] = 0.050 mol dm⁻³, [NH₃] = 0.50 mol dm⁻³ and
[[Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺] = 8.0 × 10⁻⁹ mol dm⁻³. Calculate Kstab.*

    [NH₃]⁶ = (0.50)⁶ = 0.015625
    Kstab = 0.050 / (8.0 × 10⁻⁹ × 0.015625)
          = 0.050 / (1.25 × 10⁻¹⁰)
          = 4.0 × 10⁸ mol⁻⁶ dm¹⁸

## Calculation type 2: finding one equilibrium concentration

Rearrange the expression to make the unknown the subject **before** substituting.

*Kstab for [Cu(EDTA)]²⁻ is 6.3 × 10¹⁸ mol⁻¹ dm³. At equilibrium, [[Cu(EDTA)]²⁻] = 0.020 mol dm⁻³ and
[EDTA⁴⁻] = 0.0010 mol dm⁻³. Calculate the concentration of the aqua ion.*

    [[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺] = [[Cu(EDTA)]²⁻] / ( Kstab × [EDTA⁴⁻] )
                   = 0.020 / (6.3 × 10¹⁸ × 0.0010)
                   = 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁸ mol dm⁻³

## Calculation type 3: a large excess of ligand

This is the most demanding type, and a worked example is worth studying line by line.

*0.010 mol of copper(II) ions is dissolved in 1.00 dm³ of solution containing 1.00 mol of NH₃. Kstab for
[Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ is 1.0 × 10¹³ mol⁻⁴ dm¹². Calculate the concentration of [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ remaining.*

**Step 1: assume almost complete complex formation** (justified by the very large Kstab).
[[Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺] ≈ 0.010 mol dm⁻³.

**Step 2: ammonia used.** Four NH₃ per copper: 4 × 0.010 = 0.040 mol dm⁻³ used.
[NH₃] at equilibrium = 1.00 − 0.040 = 0.96 mol dm⁻³.

**Step 3: rearrange and substitute.**

    [[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺] = 0.010 / (1.0 × 10¹³ × 0.96⁴)
                   = 0.010 / (1.0 × 10¹³ × 0.849)
                   = 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ mol dm⁻³

**Step 4: check the assumption.** 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ is negligible compared with 0.010, so treating the copper as
fully complexed was valid.

The common error is forgetting step 2 and using 1.00 for [NH₃]. Here the answer would change only slightly,
but in questions where the ligand is not in large excess, the difference is large.

## Explaining ligand exchange with Kstab

When a new ligand is added to a complex, ligands compete for the metal ion. The complex with the **larger
Kstab** is the more stable one, so the equilibrium position favours it.

*Illustrative lg Kstab values for copper(II), each for formation from [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺:*

| Complex | lg Kstab |
|---|---|
| [CuCl₄]²⁻ | 5.6 |
| [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ | 13.1 |
| [Cu(EDTA)]²⁻ | 18.8 |

**Annotated model answer.** *Use the data to explain what happens when EDTA⁴⁻ is added to a deep blue solution
of [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺.*

> Kstab for [Cu(EDTA)]²⁻ is larger than Kstab for [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ **[comparison made]**, so [Cu(EDTA)]²⁻
> is the more stable complex **[stability linked to Kstab]**. EDTA⁴⁻ replaces the NH₃ and H₂O ligands:
> [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ + EDTA⁴⁻ → [Cu(EDTA)]²⁻ + 4NH₃ + 2H₂O **[equation]**. The position of equilibrium lies
> well to the right **[direction stated]**.

**Dividing the expressions.** For this exchange, the equilibrium constant equals
Kstab([Cu(EDTA)]²⁻) ÷ Kstab([Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺) = 10¹⁸·⁸ ÷ 10¹³·¹ = 10⁵·⁷ ≈ 5 × 10⁵. This follows directly from the
two expressions, because [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ cancels, and confirms that the exchange goes almost to completion.

**Concentration can still matter.** [CuCl₄]²⁻ has a much smaller Kstab than the ammine complex, yet it does
form when concentrated hydrochloric acid is added, because a very high [Cl⁻] shifts the equilibrium to the
right. Diluting with water reverses it. A small Kstab means the complex forms only when the ligand
concentration is high, not that it never forms.

## Why a large Kstab means a stable complex

A large Kstab means that, at equilibrium, very little of the metal remains as the aqua ion: the complex is
**stable** with respect to losing its ligands back to water. Polydentate ligands such as EDTA⁴⁻ give
particularly large values. One reason links to entropy from Topic 23: one EDTA⁴⁻ ion releases six water
molecules, so the number of particles in solution increases from two to seven, which favours the forward
reaction.

## Common mistakes

- **Including [H₂O]** in the expression, or leaving out the aqua ion.
- **Wrong power on the ligand**: it is the number of ligand particles in the balanced equation.
- **Units**: work them out from the expression; do not memorise one set.
- **Using initial instead of equilibrium concentrations**, especially in type 3 questions.
- **Explaining exchange without the word "stable"**: link the larger Kstab to the more stable complex.
- **Writing the equation backwards**: Kstab is for formation of the complex.

## Exam technique for Cambridge command words

- **Define**: use the syllabus wording, including "in a solvent" and "from its constituent ions or molecules".
- **Write an expression**: square brackets for concentrations, the complex on top, no water, units if asked.
- **Calculate**: rearrange first, show each substitution, give units and sensible significant figures.
- **Explain** a ligand exchange: compare Kstab values, name the more stable complex, write the equation and
  state the direction of the equilibrium.
