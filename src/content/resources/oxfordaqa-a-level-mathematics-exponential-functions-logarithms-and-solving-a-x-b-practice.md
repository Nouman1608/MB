## Questions

**1.** The curve C has equation y = 5^x.

**(a)** State the coordinates of the point where C crosses the y-axis. **[1]**
**(b)** Write down the equation of the asymptote of C. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the value of y when x = −2. **[1]**

**2.** **(a)** Write 2⁻³ = 1/8 in the form log_a b = c. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the value of x for which log_x 81 = 4. **[2]**

**3.** Evaluate, showing your working:

**(a)** 2 log₅15 − log₅9 **[3]**
**(b)** log₉27 **[2]**

**4.** Suppose log_a 2 = p and log_a 3 = q. Write each of the following in terms of p and q:

**(a)** log_a 12 **[2]**
**(b)** log_a (9/8) **[2]**

**5.** Solve 16^x = 8^(x−1). **[4]**

**6.** Solve 6^x = 100, giving your answer to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**7.** Solve 3^x = 4^(x−1), giving your answer to 3 significant figures. **[5]**

**8.** Solve log₂(x − 1) + log₂(x + 3) = 5. **[5]**

**9.** A sum of £5000 is invested at 6% compound interest per year, so after n years it is worth 5000 × 1.06^n pounds.

**(a)** Show that the investment is worth at least £8000 when 1.06^n ≥ 1.6. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the least whole number of years for this to happen. **[3]**
**(c)** Verify your answer to part (b) by evaluating the investment after n − 1 years and after n years. **[2]**

**10.** The mass of a sample, in grams, is modelled by M = 500 × 0.92^t, where t is the time in days.

**(a)** Find the values of t for which M < 120, giving the boundary value to 3 significant figures. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the least whole number of days after which the mass is below 120 grams, and justify your answer with two values of M. **[2]**

**11.** The curve y = a^x, where a > 0, passes through the point (−2, 1/49).

**(a)** Find the value of a. **[2]**
**(b)** Solve 7^(2x − 1) = 30, giving your answer to 3 significant figures. **[4]**
**(c)** Describe the single transformation that maps the curve y = 7^x onto the curve y = 7^(−x). **[1]**
**(d)** Solve 7^(−x) = 0.4, giving your answer to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**12.** **(a)** Show that 2 log₅x − log₅(x − 4) can be written as log₅[x² / (x − 4)]. **[2]**
**(b)** Hence solve 2 log₅x − log₅(x − 4) = 2. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** a⁰ = 1 for any base, so C crosses at **(0, 1)** [1].
**(b)** The curve approaches but never reaches the x-axis: **y = 0** [1].
**(c)** 5⁻² = 1/5² = **1/25** (or 0.04) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the intercept as a coordinate pair, not just "1", and write the asymptote as an equation. Answers to short recall parts are easy to lose by giving only half of what is asked.

**2.** **(a)** The base 2 stays the base, and the index −3 becomes the logarithm: **log₂(1/8) = −3** [1].
**(b)** x⁴ = 81 [1], so **x = 3**, since a base must be positive [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (b), stating that x = −3 is rejected because a logarithm base must be positive shows the reasoning and protects you if you wrote both values.

**3.** **(a)** 2 log₅15 = log₅225 [1]. Then log₅225 − log₅9 = log₅25 [1]. Since 5² = 25, the value is **2** [1].
**(b)** Let log₉27 = k, so 9^k = 27, which is 3^(2k) = 3³ [1]. So 2k = 3 and **k = 3/2** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "Showing your working" means the law you use should be visible. A bare answer with no log step gives the reader nothing to follow if it is wrong.

**4.** **(a)** 12 = 2² × 3, so log_a 12 = 2 log_a 2 + log_a 3 [1] = **2p + q** [1].
**(b)** log_a(9/8) = log_a 9 − log_a 8 = 2 log_a 3 − 3 log_a 2 [1] = **2q − 3p** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Factorise into powers of 2 and 3 first, then apply the laws. Turning log_a 12 into log_a 4 + log_a 3 and stopping is an incomplete answer, because log_a 4 is not in terms of p.

**5.** 16 = 2⁴ and 8 = 2³, so 2^(4x) = 2^(3(x−1)) [2]. Equating indices: 4x = 3x − 3 [1]. So **x = −3** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Write each side as a power of the same base before equating indices. Check by substitution: 16⁻³ and 8⁻⁴ are both 1/4096.

**6.** Take logarithms of both sides: x log 6 = log 100 [1]. So x = log 100 / log 6 [1] = **2.57** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* log 100 is 2 if you use base 10, but any base gives the same quotient. Quote the exact fraction before the decimal, and give the decimal to 3 significant figures as the question asks.

**7.** Take logarithms: x ln 3 = (x − 1) ln 4 [1]. Expand: x ln 3 = x ln 4 − ln 4 [1]. Collect: ln 4 = x(ln 4 − ln 3) [1]. So x = ln 4 / (ln 4 − ln 3) [1] = **4.82** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Unknowns on both sides need collecting and factorising before you divide. Do not divide log 4 by log 3 and treat the exponent x − 1 as if it were x.

**8.** Combine: log₂[(x − 1)(x + 3)] = 5 [1]. Convert: (x − 1)(x + 3) = 2⁵ = 32 [1]. Expand: x² + 2x − 35 = 0 [1]. Factorise: (x − 5)(x + 7) = 0 [1]. For x = −7 the term log₂(x − 1) is not defined, so reject it, leaving **x = 5** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A quadratic gives two roots, but they may not both be solutions. A log equation needs a final check that every argument is positive, and the rejection should be stated.

**9.** **(a)** 5000 × 1.06^n ≥ 8000 gives 1.06^n ≥ 8000/5000 = 1.6 [1].
**(b)** n ln 1.06 ≥ ln 1.6 [1]. Since ln 1.06 > 0, the inequality keeps its direction: n ≥ ln 1.6 / ln 1.06 = 8.07 [1]. The least whole number is **n = 9** [1].
**(c)** After 8 years: 5000 × 1.06⁸ = £7969.24, which is below £8000 [1]. After 9 years: 5000 × 1.06⁹ = £8447.39, which is above £8000 [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Round the boundary 8.07 up to 9, not down to 8. For a "least whole number" question, reading the context carefully is what earns the final credit.

**10.** **(a)** 500 × 0.92^t < 120, so 0.92^t < 0.24 [1]. Take logarithms: t ln 0.92 < ln 0.24 [1]. ln 0.92 is negative, so dividing reverses the inequality: t > ln 0.24 / ln 0.92 [1], that is **t > 17.1** [1].
**(b)** The least whole number is **18 days** [1]. Check: M(17) = 121.16, which is above 120, and M(18) = 111.47, which is below 120 [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The sign reversal in part (a) is the usual place to lose credit. If you divide by ln 0.92 and keep the original sign, you get t < 17.1, which contradicts the fact that the mass is falling.

**11.** **(a)** a⁻² = 1/49, so a² = 49 [1]. Since a > 0, **a = 7** [1].
**(b)** (2x − 1) ln 7 = ln 30 [1]. So 2x − 1 = ln 30 / ln 7 = 1.748 [1]. Then 2x = 2.748 [1] and **x = 1.37** [1].
**(c)** **A reflection in the y-axis** [1].
**(d)** −x ln 7 = ln 0.4 [1], so −x = −0.471 and **x = 0.471** [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* A transformation answer needs the type and the line: "reflection" alone leaves out half of what is asked, and a wrong line such as the x-axis changes the curve. In part (d), taking logarithms of 0.4 gives a negative value, so watch the sign when you change −x to x.

**12.** **(a)** 2 log₅x = log₅(x²) [1]. Then log₅(x²) − log₅(x − 4) = log₅[x² / (x − 4)] [1].
**(b)** log₅[x² / (x − 4)] = 2, so x² / (x − 4) = 5² = 25 [1]. Then x² = 25(x − 4) = 25x − 100 [1]. Rearrange: x² − 25x + 100 = 0 [1]. Factorise: (x − 5)(x − 20) = 0 [1], so x = 5 or x = 20 [1]. Both are greater than 4, so every logarithm is defined and both are valid. Check x = 20: 2 log₅20 − log₅16 = log₅25 = 2 [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* On a "show that" part, state which law you use at each line. Do not skip the middle step 2 log₅x = log₅(x²), because it is the whole of the demonstration.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding or subtracting logarithms with different bases without converting first.
- Splitting log(x + y) into log x + log y, or log(x − y) into log x − log y.
- Dividing by a negative logarithm in an inequality and keeping the original inequality sign.
- Rounding intermediate values, such as 1.748 or 8.07, and then giving a final answer that differs in the third figure.
- Failing to check log equations, so that a root making an argument negative stays in the answer.
- Rounding "least whole number" answers to the nearest whole number instead of rounding up.
- Taking logarithms of both sides before isolating the power, for example in 5 × 3^x = 40.
- Describing a transformation by type only, such as "reflection", without the mirror line.
- Giving the intercept as a number only, or leaving the asymptote off a sketch of y = a^x.
