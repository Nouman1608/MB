Cambridge International AS & A Level Computer Science (9618) combines
the theory of how computers represent data, communicate and process
instructions with the practical skills of designing algorithms and
writing programs. This page is based on the 2026 syllabus; Cambridge's
2027-2029 syllabus sets the same four papers with the same durations,
marks and weightings. The A Level is assessed through four externally
assessed papers, each carrying 75 marks and 25% of the full A Level.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus has 20 topics. Topics 1 to 12 are AS content and Topics 13
to 20 are A Level content. At both stages the topics divide into a
theory strand and a problem-solving and programming strand.

**AS Level theory (Topics 1 to 8).** Information representation (data
representation, multimedia and compression), Communication (networks
including the internet), Hardware (components, logic gates and logic
circuits), Processor fundamentals (CPU architecture, assembly language
and bit manipulation), System software, Security, privacy and data
integrity, Ethics and ownership, and Databases (including DDL and DML).

**AS Level problem-solving and programming (Topics 9 to 12).** Algorithm
design and problem-solving, Data types and structures (records, arrays,
files and an introduction to abstract data types), Programming (basics,
constructs and structured programming), and Software development (the
program development life cycle, design, testing and maintenance).

**A Level theory (Topics 13 to 18).** Data representation (user-defined
types, file organisation and floating-point numbers), Communication and
internet technologies (protocols, circuit and packet switching), Hardware
and virtual machines (parallel processing, Boolean algebra and logic
circuits), System software, Security (encryption, protocols and digital
certificates) and Artificial intelligence.

**A Level problem-solving and programming (Topics 19 and 20).**
Computational thinking and problem-solving (algorithms and recursion) and
Further programming (programming paradigms, file processing and
exception handling).

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Stage | Duration | Marks | Weighting (full A Level) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paper 1 | Theory Fundamentals | AS | 90 minutes | 75 | 25% |
| Paper 2 | Fundamental Problem-solving and Programming Skills | AS | 120 minutes | 75 | 25% |
| Paper 3 | Advanced Theory | A Level | 90 minutes | 75 | 25% |
| Paper 4 | Practical | A Level | 150 minutes | 75 | 25% |

Papers 1 to 3 are written examinations. Paper 4 is a practical paper in
which candidates submit complete program code and testing evidence,
working on a computer with no internet or email access. Calculators must
not be used in any paper.

9618 offers flexible routes. Papers 1 and 2 can be taken as a standalone
Cambridge International AS Level, each worth 50% of the AS. They can also
form the first half of a full A Level staged over two years, carrying the
AS result forward, or all four papers can be taken together in the same
series. Check with your school which route you are on.

## How the parts fit together

The paper titles mirror the two strands. Theory Fundamentals and Advanced
Theory line up with the theory topics at each stage, while Fundamental
Problem-solving and Programming Skills and the Practical paper line up
with the problem-solving and programming topics. Several areas appear at both stages and
deepen the second time: data representation moves on to floating-point
numbers, communication moves on to protocols and switching, hardware
moves on to Boolean algebra and virtual machines, and system software
moves on to the purposes of an operating system and translation software.

Programming connects the whole course. The algorithms, data structures
and structured programming from Topics 9 to 12 are the foundations for
recursion, programming paradigms and exception handling in Topics 19 and
20, and for the practical work in Paper 4. Because no calculator is
allowed, number-base conversions, binary arithmetic and floating-point
work also need to be practised by hand from the start.

## Planning your study

Running the theory and programming strands in parallel tends to work
better than finishing one before starting the other:

- **AS, first half:** Topics 1, 3 and 4 alongside Topics 9 and 11, so
  that data representation and processor ideas develop together with
  early programming.
- **AS, second half:** Topics 2 and 5 to 8 alongside Topics 10 and 12,
  with regular small programming projects.
- **A Level, first half:** Topics 13 to 15, revisiting each matching AS
  topic, alongside Topic 19.
- **A Level, second half:** Topics 16 to 18 alongside Topic 20, with
  extended programming practice in preparation for Paper 4.

Write code every week rather than in occasional bursts; programming
fluency builds slowly. Start past-paper questions on each block once it
is complete, and leave full timed papers and paper-specific technique to
the revision period, using the exam preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge A Level Computer Science 9618?**
There are four papers, each worth 75 marks and 25% of the full A Level. Papers 1 and 2 form the AS Level, and Papers 3 and 4 complete the A Level.

**Can I use a calculator in 9618 exams?**
No. Calculators must not be used in any of the four papers.

**What happens in Paper 4 Practical?**
Paper 4 lasts 150 minutes. Candidates submit complete program code and testing evidence, working on a computer with no internet or email access.

**Can I take all four 9618 papers in one series?**
Yes. All four papers can be taken together in the same series, or the A Level can be staged over two years with the AS result carried forward. Papers 1 and 2 can also be a standalone AS Level.

**Does the 2027-2029 syllabus change the papers?**
No. The 2027-2029 syllabus sets the same four papers with the same durations, marks and weightings as the 2026 syllabus.
