These questions cover subtopic 6.1, Pythagoras' theorem, in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. The statement is the same for Core and Extended. Questions 6 and 11 are labelled **(Extended)** because they also use surds or the length of a line segment from coordinates, which are Extended skills. Each question is labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator); give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures.

For explanations, read the [Pythagoras' theorem study guide](/resources/igcse-mathematics-0580-pythagoras-theorem/); for a summary, use the [Pythagoras' theorem revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-pythagoras-theorem-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** (non-calculator) A right-angled triangle has shorter sides 9 cm and 40 cm. Find the length of the hypotenuse. **[2]**

**2.** (non-calculator) A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 26 cm and one shorter side 10 cm. Find the third side. **[2]**

**3.** (calculator) A right-angled triangle has shorter sides 6.3 cm and 8.9 cm. Calculate the hypotenuse. **[2]**

**4.** (calculator) A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 15.2 cm and one shorter side 11.7 cm. Calculate the third side. **[2]**

**5.** (non-calculator) A triangle has sides 20 cm, 21 cm and 29 cm. Show that it is right-angled, and state which side is opposite the right angle. **[2]**

**6.** (Extended) (non-calculator) A right-angled triangle has shorter sides 3 cm and 6 cm. Find the exact length of the hypotenuse in its simplest form. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** (calculator) A rectangular screen is 96 cm wide and its diagonal is 110 cm.

**(a)** Calculate the height of the screen. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the area of the screen. **[2]**

**8.** (non-calculator) An isosceles triangle has two sides of 13 cm and a base of 24 cm.

**(a)** Find its perpendicular height. **[2]**

**(b)** Find its area. **[1]**

**9.** (non-calculator) A boat sails 12 km due north and then 35 km due east.

**(a)** Find its direct distance from its starting point. **[2]**

**(b)** How much shorter is the direct route than the route the boat took? **[1]**

**10.** (calculator) A ladder 6.5 m long leans against a vertical wall, with its foot on horizontal ground 2.5 m from the wall.

**(a)** Show that the top of the ladder is 6 m above the ground. **[2]**

**(b)** The foot of the ladder slides 1.4 m further from the wall. How far does the top of the ladder slide down the wall? **[2]**

**11.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Find the length of the line segment joining A(−3, 2) and B(5, 17). **[2]**

**12.** (non-calculator) Quadrilateral ABCD is made of two right-angled triangles. In triangle ABC, angle ABC = 90°, AB = 8 cm and BC = 6 cm. In triangle ACD, angle ACD = 90° and CD = 24 cm.

**(a)** Find AC. **[2]**

**(b)** Find AD. **[2]**

**13.** (non-calculator) A rhombus has diagonals of length 18 cm and 24 cm. Find its perimeter. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** √(81 + 1600) = √1681 [1] = **41 cm** [1]

**2.** √(676 − 100) = √576 [1] = **24 cm** [1]

*Examiner insight:* (26 − 10)(26 + 10) = 16 × 36 = 576 avoids squaring 26 by hand.

**3.** √(6.3² + 8.9²) = √118.9 [1] = **10.9 cm** (3 s.f.) [1]

**4.** √(15.2² − 11.7²) = √94.15 [1] = **9.70 cm** (3 s.f.) [1]

*Examiner insight:* adding the squares here gives 19.2 cm, longer than the hypotenuse, which is impossible for a shorter side.

**5.** 20² + 21² = 400 + 441 = 841 and 29² = 841 [1]; the squares of the two shorter sides add to the square of the longest, so the triangle is right-angled, with the right angle opposite the **29 cm** side [1]

**6.** √(3² + 6²) = √45 [1] = **3√5 cm** [1]

**7. (a)** √(110² − 96²) = √2884 [1] = **53.7 cm** (3 s.f.) [1]

**(b)** 96 × 53.70… [1] = **5160 cm²** (3 s.f.) [1]

*Examiner insight:* multiply by the unrounded height; 96 × 53.7 = 5155.2, which also rounds to 5160 here, but early rounding is risky in general.

**8. (a)** Half the base is 12 cm; height = √(169 − 144) [1] = **5 cm** [1]

**(b)** ½ × 24 × 5 = **60 cm²** [1]

**9. (a)** √(12² + 35²) = √1369 [1] = **37 km** [1]

**(b)** (12 + 35) − 37 = **10 km** [1]

**10. (a)** √(6.5² − 2.5²) = √(42.25 − 6.25) = √36 [1] = 6 m [1]

**(b)** New distance from the wall = 3.9 m; new height = √(6.5² − 3.9²) = √27.04 = 5.2 m [1]; the top slides down 6 − 5.2 = **0.8 m** [1]

**11.** Differences: 5 − (−3) = 8 and 17 − 2 = 15 [1]; AB = √(64 + 225) = √289 = **17 units** [1]

**12. (a)** √(8² + 6²) = √100 [1] = **10 cm** [1]

**(b)** √(10² + 24²) = √676 [1] = **26 cm** [1]

**13.** The diagonals bisect each other at right angles, so the half-diagonals are 9 cm and 12 cm [1]; side = √(81 + 144) = 15 cm [1]; perimeter = 4 × 15 = **60 cm** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding squares when the hypotenuse is given and a shorter side is wanted.
- Forgetting to take the square root.
- Using the full base of an isosceles triangle, or full diagonals of a rhombus.
- Squaring a negative coordinate difference incorrectly.
- In "show that" questions, not showing the squares and the square root clearly.
- Giving the hypotenuse when the question asks for a difference in distances.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I know whether to add or subtract?**
Add the squares to find the hypotenuse; subtract the smaller square from the hypotenuse squared to find a shorter side. Identify the right angle first.

**Do I need to give exact answers?**
Only when the question asks for an exact answer or simplest form. Otherwise, on a calculator paper, give 3 significant figures.
