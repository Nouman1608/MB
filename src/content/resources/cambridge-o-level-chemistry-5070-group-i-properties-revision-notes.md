Condensed recall for Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 subtopic **8.2 Group I properties**. The full explanation, data and worked predictions are in the [Group I properties study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-group-i-properties/).

## What you must know

- The named metals: **lithium, sodium, potassium**.
- They are **relatively soft metals** (cut with a knife), stored under oil.
- Three trends down the group, and only these three are required:
  - melting point **decreases**
  - density **increases** (general trend)
  - reactivity **increases**
- Predict the properties of other Group I elements (rubidium, caesium) from given information.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Alkali metal | A Group I element; reacts with water to give an alkaline hydroxide solution |
| Relatively soft | Can be cut with a knife, unlike most metals |
| Reactivity | How readily the metal reacts; for Group I, how easily the outer electron is lost |
| Hydroxide | Product of the reaction with water, formula MOH |
| Trend | A steady change in a property down the group |
| Tarnish | Dulling of a freshly cut surface by reaction with air |

## Core data

| Metal | Melting point / °C | Density / g cm⁻³ | Reaction with cold water |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lithium | 181 | 0.53 | Fizzes steadily, floats, slowly disappears |
| Sodium | 98 | 0.97 | Melts into a ball, darts about, fizzes rapidly |
| Potassium | 63 | 0.86 | Very vigorous; hydrogen burns with a lilac flame |

Potassium's density is below sodium's in real data: the syllabus describes the **general** trend as an increase. All three float on water.

## Equations

```
2Li + 2H₂O → 2LiOH + H₂
2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂
2K  + 2H₂O → 2KOH  + H₂
```

General: metal + water → metal hydroxide + hydrogen. The solution is alkaline (universal indicator turns blue or purple).

## Why reactivity increases (one sentence)

Down the group the outer electron is in a shell further from the nucleus, so it is lost more easily.

## Observations versus products

| What the question asks | What to write |
|---|---|
| What is **seen**? | floats; melts into a ball (Na, K); moves about; fizzes; lilac flame (K); gets smaller and disappears |
| What is **formed**? | the metal hydroxide (dissolved) and hydrogen gas |
| What is the **pH** of the solution? | above 7 / alkaline, because the hydroxide gives OH⁻ ions |
| What type of reaction (energy)? | exothermic: heat released melts sodium and ignites hydrogen from potassium |

Mixing up the first two rows is the single most common way to lose marks on this subtopic.

## Linked subtopics

- **9.1 Properties of metals** asks for the general reactions of metals with cold water; Group I metals are the examples that react fastest.
- **9.4 Reactivity series** places potassium above sodium, and both above calcium, at the top of the series. The order potassium > sodium > lithium agrees with the Group I trend.
- **8.1 Arrangement of elements**: all three have one outer electron, which is why they form 1+ ions and hydroxides with the same formula type.

## Predicting rubidium and caesium

| Property | Change it | Keep it |
|---|---|---|
| Melting point | Lower than the element above | — |
| Density | Higher than the element above (general trend) | — |
| Reactivity with water | More violent than the element above | Products: hydroxide + hydrogen |
| Ion and formulae | — | 1+ ion; MOH, MCl, M₂O |
| Solution formed | — | Alkaline |

Use both columns: what changes **and** what stays the same.

## Exam traps

- Products with water are the **hydroxide and hydrogen**, never the oxide and never oxygen.
- "Hydrogen is produced" is not an observation; "fizzing" or "bubbles" is.
- Only potassium gives a **lilac** flame in this reaction.
- Group I melting points **decrease** down the group; do not confuse with Group VII, where boiling points increase.
- Do not say all Group I metals float: rubidium and caesium are denser than water.
- Lithium does not melt on water; sodium and potassium do.
- Keep predictions in the right direction for **every** property asked, and give a reason each time.
- The formula of the hydroxide does not change down the group.

## Self-test

1. Name the three Group I metals the syllabus specifies.
2. State the trend in melting point down Group I.
3. Give two observations when sodium is added to cold water.
4. Write a balanced equation for the reaction of potassium with water.
5. Explain why the solution formed is alkaline.
6. State one observation that distinguishes potassium's reaction with water from sodium's.
7. Why are Group I metals stored under oil?
8. Predict the formula of caesium chloride.
9. Predict how rubidium reacts with water compared with potassium.
10. Lithium has a density of 0.53 g cm⁻³. State what you would see that depends on this when it is added to water.
11. Explain why sodium melts into a ball on water but lithium does not.
12. An unknown Group I metal melts at 39 °C. Is it above or below potassium in the group? Give a reason.

**Answers:** 1. Lithium, sodium, potassium. 2. Melting point decreases down the group. 3. Any two: floats; melts into a ball; moves around on the surface; fizzes; disappears. 4. 2K + 2H₂O → 2KOH + H₂. 5. A metal hydroxide (e.g. NaOH) forms and dissolves, giving hydroxide ions in solution. 6. A lilac flame (the hydrogen ignites); the reaction is more violent. 7. To stop them reacting with oxygen and water vapour in the air. 8. CsCl. 9. More violently (possibly explosively), forming rubidium hydroxide and hydrogen. 10. It floats. 11. Sodium's melting point (98 °C) is lower and its reaction releases heat faster, enough to melt it; lithium's melting point is higher (181 °C) and its reaction slower. 12. Below potassium, because its melting point is lower than potassium's (63 °C) and melting point decreases down the group.
