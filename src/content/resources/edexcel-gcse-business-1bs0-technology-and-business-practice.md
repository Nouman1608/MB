## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following is an example of a payment system? **[1]**

A: A video call with a supplier

B: A contactless card reader

C: A social media advert

D: An email newsletter

**2.** Which **two** of the following are areas of business activity that the specification says technology influences? **[2]**

A: Sales

B: Weather

C: Costs

D: Interest rates

E: Exchange rates

**3.** Define the term e-commerce. **[1]**

**4.** State **one** cost to a small business of selling through an online marketplace. **[1]**

**5.** Explain **one** way digital communication can reduce a business's costs. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **one** risk to a business of using social media. **[3]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** Hartley Hardware takes £12,000 a month in card payments. Its payment provider charges 1.6% of card sales plus £25 a month for the card reader.

**(a)** Calculate the total monthly cost of taking card payments. **[2]**

**(b)** A rival provider charges 1.9% with no rental fee. Calculate whether Hartley Hardware would save money by switching. **[2]**

**8.** Outline **one** way e-commerce could affect the price element of the marketing mix for a small electronics shop. **[2]**

**9.** Penny's Preserves sells jam at farmers' markets. Last year its sales revenue was £18,500. After opening a website, sales revenue rose to £24,050.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in sales revenue. **[2]**

**(b)** Outline **one** cost Penny's Preserves has taken on by selling online. **[2]**

**10.** Ridgeway Taxis is a small taxi firm that takes bookings by phone. Analyse the impact on Ridgeway Taxis of introducing a booking app with in-app payment. **[6]**

**11.** Explain **one** reason why a new technology may not give a business a long-term advantage over its rivals. **[3]**

**12.** Moss & Marlow is a gift shop with weekly sales revenue of £4,000. The owner is choosing between two uses of £2,400.

Option 1: build an e-commerce website. Forecast extra weekly online sales revenue £500, with extra costs of postage and packaging of 20% of online sales.

Option 2: spend the £2,400 on paid social media adverts over 12 weeks to bring more customers into the shop. Forecast extra weekly sales revenue in the shop £350 during the 12 weeks, with no extra delivery costs.

Justify which option the owner should choose. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** A [1]. C [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Buying and selling goods or services online [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Any one from: a fee on each sale; postage and packaging; the cost of handling returns [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Digital communication, such as video calls, lets staff meet suppliers or customers without travelling [1]. This saves fuel, train fares and travel time [1], so staff can spend more of their working day on productive tasks and costs fall [1]. (Alternatively: email and online documents reduce printing and postage.) **[3]**

**6.** Customers can post complaints or poor reviews publicly [1]. These can be seen and shared by many people very quickly [1], so one poor experience could damage the business's reputation and reduce sales [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** Fee = 1.6% × £12,000 = £192 [1]. Total = £192 + £25 = **£217** [1]

**(b)** Rival cost = 1.9% × £12,000 = **£228** [1]. This is £11 more than £217, so Hartley Hardware would **not** save money by switching [1] **[4]**

**8.** Customers can compare the shop's prices with large online retailers in seconds [1], so it may have to lower its prices on popular items to stay competitive, which could reduce its profit per sale [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** Increase = £24,050 − £18,500 = £5,550 [1]. £5,550 ÷ £18,500 × 100 = **30%** [1]

**(b)** Jam is heavy and in glass jars, so it needs careful packaging and postage [1], which adds a cost to every online order and may lead to breakages that must be refunded [1]. **[4]**

**10.** An app lets customers book a taxi at any time without phoning [1], which could increase bookings from younger customers who prefer apps [1]. In-app payment means drivers carry less cash, reducing the risk of theft and time spent handling money [1]. However, building or renting an app and paying a fee on each payment adds costs [1], so Ridgeway Taxis must win enough extra bookings to cover them [1]. Large taxi apps already exist, so Ridgeway may find it hard to attract customers to its own app unless it offers something different, such as lower prices or local knowledge [1]. **[6]**

**11.** Rivals can buy or copy the same technology [1]. Once they do, customers can get the same convenience from them [1], so the technology stops being a reason to choose the business, and any extra sales it brought may fall back [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Option 1: extra weekly revenue £500, costs 20% × £500 = £100, so £400 a week [1]. The £2,400 is recovered in £2,400 ÷ £400 = 6 weeks, and the website keeps earning after that [1].

Option 2: £350 extra a week for 12 weeks = £4,200 [1], which is £1,800 more than the £2,400 spent, but the extra sales stop when the adverts end [1].

Over 12 weeks Option 1 brings £400 × 12 = £4,800 after delivery costs, which is more than Option 2's £4,200 [1].

For Option 2: the adverts bring customers into the shop, where they may buy more items than planned [1], and there is no delivery or returns work.

For Option 1: the website reaches customers beyond the local area and lasts beyond 12 weeks [1], but online customers compare prices with large retailers, and the forecast of £500 a week is uncertain for a new site [1].

Judgement: the owner should choose Option 1, because it is forecast to bring more over 12 weeks and continues to earn after that [1]. This depends on the website reaching the forecast, and the figures exclude the cost of the stock sold, which applies to both options. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* Comparing both options over the same period (12 weeks) makes the comparison fair. Strong answers also notice which benefits last after the money is spent.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting the monthly rental in Question 7.
- Dividing by the new figure, not the old, in percentage-change calculations.
- Saying technology always cuts costs.
- Writing about apps in general in Question 10 rather than Ridgeway's phone bookings and drivers.
- Comparing options over different time periods in Question 12.
