Dynasties and rulers (750-1500) is world history topic 3 for Paper 2. Revise at least two rulers or dynasties from different regions under four themes: legitimacy, gaining and consolidating power, rulers and religion, and expansion, succession and decline.

Related: [Dynasties and rulers study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-world-history-dp-history-ib-dp-world-history-world-history-topics-3/) and [Dynasties and rulers practice questions](/resources/ib-ib-dp-world-history-dp-history-ib-dp-world-history-world-history-topics-3-practice/).

## Ruler profiles

**Charlemagne (r. 768-814), Franks: Europe**
- Gained: inherited with his brother Carloman (sole ruler from 771).
- Methods: annual campaigns; counts and *missi dominici*; capitularies; oaths of loyalty; patronage of learning (Alcuin).
- Religion: alliance with the papacy; imperial coronation by Leo III (800); forced conversion of the Saxons.
- Legacy: empire divided among his grandsons at Verdun (843).

**Song Taizu (r. 960-976), Song dynasty: Asia and Oceania**
- Gained: proclaimed emperor by his troops in 960.
- Methods: retired leading generals with rewards; civilian control of armies; expanded civil service examinations; centralised revenue.
- Legitimacy: Mandate of Heaven; reunification of most of China.
- Legacy: stable civilian government; recurring weakness against northern states such as the Liao, Xi Xia and Jin.

**William I (r. 1066-1087), England: Europe**
- Gained: conquest at Hastings (1066), with a claim to be Edward the Confessor's heir.
- Methods: castles; Harrying of the North (1069-1070); a new Norman aristocracy; Domesday survey (1086); the oath of Salisbury (1086).
- Religion: papal banner for the invasion; Lanfranc as Archbishop of Canterbury; reform of the English Church.
- Legacy: a strong, centralised monarchy; the Anglo-Norman realm.

**Mansa Musa (r. c.1312-c.1337), Mali: Africa and the Middle East**
- Gained: succeeded as *mansa* within the ruling dynasty.
- Methods: control of gold trade and trade towns, including Timbuktu and Gao; provincial governors.
- Religion: pilgrimage to Mecca (1324-1325); mosques and Islamic learning in Timbuktu.
- Legacy: Mali's fame across the Islamic world and Europe; later decline under weaker successors.

**The Hongwu Emperor (r. 1368-1398), Ming: Asia and Oceania**
- Gained: rose through the rebellions against the Mongol Yuan; founded the Ming (1368).
- Methods: abolished the chancellorship (1380); direct control of ministries; purges; the Embroidered Uniform Guard as secret police; household registration.
- Legitimacy: Mandate of Heaven; restoration of Chinese rule after the Mongols.
- Legacy: a highly centralised autocracy.

## Sources of legitimacy

| Source | Explanation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Divine sanction | Authority granted by God or Heaven | Mandate of Heaven; papal coronation of Charlemagne |
| Descent | Membership of a ruling lineage | Abbasid descent from al-Abbas |
| Designation | Named by a predecessor | William's claim through Edward the Confessor |
| Conquest | Victory as proof of right | Hastings (1066) |
| Election or acclamation | Chosen by an assembly or army | Song Taizu proclaimed by troops; Holy Roman Emperor chosen by princes |

## Methods of consolidation

| Method | Purpose | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Military force | Suppress rivals and rebels | Harrying of the North; Charlemagne's Saxon wars |
| Patronage and reward | Bind elites to the ruler | Land grants to Norman followers; honours for retired Song generals |
| Bureaucracy | Extend reach and gather revenue | Song examinations; *missi dominici*; Domesday |
| Law | Standardise rule and project justice | Capitularies; Ming legal code |
| Religion | Win acceptance and divine backing | Coronation in 800; Mansa Musa's pilgrimage |
| Terror and surveillance | Deter opposition | Hongwu purges |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Dynasty | A succession of rulers from the same family |
| Mandate of Heaven | Chinese idea that Heaven grants the right to rule to a just ruler and withdraws it from an unjust one |
| *Missi dominici* | "Envoys of the lord": Charlemagne's royal inspectors |
| Capitulary | A royal decree of the Carolingian rulers |
| Tenant-in-chief | A lord holding land directly from the king |
| Investiture Contest | Dispute over who should appoint bishops, the pope or secular rulers |
| Caliph | Successor of the Prophet as leader of the Muslim community |
| *Mansa* | Title of the ruler of Mali |
| Partible inheritance | Dividing lands among several heirs |

## Exam traps

- **Biography instead of analysis.** Organise by theme: legitimacy, methods, religion, decline.
- **Success without criteria.** Define success: security, expansion, stability, dynastic survival.
- **Personal power only.** Show the institutions and elites that rulers relied on.
- **Wrong period.** Mehmed II's reign extends to 1481, but Suleiman and Akbar are outside this topic.
- **Region rules.** William I and Charlemagne are both Europe.
- **Legend as fact.** Stories of Mansa Musa's gold or Charlemagne's learning need careful wording.

## Self-test

1. Who crowned Charlemagne emperor, and when?
   *Pope Leo III, on Christmas Day 800.*
2. What were the *missi dominici*?
   *Royal agents sent in pairs to inspect local government for Charlemagne.*
3. Which treaty divided the Carolingian empire in 843?
   *The Treaty of Verdun.*
4. How did Song Taizu reduce the threat from his generals?
   *He persuaded them to retire with rewards and placed the armies under civilian control.*
5. What was the Mandate of Heaven?
   *The idea that Heaven granted the right to rule to a just emperor and could withdraw it from an unjust one.*
6. What was the purpose of the Domesday survey of 1086?
   *To record landholding and resources, mainly for taxation and royal control.*
7. What was the Harrying of the North?
   *William I's devastation of northern England in 1069-1070 to crush rebellion.*
8. How did Mansa Musa's pilgrimage strengthen his rule?
   *It displayed Mali's wealth and linked his authority to the Islamic world, raising his prestige at home and abroad.*
9. Which office did the Hongwu Emperor abolish in 1380?
   *The chancellorship.*
10. What happened at Canossa in 1077?
    *Emperor Henry IV did penance before Pope Gregory VII during the Investiture Contest.*
11. Who took control of Baghdad in 945, and what did this mean for the Abbasid caliph?
    *The Buyids; the caliph kept religious authority but lost political power.*
12. Give one reason why succession was dangerous for medieval dynasties.
    *Disputes among heirs, or division of lands among sons, could break up a realm, as after Louis the Pious.*
