This guide covers outcome **1.3.7 Statements of profit or loss (and other comprehensive income) in columnar format to show departmental profits/losses**. It sits in Topic 1.3, Financial statements of organisations, in Unit 1 (The Accounting System and Costing) of the Pearson Edexcel International Advanced Subsidiary/Advanced Level in Accounting (YAC11), Specification Issue 2, September 2018. This is International AS content. All amounts are in dollars and every business named is invented.

Use it with the [financial statements of organisations revision notes](/resources/edexcel-ial-accounting-financial-statements-of-organisations-revision-notes/) and the [departmental records practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-accounting-yac11-departmental-records-practice/). The basic statement is taught in the [sole trader guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-accounting-yac11-financial-statements-of-sole-traders/), and the costing ideas behind apportionment are developed in the [overhead costs guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-accounting-yac11-overhead-costs/).

## What the specification asks for

| Outcome | Wording | What you must be able to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1.3.7 | Statements of profit or loss (and other comprehensive income) in columnar format to show departmental profits/losses | Prepare a columnar statement with one column per department and a total column; apportion shared costs on sensible bases; comment on departmental results |

A business with several departments, such as a shop selling clothing, homeware and electrical goods, needs to know which parts earn profit and which do not. A single statement of profit or loss hides this. A **columnar** statement places the departments side by side with a total column, and the total column equals the statement for the whole business.

## Allocation and apportionment

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Allocation** (direct) | A cost that belongs wholly to one department is charged to it in full | Wages of staff who work only in the electrical department |
| **Apportionment** | A cost shared by departments is divided between them on a fair basis | Rent of the whole building |

Choose a basis that reflects what causes the cost. These are the usual ones.

| Cost | Common basis |
|---|---|
| Rent, rates, insurance of premises, heating and lighting | Floor area (square metres) |
| Advertising, general administration | Revenue |
| Supervisory wages, canteen, staff costs shared | Number of employees |
| Insurance of inventory | Value of inventory held |
| Depreciation of shared equipment | Cost or book value of equipment used |

The question normally states the basis. If it does not, choose one and write a note saying which and why. The total of each apportioned cost across the columns must equal the original figure.

## The layout

Cost of sales is calculated for each department using that department's opening inventory, purchases and closing inventory. Gross profit is then found for each department. Expenses follow, with direct expenses first and apportioned expenses after. The final line is the profit or loss of each department, and the total column gives the profit for the year of the business as a whole.

## Worked example: Riverside Stores

Riverside Stores has three departments: Clothing, Homeware and Electrical. For the year ended 31 March 2026 the figures are:

| | Clothing | Homeware | Electrical |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | 96,000 | 64,000 | 40,000 |
| Opening inventory | 8,000 | 6,000 | 5,000 |
| Purchases | 52,000 | 35,000 | 27,000 |
| Closing inventory | 9,000 | 5,000 | 4,000 |
| Direct wages | 14,000 | 9,000 | 6,000 |
| Floor area (square metres) | 300 | 200 | 100 |

Shared expenses: rent 24,000 and lighting 7,200 (both by floor area); advertising 6,000 and administration 12,000 (both by revenue).

**Step 1: apportionment fractions.** Floor area is 300 : 200 : 100, a total of 600, so the shares are 1/2, 1/3 and 1/6. Revenue is 96,000 : 64,000 : 40,000, a total of 200,000, so the shares are 48%, 32% and 20%.

| Expense | Basis | Clothing | Homeware | Electrical | Total |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Rent | Floor area | 12,000 | 8,000 | 4,000 | 24,000 |
| Lighting | Floor area | 3,600 | 2,400 | 1,200 | 7,200 |
| Advertising | Revenue | 2,880 | 1,920 | 1,200 | 6,000 |
| Administration | Revenue | 5,760 | 3,840 | 2,400 | 12,000 |

**Step 2: the columnar statement**

| | Clothing | Homeware | Electrical | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | 96,000 | 64,000 | 40,000 | 200,000 |
| Opening inventory | 8,000 | 6,000 | 5,000 | 19,000 |
| Purchases | 52,000 | 35,000 | 27,000 | 114,000 |
| | 60,000 | 41,000 | 32,000 | 133,000 |
| Closing inventory | (9,000) | (5,000) | (4,000) | (18,000) |
| **Cost of sales** | (51,000) | (36,000) | (28,000) | (115,000) |
| **Gross profit** | 45,000 | 28,000 | 12,000 | 85,000 |
| Direct wages | 14,000 | 9,000 | 6,000 | 29,000 |
| Rent | 12,000 | 8,000 | 4,000 | 24,000 |
| Lighting | 3,600 | 2,400 | 1,200 | 7,200 |
| Advertising | 2,880 | 1,920 | 1,200 | 6,000 |
| Administration | 5,760 | 3,840 | 2,400 | 12,000 |
| **Total expenses** | (38,240) | (25,160) | (14,800) | (78,200) |
| **Profit/(loss) for the year** | **6,760** | **2,840** | **(2,800)** | **6,800** |

Check each column: Clothing 96,000 − 51,000 = 45,000, and 45,000 − 38,240 = 6,760. Homeware 64,000 − 36,000 = 28,000, and 28,000 − 25,160 = 2,840. Electrical 40,000 − 28,000 = 12,000, and 12,000 − 14,800 = −2,800. The profits add up to 6,760 + 2,840 − 2,800 = 6,800, the same as the total column.

## Interpreting the results

Gross profit as a percentage of revenue shows the trading margin of each department.

- Clothing: 45,000 ÷ 96,000 × 100 = 46.9%
- Homeware: 28,000 ÷ 64,000 × 100 = 43.8%
- Electrical: 12,000 ÷ 40,000 × 100 = 30.0%
- Whole business: 85,000 ÷ 200,000 × 100 = 42.5%

Electrical sells at a much lower margin and makes an overall loss of 2,800. A hasty reader would say it should close. The better answer uses **contribution**, which is gross profit less the costs that belong directly to the department.

| | Clothing | Homeware | Electrical | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Gross profit | 45,000 | 28,000 | 12,000 | 85,000 |
| Less direct wages | (14,000) | (9,000) | (6,000) | (29,000) |
| **Contribution to shared costs** | 31,000 | 19,000 | 6,000 | 56,000 |

The shared costs total 24,000 + 7,200 + 6,000 + 12,000 = 49,200, and 56,000 − 49,200 = 6,800, the profit for the year. Electrical contributes 6,000 towards costs that would continue anyway. If the building, advertising and administration cost the same without the department, closing it would reduce total profit from 6,800 to 800. The apparent loss comes from apportioned costs, not from the department's own trading.

This does not mean Electrical must stay. The decision should also consider whether it could be improved, whether the floor space could earn more elsewhere, whether Electrical brings customers who buy other goods, and whether any of the shared costs would genuinely fall.

## Limitations of departmental statements

- Apportionment is judgement. A different basis gives different departmental profits, but the total profit is unchanged.
- Departments depend on each other, for example when electrical customers also buy homeware.
- The statement looks backwards. It does not predict what happens if a department is closed or expanded.
- Costs that cannot sensibly be shared, such as loan interest, may be shown only in the total column.
- Non-financial factors, such as customer service or staff morale, are not shown.

## Common mistakes

- Using one set of inventory figures for the whole business instead of one set for each department.
- Apportioning a cost on a basis that does not reflect what causes it, such as rent by revenue.
- Apportioned figures that do not add up to the original total.
- Putting direct wages in the shared costs.
- Concluding that a department making a loss should always be closed, without considering contribution.
- Forgetting the total column, or a total column that does not match the sum of the departments.
- Calculating percentages on the total revenue instead of the revenue of the department.

## Exam technique

- **Prepare** means set out the columns clearly with a heading for each department and a total. Show the apportionment working in a separate table: it earns method marks.
- **Calculate** the apportioned figure by writing the fraction first (for example 24,000 × 300 ÷ 600).
- **Comment** needs a statement based on the figures and why it matters. "Electrical makes a loss, but contributes 6,000 to shared costs" is a comment. "Electrical makes a loss" is only a statement.
- **Evaluate** or **recommend** needs both sides, a decision, and a supported reason.
- Check that the departmental profits add up to the total profit before you move on.
