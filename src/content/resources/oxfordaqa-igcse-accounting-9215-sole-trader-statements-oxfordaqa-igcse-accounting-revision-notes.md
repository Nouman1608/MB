These notes cover the section 3.4 statements on preparing sole traders' financial statements "from ledger accounts" and "from a trial balance including adjustments", for service and trading businesses, and commenting on them. The full worked example is in the [sole trader statements study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-9215-sole-trader-statements-oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting/); practise with the [sole trader statements practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-9215-sole-trader-statements-oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-practice/). Figures are invented and in dollars.

## Income statement layout (trading business)

```
Revenue
  less returns inwards                       = net revenue
Opening inventory
  + purchases less returns outwards
  + carriage inwards
  − closing inventory                        = cost of sales
Net revenue − cost of sales                  = gross profit
  + other income (discount received, rent or commission received, profit on disposal)
  − expenses (including carriage outwards, discount allowed, depreciation)
                                             = profit for the year
```

A **service business** has no cost of sales: fees + other income − expenses = profit for the year.

## Statement of financial position layout

```
Non-current assets (cost − depreciation = NBV)
Current assets: inventory, trade receivables, other receivables, bank, cash
Current liabilities: trade payables, other payables, overdraft
Net current assets = current assets − current liabilities
Non-current liabilities: long-term loans
Net assets
Capital: opening capital + profit − drawings = closing capital (equals net assets)
```

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Returns inwards | Goods returned by customers; deducted from revenue |
| Returns outwards | Goods returned to suppliers; deducted from purchases |
| Carriage inwards | Delivery cost on goods bought; part of cost of sales |
| Carriage outwards | Delivery cost on goods sold; an expense |
| Discount allowed | Reduction given to customers for prompt payment; an expense |
| Discount received | Reduction from suppliers for prompt payment; income |
| Other receivables | Expenses prepaid and income due; current assets |
| Other payables | Expenses owing and income received in advance; current liabilities |
| Net current assets | Current assets less current liabilities |

## Adjustments in one table

| Adjustment | Income statement | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|
| Expense owing | Add to expense | Other payable |
| Expense prepaid | Deduct from expense | Other receivable |
| Income due | Add to income | Other receivable |
| Income received in advance | Deduct from income | Other payable |
| Loan interest owing | Rate × loan, less amount paid, added | Other payable |
| Depreciation | Expense | Add to provision; NBV falls |
| Closing inventory | Deducted in cost of sales | Current asset |

Every adjustment appears **twice**: once in each statement. If net assets do not equal closing capital, look for an adjustment entered only once.

## Closing ledger accounts

- Transfer the year's amount to the income statement.
- Carry down any prepayment or income due as a **debit** balance (an asset).
- Carry down any amount owing or received in advance as a **credit** balance (a liability).
- Transfer profit to the credit of the capital account and drawings to its debit.

## Mini example

Revenue 90,000; returns inwards 1,000; opening inventory 6,000; purchases 52,000; returns outwards 800; carriage inwards 600; closing inventory 7,300.

- Net revenue: 90,000 − 1,000 = 89,000.
- Cost of sales: 6,000 + (52,000 − 800) + 600 − 7,300 = 50,500.
- Gross profit: 89,000 − 50,500 = 38,500.

## Writing a comment

Use the frame **figure, comparison, meaning for the owner**:

- Gross margin = gross profit ÷ net revenue × 100. A low margin suggests low prices or high purchase costs.
- Profit ÷ revenue shows how much expenses absorb.
- Drawings compared with profit: if drawings are higher, capital is falling.
- Current assets compared with current liabilities, and how much of current assets is inventory.
- Long-term loans: no short-term pressure, but a fixed interest cost.

## Order of work in the exam

1. Read every adjustment and mark each trial balance line it affects, writing the adjusted figure beside it.
2. Calculate depreciation and any provisions before starting the statements.
3. Write the income statement, then the statement of financial position.
4. Check that net assets equal closing capital before moving on.

## Exam traps

- Returns inwards deducted from purchases instead of revenue.
- Carriage outwards placed in cost of sales.
- Loan interest taken only as the amount paid.
- Drawings shown as an expense.
- Income received in advance shown as a current asset.
- Statements without full headings, such as "Income statement for the year ended 31 March 2026".
- Closing inventory left out of current assets after being used in cost of sales.

## Self-test

1. Where are returns outwards shown?
2. Is carriage outwards part of cost of sales?
3. Calculate cost of sales: opening inventory 4,000; purchases 30,000; returns outwards 500; carriage inwards 300; closing inventory 5,200.
4. A loan of 20,000 at 6% a year; interest paid 700. How much is owing at the year end?
5. Rent received in the trial balance is 4,800, including 400 for next year. What figure goes in the income statement?
6. Which balance is carried down when an expense is prepaid, and on which side?
7. Opening capital 18,000, profit 9,500, drawings 11,000. Calculate closing capital.
8. Why does a service business have no gross profit?
9. Name two items added after gross profit.
10. Net assets do not equal closing capital. Give one likely cause.
11. Gross profit 24,000 on net revenue of 80,000. Calculate the gross margin.

**Answers**

1. Deducted from purchases in cost of sales.
2. No. It is an expense.
3. 4,000 + 29,500 + 300 − 5,200 = 28,600.
4. 6% × 20,000 = 1,200; owing 1,200 − 700 = 500.
5. 4,800 − 400 = 4,400.
6. The prepaid amount, as a debit balance (an other receivable).
7. 18,000 + 9,500 − 11,000 = 16,500.
8. It sells services, not goods, so there is no cost of goods sold to deduct.
9. Any two: discount received, rent received, commission received, profit on disposal.
10. An adjustment entered in one statement only (for example, an accrual added to the expense but not shown as an other payable).
11. 24,000 ÷ 80,000 × 100 = 30%.
