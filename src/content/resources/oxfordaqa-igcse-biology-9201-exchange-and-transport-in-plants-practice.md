These questions cover section **3.2.2 Exchange and transport in plants** of the OxfordAQA International GCSE
Biology (9201) specification, Version 5.2, for exams in May/June 2018 onwards. The whole section is Biology only
(marked B). All data are invented for practice. A calculator is useful for questions 7 to 10.

Learn the content first in the [Exchange and transport in plants study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-biology-9201-exchange-and-transport-in-plants/)
and the [Exchange and transport in plants revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-biology-9201-exchange-and-transport-in-plants-revision-notes/).
For mixed questions on 3.2.1 and 3.2.2, use the [Photosynthesis, limiting factors and transport in plants practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-biology-photosynthesis-limiting-factors-and-transport-in-plants-practice/).

## Section A

**1.**

**(a)** Name the cells that control the size of a stoma. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the process by which carbon dioxide moves into a leaf. **[1]**

**2.** Name the tissue that transports:

**(a)** sugars from the leaves to a potato tuber **[1]**
**(b)** mineral ions from the roots to the leaves. **[1]**

**3.** Describe what is meant by the transpiration stream. **[2]**

**4.** Explain how the air spaces inside a leaf help photosynthesis. **[2]**

**5.** On a hot afternoon, the stomata of a sunflower close.

**(a)** Explain how closing the stomata prevents wilting. **[2]**
**(b)** Give **one** disadvantage to the plant of closing its stomata. **[1]**

**6.** Name **two** types of region in a plant that receive dissolved sugars from the leaves. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Four similar leaves are hung up for 24 hours. Petroleum jelly is spread on some surfaces to stop water
passing through.

| Leaf | Surface covered | Mass at start (g) | Mass after 24 h (g) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | None | 1.80 | 1.17 |
| B | Upper only | 1.84 | 1.24 |
| C | Lower only | 1.76 | 1.69 |
| D | Both | 1.82 | 1.80 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage loss in mass for leaf A and for leaf C. Give each answer to 2 significant
figures. **[2]**
**(b)** What do the results show about where water vapour is lost from these leaves? Explain your answer. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why percentage loss is used rather than the loss in grams. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest why leaf D still lost a little mass. **[1]**

**8.** A student paints clear nail varnish on both surfaces of a leaf, peels it off and counts the stomata in one
field of view of area 0.20 mm². She counts 46 stomata on the lower surface and 6 on the upper surface.

**(a)** Calculate the number of stomata per mm² on each surface. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times more stomata there are per mm² on the lower surface. Give your answer to 2
significant figures. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest **one** advantage to the plant of having most stomata on the lower surface. **[1]**

**9.** Two celery stalks are stood in coloured water for 30 minutes. In a still room the dye rises 12 cm. Next
to a fan, the dye rises 21 cm.

**(a)** Calculate the speed of the dye in each stalk in cm per minute. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in speed caused by the fan. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the fan increases the speed of water movement in the xylem. **[2]**

**10.** Two groups of seedlings are transplanted into pots. In group X the root hairs are left undamaged; in
group Y most root hairs are torn off. Water uptake is measured over 4 hours: group X takes up 4.8 cm³ and group
Y takes up 1.2 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of water uptake in cm³ per hour for each group. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the difference between the groups. **[2]**

**11.** Compare the structure and function of xylem and phloem. **[4]**

**12.** On a hot, windy afternoon a plant wilts. By the next morning it has recovered. Describe and explain what
happens to the plant over this time. **[6]**

**13.** In bright light, a leaf takes in carbon dioxide and gives out oxygen through its stomata. Explain why
these gases move in these directions. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Guard cells [1]
**(b)** Diffusion [1]

**2. (a)** Phloem [1]
**(b)** Xylem [1]

**3.** The movement of water from the roots, up through the xylem [1]; and out of the leaves (as water vapour) [1]

**4.** They allow carbon dioxide to diffuse from the stomata to the cells inside the leaf [1]; they give a large
surface area (to volume ratio) so gas exchange with the cells is fast [1]

**5. (a)** Most water vapour is lost through the stomata [1]; closing them reduces water loss, so the plant does
not lose water faster than the roots replace it [1]
**(b)** Less carbon dioxide can enter, so photosynthesis slows [1]

**6.** Growing regions (such as shoot tips or young leaves) [1]; storage organs (such as tubers or roots) [1]

**7. (a)** A: (1.80 − 1.17) ÷ 1.80 × 100 = **35%** [1]; C: (1.76 − 1.69) ÷ 1.76 × 100 = **4.0%** [1]
**(b)** Most water vapour is lost from the lower surface [1]; because covering the lower surface cut the loss from
35% to 4.0%, while covering the upper surface had little effect, showing most stomata are on the lower surface
[1]
**(c)** The leaves had different starting masses, so percentages allow a fair comparison [1]
**(d)** Some water is lost through parts of the leaf not covered, such as the cut stalk, or through the jelly
layer if it is not complete [1]

**8. (a)** Lower: 46 ÷ 0.20 = **230 per mm²** [1]; upper: 6 ÷ 0.20 = **30 per mm²** [1]
**(b)** 230 ÷ 30 = **7.7 times** [1]
**(c)** The lower surface is shaded from direct sunlight and cooler, so less water evaporates through the stomata
[1]

**9. (a)** Still room: 12 ÷ 30 = **0.40 cm per minute** [1]; fan: 21 ÷ 30 = **0.70 cm per minute** [1]
**(b)** (0.70 − 0.40) ÷ 0.40 × 100 = **75%** [1]
**(c)** Wind blows water vapour away from the leaves, keeping the concentration gradient steep, so more water
evaporates [1]; more water is pulled up the xylem to replace it, so the transpiration stream is faster [1]

**10. (a)** X: 4.8 ÷ 4 = **1.2 cm³ per hour** [1]; Y: 1.2 ÷ 4 = **0.3 cm³ per hour** [1]
**(b)** Root hairs increase the surface area of the roots [1]; with fewer root hairs, group Y has a smaller surface
area, so it absorbs water more slowly [1]

**11.** Any four of the following, 1 mark each [4]: xylem carries water and mineral ions, whereas phloem carries
dissolved sugars; xylem carries material from the roots to the stem and leaves, whereas phloem carries sugars from
the leaves to the rest of the plant; xylem moves water in the transpiration stream, whereas phloem moves sugars by
translocation; xylem transport is upwards only, whereas translocation can be up or down; xylem is made of dead
hollow cells with no end walls, whereas phloem is made of living cells with pores in the end walls; xylem walls
are strengthened with lignin

**12.** Marking points, 1 mark each [6]:
- hot, windy conditions increase evaporation of water from the leaves
- the plant loses water through its stomata faster than the roots can replace it, so it wilts
- the guard cells lose water and the stomata close
- closing the stomata reduces water loss
- in the evening it is cooler and less windy, so less water evaporates
- the roots continue to absorb water, which moves up the xylem, so the cells regain water and the plant
  recovers

**13.** In bright light photosynthesis uses carbon dioxide, so its concentration inside the leaf is lower than
outside, and it diffuses in [1]; photosynthesis produces oxygen, so its concentration inside the leaf is higher
than outside, and it diffuses out [1]; the gases move through the open stomata down their concentration gradients
[1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Swapping the contents of xylem and phloem.
- Comparing raw mass losses when leaves have different starting masses.
- Saying stomata close "to save the plant" without linking to water loss.
- Forgetting the disadvantage of closed stomata for photosynthesis.
- Writing that leaves "breathe in" carbon dioxide.
- "Compare" answers that describe xylem and phloem separately instead of pairing points.
