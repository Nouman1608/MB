These notes condense content item 1.5.2, Technology and business, in Topic 1.5 (Understanding external influences on business) of the Pearson Edexcel GCSE (9-1) Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022. The [technology study guide](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-technology-and-business/) includes a worked cashless-payment example; test yourself with the [technology practice questions](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-technology-and-business-practice/). The wider [stakeholders and technology revision notes](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-stakeholders-and-technology-revision-notes/) cover 1.5.1 too.

## What the specification lists

- Different types of technology used by business: e-commerce, social media, digital communication, payment systems.
- How technology influences business activity in terms of: sales, costs, marketing mix.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| E-commerce | Buying and selling goods and services online |
| Online marketplace | A website where many sellers list products, usually paying a fee per sale |
| Social media | Online platforms where users share content; businesses post, reply and advertise |
| Digital communication | Electronic exchange of information, such as email, messaging and video calls |
| Payment systems | Electronic ways to pay and be paid: card readers, contactless, online checkouts, mobile payments |
| Transaction fee | A charge, often a percentage, taken by the payment provider on each payment |
| Cashless | Accepting only electronic payments |

## Effects grid

| Technology | Sales | Costs | Marketing mix |
|---|---|---|---|
| E-commerce | Wider market; sales 24 hours a day | Website, postage, packaging, returns, marketplace fees; maybe lower rent | Place (new channel); price (easy comparison) |
| Social media | Awareness; customers share posts | Staff time; paid adverts | Promotion; feedback for product |
| Digital communication | Quick replies turn enquiries into orders | Less printing, postage and travel | Promotion (emails); place (ordering by message) |
| Payment systems | Customers without cash can buy; shorter queues | Card reader rental; fee per transaction; less cash handling | Place (how the customer completes the purchase) |

## Short run v longer run

- Short run: set-up costs, training, teething problems.
- Longer run: lower running costs and wider sales, but rivals catch up.

## Benefits and drawbacks to weigh

**Benefits:** reach more customers; faster service; lower some costs; better information about customers.

**Drawbacks:** set-up and running costs; fees; staff time; system failures or security problems; online complaints spread fast; competitors use the same tools.

## Calculations to practise

- Fee = fee percentage × value of sales paid that way.
- Net saving = savings − extra costs.
- Percentage change in sales = (new − old) ÷ old × 100.

Example: online sales rise from £2,500 to £3,100 a month. Change = £600; £600 ÷ £2,500 × 100 = 24%.

## Exam traps

- Name the specific technology; "the internet" is too vague.
- Technology does not always reduce costs; mention fees and set-up costs.
- Social media is not free once staff time and adverts are counted.
- Link a technology to a named element of the mix, then follow the chain.
- A benefit that every rival also gains is not a lasting advantage.
- Use the figures in the source; compare costs with benefits.

## Worked reminder: Oriel Barbers adds online booking

Oriel Barbers pays £30 a month for an online booking and payment system. Customers pay when they book, at a fee of 2% of each payment. It takes 400 online bookings a month at £20.

| Step | Working | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Value of online bookings | 400 × £20 | £8,000 |
| Payment fees | 2% × £8,000 | £160 |
| Total monthly cost | £160 + £30 | £190 |
| Missed appointments before | 40 a month | Lost revenue £800 |
| Missed appointments after (paid in advance) | 10 a month | Lost revenue £200 |
| Saving from fewer missed appointments | £800 − £200 | £600 |
| Net gain | £600 − £190 | £410 a month |

The calculation shows technology affecting **sales** (fewer gaps in the diary), **costs** (fees and subscription) and the **marketing mix** (place: booking online at any time). It depends on customers being willing to pay in advance.

## Chains to learn

- E-commerce → customers anywhere → more sales → but postage and returns raise costs.
- Social media → cheap promotion → more awareness → but complaints spread fast.
- Digital communication → fewer printed letters and journeys → lower costs → but staff time is needed to reply quickly.
- Payment systems → quicker payment → shorter queues → more customers served → but a fee on each payment.

## Self-test

1. Name the four types of technology in the specification.
2. Name the three areas of business activity technology influences, according to the specification.
3. Give one way e-commerce can increase sales.
4. Give two costs of selling through e-commerce.
5. Which element of the marketing mix does social media mainly affect?
6. Give one risk of social media for a business.
7. Give one way digital communication can reduce costs.
8. Give one benefit to a business of accepting contactless payments.
9. A shop takes £8,000 a month in card payments at a fee of 1.75%. Calculate the monthly fee.
10. Why might going cashless reduce a café's sales?
11. Explain why the benefit of a new technology may not last.
12. Give one way e-commerce affects price.

### Answers

1. E-commerce, social media, digital communication, payment systems.
2. Sales, costs, marketing mix.
3. Any one: customers outside the local area can buy; orders can be taken at any time.
4. Any two: website and hosting; postage; packaging; returns; marketplace fees.
5. Promotion.
6. Any one: complaints or bad reviews are seen widely and quickly; takes staff time.
7. Any one: less printing and postage; fewer journeys to meetings.
8. Any one: faster payment and shorter queues; customers without cash can still buy; less cash to count and bank.
9. 1.75% × £8,000 = £140.
10. Customers who prefer or only have cash may buy elsewhere.
11. Rivals can adopt the same technology, so it stops being a point of difference.
12. Customers compare prices quickly online, so the business may have to keep prices competitive.
