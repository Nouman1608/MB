This guide covers section 1.01, Calculations with integers, of the OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560 specification, Version 2.1 (August 2026), for first assessment in 2017. The statement sits in the first column of the specification, so it is content for **both tiers**. You need it most on the non-calculator papers (Paper 2 for Foundation, Paper 5 for Higher), and it underpins multi-step questions on every paper.

## What the specification asks

Section 1.01a, Four rules, asks you to "use non-calculator methods to calculate the sum, difference, product and quotient of positive and negative whole numbers".

Three words in that statement matter:

- **Non-calculator methods.** You need a written method you can carry out reliably by hand, and you must show it.
- **Sum, difference, product and quotient.** These are the results of adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing. A question may use these words instead of the symbols.
- **Positive and negative whole numbers.** Every method must also work when one or both numbers are negative.

| Word | Operation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Sum | Addition | The sum of −8 and 13 is 5 |
| Difference | Subtraction | The difference between 21 and −4 is 25 |
| Product | Multiplication | The product of −6 and 9 is −54 |
| Quotient | Division | The quotient of −72 and −8 is 9 |

## Adding and subtracting directed numbers

A directed number has a sign that shows its direction from zero. Picture a horizontal number line.

- Adding a positive number moves right. Subtracting a positive number moves left.
- Subtracting a negative number is the reverse of subtracting a positive one, so it moves right. That is why −(−6) has the same effect as +6.
- Adding a negative number moves left, the same as subtracting.

**Worked example 1.** Work out −14 + 9 − (−6) − 11.

```
−14 + 9     = −5      (move 9 right from −14)
−5 − (−6)   = −5 + 6 = 1
1 − 11      = −10
```

The answer is **−10**. Work strictly from left to right and rewrite each double sign before you move.

**Differences in context.** "The difference between" two values is the distance between them on the number line. A freezer is at −18 °C and the kitchen is at 21 °C. The difference is 21 − (−18) = 21 + 18 = **39 degrees**. Counting through zero gives the same answer: 18 degrees up to 0, then 21 more.

## Multiplying and dividing directed numbers

The sign rules follow from patterns. Multiply −3 by 2, 1, 0, −1, −2: the answers are −6, −3, 0, then must continue to 3 and 6, because each step adds 3. So a negative times a negative is positive.

| Signs | Product or quotient |
|---|---|
| Same signs (+ and +, or − and −) | Positive |
| Different signs | Negative |

Division follows the same rule because it undoes multiplication: (−252) ÷ (−14) = 18, since 18 × (−14) = −252.

With more than two numbers, count the negatives. An even number of negative factors gives a positive result; an odd number gives a negative result.

```
(−3) × (−2) × (−5) × 4
three negatives (odd), so the answer is negative
3 × 2 × 5 × 4 = 120
answer: −120
```

Decide the sign first, then calculate with the sizes alone. This keeps the written method clean.

## Long multiplication: two layouts

Both layouts below are acceptable non-calculator methods. Pick one and use it every time.

**Worked example 2 (grid method).** Work out 476 × 38.

```
 ×   |  400  |  70  |  6
 30  | 12000 | 2100 | 180   → 14 280
  8  |  3200 |  560 |  48   →  3 808
                         total 18 088
```

**The same calculation in columns.**

```
      476
   ×   38
   ------
     3808     (476 × 8)
    14280     (476 × 30: write the 0, then 476 × 3)
   ------
    18088
```

The answer is **18 088**. A quick estimate, 500 × 40 = 20 000, confirms the size. The zero in the second row is the place-value step that is most often forgotten: without it you would add 1 428 instead of 14 280.

For (−476) × 38, decide the sign first (different signs, so negative) and reuse the work: the answer is −18 088.

## Long division with a running remainder

**Worked example 3.** Work out 9 342 ÷ 18.

```
18 into 93:   5 × 18 = 90,   remainder 3
bring down 4: 34
18 into 34:   1 × 18 = 18,   remainder 16
bring down 2: 162
18 into 162:  9 × 18 = 162,  remainder 0
answer: 519
```

Writing out the 18 times table first (18, 36, 54, 72, 90, 108, 126, 144, 162) saves time and errors. Check by multiplying back: 519 × 18 = 9 000 + 342 = 9 342.

**Short division** works the same way when the divisor is a single digit. For 3 984 ÷ 8, carry the remainders: 39 ÷ 8 = 4 remainder 7, 78 ÷ 8 = 9 remainder 6, 64 ÷ 8 = 8, giving **498**.

## Remainders in context

When a division does not come out exactly, the context decides what to do with the remainder.

| Question | Calculation | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 230 students travel in coaches with 52 seats. How many coaches are needed? | 230 ÷ 52 = 4 remainder 22 | **5 coaches** (round up: 22 students still need seats) |
| 1 000 eggs are packed in boxes of 24. How many full boxes? | 1 000 ÷ 24 = 41 remainder 16 | **41 boxes** (round down: 16 eggs do not fill a box) |
| How many eggs are left over? | 1 000 − 41 × 24 = 1 000 − 984 | **16 eggs** |

Write a short sentence giving the answer in the context's own words. A bare "4.4" or "4 r 22" does not answer "how many coaches".

## Multi-step problems with negatives

**Worked example 4.** A bank balance is −£135 (overdrawn). The owner pays in £210, then spends £96. Find the new balance.

```
−135 + 210 = 75
75 − 96    = −21
```

The balance is **−£21**, so the account is overdrawn by £21.

**Worked example 5.** A club records its profit each month for five months: −£48, £125, −£67, −£32, £7. Find the total and the mean monthly profit.

```
total: −48 + 125 = 77
       77 − 67   = 10
       10 − 32   = −22
       −22 + 7   = −15
mean:  −15 ÷ 5   = −3
```

The total is **−£15** and the mean is **−£3** a month, a loss. Grouping the positives and negatives separately is a useful check: positives 125 + 7 = 132, negatives 48 + 67 + 32 = 147, and 132 − 147 = −15.

## Common mistakes

- Reading −5 − (−6) as −11. Two adjacent minus signs become a plus.
- Applying "two negatives make a positive" to addition: −4 + (−7) is −11, not 11. The rule is for multiplying and dividing.
- Forgetting the place-value zero in the second row of a column multiplication.
- Losing a remainder in long division because the next digit was brought down too early.
- Rounding a remainder the wrong way in context: coaches round up, full boxes round down.
- Giving the sign of a product of several numbers from the last pair only, instead of counting all the negatives.

## Exam technique

- **"Work out"** or **"Calculate"** on a non-calculator paper: show the written method. If the final answer is wrong, method marks can only be given for working you have written down.
- **"Show that"**: the answer is given, so the marks are for the steps. Write every line, including the multiplying back or the final comparison.
- **Context questions**: finish with a sentence that uses the units and words of the question, such as "5 coaches are needed".
- Check a product with an estimate and a quotient by multiplying back. Both take seconds and catch most slips.
