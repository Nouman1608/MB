## The hierarchy, fixed in order

Learn the four sources as an ordered hierarchy, not a flat list —
most exam questions on this section reward being able to explain why
one source outranks another:

1. **Qur'an** — the primary, directly revealed source.
2. **Hadith and Sunnah** — record the Prophet's (pbuh) words and
practice, explaining and applying the Qur'an's guidance in practice.
3. **Ijma (consensus)** and **Qiyas (analogy)** — secondary sources
scholars use only where the Qur'an and Hadith give no explicit ruling.

**Why this order matters for exam answers:** a question that asks "why
is the Hadith needed alongside the Qur'an" is really asking you to
explain position 2's role relative to position 1 — the Qur'an gives
commands, but Hadith supplies the practical detail of how to carry them
out.

## The Qur'an: three separate stages

Do not blur these together — they are frequently tested as distinct
steps:

| Stage | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Revelation** | How the Qur'an was received |
| **Preservation** | How it was memorised and recorded |
| **Compilation** | How it was assembled into a single text |

A strong answer names which of the three stages a question is actually
asking about, rather than describing the Qur'an's history as one
undifferentiated process.

## Hadith reliability: two tests, not one

The four Hadith categories — **Sahih** (sound), **Hasan** (good),
**Daeef** (weak), **Maudu** (fabricated) — are assessed using two
separate checks:

- **Isnad** — the chain of narrators transmitting the Hadith back to the
Prophet (pbuh).
- **Matn** — the actual text/content of the Hadith itself.

**Common confusion to avoid:** Isnad and Matn test different things —
a Hadith can have a strong chain of narrators (Isnad) but still raise
questions about its content (Matn), or vice versa. Conflating the two
into one generic "reliability check" loses marks.

## Ijma and Qiyas — know one example mechanism each

- **Ijma (consensus)** — scholars agree on a ruling where the Qur'an and
Hadith are silent.
- **Qiyas (analogy)** — a new situation is reasoned about by comparison
to a similar situation already addressed in the Qur'an or Hadith.

Both exist specifically to extend guidance to situations the primary
sources do not explicitly cover — state this purpose explicitly rather
than just naming the two terms.

## The point-evidence-explain-link structure

For "explain the importance of ___" questions (a recurring format in
this section), use:

1. **Point** — state the claim.
2. **Evidence** — a specific example (e.g. Salah or Zakah as Qur'anic
commands).
3. **Explain** — how the Hadith supplies the practical detail the
Qur'an does not.
4. **Link** — back to the source's position in the hierarchy.

## Sahih Bukhari and Sahih Muslim — one fact each

Do not name these two major Hadith collections without being able to
say something specific about their significance as compilations — a
bare name-drop earns little on its own; a factual point about what
makes each collection significant does.

## Exam traps

- Treating Ijma and Qiyas as equal in authority to the Qur'an and
Hadith, rather than as secondary, situation-filling sources.
- Confusing Isnad (chain of narrators) with Matn (the text itself).
- Describing the Qur'an's history as one process rather than three
distinct stages (revelation, preservation, compilation).
- Naming Sahih Bukhari or Sahih Muslim with no further detail.
- Skipping the "link back to the hierarchy" step in an "explain the
importance" answer.

## Self-test

1. Place the four sources of authority in order and explain why Ijma
and Qiyas sit below the Qur'an and Hadith.
2. Name the Qur'an's three distinct historical stages.
3. What do Isnad and Matn each test?
4. Name the four Hadith reliability categories.
5. What is the point-evidence-explain-link structure used for?

**Answers:** 1. Qur'an, then Hadith/Sunnah, then Ijma and Qiyas; Ijma
and Qiyas are secondary sources used by scholars only where the Qur'an
and Hadith give no explicit ruling, so they rank below both. 2.
Revelation, preservation, compilation. 3. Isnad tests the chain of
narrators transmitting a Hadith back to the Prophet (pbuh); Matn tests
the content of the Hadith text itself. 4. Sahih, Hasan, Daeef, Maudu.
5. Answering "explain the importance of ___" questions: state the
point, give specific evidence, explain the mechanism, then link back to
the source's position in the hierarchy of authority.
