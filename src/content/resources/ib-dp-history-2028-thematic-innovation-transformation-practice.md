These tasks follow the paper 2 structure for the thematic study of innovation and transformation: a concept question (6 marks), a short response (4 marks) and an essay (15 marks). The study prescribes no examples, so the suggested answers are only one route. Any accurate and relevant examples earn credit.

## Section A: concepts

**1.** Using one example from your thematic study, analyse the concept of significance. **[6]**

**2.** Using one example from your thematic study, analyse the concept of continuity and change. **[6]**

## Section B: short responses

**3.** Explain how one innovation was resisted by established authorities. **[4]**

**4.** Explain the experiences of women in one innovation or period of transformation you have studied. **[4]**

## Section B: essays

**5.** To what extent did innovations transform the societies in which they emerged? Use examples from at least two regions. **[15]**

**6.** Discuss the view that resistance to innovation was always unsuccessful. Use examples from at least two regions. **[15]**

## Answers

**1.** Marking points (6). Credit:
- a definition: value judgements about people and events, with the question of which stories should be told **[1]**;
- a specific example, such as the printing press in Europe, with detail (Gutenberg, about 1450) **[1]**;
- analysis of significance against criteria: scale (spread of books across Europe), depth (changes to religion and education, as in the Reformation from 1517), duration (long-term effects on literacy) **[2]**;
- recognition that significance is constructed, for example that historians have different views, or that what the innovation reveals (about trade networks and literacy) also matters **[1]**;
- a judgement **[1]**.
Bands: 1 to 2 describes the concept with minimal analysis; 3 to 4 partial analysis with a relevant example; 5 to 6 clear and accurate analysis supported by a specific example.

**2.** Marking points (6). Credit:
- a definition: societies becoming different while keeping similarity across time and place **[1]**;
- a specific example, for example Meiji Japan **[1]**;
- change: railways, factories, conscription, the end of samurai privileges **[1 to 2]**;
- continuity: the emperor as source of legitimacy, many rural customs and family structures **[1 to 2]**;
- recognition of pace or of who experienced it positively or negatively, and a judgement **[1]**.

**3.** Marking points (4). Award:
- identification of a relevant example, such as the control of printing in early modern Europe **[1]**;
- accurate description of the authorities' action, such as licensing, censorship, or listing forbidden books **[1]**;
- explanation of the reasons, such as fear that print would spread challenges to religious or political authority **[1]**;
- explanation of the effect or limit of the action, for example that books still crossed borders and the spread of print continued **[1]**.

**4.** Marking points (4). Award:
- identification of a relevant example **[1]**;
- description of women's role, for example as mill workers in the early cotton industry in Britain or at Lowell in the United States **[1]**;
- explanation of the experience, for example long hours, low pay and independence from the family **[1]**;
- explanation of a limit or change, for example that women were paid less than men and excluded from skilled and managerial work **[1]**.

**5.** Essay plan with marking points. A 13 to 15 answer will:
- state a thesis, for example that innovations transformed economies and cultures deeply but unevenly, with continuities for many people **[2]**;
- use two or more examples from at least two regions, such as the British Industrial Revolution (Europe) and the Green Revolution in South Asia or the Abbasid Caliphate (Asia or the Middle East) **[3]**;
- analyse transformation across the lines of inquiry (economic, political, environmental, cultural change) with specific evidence **[4]**;
- connect the examples: similarities (new classes, new inequalities), differences (state-led compared with market-led), and exceptions or limits (regions little affected) **[4]**;
- reach a reasoned judgement **[2]**.

**6.** Essay plan with marking points. A 13 to 15 answer will:
- state a thesis, for example that resistance rarely stopped innovation, but it could shape its pace, form and regulation **[2]**;
- use two or more examples from at least two regions: for example the Luddites (Europe) and an authority's restrictions on printing or other innovations elsewhere, with careful statements where historians disagree **[3]**;
- analyse forms of resistance (authorities, popular, traditional ideas, competing innovations) with evidence **[4]**;
- connect the examples and consider cases where resistance had effects, such as factory legislation after campaigns **[4]**;
- reach a reasoned judgement on the word "always" **[2]**.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use an innovation that is not technological?** Yes. The guide includes intellectual, cultural, social and religious innovations.

**How do I show connections between examples?** Compare them directly: say what is similar, what is different, and what the comparison tells you about the question.
