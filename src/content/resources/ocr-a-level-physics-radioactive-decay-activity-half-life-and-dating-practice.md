## Questions

**1.** State what is meant by saying that radioactive decay is (i) spontaneous and (ii) random. **[2]**

**2.** **(a)** State what an α-particle is and name a material that stops it. **[2]**
**(b)** State how the penetration of β-radiation differs from that of γ-radiation. **[2]**

**3.** Complete each decay equation by giving the missing particle or nuclide.
**(a)** ²¹⁰₈₄Po → ²⁰⁶₈₂Pb + ? **[1]**
**(b)** ³²₁₅P → ? + ⁰₋₁e + ? **[2]**
**(c)** ¹¹₆C → ¹¹₅B + ? + ? **[2]**

**4.** Describe an experiment to show that a β-source is absorbed by aluminium sheets of increasing thickness. Include the measurement of background and two safety precautions. **[6]**

**5.** A sample contains 2.0 mg of an isotope of molar mass 90 g mol⁻¹. Its activity is 1.0 × 10¹⁰ Bq.
**(a)** Determine how many nuclei the sample contains. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the decay constant, in s⁻¹. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the half-life, in years. **[2]**

**6.** A source has an initial activity of 3.2 × 10⁶ Bq and a half-life of 4.0 years.
**(a)** Calculate the decay constant, in year⁻¹. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the activity after 10 years. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the time for the activity to fall to 4.0 × 10⁵ Bq. **[2]**

**7.** A class simulates decay with 150 dice. After each throw, every die showing a 4 is removed.
**(a)** Calculate the expected number of dice left after four throws. **[2]**
**(b)** Show that the half-life of this model is about 3.8 throws. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why a class's results will not match the expected values exactly. **[1]**

**8.** A spreadsheet models ΔN = −λNΔt with N₀ = 8000, λ = 0.020 s⁻¹ and Δt = 5.0 s.
**(a)** Write the formula for the value in a row in terms of the value in the row above, and calculate N at 5.0 s and 10.0 s. **[3]**
**(b)** The exact value at 10.0 s from N = N₀e^(−λt) is 6550. State why the model gives a different value. **[1]**
**(c)** State one change that makes the model more accurate. **[1]**

**9.** Corrected count rate C (after subtracting background) from a protactinium source is recorded.

| t / s | 0 | 30 | 60 | 90 | 120 | 150 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C / s⁻¹ | 160 | 121 | 92 | 69 | 52 | 40 |

**(a)** Explain why a graph of ln C against t is plotted. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the gradient of the line using the first and last points. **[2]**
**(c)** Determine λ and the half-life. **[2]**
**(d)** State one reason why the plotted points do not lie exactly on a line. **[1]**

**10.** Living wood gives 0.230 Bq per gram of carbon. A bone sample gives 0.092 Bq per gram. Take the half-life of carbon-14 as 5730 years.
**(a)** Calculate the decay constant, in year⁻¹. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the age of the bone. **[2]**
**(c)** State two assumptions made in the calculation. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why the method is not suitable for a sample about 200 000 years old. **[1]**

**11.** The activity of a source is 4.8 × 10⁵ Bq at 09:00 and 1.5 × 10⁵ Bq at 15:00 on the same day. Background has been subtracted.
**(a)** Calculate the decay constant, in h⁻¹, and the half-life. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of undecayed nuclei at 09:00. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the time after 09:00 at which the activity is 1.0 × 10⁴ Bq. **[2]**
**(d)** A technician suggests heating the source to make it decay faster. Evaluate the suggestion. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** (i) Decay happens without any external cause or trigger, and is unaffected by conditions such as temperature [1]. (ii) It is impossible to predict when a given nucleus will decay; every undecayed nucleus has the same fixed chance of decaying in each second [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers tend to gain credit when each word is defined separately, so avoid giving a single sentence that blends the two.

**2. (a)** An α-particle is a helium nucleus (two protons and two neutrons) [1]. A sheet of paper, or a few centimetres of air, stops it [1].
**(b)** β is stopped by a few millimetres of aluminium [1], whereas γ is only reduced in intensity by thick lead and is never stopped completely [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming a material together with an approximate thickness makes a penetration comparison much stronger than "β goes further than α".

**3. (a)** ⁴₂α, a helium nucleus [1].
**(b)** The nuclide is ³²₁₆S [1] and the other particle is an antineutrino, ν̄ [1].
**(c)** The particle is a positron ⁰₊₁e [1] with a neutrino ν [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Check each line by adding the nucleon numbers and the proton numbers on both sides; omitting the neutrino or antineutrino is a common way to lose credit.

**4.** Measure the background count with no source nearby over a long time, such as five minutes, and find the rate [1]. Place the source and GM tube a fixed distance apart [1]. Put one aluminium sheet between them, count for a fixed time such as 60 s, and record the thickness with a micrometer [1]. Add more sheets and repeat [1]. Subtract the background rate from each reading and plot corrected count rate against thickness, which should fall to the background level at a definite thickness [1]. Safety: handle the source with tongs, keep it stored when not in use and never point it at anyone [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A description is easier to credit when it names what is kept constant and what is varied; "measure the count" alone leaves the method incomplete.

**5. (a)** Moles = 2.0 × 10⁻³ / 90 = 2.22 × 10⁻⁵ mol [1]; N = 2.22 × 10⁻⁵ × 6.02 × 10²³ = **1.3 × 10¹⁹** [1].
**(b)** λ = A/N = 1.0 × 10¹⁰ / 1.34 × 10¹⁹ = **7.5 × 10⁻¹⁰ s⁻¹** [1].
**(c)** t½ = 0.693 / 7.48 × 10⁻¹⁰ = 9.27 × 10⁸ s [1]; dividing by 3.16 × 10⁷ gives **29 years** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Carry unrounded values between parts, and convert the mass to grams or the molar mass to kilograms consistently.

**6. (a)** λ = ln 2 / 4.0 = **0.173 year⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** A = 3.2 × 10⁶ × e^(−0.173 × 10) [1] = **5.7 × 10⁵ Bq** [1].
**(c)** The ratio 3.2 × 10⁶ / 4.0 × 10⁵ = 8, which is three half-lives [1], so t = **12 years** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Spotting a whole number of half-lives gives a quick route and a check on the logarithm method.

**7. (a)** The chance a die survives a throw is 5/6 [1], so expected number = 150 × (5/6)⁴ = **72** [1].
**(b)** (5/6)ⁿ = e^(−λn) gives λ = ln(6/5) = 0.182 per throw [1]; t½ = ln 2 / 0.182 = **3.8 throws** [1].
**(c)** Each die is removed at random, so real counts scatter about the expected values [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In a "show that" answer, writing out the intermediate value of λ is what lets a reader follow the route to the given result.

**8. (a)** N(new) = N(old) − 0.020 × N(old) × 5.0 [1]; N = **7200** at 5.0 s [1] and **6480** at 10.0 s [1].
**(b)** The model uses the number at the start of each step for the whole step, so it removes too many nuclei [1].
**(c)** Use a smaller time step [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A reason for the difference is stronger when it refers to the way the step is calculated, not to "rounding" or "error".

**9. (a)** Since ln C = ln C₀ − λt, a straight line confirms exponential decay and its gradient gives −λ [1].
**(b)** ln 160 = 5.075 and ln 40 = 3.689 [1]; gradient = (3.689 − 5.075) / 150 = **−0.0092 s⁻¹** [1].
**(c)** λ = **0.0092 s⁻¹** [1]; t½ = 0.693 / 0.00924 = **75 s** [1].
**(d)** Decay is random, so the counts fluctuate [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Reading the gradient from the ends of the line, as here, reduces the effect of any one scattered point.

**10. (a)** λ = ln 2 / 5730 = **1.21 × 10⁻⁴ year⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** t = ln(0.230 / 0.092) / λ = 0.916 / 1.21 × 10⁻⁴ [1] = **7600 years** [1].
**(c)** The ratio of carbon-14 in the atmosphere was the same in the past as now [1]; the sample has not been contaminated by other carbon [1].
**(d)** Too few carbon-14 nuclei remain, so the activity cannot be distinguished from background [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the age to the precision supported by the data, which here is two significant figures.

**11. (a)** λ = ln(4.8 × 10⁵ / 1.5 × 10⁵) / 6.0 [1] = **0.194 h⁻¹** [1]; t½ = 0.693 / 0.194 = **3.6 h** [1].
**(b)** λ = 0.194 / 3600 = 5.38 × 10⁻⁵ s⁻¹ [1]; N = A/λ [1] = 4.8 × 10⁵ / 5.38 × 10⁻⁵ = **8.9 × 10⁹** [1].
**(c)** t = ln(4.8 × 10⁵ / 1.0 × 10⁴) / 0.194 = ln 48 / 0.194 [1] = **20 h**, which is about 05:00 the next day [1].
**(d)** Heating would not help: decay is spontaneous, so temperature does not change the decay rate [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* When a calculation moves between hours and seconds, stating the unit of λ at each step makes slips easy to spot.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Failing to subtract background before using a count rate.
- Using minutes or years for t with λ in s⁻¹.
- Leaving the minus sign out of the exponent.
- Leaving the neutrino or antineutrino out of a β equation.
- Describing γ as having a fixed range.
- Giving "careful" instead of a specific safety precaution.
- Rounding λ early and carrying an error into a later part.
- Describing the dice model but not saying what is varied or counted.
