Condensed recall for **8.2 Changing populations and places** (Unit 1: Changing population). For
the full explanation, read the
[Population Distribution study guide](/resources/ib-dp-geography-population-distribution/).

## Two components of population change

- **Natural change** = births minus deaths.
- **Net migration** = immigration minus emigration.

State them separately. Illustration: 120 births and 80 deaths gives +40; 30 immigrants and 50
emigrants gives -20; total change is +20.

## Reading a population pyramid

| Feature | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Wide base | High birth rate |
| Sharply narrowing top | High death rate and lower life expectancy |
| Relatively wide top | Ageing population with high life expectancy |
| Narrow base | Low (and likely falling) birth rate |
| Unusually narrow or wide band | A historical event affecting that cohort: war, a baby boom or mass migration |

A pyramid with a narrow base, wide middle and wide top indicates an **ageing population** at a later
stage of the demographic transition.

## Method for a data-response answer

1. Describe the shape first: base, middle, top, any odd bands.
2. Quote figures or age groups with units.
3. Explain with named factors (birth rate, life expectancy, migration, events).
4. Comment on consequences for the place, such as demand for services.

Paper questions often use unseen data, so practise reading unfamiliar pyramids under timed
conditions instead of memorising a few examples.

## Exam traps

- Describing the pyramid as "bad" or "good" instead of interpreting it.
- Assuming a narrow band always means war; it could be migration or low births.
- Forgetting that migration changes the shape of a pyramid for a particular place.

## Self-test

1. Calculate natural change from 120 births and 80 deaths.
2. What does a wide base indicate?
3. What does a relatively wide top indicate?
4. What might an unusually narrow band show?
5. Describe the pyramid of an ageing population.

**Answers:** 1. +40. 2. A high birth rate. 3. An ageing population with high life expectancy. 4. A
historical event affecting that cohort, such as war or mass migration. 5. Narrow base, wide middle and
relatively wide top.
