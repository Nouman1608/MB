This guide covers subtopic **30.1, Arenes**, from Topic 30, Hydrocarbons, of Cambridge International AS & A
Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027 series. It is A Level content. The combined page
[Arenes and Halogenoarenes: Electrophilic Substitution and Reactivity](/resources/a-arenes-and-halogenoarenes/)
sets out benzene's reactions and the nitration mechanism. This page concentrates on what the syllabus
adds with **methylbenzene**, on the **bromination** mechanism, and on using **directing effects** to plan the
order of steps in a synthesis, which is where most marks on this subtopic are won or lost. Afterwards, use the
[arenes revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-chemistry-9701-a-arenes-revision-notes/) and the
[arenes practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-chemistry-9701-a-arenes-practice/).

## What the syllabus asks for

1. describe the chemistry of arenes as exemplified by the following reactions of **benzene and
   methylbenzene**: (a) substitution with Cl₂ and Br₂ with an AlCl₃ or AlBr₃ catalyst; (b) nitration with
   concentrated HNO₃ and concentrated H₂SO₄ at 25–60 °C; (c) Friedel–Crafts alkylation by CH₃Cl and AlCl₃ and
   heat; (d) Friedel–Crafts acylation by CH₃COCl and AlCl₃ and heat; (e) complete oxidation of the side-chain
   using hot alkaline KMnO₄ and then dilute acid to give a benzoic acid; (f) hydrogenation of the ring using
   H₂ and a Pt or Ni catalyst and heat to form a cyclohexane ring;
2. describe the **mechanism of electrophilic substitution**, exemplified by the formation of **nitrobenzene
   and bromobenzene**, and explain the predominance of substitution over addition using delocalisation;
3. **predict whether halogenation occurs in the side-chain or the ring** depending on conditions;
4. describe how different substituents **direct** to different ring positions (limited to –NH₂, –OH, –R,
   –NO₂, –COOH and –COR).

## Benzene and methylbenzene side by side

The methyl group changes two things: it makes the ring **more reactive** and it **directs** incoming groups to
the 2- and 4-positions. It also adds a side-chain that can react on its own.

| Reaction and conditions | Benzene gives | Methylbenzene gives |
|---|---|---|
| Cl₂, AlCl₃ | chlorobenzene | 2-chloromethylbenzene + 4-chloromethylbenzene |
| Br₂, AlBr₃ | bromobenzene | 2-bromomethylbenzene + 4-bromomethylbenzene |
| conc. HNO₃ + conc. H₂SO₄, 25–60 °C | nitrobenzene | 2-nitromethylbenzene + 4-nitromethylbenzene |
| CH₃Cl, AlCl₃, heat | methylbenzene | new –CH₃ group mainly at the 2- and 4-positions |
| CH₃COCl, AlCl₃, heat | phenylethanone | –COCH₃ group mainly at the 2- and 4-positions |
| hot alkaline KMnO₄, then dilute acid | no reaction (no side-chain) | benzoic acid |
| H₂, Pt or Ni, heat | cyclohexane | methylcyclohexane |
| Cl₂, UV light, no catalyst | — | (chloromethyl)benzene, C₆H₅CH₂Cl |

Two details worth knowing:

- **Alkylation can continue.** The alkyl group added in Friedel–Crafts alkylation activates the ring, so the
  product reacts further and a mixture of polysubstituted products can form.
- **Acylation stops after one substitution.** The –COR group deactivates the ring, so the ketone formed is less
  reactive than the starting arene.

## Side-chain oxidation

Hot alkaline KMnO₄ oxidises an alkyl side-chain attached to a benzene ring all the way to a –COO⁻ group. Adding
dilute acid afterwards converts the benzoate ion into benzoic acid. The purple manganate(VII) is reduced to a
brown precipitate of manganese(IV) oxide under alkaline conditions.

    C₆H₅CH₃ + 3[O] → C₆H₅COOH + H₂O

The ring itself is not oxidised. Whatever the length of the side-chain, the ring keeps **one carbon** of it,
as –COOH. So ethylbenzene also gives benzoic acid, and 1,4-dimethylbenzene gives benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid,
HOOCC₆H₄COOH.

## Hydrogenation

    C₆H₆ + 3H₂ → C₆H₁₂          (H₂, Pt or Ni catalyst, heat)

This is the one addition reaction of the ring in the syllabus. It needs a catalyst and heat because the
delocalised system must be destroyed. Every carbon becomes sp³, and the product ring is no longer planar.

## Mechanism: bromination of benzene

**Worked example 1: an annotated model answer.**

*Describe the mechanism for the reaction of benzene with bromine in the presence of AlBr₃.*

> **Electrophile:** Br₂ + AlBr₃ → Br⁺ + AlBr₄⁻ **[equation generating the electrophile]**
>
> **Step 1:** a curly arrow from the delocalised π system of the ring to Br⁺ **[arrow starts at the ring]**. An
> intermediate forms in which the carbon attacked carries both H and Br; the positive charge is spread over the
> other five carbons, drawn as a partial ring with a + sign **[intermediate correct]**.
>
> **Step 2:** a curly arrow from the C–H bond into the ring **[arrow from the C–H bond]**. H⁺ is lost and the
> delocalised ring is restored, giving bromobenzene **[product and H⁺]**.
>
> **Catalyst regenerated:** H⁺ + AlBr₄⁻ → HBr + AlBr₃ **[catalyst shown as regenerated]**

Overall: C₆H₆ + Br₂ → C₆H₅Br + HBr. Nitration follows the same two steps with NO₂⁺ as the electrophile.

**Why not addition?** Addition would leave a product with no delocalised ring, losing the extra stability that
the ring provides. Substitution disrupts delocalisation only in the intermediate and then restores it, so it
is energetically favoured.

## Ring or side-chain?

| Conditions | Mechanism | Where Cl or Br goes | Product from methylbenzene |
|---|---|---|---|
| halogen + AlCl₃ / AlBr₃, no UV | electrophilic substitution | ring, positions 2 and 4 | 2- and 4-chloromethylbenzene |
| halogen + UV light, no catalyst | free-radical substitution | side-chain CH₃ | (chloromethyl)benzene |

With excess chlorine in UV light, further side-chain hydrogens are replaced: C₆H₅CHCl₂ and then C₆H₅CCl₃ form.

## Directing effects

| Substituent already on the ring | Directs the next group to |
|---|---|
| –NH₂, –OH, –R (alkyl) | 2- and 4- (and 6-) positions |
| –NO₂, –COOH, –COR | 3- (and 5-) position |

–NH₂, –OH and –R increase the electron density of the ring and make it more reactive; –NO₂, –COOH and –COR
withdraw electron density and make it less reactive.

## Worked example 2: choosing the order of steps

*Starting from methylbenzene, plan two-step syntheses of (a) 4-nitrobenzoic acid and (b) 3-nitrobenzoic acid.*

**(a) 4-nitrobenzoic acid: nitrate first, then oxidise.**

1. Concentrated HNO₃ and concentrated H₂SO₄, 25–60 °C. The –CH₃ group directs to the 2- and 4-positions,
   giving 4-nitromethylbenzene (separated from the 2-isomer).
2. Hot alkaline KMnO₄, then dilute acid. The –CH₃ is oxidised to –COOH; the –NO₂ is unchanged.

**(b) 3-nitrobenzoic acid: oxidise first, then nitrate.**

1. Hot alkaline KMnO₄, then dilute acid, giving benzoic acid.
2. Concentrated HNO₃ and concentrated H₂SO₄. The –COOH group directs to the 3-position, giving
   3-nitrobenzoic acid.

The same two reactions in a different order give a different isomer. In any planning question, ask: **which
group is on the ring when the new group goes on, and where does that group direct?**

## Common mistakes

- **Leaving out the catalyst** for halogenation, or using UV light when ring substitution is wanted.
- **Quoting 3-substitution for methylbenzene**: –CH₃ directs 2 and 4.
- **Forgetting "then dilute acid"** after alkaline KMnO₄; without it you have the benzoate salt.
- **Writing a longer acid** from ethylbenzene oxidation: it gives benzoic acid.
- **Starting the step 1 curly arrow at a carbon atom** instead of the delocalised ring.
- **Omitting H⁺ loss** or catalyst regeneration in the mechanism.
- **Planning a synthesis in the wrong order**, so the first group directs the second to the wrong position.

## Exam technique for Cambridge command words

- **State the reagents and conditions**: give the reagent, catalyst and temperature or "heat", and
  "concentrated" or "dilute" where it matters.
- **Describe the mechanism**: electrophile equation, two curly-arrow steps, intermediate, products, catalyst
  regeneration.
- **Predict** the product: identify the directing group first, then give the positions.
- **Suggest a synthesis**: number the steps, give reagents and conditions for each, and name each intermediate.
- **Explain** substitution over addition: refer to the delocalised π system being restored.
