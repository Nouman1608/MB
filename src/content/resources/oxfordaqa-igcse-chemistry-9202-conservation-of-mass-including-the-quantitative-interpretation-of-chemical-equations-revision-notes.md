## Five things to know

1. Reactions can be written as **word equations** or **balanced symbol equations** (3.6.1 a).
2. **State symbols**: (s) solid, (l) liquid, (g) gas, (aq) dissolved in water (3.6.1 b).
3. No atoms are made or lost, so **mass of products = mass of reactants** (3.6.1 c).
4. Masses can be calculated from other masses or from a balanced equation (3.6.1 d).
5. The calculated mass of product is not always obtained: **reversible** reactions, **losses during separation**, **side reactions** (3.6.1 e).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Word equation | Names of reactants and products joined by an arrow |
| Symbol equation | Formulae of reactants and products |
| Balanced | Same number of atoms of each element on both sides |
| Coefficient | Number in front of a formula, multiplying the whole formula |
| Subscript | Small number inside a formula; never changed when balancing |
| Closed system | Nothing can enter or leave, so total mass stays constant |
| Open system | Gases can escape or enter, so measured mass can change |
| Aqueous | Dissolved in water |

## Balancing in four moves

1. Correct formulae first.
2. Balance an element found in just one substance per side.
3. Leave single elements (O₂, H₂, Cl₂) until last.
4. Recount everything.

Practice set with answers:

- Al + O₂ → Al₂O₃ becomes **4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃**
- CH₄ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O becomes **CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O**
- Fe + Cl₂ → FeCl₃ becomes **2Fe + 3Cl₂ → 2FeCl₃**
- C₂H₆ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O becomes **2C₂H₆ + 7O₂ → 4CO₂ + 6H₂O**

If a balance needs a half (such as 3½O₂), double every coefficient.

## State symbol checklist

- Water formed in a reaction: **(l)**.
- Acids, alkalis, salts in solution: **(aq)**.
- Precipitates, metals, insoluble oxides, carbonates: **(s)**.
- Hydrogen, oxygen, carbon dioxide, chlorine, ammonia at room temperature: **(g)**.

## When does measured mass change?

| Mass reading | Cause | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Decreases | A gaseous product escapes | Carbonate + acid in an open flask; heating a carbonate |
| Increases | A gas from the air joins the product | Metal heated in air forms an oxide |
| Constant | Nothing crosses the boundary | Precipitation; any reaction in a sealed container |

The change in mass equals the mass of gas that left or joined.

## Reacting-mass method

1. moles = mass ÷ Mᵣ for the substance you know.
2. Multiply by the ratio of coefficients (wanted ÷ known).
3. mass = moles × Mᵣ for the substance you want.

Shortcut for large masses: mass ratio from the equation, then scale. For 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO, 48 g of Mg makes 80 g of MgO, so 4.8 tonnes of Mg makes 8.0 tonnes of MgO.

Quick check: total mass of reactants used must equal total mass of products.

## Three reasons for less product (3.6.1 e)

| Reason | Key phrase to use |
|---|---|
| Reversible reaction | "does not go to completion" |
| Separation losses | "some product lost when separated from the reaction mixture" |
| Side reactions | "some reactants react in a different, unexpected way" |

## Exam traps

- Changing a subscript to balance.
- Writing H₂O(aq).
- Saying mass is "destroyed" or "used up" when a gas escapes.
- Forgetting the coefficient when finding moles of a product (using 1 : 1 when the ratio is 1 : 2).
- Adding masses of reactants that were in excess, when only the mass that reacted counts.
- Giving "the reaction was too slow" as a reason for less product; it is not one of the three.

## Quick self-test

1. Balance: Na + Cl₂ → NaCl
2. Balance: Fe₂O₃ + CO → Fe + CO₂
3. Give the state symbol for magnesium chloride dissolved in water.
4. Give the state symbol for barium sulfate precipitated from solution.
5. 6.20 g of copper carbonate is heated and 3.99 g of solid remains. What mass of carbon dioxide escaped?
6. Explain why magnesium ribbon gains mass when heated in a crucible with the lid lifted.
7. A reaction takes place in a sealed flask. What happens to the total mass?
8. In 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, how many moles of water form from 3 mol of hydrogen?
9. 4.0 g of hydrogen burns completely. What mass of water forms? (Aᵣ: H = 1, O = 16)
10. State the three reasons why the calculated mass of product may not be obtained.
11. Why does burning a hydrocarbon in limited air give less carbon dioxide than calculated?
12. In 4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃, what mass of aluminium oxide forms from 10.8 g of aluminium? (Aᵣ: Al = 27, O = 16)

### Answers

1. 2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl
2. Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂
3. (aq)
4. (s)
5. 6.20 − 3.99 = 2.21 g
6. Oxygen from the air combines with the magnesium to form magnesium oxide, so the solid gains the mass of that oxygen.
7. It stays the same.
8. 3 mol (ratio H₂ : H₂O = 1 : 1).
9. 4.0 ÷ 2 = 2.0 mol H₂ → 2.0 mol H₂O → 2.0 × 18 = 36 g.
10. The reaction is reversible; product is lost during separation; reactants react in different, unexpected ways.
11. Some carbon reacts in a different way, forming carbon monoxide (or carbon) instead of carbon dioxide.
12. 10.8 ÷ 27 = 0.400 mol Al → 0.200 mol Al₂O₃ → 0.200 × 102 = 20.4 g.
