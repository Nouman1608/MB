This page is a guide to AQA GCSE English Literature (8702) past papers for the June 2026, June 2025 and June 2024 series. It gives the grade thresholds, copied from AQA's grade-boundary documents, and a short account of what examiners reported, written by us from AQA's Reports on the Examination. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on AQA's own site, linked below.

Use the three documents together. Sit a paper first, under timed conditions and closed book, as in the real exam. Mark it with the mark scheme, which shows what each level of response needs. Then read the report on the examination for that paper: it explains why answers that looked reasonable still sat in a lower level. The notes for each series below are a quick way into those reports. Which questions you can attempt depends on the texts you studied, so only use the sections that match your set texts.

Check which specification a paper was set on. 8702 is a linear, untiered, closed-book qualification, first taught from September 2015 and first examined in June 2017. The two papers have unequal weight: Paper 1 (Shakespeare and the 19th-century novel) is 1 hour 45 minutes, 64 marks and 40 per cent; Paper 2 (Modern texts and poetry) is 2 hours 15 minutes, 96 marks and 60 per cent. All the series on this page were set on this specification.

## June 2025

Both June 2025 reports are warm about the standard of preparation. Examiners praised secure knowledge of the texts, answers that begin with a clear line of argument, and good timekeeping on the longer paper.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (8702/1): confusing a question that asks how a writer presents something with one that asks how far, which weakened the argument that followed. Pre-prepared paragraphs, often picked up from social media, were fitted into essays whatever the question asked. A rise in critical theory, especially Freudian readings, was often attached to the answer in a way that got in the way of showing understanding of the text. Some candidates spent far longer on one section than the other, although the two essays carry equal marks.
- Paper 2 (8702/2): the same bolt-on use of critical theory, more often than not clumsy and unconnected to a moment in the text. Technical terms were named without any comment on their effect. The unseen poetry question that asked for a single-poem response was a highlight, but the comparison question that followed did not always keep up that standard.

**What stronger answers did:** moved between the extract and the rest of the text, signposting the move simply, kept the opening argument in view throughout, wrote about characterisation and stagecraft as well as imagery, and gave personal, well-supported readings.

## June 2024

The June 2024 reports describe fluent, well-structured answers and effective use of time, and the Paper 2 report says candidates completed all sections of that long paper more often than in any earlier year.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (8702/1): rigid paragraph formulas such as PEE and its variants, which made answers repetitive. Subject terms were sometimes named without being used, and some references were chosen because the candidate had learned them, not because they suited the question. Lengthy biography or history was now much less common, but the strongest answers still chose only the context that served the question.
- Paper 2 (8702/2): pre-taught structures and learned introductions that stopped candidates engaging with the meaning of the text, and memorised phrases about genre that were not then explored. Answering Section B or C first often led to a briefer, weaker answer on the modern text. Some political ideas were misunderstood, some contextual points were tacked on from the writer's biography instead of the themes, and long answers on the modern text left too little time for the poetry.

**What stronger answers did:** opened with a thesis and returned to it in the conclusion, treated methods as anything the writer does to make meaning (setting, relationship, symbol and structure as well as word choice), and took the themes and ideas of the question as their context.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find AQA 8702 past papers?**
AQA publishes question papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination on the assessment-resources page for 8702, and its past-paper finder lists papers by series.

**Why do the two papers carry different weights?**
Paper 1 is worth 64 marks (40 per cent) and Paper 2 is worth 96 marks (60 per cent). The subject threshold is set on the total of the two.

**Should I use a fixed essay structure?**
The June 2024 reports say rigid acronym structures can make answers repetitive and that a clear thesis, followed by an argument driven by the question, works better.

**Is critical theory expected at GCSE?**
The June 2025 reports describe a rise in candidates adding theory, especially Freudian readings, and say it was often a bolt-on that hindered the answer. They suggest it helps only when it serves the point being made.

**Can grade thresholds tell me what mark I need next time?**
No. Thresholds change every series and should only be used as a rough guide for the series they belong to, never to predict future ones.
