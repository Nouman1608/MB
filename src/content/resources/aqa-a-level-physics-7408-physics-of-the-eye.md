## Lens basics with the "real is positive" convention

A **converging** lens brings parallel rays to a real **principal focus**; a **diverging** lens spreads them so they appear to come from a virtual principal focus. The **focal length** f is the distance from the lens to the principal focus, and the **power** is P = 1/f, in dioptres (D) when f is in metres.

The lens equation is 1/u + 1/v = 1/f. In the "real is positive" convention used here, distances to real objects and real images are positive, distances to virtual images are negative, and a diverging lens has a negative focal length (negative power). Magnification is m = v/u; use the magnitudes for size, and the sign of v to say whether the image is real or virtual.

**Worked example 1: three images.**

```
Converging, f = +0.10 m, object at 0.15 m:
  1/v = 1/0.10 − 1/0.15  →  v = +0.30 m (real, inverted), m = 0.30/0.15 = 2.0
Converging, f = +0.10 m, object at 0.060 m (inside f):
  1/v = 1/0.10 − 1/0.060  →  v = −0.15 m (virtual, upright), m = 2.5
Diverging, f = −0.20 m, object at 0.30 m:
  1/v = −1/0.20 − 1/0.30  →  v = −0.12 m (virtual, upright), m = 0.40
```

A diverging lens always gives a virtual, upright, diminished image of a real object.

## The eye as an optical system

Light is refracted at the cornea, which provides most of the eye's converging power, and then by the lens. A real, inverted image forms on the retina. The ciliary muscles change the shape of the lens, and so its power, to keep the image focused on the retina as the object distance changes; this is accommodation.

For ray diagrams and calculations the eye is modelled as a **single converging lens** a fixed distance in front of the retina. The **far point** is the furthest point that can be focused (infinity for a normal eye) and the **near point** is the closest (taken as 0.25 m for a normal eye).

**Worked example 2: a model eye.** In a simplified model the lens is 0.020 m from the retina.

```
Object at infinity:  P = 1/u + 1/v = 0 + 1/0.020 = 50 D
Object at 0.25 m:    P = 1/0.25 + 1/0.020 = 4 + 50 = 54 D
```

The eye must be able to add 4 D of power to focus from infinity to the near point.

**Image size on the retina.** A 10 m tree 50 m away gives an image of height 10 × 0.020/50 = 4.0 × 10⁻³ m, because m = v/u = 0.020/50.

## Sensitivity and spectral response

The retina contains two kinds of photoreceptor.

- **Rods** are very sensitive and work in dim light. There is one type, so rods give no colour information. They are spread across the retina but absent from the centre of the fovea.
- **Cones** need brighter light. There are three types, with peak sensitivities in different parts of the spectrum (roughly blue, green and red, near 420 nm, 530 nm and 560 nm). Comparing their responses gives colour vision. The fovea is almost entirely cones.

As a photodetector, the eye responds roughly from 400 nm to 700 nm. Its **spectral response** curve shows sensitivity against wavelength: the rods' curve peaks at a shorter wavelength than the overall daylight response of the cones. In dim light, when only rods operate, colour cannot be distinguished.

## Spatial resolution

Spatial resolution is the ability to see two close objects as separate. Each cone in the fovea has its own connection to the optic nerve, so neighbouring cones send separate signals. Two point images are seen as separate if they fall on two cones with at least one unstimulated cone between them.

Many rods share a single nerve fibre. If two images fall on rods connected to the same fibre, the brain receives one signal and the objects cannot be distinguished. So:

- resolution is best when the image falls on the **fovea in bright light** (cones, individual connections);
- resolution is poorer in the **periphery** and in **dim light** (rods, shared connections), even though sensitivity is higher there.

If a question gives a cone spacing s and an eye length L, the smallest separation on the retina is about 2s (one unstimulated cone between), so the minimum angular separation is about 2s/L.

## Defects of vision and correction

**Myopia (short sight).** The far point is closer than infinity: the eye is too powerful for distant objects, which focus in front of the retina. Correct with a **diverging** lens that forms a virtual image of a distant object at the person's far point.

**Worked example 3.** Far point 0.50 m; uncorrected near point 0.12 m.

```
u = ∞, v = −0.50 m:  P = 1/f = 0 + 1/(−0.50) = −2.0 D
With the lens, the near point moves out. Image at v = −0.12 m:
  1/u = P − 1/v = −2.0 + 8.33 = 6.33  →  u = 0.16 m
```

The glasses let the person see distant objects clearly, but the closest object they can now focus on is 0.16 m away instead of 0.12 m.

**Hypermetropia (long sight).** The near point is further than 0.25 m: the eye is not powerful enough for near objects, whose images would form behind the retina. Correct with a **converging** lens that forms a virtual image, at the person's near point, of an object 0.25 m away.

**Worked example 4.** Near point 1.2 m.

```
u = 0.25 m, v = −1.2 m:  P = 1/0.25 + 1/(−1.2) = 4.00 − 0.83 = +3.2 D
```

**Ray diagrams.** For myopia, draw parallel rays meeting in front of the retina, then add a diverging lens that makes them diverge as if from the far point so the eye focuses them on the retina. For hypermetropia, draw rays from a near object meeting behind the retina, then add a converging lens that makes them diverge less, as if from the near point.

**Astigmatism.** The cornea (or lens) has different curvatures, and so different powers, in different planes, so lines in one orientation are focused while lines at right angles are blurred. It is corrected with a **cylindrical** lens, which has power in one plane only. A prescription for astigmatism therefore gives three values: a **spherical** power in dioptres, a **cylindrical** power in dioptres, and an **axis** in degrees (between 0° and 180°) giving the orientation of the cylinder. A question will show the layout it uses.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting the minus sign for a virtual image or for a diverging lens.
- Giving power in dioptres with f in centimetres.
- Correcting myopia with a converging lens, or describing the image as "on the retina" rather than at the far point.
- Using 0.25 m as v rather than u in hypermetropia calculations.
- Saying rods give better resolution because they are more sensitive.
- Describing astigmatism as blurred vision at all distances without mentioning orientation.

## Exam technique for AQA command words

- **Calculate** (corrective power): write u and v with signs before substituting.
- **Explain** (resolution): mention individual versus shared nerve connections and where cones and rods are found.
- **Draw** (correction): show where rays focus without the lens, then with it, with arrows.
- **State** (prescription): name all three parts and their units.
