## Section A

**1.** Name the enzyme, the substrate and the products for amylase, protease and lipase. **[6]**

**2.** State where bile is made, where it is stored, and give **two** of its functions. **[4]**

## Section B

**3.** Describe the path of a red blood cell from the vena cava to the aorta, naming each chamber and vessel in order. **[5]**

**4.** Compare arteries, veins and capillaries in terms of wall thickness, lumen size and the presence of valves. **[6]**

**5.** The alveoli are adapted for gas exchange.

**(a)** Give **three** adaptations and explain how each increases the rate of diffusion. **[6]**
**(b)** Explain why a person with damaged alveoli becomes breathless during exercise. **[3]**

**6.** Explain how water moves from the soil to the leaf, naming the tissue involved and the process that drives it. **[4]**

---

## Answers

**1. Amylase** — starch → maltose (simple sugars) [1] [1]. **Protease** — protein → amino acids [1] [1]. **Lipase** — lipids → fatty acids and glycerol [1] [1].

**2.** Made in the **liver** [1]; stored in the **gall bladder** [1]. It **neutralises stomach acid**, providing the alkaline pH that intestinal enzymes need [1]; it **emulsifies fats into small droplets**, increasing the surface area for lipase to act on [1].

**3.** Vena cava → **right atrium** [1] → right ventricle → **pulmonary artery** [1] → lungs → **pulmonary vein** [1] → **left atrium** [1] → left ventricle → **aorta** [1].

**4. Arteries** — **thick, muscular and elastic walls** to withstand high pressure [1]; **narrow lumen** [1]; no valves except at the heart.
**Veins** — **thin walls**, as the pressure is low [1]; **wide lumen** [1]; **valves present** to prevent backflow [1].
**Capillaries** — walls **one cell thick** for short diffusion distance [1]; very narrow lumen; no valves.

**5. (a)** There are **millions of alveoli, giving a very large surface area** [1] so more gas diffuses at once [1]. The wall is **one cell thick** [1], giving a very short diffusion distance [1]. They have a **rich blood supply** [1], which maintains a steep concentration gradient by constantly removing oxygen and delivering carbon dioxide [1]. (Also: moist lining so gases dissolve.)
**(b)** The **surface area for gas exchange is reduced** [1], so **less oxygen diffuses into the blood** [1]; during exercise the muscles demand more oxygen, so the person breathes faster and deeper but still cannot meet the demand [1].

**6.** Water is absorbed by **root hair cells by osmosis** [1] and travels up the **xylem** [1]. Water **evaporates from the leaf and diffuses out through the stomata — transpiration** [1], creating a **tension that pulls the continuous column of water up**, aided by cohesion between water molecules [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying bile digests fat — it emulsifies it.
- Getting the heart chambers in the wrong order.
- Listing alveolar adaptations without explaining the effect on diffusion.
- Saying water is pushed up the xylem.
