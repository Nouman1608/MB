## What the syllabus asks

1. The use and purpose of a bank statement.
2. Updating the cash book for bank charges, bank interest paid and received,
correction of errors, credit transfers, direct debits and standing orders.
3. Preparing a bank reconciliation statement with bank errors, uncredited
deposits and unpresented cheques.
4. The impact of digital business transactions on the process.

## Core sequence

**Compare → Update the cash book → Reconcile → Use the updated cash book
figure in the statement of financial position.**

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Bank statement | The bank's record of the account: receipts, payments and balance |
| Unpresented cheque | Cheque paid out and entered in the cash book but not yet paid by the bank |
| Uncredited deposit | Money paid in and entered in the cash book but not yet shown on the statement |
| Credit transfer | Money paid directly into the account by another party |
| Standing order | Instruction to the bank to pay a fixed sum at regular intervals |
| Direct debit | Authority for a supplier to collect amounts, which may vary, from the account |
| Bank charges | Fees taken by the bank for running the account |
| Dishonoured cheque | Cheque received and paid in that the payer's bank refuses to pay |
| Bank reconciliation statement | Statement explaining the difference between the cash book and bank statement balances at a date |

## Sorting the differences

**Update the cash book (debit side, money in):**

- Credit transfers received
- Bank interest received
- Cash book errors that understated receipts or overstated payments

**Update the cash book (credit side, money out):**

- Bank charges
- Bank interest paid
- Standing orders and direct debits
- Dishonoured cheques
- Cash book errors that overstated receipts or understated payments

**Bank reconciliation statement only:**

- Unpresented cheques
- Uncredited deposits
- Errors made by the bank

## Layouts

**Starting from the bank statement balance**

```
Balance as per bank statement                X
Add  Uncredited deposits                     X
Add  Bank errors that understated balance    X
Less Unpresented cheques                    (X)
Balance as per updated cash book             X
```

**Starting from the updated cash book balance**

```
Balance as per updated cash book             X
Add  Unpresented cheques                     X
Less Uncredited deposits                    (X)
Balance as per bank statement                X
```

Overdrafts: write them in brackets and keep the same rules. Adding to an
overdraft in brackets makes it smaller.

## Direction rules

- Bank statement: **Cr** = in funds; **Dr** or **OD** = overdrawn.
- Cash book: **Dr** balance = in funds; **Cr** balance = overdrawn.
- The figure for the statement of financial position is always the
**updated cash book** balance.

## Purposes of reconciliation

- Explains the difference between the two balances.
- Confirms the cash book figure before statements are prepared.
- Finds errors by the business or the bank.
- Helps to detect fraud and unauthorised payments.
- Identifies cheques that are not being presented.

## Digital transactions: effects

- Fewer unpresented cheques and uncredited deposits.
- More items reach the statement before the cash book: direct debits, card
fees, transfers from customers.
- Online banking allows frequent reconciliation.
- Software can match bank transactions automatically; unmatched items still
need human review.
- Errors or unauthorised payments can be found quickly if reconciliation is
done often.

## Exam traps

- Bank charges, direct debits and standing orders go in the **cash book**,
not the reconciliation statement.
- Cash book errors are corrected by the **difference** only.
- Start from the side the question names, and end on the other balance.
- A balance labelled "Dr" on a bank statement is an overdraft.
- Do not use the original cash book balance once it has been updated.
- Date the statement "at" a single date.

## Quick self-test

1. State one purpose of a bank statement for a business.
2. Name **two** items that are entered in the cash book after comparing it
with the bank statement.
3. Define "unpresented cheque".
4. A bank statement shows "1,450 Dr". What does this mean?
5. Starting from the bank statement balance, is an uncredited deposit added
or deducted?
6. Starting from the cash book balance, is an unpresented cheque added or
deducted?
7. A cheque received for $275 was entered in the cash book as $257. How is
the cash book corrected?
8. A customer's cheque of $300 paid in was dishonoured. Which side of the
cash book is it entered on?
9. Bank statement balance $2,800 credit; unpresented cheques $940;
uncredited deposit $360. Calculate the cash book balance.
10. Updated cash book overdraft $500; unpresented cheques $700. What does the
bank statement show?
11. State **one** effect of increased use of online payments on bank
reconciliation.
12. Which balance appears in the statement of financial position?

### Answers

1. Any one: to check the cash book; to find items the bank has processed but
the business has not recorded; as a source document.
2. Any two: bank charges; interest paid or received; credit transfers;
direct debits; standing orders; dishonoured cheques; correction of cash
book errors.
3. A cheque that has been paid out and entered in the cash book but has not
yet been paid by the bank, so it is not on the statement.
4. The account is overdrawn by $1,450.
5. Added.
6. Added.
7. Debit the cash book with the $18 difference.
8. The credit side (money out), reinstating the debt.
9. $2,800 + $360 − $940 = $2,220 debit (in funds).
10. −$500 + $700 = $200 in funds: a credit balance of $200 on the bank
statement.
11. Any one: fewer unpresented cheques; more items appear on the statement
first; reconciliation can be done more often; software can match items
automatically.
12. The updated cash book balance.
