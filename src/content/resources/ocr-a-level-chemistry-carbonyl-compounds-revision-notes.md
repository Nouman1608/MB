## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Carbonyl group | C=O; polar, with C δ+ and O δ− |
| Aldehyde | Carbonyl carbon bonded to at least one H: –CHO, at the end of a chain |
| Ketone | Carbonyl carbon bonded to two carbons: –CO– inside a chain |
| Nucleophile | Electron-pair donor, e.g. H⁻ or CN⁻ |
| Nucleophilic addition | A nucleophile attacks the δ+ carbon and a group adds across C=O; nothing leaves |
| Hydroxynitrile | Product of HCN addition: –OH and –C≡N on the same carbon |
| [O], [H] | Oxidising and reducing agents in organic redox equations |
| Derivative | The solid formed from 2,4-DNP and a particular carbonyl compound |

## Reaction summary: 6.1.2(a) and (b)

| Outcome | Reagent and conditions | Aldehyde gives | Ketone gives | Equation pattern |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) | Cr₂O₇²⁻/H⁺ (K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄), heat under reflux | Carboxylic acid | No reaction | RCHO + [O] → RCOOH |
| (b)(i) | NaBH₄ (aqueous) | Primary alcohol | Secondary alcohol | add 2[H] across C=O |
| (b)(ii) | NaCN(aq)/H⁺(aq), i.e. HCN | Hydroxynitrile | Hydroxynitrile | add H and CN across C=O |

Colour for (a): orange Cr₂O₇²⁻ to green Cr³⁺ when an aldehyde is present. A ketone leaves it orange.

The HCN reaction forms a new C–C bond and adds one carbon to the chain (see also 6.2.4(b)(ii)).

### Method: write a product formula

1. Find the C=O carbon.
2. NaBH₄: put H on that carbon and H on the oxygen.
3. HCN: put CN on that carbon and H on the oxygen.
4. [O] with an aldehyde: change –CHO to –COOH.
5. Check: carbon skeleton unchanged (NaBH₄, [O]) or one carbon longer (HCN).

### Naming hydroxynitriles

The nitrile carbon is C1, so the former carbonyl carbon is C2.

- Ethanal → 2-hydroxypropanenitrile
- Butanal → 2-hydroxypentanenitrile, C₅H₉NO
- Propanone → 2-hydroxy-2-methylpropanenitrile

## 6.1.2(c): Mechanisms in steps

**Stage 1, nucleophilic attack (both reactions)**

- Draw the δ+ and δ− on C=O.
- Arrow from the nucleophile's lone pair to the carbonyl carbon.
  - NaBH₄: the nucleophile is H⁻.
  - HCN: the nucleophile is CN⁻; the arrow starts at the lone pair on carbon.
- The C=O π pair moves onto O (arrow starts on the double bond).
- Intermediate: O⁻ with a lone pair, on a carbon that now has four bonds.

**Stage 2, protonation**

- NaBH₄: arrow from the O⁻ lone pair to H of H₂O, plus an arrow from that O–H bond onto oxygen. By-product OH⁻.
- HCN: protonate with H₂O (as above) or with H⁺ (one arrow, O⁻ lone pair to H⁺). The specification accepts either.

### Must-know distinctions

| Compare | Point |
|---|---|
| C=C vs C=O | C=C is non-polar and undergoes electrophilic addition; C=O is polar and undergoes nucleophilic addition |
| NaBH₄ product from aldehyde vs ketone | Primary alcohol vs secondary alcohol |
| [H] vs H₂ | Use 2[H] for NaBH₄ equations, not H₂ |
| HCN vs NaCN/H⁺ | Same reaction; HCN is very toxic and volatile, so the specification gives the reagent as NaCN(aq)/H⁺(aq) |
| CN⁻ arrow | Starts at carbon, never at nitrogen |

## 6.1.2(d): 2,4-DNP

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| Test (d)(i) | Add the unknown to 2,4-DNP solution |
| Positive result | Orange (yellow-orange) precipitate: aldehyde or ketone present |
| Not required | The equation and the structure of the derivative |
| Identify (d)(ii) | Filter, recrystallise, dry, measure melting point, compare with a data table |
| Why recrystallise | An impure solid melts low and over a range |

Carboxylic acids and esters contain C=O but give no precipitate with 2,4-DNP.

## 6.1.2(e): Tollens' reagent

- Ammoniacal silver nitrate; Ag⁺ ions are the oxidising agent.
- Warm in a water bath, not a flame (flammable organics).
- **Aldehyde:** silver mirror (or grey-black precipitate).
- **Ketone:** no change.

Explanation for (e)(ii):

- The aldehyde is oxidised to a carboxylic acid: RCHO + [O] → RCOOH.
- Each Ag⁺ ion gains an electron and becomes silver metal: Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag.
- One mole of aldehyde gives two moles of Ag (two electrons lost per –CHO).

### Small worked reminder

0.290 g of propanal, CH₃CH₂CHO (Mᵣ 58.0), reacts fully with Tollens' reagent.

- n(propanal) = 0.290 ÷ 58.0 = 0.00500 mol
- n(Ag) = 2 × 0.00500 = 0.0100 mol
- mass Ag = 0.0100 × 107.9 = **1.08 g**

## Quick self-test

1. State the reagents written as Cr₂O₇²⁻/H⁺.
2. Write the equation, using [O], for the oxidation of butanal.
3. Name the product when pentan-3-one is reduced by NaBH₄, and state its class.
4. Name the nucleophile in the NaBH₄ mechanism and the species that protonates the intermediate.
5. Give the formula of the product when butanal reacts with HCN, and its Mᵣ (H 1.0, C 12.0, O 16.0, N 14.0).
6. Why does the cyanide arrow start at carbon?
7. What do you see when 2,4-DNP is added to a ketone?
8. Give two reasons the derivative must be recrystallised before its melting point is measured.
9. Explain why a ketone gives no silver mirror with Tollens' reagent.
10. 2.10 g of cyclopentanone (Mᵣ 84.0) is reduced with NaBH₄, giving 1.72 g of cyclopentanol (Mᵣ 86.0). Calculate the percentage yield.
11. Compound J gives an orange precipitate with 2,4-DNP and turns acidified dichromate green. Is J an aldehyde or a ketone?
12. Which carbonyl compound reacts with HCN to give CH₃CH(OH)CN?

### Answers

1. K₂Cr₂O₇ (potassium dichromate) acidified with dilute H₂SO₄.
2. CH₃CH₂CH₂CHO + [O] → CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH (butanoic acid).
3. Pentan-3-ol; a secondary alcohol.
4. The hydride ion, H⁻; water, H₂O.
5. CH₃CH₂CH₂CH(OH)CN, C₅H₉NO; Mᵣ = 5(12.0) + 9(1.0) + 14.0 + 16.0 = 99.0.
6. The new bond forms between the cyanide carbon and the carbonyl carbon, so the electron pair comes from the carbon's lone pair.
7. An orange (yellow-orange) precipitate.
8. An impure sample melts below the true value, and over a spread of temperatures rather than sharply; either effect can give a false match.
9. Tollens' reagent works by oxidising the compound; a ketone is not oxidised by it, so Ag⁺ is not reduced to Ag.
10. n = 2.10 ÷ 84.0 = 0.0250 mol; theoretical mass = 0.0250 × 86.0 = 2.15 g; yield = 1.72 ÷ 2.15 × 100 = 80.0%.
11. An aldehyde: the precipitate shows C=O, and only the aldehyde is oxidised by dichromate (a ketone would leave it orange).
12. Ethanal, CH₃CHO: remove the CN carbon and turn the C(OH) back into C=O.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing H₂ or 2H in place of 2[H] for NaBH₄ reductions.
- Saying a ketone is oxidised by acidified dichromate, or giving no observation for the ketone (it stays orange).
- Starting the CN⁻ arrow at nitrogen rather than at the lone pair on carbon.
- Missing the arrow from the C=O bond to oxygen, so the oxygen never gains its negative charge.
- Leaving out the protonation step, or not showing OH⁻ formed when water protonates the intermediate.
- Describing 2,4-DNP as a colour change of the solution rather than a precipitate.
- Comparing an unpurified derivative's melting point with the data.
- Explaining the Tollens' test without saying which species is oxidised and which is reduced.
- Heating Tollens' reagent over a flame instead of a water bath.
- Numbering a hydroxynitrile from the wrong end.
