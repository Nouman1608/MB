These revision notes are for **higher level (HL) only**. The independence of India (1857 to 1964) is a regional study examined on Paper 3, which standard level students do not sit. They condense the IB *History guide*, first assessment 2028. Read the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-independence-of-india/) for the full account, then use the [practice set](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-independence-of-india-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Raj | British rule in India (1858 to 1947) |
| Swaraj | Self-rule; *purna swaraj* means complete independence |
| Satyagraha | Gandhi's method of non-violent resistance, "holding to truth" |
| Swadeshi | Boycott of British goods and promotion of Indian-made goods |
| Dyarchy | Divided government in the provinces under the 1919 Act |
| Separate electorates | Voting by religious community for reserved seats |
| Two-Nation theory | The idea that Hindus and Muslims were separate nations |
| Hartal | A general strike or shutdown of business |
| Princely states | Territories ruled by Indian princes under British protection |
| Non-alignment | Not joining either bloc in the Cold War |

## Causes of nationalism: a quick list

1. 1857 and the memory of resistance.
2. An educated middle class denied senior posts.
3. Economic grievances: the "drain of wealth", famine, taxes.
4. Repressive acts: Vernacular Press Act, Rowlatt Acts.
5. Racial attitudes: the Ilbert Bill row.
6. The First World War: sacrifice without reward; Jallianwala Bagh.

## British reforms compared

| Reform | Year | Main feature | Reaction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Morley–Minto | 1909 | More elected members; separate Muslim electorates | Muslim League pleased; Congress wary |
| Government of India Act | 1919 | Dyarchy in the provinces | Congress called it inadequate |
| Government of India Act | 1935 | Provincial autonomy; plan for federation | Provinces worked; federation never began |

## Methods and results

| Method | Example | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Non-cooperation | 1920 to 1922 | Mass involvement; ended after Chauri Chaura |
| Civil disobedience | 1930 to 1934 | Salt March; jail for tens of thousands; Gandhi–Irwin Pact |
| Constitutional | 1937 elections, ministries | Congress ministries; resigned in 1939 |
| Quit India | 1942 | Leaders jailed; widespread unrest; harsh repression |
| Negotiation | 1946 to 1947 | Cabinet Mission; Mountbatten plan |

## Leaders: one-paragraph profiles

- **Gandhi:** mass mobilisation and non-violence; his symbols (cloth, salt) reached the poor; criticised for being too religious in language and for calling off campaigns.
- **Nehru:** modern, socialist and secular; first prime minister.
- **Jinnah:** moved from Congress to the League; leader of the demand for Pakistan.
- **Bose:** radical; the Indian National Army; a symbol more than a military force.

## Partition: causes and consequences

- **Causes:** the League's growth in the 1930s; the Two-Nation theory; Lahore (1940); breakdown of the Cabinet Mission Plan; Direct Action Day; Britain's fast withdrawal under Mountbatten.
- **Consequences:** 10 to 15 million migrants; perhaps a million deaths; abduction of women; Radcliffe's borders; refugee camps; lasting India–Pakistan hostility.
- **Groups:** Sikhs and Punjabis lost homes; Bengalis were divided; women faced specific violence and, afterwards, recovery programmes that were controversial.

## After 1947: challenges

| Challenge | Detail |
|---|---|
| Nation-building | Constitution (1950); elections (1951 to 1952); Five-Year Plans |
| Language and region | Andhra (1953); States Reorganisation Act (1956) |
| Conflict | Naga insurgency; communal violence |
| Princely states | Junagadh, Hyderabad (1948), Kashmir (1947 to 1949) |
| Foreign policy | Non-alignment; Bandung; war with China (1962) |

## Perspectives to cite

- Nationalist (Bipan Chandra), Cambridge school (Seal), Subaltern Studies (Guha), Jalal on Jinnah, oral-history work on Partition (Butalia, Khan).

## Worked example: a short plan

**Question:** "Evaluate the importance of the Muslim League in the Partition of India."

1. Thesis: the League was essential to the demand but the outcome came from several actors.
2. League: formed in 1906, growth after 1937, Lahore Resolution, Jinnah's leadership.
3. Congress: policy in 1937 to 1939, unwillingness to share power in some provinces.
4. Britain: Cabinet Mission failure, haste under Mountbatten.
5. Violence: Direct Action Day made partition seem inevitable.
6. Perspectives (Jalal) and a judgement.

## Common mistakes

- Giving Jinnah's Pakistan as a fixed plan from 1906.
- Forgetting the 1935 Act's importance.
- Confusing non-cooperation (1920 to 1922) with civil disobedience (1930).
- Omitting the princely states.

## Self-test

1. Who founded the Congress with Indian leaders in 1885?
2. What was dyarchy?
3. What was the Rowlatt Act?
4. Why was salt chosen for the 1930 protest?
5. Which resolution of 1940 called for Muslim-majority states?
6. Who drew the border in 1947?
7. Which princely state was taken in a police action in 1948?
8. What caused the 1962 defeat to hurt Nehru?

**Answers:** 1. A. O. Hume with Indian leaders. 2. Division of provincial power between elected ministers and British officials. 3. A law extending wartime emergency powers, which led to protests. 4. Everyone used salt and the tax hit the poor, so the protest was simple and widely felt. 5. The Lahore Resolution. 6. Cyril Radcliffe. 7. Hyderabad. 8. India lost to China in a short border war, which damaged his foreign-policy reputation.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which leader should I know best?**
Gandhi and Jinnah are the most often needed, with Nehru close behind. Be able to compare them.

**Is Partition an inevitable outcome?**
That is itself a debate. Use the historians to show the range of views.
