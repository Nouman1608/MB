**These notes condense content area 3.2.2.5 of 9237: "The origins and role of the major law schools in Islam including: Hanafi, Shaf'i, Maliki, Hanbali and Ja'fari."** The full explanation is in the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-islamiyat-9237-different-schools-of-thought-in-islam/).

## Key ideas

- A **madhhab** is a school of law (fiqh) with a method for deriving rulings.
- **Ijtihad** is independent reasoning by a qualified scholar; **taqlid** is following a school or scholar.
- All schools accept the Qur'an and Sunnah and the Five Pillars. They differ on some details.
- The schools are schools of law, not separate religions or sects.

## Four causes of difference

| Cause | Example |
|---|---|
| Words with more than one meaning | Qur'an 2:228, *quru'*: menstrual periods (Hanafi, Hanbali) or periods of purity (Maliki, Shafi'i) |
| Hadith known in different places | A report known in Madinah but not in Kufa |
| Different methods of reasoning | Practice of Madinah; analogy; texts first |
| Different circumstances | Customs and conditions in Iraq, the Hijaz and Egypt |

## Founders in time order

| Founder | School | Dates (approx.) | City |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ja'far al-Sadiq | Ja'fari | 83-148 AH | Madinah |
| Abu Hanifa | Hanafi | 80-150 AH | Kufa |
| Malik ibn Anas | Maliki | 93-179 AH | Madinah |
| Al-Shafi'i | Shafi'i | 150-204 AH | Madinah, Iraq, Egypt |
| Ahmad ibn Hanbal | Hanbali | 164-241 AH | Baghdad |

**Links:** al-Shafi'i studied under Malik; Ahmad studied under al-Shafi'i.

## One school at a time

| School | Method | Key work or helper | Region |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hanafi | Qiyas, istihsan, custom | Abu Yusuf and al-Shaybani recorded his teaching | South Asia, Turkey, Central Asia |
| Maliki | Practice of Madinah; maslahah | al-Muwatta | North and West Africa |
| Shafi'i | Qur'an, Sunnah, Ijma, Qiyas in order | al-Risala | Egypt, East Africa, Yemen, South-East Asia |
| Hanbali | Hadith and companions first; little analogy | al-Musnad | Saudi Arabia, Qatar |
| Ja'fari | Imams of the Ahl al-Bayt; reason ('aql) | Teachings collected by students | Iran, Iraq, Bahrain, Lebanon, Azerbaijan |

## Examples of differences

- Hands in prayer: Hanafi below the navel; Shafi'i above; Ja'fari at the sides; Maliki commonly at the sides.
- Witr: Hanafi call it wajib; most others, strongly recommended.
- Combining prayers: Ja'fari regularly; others only in special situations.
- Prostration: Ja'fari on natural earth or a clay tablet.
- **Shared:** five prayers, Ramadhan fasting, Zakah, Hajj.

## Role of the schools

1. **Guidance** for ordinary Muslims.
2. **Consistency** in courts and communities.
3. **Flexibility** for new situations.
4. **Protection of the sources** from careless interpretation.
5. **Mutual recognition:** the Amman Message (2005) recognised the four Sunni schools and the Ja'fari school, among others.

## The prescribed passage: Qur'an 4:59

| Phrase | Link to the schools |
|---|---|
| "obey Allah and obey the Messenger" | The sources every school uses |
| "those in authority among you" | Many include scholars: a basis for following jurists |
| "if you disagree over anything" | Disagreement is expected |
| "refer it to Allah and the Messenger" | The method: return to the sources |
| "best [way] and best in result" | This way brings good results |

## Model answer: a 4-mark question

**Question:** Explain **two** features of the Maliki school. **[4]**

> The Maliki school treats the practice of the people of Madinah as evidence of the Sunnah [1], because Imam Malik believed the city's way of life passed on the Prophet's (pbuh) example [1]. It also uses public interest (maslahah) [1], which lets scholars weigh benefit and harm when the texts do not settle a question [1].

## Evaluating a statement

**Statement:** "Having different schools of law weakens the Muslim community."

| Agree | Disagree |
|---|---|
| Differences in practice may cause disputes or pride in one school | The schools agree on belief and the pillars |
| Some may treat a school as a division of faith | The Sunni schools recognise each other; the Amman Message recognised more |
| | Variety gives flexibility and a record of careful scholarship |
| | Qur'an 4:59 expects disagreement and gives the method to handle it |

**Judgement:** the schools weaken unity only when differences of method are treated as differences of faith.

## How the schools spread

- **Hanafi:** supported by the Abbasid courts (Abu Yusuf served as a chief judge) and later the official school of the Ottoman Empire.
- **Maliki:** carried west to North Africa, West Africa and Muslim Spain.
- **Shafi'i:** established in Egypt, then spread to East Africa, Yemen and South-East Asia through trade and teaching.
- **Hanbali:** strong in Baghdad, and today the main school in Saudi Arabia and Qatar.
- **Ja'fari:** the school of the Twelver Shi'a community, with centres in Iran and Iraq.

**Pattern to remember:** schools spread through teachers, students, judges, rulers and traders, not by force of argument alone.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Madhhab | School of law |
| Fiqh | Understanding of law; jurisprudence |
| Usul al-fiqh | Principles of jurisprudence |
| Ikhtilaf | Difference of opinion |
| Istihsan | Preferring a fairer ruling |
| Maslahah | Public interest |
| 'Aql | Reason |
| Mujtahid | A scholar qualified for ijtihad |
| Marja' | A senior Ja'fari scholar followed by others |

## Exam traps

- Name all five schools, not four.
- Do not call the schools sects.
- Differences are about details, never the pillars.
- Be able to explain a cause of difference, not just a list of founders.
- Use 4:59 for both the sources and the handling of disagreement.

## Self-test

1. What is a madhhab?
2. Which school treats the practice of Madinah as a source?
3. Who wrote al-Risala?
4. Which Imam compiled al-Musnad?
5. Give two causes of difference between the schools.
6. What is the difference between ijtihad and taqlid?
7. Which verse describes divorce waiting periods using the word *quru'*?
8. Who is the founder of the Ja'fari school?
9. Give one difference in practice between two schools.
10. Which phrase in 4:59 accepts disagreement?

**Answers:** 1. A school of Islamic law with its own method for deriving rulings. 2. The Maliki school. 3. Imam al-Shafi'i. 4. Ahmad ibn Hanbal. 5. Any two: words with more than one meaning; Hadith known in different places; different methods of reasoning; different circumstances. 6. Ijtihad is independent reasoning by a qualified scholar; taqlid is following a school or scholar. 7. Qur'an 2:228. 8. Ja'far al-Sadiq. 9. Any one, for example hands in prayer, combining prayers or the status of Witr. 10. "If you disagree over anything."
