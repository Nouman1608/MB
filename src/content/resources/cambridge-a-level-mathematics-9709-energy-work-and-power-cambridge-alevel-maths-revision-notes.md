Condensed recall for subtopic 4.5 of Cambridge 9709 Paper 4. For full explanations and worked examples, see the [energy, work and power study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-mathematics-9709-energy-work-and-power-cambridge-alevel-maths/); for exam-style problems, use the [energy, work and power practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-mathematics-9709-energy-work-and-power-cambridge-alevel-maths-practice/).

## Formula sheet

```
Work done by a constant force     W = Fd cos θ      (θ between force and displacement)
Kinetic energy                    KE = ½mv²
Change in gravitational PE        ΔPE = mgh         (h = vertical height)
Average power                     P = work done ÷ time taken
Power of a force along motion     P = Fv
Driving force from power          D = P ÷ v
Maximum speed (a = 0)             v_max = P ÷ (total resistance)
```

Units: joules (J) for work and energy, watts (W) for power. 1 kW = 1000 W.

## The work–energy equation

```
KE₁ + PE₁ + work done by driving forces = KE₂ + PE₂ + work done against resistances
```

- No driving forces and no resistances: mechanical energy is conserved, KE + PE = constant.
- Path shape is irrelevant for PE: only the change in vertical height counts.
- Use it when the path is curved, when a force varies, or when the question says "use an energy method".

## Which forces do work?

| Force | Work done during the motion |
|---|---|
| Driving force or tension along the motion | Fd (positive) |
| Force at θ to the motion | Fd cos θ |
| Friction or resistance | Fd done against it |
| Normal reaction | none (perpendicular to motion) |
| Weight, horizontal motion | none |
| Weight, motion through vertical height h | mgh, appearing as a change in PE |

## Vehicles at constant power

1. Driving force D = P ÷ v at the stated speed.
2. Along the road, forward positive: D − resistance − mg sin θ (uphill) = ma.
3. Downhill, the weight component is **+mg sin θ** on the left-hand side.
4. Maximum speed: a = 0, so D equals the total resistance along the road.

Constant power means the driving force **decreases** as speed increases, so the acceleration is not constant and constant-acceleration formulae do **not** apply over the whole motion.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Work done | energy transferred by a force: force component along displacement × distance |
| Kinetic energy | energy due to motion, ½mv² |
| Gravitational potential energy | energy due to height; changes by mgh |
| Conservation of energy | total mechanical energy is constant when only weight does work |
| Work–energy principle | change in energy equals work done by external forces other than weight |
| Power | rate of doing work |
| Average power | total work ÷ total time |
| Maximum (terminal) speed | speed at which driving force equals total resistance |

## Exam traps

- **Full force for work.** A 40 N force at 30° to the motion does 40 cos 30° × d of work, not 40d.
- **Slope distance in mgh.** A particle moving 5 m along a 30° slope rises 5 sin 30° = 2.5 m.
- **Normal reaction with an angled pull.** The vertical component reduces R, so friction is less than μmg.
- **P = Fv with the wrong F.** F is the driving force, not the resultant.
- **kW not converted.** 24 kW is 24 000 W.
- **Constant acceleration assumed at constant power.** It is not constant; use energy or instantaneous Newton's second law.
- **Sign of the weight component.** Against the car going uphill; with it going downhill.
- **"Work done by friction" positive.** Friction removes energy; in the energy equation it sits with the losses.

## Quick method choice

| Question gives or asks for | Best tool |
|---|---|
| Speed after a curved path | conservation of energy or work–energy |
| Distance travelled against known friction, speeds known | work–energy |
| Acceleration at an instant | Newton's second law with D = P ÷ v |
| Time to reach a speed with constant forces | Newton's second law then kinematics |
| Engine's work over a stretch with variable force | work–energy, then average power |

## Self-test

1. A 25 N force at 40° to the horizontal moves a box 8 m along level ground. Find the work done by the force.
2. A 30 kg crate is lifted 4 m vertically at constant speed in 5 s. Find the work done and the average power.
3. A child slides from rest down a smooth curved slide with a vertical drop of 2.45 m. Find the speed at the bottom.
4. A car moves at a constant 25 m s⁻¹ on a level road against a resistance of 800 N. Find the power of its engine.
5. A car's engine works at 24 kW. Find the driving force at 16 m s⁻¹.
6. A car with power 30 kW has maximum speed 40 m s⁻¹ on a level road. Find the resistance.
7. A particle of mass 2 kg slides 5 m down a smooth slope inclined at 30°. Find its loss of PE.
8. A 4 kg particle slows from 6 m s⁻¹ to 2 m s⁻¹ on rough horizontal ground in 4 m. Find the friction force.
9. Why does the normal reaction do no work on a sliding particle?
10. A cyclist with total mass 75 kg rides up a slope with sin θ = 0.04 at constant 5 m s⁻¹ against a 20 N resistance. Find the power.
11. A pump lifts 120 kg of water through 5 m every minute. Find the power needed to increase its PE.
12. A car at constant power accelerates on a level road. Is its acceleration constant? Why?

### Answers

1. 25 cos 40° × 8 = **153 J** (3 s.f.).
2. Work 30 × 10 × 4 = **1200 J**; power 1200 ÷ 5 = **240 W**.
3. v² = 2 × 10 × 2.45 = 49, **v = 7 m s⁻¹**.
4. P = 800 × 25 = **20 000 W (20 kW)**.
5. 24 000 ÷ 16 = **1500 N**.
6. 30 000 ÷ 40 = **750 N**.
7. 2 × 10 × 5 sin 30° = **50 J**.
8. KE lost = ½ × 4 × (36 − 4) = 64 J; F = 64 ÷ 4 = **16 N**.
9. It acts **perpendicular to the displacement**, so cos 90° = 0.
10. Driving force = 75 × 10 × 0.04 + 20 = 50 N; P = 50 × 5 = **250 W**.
11. 120 × 10 × 5 = 6000 J per 60 s = **100 W**.
12. **No**: D = P ÷ v falls as v rises, so the resultant force, and hence the acceleration, decreases.
