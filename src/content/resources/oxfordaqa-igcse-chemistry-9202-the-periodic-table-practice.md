## Section A

**1.** The modern periodic table is arranged in order of:

A: atomic mass

B: atomic number

C: number of neutrons

D: reactivity

**[1]**

**2.** State the name given to:

**(a)** a vertical column in the periodic table **[1]**

**(b)** the elements in Group 0 **[1]**

**3.** State how many electrons are in the outer energy level of:

**(a)** a neon atom **[1]**

**(b)** a helium atom **[1]**

**4.** Name the scientist whose work is the basis of the current periodic table. **[1]**

**5.** Element R has the electronic structure 2,8,8,1. State the group and the period of R. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why lithium and sodium have similar chemical properties. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Mendeleev arranged the elements known in 1869 into a table.

**(a)** Describe **two** features of the way Mendeleev arranged the elements. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the discovery of new elements after 1869 made scientists more confident in Mendeleev's table. **[2]**

**8.** Tellurium has a relative atomic mass of 128 and an atomic number of 52. Iodine has a relative atomic mass of 127 and an atomic number of 53.

**(a)** State the order of these two elements if they were placed strictly in order of relative atomic mass. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why tellurium is placed before iodine in the modern periodic table. **[2]**

**9.** The table shows the electronic structures of four elements, W, X, Y and Z (not their real symbols).

| Element | Electronic structure |
|---|---|
| W | 2,8,1 |
| X | 2,8,8 |
| Y | 2,1 |
| Z | 2,8,7 |

**(a)** Identify the **two** elements in the same group. Give a reason. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify the element that is a noble gas. **[1]**

**(c)** Identify the element in Period 3, Group 7. **[1]**

**(d)** Identify element Z by name. **[1]**

**10.** Argon is used to fill light bulbs because it is unreactive. Explain, in terms of electrons, why argon is unreactive. **[2]**

**11.** The noble gases were not in Mendeleev's 1869 table. Suggest why, and explain why the discovery of these gases did not show that his table was wrong. **[3]**

**12.** Element 20 is calcium.

**(a)** Give its electronic structure. **[1]**

**(b)** Name one other element in the same group as calcium and give its electronic structure. **[2]**

**(c)** Predict whether calcium is more similar in its chemical reactions to magnesium or to potassium. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**13.** A student says: "Elements in the same period have similar properties because they have the same number of shells." Explain what is wrong with this statement. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2. (a)** Group [1]

**(b)** Noble gases [1]

**[2]**

**3. (a)** 8 [1]

**(b)** 2 [1]

**[2]**

**4.** Mendeleev [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Group 1 [1]; Period 4 [1]. **[2]**

**6.** They are in the same group / both have one outer electron (Li 2,1; Na 2,8,1) [1]; chemical properties depend on the number of outer electrons [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Any two [2]: he placed elements with similar properties in the same group; he arranged them mainly in order of atomic mass; he swapped the order of some pairs to fit properties; he left gaps for undiscovered elements.

**(b)** Mendeleev had predicted the properties of undiscovered elements [1]; when elements were discovered to fill the gaps, their properties matched his predictions [1].

**[4]**

**8. (a)** Iodine (127) before tellurium (128) [1].

**(b)** The modern table is ordered by atomic number [1]; tellurium has the lower atomic number (52), so it comes first, which also puts iodine in the group of elements with similar properties [1].

**[3]**

**9. (a)** W and Y [1]: both have one outer electron [1].

**(b)** X [1]

**(c)** Z [1]

**(d)** Chlorine [1]

**[5]**

**10.** Argon atoms have eight electrons in the outer energy level / a full outer shell [1]; this is a stable arrangement, so argon does not gain, lose or share electrons easily [1]. **[2]**

**11.** They had not been discovered in 1869 (they are unreactive, so they were hard to detect) [1]. When they were discovered, they had similar properties to each other [1] and fitted into the table as a complete new group without disturbing the existing groups [1]. **[3]**

**12. (a)** 2,8,8,2 [1]

**(b)** Magnesium [1], 2,8,2 [1] (or beryllium, 2,2)

**(c)** Magnesium [1]: both have two outer electrons / both are in Group 2, and elements with the same number of outer electrons have similar chemical properties [1].

**[5]**

**13.** Elements with similar properties are in the same **group**, not the same period [1]; the properties depend on the number of **outer electrons**, which is the same within a group, not on the number of shells [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying the modern table is ordered by atomic mass.
- Confusing groups with periods.
- Giving helium eight outer electrons.
- Describing Mendeleev's gaps without mentioning his predictions.
- Explaining "similar properties" without linking it to outer electrons.
- Forgetting that the noble gases were discovered after 1869.
