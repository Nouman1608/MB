## Section A

**1.** State the three functions of transport in the supply chain. **[3]**

**2.** Name **two** types of ship used in sea transport. **[2]**

**3.** State **one** advantage of using rail to transport goods. **[1]**

**4.** State **one** disadvantage of using air transport. **[1]**

**5.** State what is meant by **containerisation**. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** A business sends 800 kg of goods overseas. Air freight costs $3.50 per kg and takes 3 days. Sea freight costs $0.45 per kg and takes 28 days.

**(a)** Calculate the cost by air and by sea. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the difference in cost. **[1]**

**(c)** The goods are spare parts that a factory needs urgently because production has stopped. Justify the method of transport the business should choose. **[2]**

**7.** A container holds 3,000 boxes of tiles. Shipping the full container costs $4,500.

**(a)** Calculate the cost per box. **[1]**

**(b)** The container is only 60% full. Calculate the number of boxes and the new cost per box. **[3]**

**8.** Explain why a pipeline is suitable for transporting oil from an oil field to a refinery. **[3]**

**9.** Describe **two** advantages of containerisation to an exporter. **[4]**

**10.** Explain **two** factors a business should consider when choosing a method of transport for a delivery of fresh fruit. **[4]**

**11.** Explain what is meant by **transshipment**. **[2]**

**12.** A furniture manufacturer delivers to shops in the same city using its own vans. Explain **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of road transport for this business. **[4]**

**13.** Evaluate whether a company exporting televisions to another continent should use air or sea transport. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** Time [1]; place [1]; quantity [1].

**2.** Any two from: ferry; tanker; container ship; cargo liner [1 each].

**3.** Any one from: carries heavy, bulky loads cheaply over long distances; not affected by road traffic; lower pollution per tonne [1].

**4.** Any one from: most expensive method; limits on size and weight; airports may be far from the customer; weather can delay flights [1].

**5.** Moving goods in standard-sized containers that are packed once and moved by ship, rail and road [1].

**6.** **(a)** Air: 800 × 3.50 = $2,800 [1]. Sea: 800 × 0.45 = $360 [1].

**(b)** 2,800 − 360 = $2,440 [1].

**(c)** Air [1], because the parts are needed urgently and 3 days is much faster than 28 days, so the cost of stopped production is likely to be greater than the extra $2,440 [1].

**7.** **(a)** 4,500 ÷ 3,000 = $1.50 [1].

**(b)** 60% of 3,000 = 1,800 boxes [1]. 4,500 ÷ 1,800 = $2.50 [1] per box. The cost per box rises because the same cost is shared over fewer boxes [1].

**8.** Oil is a liquid, which can flow through a pipe [1]. A pipeline gives a continuous flow of large quantities at low running cost [1] and is safe, with little handling and low labour costs [1].

**9.** Goods are protected from damage, weather and theft because they are sealed in a container [1], so there are fewer claims and lower insurance costs [1]. Loading and unloading are fast because standard containers are lifted by crane [1], which reduces labour costs and time in port [1]. Goods can be switched between ship, train and lorry without unpacking [1], which reduces handling and delay [1]. Maximum 4.

**10.** Nature of the goods: fresh fruit is perishable [1], so it needs fast or refrigerated transport [1]. Urgency: the fruit must reach the customer before it spoils [1], so air or fast road transport is needed [1]. Cost: the fruit is relatively cheap, so costly transport may make the product too expensive [1], so the business must balance speed against cost [1]. Maximum 4 (two developed factors).

**11.** Moving goods from one ship or vehicle to another during the journey [1], for example from a large ship at a hub port to a smaller ship going to the final port [1].

**12.** Advantage: vans give door-to-door delivery [1], so furniture goes straight to each shop without extra handling [1]. Disadvantage: traffic congestion can cause delays [1] and the vans carry limited loads, so several trips may be needed [1]. Accept: fuel and driver costs.

**13.** Any five from: for air: fast, so televisions reach shops quickly [1]; secure, with less chance of theft for high-value items [1]; against air: expensive, which raises prices [1]; for sea: much cheaper per unit, especially in containers [1]; against sea: slow, so stock is tied up for weeks [1]; televisions are not perishable, so speed is less important [1]; a judgement, for example sea freight for regular stock and air for urgent orders [1]. Maximum 5.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 6(c): choosing air without a reason, or choosing sea and ignoring urgency.
- Question 7(b): taking 60% of 4,500 instead of 3,000.
- Question 8: saying pipelines are cheap to build. They are very expensive to build and cheap to run.
- Question 9: stating advantages without saying why they help the exporter.
- Question 11: saying transshipment means unloading goods at the final destination.
- Question 13: giving only one method or no judgement.
