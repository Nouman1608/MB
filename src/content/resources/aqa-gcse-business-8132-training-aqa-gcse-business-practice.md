## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Define the term *off-the-job training*. **[2]**

**2.** State **two** topics that would usually be covered in induction training. **[2]**

**3.** Which **one** of the following is an example of on-the-job training? A: a week-long course at a college. B: a new cashier shadowing a supervisor at the till. C: an online course taken at a training centre. D: a conference for managers. **[1]**

**4.** Explain **one** way training helps a business adapt to technological change. **[3]**

**5.** Explain **one** disadvantage to a business of training its employees. **[2]**

**6.** Ingram Dental sends 3 nurses on a course costing £480 each. Their wages of £410 each for the week are still paid. Travel costs £150 in total. Calculate the total cost of the course to Ingram Dental. **[3]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** Hollin Fitness is opening a new gym and will employ 14 new staff.

**(a)** Explain **one** benefit to Hollin Fitness of induction training. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain **one** reason why a poor induction could increase Hollin Fitness's costs. **[3]**

**8.** Cresswell Foods trains new packers on the job. A supervisor normally packs £900 of orders a day. While training a new packer, she packs only £600 of orders a day. Training lasts 5 days.

**(a)** Calculate the value of output lost while the supervisor trains one new packer. **[2]**
**(b)** Analyse **one** other disadvantage of on-the-job training for Cresswell Foods. **[4]**

**9.** Explain how training can improve customer service in a mobile phone shop. **[3]**

**10.** Teversal Engineering spent £7,500 training its welders in a new technique. The training is expected to reduce faulty products by £1,250 a month.

**(a)** Calculate how many months it will take for the savings to cover the cost of the training. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain **one** reason why the training might not pay for itself in this time. **[2]**

**11.** Ashby Pharmacy needs its counter assistants to learn about new medicines rules. Option A: the pharmacist trains them on the job during quiet periods. Option B: they attend a two-day off-the-job course run by specialist trainers. Analyse **one** advantage of each option for Ashby Pharmacy. **[6]**

**12.** Kilnsey Coffee is a chain of eight coffee shops. It is introducing a new ordering app that customers will use on their phones, linked to new screens behind the counter. The finance director wants staff trained on the job by shop managers to save money. The operations manager wants all staff to attend an off-the-job session run by the app's supplier. Evaluate which approach Kilnsey Coffee should take. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** Training that takes place away from the employee's normal place of work [1], for example at a college or a supplier's training centre [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any two of: health and safety, layout of the workplace, the business's rules and policies, introductions to colleagues, basic tasks of the job [1] [1]. **[2]**

**3.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Staff learn how to use new equipment or software [1], so the business can use the technology fully rather than having it sit idle or be misused [1], which means the investment raises productivity as intended [1]. **[3]**

**5.** It costs money, for example course fees and wages paid while staff are not working [1], which reduces profit in the short term [1]. Also accept trained staff leaving for a competitor, or lost output, with a development. **[2]**

**6.** Fees: 3 × £480 = £1,440 [1]. Wages: 3 × £410 = £1,230 [1]. Total: £1,440 + £1,230 + £150 = **£2,820** [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** New staff learn the gym's safety procedures and how the equipment works [1], so they can supervise members safely from the start [1], reducing the risk of injuries and protecting the new gym's reputation [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** New staff who feel unsupported may leave in their first weeks [1], so Hollin must recruit again [1], paying again for advertising, interviews and training [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** £900 − £600 = £300 a day [1]; £300 × 5 = **£1,500** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The supervisor may not be a skilled teacher, or may pass on shortcuts [1], so the new packer learns bad habits [1], leading to packing errors such as wrong items sent [1], which cause returns and complaints and cost Cresswell money [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Training gives staff detailed knowledge of the phones and contracts [1], so they can answer questions and match customers to the right product [1], making customers more satisfied and more likely to return or recommend the shop [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** £7,500 ÷ £1,250 [1] = **6 months** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Welders might leave after being trained [1], so the business loses the skills before the savings are made [1]. Also accept: the fall in faults may be smaller than forecast. **[2]**

**11.** Option A: there are no course fees and the assistants stay at the pharmacy [1], so the counter remains staffed for customers [1], and training can be fitted to Ashby's own products and customers [1]. Option B: specialist trainers know the new rules in detail [1], so assistants are less likely to make mistakes when advising customers [1], which matters because errors with medicines could harm customers and the pharmacy's reputation [1]. **[6]**

**12.** Knowledge: on-the-job training is at work, often by a manager; off-the-job is away from the workplace, here by the supplier [1].
Application: eight shops, so on-the-job training relies on eight different managers [1]; the app is new, so managers have no experience of it either [1].
Analysis: on-the-job saves fees and keeps shops open with full staff [1], and staff practise on the real screens [1]; the supplier knows the system in detail, so staff learn it correctly and consistently in every shop [1].
Evaluation: off-the-job means staff are away from the shops, so cover or closure costs money [1]; if managers are trained badly, mistakes with customer orders could spread across all eight shops and harm the brand [1]. Judgement: a combination, with managers trained off the job by the supplier and then training their own staff on the job, keeps costs down while making sure the expert knowledge reaches every shop [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* A combined approach is a valid judgement if it is justified from the case, here the number of shops and the newness of the app.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining off-the-job training as training outside working hours.
- Leaving the wages paid during a course out of its total cost.
- Describing a benefit of training without linking it to costs, sales or reputation.
- Ignoring the lost output of the person who delivers on-the-job training.
- Choosing a method without using the size of the business or the type of skill in the case.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I recommend both types of training in an evaluate question?**
Yes, if you explain why the combination suits the case, for example expert off-the-job training for managers followed by on-the-job training for their teams.

**What costs should I include for off-the-job training?**
Course fees, wages paid while staff are away, travel and any cover needed for their work.
