These questions cover content references 3.02f to 3.02i of the OCR A Level Mathematics A (H240) specification, Version 3.1 (August 2026), for first assessment in 2018. Questions 1 to 3 are Stage 1 (shared with AS). Questions 4 to 12 are **A Level only**. Take g = 9.8 m s⁻² and give decimals to 3 significant figures. Learn the methods first from the [study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-mathematics-variable-acceleration-motion-under-gravity-and/). The [free diagnostics](/diagnostics/) show where to focus.

## Questions

**1.** A particle moves on a straight line with acceleration a = 12t − 8 m s⁻². When t = 1 its velocity is 3 m s⁻¹ and its displacement from O is 2 m. Find an expression for its displacement s at time t. **[4]**

**2.** A particle moves on a straight line with displacement s = 2t³ − 21t² + 60t metres from O at time t seconds.

**(a)** Find the values of t at which the particle is instantaneously at rest. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the acceleration at the later of these times, and the total distance travelled in the first 5 seconds. **[4]**

**3.** A particle moves on a straight line with velocity v = 12 − 3√t m s⁻¹ for t ≥ 0 and starts at O.

**(a)** Find the acceleration when t = 4. **[2]**
**(b)** Show that the displacement at time t is 12t − 2t^(3/2). **[3]**
**(c)** Find the time at which the particle is first at rest, and its displacement then. **[2]**

**4.** (A Level only) A particle has position r = (t² − 6t)i + (t³ − 12t)j metres at time t seconds.

**(a)** Show that at t = 2 the particle moves parallel to i. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the magnitude of the acceleration at t = 2. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the time at which the particle moves parallel to j, and its position then. **[2]**

**5.** (A Level only) A particle has acceleration a = (12t − 6)i + 6tj m s⁻². At t = 0 its velocity is (4i − 12j) m s⁻¹ and its position is (i + 10j) m.

**(a)** Find the velocity at time t. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the position vector at time t. **[3]**
**(c)** Show that when t = 2 the particle moves parallel to i, and find its speed then. **[3]**

**6.** (A Level only) A ball moves with acceleration −9.8j m s⁻² and is projected from the origin with velocity (4i + 14j) m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Find its position vector at t = 1.5. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the times at which it is 6 m above the level of projection. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the greatest height reached, and the ball's speed at that point. **[2]**

**7.** (A Level only) A ball is kicked from level ground with speed 32 m s⁻¹ at 25° above the horizontal.

**(a)** Find the time of flight. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the horizontal range. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the greatest height reached. **[2]**

**8.** (A Level only) A stone is thrown horizontally at 14 m s⁻¹ from the top of a cliff 60 m above the sea.

**(a)** Find the time taken to reach the sea. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the horizontal distance from the foot of the cliff at which it lands. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the speed of the stone as it lands and the angle its path makes with the horizontal. **[3]**

**9.** (A Level only) A projectile is launched from the origin with speed u at angle α above the horizontal. Show that its path has equation y = x tan α − g x² / (2u² cos²α). **[4]**

**10.** (A Level only) A projectile is launched from the origin at 22 m s⁻¹ and passes through the point (30, 10), where x is horizontal and y vertical, both in metres.

**(a)** Find the two possible angles of projection, to 1 decimal place. **[5]**
**(b)** Explain why there are two angles. **[1]**

**11.** (A Level only) A ball is kicked from level ground with speed 21 m s⁻¹ at 50° above the horizontal towards a vertical wall 12 m high, whose base is 18 m away on the same ground.

**(a)** Find the height of the ball when it is level with the wall. **[3]**
**(b)** State whether it clears the wall, and by how much. **[1]**
**(c)** Show that the ball is still rising as it passes the wall. **[2]**
**(d)** Give one reason why a real ball might not clear the wall. **[1]**

**12.** (A Level only) A particle is projected from a point A, 20 m above level ground, with velocity (8i + 6j) m s⁻¹, where i is horizontal and j is upward.

**(a)** Find the time taken to reach the ground. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the horizontal distance travelled in that time. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the speed on landing and the angle of the velocity below the horizontal. **[3]**
**(d)** Find the time at which the particle moves at 45° below the horizontal. **[2]**
**(e)** State one assumption of the model that makes part (a) unrealistic. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** v = ∫(12t − 8) dt = 6t² − 8t + C [1]. At t = 1, 6 − 8 + C = 3 so C = 5 [1]. s = ∫(6t² − 8t + 5) dt = 2t³ − 4t² + 5t + D [1]. At t = 1, 2 − 4 + 5 + D = 2, so D = −1 and **s = 2t³ − 4t² + 5t − 1** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each integration needs its own constant.

**2. (a)** v = ds/dt = 6t² − 42t + 60 [1]. Set v = 0: 6(t − 2)(t − 5) = 0 [1]. **t = 2 and t = 5** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** a = 12t − 42, so at t = 5, **a = 18 m s⁻²** [1]. s(2) = 52 [1] and s(5) = 25 [1]. It moves 52 m, then returns 27 m: 52 + 27 = **79 m** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* For distance travelled, show where the direction changes. Here s(5) − s(0) = 25 is only the displacement.

**3. (a)** a = dv/dt = −(3/2)t^(−1/2) [1], so at t = 4, **a = −3/4 m s⁻²** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** s = ∫(12 − 3t^(1/2)) dt = 12t − 2t^(3/2) + C [1]. s = 0 at t = 0 gives C = 0 [1]. So **s = 12t − 2t^(3/2)** [1] **[3]**
**(c)** v = 0 gives √t = 4, so **t = 16 s** [1]. s = 12(16) − 2(64) = **64 m** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Apply the starting condition to find the constant before quoting the given result.

**4. (a)** v = (2t − 6)i + (3t² − 12)j [1]. At t = 2 the j-component is 3(4) − 12 = 0 [1]. So v = −2i, which is parallel to i [1] **[3]**
**(b)** a = 2i + 6tj [1]. At t = 2, a = 2i + 12j [1]. |a| = √(4 + 144) = √148 = **12.2 m s⁻²** [1] **[3]**
**(c)** The i-component is zero when 2t − 6 = 0, so **t = 3** [1]. Then r = (9 − 18)i + (27 − 36)j = **−9i − 9j** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* For "parallel to i", say which component you set to zero.

**5. (a)** Integrate each component: v = (6t² − 6t)i + 3t²j + c [1]. At t = 0, c = 4i − 12j [1]. So **v = (6t² − 6t + 4)i + (3t² − 12)j** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** r = (2t³ − 3t² + 4t)i + (t³ − 12t)j + d [1]. At t = 0, d = i + 10j [1]. So **r = (2t³ − 3t² + 4t + 1)i + (t³ − 12t + 10)j** [1] **[3]**
**(c)** At t = 2 the j-component is 3(4) − 12 = 0 [1], so the particle moves parallel to i. The i-component is 24 − 12 + 4 = 16 [1]. Speed = **16 m s⁻¹** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The constant of integration is a vector, so write it as one.

**6. (a)** r = (4i + 14j)(1.5) + ½(−9.8j)(1.5²) [1] = **6i + 9.98j** (m) [1] **[2]**
**(b)** 14t − 4.9t² = 6, so 4.9t² − 14t + 6 = 0 [1]. t = (14 ± √78.4)/9.8 [1]. **t = 0.525 s and t = 2.33 s** [1] **[3]**
**(c)** At the top 14 − 9.8t = 0, so t = 1.43 s, and the height is 14²/19.6 = **10.0 m** [1]. The vertical velocity is 0 there, so the speed is the horizontal component, **4 m s⁻¹** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Both roots are valid here: the ball passes 6 m going up and coming down.

**7. (a)** Vertical component 32 sin 25° = 13.52 [1]. Solve 13.52t − 4.9t² = 0 [1]: **T = 2.76 s** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** Horizontal component 32 cos 25° = 29.0, so range = 29.0 × 2.760 = **80.0 m** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** H = 13.52²/(2 × 9.8) [1] = **9.33 m** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Carry unrounded values forward.

**8. (a)** The initial vertical velocity is 0, so 60 = ½(9.8)t² [1], giving **t = 3.50 s** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Distance = 14 × 3.499 = **49.0 m** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** v_y = 9.8 × 3.499 = 34.3 [1]. Speed = √(14² + 34.3²) = **37.0 m s⁻¹** [1]. Angle = tan⁻¹(34.3/14) = **67.8° below the horizontal** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* State what the angle is measured from.

**9.** Horizontally x = (u cos α)t [1]. Vertically y = (u sin α)t − ½gt² [1]. Eliminating t = x/(u cos α) gives y = (u sin α)x/(u cos α) − ½g x²/(u² cos²α) [1]. Since sin α / cos α = tan α, y = x tan α − g x² / (2u² cos²α) [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the substitution for t and the use of tan α = sin α / cos α.

**10. (a)** Substitute: 10 = 30 tan α − 9.8(900) / (2 × 22² × cos²α) [1]. Use 1/cos²α = 1 + tan²α, T = tan α: 10 = 30T − 9.1116(1 + T²) [1]. Rearranged: 9.11T² − 30T + 19.11 = 0 [1]. T = 0.864 or T = 2.43 [1]. **α = 40.8° or α = 67.6°** [1] **[5]**
**(b)** A flatter path and a steeper path can pass through the same point at one launch speed [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Replacing 1/cos²α by 1 + tan²α makes this a quadratic. Name the identity.

**11. (a)** y = x tan α − g x² / (2u² cos²α) [1]. With x = 18: 18 tan 50° = 21.45 and the second term is 9.8(324)/(2 × 441 × cos²50°) = 8.71 [1]. **y = 12.7 m** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** 12.74 − 12 = 0.74, so the ball **clears the wall by about 0.74 m** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** Time to the wall = 18/(21 cos 50°) = 1.33 s [1]. v_y = 21 sin 50° − 9.8(1.333) = 3.02 > 0, so it is still rising [1] **[2]**
**(d)** Air resistance would slow the ball, so it may be lower at the wall [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* A margin of 0.74 m is small, so keep extra digits until the end.

**12. (a)** 20 + 6t − 4.9t² = 0 [1]. t = (6 + √(36 + 392))/9.8 [1]. **t = 2.72 s** (negative root rejected) [1] **[3]**
**(b)** Distance = 8 × 2.723 = **21.8 m** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** v_y = 6 − 9.8(2.723) = −20.7 [1]. Speed = √(8² + 20.7²) = **22.2 m s⁻¹** [1]. Angle = tan⁻¹(20.7/8) = **68.9° below the horizontal** [1] **[3]**
**(d)** 45° below the horizontal means v_y = −8 [1]. 6 − 9.8t = −8, so **t = 1.43 s** [1] **[2]**
**(e)** Air resistance is ignored, so a real flight would be shorter and slower on landing [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Reject the negative root and interpret the sign of v_y.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dropping the constant after integrating, or using the wrong condition.
- Giving displacement when distance travelled is asked.
- Setting the wrong component to zero for "parallel to i".
- Using u instead of its components in the equations.
- Using the range formula for different launch and landing heights.
