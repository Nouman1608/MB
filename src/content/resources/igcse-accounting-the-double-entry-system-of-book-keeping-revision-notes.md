## Debit and credit rules

| Account type | Debit side | Credit side |
|---|---|---|
| Assets | Rise | Fall |
| Drawings and expenses | Rise | Fall |
| Liabilities | Fall | Rise |
| Capital | Fall | Rise |
| Income, such as sales | Fall | Rise |

Memory aid: debit assets, expenses and drawings when they rise; credit liabilities, capital and income when they rise. Debit is not "bad" and credit is not "good". They are only the left and the right.

Returns accounts take the opposite side to the account they reduce: sales returns are debited like an expense, purchases returns are credited like income.

## Common entries

| Transaction | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Owner pays more money into the bank | Bank | Capital |
| Bank loan received | Bank | Loan |
| Non-current asset bought by cheque | The asset | Bank |
| Non-current asset bought on credit | The asset | Supplier's account |
| Goods for resale bought on credit | Purchases | Supplier's account |
| Goods sold on credit | Customer's account | Sales |
| Goods sold, money received into the bank | Bank | Sales |
| Goods sent back to a supplier | Supplier's account | Purchases returns |
| Goods sent back by a customer | Sales returns | Customer's account |
| Customer pays | Bank | Customer's account |
| Supplier paid | Supplier's account | Bank |
| Expense paid from the bank | The expense | Bank |
| Owner takes money for personal use | Drawings | Bank |

## Method: posting

1. Identify the two accounts.
2. Decide whether each is increasing or decreasing.
3. Use the rules to choose debit or credit.
4. Write the date, the **other** account's name and the amount in each account.

## Method: balancing a two-sided account

1. Total both sides.
2. Subtract to find the balance.
3. Put the balance on the smaller side as Balance c/d.
4. Write the same total on both sides.
5. Show the balance again, below the totals, on the other side as Balance b/d.

Torrance Hill Lamps sells lamps on credit to Wyndcombe Hotel for 2,640 on 6 June. The hotel pays 1,000 on 14 June and returns goods worth 340 on 26 June.

```
Wyndcombe Hotel account (June)
Dr                            |Cr
 6 Sales           2,640      |14 Bank              1,000
                              |26 Sales returns       340
                              |30 Balance c/d       1,300
                   2,640      |                     2,640
 1 Balance b/d     1,300
```

The hotel still owes 1,300, so this is a debit balance and a trade receivable. The debit total (2,640) minus the credit entries (1,000 and 340) gives 1,300.

## Running balance reminders

Same entries, one extra column. Work out the balance after every line and label it.

```
Sundby Metals account (purchases ledger), amounts in $
Date    Details            Debit    Credit    Balance
1 Jun   Purchases                    1,800    1,800 Cr
10 Jun  Purchases returns     200             1,600 Cr
20 Jun  Bank                1,000                600 Cr
```

- When the balance is a debit, add debits and subtract credits. When it is a credit, add credits and subtract debits.
- If a payment is larger than the balance, the label flips from Cr to Dr.
- The final line is the closing balance, so no balancing step is needed.

## Transfers to the financial statements

| Balance at the year end | Where it goes |
|---|---|
| Sales (credit) | Statement of profit or loss |
| Sales returns (debit) | Statement of profit or loss |
| Purchases and purchases returns | Statement of profit or loss |
| Wages, insurance and other expenses (debit) | Statement of profit or loss |
| Motor vehicles (debit) | Statement of financial position, non-current assets |
| Bank, customers' accounts (debit) | Statement of financial position, current assets |
| Suppliers' accounts (credit) | Statement of financial position, current liabilities |
| Loan repayable after more than a year | Statement of financial position, non-current liabilities |
| Capital (credit) | Statement of financial position, capital |
| Drawings (debit) | Deducted from capital |

Closing an expense account is a credit in the expense account. Closing an income account is a debit in the income account.

## Reading balances

| Account | Debit balance | Credit balance |
|---|---|---|
| Customer | Customer owes you | You owe the customer |
| Supplier | Supplier owes you | You owe the supplier |
| Bank | Cash at bank | Overdraft |

Say what the figure means in words, not only "debit" or "credit". A debit balance on a supplier's account or a credit balance on a customer's account is unusual, and it is often caused by an overpayment or a return after payment.

## Ledger divisions

| Ledger | Holds |
|---|---|
| Sales ledger | Credit customers' accounts |
| Purchases ledger | Credit suppliers' accounts |
| Nominal (general) ledger | All other accounts |

## Digital ledgers

- Enter a transaction once; the software posts both sides and updates balances.
- Benefits: speed, fewer arithmetic slips, searching, backup, reports.
- Limitations: cost, training, system or power failure, security risk, and wrong account choices post everywhere.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Balance c/d versus a transfer.** Carrying down keeps a balance for next period. A transfer to the statement of profit or loss closes the account to nil.
- **Personal versus nominal accounts.** Customers and suppliers are personal accounts in their own ledgers; everything else is nominal.
- **Two-sided versus running balance.** Layout differs; the double entry does not.

## Quick self-test

All amounts are in dollars.

1. On which side is an expense account increased?
2. State the debit and credit for the owner withdrawing 300 from the bank.
3. State the debit and credit for a bank loan of 5,000 being received.
4. In which ledger is a supplier's account kept?
5. A customer's account shows 480 Dr. Goods worth 220 are sold on credit, goods worth 60 are returned and 400 is paid. What is the new balance?
6. A supplier's account shows a balance of 150 Dr. What does it mean?
7. The debit side of an account totals 7,350 and the credit side totals 6,100. State the side and amount of the balance c/d and the balance b/d.
8. Which of wages, motor vehicles, sales returns and drawings are transferred to the statement of profit or loss?
9. Why must you still understand debit and credit if you use accounting software?
10. A bank account starts with 800 Dr, receives 5,200 and pays out 6,450. State the closing balance and what it means.

### Answers

1. The debit side.
2. Debit Drawings 300; credit Bank 300.
3. Debit Bank 5,000; credit Loan 5,000.
4. The purchases ledger.
5. 240 Dr (480 + 220 - 60 - 400).
6. The supplier owes the business, perhaps through an overpayment or returns that exceed the amount owing.
7. Balance c/d 1,250 on the credit side; balance b/d 1,250 on the debit side.
8. Wages and sales returns. Motor vehicles are in the statement of financial position, and drawings are deducted from capital.
9. Software posts what you tell it. If the wrong account is chosen, the error is posted automatically everywhere.
10. 450 Cr (800 + 5,200 - 6,450 = -450). The bank account is overdrawn, so the business owes the bank.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting an entry on the right account but the wrong side.
- Writing the account's own name in the details column.
- Leaving off Balance b/d, so the next period's opening figure is missing.
- Totals that differ on the two sides of a balanced account.
- Omitting Dr or Cr on a running balance, or getting the label wrong when a balance changes sign.
- Calling every debit balance an amount owed to the business.
- Sending drawings to the statement of profit or loss.
- Using vague words such as "wrong" for an unusual balance, instead of saying who owes whom.
