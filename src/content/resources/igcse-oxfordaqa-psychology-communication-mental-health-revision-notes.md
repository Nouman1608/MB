Condensed for the final weeks. These notes cover two Paper 2 topics of OxfordAQA International GCSE
Psychology (9218): **3.2.1 Communication** and **3.2.3 Mental health**. For the full explanations,
use the [Communication study guide](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-psychology-communication/) and the
[Mental Health study guide](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-psychology-mental-health/).

## 3.2.1 Communication — key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Piaget's theory** | Language depends on thought — concepts first, words later |
| **Sapir-Whorf hypothesis** | Thinking depends on language; strong version = determines, weak version = influences |
| **Verbal communication** | Spoken or written words |
| **Non-verbal communication** | Messages sent without words: eyes, face, posture, touch, space |
| **Postural echo** | Mirroring another person's posture — signals rapport |
| **Personal space** | The area around us we treat as our own; varies with culture, status and gender |

## Language and thought

- **Piaget:** a child understands object permanence before having words for it, so thought comes first.
- **Sapir-Whorf:** the language you speak shapes what you notice and remember.
- **Evidence:** in Native American cultures, the Zuni have one word for yellow and orange and confuse
  them more in colour memory tasks than English speakers. This supports the **weak** version —
  language helps memory, it does not stop perception.

## Human versus animal communication

- Animal communication has **four limited functions**: survival, reproduction, territory, food.
- **Von Frisch:** returning bees perform a **round dance** (food near) or a **waggle dance** (food
  far); the dance angle shows **direction** relative to the sun, the waggling shows **distance**.
- Human language can **plan ahead and discuss future events**, refer to the past and to absent or
  imaginary things, and create unlimited new messages.

## Non-verbal communication

- **Eye contact:** regulates conversation flow, signals attraction, expresses emotion.
- **Body language:** open versus closed posture; postural echo; touch (also signals status).
- **Personal space:** cultural, status (more distance from higher status) and gender differences.

| Innate (Darwin) | Learned |
|---|---|
| Evolved because it was adaptive | Display rules and gestures vary by culture |
| Neonates show expressions without learning | **Yuki (2007):** Japanese participants focus on the **eyes**, Americans on the **mouth** when reading emotion |
| People born blind produce the same basic expressions | |

## 3.2.3 Mental health — incidence and effects

**Why incidence changes:** cultural beliefs about mental health; challenges of modern living (e.g.
isolation); more recognition and less stigma, so more people seek diagnosis.

**Characteristics of mental health:** positive engagement with society; effective coping with
challenges.

| Effects on individuals | Effects on society |
|---|---|
| Damaged relationships | More social care needed |
| Difficulty with day-to-day life | Increased crime rates |
| Poorer physical wellbeing | Costs to the economy |

## OCD and PTSD side by side

| | **OCD** | **PTSD** |
|---|---|---|
| **Diagnosis** | ICD: obsessions (intrusive thoughts) and/or compulsions (repetitive acts) causing distress or taking up time | ICD: after an extremely threatening event — re-experiencing, avoidance, sense of current threat |
| **Biological explanation** | Genes (twin concordance), low serotonin, frontal brain circuits | Genetic vulnerability; over-active amygdala; hippocampus and stress hormones |
| **Psychological explanation** | **Cognitive:** overestimating threat, inflated responsibility, thought suppression | **Dual representation theory:** strong sensory memory poorly linked to verbal, contextual memory, so flashbacks intrude |
| **Therapy** | **CBT**, including exposure and response prevention | **Prolonged exposure therapy:** imaginal and in-vivo exposure |
| **Named study** | **Kearns** — effectiveness of CBT for OCD | **Eftekhari** — effectiveness of prolonged exposure therapy, with US military veterans |

## Worked example

*Sami checks that the front door is locked twenty times before leaving home and feels intense anxiety
if he is stopped. Explain his behaviour using the cognitive explanation of OCD.* (4 marks)

```
1. Obsession: an intrusive thought that the house will be burgled.
2. Faulty thinking: Sami overestimates the likelihood of a burglary
   and feels it would be his fault (inflated responsibility).
3. Compulsion: checking the lock reduces the anxiety briefly.
4. Maintenance: the relief reinforces checking, so the cycle repeats.
```

## Exam traps

- Reversing Piaget and Sapir-Whorf.
- Using Yuki's study as evidence for Darwin's innate view — it supports learning.
- Mixing up obsessions (thoughts) and compulsions (behaviours).
- Saying the rise in recorded mental illness must be a real rise.
- Describing a therapy without linking it to the explanation it targets.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is Paper 2 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Psychology structured?**
2 hours, 100 marks, four 25-mark sections: Communication, Social influences, Mental health and Research methods, with multiple-choice, short-answer and extended-writing questions.

**Why might the incidence of mental health problems seem to rise?**
The specification names increased challenges of modern living, such as isolation, and increased recognition with lessening stigma. The second means some of the rise is in recorded cases rather than real cases.

**Is non-verbal communication innate or learned?**
Both sides have evidence: neonates and people who are sensory deprived show expressions they could not have learned, supporting Darwin, while cultural differences such as Yuki's emoticon study show learning.

## Self-test

1. State the four functions of animal communication.
2. What two pieces of information does a bee's waggle dance give?
3. Give one piece of evidence that non-verbal behaviour is innate.
4. Name the therapy and the named study for PTSD.
5. Give two effects of significant mental health problems on society.

**Answers:** 1. Survival, reproduction, territory, food. 2. The direction and the distance of the
food. 3. Neonates show facial expressions before they could have learned them, or people born blind
produce the same basic expressions as sighted people. 4. Prolonged exposure therapy; Eftekhari's study.
5. Any two of: the need for more social care, increased crime rates, implications for the economy.
