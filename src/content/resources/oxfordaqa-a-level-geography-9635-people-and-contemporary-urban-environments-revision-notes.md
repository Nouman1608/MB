## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Urbanisation | Rise in the proportion of people living in urban areas |
| Urban growth | Rise in the absolute number of urban dwellers |
| Megacity | Urban area of more than 10 million people |
| World city | City with a major command role in the global economy |
| Deindustrialisation | Decline of manufacturing employment |
| Decentralisation | Movement of people, jobs and retail from the centre to the edge |
| Suburbanisation | Growth of residential areas at the urban edge |
| Counter-urbanisation | Movement from cities to small towns and rural areas |
| Urban resurgence | Renewed population and investment in inner areas |
| Informal sector | Unregistered, untaxed work outside state regulation |
| Fortress development | Gated, guarded space with controlled access |
| Gentrification | Renovation of older districts by higher-income newcomers, often displacing existing residents |
| Edge city | Large office and retail centre on the urban fringe |
| Post-modern city | Fragmented, multi-centred city with sharp contrasts and privatised space |
| Urban heat island | Higher temperatures in a city than in its rural surroundings |
| Photochemical smog | Pollution from sunlight acting on nitrogen oxides and hydrocarbons, producing ozone |
| Leachate | Contaminated liquid draining from waste |
| Waste stream | A category of waste with a common origin or material |
| Morbidity | The rate of illness in a population |
| Vector | An organism, such as a mosquito, that carries a disease to people |
| Ecological footprint | Land and water area needed to supply a population and absorb its waste |
| Liveability | The quality of life a city offers its residents |
| SuDS | Sustainable urban drainage systems that slow and store run-off |

## Case facts at a glance

| Item | Detail you can quote | One evaluation point |
|---|---|---|
| UN *World Urbanization Prospects 2018* | 30% urban in 1950, 55% in 2018, 68% projected for 2050; 33 megacities in 2018 | Projections depend on how each country defines "urban" |
| Ruth Glass, 1964 | Coined "gentrification" describing London | Some see gentrification as investment; others as displacement |
| Joel Garreau, 1991 | *Edge City*: offices, retail, more jobs than bedrooms | Fits US cities best; less common elsewhere |
| Wackernagel and Rees, 1996 | Ecological footprint in global hectares | Simplifies complex impacts into one number |
| Eko Atlantic, Lagos | About 9 km² reclaimed from the sea; 8.5 km sea barrier | Locals link works to erosion and surges |
| Makoko, Lagos | Stilt settlement on Lagos Lagoon; July 2012 demolitions left nearly 3,000 homeless | State cites safety; campaigners cite displacement |
| Olusosun landfill, Lagos | About 40 ha; up to 10,000 tonnes a day; now surrounded by housing | Burning and leachate harm nearby residents |
| Singapore HDB and Ethnic Integration Policy | HDB formed 1960; close to 80% in public housing; ethnic quotas since 1989 | Prevents enclaves but limits sellers' choice |
| Singapore waste | Incineration cuts waste by up to 90%; ash to offshore Semakau Landfill (opened 1999) | Needs land-scarce, high-income conditions |
| Singapore ERP | Road pricing from 1 April 1998 | Works with strong public transport |
| Malaria | Female *Anopheles* vector; WHO African Region about 95% of cases and deaths (2024); Singapore malaria-free 1982 | Urban risk depends on drainage and water storage |
| *Anopheles stephensi* | Invasive urban vector found in Djibouti around 2012 | Breeds in containers, so rural-style control is weaker |

## Must-know distinctions

- **Urbanisation versus urban growth:** a proportion versus an absolute number.
- **Megacity versus world city:** size versus global economic function. Singapore is a world city but not a megacity.
- **Suburbanisation versus counter-urbanisation:** moving to the urban edge versus leaving the urban area.
- **Formal versus informal sector:** registered, taxed work versus unregistered work.
- **Fortress development versus gentrified area:** built to exclude versus an existing area changing occupants.
- **Particulate versus photochemical pollution:** solid particles from burning and dust versus ozone from sunlight on exhaust gases.
- **Incineration versus landfill:** volume reduction and energy recovery versus cheap burial with methane and leachate.
- **Communicable versus non-communicable disease:** malaria (vector-borne) versus asthma (not infectious).
- **Mortality versus morbidity:** death rate versus illness rate.
- **Sustainability versus liveability:** long-term environmental, social and economic balance versus present quality of life.

## Urban climate in one view

| Element | Urban effect | Main cause |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | Warmer, especially calm clear nights | Heat storage, waste heat, less evaporation |
| Precipitation | More frequent, intense convective rain | Rising warm air, extra condensation nuclei |
| Fog and thunderstorms | More fog; more summer storms | Nuclei; strong heating |
| Wind | Lower average speed; local funnelling and gusts | Rough surface; canyon effect |
| Air quality | Particulates and photochemical smog | Traffic, industry, burning, sunlight |

## Mini essay plan

**Question type:** "With reference to two contrasting urban areas, evaluate the success of strategies to manage environmental problems."

1. **Introduction:** define environmental problems (air, water, drainage, waste, dereliction). Name both cities. Set the criterion: success means lasting improvement for all residents, not only the wealthy.
2. **Singapore, waste:** incineration and Semakau. Strength: up to 90% volume reduction and energy recovery. Limit: landfill space is finite and incineration still needs ash disposal.
3. **Singapore, air quality:** ERP since 1998 manages congestion. Strength: linked to good public transport. Limit: pricing alone does not cut regional haze or all emissions.
4. **Lagos, waste:** Olusosun landfill. Limit: burning and leachate harm surrounding residents. Context: rapid growth outpaced planning.
5. **Lagos, transport and drainage:** BRT from 2008 and the Blue Line from 2023. Strength: alternatives to congested roads. Limit: drainage still fails where drains are too few or poorly maintained.
6. **Judgement:** strategies succeed most where the state controls land and can fund infrastructure. They are weakest where informal growth outpaces planning. Success also varies within each city.

## Quick self-test

1. State the difference between urbanisation and urban growth.
2. Give one city that is a world city but not a megacity.
3. Name the four stages of urban change in the more developed world listed in the specification.
4. Define a fortress development.
5. Why is the urban heat island usually strongest on calm, clear nights?
6. What is photochemical smog?
7. Give two environmental impacts of landfill.
8. What is *Anopheles stephensi*, and why does it matter for cities?
9. Name one non-communicable disease and one urban factor linked to it.
10. What does an ecological footprint measure?
11. What policy has Singapore used since 1989 to reduce ethnic segregation in public housing?
12. What two themes must the two contrasting case studies include?

### Answers

1. Urbanisation is a rise in the proportion urban; urban growth is a rise in the number of urban dwellers.
2. Singapore.
3. Urbanisation, suburbanisation, counter-urbanisation and urban resurgence.
4. A gated, guarded development with controlled access.
5. Clear skies let heat radiate away from rural areas fast, while city surfaces release stored heat; calm air means the warm air is not mixed away.
6. Ground-level ozone pollution formed when sunlight acts on nitrogen oxides and hydrocarbons.
7. Two from: methane emissions, leachate entering groundwater, land take, odour and pests.
8. An invasive malaria mosquito that breeds in urban water containers; it can spread malaria in cities.
9. Asthma; traffic pollution near main roads, or damp housing.
10. The land and water area needed to supply a population's resources and absorb its waste.
11. The Ethnic Integration Policy.
12. Patterns of economic and social wellbeing; the nature and impact of physical environmental conditions.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using "megacity" when the question asks about world cities, or treating the two as one idea.
- Listing the four stages of urban change without saying what drives each one.
- Describing new urban landscapes with no named example or no link to inequality or segregation.
- Saying the heat island peaks at midday, or forgetting the role of calm, clear conditions.
- Comparing incineration and landfill in general instead of in a specified urban area, as the specification requires.
- Treating malaria as purely rural and missing drainage, water storage and invasive urban vectors.
- Naming a broad group such as "cancer" rather than one specific non-communicable disease.
- Writing case studies as fact lists with no link to sustainability or residents' experience and attitudes.
- Giving a final judgement that just repeats the question rather than weighing evidence.
