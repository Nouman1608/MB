## Section A

**1.** Define the term **export**. **[1]**

**2.** State **two** benefits of international trade to a country. **[2]**

**3.** State **two** challenges of international trade for an importer. **[2]**

**4.** Calculate the Balance of Trade for a country with exports of goods worth $48 million and imports of goods worth $62 million. State whether this is a surplus or a deficit. **[2]**

**5.** Identify the trade restriction described: "a ban on trading with a country". **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** A country's trade figures for one year are shown ($ million).

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Exports of goods | 520 |
| Imports of goods | 480 |
| Exports of services | 130 |
| Imports of services | 175 |
| Net income and investment flowing in | 15 |

**(a)** Calculate the Balance of Trade. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the Balance of Payments using all the items. **[3]**

**(c)** Interpret your answers to (a) and (b). **[2]**

**7.** Explain **two** ways in which a hotel chain could improve a country's Balance of Payments. **[4]**

**8.** An importer must pay a supplier 18,000 units of a foreign currency. At first, 1 dollar buys 3.0 units. Later 1 dollar buys 2.0 units. Calculate the cost in dollars at each rate, and state the effect on the importer. **[4]**

**9.** An imported tablet costs a retailer $250. The government puts a 30% tariff on imported tablets. Calculate the new cost of the tablet to the retailer and explain one effect on the retailer's business. **[4]**

**10.** Describe **two** features of a trading bloc. **[2]**

**11.** Discuss whether a small country that exports coffee should join a trading bloc. **[6]**

**12.** Explain how an insurance guarantee supports an exporter. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** A good or service sold to another country [1].

**2.** Any two from: wider choice of goods; higher standard of living; lower cost of imports; more investment; creation of jobs [1 each].

**3.** Any two from: distance; language; methods of payment; documentation; transport; customs duties; non-payment; currencies; foreign exchange rates; competition; different legal systems [1 each].

**4.** 48 − 62 = −$14 million [1]; a deficit [1].

**5.** An embargo [1].

**6.** **(a)** 520 − 480 = +$40 million [1].

**(b)** Balance on services = 130 − 175 = −45 [1]. Balance of Payments = 40 − 45 + 15 [1] = +$10 million [1].

**(c)** The Balance of Trade is a surplus because exports of goods are greater than imports of goods [1]. The Balance of Payments is also a surplus (+10), because the services deficit is smaller than the surplus from goods and the income from abroad [1].

**7.** Any two developed: the hotels attract foreign tourists [1] who pay in foreign currency, which is an invisible export that brings money into the country [1]; the hotel chain may invest abroad or sell services abroad [1] and earn income that flows back to the country [1]; the hotels buy locally produced food [1], which reduces the need for imports [1]. Maximum 4.

**8.** At 3.0 units per dollar: 18,000 ÷ 3.0 = $6,000 [1]. At 2.0 units per dollar: 18,000 ÷ 2.0 = $9,000 [1]. The cost rises by $3,000 [1], so the importer pays more for the same goods and may have to raise prices or accept lower profit [1].

**9.** Tariff = 30% × $250 = $75 [1]. New cost = $325 [1]. Effect, for example: the retailer must raise the selling price [1], so some customers may buy less or choose locally made tablets [1].

**10.** Any two from: members reduce or remove tariffs and quotas on trade with each other [1]; members agree common rules [1]; there may be a common tariff on goods from outside the bloc [1].

**11.** Any six from: for joining: larger market for coffee exports with no or lower tariffs [1]; more sales and jobs in coffee growing [1]; cheaper imports of machinery and other goods from members [1]; may attract foreign investment [1]; against joining: must follow the bloc's rules [1]; local firms face more competition from members [1]; may lose trade with countries outside the bloc if the bloc has a common tariff [1]; a judgement that is justified, such as that joining is likely to help if most of its coffee customers are already in the bloc [1]. Maximum 6.

**12.** It protects the exporter [1] if a foreign buyer does not pay, so the insurer pays part or all of the money owed [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 4: writing 14 without a minus sign or the word deficit.
- Question 6: leaving out the services deficit, or adding it instead of subtracting.
- Question 6(c): stating the numbers again without interpreting.
- Question 8: dividing the wrong way round or not saying what the change means.
- Question 9: calculating 30% but not adding it to $250.
- Question 11: listing points for one side only and no judgement.

## Quick reference for checking your answers

| Item | Key point |
|---|---|
| Balance of Trade | Exports of goods − imports of goods |
| Balance of Payments | All money flows in and out |
| Tariff | Tax on imports |
| Quota | Limit on imports |
| Embargo | Ban |
