2027-2029 Cambridge O Level Environmental Management (5014) syllabus. The contexts are an island

## Section A

**1.** Define *population density*. **[1]**

**2.** State the unit usually used for population density. **[1]**

**3.** State the age range of the elderly dependent population. **[1]**

**4.** State what the letters MIC stand for. **[1]**

## Section B

**5.** An island country has an area of 9 000 km² and a population of 1 350 000. Most people live in a
narrow strip around the coast; the mountainous interior has very few people.
(a) Calculate the population density of the island. **[1]**
(b) Explain why the density value alone does not describe how people live on the island. **[2]**
(c) Suggest **two** reasons why few people live in the interior. **[2]**

**6.** The table shows data for three districts of a country. *The data are invented for practice.*

| District | Population | Area / km² |
|---|---|---|
| North | 480 000 | 12 000 |
| Central | 2 100 000 | 3 500 |
| South | 90 000 | 15 000 |

(a) Calculate the population density of each district. **[3]**
(b) Identify the most sparsely populated district. **[1]**
(c) Describe the population distribution of the country. **[2]**

**7.** A population pyramid for Country X shows these totals for both sexes: 0-14 years, 44%; 15-64
years, 53%; 65 and over, 3%.
(a) Calculate the percentage of the population that is dependent. **[1]**
(b) Calculate the dependency ratio. **[2]**
(c) Describe the population structure of Country X. **[3]**

**8.** A population pyramid for Country Y has a narrow base, straight sides up to the 60-64 group and a wide
top. In the 80+ group the female bar is twice as long as the male bar.
(a) Describe what the pyramid shows about birth rate and life expectancy. **[2]**
(b) Interpret the difference between males and females in the 80+ group. **[1]**

**9.** In a pyramid for a mining town, the male bars for ages 20-44 are much longer than the female bars.
Suggest a reason for this. **[2]**

**10.** Describe how the World Bank classifies countries into income groups. **[2]**

**11.** Compare the population structure of a typical LIC with that of a typical HIC. **[4]**

**12.** Describe **two** problems that a large young dependent population causes for a government. **[2]**

## Section C

**13.** Using the idea of dependants and the working population, discuss whether Country X (Question 7)
or Country Y (Question 8) faces the greater challenge in providing services. **[6]**

**14.** Discuss the usefulness and limitations of population density and population pyramids for planning
schools and hospitals in a country. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** The number of people living in an area. **[1]**

**2.** People per km² (square kilometre). **[1]**

**3.** 65 years and over. **[1]**

**4.** Middle-income country. **[1]**

**5.** (a) 1 350 000 / 9 000 = **150 people per km²**. **[1]**
(b) Density is an average for the whole island **[1]**, but the distribution is uneven: the coastal strip is far
more densely populated than 150 per km², and the interior far less **[1]**.
(c) Any two: steep slopes make building and farming difficult **[1]**; colder climate or poor, thin soils at
altitude **[1]**; poor transport links and few jobs or services **[1]**. **[max 2]**

**6.** (a) North: 480 000 / 12 000 = **40 per km²** **[1]**; Central: 2 100 000 / 3 500 = **600 per km²**
**[1]**; South: 90 000 / 15 000 = **6 per km²** **[1]**.
(b) South. **[1]**
(c) The distribution is uneven **[1]**: people are concentrated in the Central district (600 per km²), while the
South is sparsely populated (6 per km²) **[1]**.

**7.** (a) 44 + 3 = **47%**. **[1]**
(b) 47 / 53 **[1]** x 100 = **88.7** dependants per 100 working-age people **[1]**.
(c) A very large young population (44% aged 0-14) shows a high birth rate **[1]**; very few elderly (3%) shows
a low life expectancy or high death rate **[1]**; the pyramid would have a wide base and narrow top, with
just over half the population of working age (53%) **[1]**.

**8.** (a) Narrow base shows a low birth rate **[1]**; straight sides and a wide top show a low death rate and
high life expectancy **[1]**.
(b) Women live longer than men, so twice as many women as men survive beyond 80. **[1]**

**9.** Men have migrated to the town to work in the mines **[1]**, which are jobs that employ mainly men of
working age, while women and children stay in their home areas **[1]**.

**10.** By gross national income (GNI) per person **[1]** into low-income, middle-income and high-income
groups, with thresholds updated regularly **[1]**.

**11.** Any four paired comparisons, one mark each: the LIC has a wider base than the HIC, showing a higher
birth rate **[1]**; the LIC's sides narrow more quickly, whereas the HIC's are straighter **[1]**; the HIC has a
wider top, with many more elderly people **[1]**; life expectancy is higher in the HIC **[1]**; the LIC's
dependants are mostly young, whereas the HIC's are increasingly elderly **[1]**. **[max 4]**

**12.** Any two: more schools and teachers are needed **[1]**; more child healthcare and vaccination
**[1]**; many jobs must be created as the young reach working age **[1]**; more housing will be needed **[1]**.
**[max 2]**

**13.** **[6]** One mark per point, up to six. Country X: dependency ratio of 88.7, higher than many
countries **[1]**; most dependants are young, so it needs schools, child health and jobs **[1]**; but the
young will become workers, so pressure may ease later **[1]**. Country Y: low birth rate means fewer
future workers **[1]**; many elderly dependants need pensions and expensive long-term healthcare **[1]**;
the number of elderly will keep rising as life expectancy is high **[1]**. Conclusion: Country X faces the
larger immediate challenge because of its high ratio, while Country Y faces a growing long-term challenge
**[1]**. **[max 6]**

**14.** **[6]** One mark per point, up to six. Density: shows where people are concentrated, so services
can be placed where most people live **[1]**; but a national average hides uneven distribution, so district
values are needed **[1]**. Pyramids: show how many children will need schools **[1]** and how many elderly
will need hospitals and care **[1]**; but they are a snapshot and do not show future migration **[1]**, and a
national pyramid may hide differences between regions **[1]**. Conclusion: the two are most useful together,
at district level and updated regularly **[1]**. **[max 6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out the unit for density.
- Describing a pyramid without quoting any values.
- Writing two separate descriptions in a compare question.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to know the World Bank income thresholds?**
No. The syllabus asks you to describe the income groups (LICs, MICs and HICs), and the thresholds are
revised each year.

**Is the dependency ratio named in the syllabus?**
The syllabus names young and elderly dependent populations and the working population; the dependency
ratio is a calculation that combines them, and the syllabus's mathematical requirements include ratios and
percentages.
