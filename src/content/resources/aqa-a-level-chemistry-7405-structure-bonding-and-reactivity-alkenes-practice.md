These questions cover section **3.3.4.1 Structure, bonding and reactivity** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2. Learn the ideas first in the [Structure, bonding and reactivity study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-structure-bonding-and-reactivity-alkenes/) and the [Structure, bonding and reactivity revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-structure-bonding-and-reactivity-alkenes-revision-notes/). Use mean bond enthalpies in kJ mol⁻¹: C–C 347, C=C 614, C–H 413, H–H 436. Use Aᵣ values H = 1.0, C = 12.0.

## Questions

**1.** Define the term unsaturated, as applied to a hydrocarbon. **[1]**

**2.** Propene is CH₃CH=CH₂.

**(a)** State the number of sigma bonds and the number of pi bonds in one molecule of propene. **[2]**

**(b)** State the bond angle around the carbon atoms of the double bond. **[1]**

**(c)** State the shape around the carbon atoms of the double bond. **[1]**

**3.** Describe how the pi bond in an alkene is formed and where its electron density lies. **[3]**

**4.** Explain why there is no free rotation about the carbon–carbon double bond. **[2]**

**5.** But-2-ene shows E/Z isomerism, but but-1-ene does not.

**(a)** Explain why but-1-ene does not show E/Z isomerism. **[1]**

**(b)** Draw or describe the (Z) isomer of but-2-ene. **[1]**

**6.** Alkenes are more reactive than alkanes. Explain why, referring to electrons in the bond and the type of species that attack alkenes. **[3]**

**7.** Use the mean bond enthalpies to calculate the enthalpy change for the reaction CH₂=CH₂ + H₂ → CH₃CH₃. **[3]**

**8.** Use the mean bond enthalpy values to estimate the bond enthalpy of the pi bond in a C=C bond. State what your answer shows about the strength of the pi bond compared with the sigma bond. **[2]**

**9.** A hydrocarbon X has relative molecular mass 84.0 and contains 85.7 % carbon by mass.

**(a)** Calculate the molecular formula of X. **[2]**

**(b)** X decolourises bromine water. State what this shows about X, and state why the molecular formula alone does not prove this. **[2]**

**10.** Alkenes with the molecular formula C₅H₁₀ include straight-chain isomers.

**(a)** Name all the straight-chain alkenes of formula C₅H₁₀, including stereoisomers. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why the stereoisomers you named are able to exist. **[2]**

**11.** Explain, with reference to the bonding in ethene and in ethane, why ethene reacts with bromine at room temperature in the dark whereas ethane does not. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** It contains at least one carbon–carbon double (or triple) bond, so it holds fewer hydrogen atoms than a saturated hydrocarbon with the same number of carbons [1]. **[1]**

**2. (a)** 8 sigma bonds [1]; 1 pi bond [1]

**(b)** About 120° [1]

**(c)** Trigonal planar [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Count the C–H bonds first (six in propene), then the two carbon–carbon sigma bonds, and remember that the double bond contributes one sigma bond only.

**3.** Formed by sideways overlap of p orbitals on the two carbon atoms [1]; electron density lies above and below the plane of the molecule [1]; it lies in two regions, not directly between the nuclei [1]. **[3]**

**4.** Rotation would twist the p orbitals out of alignment [1]; so the sideways overlap would be lost and the pi bond would break [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** One carbon of the double bond has two identical groups (two hydrogen atoms) [1]

**(b)** The two CH₃ groups are on the same side of the double bond (and the two H atoms are on the same side) [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Alkenes have a pi bond, a region of high electron density [1]; the pi electrons are exposed above and below the plane and are less tightly held [1]; they attract electrophiles, whereas the sigma bonds in alkanes are non-polar and not exposed [1]. **[3]**

**7.** Bonds broken: C=C (614) + H–H (436) = 1050 kJ [1]; bonds formed: C–C (347) + 2 × C–H (2 × 413 = 826) = 1173 kJ [1]; ΔH = 1050 − 1173 = **−123 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[3]**

**8.** 614 − 347 = **267 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]; this is less than the C–C bond enthalpy of 347 kJ mol⁻¹, so the pi bond is weaker than the sigma bond [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** n = 84.0 ÷ 14.0 = 6 (the empirical formula is CH₂ since 85.7 % C gives C : H = 1 : 2) [1]; molecular formula **C₆H₁₂** [1]

**(b)** It is unsaturated and contains a C=C double bond [1]; cycloalkanes such as cyclohexane have the same formula, C₆H₁₂, but do not decolourise bromine water [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** pent-1-ene [1]; (E)-pent-2-ene [1]; (Z)-pent-2-ene [1]

**(b)** Rotation about C=C is restricted [1]; and each carbon of the double bond in pent-2-ene carries two different groups, so the groups can be arranged on the same side or on opposite sides [1]. **[5]**

**11.** Ethene has a pi bond, a region of high electron density [1]; the pi electrons are exposed above and below the plane and can induce a dipole in a bromine molecule [1]; bromine acts as an electrophile and bonds to the carbon atoms, so addition occurs [1]; ethane has only non-polar sigma bonds with no exposed electron pair, so it needs ultraviolet light to react by a radical mechanism [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Mention the pi bond for ethene and the absence of a pi bond for ethane; a mark is usually given for the comparison.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling the double bond "two sigma bonds".
- Using 109.5° for the angle around the double bond.
- Forgetting that the pi electrons lie above and below the plane.
- Explaining reactivity by the strength of the double bond rather than by the pi bond's electron density.
- Failing to say why a molecular formula alone does not prove an alkene.
