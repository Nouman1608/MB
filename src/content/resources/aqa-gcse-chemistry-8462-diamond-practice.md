## Section A

**1.** **(a)** State how many covalent bonds each carbon atom forms in diamond. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the type of structure found in diamond. **[1]**

**2.** Diamond is used on the tips of some drills.

**(a)** State the property of diamond that makes it suitable for this use. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the type of bond that gives diamond this property. **[1]**

**3.** State whether diamond conducts electricity. **[1]**

**4.** A diamond crystal is described as having 800 carbon atoms. Each atom is bonded to four others and each
bond joins two atoms. Calculate the number of covalent bonds. **[2]**

## Section B

**5.** Explain why diamond has a very high melting point. **[3]**

**6.** Explain why diamond does not conduct electricity. **[2]**

**7.** A student says: "Diamond has a very high melting point because there are strong forces between its
molecules."

**(a)** Give **two** errors in the student's statement. **[2]**
**(b)** Write a correct explanation. **[3]**

**8.** Diamond and graphite are both forms of carbon.

**(a)** State **one** similarity in the bonding of diamond and graphite. **[1]**
**(b)** State **one** difference in the bonding of diamond and graphite. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why diamond is very hard. **[3]**

**9.** The table gives information about three solids.

| Solid | Hardness | Melting point / °C | Conducts when solid? | Contains |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E | Very hard | above 3500 | No | Carbon only |
| F | Soft | 115 | No | Sulfur only |
| G | Hard | 801 | No | Sodium and chlorine |

**(a)** Identify the solid that is diamond. **[1]**
**(b)** Give **two** pieces of evidence from the table. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why solid G does not conduct although it contains charged particles. **[2]**

**10.** Explain, in terms of structure and bonding, why diamond is very hard, has a very high melting point and
does not conduct electricity. **[6]**

**11.** A student builds a model of diamond using balls for atoms and sticks for bonds.

**(a)** State how many sticks should be attached to each ball inside the model. **[1]**
**(b)** Give **one** limitation of a ball and stick model of diamond. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Four** [1]
**(b)** **Giant covalent** [1]

**2. (a)** It is **very hard** [1]
**(b)** **Covalent** bonds [1]

**3.** **No** [1]

**4.** Bonds = 800 × 4 ÷ 2 [1] = **1600** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Diamond has a giant covalent structure [1]; all the atoms are joined by strong covalent bonds, which must be
broken to melt it [1]; there are very many bonds, so a very large amount of energy is needed [1]. **[3]**

**6.** All four outer electrons of each carbon atom are used in covalent bonds [1]; there are no delocalised
electrons and no ions to carry charge [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Diamond has **no molecules** [1]; the bonds that must be broken are strong **covalent bonds**, not
forces between molecules [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Giant covalent structure [1]; strong covalent bonds must be broken [1]; large amount of energy needed
because there are many bonds [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** Any one: both have **covalent bonds**; both are **giant covalent** structures; both contain only carbon
[1]
**(b)** Each carbon atom in diamond forms **four** bonds, but in graphite it forms **three** [1]
**(c)** Each carbon is bonded to four others by strong covalent bonds [1]; this forms a rigid 3D network in which
atoms cannot slide [1]; the covalent bonds must be broken to scratch or deform it [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** **E** [1]
**(b)** Any two [1] [1]: **very hard**; **melting point above 3500 °C**; **contains only carbon**; **does not
conduct**. **[2]**
**(c)** The ions in G are **held in fixed positions** in the lattice [1] and cannot move to carry charge [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Indicative content, one mark per point, up to 6:
- Diamond is a giant covalent structure [1].
- Each carbon atom forms four strong covalent bonds with other carbon atoms [1].
- The rigid 3D network of strong bonds means atoms cannot slide, so it is very hard [1].
- The covalent bonds must be broken to melt it, and there are very many [1], so it needs a very large amount of
  energy and has a very high melting point [1].
- All four outer electrons are used in bonding, so there are no delocalised electrons or ions and it does not
  conduct [1]. **[6]**

**11. (a)** **Four** [1]
**(b)** Any one: the **sticks are not real bonds** (bonds are shared pairs of electrons); the **atoms are not to
scale**; it shows only a small part of the structure [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using "molecules" or "intermolecular forces" for diamond.
- Not saying that covalent bonds are broken.
- Confusing the number of bonds per carbon atom in diamond and graphite.
- Explaining non-conduction without mentioning that all outer electrons are used in bonds.
- Counting each bond twice in a bond calculation.
