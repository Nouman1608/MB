## 4.1.1 The model in five lines

1. **Bilayer:** phospholipid heads (hydrophilic) face the watery cytoplasm and tissue fluid; tails (hydrophobic) form the core.
2. **Fluid:** phospholipids and many proteins move sideways within a layer; flipping between layers is rare.
3. **Mosaic:** proteins scattered irregularly through the bilayer.
4. **Self-sealing:** an open edge would expose tails to water, so membranes form closed compartments.
5. **Barrier:** ions and polar molecules cannot easily cross the hydrophobic core.

### Protein positions

| Type | Position | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Transmembrane (intrinsic) | Spans the bilayer | Hydrophobic R groups in the core region; hydrophilic R groups at both surfaces |
| Extrinsic (peripheral) | On one surface | Interacts with phospholipid heads or other proteins; no hydrophobic region in the core |

## 4.1.2 Positions to remember

- **Cholesterol:** between phospholipids in **both** layers; –OH near the heads, rings among the tails.
- **Glycolipids:** outer layer only; carbohydrate chain outside.
- **Glycoproteins:** carbohydrate chains on the outer surface only.
- No carbohydrate chain faces the cytoplasm.

## 4.1.3 Roles

| Component | Stability | Fluidity | Permeability | Transport | Signalling | Recognition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phospholipids | Bilayer held by hydrophobic interactions | Sideways movement | Core blocks ions and polar molecules | — | — | — |
| Cholesterol | Packs between phospholipids | Regulates: less fluid when warm, not rigid when cold | Reduces leakage of ions and polar molecules | — | — | — |
| Channel proteins | — | — | Specific routes | Water-filled pores for specific ions | — | — |
| Carrier proteins | — | — | Specific routes | Bind and change shape; facilitated diffusion or active transport | — | — |
| Receptors | — | — | — | — | Binding site complementary to a ligand | — |
| Glycoproteins and glycolipids | Hydrogen bonds with water outside | — | — | — | Some act as receptors | Cell surface antigens |

### Fluidity factors

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Higher temperature | More kinetic energy → more fluid, more permeable |
| More unsaturated fatty acid tails | Kinks stop close packing → more fluid |
| Cholesterol | Buffers fluidity in both directions |

## Permeability investigation in steps

1. Cut equal discs of beetroot; wash to remove pigment from damaged cells.
2. Place equal numbers in equal volumes of water at a range of temperatures for the same time.
3. Measure absorbance of the water with a colorimeter.
4. Interpret: slow rise at lower temperatures (more fluid bilayer); steep rise once membrane proteins denature and the bilayer is disrupted.

Controls: disc size and number, water volume, time, washing. Repeat for means.

## 4.1.4 Cell signalling in three stages

1. **Secretion** of a specific ligand from a cell.
2. **Transport** of the ligand to target cells (blood for hormones; diffusion over short distances).
3. **Binding** to a cell surface receptor with a complementary binding site → receptor changes shape → specific response inside the cell.

Only cells with the matching receptor respond, even though the ligand reaches many cells.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hydrophilic | Interacts with water; polar |
| Hydrophobic | Does not interact with water; non-polar |
| Phospholipid bilayer | Two layers of phospholipids, tails inwards |
| Fluid mosaic model | Fluid bilayer with a scattered mosaic of proteins |
| Transmembrane protein | A protein spanning the bilayer |
| Glycoprotein | Protein with attached carbohydrate chains |
| Glycolipid | Lipid with an attached carbohydrate chain |
| Ligand | A signalling molecule that binds to a specific receptor |
| Cell surface antigen | A molecule, often a glycoprotein, used in cell recognition |

## Exam traps

- Heads face water because they are hydrophilic: say both parts.
- Carbohydrate chains are on the outer face only.
- Cholesterol **regulates** fluidity; it does not simply make the membrane more or less fluid.
- "Fluid" means sideways movement of molecules, not that the membrane is a liquid.
- The three signalling stages must include transport.
- Denaturation is of proteins; phospholipids do not denature, they become more fluid.

## Quick self-test

1. Why do phospholipids form a bilayer in water?
2. What makes the membrane "fluid"?
3. Why can a transmembrane protein not drift out of the bilayer?
4. Where is cholesterol found in the membrane?
5. State the position of carbohydrate chains of glycoproteins.
6. Give two roles of cholesterol.
7. How do unsaturated fatty acid tails affect fluidity?
8. Name the membrane components involved in cell recognition.
9. Distinguish channel proteins from carrier proteins.
10. List the three stages of cell signalling in the syllabus.
11. Why does a hormone carried in the blood affect only some cells?
12. Why does beetroot pigment leak rapidly above about 40 °C?

### Answers

1. Heads are hydrophilic and interact with water; tails are hydrophobic and are hidden from water in the core.
2. Phospholipids and many proteins move sideways within their layer.
3. Its hydrophobic region would be exposed to water, which is unstable.
4. Between phospholipids in both layers of the bilayer.
5. On the outer surface, projecting into the tissue fluid.
6. Any two: regulates fluidity; adds stability; reduces permeability to ions and polar molecules.
7. Kinks at double bonds stop tails packing closely, so the membrane is more fluid.
8. Glycoproteins and glycolipids (acting as cell surface antigens).
9. Channels are water-filled pores used only in facilitated diffusion; carriers bind the substance and change shape, in facilitated diffusion or active transport.
10. Secretion of a ligand; transport to target cells; binding to cell surface receptors.
11. Only those cells have receptors with a binding site complementary to the hormone.
12. Membrane proteins denature, leaving gaps, and the bilayer becomes much more fluid, so it no longer acts as a barrier.
