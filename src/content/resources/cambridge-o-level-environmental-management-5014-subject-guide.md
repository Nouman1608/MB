Cambridge O Level Environmental Management (5014) is the study of the relationship between people and the environment they depend on. It looks at where resources such as rocks, energy, soil and water come from, how human activity pollutes and degrades the land, water and atmosphere, how hazards and population change affect communities, and what can be done to manage all of this sustainably. This guide describes the 2027-2029 syllabus. Candidates sit two papers, Paper 1 Principles of Environmental Management and Paper 2 Environmental Management in Context; each is 105 minutes long, carries 80 marks and counts for 50% of the qualification.

## How the course is organised

Seven topics make up the syllabus content. Rather than reading them as a list, it helps to see them as a journey from the ground beneath us to the people living on it.

| Topic | Name | Main areas |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Natural resources | Formation of rocks; extraction of rocks, ores and minerals; sustainable management of rocks, ores and minerals; energy resources; conservation and management of energy resources; fracking |
| 2 | Land | Soils and crop growth; food production and crop yield; soil erosion |
| 3 | Water | Water sources and supply; water pollution; water-related diseases; marine aquaculture; oil pollution; plastic pollution |
| 4 | The atmosphere and human activities | The atmosphere; climate change; acid rain; ozone depletion |
| 5 | Ecosystems, biodiversity and fieldwork | Ecosystems; forest ecosystems; managing biodiversity; fieldwork investigations |
| 6 | Natural hazards | Earthquakes and volcanoes; tropical cyclones; flooding; drought |
| 7 | Human population | Population density, distribution and structure; population size; managing population size |

Topics 1 to 4 concentrate on physical resources and the environmental systems people affect. Topic 5 turns to living systems and to how environmental questions are investigated in the field. Topics 6 and 7 consider the forces, natural and human, that put communities and environments under pressure.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Principles of Environmental Management | 105 minutes | 80 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | Environmental Management in Context | 105 minutes | 80 | 50% |

**What the papers contain.** Paper 1 is built from short-answer and structured questions, with extended response questions based on source material. Paper 2 uses short-answer questions, data processing and analysis, and extended response questions, also based on source material. In both papers candidates answer all questions, so there is no choice to plan around.

**Assessment objectives.** Across the qualification, AO1 accounts for 43%, AO2 for 30% and AO3 for 27%.

**Other features.**

- Both papers are externally assessed, and assessment is linear.
- The qualification is not tiered, and grades are awarded from A* to E.
- Examinations take place in the June series, and in the November series in Mauritius only.

**Editions and timing.** The 2027-2029 edition is the one to follow for examinations from 2027. The previous 2025-2026 edition still has one final sitting, in the November 2026 series in Mauritius. In that edition Paper 1 is titled Theory, with Section A worth 20 marks and Section B 60 marks, and Paper 2 is titled Management in context; durations, marks and weightings are the same as in the newer edition.

## How the parts fit together

Because Paper 1 tests principles and Paper 2 tests application in context, every topic is relevant to both papers. What changes between them is the emphasis: recall and explanation in one, interpretation and judgement about a given situation in the other.

Several threads run through the topics:

- **Sustainability.** The phrase "sustainable management" appears in Topic 1, and the same question, how to meet needs without exhausting or damaging the resource, returns in land, water, biodiversity and population.
- **Pollution pathways.** Energy use in Topic 1 leads to atmospheric change in Topic 4; farming in Topic 2 affects water in Topic 3; oil and plastic pollution in Topic 3 damage ecosystems in Topic 5.
- **Risk and vulnerability.** Natural hazards in Topic 6 have greater impact where population density in Topic 7 is high, which makes these two topics natural partners.
- **Evidence.** Fieldwork investigations in Topic 5 teach you how environmental data are gathered. That understanding helps whenever a question asks you to process, analyse or evaluate data, which is central to Paper 2.

## Planning your study

The syllabus order works well as a teaching sequence, but planning around the three threads above can make revision more efficient.

**First part of the course.** Topics 1, 2 and 3. Pay attention to the vocabulary of extraction, sustainability and pollution, since it recurs constantly.

**Second part of the course.** Topics 4 and 5. Use any practical or fieldwork opportunities to learn about sampling, measurement and the reliability of results.

**Third part of the course.** Topics 6 and 7, followed by a review that links each topic to the sustainability, pollution and risk threads.

**Final stage.** Work on full papers. Because all questions are compulsory, gaps in any topic cost marks, so use topic-by-topic practice to find weak areas before attempting complete papers. Practise handling unfamiliar source material and data regularly, as both papers draw on it. The course's exam-preparation pages explain the approach to each paper.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is Cambridge O Level Environmental Management 5014 assessed?**
Through two compulsory, externally assessed papers: Paper 1 Principles of Environmental Management and Paper 2 Environmental Management in Context. Each is 105 minutes, 80 marks and 50% of the qualification.

**Is there any choice of questions in 5014?**
No. Candidates answer all questions in both papers.

**How are the assessment objectives weighted?**
AO1 is 43%, AO2 is 30% and AO3 is 27% of the qualification.

**When are the 5014 exams held?**
In the June series, and in the November series in Mauritius only.

**What happens to the 2025-2026 edition?**
Its final sitting is the November 2026 series in Mauritius. From 2027 the 2027-2029 edition applies.
