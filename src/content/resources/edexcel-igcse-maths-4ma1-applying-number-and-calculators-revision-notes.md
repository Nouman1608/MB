## 1.10 B: Metric conversions

### Core facts

| Quantity | Facts to know |
|---|---|
| Length | 10 mm = 1 cm; 100 cm = 1 m; 1000 m = 1 km |
| Mass | 1000 mg = 1 g; 1000 g = 1 kg; 1000 kg = 1 tonne |
| Capacity | 1000 ml = 1 litre; 100 cl = 1 litre |
| Area | 1 cm² = 100 mm²; 1 m² = 10 000 cm²; 1 km² = 1 000 000 m² |
| Volume | 1 cm³ = 1000 mm³; 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³ |
| Volume ↔ capacity | 1 cm³ = 1 ml; 1000 cm³ = 1 litre; 1 m³ = 1000 litres |

### Method in steps: any unit question

```
1. Decide the unit the answer must be in.
2. Convert every LENGTH into a matching unit first.
3. Then work out the area or volume.
4. Smaller unit -> multiply; larger unit -> divide.
5. Sense-check: a room is about 10-30 m², a bath holds about 100-200 litres.
```

**Quick reminder.** A box 30 cm × 20 cm × 15 cm has volume 9000 cm³ = 9 litres. A garden 12 m × 8 m has area 96 m² = 960 000 cm².

### Must-know distinction: length factor vs area factor vs volume factor

| From → to | Length | Area | Volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| m → cm | × 100 | × 10 000 | × 1 000 000 |
| cm → mm | × 10 | × 100 | × 1000 |
| km → m | × 1000 | × 1 000 000 | not needed |

The factor is squared for area and cubed for volume.

## 1.10 C: Time

### Facts

- 60 seconds = 1 minute; 60 minutes = 1 hour; 24 hours = 1 day.
- 24-hour times use four digits: 7:05 pm = 19:05; 12:30 am = 00:30.

### Method in steps: time between two clock times

```
1. Count on to the next whole hour.
2. Count whole hours.
3. Count the leftover minutes.
4. Add the pieces. Cross midnight by counting to 00:00 (or 24:00).
```

### Decimal hours

```
decimal hours -> h and min: multiply the decimal part by 60
minutes -> decimal hours:   divide by 60
```

| Minutes | 6 | 12 | 15 | 20 | 30 | 45 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hours | 0.1 | 0.2 | 0.25 | 0.3̇ | 0.5 | 0.75 |

**Must-know distinction:** 1.5 h is 1 h 30 min, not 1 h 50 min. 1 h 50 min is 1.83 h (to 2 d.p.).

Use decimal hours for any multiplication (pay rates, flow rates, costs per hour). Use hours and minutes for clock answers.

## 1.10 C: Money

### Rules

- Money answers to 2 d.p. (the nearest penny) unless told otherwise: £7.2 → **£7.20**.
- Convert pence to pounds before mixing: 45p = £0.45.
- Do not round in the middle of a money calculation; round the final total.

### Best buy (value for money)

```
1. Find the cost of ONE common amount (per 100 g, per litre, per item),
   or the amount per £1.
2. Show both values.
3. State which is better value.
```

Without the comparison values, a correct choice earns no marks.

### Bills and wages

| Type | How to calculate |
|---|---|
| Hourly pay | hours (as a decimal) × rate |
| Overtime "time-and-a-half" | overtime hours × rate × 1.5 |
| Standing charge | number of days × daily charge |
| Units used | number of units × price per unit |
| Adding VAT at r% | subtotal × (1 + r/100) |

### Currency conversion

For a rate "£1 = k units of currency X":

```
pounds -> X:  multiply by k
X -> pounds:  divide by k
X -> Y via pounds:  divide by k_X, then multiply by k_Y
```

**Sense-check:** if £1 buys more than 1 unit of X, the X amount must be bigger than the pound amount.

**Comparing prices in two countries:** convert one price into the other currency, then subtract. Say clearly where it is cheaper and by how much.

## 1.10 A: Everyday problems

- Read the final sentence first: what is the answer and its unit?
- Label every line of working ("cost of paint =", "tins needed =").
- **Round up** when you must have enough (tins, packs, buses, bags).
- **Round down** when you can only fit or afford whole items (boxes in a lift, tickets with a budget).
- For "which option is cheaper" questions, work out both totals and state the difference.
- For "when do two tariffs cost the same", divide the difference in fixed charges by the difference in rates.

## 1.11: Calculator essentials

The specification lists minimum calculator functions on page 42: for both tiers, the four operations, squares, powers and roots, memory, brackets, Σx, Σfx, and sine, cosine, tangent and their inverses; the Higher Tier list adds standard form. Calculators with databanks, text or formula retrieval, QWERTY keyboards, symbolic algebra, or symbolic differentiation or integration are prohibited.

### Method in steps: a "use your calculator" question

```
1. Put brackets round every numerator and denominator
   (or use the fraction template).
2. Put brackets round negative numbers before powers: (−3)² = 9, −3² = −9.
3. Use the √ template over the WHOLE expression under the root.
4. Write down ALL the figures on the display.
5. Then round as asked (s.f. or d.p.).
6. Quick estimate: does the size look right?
```

### Interpreting the display

| Display | Context | Write |
|---|---|---|
| 14.7 | money | £14.70 |
| 2.25 | hours | 2 h 15 min |
| 31.104 | bags needed | 32 bags |
| 7.98 | coaches of 50 for 399 people | 8 coaches |
| 4.6E+05 or 4.6 × 10⁵ | standard form | 4.6 × 10⁵ (not 4.6⁵) |

## Quick self-test

1. Change 3.6 m² into cm².
2. Change 2 500 000 cm³ into m³.
3. How many cm³ are in 4.5 litres?
4. Write 0.85 hours in minutes.
5. Find the time from 09:38 to 14:15.
6. Write 2 hours 12 minutes in hours as a decimal.
7. £1 = €1.15. Change €230 into pounds.
8. Pens cost £2.94 for 6 or £4.75 for 10. Which is better value? Show why.
9. Work out (3.8 + 4.6) / (2.1 × 0.5).
10. Work out √(7.2² + 9.6²).
11. A calculator shows 14.7 as a price in pounds. How should you write it?
12. Hana works a 38-hour week at £11.20 per hour, plus 5 hours of overtime at time-and-a-half. Find her pay for the week.

### Answers

1. 3.6 × 10 000 = **36 000 cm²**.
2. 2 500 000 ÷ 1 000 000 = **2.5 m³**.
3. 4.5 × 1000 = **4500 cm³**.
4. 0.85 × 60 = **51 minutes**.
5. 22 min to 10:00, 4 h to 14:00, 15 min more: **4 h 37 min**.
6. 2 + 12/60 = **2.2 hours**.
7. 230 ÷ 1.15 = **£200**.
8. 6-pack: 294 ÷ 6 = 49p each. 10-pack: 475 ÷ 10 = 47.5p each. **The 10-pack is better value.**
9. 8.4 ÷ 1.05 = **8**.
10. √(51.84 + 92.16) = √144 = **12**.
11. **£14.70**.
12. 38 × 11.20 = £425.60; 5 × 11.20 × 1.5 = £84.00; total **£509.60**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing by 100 instead of 10 000 to change cm² to m², or by 1000 instead of 1 000 000 to change cm³ to m³.
- Working out an area in cm² and a cost per m² without converting, giving an answer 10 000 times too big.
- Writing 3 h 20 min as 3.2 h in a pay calculation (it is 3.3̇ h).
- Counting time across midnight or across the hour as if it were a decimal subtraction.
- Multiplying by the exchange rate when converting back into pounds.
- Comparing prices in two currencies without converting first.
- Giving a best-buy answer with no unit costs shown.
- Rounding 7.98 coaches to 7 or 31.1 bags to 31 when the context needs rounding up.
- Leaving out brackets around a denominator, so the calculator divides by only the first number.
- Rounding a value part-way through a calculation and losing the final accuracy mark.
