These questions cover section 1.2 (statements 1.2.1 and 1.2.2, and the Practical Endorsement rules in section 5g) of the OCR A Level Chemistry A (H432) specification, Version 3.1 (May 2026), for first assessment in 2017. H432 is the full A level, so no question is labelled AS or A2. The endorsement itself is teacher assessed in the lab; these written questions practise the technique knowledge that 1.1.2(a) also brings into the written papers.

Work through the [study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-practical-skills-practical-endorsement/) or the [revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-practical-skills-practical-endorsement-revision-notes/) before you start. Other H432 topics are on the [OCR A Level Chemistry hub](/boards/ocr/a-level/chemistry/), and every statement is on the [printable checklist](/checklists/ocr/a-level/chemistry/).

## Questions

**1.** **(a)** State how a Practical Endorsement result is reported on the certificate. **[1]**
**(b)** State the minimum number of assessed practical activities needed for a Pass, and which Practical Activity Groups they must cover. **[2]**

**2.** You prepare cyclohexene by heating cyclohexanol with concentrated phosphoric acid and distilling off the product. Cyclohexene is highly flammable; concentrated phosphoric acid is corrosive. For each substance, state the risk and a specific control measure. **[4]**

**3.** You need 250.0 cm³ of 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ potassium iodate, KIO₃ (M = 214.0 g mol⁻¹).
**(a)** Calculate the mass of solid needed. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe how to make up the standard solution from the weighed solid. **[4]**

**4.** 2.428 g of sulfamic acid, H₃NSO₃ (M = 97.1 g mol⁻¹), a monoprotic strong acid, is made up to 250.0 cm³. 25.0 cm³ portions are titrated with sodium hydroxide. Titres / cm³: 24.80 (rough), 24.35, 24.55, 24.45, 24.40.
**(a)** Calculate the mean titre. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the NaOH concentration, in mol dm⁻³. **[3]**
**(c)** The pipette is ±0.04 cm³ and each burette reading ±0.05 cm³. Calculate the percentage uncertainty in the pipette volume and in the mean titre. **[2]**
**(d)** State a suitable indicator. **[1]**

**5.** 0.0350 g of lithium reacts with water: 2Li + 2H₂O → 2LiOH + H₂. Take Ar(Li) as 6.9; one mole of gas occupies 24.0 dm³ at RTP.
**(a)** Find the hydrogen volume produced, in cm³. **[3]**
**(b)** Suggest suitable apparatus to collect and measure the gas. **[1]**

**6.** **(a)** Explain why an organic reaction mixture is heated under reflux rather than in an open flask. **[2]**
**(b)** State where the thermometer bulb and the water inlet go when distilling the product. **[2]**

**7.** 4.60 g of a crude organic solid is recrystallised from hot water. 3.52 g of dry crystals is obtained.
**(a)** Describe the recrystallisation. **[4]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage recovery. **[1]**
**(c)** Use these data: the pure compound melts at 122 °C. The crude solid melted at 117–120 °C; the crystals at 121–122 °C. Explain what the results show. **[2]**

**8.** On a TLC plate the solvent front moves 8.4 cm. A sample gives two spots, at 4.2 cm and 2.1 cm. A standard of compound A, on the same plate, has Rf 0.50.
**(a)** Work out Rf for both spots. **[2]**
**(b)** State what you can conclude. **[1]**
**(c)** Give two reasons why the baseline is drawn in pencil above the solvent level. **[2]**

**9.** A white solid may contain ammonium, sulfate and bromide ions. Describe tests, in a suitable order, with expected positive observations. **[5]**

**10.** Use these values: Cu²⁺/Cu +0.34 V; Ni²⁺/Ni −0.25 V.
**(a)** Calculate the standard cell potential. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe how to set up the cell and measure its voltage. **[3]**

**11.** A and B react to form iodine. In a clock reaction, a fixed small amount of thiosulfate and starch is included, and t is the time until the blue-black colour appears.

| Run | [A] / mol dm⁻³ | [B] / mol dm⁻³ | t / s |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.050 | 0.10 | 84 |
| 2 | 0.100 | 0.10 | 42 |
| 3 | 0.100 | 0.20 | 21 |

**(a)** Explain why the initial rate here is proportional to 1/t. **[1]**
**(b)** Deduce the order with respect to A and to B. **[2]**
**(c)** Write the rate equation. **[1]**
**(d)** Describe a continuous monitoring method for a different reaction that gives off a gas, and how you would find the initial rate. **[3]**
**(e)** State one way software helps process these results. **[1]**

**12.** Three unlabelled 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ solutions are nitric acid, propanoic acid (Ka = 1.3 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³) and potassium hydroxide. Use Kw = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ mol² dm⁻⁶.
**(a)** Calculate the expected pH of each. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe how to calibrate the pH meter. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why a pH meter is better than a universal indicator chart here. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Separately from the A level grade, as Pass or Not Classified [1]
**(b)** At least 12 [1]; covering all of PAG1 to PAG12 [1]
*Examiner insight:* Both parts of (b) are separate points; giving the number without the PAG coverage earns only one mark.

**2.** Cyclohexene vapour could ignite [1]; heat with an electric heater, no flames nearby [1]. Phosphoric acid could burn skin or eyes if spilled [1]; wear goggles and gloves and add it slowly [1].
*Examiner insight:* A control measure gains credit only when it is linked to the named hazard, so "take care" scores nothing.

**3. (a)** 0.0200 × 0.2500 × 214.0 = **1.07 g** [1]
**(b)** Dissolve the solid in distilled water in a beaker [1]; transfer to a 250 cm³ volumetric flask with rinsings of beaker and rod [1]; make up with distilled water until the bottom of the meniscus is on the line at eye level [1]; stopper and invert to mix [1]
*Examiner insight:* Rinsings and mixing are separate creditworthy steps; leaving either out loses a mark.

**4. (a)** Concordant: 24.35, 24.45, 24.40 (24.55 excluded) [1]; mean = **24.40 cm³** [1]
**(b)** c(acid) = (2.428 / 97.1) / 0.2500 = 0.1000 mol dm⁻³ [1]; n in 25.0 cm³ = 2.500 × 10⁻³ mol = n(NaOH) [1]; c(NaOH) = 2.500 × 10⁻³ / 0.02440 = **0.102 mol dm⁻³** [1]
**(c)** Pipette: 0.04 / 25.0 × 100 = **0.16%** [1]; titre: 0.10 / 24.40 × 100 = **0.41%** [1]
**(d)** **Phenolphthalein or methyl orange** (strong acid, strong base) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Using the rough or a non-concordant titre in the mean usually costs the mark for the mean, so list the titres you used to show your choice.

**5. (a)** n(Li) = 0.0350 / 6.9 = 5.07 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; n(H₂) = 2.536 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; V = 2.536 × 10⁻³ × 24 000 = **60.9 cm³** [1]
**(b)** A 100 cm³ gas syringe (or an inverted burette over water) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Missing the 2 : 1 ratio doubles the answer, and the final answer must be in the unit asked for.

**6. (a)** Volatile reactants or products would evaporate [1]; the vertical condenser returns them to the flask so heating can continue [1]
**(b)** Bulb level with the side arm [1]; water in at the bottom of the condenser [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Stops it boiling dry" is too vague; say that vapour condenses and returns.

**7. (a)** Dissolve in the minimum hot water [1]; filter hot to remove insoluble impurities [1]; cool, then in ice, to crystallise [1]; filter under reduced pressure, wash with ice-cold water and dry [1]
**(b)** 3.52 / 4.60 × 100 = **76.5%** [1]
**(c)** The crude solid melted lower and over a range, so it was impure [1]; the crystals melt sharply close to 122 °C, so they are much purer [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Minimum" and "hot" are both needed for the first mark; excess solvent explains a low recovery.

**8. (a)** 4.2 / 8.4 = **0.50** [1]; 2.1 / 8.4 = **0.25** [1]
**(b)** The sample probably contains A, plus at least one other substance [1]
**(c)** Ink would dissolve and run [1]; spots below the solvent would dissolve into it [1]
*Examiner insight:* Measure to the solvent front, not the top of the plate, or both Rf values are wrong.

**9.** Warm with NaOH(aq) [1]; gas turns damp red litmus blue, showing ammonium [1]; add barium nitrate solution: white precipitate shows sulfate [1]; add silver nitrate: cream precipitate [1]; soluble in concentrated but not dilute ammonia, showing bromide [1]
*Examiner insight:* Barium chloride would add chloride before the halide test, so name barium nitrate when a halide test follows.

**10. (a)** 0.34 − (−0.25) = **+0.59 V** [1]
**(b)** Copper in 1.00 mol dm⁻³ Cu²⁺(aq) and nickel in 1.00 mol dm⁻³ Ni²⁺(aq), at 298 K [1]; join the solutions with a salt bridge of potassium nitrate [1]; connect the electrodes to a high-resistance voltmeter [1]
*Examiner insight:* The voltmeter must be high-resistance so almost no current flows; "a voltmeter" alone may not earn that mark.

**11. (a)** Each run makes the same fixed amount of iodine, so rate is proportional to 1/t [1]
**(b)** Runs 1 to 2: [A] doubles, t halves: **first order in A** [1]; runs 2 to 3: [B] doubles, t halves: **first order in B** [1]
**(c)** **rate = k[A][B]** [1]
**(d)** Collect gas in a gas syringe [1]; record the volume at regular time intervals [1]; plot volume against time and draw a tangent at t = 0; its gradient is the initial rate [1]
**(e)** A spreadsheet calculates 1/t and plots graphs [1]
*Examiner insight:* State the change in each variable and its effect; a bare "first order" without the comparison may not gain credit.

**12. (a)** HNO₃: −log(0.0500) = **1.30** [1]; propanoic acid: [H⁺] = √(1.3 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.0500), pH = **3.09** [1]; KOH: [H⁺] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ / 0.0500, pH = **12.70** [1]
**(b)** Rinse the probe with distilled water [1]; calibrate with buffer solutions of known pH [1]
**(c)** The meter gives a more precise value, so close pH values can be told apart [1]
*Examiner insight:* Treating propanoic acid as fully dissociated gives pH 1.30, which loses the mark; use the Ka expression for a weak acid.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Control measures not linked to a named hazard.
- Rough or non-concordant titres in the mean.
- Missing the rinsing or mixing steps when making up a standard solution.
- Forgetting the 2 : 1 mole ratio in gas-volume questions.
- Placing the thermometer bulb in the liquid during distillation.
- Saying impurities raise the melting point.
- Using barium chloride before a halide test.
- Writing "voltmeter" without "high-resistance".
- Treating a weak acid as fully dissociated in a pH calculation.
