These questions cover subtopic E7.3, Magnitude of a vector, in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. The subtopic is Extended only. Each question is labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator). Column vectors are written (x, y), and |**a**| means the magnitude of **a**. Learn the methods first with the [magnitude of a vector study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-magnitude-of-a-vector-cambridge-igcse-maths/) and the [magnitude of a vector revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-magnitude-of-a-vector-cambridge-igcse-maths-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** (non-calculator) Find |(−15, 8)|. **[2]**

**2.** (non-calculator) Find the exact value of |(4, −8)|, giving your answer in its simplest form. **[2]**

**3.** (calculator) Find |(−3.6, 7.5)|, giving your answer correct to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**4.** (non-calculator) P is the point (−2, 7) and Q is the point (4, −1). Find |PQ|. **[2]**

**5.** (non-calculator) **a** = (3, −4). Find |−4**a**|. **[2]**

**6.** (non-calculator) Show that the vector (1, 2√2) has magnitude 3. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** (calculator) **a** = (5, −1) and **b** = (−2, 3).

**(a)** Find |**a** + **b**|. **[3]**

**(b)** Find |3**a** + 2**b**|. **[3]**

**8.** (non-calculator) The vector (t, t − 7) has magnitude 13. Find the two possible values of t. **[4]**

**9.** (calculator) O is the origin, OA = **a** = (6, 2) and OB = **b** = (2, 6).

**(a)** Show that triangle OAB is isosceles. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the exact length of AB. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the perimeter of triangle OAB, correct to 3 significant figures. **[1]**

**10.** (calculator) A ship sails along the displacement (12, 5) and then along the displacement (−4, 10). Distances are in kilometres.

**(a)** Find how far the ship finishes from its starting point. **[2]**

**(b)** Find how much further the ship sailed than the straight-line distance in part (a). **[2]**

**11.** (non-calculator) Find the two vectors that are parallel to (5, 12) and have magnitude 39. **[3]**

**12.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** **a** = (5, y) and |**a**| = 13. Find the possible values of y. **[2]**

**(b)** **b** = (x, x) and |**b**| = 6√2. Find the possible values of x. **[2]**

**13.** (non-calculator) **p** = (4, −2) and **q** = (1, 2). Show that |**p** + **q**| = |**p** − **q**|. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** √((−15)² + 8²) = √(225 + 64) [1] = √289 = **17** [1]

**2.** √(16 + 64) = √80 [1] = √(16 × 5) = **4√5** [1]

**3.** √(12.96 + 56.25) = √69.21 [1] = **8.32** [1]

**4.** PQ = (6, −8) [1]; |PQ| = √100 = **10** [1]

**5.** |**a**| = 5 [1]; |−4**a**| = 4 × 5 = **20** [1]

*Examiner insight:* you can also find −4**a** = (−12, 16) first; √(144 + 256) = 20. Either way the answer is positive.

**6.** 1² + (2√2)² = 1 + 8 = 9 [1]; √9 = 3, as required [1]

*Examiner insight:* (2√2)² = 4 × 2 = 8. Writing 2 × 2 = 4 is the usual slip.

**7. (a)** **a** + **b** = (3, 2) [1]; |**a** + **b**| = √13 [1] = **3.61** [1]

**(b)** 3**a** + 2**b** = (15 − 4, −3 + 6) = (11, 3) [1]; √(121 + 9) = √130 [1] = **11.4** [1]

**8.** t² + (t − 7)² = 169 [1]; 2t² − 14t + 49 = 169, so t² − 7t − 60 = 0 [1]; (t − 12)(t + 5) = 0 [1]; **t = 12 or t = −5** [1]

*Examiner insight:* (t − 7)² is t² − 14t + 49. Writing t² + 49 loses the middle term. Check: t = 12 gives (12, 5) and t = −5 gives (−5, −12), both of magnitude 13.

**9. (a)** |**a**| = √(36 + 4) = √40 and |**b**| = √(4 + 36) = √40 [1]; OA = OB, so the triangle is isosceles [1]

**(b)** AB = **b** − **a** = (−4, 4) [1]; |AB| = √32 = **4√2** [1]

**(c)** 2√40 + √32 = **18.3** [1]

**10. (a)** Resultant = (8, 15) [1]; distance = √289 = **17 km** [1]

**(b)** Distance sailed = 13 + √116 = 23.77… km [1]; difference = **6.77 km** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the distance sailed is the sum of the two magnitudes, |(12, 5)| = 13 and |(−4, 10)| = √116. The straight-line distance is the magnitude of the sum.

**11.** |(5, 12)| = 13 [1]; 39 ÷ 13 = 3, so multiply by 3 or −3 [1]; **(15, 36) and (−15, −36)** [1]

**12. (a)** 25 + y² = 169, y² = 144 [1]; **y = 12 or y = −12** [1]

**(b)** 2x² = 72 [1]; x² = 36, **x = 6 or x = −6** [1]

**13.** **p** + **q** = (5, 0), so |**p** + **q**| = 5 [1]; **p** − **q** = (3, −4), so |**p** − **q**| = 5 [1]; both equal 5, so the magnitudes are equal [1]

*Examiner insight:* in a "show that" question, give both values and a concluding statement. This happens because **p** and **q** are at right angles, so the parallelogram they form is a rectangle with equal diagonals.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting the square root.
- Squaring a negative entry without brackets.
- Adding magnitudes instead of finding the magnitude of a sum.
- Keeping only one sign when solving for an unknown entry.
- Expanding brackets such as (t − 7)² or (2√2)² wrongly.
