These questions cover section **3.1.4.3 Applications of Hess's law** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Papers 1 and 2 (and Paper 3). Learn the methods in the [Hess's law study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-applications-of-hesss-law/) and the [Hess's law revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-applications-of-hesss-law-revision-notes/). All data are in kJ mol⁻¹ at 298 K.

## Questions

**1.** State Hess's law. **[1]**

**2.** Calculate the standard enthalpy change for the combustion of propane: C₃H₈(g) + 5O₂(g) → 3CO₂(g) + 4H₂O(l). Standard enthalpies of formation: C₃H₈(g) −105, CO₂(g) −394, H₂O(l) −286. **[3]**

**3.** Aluminium reduces iron(III) oxide: Fe₂O₃(s) + 2Al(s) → Al₂O₃(s) + 2Fe(s). Standard enthalpies of formation: Fe₂O₃ −824, Al₂O₃ −1676. Calculate the enthalpy change for the reaction. **[3]**

**4.** Calculate the enthalpy change for NH₃(g) + HCl(g) → NH₄Cl(s). Standard enthalpies of formation: NH₃ −46, HCl −92, NH₄Cl −314. **[3]**

**5.** Standard enthalpies of combustion are: carbon (graphite) −394, hydrogen −286, propane −2219.

**(a)** Draw an enthalpy cycle that can be used to calculate the standard enthalpy of formation of propane. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the standard enthalpy of formation of propane. **[3]**

**6.** Calculate the enthalpy change for the hydrogenation of propene, C₃H₆(g) + H₂(g) → C₃H₈(g). Standard enthalpies of combustion: propene −2058, hydrogen −286, propane −2219. **[3]**

**7.** Use these two equations to calculate the standard enthalpy of formation of sulfur trioxide, SO₃(g).

S(s) + O₂(g) → SO₂(g) ΔH = −297 kJ mol⁻¹

SO₂(g) + ½O₂(g) → SO₃(g) ΔH = −99 kJ mol⁻¹ **[2]**

**8.** The enthalpy of solution of anhydrous copper(II) sulfate is −66 kJ mol⁻¹ and that of CuSO₄·5H₂O is +11 kJ mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Draw an enthalpy cycle linking CuSO₄(s) + 5H₂O(l), CuSO₄·5H₂O(s) and CuSO₄(aq). **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the enthalpy change for CuSO₄(s) + 5H₂O(l) → CuSO₄·5H₂O(s). **[2]**

**9.** The enthalpy change for C(s) + ½O₂(g) → CO(g) cannot be measured directly. Give two reasons. **[2]**

**10.** A student calculates the enthalpy change in question 3 as +852 kJ mol⁻¹. Identify the likely error and explain how to correct it. **[2]**

**11.** In an experiment, 1.00 mol of NaHCO₃(s) reacts with excess hydrochloric acid with ΔH = +12 kJ mol⁻¹, and 1.00 mol of Na₂CO₃(s) reacts with excess hydrochloric acid with ΔH = −29 kJ mol⁻¹.

NaHCO₃(s) + HCl(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g)

Na₂CO₃(s) + 2HCl(aq) → 2NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g)

Calculate the enthalpy change for 2NaHCO₃(s) → Na₂CO₃(s) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g). **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The enthalpy change for a reaction is independent of the route taken, provided the initial and final conditions are the same [1]. **[1]**

**2.** ΣΔfH⦵(products) = 3(−394) + 4(−286) = −1182 − 1144 = −2326 [1]; ΣΔfH⦵(reactants) = −105 [1]; ΔrH⦵ = −2326 − (−105) = **−2221 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[3]**

**3.** ΔrH⦵ = ΣΔfH⦵(products) − ΣΔfH⦵(reactants) [1] = −1676 − (−824) [1] = **−852 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. (The elements Al and Fe have ΔfH⦵ = 0.) **[3]**

**4.** Products: −314 [1]; reactants: −46 + (−92) = −138 [1]; ΔrH⦵ = −314 − (−138) = **−176 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[3]**

**5. (a)** A cycle with 3C(s) + 4H₂(g) + 5O₂(g) at the top left, C₃H₈(g) + 5O₂(g) at the top right (arrow labelled ΔfH⦵ of propane) [1], and 3CO₂(g) + 4H₂O(l) at the bottom with arrows downwards from each, labelled with combustion enthalpies multiplied by the moles [1]

**(b)** ΔfH⦵ = [3(−394) + 4(−286)] − (−2219) [1] = −2326 + 2219 [1] = **−107 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[5]**

**6.** ΔrH⦵ = ΣΔcH⦵(reactants) − ΣΔcH⦵(products) [1] = (−2058 − 286) − (−2219) [1] = **−125 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[3]**

**7.** ΔfH⦵(SO₃) is the sum of the enthalpy changes of the two steps, because they add to S(s) + 1½O₂(g) → SO₃(g) [1]; −297 + (−99) = **−396 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** CuSO₄(s) + 5H₂O(l) on the left, CuSO₄·5H₂O(s) on the right with an arrow labelled ΔH(hydration) [1]; both lead down to CuSO₄(aq) with arrows labelled with the enthalpies of solution (−66 and +11) [1]

**(b)** ΔH(hydration) + (+11) = −66 [1]; ΔH(hydration) = **−77 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Any two of: burning carbon in limited oxygen gives a mixture of CO and CO₂, not pure CO [1]; the reaction cannot be stopped at CO [1]; CO is toxic and difficult to handle in a calorimeter [1]. **[2]**

**10.** The student has subtracted in the wrong order (reactants minus products) [1]; the correct formula for formation data is products minus reactants, which gives −852 kJ mol⁻¹ [1]. **[2]**

**11.** Double the first equation (ΔH = 2 × 12 = +24) [1]; reverse the second equation (ΔH = +29) [1]; add them, so that NaCl, HCl and the extra water and carbon dioxide cancel: ΔH = 24 + 29 = **+53 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Doubling the first equation gives two moles of NaHCO₃ reacting; subtracting the second equation then leaves the target. Using +12 alone loses the factor of two.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting to multiply by the number of moles in the balanced equation.
- Subtracting in the wrong order for formation or combustion data.
- Reversing an equation without changing the sign.
- Leaving off the sign of the final answer.
- Drawing arrows in the wrong direction on a cycle.
