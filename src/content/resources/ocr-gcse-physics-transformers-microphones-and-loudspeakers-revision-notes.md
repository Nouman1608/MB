## P4.2g: how a transformer works

- Two coils, **primary** (input) and **secondary** (output), on the same core. No electrical connection between them.
- Alternating current in the primary gives a **changing** magnetic field in the core.
- The secondary is in this changing field, so a **potential difference is induced** across it.
- A complete secondary circuit carries an alternating current.
- A battery (direct current) gives a **steady** field, so nothing is induced. Transformers need a.c.

### Method in steps: explain a transformer

1. Alternating current in the primary coil.
2. It produces a changing magnetic field in the core.
3. The changing field passes through the secondary coil.
4. A potential difference is induced across the secondary.
5. It drives an alternating current if the circuit is complete.

Use the words **alternating**, **changing** and **induced** in every explanation.

## P4.2h: turns and potential difference

| Type | Turns | Potential difference | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Step-up | Ns > Np | Vs > Vp | Secondary has more turns |
| Step-down | Ns < Np | Vs < Vp | Secondary has fewer turns |
| Equal | Ns = Np | Vs = Vp | Isolates two circuits |

Explaining the dependence: each turn of the secondary is in the same changing field, so each adds to the induced potential difference. More turns therefore means a bigger total. Doubling Ns doubles Vs. Doubling Np (with Vp and Ns unchanged) halves Vs.

## P4.2i: equations

| Equation | Use |
|---|---|
| Vp / Vs = Np / Ns | Potential differences and turns |
| Vp × Ip = Vs × Is | Power in equals power out, ideal transformer |

Rearrangements:

| Find | Formula |
|---|---|
| Vs | Vp × Ns / Np |
| Vp | Vs × Np / Ns |
| Ns | Np × Vs / Vp |
| Np | Ns × Vp / Vs |
| Ip | Vs × Is / Vp |
| Is | Vp × Ip / Vs |

### Method in steps: transformer calculation

1. Write down what you know: Vp, Vs, Np, Ns (and Ip or Is).
2. Choose the equation: turns and potential difference, or power.
3. Rearrange for the unknown before you substitute.
4. Substitute with units.
5. Check: bigger potential difference with bigger turns; bigger potential difference with smaller current.

### Worked reminder

A transformer has 500 turns on the primary and 125 turns on the secondary. The primary potential difference is 80 V.

```
Vs = Vp × Ns / Np = 80 × 125 / 500 = 20 V
```

Fewer secondary turns, so this is step-down.

If the secondary current is 1.5 A and the transformer is ideal:

```
Vp × Ip = Vs × Is
80 × Ip = 20 × 1.5 = 30
Ip = 30 / 80 = 0.375 A
```

The current in the primary is smaller than in the secondary, as it must be when the potential difference is larger.

### Second worked reminder: find the turns

A transformer must change 160 V to 20 V. The primary has 960 turns.

```
Ns = Np × Vs / Vp = 960 × 20 / 160 = 120 turns
```

The ratio of turns is 960 : 120, which is 8 : 1, the same as the ratio of the potential differences, 160 : 20. A quick check like this catches inverted fractions.

### Model answer: explain why Vs is greater than Vp

Use this sentence pattern for any "explain" question on a step-up transformer:

1. The secondary coil has more turns than the primary coil.
2. Each turn is in the same changing magnetic field, so each turn has a potential difference induced across it.
3. The potential differences add along the coil, so the total across the secondary is greater than across the primary.
4. The ratio Vs / Vp is the same as Ns / Np.

## P4.2j: microphone, loudspeaker and headphones

### Microphone (sound to current)

1. Sound wave: pressure variations push the diaphragm.
2. The diaphragm and its attached coil vibrate.
3. The coil moves in the field of a permanent magnet.
4. A potential difference is induced (electromagnetic induction).
5. An alternating current flows, with the same frequency as the sound.

### Loudspeaker and headphones (current to sound)

1. A varying current flows in the coil.
2. The coil is in the field of a permanent magnet, so it feels a force.
3. The force changes direction with the current, so the coil and cone vibrate.
4. The cone makes pressure variations in the air.
5. These pressure variations are the sound wave.

Headphones work in the same way as a loudspeaker.

### Comparing the two

| | Microphone | Loudspeaker |
|---|---|---|
| In | Sound | Current |
| Out | Current | Sound |
| Coil | Moved by the diaphragm | Pushed by the force |
| Louder sound | Larger movement, larger induced potential difference | Larger current, larger force, larger movement |
| Higher pitch | Faster vibration, higher frequency current | Higher frequency current, faster vibration |

### Model answer: explain how a loudspeaker makes sound

1. A varying current from the amplifier flows in the coil.
2. The coil is in the magnetic field of the permanent magnet, so a force acts on it.
3. The current keeps changing direction, so the force on the coil keeps changing direction.
4. The coil and the cone attached to it move back and forth, at the frequency of the current.
5. The cone pushes the air, making pressure variations that travel away as a sound wave.

For a microphone, reverse the chain: sound, diaphragm, coil moving in a field, induced potential difference, varying current.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Primary** and **secondary**: input and output, not first and second in size.
- **Step-up** and **step-down** describe the potential difference, not the current or the power.
- **Potential difference** is stepped up or down; **power** is conserved in an ideal transformer.
- **Induced potential difference** (microphone, transformer secondary) is not the same as **force on a current** (loudspeaker).
- **Alternating** supply gives a changing field; **direct** supply gives a steady one.

## Quick self-test

All questions are Higher Tier only.

1. What is a transformer used for?
2. Why must the supply to the primary coil be alternating?
3. A transformer has 50 primary turns, 400 secondary turns and a 3.0 V a.c. input. Calculate the output.
4. A step-down transformer has Vp = 48 V, Vs = 6.0 V and 90 secondary turns. Calculate the number of primary turns.
5. An ideal transformer has Vs = 12 V, Is = 4.0 A and Vp = 240 V. Calculate Ip.
6. A transformer has 600 primary turns and Vp = 36 V. It needs an output of 9.0 V. How many secondary turns does it need?
7. A transformer has 300 primary turns, 1500 secondary turns and Vp = 20 V. Calculate Vs, and say whether it is step-up or step-down.
8. State what happens to Vs if the number of primary turns is doubled and everything else stays the same.
9. In a microphone, what moves in the magnetic field, and what is induced?
10. What effect makes the cone of a loudspeaker move?
11. Which two devices in this topic change sound into current and current into sound?

### Answers

1. To change the size of an alternating potential difference.
2. A changing current gives a changing magnetic field, which induces a potential difference in the secondary. Direct current gives a steady field and no induced output.
3. Vs = 3.0 × 400 / 50 = **24 V**.
4. Np = Ns × Vp / Vs = 90 × 48 / 6.0 = **720 turns**.
5. Ip = Vs × Is / Vp = 12 × 4.0 / 240 = **0.20 A**.
6. Ns = Np × Vs / Vp = 600 × 9.0 / 36 = **150 turns**.
7. Vs = 20 × 1500 / 300 = **100 V**; step-up, as Ns > Np.
8. Vs is **halved**.
9. The coil, which is attached to the diaphragm, moves; a **potential difference** is induced across it.
10. A force acts on the current-carrying coil in the permanent magnet's field (a current in a magnetic field gives a force).
11. A microphone changes sound to current. A loudspeaker (or headphones) changes current to sound.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out "alternating", "changing" or "induced" in a transformer explanation.
- Saying the current passes through the core from one coil to the other.
- Writing Vp / Vs = Ns / Np, with the coils the wrong way round.
- Not rearranging first, then substituting numbers into the wrong place.
- Calling a transformer with fewer secondary turns step-up.
- Writing that a step-up transformer also steps up the current.
- Describing a microphone as using a force on a coil, or a loudspeaker as using induction.
- Giving no unit, or the wrong unit, on the answer.
