These notes condense subtopic **4.3 Scale drawings** from Topic 4 of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The Core and Extended statements are identical: draw and interpret scale drawings, and use and interpret three-figure bearings, measured clockwise from north (000° to 360°), including the terms north, east, south and west. For worked examples, read the [scale drawings study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-scale-drawings-cambridge-igcse-maths/); for exam-style questions, use the [scale drawings practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-scale-drawings-cambridge-igcse-maths-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Scale drawing | An accurate drawing with every length multiplied by the same factor |
| Scale (ratio) | 1 : n, both parts in the same unit; 1 cm on the drawing is n cm in reality |
| Scale (statement) | e.g. "1 cm represents 3 km" |
| Bearing | Angle measured clockwise from north, three figures |
| North line | Line pointing north drawn at the point you measure from |
| Due east (west, north, south) | Exactly 090° (270°, 000°, 180°) |
| Reverse (back) bearing | Bearing of the return direction |

## Scale conversions

```
1 m = 100 cm      1 km = 1000 m = 100 000 cm

Statement -> ratio: write both in cm
  1 cm to 8 m      ->  1 : 800
  1 cm to 0.5 km   ->  1 : 50 000
Ratio -> statement: divide n to sensible units
  1 : 2500         ->  1 cm to 25 m
  1 : 400 000      ->  1 cm to 4 km
```

**Drawing → real: multiply. Real → drawing: divide.**

| Scale | Drawing | Real |
|---|---|---|
| 1 : 50 | 6.2 cm | 310 cm = 3.1 m |
| 1 : 25 000 | 8 cm | 200 000 cm = 2 km |
| 1 : 100 000 | 3.5 cm | 3.5 km |
| 1 cm to 5 m | 7.4 cm | 37 m |
| 1 : 200 | 1.8 cm | 360 cm = 3.6 m |

Convert once, at the end. Check the answer is sensible: a classroom is a few metres, not a few kilometres.

**Choosing a scale**: divide the longest real length by the space available, then round **up** to a convenient value (1, 2, 5, 10 units per cm).

## Bearings

```
Clockwise from north.  Three figures: 8 -> 008, 63 -> 063.
N = 000   E = 090   S = 180   W = 270
"Bearing of X from Y": stand at Y, north line at Y, measure to X.
```

**Reverse bearings**

```
bearing < 180   ->  add 180
bearing >= 180  ->  subtract 180

070 -> 250     160 -> 340     180 -> 000     245 -> 065     330 -> 150
```

Reason: north lines are parallel, so the angles at each end are co-interior (they add to 180°).

**Directions described from other compass points**

| Description | Bearing |
|---|---|
| 30° east of north | 030° |
| 30° west of north | 330° |
| 30° east of south | 150° |
| 30° west of south | 210° |
| 30° north of east | 060° |
| 30° south of west | 240° |

**Combining due directions**: equal distances due south then due west give 225° from the start; due north then due east, 045°.

## Drawing method with bearings

```
1. Choose or read the scale; convert distances to cm.
2. Mark the start point; draw a north line.
3. Protractor zero on north; measure clockwise (for bearings > 180,
   measure 360 - bearing anticlockwise).
4. Rule the line to the scaled length.
5. New north line at each new point.
6. Measure answers on the drawing; convert back with the scale.
```

## Exam traps

- Measuring anticlockwise from north, or clockwise from east.
- Measuring at the wrong point ("from" tells you where to stand).
- Two-figure bearings: 45° must be 045°.
- Adding 180° to 250° to get 430°.
- Converting km to cm with 10 000 instead of 100 000.
- Forgetting to convert the measured length back to real units.
- Lines drawn freehand: a ruler is required for every straight edge.

## Self-test

1. Write the scale "1 cm represents 4 m" as a ratio.
2. Write 1 : 75 000 as "1 cm represents … km".
3. On a 1 : 50 plan a table is 3.2 cm long. Find its real length in metres.
4. A path is 1.8 km long. How long is it on a 1 : 20 000 map?
5. Write a bearing of 9° in three-figure form.
6. Write down the bearing of due west.
7. The bearing of B from A is 125°. Find the bearing of A from B.
8. The bearing of D from C is 302°. Find the bearing of C from D.
9. Convert "40° west of south" to a three-figure bearing.
10. P is 3 km due north of Q. Write down the bearing of Q from P.
11. A wall 12 m long must fit in 10 cm on a drawing. Suggest a suitable scale.
12. G is due east of F, and H is due north of G with GH = FG. Find the bearing of H from F.

### Answers

1. 4 m = 400 cm: **1 : 400**
2. 75 000 cm = **0.75 km**
3. 3.2 × 50 = 160 cm = **1.6 m**
4. 180 000 ÷ 20 000 = **9 cm**
5. **009°**
6. **270°**
7. 125 + 180 = **305°**
8. 302 – 180 = **122°**
9. 180 + 40 = **220°**
10. **180°**
11. 12 m ÷ 10 cm = 1.2 m per cm, so use **1 cm to 2 m** (1 : 200), giving 6 cm
12. Triangle FGH is right-angled and isosceles, so H is 45° north of east: **045°**
