These questions cover subtopic 4.7, Circle theorems, in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. Section A uses the two Core properties (angle in a semicircle, and angle between tangent and radius) and suits every candidate. Section B is labelled **(Extended)**: it uses the four properties found only in E4.7. O is always the centre of the circle. Diagrams are described in words, so sketch each one first. Only Question 3 needs a calculator.

For explanations, read the [circle theorems study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-circle-theorems-cambridge-igcse-maths/); for a summary, use the [circle theorems revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-circle-theorems-cambridge-igcse-maths-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** AB is a diameter of a circle and C is a point on the circle. Angle CAB = 2x° and angle CBA = (3x + 10)°.

**(a)** Write down angle ACB, giving a reason. **[1]**

**(b)** Find x. **[2]**

**2.** PQ is a tangent to a circle at P. Angle POQ = 54°. Find angle OQP, giving reasons. **[2]**

**3.** (calculator) A circle has radius 7 cm. A point A is 25 cm from O. A tangent from A touches the circle at T.

**(a)** Calculate AT. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate angle OAT. **[2]**

**4.** AB is a diameter of a circle and C is a point on the circle. Angle OCB = 52°. Find angle BAC, giving reasons. **[3]**

**5.** A triangle is drawn with all three vertices on a circle, and one side passes through the centre. Explain why the triangle must be right-angled. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** (Extended) A, B and C lie on a circle. B is on the major arc AC and angle ABC = 63°.

**(a)** Find angle AOC. **[1]**

**(b)** Find angle OAC, giving a reason. **[2]**

**7.** (Extended) PQRS is a cyclic quadrilateral. Angle P = (2x + 10)°, angle R = (3x − 5)°, angle Q = 4y° and angle S = 5y°. Find x and y. **[4]**

**8.** (Extended) A, B, C and D lie on a circle, in that order. The chords AC and BD cross at X. Angle BAC = 34° and angle ACD = 48°.

**(a)** Find angle BDC, giving a reason. **[2]**

**(b)** Find angle AXB, giving reasons. **[2]**

**9.** (Extended) A tangent touches a circle at A. P and Q are points on the tangent on either side of A. B and C lie on the circle, with B on the same side of A as P. Angle PAB = 70° and angle BAC = 46°.

**(a)** Find angle ACB, giving a reason. **[2]**

**(b)** Find angle ABC, giving reasons. **[3]**

**10.** (Extended) TA is a tangent to a circle at A, and AB is a chord. Angle TAB = 62°.

**(a)** Find angle OAB. **[2]**

**(b)** Find angle AOB. **[2]**

**11.** (Extended) A, B and C lie on a circle, with C on the minor arc AB. Angle ACB = 118°.

**(a)** Find the obtuse angle AOB. **[3]**

**(b)** Find angle OAB. **[1]**

**12.** (Extended) ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral. The side DC is extended beyond C to a point E. Show that angle BCE = angle BAD, giving a reason for each step. **[3]**

**13.** (Extended) A, B, C and D lie on a circle, in that order, and AC is a diameter. Angle BAC = 28°.

**(a)** Find angle BDC, giving a reason. **[2]**

**(b)** Write down angle ADC, giving a reason. **[1]**

**(c)** Find angle ADB. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **90°**, because the angle in a semicircle = 90° [1]

**(b)** 2x + 3x + 10 = 90 [1]; **x = 16** [1]

**2.** Angle OPQ = 90° (angle between tangent and radius = 90°) [1]; angle OQP = 180° − 90° − 54° = **36°** (angle sum of a triangle) [1]

**3. (a)** Angle OTA = 90°, so AT² = 25² − 7² = 576 [1]; **AT = 24 cm** [1]

**(b)** sin(angle OAT) = 7/25 [1]; angle OAT = **16.3°** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the hypotenuse is OA, because the right angle is at T; using 25 as a shorter side gives an impossible square root of a negative number or a wrong ratio.

**4.** OB = OC (radii), so angle OBC = 52° [1]; angle ACB = 90° (angle in a semicircle = 90°) [1]; angle BAC = 180° − 90° − 52° = **38°** [1]

**5.** The side through the centre is a diameter, and the angle opposite it is an angle in a semicircle, which is 90° [1]

**6. (a)** 2 × 63° = **126°** [1]

**(b)** OA = OC (radii), so triangle OAC is isosceles [1]; angle OAC = (180° − 126°) ÷ 2 = **27°** [1]

**7.** Angles P and R are opposite: 2x + 10 + 3x − 5 = 180 [1]; **x = 35** [1]. Angles Q and S are opposite: 9y = 180 [1]; **y = 20** [1]

*Examiner insight:* check the angles: 80°, 80°, 100° and 100° add to 360°, the angle sum of a quadrilateral.

**8. (a)** **34°** [1]; angles in the same segment are equal (both stand on chord BC) [1]

**(b)** Angle ABD = angle ACD = 48° (angles in the same segment are equal, chord AD) [1]; angle AXB = 180° − 34° − 48° = **98°** [1]

**9. (a)** **70°** [1]; alternate segment theorem (chord AB) [1]

**(b)** Angle QAC = 180° − 70° − 46° = 64° [1] (angles on a straight line sum to 180°) [1]; angle ABC = **64°** by the alternate segment theorem (chord AC) [1]

*Examiner insight:* check: 46° + 64° + 70° = 180°, the angle sum of triangle ABC.

**10. (a)** Angle OAT = 90° (angle between tangent and radius = 90°) [1]; angle OAB = 90° − 62° = **28°** [1]

**(b)** OA = OB, so angle OBA = 28° [1]; angle AOB = 180° − 56° = **124°** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the alternate segment theorem agrees: the angle at the circumference on chord AB in the opposite segment is 62°, and 124° = 2 × 62°.

**11. (a)** Angle ACB stands on the major arc, so the angle at the centre on that arc is the reflex angle AOB = 2 × 118° = 236° [1] (angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference) [1]; obtuse angle AOB = 360° − 236° = **124°** [1]

**(b)** (180° − 124°) ÷ 2 = **28°** [1]

**12.** Angle BCD = 180° − angle BAD (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°) [1]. Angle BCE = 180° − angle BCD (angles on a straight line sum to 180°) [1] = 180° − (180° − angle BAD) = angle BAD [1]

**13. (a)** **28°** [1]; angles in the same segment are equal (both stand on chord BC) [1]

**(b)** **90°**, angle in a semicircle = 90° (AC is a diameter) [1]

**(c)** 90° − 28° = **62°** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using a short reason such as "circle theorem" instead of the full property.
- Putting the semicircle right angle at the wrong vertex.
- Missing the isosceles triangle formed by two radii.
- (Extended) Doubling an angle at the circumference that stands on a different arc from the centre angle.
- (Extended) Matching the tangent–chord angle with the angle in the same segment instead of the alternate segment.
- (Extended) Writing "angles in the same segment" without naming, or checking, the chord both angles stand on.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I always need to give reasons in circle theorem questions?**
Give them whenever the question asks. The 0580 syllabus expects the properties as listed in the syllabus to be used as reasons, so learn those wordings.

**How do I know which chord two angles stand on?**
Follow the two arms of each angle to the circle. If both angles reach the same two points, and are on the same side of the chord joining them, they are in the same segment.
