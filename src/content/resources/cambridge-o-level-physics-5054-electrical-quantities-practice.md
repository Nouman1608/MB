## Section A

**1.** State what happens when two negatively charged objects are brought close together, and name the particles transferred when an object is charged by friction. **[2]**

**2.** Define *electric current* and state its unit. **[2]**

**3.** A charge of 240 C passes a point in a circuit in 4.0 minutes. Calculate the current. **[3]**

**4.** State the difference between direct current and alternating current. **[2]**

**5.** Three identical cells, each of e.m.f. 2.0 V, are available. State the total e.m.f. when they are connected

**(a)** in parallel **[1]**
**(b)** in series, all the same way round. **[1]**

**6.** Name **two** electrical conductors and **two** electrical insulators. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** An acetate rod becomes positively charged when it is rubbed with a dry cloth.

**(a)** Explain, in terms of electrons, how the rod becomes positively charged. **[2]**
**(b)** State the charge left on the cloth. **[1]**
**(c)** The acetate rod is hung in a paper stirrup. A negatively charged polythene rod is brought near one end. State and explain what happens. **[2]**

**8.** This question is about electric fields.

**(a)** Define an *electric field*. **[1]**
**(b)** State how the direction of an electric field line at a point is defined. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe the field pattern around a positively charged conducting sphere. **[2]**
**(d)** Describe the field between two oppositely charged parallel conducting plates. **[2]**

**9.** A 6.0 V battery is connected in series with a lamp and a resistor. The p.d. across the lamp is 4.5 V. A current of 0.50 A flows for 2.0 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the charge that flows. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the work done by the battery. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the energy transferred in the lamp. **[1]**
**(d)** Define *potential difference*. **[1]**

**10.** A student wants to find the resistance of a resistor.

**(a)** Describe the circuit she should set up, stating how each meter is connected and how she can obtain several readings. **[3]**
**(b)** Her readings are 1.0 V and 0.20 A, 2.0 V and 0.40 A, and 3.0 V and 0.60 A. Calculate the resistance. **[2]**
**(c)** State whether the resistor obeys Ohm's law over this range, and explain your answer. **[2]**

**11.** A 1.5 m length of metal wire has a resistance of 6.0 Ω. Calculate the resistance of

**(a)** 4.5 m of the same wire **[1]**
**(b)** 1.5 m of wire of the same material with half the cross-sectional area **[1]**
**(c)** 3.0 m of wire of the same material with twice the cross-sectional area. **[2]**

**12.** A filament lamp takes a current of 0.25 A at 3.0 V and 0.50 A at 12 V.

**(a)** Describe the shape of the current–voltage graph for a filament lamp. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the resistance of the lamp at 3.0 V and at 12 V. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the resistance changes. **[2]**

**13.** **(a)** Describe the current–voltage graph for a diode, for both directions of p.d. **[2]**
**(b)** State what happens to the current if the diode in a working circuit is turned round. **[1]**

**14.** Use a simple electron model to explain why copper conducts electricity but polythene does not, and describe an experiment to show the difference. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** They **repel** [1]. **Electrons** are transferred [1].

**2.** Current is the **charge passing a point per unit time** [1]; unit **ampere (A)**, equal to 1 C/s [1].

**3.** t = 4.0 × 60 = 240 s [1]; I = Q ÷ t = 240 ÷ 240 [1] = **1.0 A** [1].

**4.** Direct current **flows in one direction only** [1]; alternating current **repeatedly reverses direction** [1].

**5. (a)** **2.0 V** [1].
**(b)** **6.0 V** [1].

**6.** Conductors, any two: **copper, aluminium, any named metal, graphite** [1]. Insulators, any two: **plastic (polythene), rubber, glass, dry wood** [1].

**7. (a)** **Electrons are transferred from the rod to the cloth** [1]; the rod is left with **fewer electrons than protons**, so it has an overall positive charge [1].
**(b)** An **equal negative charge** [1].
**(c)** The end of the acetate rod **moves towards the polythene rod (attraction)** [1], because **unlike charges attract** [1].

**8. (a)** A **region in which an electric charge experiences a force** [1].
**(b)** It is the **direction of the force on a positive charge** at that point [1].
**(c)** **Straight radial lines** leaving the surface **at right angles** [1], with **arrows pointing outwards, away from the sphere** [1].
**(d)** **Parallel, evenly spaced lines** between the plates (a uniform field) [1], directed **from the positive plate to the negative plate** [1].

**9. (a)** Q = I t = 0.50 × 120 [1] = **60 C** [1].
**(b)** W = E Q = 6.0 × 60 [1] = **360 J** [1].
**(c)** W = V Q = 4.5 × 60 = **270 J** [1].
**(d)** The **work done by unit charge passing through a component** (energy transferred per coulomb) [1].

**10. (a)** **Ammeter in series** with the resistor [1]; **voltmeter in parallel** across the resistor [1]; a **variable resistor in series** (or a variable supply) changed to give several pairs of readings [1].
**(b)** R = V ÷ I = 1.0 ÷ 0.20 [1] = **5.0 Ω**, the same for every pair [1].
**(c)** **Yes** [1]: the current is **directly proportional to the p.d.** — doubling or tripling V doubles or triples I, so V ÷ I is constant [1].

**11. (a)** Three times the length: R = 3 × 6.0 = **18 Ω** [1].
**(b)** Half the area: R = 2 × 6.0 = **12 Ω** [1].
**(c)** Double length doubles R to 12 Ω [1]; double area halves it, so R = **6.0 Ω** [1].

**12. (a)** A **curve through the origin** [1] whose **gradient decreases as the p.d. increases** (it flattens), with the same shape for negative p.d. [1].
**(b)** At 3.0 V: R = 3.0 ÷ 0.25 = **12 Ω** [1]. At 12 V: R = 12 ÷ 0.50 = **24 Ω** [1].
**(c)** A larger current **heats the filament to a higher temperature** [1]; the **resistance of the metal increases with temperature**, as the vibrating ions obstruct the electrons more [1].

**13. (a)** In the **reverse direction the current is almost zero** [1]; in the **forward direction the current rises steeply once a small p.d. is exceeded** [1].
**(b)** The current **stops (falls to almost zero)** [1].

**14.** Copper contains **free electrons that can move through the metal** [1]; in polythene the **electrons are held to their atoms and cannot move through the material** [1]. Experiment: connect each material in turn **between two crocodile clips in a series circuit with a cell and a lamp (or ammeter)** [1]; the **lamp lights (current reading) with copper but not with polythene** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying positive charges move during friction charging; only electrons are transferred.
- Forgetting to convert minutes to seconds in I = Q ÷ t and Q = I t.
- Adding the e.m.f.s of identical cells connected in parallel.
- Defining p.d. as "the voltage across a component" — use work done (energy transferred) per unit charge.
- Stating Ohm's law without the condition of constant temperature.
- Using the gradient of a curved I–V graph as the resistance instead of V ÷ I at that point.
