OxfordAQA International GCSE Pakistan Studies (9236) brings together two
disciplines in one qualification: the history and culture of Pakistan,
and its human and physical geography. Students take it to understand how
the country came into being, how it has developed since independence, and
how its landscape, resources, people and economy shape its future. The
course is assessed by two written papers, each lasting 90 minutes, each
marked out of 77, and each worth 50% of the qualification, with no
coursework.

## How the course is organised

The specification is built around its two papers, and each paper has
the same internal shape: two compulsory topics that every candidate
studies, plus a choice of one from two optional topics.

**Paper 1, The History and Culture of Pakistan**

- *The Pakistan Movement (1857–1947)*, compulsory: the long road from the
  mid-nineteenth century to the creation of Pakistan.
- *Pakistan into the 21st Century: From Independence to 2022*,
  compulsory: the country's political, social and economic story after
  1947.
- *Islam in the Subcontinent (c700–c1831)*, optional: the arrival and
  spread of Islam across the region over many centuries.
- *The Mughal Empire (c1526–c1850s)*, optional: the rise, achievements
  and decline of the Mughal state.

**Paper 2, The Human and Physical Geography of Pakistan**

- *The Landscape and Natural Resources of Pakistan*, compulsory: relief,
  climate, rivers and the resources the land provides.
- *The People and Economy of Pakistan*, compulsory: population, settlement
  and the sectors of the economy.
- *Globalisation, Transport and Communication in Pakistan*, optional: how
  the country connects internally and with the wider world.
- *Creating a Sustainable Future for Pakistan*, optional: the
  environmental and development choices facing the country.

Your school normally decides which optional topic you study in each
paper, so confirm both choices with your teacher at the start.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | The History and Culture of Pakistan | 90 minutes | 77 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | The Human and Physical Geography of Pakistan | 90 minutes | 77 | 50% |

Both are written exams and the qualification is not tiered, so every
candidate sits the same papers. Each paper has three sections: one for
each compulsory topic and one for the chosen optional topic. The raw
marks are split 28, 28 and 21, which means the two compulsory topics
together carry the larger share of each paper while the optional topic
still makes up a substantial part of it.

The course is linear, so both papers are sat in the same series at the
end. The specification version this page follows (Version 1.9) applies
to International GCSE exams from May/June 2026 onwards; the document does
not state a separate first-teaching date.

## How the parts fit together

At first sight the two papers look like separate subjects, but they
inform each other throughout:

- **History explains geography.** Partition, the movement of people in
  1947 and the decisions taken in the decades after independence help to
  explain where people live today, how water and land are shared, and why
  certain regions developed faster than others.
- **Geography explains history.** Rivers, mountains and fertile plains
  shaped where empires grew, how trade moved and why particular regions
  mattered strategically, from the Mughal period onwards.
- **The modern history topic and the economy topic overlap.** Studying
  Pakistan from independence to 2022 alongside the people and economy
  topic gives you two angles on the same national development story.
- **The optional topics extend the compulsory core.** Islam in the
  Subcontinent and the Mughal Empire provide the long background to the
  Pakistan Movement; globalisation and sustainability build on the
  landscape, resources and economy topics.

The skills also carry across. Both papers reward precise factual
knowledge, clear explanation of causes and consequences, and the ability
to support a judgement with specific evidence, whether that evidence is
a historical event or a geographical statistic.

## Planning your study

Because each paper is half compulsory core and part option, it makes
sense to secure the compulsory topics first and fit the optional topics
around them. One workable order over the course:

- **Start of the course:** the Pakistan Movement in history and the
  landscape and natural resources topic in geography. Together they give
  you the chronology to 1947 and the physical framework of the country.
- **Middle of the course:** Pakistan from independence to 2022, alongside
  the people and economy topic, so that political events and economic
  change are learned side by side.
- **Later in the course:** your two optional topics. If your history
  option is Islam in the Subcontinent or the Mughal Empire, keep a
  timeline that runs into the Pakistan Movement so the periods connect.
- **Final months:** review all six topics you will be examined on, and
  practise writing to each paper's three-section structure.

Begin answering exam-style questions as soon as the first compulsory
topic is finished in each paper, rather than waiting until everything is
taught, and move on to full paper practice once all three sections of a
paper are covered. For section-by-section technique, use the course's
exam preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in OxfordAQA IGCSE Pakistan Studies?**
Two. Paper 1 is The History and Culture of Pakistan and Paper 2 is The Human and Physical Geography of Pakistan. Each lasts 90 minutes, is marked out of 77 and is worth 50%.

**Do I study every topic in the specification?**
No. In each paper you study both compulsory topics and one of the two optional topics, so you are examined on three topics per paper.

**How are the marks divided within each paper?**
Each paper has three sections worth 28, 28 and 21 raw marks: one for each compulsory topic and one for your chosen optional topic.

**Is there coursework in 9236?**
No. The qualification is linear and assessed only by the two written papers, sat at the end of the course.

**Which version of the specification applies?**
This page follows Version 1.9 of the specification, which applies to International GCSE exams from May/June 2026 onwards.
