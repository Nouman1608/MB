These notes condense section 3.4.1 Force, energy and momentum (3.4.1.1 to 3.4.1.8) of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408), including Required practical 3. Full explanations and worked examples are in the [force, energy and momentum study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-force-energy-and-momentum/); test yourself with the [force, energy and momentum practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-force-energy-and-momentum-practice/). The materials half of section 3.4 is in the [Materials revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-materials-revision-notes/). Take g = 9.81 m s⁻².

## Equations

| Equation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fₓ = F cos θ, F_y = F sin θ | Resolving a force |
| moment = Fd | d is the perpendicular distance to the line of action |
| v = u + at, s = ½(u + v)t, s = ut + ½at², v² = u² + 2as | Uniform acceleration |
| F = ma | Resultant force, constant mass |
| p = mv | Momentum |
| F = Δ(mv)/Δt | Newton's second law, general form |
| FΔt = Δ(mv) | Impulse |
| W = Fs cos θ | Work done |
| P = ΔW/Δt = Fv | Power |
| efficiency = useful output power / input power | Efficiency |
| ΔEp = mgΔh, Ek = ½mv² | Energy stores |

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Vector / scalar | Has direction and magnitude / magnitude only |
| Equilibrium | Resultant force and resultant moment both zero |
| Moment | Force × perpendicular distance from the point to the line of action |
| Couple | Two equal, opposite, parallel forces not in line |
| Centre of mass | Point where the whole weight can be taken to act |
| Terminal speed | Speed at which drag equals the driving force or weight, so a = 0 |
| Impulse | Change of momentum; area under a force–time graph |
| Elastic collision | Total kinetic energy conserved |
| Closed system | No external resultant force acts |
| Power | Rate of doing work |

## Graphs

| Graph | Gradient | Area |
|---|---|---|
| s–t | Velocity | — |
| v–t | Acceleration | Displacement |
| a–t | — | Change in velocity |
| F–t | — | Impulse (change in momentum) |
| F–s | — | Work done |

Instantaneous velocity is the gradient of the tangent to the s–t curve at that time.

## suvat method

1. Choose a positive direction.
2. List s, u, v, a, t; mark known and wanted.
3. Pick the equation without the unused variable.
4. Solve; check sign and size.

Worked reminder: a car brakes from 22 m s⁻¹ to rest in 40 m. v² = u² + 2as gives 0 = 484 + 80a, so **a = −6.05 m s⁻²**.

**Required practical 3**: plot h against t² for free fall from rest; gradient = g/2. Electromagnet release delay is a systematic error; timing scatter is random.

## Projectiles

- Resolve the launch velocity: uₓ = u cos θ, u_y = u sin θ.
- Horizontal: constant velocity, s = uₓt.
- Vertical: acceleration −g; use suvat.
- Time is the link between them.

Worked reminder: a ball rolls off a 1.25 m high table at 3.0 m s⁻¹. Time to fall: t = √(2 × 1.25 / 9.81) = 0.505 s; horizontal distance = 3.0 × 0.505 = **1.5 m**.

Air resistance reduces range and maximum height; the descent is steeper than the ascent.

## Newton's laws and momentum

- First law: no resultant force → constant velocity (or rest).
- Second law: F = ma (or F = rate of change of momentum).
- Third law: equal and opposite forces of the same type on *different* objects.

Worked reminder: a 1200 kg car accelerates at 2.0 m s⁻² against 800 N of resistance. Driving force = 1200 × 2.0 + 800 = **3200 N**.

**Collisions method**
1. Choose a positive direction; give velocities signs.
2. Total momentum before = total momentum after.
3. Compare total Ek before and after to decide elastic or inelastic.

Safety features (crumple zones, airbags, packaging) increase the contact time, so for the same change in momentum the force is smaller.

## Work, power, energy

- No work is done by a force perpendicular to the motion (cos 90° = 0).
- At constant velocity, driving force = resistive force, so useful power = resistive force × v.
- Ep lost = Ek gained + work done against resistance.

Worked reminder: a 70 kg climber gains 350 m of height in 25 minutes. Power = 70 × 9.81 × 350 / 1500 = **160 W**.

## Exam traps

- Taking a moment with a distance that is not perpendicular to the force.
- Using u = 0 for a ball thrown upwards (u is the launch speed; v = 0 at the top).
- Using total launch speed for vertical motion of a projectile.
- Pairing weight with the normal reaction as a third-law pair.
- Adding momenta as scalars when the objects move in opposite directions.
- Assuming a collision is elastic because the objects bounce.
- Using W = Fs when the force is at an angle to the displacement.
- Writing efficiency greater than 100% after swapping input and output.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Distance vs displacement; speed vs velocity**: scalar vs vector.
- **Mass vs weight**: kg (scalar) vs N (force, mg).
- **Elastic vs inelastic**: Ek conserved vs not; momentum conserved in both.

## Quick self-test

1. Resolve a 60 N force at 20° above the horizontal into components.
2. A uniform 2.0 m beam of weight 80 N is pivoted at one end and held horizontal by a vertical force at the other end. Find that force.
3. A car accelerates uniformly from 5.0 m s⁻¹ to 25 m s⁻¹ in 8.0 s. Find its acceleration and distance.
4. State what the area under a velocity–time graph represents.
5. A stone is dropped from rest from a 45 m cliff. How long does it take to fall?
6. A ball is thrown horizontally at 12 m s⁻¹ and lands 1.2 s later. Find the horizontal distance.
7. A resultant force of 900 N acts on a 1500 kg car. Find its acceleration.
8. A 0.15 kg ball moving at 8.0 m s⁻¹ hits a wall and rebounds at 6.0 m s⁻¹. Find the change in momentum.
9. A 2.0 kg trolley at 3.0 m s⁻¹ collides with a 4.0 kg trolley at rest; they move off together. Find their speed.
10. Is the collision in question 9 elastic? Justify.
11. A 40 N force pulls a box 5.0 m along the floor at 60° to the horizontal. Find the work done.
12. A motor lifts 200 kg through 15 m in 20 s. Find the useful power output.

### Answers

1. 60 cos 20° = **56 N** horizontal; 60 sin 20° = **21 N** vertical.
2. Moments about the pivot: F × 2.0 = 80 × 1.0, so **F = 40 N**.
3. a = 20/8.0 = **2.5 m s⁻²**; s = ½(5.0 + 25) × 8.0 = **120 m**.
4. **Displacement**.
5. t = √(2 × 45 / 9.81) = **3.0 s**.
6. 12 × 1.2 = **14 m** (14.4 m).
7. a = 900/1500 = **0.60 m s⁻²**.
8. 0.15 × (6.0 − (−8.0)) = **2.1 kg m s⁻¹** (2.1 N s), directed away from the wall.
9. 2.0 × 3.0 = 6.0 v, so **v = 1.0 m s⁻¹**.
10. Ek before = 9.0 J; after = ½ × 6.0 × 1.0² = 3.0 J. Ek is lost, so it is **inelastic**.
11. W = 40 × 5.0 × cos 60° = **100 J**.
12. P = 200 × 9.81 × 15 / 20 = **1.5 kW** (1470 W).

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I choose which suvat equation to use?**
Write down the five quantities s, u, v, a and t, mark the three you know and the one you need, and pick the equation that does not contain the fifth.
