## Definition and equation

**Density is mass per unit volume.**

```
ρ = m / V        m = ρ V        V = m / ρ
```

Units: **kg/m³** (SI) or g/cm³. **1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³.**

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Density (ρ)** | Mass per unit volume; a property of the material |
| **Displacement** | Rise in liquid level when a sinking solid is submerged; equals the solid's volume |
| **Meniscus** | Curved liquid surface; read the bottom for water |
| **Tare** | Zeroing a balance with a container on it |
| **Regular solid** | Shape whose volume can be calculated from measured dimensions |
| **Irregular solid** | Shape whose volume must be found by displacement |

## Conversions to practise

| From | To | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| g/cm³ | kg/m³ | × 1000 | 7.8 g/cm³ = 7800 kg/m³ |
| kg/m³ | g/cm³ | ÷ 1000 | 850 kg/m³ = 0.85 g/cm³ |
| cm³ | m³ | ÷ 1 000 000 | 250 cm³ = 2.5 × 10⁻⁴ m³ |
| g | kg | ÷ 1000 | 340 g = 0.340 kg |

## The three methods in brief

**Liquid**
- Mass of empty cylinder (or tare) → add measured volume → new mass → subtract → ρ = m ÷ V.
- Example: 40.0 cm³ of liquid, mass 50.4 g → ρ = **1.26 g/cm³**.

**Regular solid**
- Measure dimensions (ruler or micrometer), calculate volume, measure mass, divide.
- Cuboid V = l × w × h; cylinder V = πr²l.
- Example: cuboid 4.0 cm × 2.5 cm × 2.0 cm = 20 cm³, mass 54 g → ρ = **2.7 g/cm³**.

**Irregular solid that sinks**
- Mass first (dry) → initial cylinder reading → lower on thread until submerged → final reading →
  volume = rise → ρ = m ÷ V.
- Example: rise 25.0 → 33.5 cm³ = 8.5 cm³; mass 22.95 g → ρ = **2.7 g/cm³**.

## Errors in the displacement method

- **Air bubbles** → volume too large → density too small.
- **Splashing** → rise too small → density too large.
- **Wet stone weighed** → mass too large → density too large.
- **Coarse scale** → large uncertainty → use the narrowest cylinder.

## Improving any density measurement

- Use larger samples: the same reading uncertainty is a smaller percentage.
- Repeat and average.
- Plot mass against volume for several samples or volumes; the gradient is the density.

## Worked calculations in exam layout

**1. Regular solid (cylinder).** A brass rod has diameter 3.0 cm, length 4.0 cm and mass 240 g.

```
r = 1.5 cm
V = π × 1.5² × 4.0 = 28.3 cm³
ρ = 240 / 28.3 = 8.5 g/cm³  (8500 kg/m³)
```

**2. Mass from density.** A fish tank holds 0.090 m³ of water (density 1000 kg/m³).

```
m = ρ V = 1000 × 0.090 = 90 kg
```

**3. Volume from density, with unit change.** A 1.35 kg block of aluminium (2700 kg/m³):

```
V = m / ρ = 1.35 / 2700 = 5.0 × 10⁻⁴ m³ = 500 cm³
```

Layout to copy every time: equation → substitution with units → answer with unit → check the
size is sensible (most solids lie between about 1 and 20 g/cm³).

## Where density links to other subtopics

- **1.1:** measuring cylinder technique, displacement and micrometer readings supply the volume.
- **1.3:** the electronic balance supplies the mass.
- **1.8 Pressure:** density appears in Δp = ρgΔh for liquids.
- **2.2 and 2.3:** heating makes a fluid expand, so its density falls; this drives convection.

## Exam traps

- The solid's volume is the **difference** between readings, not the final reading.
- Use **radius**, not diameter, in πr²l.
- Keep units consistent: g with cm³, kg with m³.
- Multiply by 1000 to go from g/cm³ to kg/m³.
- Subtract the container's mass for a liquid.
- Weigh the stone **before** it goes into the water.
- Density does not depend on the size of the sample.

## Choosing the method

- Liquid → measuring cylinder on a balance.
- Shape you can measure with a ruler or micrometer → calculate the volume.
- Irregular shape that sinks → displacement.
- For a small sample, choose the finest instrument available: a micrometer for thin dimensions,
  the narrowest cylinder for displacement.

## Self-test

1. Define density.
2. Convert 2.7 g/cm³ to kg/m³.
3. Convert 920 kg/m³ to g/cm³.
4. A cuboid measures 5.0 cm × 2.0 cm × 1.5 cm and has mass 40.5 g. Find its density.
5. Petrol has density 750 kg/m³. Find the mass of 0.050 m³ of petrol.
6. Find the volume of 0.50 kg of gold (density 19 300 kg/m³), in cm³.
7. Why must the mass of the empty container be subtracted for a liquid?
8. A stone is weighed after the displacement step. Is the density too high or too low?
9. Why must the irregular solid sink?
10. A rod has diameter 1.0 cm and length 10.0 cm. Find its volume.
11. What does the gradient of a mass–volume graph give?
12. A block is cut in half. What happens to its density?

### Answers

1. Mass per unit volume.
2. **2700 kg/m³**.
3. **0.92 g/cm³**.
4. V = 15 cm³; ρ = 40.5 ÷ 15 = **2.7 g/cm³**.
5. m = 750 × 0.050 = **37.5 kg**.
6. V = 0.50 ÷ 19 300 = 2.59 × 10⁻⁵ m³ = **25.9 cm³**.
7. Otherwise the measured mass includes the container, so the density would be too large.
8. Too high: water clinging to it adds mass.
9. It must be fully submerged so the rise in level equals its whole volume.
10. V = π × 0.50² × 10.0 = **7.85 cm³**.
11. The density.
12. It stays the same: mass and volume both halve.
