## Equations to know

| Equation | Use |
|---|---|
| mgh = ½mv², so v = √(2gh) | Speed of water after falling through head h, ignoring losses |
| Power available = ρQgh | Water at volume flow rate Q falling through head h |
| Maximum power = ½πr²ρv³ (ρ of water) | Turbine of blade length r in a stream of speed v |
| Energy stored = ρVgh | Volume V of water raised through head h |
| Energy to pump = stored energy ÷ pumping efficiency | Electrical input to fill the upper reservoir |
| Energy returned = stored energy × generating efficiency | Electrical output when emptying it |
| Round-trip efficiency = pumping efficiency × generating efficiency | Overall performance of pumped storage |

Density of water 1000 kg m⁻³; g = 9.81 N kg⁻¹.

## Condensed recall

- **Turbine:** turned by moving water; kinetic energy of the water → kinetic energy of the shaft.
- **Generator:** coils rotate relative to a magnetic field; changing flux linkage induces an emf; kinetic energy → electrical energy.
- **Energy chain:** gravitational potential energy → kinetic energy of water → kinetic energy of turbine → electrical energy, with losses to friction, turbulence and heating.
- The kinetic energy delivered per second equals the potential energy lost per second when losses are ignored.
- ½πr²ρv³ is the same expression as for wind, with water's density, about 800 times that of air: water turbines can be smaller for the same power.
- **Base-power stations:** run continuously at steady output to meet the constant minimum demand.
- **Back-up stations:** started when demand rises; must respond quickly. Hydroelectric stations reach full output within minutes.
- **Pumped storage:** at low demand, spare electricity pumps water uphill (stores gravitational potential energy); at high demand, water flows back through turbines to generate.
- Pumped storage is a store, not a source: it returns less energy than it uses. It lets base stations run steadily and can absorb surplus from intermittent sources.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Head | Vertical height through which the water falls |
| Penstock | Pipe carrying water from the reservoir to the turbine |
| Turbine | Machine turned by moving water |
| Generator | Machine that converts rotational kinetic energy into electrical energy by electromagnetic induction |
| Base-power station | Station run continuously to meet the minimum demand |
| Back-up power station | Station brought into use quickly when demand rises |
| Pumped storage | System that pumps water uphill to store energy and releases it to generate later |
| Round-trip efficiency | Electrical energy returned divided by electrical energy used for pumping |

## Two kinds of water calculation

| Situation | Clue in the question | Equation |
|---|---|---|
| Water falling from a reservoir | Head h and flow rate Q (or mass per second) | ρQgh |
| Turbine in a river or tidal stream | Blade length r and current speed v | ½πr²ρv³ |

## Exam traps

- Air density used for water, or the reverse.
- ρQgh and ½πr²ρv³ confused.
- Pumped storage called an energy source.
- Pumping energy found by multiplying by the efficiency instead of dividing.
- The turbine said to produce electricity; the generator does.
- Volumes left in litres; 1 m³ = 1000 litres.

## Quick self-test

1. State the function of the turbine and of the generator.
2. Write the energy chain for a hydroelectric station.
3. Find the speed of water after falling 20 m, ignoring losses.
4. Find the power available from 12 m³ s⁻¹ of water falling through 50 m.
5. Find the maximum power from a river turbine with 1.5 m blades in a 2.0 m s⁻¹ current.
6. Why can a water turbine be much smaller than a wind turbine of the same power?
7. What is a base-power station?
8. Why are hydroelectric stations suitable as back-up stations?
9. When does a pumped storage station pump, and when does it generate?
10. Pumping is 80% efficient and generating 90% efficient. Find the round-trip efficiency.
11. Find the energy stored when 2.0 × 10⁵ m³ of water is raised through 150 m.
12. Why is pumped storage not an energy source?

### Answers

1. The turbine is turned by the moving water; the generator converts the turbine's rotation into electrical energy.
2. Gravitational potential energy → kinetic energy of water → kinetic energy of turbine → electrical energy.
3. v = √(2 × 9.81 × 20) = 20 m s⁻¹ (19.8 m s⁻¹).
4. P = 1000 × 12 × 9.81 × 50 = 5.9 × 10⁶ W.
5. P = ½ × π × 1.5² × 1000 × 2.0³ = 2.8 × 10⁴ W.
6. Water is about 800 times denser than air, so far more kinetic energy passes through each square metre each second at a given speed.
7. A station that runs continuously at steady output to meet the minimum demand.
8. They reach full output within minutes by opening valves; no fuel or boiler has to be heated.
9. It pumps when demand is low and spare electricity is available; it generates when demand is high.
10. 0.80 × 0.90 = 0.72, or 72%.
11. E = 1000 × 2.0 × 10⁵ × 9.81 × 150 = 2.9 × 10¹¹ J.
12. It only stores energy that was generated elsewhere, and returns less than it uses because of losses.
