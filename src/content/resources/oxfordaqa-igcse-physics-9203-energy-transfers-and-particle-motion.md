This guide covers one subtopic in depth: **3.4.2 Energy transfers and particle motion**, statements a to e of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203) specification, for exams May/June 2018 onwards. Almost every mark in this subtopic is for an **explanation in terms of particles**, so the guide concentrates on building those explanations step by step and applying them to the devices and animal adaptations the specification names.

For an overview that also covers specific and latent heat, see the [kinetic theory, specific heat and latent heat guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-kinetic-theory-specific-heat-and-latent-heat/). When you have finished here, use the [energy transfers revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-energy-transfers-and-particle-motion-revision-notes/) and the [energy transfers practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-energy-transfers-and-particle-motion-practice/).

## What the specification asks for

| Statement | Content |
|---|---|
| a | Conduction and convection explained with particles; conductors and insulators; free electrons in metals; convection from fluids becoming less dense |
| b | Evaporation and condensation; the cooling effect of evaporation; factors affecting the rate of evaporation |
| c | Rate of energy transfer by heating depends on surface area and volume, material, and the surface the object is in contact with; devices such as cooling fins; animal adaptations such as ear size |
| d | The bigger the temperature difference with the surroundings, the faster the rate of energy transfer |
| e | Most substances expand when heated: a hazard (roofs, bridges) or useful (bi-metallic strip thermostat) |

## Conduction (statement a)

Conduction is energy transfer through a material without the material itself moving. It is most important in solids.

**In any solid:** particles at the hot end gain energy and vibrate more. Because the particles are close together and linked, they pass the vibration to their neighbours, and energy spreads through the solid towards the cold end.

**In a metal:** there is a second, much faster mechanism. Metals contain **free electrons** that can move through the structure. At the hot end they gain kinetic energy, travel quickly through the metal and collide with particles further along, transferring energy. This is why metals are good conductors.

**Insulators** have no free electrons, so they rely on vibrations alone. Liquids and gases are poor conductors because their particles are further apart (gases especially), so collisions pass energy on less often. Materials that trap air, such as wool, foam and feathers, are good insulators because the air is a poor conductor and cannot circulate.

## Convection (statement a)

Convection happens only in **fluids** (liquids and gases), because the particles must be free to move. A strong explanation has four linked steps:

1. The fluid near the heat source is heated, and its particles move faster and further apart.
2. The fluid expands, so the same mass takes up more volume and becomes **less dense**.
3. The less dense fluid rises; cooler, denser fluid sinks to take its place.
4. This sets up a circulating **convection current** that carries energy around the fluid.

Typical applications: a heater placed low in a room, the freezing compartment placed at the top of a fridge, and sea breezes. Note the particles themselves do not expand; the spaces between them grow.

## Evaporation and condensation (statement b)

Particles in a liquid have a range of speeds. **Evaporation** happens when the fastest particles near the surface have enough energy to escape the attractive forces and leave as a gas. It happens at any temperature, not only at the boiling point.

**The cooling effect.** Because the most energetic particles leave, the average kinetic energy of the particles left behind falls, so the temperature of the liquid falls. This is why sweating cools the body and why wet skin feels cold in a breeze.

**Factors increasing the rate of evaporation:**

- higher temperature of the liquid (more particles have enough energy to escape);
- larger surface area (more particles are at the surface);
- air moving over the surface (escaped particles are carried away before they can return);
- drier surrounding air (fewer gas particles return into the liquid).

**Condensation** is the reverse: gas particles lose energy when they meet a cooler surface, slow down, and are held together as a liquid. Condensation transfers energy to the surface, which is why steam burns are severe.

## Rate of energy transfer by heating (statements c and d)

The specification lists three factors for how fast an object transfers energy by heating.

**Surface area and volume.** Energy leaves through the surface, but the amount of energy stored depends on the volume. The **surface area to volume ratio** decides how quickly an object cools.

**Worked example.** Compare a 4 cm cube with a 16 cm × 4 cm × 1 cm block. Both have a volume of 64 cm³.

```
Cube:   surface area = 6 × 4 × 4                     =  96 cm²
        ratio = 96 ÷ 64                              = 1.5 per cm
Block:  surface area = 2 × (16×4 + 4×1 + 16×1)
                     = 2 × 84                        = 168 cm²
        ratio = 168 ÷ 64                             = 2.6 per cm
```

The thin block has a much larger ratio, so it transfers energy to the surroundings faster. **Cooling fins** on engines, computer processors and the backs of fridges use this idea: thin fins add a large surface area with little extra volume.

**Animal adaptations.** Animals in hot deserts tend to have large, thin ears, which give a large surface area for losing energy. Animals in cold regions tend to have small ears and compact, rounded bodies, which reduce the surface area through which energy is lost. A large animal has a smaller surface area to volume ratio than a small one, so it cools more slowly.

**Material.** A metal object transfers energy faster than an identical plastic one because metal is a better conductor. A container made of a good insulator, or with an insulating layer, keeps its contents hot for longer.

**The surface in contact.** A hot pan placed on a metal worktop cools faster than on a wooden board, because metal conducts energy away from the pan more quickly. Standing barefoot on tiles feels colder than on carpet for the same reason.

**Temperature difference (d).** The bigger the difference between an object and its surroundings, the faster energy is transferred. A cup of tea cools quickly at first and then more and more slowly as it approaches room temperature.

## Expansion (statement e)

When a substance is heated, its particles move or vibrate more and take up more space, so the substance expands. The particles themselves do not get bigger.

- **Hazards:** bridges and railway tracks have expansion gaps so that they do not buckle on hot days; metal roofs can creak, warp or work loose if they cannot expand freely; overhead cables are hung with some slack so they do not snap when they contract in cold weather.
- **Useful:** a **bi-metallic strip** is made of two metals bonded together that expand by different amounts. When heated, the strip bends with the metal that expands more on the outside of the curve. In a thermostat, the bending strip breaks a circuit contact at a set temperature, switching a heater off; as it cools it straightens and reconnects the circuit.

## Common mistakes

- Saying "heat rises". It is the warmer, less dense fluid that rises.
- Saying particles expand when heated. The spaces between them increase.
- Describing convection in a solid. Solids conduct; only fluids convect.
- Explaining evaporation cooling without saying that the **most energetic** particles escape.
- Saying a larger object cools faster because it has more surface area. Compare the ratio, not the area alone.
- Forgetting free electrons when explaining why metals conduct well.

## Exam technique

- **Explain** answers need a chain: particle behaviour, then effect on density or energy, then the result.
- **Suggest** a design feature: name the feature, the factor it changes (for example surface area), and the effect on the rate of energy transfer.
- **Compare** two animals or devices: give a comparative statement for each ("larger surface area to volume ratio, so...").
- **Describe** the bi-metallic strip: two metals, different expansion, the strip bends, circuit breaks.
