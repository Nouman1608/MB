## Questions

**1.** (Higher Tier only) Define the moment of a force, write down the equation used to calculate it, and give the unit. **[3]**

**2.** (Higher Tier only) A see-saw is pivoted at its middle. A child on the right pushes down on the seat.

**(a)** State the direction of the turning effect of this force about the pivot. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe what happens to the left-hand end of the see-saw. **[1]**
**(c)** A second child sits directly above the pivot. Explain why this child's weight has no turning effect. **[1]**

**3.** (Higher Tier only) A cyclist pushes down on a pedal with a force of 220 N. The crank is horizontal and 0.17 m long, and the force is vertical.

**(a)** Calculate the moment of the force about the axle. **[2]**
**(b)** The crank is now vertical, with the same vertical force. State the moment and explain your answer. **[2]**

**4.** (Higher Tier only) A nut needs a moment of 85 N m to loosen it. A spanner has an effective length of 0.34 m.

**(a)** Calculate the force needed at the end of the spanner, at right angles to it. **[2]**
**(b)** A pipe is slid over the handle so that the effective length becomes 0.85 m. Calculate the new force needed. **[2]**

**5.** (Higher Tier only) A uniform plank is pivoted at its centre. A girl of weight 520 N sits 1.5 m from the pivot. A boy sits on the other side, 1.95 m from the pivot, and the plank balances. The plank weighs 180 N.

**(a)** Calculate the weight of the boy. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the upward force of the pivot on the plank and explain how you know it. **[2]**

**6.** (Higher Tier only) A uniform metre rule of mass 0.100 kg is pivoted at its 50 cm mark. A mass of 0.150 kg hangs from the 20 cm mark. A 0.100 kg mass is hung on the right-hand side to balance the rule.

**(a)** Calculate the distance from the pivot at which the 0.100 kg mass must hang. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why the weight of the rule can be left out of your calculation. **[1]**

**7.** (Higher Tier only) A uniform plank is 3.0 m long and weighs 240 N. It rests on a pivot 1.0 m from its left end. A person of weight 300 N stands on the left end. A weight is placed on the right end so the plank balances.

**(a)** Calculate the weight placed on the right end. **[4]**
**(b)** Calculate the upward force of the pivot on the plank. **[2]**
**(c)** The right-hand weight is removed. State, with a reason, the direction in which the plank starts to turn. **[2]**

**8.** (Higher Tier only) A crowbar is used to lift the lid of a crate. The crowbar presses on the lid 0.10 m from the pivot with a force of 1650 N. The hand pushes at right angles to the bar 1.1 m from the pivot.

**(a)** Calculate the force applied by the hand. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why a crowbar is called a force multiplier, and state one disadvantage of using it. **[2]**

**9.** (Higher Tier only) A driver gear with 18 teeth applies a moment of 4.2 N m and turns at 150 revolutions per minute. It meshes with a driven gear with 54 teeth. Ignore friction.

**(a)** Calculate the gear ratio. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the moment on the driven gear. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the speed of the driven gear. **[2]**
**(d)** State the direction in which the driven gear turns, compared with the driver. **[1]**

**10.** (Higher Tier only) In a gear train, gear A (10 teeth) is on a motor shaft and drives gear B (40 teeth). Gear C (12 teeth) is on the same shaft as B and drives gear D (36 teeth). The motor supplies a moment of 0.80 N m at 3600 revolutions per minute. Ignore friction.

**(a)** Calculate the gear ratio for each pair. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the overall ratio. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the moment and the speed of gear D. **[3]**

**11.** (Higher Tier only) A uniform horizontal pole of length 1.2 m and weight 80 N is hinged at a wall. A sign of weight 150 N hangs from its far end. A cable from the wall to the pole holds it level. The perpendicular distance from the hinge to the line of the cable is 0.76 m.

**(a)** Calculate the total clockwise moment of the pole and the sign about the hinge. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the force in the cable. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the distance 0.76 m is used, and not the length of the cable or of the pole. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest why the force in the cable is greater than the total weight of 230 N. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** A moment measures how strongly a force tends to turn an object about a pivot [1]. Moment = force × perpendicular distance between the pivot and the force's line of action [1]. The newton metre (N m) is the unit [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition that says "force × distance" without "perpendicular" is usually incomplete, and a unit given as N alone is a different quantity.

**2. (a)** The push is to the right of the pivot, so the turning effect is **clockwise** [1].
**(b)** The left-hand end moves **upwards** [1].
**(c)** The weight acts through the pivot, so the perpendicular distance is zero and so is the moment [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the perpendicular distance and the line of action, rather than saying the child is "in the middle".

**3. (a)** M = F × d = 220 × 0.17 [1] = **37.4 N m** [1].
**(b)** The moment is **zero** [1]. The line of action of the vertical force passes through the axle, so the perpendicular distance is zero [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the substitution and the unit, so that an arithmetic slip still leaves working to credit.

**4. (a)** F = M ÷ d = 85 ÷ 0.34 [1] = **250 N** [1].
**(b)** F = 85 ÷ 0.85 [1] = **100 N** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the rearrangement before substituting. In part (b) say that the force falls because the distance has increased, since the moment needed has not changed.

**5. (a)** Moment of girl = 520 × 1.5 = 780 N m [1]. Boy's moment = W × 1.95, equal to 780 for balance [1]. W = 780 ÷ 1.95 = **400 N** [1].
**(b)** The plank is not accelerating, so the upward force equals the total downward force [1]. 520 + 400 + 180 = **1100 N** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The plank's weight adds to the downward force but not to the moments here, so leaving it out of the total is a common slip.

**6. (a)** Weight of left mass = 0.150 × 10 = 1.5 N [1]. Moment = 1.5 × (0.50 − 0.20) = 0.45 N m [1]. Weight of right mass = 0.100 × 10 = 1.0 N [1]. d = 0.45 ÷ 1.0 = **0.45 m**, so it hangs at the 95 cm mark [1].
**(b)** The rule is uniform, so its weight acts at the 50 cm mark, which is the pivot, and has zero moment [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert masses into weights before taking moments, and say whether the answer is a distance from the pivot or a position on the rule.

**7. (a)** The weight of the plank acts at the midpoint, 1.5 m from the left end, which is 0.5 m to the right of the pivot [1]. Clockwise moment of the plank = 240 × 0.5 = 120 N m [1]. Anticlockwise moment of the person = 300 × 1.0 = 300 N m [1]. W × 2.0 + 120 = 300, so W = 180 ÷ 2.0 = **90 N** [1].
**(b)** The upward force equals the total downward force [1]. 300 + 240 + 90 = **630 N** [1].
**(c)** The plank turns **anticlockwise**, so the left end goes down [1], because the anticlockwise moment (300 N m) is bigger than the clockwise moment (120 N m) [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* When the pivot is not at the centre, state where the plank's weight acts and its distance from the pivot, because that is the step most often missed.

**8. (a)** Effort × 1.1 = 1650 × 0.10 [1] = 165 [1]. Effort = 165 ÷ 1.1 = **150 N** [1].
**(b)** The effort is smaller than the load because it acts further from the pivot (a ratio of 11) [1]. The hand must move through a larger distance than the lid moves [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A disadvantage must be specific, such as the larger distance moved. "It is harder" does not describe the trade-off.

**9. (a)** Ratio = 54 ÷ 18 = **3** [1].
**(b)** Output moment = 4.2 × 3 [1] = **12.6 N m** [1].
**(c)** Output speed = 150 ÷ 3 [1] = **50 revolutions per minute** [1].
**(d)** The driven gear turns in the **opposite direction** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that the driven gear turns more slowly while its moment is larger. One without the other leaves the idea half explained.

**10. (a)** A to B: 40 ÷ 10 = **4** [1]. C to D: 36 ÷ 12 = **3** [1].
**(b)** Overall ratio = 4 × 3 = **12** [1].
**(c)** Moment = 0.80 × 12 = **9.6 N m** [1]. Speed = 3600 ÷ 12 [1] = **300 revolutions per minute** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Multiply the ratios of the pairs rather than adding them, and write each ratio separately.

**11. (a)** Moment of pole = 80 × 0.6 (weight acts at the midpoint) = 48 N m [1]. Moment of sign = 150 × 1.2 = 180 N m [1]. Total = 48 + 180 = **228 N m** [1].
**(b)** For balance, T × 0.76 = 228 [1]. T = 228 ÷ 0.76 = **300 N** [1].
**(c)** The distance in a moment is measured at 90 degrees to the force, from the pivot [1]. The cable is at an angle to the pole, so its length is not that distance [1].
**(d)** The cable pulls at an angle and acts closer to the hinge than the weights do, so it needs a larger force for the same moment [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A reason that mentions the perpendicular distance to the line of action scores better than "because that is the right distance".

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using a distance in centimetres without converting to metres.
- Measuring to the end of the bar instead of to the pivot.
- Forgetting that the weight of a uniform bar acts at its midpoint when the pivot is elsewhere.
- Mixing up the driver and driven teeth, so the ratio is inverted.
- Describing a force multiplier without the trade-off in distance or speed.
- Adding clockwise moments to anticlockwise moments.
