## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Circulatory system | Blood vessels with a pump (heart) and valves that ensure one-way flow of blood |
| Double circulation | Blood passes through the heart twice for each complete circuit |
| Artery | Vessel carrying blood away from the heart |
| Vein | Vessel returning blood to the heart |
| Capillary | Vessel with a wall one cell thick, where exchange with tissues happens |
| Coronary heart disease (CHD) | Blockage of the coronary arteries, so heart muscle gets too little oxygen |
| Tissue fluid | Fluid from plasma that leaves capillaries and bathes body cells |

## 11.1 Circulation in one line each

- **Pulmonary circuit**: right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs → pulmonary vein → left atrium. **Low pressure.**
- **Systemic circuit**: left ventricle → aorta → body organs → vena cava → right atrium. **High pressure.**
- **Why both?** Low pressure protects the lungs; high pressure delivers blood quickly to all body tissues.

## 11.2 The heart

### Labels to know

`muscular wall · septum · right atrium · left atrium · right ventricle · left ventricle · atrioventricular valves · semilunar valves · coronary arteries`

- **Septum**: separates left and right sides, so oxygenated and deoxygenated blood do not mix.
- **Atrioventricular valves**: between atria and ventricles.
- **Semilunar valves**: where the aorta and pulmonary artery leave the ventricles.
- **Coronary arteries**: supply the heart muscle itself.

### Wall thickness: the two comparisons

| Comparison | Thicker | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Left vs right ventricle | Left | Pumps blood round the whole body at high pressure; right pumps only to the nearby lungs |
| Atria vs ventricles | Ventricles | Pump blood out of the heart into arteries; atria push blood only into the ventricles |

### Method in steps: one heartbeat

1. Atria contract → blood through open AV valves into ventricles.
2. Ventricles contract → AV valves **close** (stops backflow to atria).
3. Semilunar valves **open** → blood into aorta and pulmonary artery.
4. Ventricles relax → semilunar valves **close** (stops backflow to ventricles).
5. Atria refill from the veins.

Rule: a valve opens when pressure behind it is higher, and closes when pressure in front is higher.

### Monitoring heart activity

- **ECG**: trace of electrical activity; one repeating pattern = one beat.
- **Pulse rate**: beats per minute, at wrist or neck.
- **Valve sounds**: heard with a stethoscope; first sound = AV valves closing, second = semilunar valves closing.

| Convert | Do this |
|---|---|
| Pulse count over t seconds → beats per minute | count × (60 ÷ t) |
| ECG time per beat (s) → beats per minute | 60 ÷ time per beat |
| ECG distance → time | distance ÷ paper speed |

### Heart rate and physical activity

- **Investigate**: resting pulse (repeat, mean) → fixed exercise for fixed time → pulse at once and each minute until back to resting. Control person, exercise, duration, counting time.
- **Explain**: muscles respire faster → need more oxygen and glucose, make more carbon dioxide → heart beats faster (and harder) → faster delivery and removal.

### Coronary heart disease

- **What**: coronary artery blocked (fatty deposits, clot) → heart muscle short of oxygen and glucose → cannot respire and contract → may die (heart attack).
- **Risk factors**: diet, sedentary lifestyle, stress, smoking, genetic predisposition, age, gender.
- **Diet**: less saturated fat, cholesterol and salt; avoid excess energy intake.
- **Exercise**: strengthens heart muscle; helps control body mass, blood pressure and cholesterol.
- **Limit**: genes, age and gender cannot be changed.

## 11.3 Blood vessels

### The nine named vessels

| Vessel | From → to |
|---|---|
| Aorta | Left ventricle → body |
| Vena cava | Body → right atrium |
| Pulmonary artery | Right ventricle → lungs |
| Pulmonary vein | Lungs → left atrium |
| Hepatic artery | Aorta → liver |
| Hepatic portal vein | Small intestine → liver |
| Hepatic vein | Liver → vena cava |
| Renal artery | Aorta → kidney |
| Renal vein | Kidney → vena cava |

### Must-know distinctions: artery, vein, capillary

| | Artery | Vein | Capillary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blood pressure | High | Low | Lower than arteries, falling along the capillary |
| Wall | Thick | Thin | One cell thick |
| Muscle and elastic tissue | Much | Little | None |
| Lumen | Narrow | Wide | Very narrow |
| Valves | No | Yes | No |
| Structure linked to pressure | Thick, elastic wall withstands and evens out surges | Wide lumen eases flow; valves stop backflow | Thin wall gives short diffusion distance |

## 11.4 Blood

### Components and functions

| Component | Function |
|---|---|
| Red blood cells | Oxygen transport |
| Lymphocytes | Produce antibodies |
| Phagocytes | Engulf pathogens |
| Platelets | Clotting: soluble **fibrinogen** → insoluble **fibrin**; stops blood loss and entry of pathogens |
| Plasma | Transports blood cells, ions, glucose, amino acids, hormones, carbon dioxide, urea, vitamins, plasma proteins |

### Spotting cells on a slide

- **Red cell**: no nucleus, pale centre, very many.
- **Lymphocyte**: big round nucleus, thin rim of cytoplasm.
- **Phagocyte**: lobed nucleus, lots of cytoplasm, largest.

Magnification: **actual size = image size ÷ magnification** (convert mm to µm by × 1000 first).

### Tissue fluid in four steps

1. Pressure forces plasma out of capillaries → tissue fluid around cells.
2. Oxygen, glucose, amino acids: blood → tissue fluid → cells (diffusion).
3. Carbon dioxide, urea: cells → tissue fluid → blood.
4. Much of the fluid returns to the capillaries.

## More must-know distinctions

| Pair | The difference |
|---|---|
| Pulmonary artery vs pulmonary vein | Artery: right ventricle to lungs, deoxygenated blood. Vein: lungs to left atrium, oxygenated blood |
| Hepatic portal vein vs hepatic vein | Portal vein: gut to liver, rich in absorbed food. Hepatic vein: liver to vena cava |
| AV valves vs semilunar valves | AV: close when ventricles contract. Semilunar: open when ventricles contract, close when they relax |
| Lymphocyte vs phagocyte | Lymphocyte: round nucleus, makes antibodies. Phagocyte: lobed nucleus, engulfs pathogens |
| Plasma vs tissue fluid | Plasma: liquid part of blood, inside vessels. Tissue fluid: plasma that has left capillaries, around cells, with few proteins and no red cells |
| Fibrinogen vs fibrin | Fibrinogen: soluble, always in plasma. Fibrin: insoluble threads of the clot |

## Small worked reminders

**Pulse to heart rate.** 26 beats in 20 s → 26 × (60 ÷ 20) = **78 beats per minute**. Always convert to per minute before comparing.

**Percentage change.** Resting 78, after exercise 117: (117 − 78) ÷ 78 × 100 = **50%**. Divide by the starting (resting) value.

**ECG.** Peaks 15 mm apart at 25 mm per second: 15 ÷ 25 = 0.6 s per beat → 60 ÷ 0.6 = **100 beats per minute**.

**Recovery time.** Read the first time at which the rate equals the resting value again, counting from the end of the exercise.

**Explaining in a chain.** For "explain" answers, link each step with "so": more exercise → faster respiration in muscle → more oxygen and glucose needed → heart beats faster → more blood delivered per minute.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining double circulation as "two circuits" without saying blood passes through the heart **twice per complete circuit**.
- Stating that the left ventricle is thicker "because it pumps more blood" instead of "to a higher pressure, round the whole body".
- Putting events of a heartbeat in the wrong order, or opening the semilunar valves before the AV valves close.
- Writing that arteries carry oxygenated blood and veins deoxygenated blood, which the pulmonary vessels contradict.
- Giving a pulse rate per 15 s or per 30 s and calling it the heart rate.
- Confusing the hepatic portal vein with the hepatic vein.
- Saying capillaries have thin walls "so blood can pass through". Substances diffuse through; blood cells stay inside.
- Writing that platelets "clot the blood" without fibrinogen and fibrin, or saying fibrin is soluble.
- Listing CHD risk factors with no link to blocked coronary arteries and starved heart muscle.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a double circulation?**
In a double circulation, blood passes through the heart twice for each complete circuit of the body: once in the pulmonary circuit to the lungs and once in the systemic circuit to the rest of the body. Writing only "two circuits" is not enough.

**What does the septum do?**
The septum separates the left and right sides of the heart, so oxygenated and deoxygenated blood do not mix.

**How is tissue fluid involved in exchange between blood and cells?**
Pressure forces plasma out of capillaries to form tissue fluid around the cells. Oxygen, glucose and amino acids diffuse from the blood through the tissue fluid to the cells, carbon dioxide and urea move the other way, and much of the fluid returns to the capillaries.

**How can diet and exercise reduce the risk of coronary heart disease?**
Eating less saturated fat, cholesterol and salt and avoiding excess energy intake reduce the risk. Regular exercise strengthens heart muscle and helps control body mass, blood pressure and cholesterol.
