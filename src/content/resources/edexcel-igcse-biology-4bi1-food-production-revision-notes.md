These revision notes cover sub-topic 5(a), **Food production** (specification points 5.1 to 5.9B), of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Biology (4BI1) specification, Issue 3. The qualification is untiered. Points 5.1 to 5.8 can be examined on **either paper**. Point 5.9B is bold with a **B** reference and is **Paper 2 only**. They apply to the June and November series examined under Issue 3.

For full explanations and worked examples, read the [food production study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-biology-4bi1-food-production/) first. Then test yourself with the [food production practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-biology-4bi1-food-production-practice/). The course hub is [Edexcel IGCSE Biology](/boards/edexcel/igcse/biology/).

## Key definitions

- **Yield:** the mass of crop or product harvested from an area or in a time.
- **Glasshouse:** a glass structure that traps heat and lets growers control conditions.
- **Polythene tunnel:** a cheaper plastic-covered structure that works the same way.
- **Fertiliser:** a substance that supplies minerals to the soil for plant growth.
- **Pest:** an organism that damages crops or reduces yield.
- **Pesticide:** a chemical that kills pests.
- **Biological control:** using a living organism to reduce a pest.
- **Anaerobic respiration (yeast):** glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide, without oxygen.
- **Fermenter:** a vessel for growing microorganisms in large numbers under controlled conditions.
- **Aseptic:** free from unwanted microorganisms.

## 5.1 and 5.2 Crop plants under cover

| Factor | Effect on yield | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Glasshouse or tunnel | Warmer, protected, longer season | Cost of building and heating |
| More carbon dioxide | Faster photosynthesis, more growth | Stops helping when light or temperature limits |
| Higher temperature | Faster enzyme activity up to the optimum | Enzymes denature above the optimum |

## 5.3 Fertiliser

- Harvest removes minerals from soil.
- Fertiliser puts nitrates, phosphates and potassium back.
- Nitrate is used to make amino acids and proteins, so growth increases.
- Too much can leach into streams (eutrophication).

## 5.4 Pest control

| Method | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| Pesticide | Fast, effective, cheap | Kills harmless insects, builds up in food chains, resistance, pollution |
| Biological control | No chemical residue, specific | Slow, may not clear all pests, control organism may spread |

Examples of biological control: ladybirds against aphids; a parasitic wasp against whitefly in a glasshouse.

## 5.5 and 5.6 Yeast

- Yeast is a single-celled fungus.
- Bread: yeast respires sugar anaerobically in dough, making carbon dioxide, which makes bubbles and raises the dough. Baking kills the yeast.
- Equation in words: glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide.
- Practical: yeast and glucose in a tube, oil layer to exclude oxygen, water bath at a chosen temperature, count bubbles or collect gas.
- Control variables: mass of yeast, volume and concentration of glucose, time.
- Result: rate rises to an optimum, then falls as enzymes denature.

## 5.7 Yoghurt

1. Heat milk to kill unwanted microorganisms.
2. Cool to about 40 to 45 °C.
3. Add *Lactobacillus*.
4. Keep warm. Bacteria turn lactose into **lactic acid**.
5. Acid lowers the pH, milk proteins clump, and the milk thickens.

## 5.8 Fermenter conditions

| Condition | Reason |
|---|---|
| Aseptic precautions | Stop other microorganisms competing or spoiling the product |
| Nutrients | Energy and materials for growth |
| Optimum temperature | Fast enzyme activity; cooling jacket removes heat from respiration |
| Optimum pH | Enzymes work best at their optimum pH |
| Oxygenation | Aerobic respiration |
| Agitation | Mixes contents evenly |

## 5.9B Fish farming (Paper 2 only)

- **Water quality:** keep oxygen high, filter ammonia, control temperature and pH.
- **Intraspecific predation:** grade fish by size.
- **Interspecific predation:** nets and covers keep out predators.
- **Disease:** lower density, vaccines, treatments.
- **Waste removal:** filter or change the water.
- **Feeding:** high-protein feed at controlled times and amounts.
- **Selective breeding:** choose fast-growing, disease-resistant fish.

## Two calculations to know

**Percentage increase in yield** = (new − original) ÷ original × 100.
Example: 4.0 to 5.5 tonnes per hectare gives (5.5 − 4.0) ÷ 4.0 × 100 = 37.5%.

**Doubling population.** Final number = start × 2ⁿ where n is the number of doublings.
Example: 500 bacteria doubling 9 times gives 500 × 512 = 256 000.

## Method in steps: planning the yeast practical

1. State the variable you will change (for example temperature).
2. List the variables you keep the same.
3. Say how you will measure the rate (bubbles per minute or gas volume).
4. Add an oil layer to exclude oxygen.
5. Repeat at each temperature and calculate a mean.

## Answer frame: "Suggest why X condition is controlled in a fermenter"

Name the condition, say which part of the cell or process it affects (enzymes, respiration, contamination), and link to the growth of microorganisms and the amount of product.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Pesticide vs biological control:** chemical vs living organism.
- **Aerobic vs anaerobic respiration in yeast:** with oxygen (water and carbon dioxide) vs without (ethanol and carbon dioxide).
- **Lactic acid vs ethanol:** *Lactobacillus* vs yeast.
- **Oxygenation vs agitation:** supplying oxygen vs mixing.
- **Intraspecific vs interspecific predation:** same species vs a different species.

## Quick self-test

1. State two ways a polythene tunnel increases yield.
2. Why does adding carbon dioxide raise the yield in a glasshouse?
3. Why does yield stop rising when too much heat is added?
4. State one disadvantage of using pesticides.
5. Name the gas that makes dough rise.
6. What is the purpose of the oil layer in the yeast practical?
7. Name the acid made by *Lactobacillus* in yoghurt.
8. Why does a fermenter have a cooling jacket?
9. A yield rises from 4.0 to 5.5 tonnes per hectare. Calculate the percentage increase.
10. (Paper 2 only) Why are fish of different sizes kept apart?

### Answers

1. Any two of: warmer, protected from wind/rain/pests, longer growing season.
2. It is a raw material for photosynthesis, so more glucose is made.
3. Enzymes denature above their optimum temperature.
4. Any one of: kills useful insects, resistance, builds up in food chains, pollution.
5. Carbon dioxide.
6. To keep oxygen out, so respiration is anaerobic.
7. Lactic acid.
8. Respiration releases heat, and the temperature must stay at the optimum.
9. (5.5 − 4.0) ÷ 4.0 × 100 = **37.5%**.
10. Larger fish may eat smaller ones (intraspecific predation).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that carbon dioxide or temperature can be increased without limit.
- Naming the wrong gas for bread, or saying that yeast "uses" carbon dioxide.
- Forgetting to give a reason for each fermenter condition.
- Confusing agitation with oxygenation.
- Giving only an advantage when the question asks for both advantage and disadvantage.
- Writing "the oil adds oxygen".

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is milk heated before making yoghurt?**
To kill other microorganisms that could spoil it. It is then cooled so the *Lactobacillus* is not killed.

**Does yeast always respire anaerobically?**
No. With oxygen it respires aerobically. In dough, oxygen is limited, so it respires anaerobically.

**Do I need the names of pesticides?**
No. You need the advantages and disadvantages of the method.
