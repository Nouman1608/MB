## Section A

**1.** Write down the name of a polygon with

**(a)** 8 sides **[1]**

**(b)** 5 sides. **[1]**

**2.** Three angles of a quadrilateral are 74°, 106° and 95°. Work out the fourth angle. **[2]**

**3.** Work out the sum of the interior angles of an octagon. **[2]**

**4.** For a regular pentagon, work out

**(a)** the size of each exterior angle **[1]**

**(b)** the size of each interior angle. **[1]**

**5.** Write down the name of the quadrilateral described.

**(a)** All four sides are equal, the diagonals cross at right angles, and no angle is 90°. **[1]**

**(b)** It has exactly one pair of parallel sides. **[1]**

**6.** **(a)** Triangle X has sides 3 cm, 4 cm and 5 cm. Triangle Y has sides 5 cm, 3 cm and 4 cm. Are X and Y congruent? Give a reason. **[1]**

**(b)** Are a square of side 4 cm and a square of side 6 cm congruent? Give a reason. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Each exterior angle of a regular polygon is 24°.

**(a)** Work out the number of sides. **[1]**

**(b)** Work out the sum of its interior angles. **[2]**

**8.** Explain why a regular polygon cannot have interior angles of 145°. **[2]**

**9.** ABCD is a kite with AB = AD and CB = CD. Angle BAD = 124° and angle BCD = 68°. Work out the size of angle ABC. Give a reason. **[2]**

**10.** The angles of a pentagon are x°, x°, 2x°, (2x + 20)° and (3x − 20)°. Work out the size of the largest angle. **[3]**

**11.** ABCDE is a regular pentagon. Work out the size of angle BAC. Give reasons for your working. **[3]**

**12.** In a parallelogram, one angle is (5x − 20)° and the opposite angle is (3x + 30)°.

**(a)** Work out the value of x. **[2]**

**(b)** Work out the size of each of the other two angles. **[1]**

**13.** In a regular polygon, each interior angle is 5 times the size of each exterior angle. Work out the number of sides. **[3]**

**14.** A square and a regular pentagon share one side, and lie on opposite sides of it. At one end of the shared side, work out the angle between the other side of the square and the other side of the pentagon, measured outside both shapes. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Octagon** [1].

**(b)** **Pentagon** [1].

**2.** 360 − (74 + 106 + 95) [1] = 360 − 275 = **85°** [1].

**3.** (8 − 2) × 180 [1] = **1080°** [1].

**4. (a)** 360 ÷ 5 = **72°** [1].

**(b)** 180 − 72 = **108°** [1].

**5. (a)** **Rhombus** [1].

**(b)** **Trapezium** [1].

**6. (a)** **Yes**: they have the same three side lengths, so they are the same shape and size [1].

**(b)** **No**: they are the same shape but not the same size [1].

**7. (a)** 360 ÷ 24 = **15** [1].

**(b)** (15 − 2) × 180 [1] = **2340°** [1].

**8.** The exterior angle would be 180 − 145 = 35° [1]; 360 ÷ 35 = 10.28..., which is not a whole number, so the polygon cannot exist [1].

**9.** The equal angles of the kite are ABC and ADC (between the unequal sides), and the angles of a quadrilateral add up to 360° [1]; angle ABC = (360 − 124 − 68) ÷ 2 = **84°** [1].

**10.** Angle sum of a pentagon = 540° [1]; x + x + 2x + 2x + 20 + 3x − 20 = 9x = 540, so x = 60 [1]; the angles are 60°, 60°, 120°, 140° and 160°, so the largest is **160°** [1].

**11.** Interior angle ABC = 180 − 360 ÷ 5 = 108° (exterior angles of a regular polygon add up to 360°) [1]. AB = BC because the pentagon is regular, so triangle ABC is isosceles and angle BAC = angle BCA [1]. Angle BAC = (180 − 108) ÷ 2 = **36°** (angles in a triangle add up to 180°) [1].

**12. (a)** Opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal, so 5x − 20 = 3x + 30 [1]; 2x = 50, **x = 25** [1].

**(b)** The angle is 105°, so each of the other two is 180 − 105 = **75°** [1].

**13.** Let the exterior angle be e°. Then 5e + e = 180 (interior + exterior = 180°) [1]; e = 30 [1]; n = 360 ÷ 30 = **12** [1].

**14.** Angle in the square 90°, interior angle of the pentagon 108° [1]; angles at a point: 360 − 90 − 108 = **162°** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 3: using 8 × 180 = 1440° or (8 − 1) × 180 = 1260°.
- Question 4(b): giving 72° as the interior angle. 360 ÷ n is the exterior angle.
- Question 6(a): saying "no" because the sides are listed in a different order. The order of listing does not change the triangle.
- Question 8: only stating that 145° "does not work", without showing the division by the exterior angle.
- Question 9: making angle BAD equal to angle BCD. In this kite those angles are not equal.
- Question 10: finding x = 60 and stopping, when the question asks for the largest angle.
- Question 11: assuming angle BAC is 108° ÷ 3 = 36° by dividing the angle into equal parts. The answer happens to be the same, but the reasoning is wrong and earns no reason marks.
- Question 12: adding the opposite angles to 180°. It is neighbouring angles that add to 180°; opposite angles are equal.
- Question 13: writing interior = 5 × 180 or forgetting that interior + exterior = 180°.

## A quick check for regular polygon questions

Every answer about a regular polygon can be checked with two facts: the exterior angle times the number of sides is 360°, and the interior and exterior angles add up to 180°. For question 7, 15 × 24 = 360 and the interior angle is 156°, so the angle sum is 15 × 156 = 2340°, the same as (15 − 2) × 180°. For question 13, an interior angle of 150° is indeed 5 times 30°.
