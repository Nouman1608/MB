This guide goes deeper into **3.1.1 Use of SI units and their prefixes**, the opening subtopic of Section 3.1 Measurements and their errors in the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630) specification. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 1 (Mechanics, materials and atoms), but every later unit depends on it: a calculation with a wrongly converted prefix loses its final mark however good the physics.

When you have read the guide, condense it with the [SI units and prefixes revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-use-of-si-units-and-their-prefixes-oxfordaqa-revision-notes/) and test yourself with the [SI units and prefixes practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-use-of-si-units-and-their-prefixes-oxfordaqa-practice/). For the whole of Section 3.1, including uncertainties and estimation, use the [measurements and their errors study guide](/resources/a-level-oxfordaqa-physics-measurements-and-their-errors/) and the [SI units, error treatment and estimation guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-si-units-and-conversions-error-treatment-and-order-of/).

## What the specification asks for

The subtopic lists:

- the fundamental (base) units: mass, length, time, amount of substance, temperature and electric current, with their SI units
- SI derived units
- knowledge and use of the SI prefixes T, G, M, k, c, m, μ, n, p and f, their values, and standard form
- converting between different units of the same quantity, for example J and eV, and J and kW h.

It also sets limits. The candela (light intensity) is not required. You are not expected to recall definitions of the base quantities. Dimensional analysis is not required, so you will not be asked to derive an equation from units; you will be asked to express units in base units and to convert correctly.

## Base quantities and units

| Base quantity | SI unit | Symbol |
|---|---|---|
| Mass | kilogram | kg |
| Length | metre | m |
| Time | second | s |
| Amount of substance | mole | mol |
| Temperature | kelvin | K |
| Electric current | ampere | A |

Two points catch students out. First, the base unit of mass is the **kilogram**, not the gram: kg is the only base unit that already contains a prefix. Second, the kelvin is the base unit of temperature. A temperature in degrees Celsius converts by adding 273 (273.15 when the data justify it); a temperature *difference* is the same number in K and °C.

## Derived units

Every other unit is built from base units through a defining equation. To find a derived unit, write the equation, then replace each quantity by its unit.

| Quantity | Defining equation | Named unit | Base units |
|---|---|---|---|
| Velocity | v = Δs/Δt | none | m s⁻¹ |
| Acceleration | a = Δv/Δt | none | m s⁻² |
| Force | F = ma | newton, N | kg m s⁻² |
| Work, energy | W = Fs | joule, J | kg m² s⁻² |
| Power | P = W/t | watt, W | kg m² s⁻³ |
| Pressure, stress | p = F/A | pascal, Pa | kg m⁻¹ s⁻² |
| Charge | Q = It | coulomb, C | A s |
| Momentum | p = mv | none (N s also used) | kg m s⁻¹ |
| Frequency | f = 1/T | hertz, Hz | s⁻¹ |

### Worked example 1: the unit of a constant

**Question.** In Hooke's law, F = kΔL. Express the unit of the spring constant k in SI base units.

**Method.**

1. Rearrange: k = F/ΔL.
2. Units: N m⁻¹.
3. Replace N by its base units: kg m s⁻² × m⁻¹ = **kg s⁻²**.

**Comment.** Write units with negative indices and a space between symbols: "kg s⁻²", not "kg/s/s" or "kgs⁻²". A missing space can change the meaning: "ms" is the millisecond, while "m s" is metre second.

## Prefixes and standard form

| Prefix | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| tera | T | 10¹² |
| giga | G | 10⁹ |
| mega | M | 10⁶ |
| kilo | k | 10³ |
| centi | c | 10⁻² |
| milli | m | 10⁻³ |
| micro | μ | 10⁻⁶ |
| nano | n | 10⁻⁹ |
| pico | p | 10⁻¹² |
| femto | f | 10⁻¹⁵ |

Case matters. M (mega) is a million times bigger than the unit; m (milli) is a thousand times smaller. In a written answer, a lower-case k for kilo and a capital M for mega are expected.

**The safe method.** Before substituting into any equation, replace each prefix by its power of ten: 470 nm becomes 470 × 10⁻⁹ m = 4.70 × 10⁻⁷ m; 3.5 GHz becomes 3.5 × 10⁹ Hz. Do the calculation in base units, then choose a prefix for the answer only if the question asks for one.

### Squared and cubed units

A prefix belongs to the unit, so when the unit is squared or cubed, the prefix is squared or cubed with it.

- 1 μm² = (10⁻⁶ m)² = 10⁻¹² m²
- 1 mm³ = (10⁻³ m)³ = 10⁻⁹ m³
- 1 cm³ = (10⁻² m)³ = 10⁻⁶ m³

### Worked example 2: area of a wire

**Question.** A wire has a diameter of 0.28 mm. Calculate its cross-sectional area in m².

**Method.**

1. Radius r = 0.14 mm = 0.14 × 10⁻³ m = 1.4 × 10⁻⁴ m.
2. A = πr² = π × (1.4 × 10⁻⁴)² = **6.2 × 10⁻⁸ m²**.

**Comment.** Converting to metres *before* squaring avoids the most common error, which is to calculate π × 0.14² = 0.0616 and then multiply by 10⁻³ only once. The correct factor is 10⁻⁶.

### Worked example 3: compound units

**Question.** Convert a density of 2.70 g cm⁻³ to kg m⁻³, and a speed of 72 km h⁻¹ to m s⁻¹.

**Method.**

- Density: 2.70 g = 2.70 × 10⁻³ kg and 1 cm³ = 10⁻⁶ m³, so 2.70 × 10⁻³ ÷ 10⁻⁶ = **2.70 × 10³ kg m⁻³**.
- Speed: 72 km = 72 000 m and 1 h = 3600 s, so 72 000 ÷ 3600 = **20 m s⁻¹**.

**Check.** Water has a density of 1.00 g cm⁻³ = 1000 kg m⁻³, so any density in g cm⁻³ is multiplied by 1000. Dividing by 3.6 converts km h⁻¹ to m s⁻¹.

## Converting between units of the same quantity

The specification names two energy conversions. Both come from a definition, so you can rebuild them in the exam.

### Joules and electronvolts

One **electronvolt** is the energy transferred when one electron moves through a potential difference of one volt. Since W = QV, 1 eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C × 1 V = **1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ J**.

- eV to J: multiply by 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹. An electron with 1.8 keV of kinetic energy has 1.8 × 10³ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 2.9 × 10⁻¹⁶ J.
- J to eV: divide by 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹. A photon of energy 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁹ J has 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁹ ÷ 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 2.0 eV.

The electronvolt is used because energies of particles and photons are tiny in joules. Prefixes apply as usual: keV, MeV, GeV.

### Joules and kilowatt-hours

One **kilowatt-hour** is the energy transferred by a 1 kW device in 1 hour: 1 kW h = 1000 W × 3600 s = **3.6 × 10⁶ J**.

### Worked example 4: an energy bill

**Question.** A 650 W microwave oven runs for 4.0 minutes. Calculate the energy transferred in J and in kW h.

**Method.**

1. In joules: E = Pt = 650 × (4.0 × 60) = 1.56 × 10⁵ J = **156 kJ**.
2. In kilowatt-hours: E = 0.650 kW × (4.0/60) h = **0.043 kW h**.
3. Check: 1.56 × 10⁵ ÷ 3.6 × 10⁶ = 0.043. Both routes agree.

**Comment.** Keep power in kW and time in hours for kW h; keep power in W and time in s for joules. Mixing the two, for example kW with seconds, gives kJ, which is a valid answer only if you label it so.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the gram as a base unit, so masses are substituted in g instead of kg.
- Converting mm² to m² by multiplying by 10⁻³ instead of 10⁻⁶.
- Confusing m (milli) with M (mega), or writing "Km" for kilometre.
- Writing "ms⁻¹" without a space, which reads as "per millisecond".
- Forgetting to convert minutes to seconds before using E = Pt for an answer in joules.
- Multiplying by 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ when converting J to eV instead of dividing. Ask whether the eV number should be larger than the J number: it always is.

## Exam technique for OxfordAQA command words

- **State** or **Give**: for example, "Give the SI base units of pressure": write kg m⁻¹ s⁻² with no working needed.
- **Show that**: show every step of a conversion, including the power of ten for each prefix, and give the final value to one more significant figure than the value quoted in the question.
- **Calculate**: convert prefixes first, then substitute; give a unit with the answer, because a missing or wrong unit can cost the final mark.
- **Explain**: for unit questions, explain the reasoning, for example why 1 cm³ = 10⁻⁶ m³ rather than 10⁻² m³.

Unit 1 includes a multiple-choice section, where prefix and unit errors are built into the wrong options. Write the conversion down rather than doing it in your head.
