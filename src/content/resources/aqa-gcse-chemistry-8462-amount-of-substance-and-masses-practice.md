These questions cover section **4.3.2 Use of amount of substance in relation to masses of pure substances** of
the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462) specification, for teaching from September 2016 onwards (version 1.2). The topic
is assessed on Paper 1. Questions 1 to 3 are for both tiers. Questions 4 to 9 test content the specification
marks (HT only) and are labelled **Higher tier only**. Relative atomic masses: H 1, C 12, O 16, Na 23, Mg 24,
Cl 35.5, Ca 40. The Avogadro constant is 6.02 × 10²³ per mole. Show your working.

Learn the content first in the [study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-amount-of-substance-and-masses/)
and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-amount-of-substance-and-masses-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** A technician dissolves 8.0 g of sodium hydroxide in water and makes the volume up to 250 cm³.
Calculate the concentration of the solution in g/dm³. **[2]**

**2.** A solution has a concentration of 20 g/dm³. Calculate the mass of solute in 150 cm³ of the solution. **[2]**

**3.** A solution contains 9.0 g of solute and has a concentration of 36 g/dm³. Calculate the volume of the
solution in cm³. **[2]**

## Section B

**4.** (Higher tier only) A sample contains 88 g of carbon dioxide, CO₂.

**(a)** Calculate the number of moles of CO₂. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of CO₂ molecules in the sample. **[1]**

**5.** (Higher tier only) State what the mass of one mole of a substance is, in grams, and calculate the mass of
3.0 mol of water, H₂O. **[2]**

**6.** (Higher tier only) Calcium carbonate reacts with hydrochloric acid: CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂
20.0 g of calcium carbonate reacts completely.

**(a)** Calculate the number of moles of CaCO₃. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of calcium chloride formed. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the mass of carbon dioxide formed. **[2]**

**7.** (Higher tier only) 2.30 g of sodium reacts with 3.55 g of chlorine, Cl₂, to form 5.85 g of sodium chloride,
NaCl. Use the masses to work out the balanced equation for the reaction. **[4]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) 4.8 g of magnesium is burned in 8.0 g of oxygen, O₂: 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

**(a)** Show that magnesium is the limiting reactant. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of magnesium oxide formed. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the mass of oxygen left over. **[2]**

**9.** A solution of potassium chloride contains 6.0 g of the salt in 0.20 dm³ of solution.

**(a)** Calculate the concentration in g/dm³. **[1]**
**(b)** (Higher tier only) The student dissolves a further 6.0 g in the same solution without changing its volume.
State the new concentration and explain how it is related to the mass of solute. **[2]**
**(c)** (Higher tier only) The student then adds water to double the volume. State the new concentration and
explain the change. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Volume = 250 ÷ 1000 = 0.25 dm³ [1]; concentration = 8.0 ÷ 0.25 = **32 g/dm³** [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Volume = 150 ÷ 1000 = 0.15 dm³ [1]; mass = 20 × 0.15 = **3.0 g** [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Volume = 9.0 ÷ 36 = 0.25 dm³ [1] = **250 cm³** [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** Mr = 12 + 32 = 44 [1]; moles = 88 ÷ 44 = **2.0 mol** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 2.0 × 6.02 × 10²³ = **1.204 × 10²⁴** (about 1.2 × 10²⁴) [1]

**5.** The mass of one mole in grams is **numerically equal to the relative formula mass** [1]; Mr of H₂O = 18, so
3.0 × 18 = **54 g** [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Mr CaCO₃ = 100; 20.0 ÷ 100 = **0.200 mol** [1]
**(b)** The ratio CaCO₃ : CaCl₂ is 1 : 1, so 0.200 mol; Mr CaCl₂ = 111 [1]; mass = 0.200 × 111 = **22.2 g** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** 0.200 mol of CO₂; Mr = 44 [1]; mass = 0.200 × 44 = **8.8 g** [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Na: 2.30 ÷ 23 = 0.10 mol; Cl₂: 3.55 ÷ 71 = 0.050 mol; NaCl: 5.85 ÷ 58.5 = 0.10 mol [3]. Ratio
0.10 : 0.050 : 0.10 = 2 : 1 : 2, so **2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl** [1]. **[4]**
(One mark for each correct number of moles, one for the equation.)

**8. (a)** Mg: 4.8 ÷ 24 = 0.20 mol; O₂: 8.0 ÷ 32 = 0.25 mol [1]; 0.20 mol Mg needs only 0.10 mol O₂ (ratio
2 : 1) [1]; there is more than that, so the oxygen is in excess and Mg is limiting [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** 0.20 mol MgO × 40 = **8.0 g** [1]
**(c)** O₂ used = 0.10 mol; left = 0.25 - 0.10 = 0.15 mol [1]; mass = 0.15 × 32 = **4.8 g** [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** 6.0 ÷ 0.20 = **30 g/dm³** [1]
**(b)** 12 ÷ 0.20 = **60 g/dm³** [1]; the concentration is **directly proportional** to the mass of solute, so
doubling the mass doubles the concentration [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** 12 ÷ 0.40 = **30 g/dm³** [1]; the concentration is **inversely proportional** to the volume, so doubling
the volume halves the concentration [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not converting cm³ to dm³ in concentration questions.
- Using masses instead of moles when comparing reactants.
- Missing a multiplier in an Mr calculation.
- Giving the limiting reactant without comparing it with the equation ratio.
- Forgetting the unit or the power of ten in the Avogadro constant.
