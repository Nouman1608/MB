## Core recall

### The specification's three parts

1. **Ethical considerations:** analyse a possible trade-off between ethics and profit; know examples, benefits and drawbacks.
2. **Environmental considerations:** traffic congestion, recycling, disposing of waste, noise and air pollution; how business and consumers accept responsibility; costs and benefits.
3. **Sustainability:** global warming and using scarce resources; analyse a possible trade-off between sustainability and profit.

### The definition to learn

"Ethical behaviour requires businesses to act in ways that stakeholders consider to be both fair and honest." Name the stakeholder every time.

### Examples of ethical behaviour

- Paying suppliers a fair price and on time.
- Buying certified fair trade inputs.
- Checking that overseas factories treat workers fairly.
- Paying staff fairly and giving safe conditions.
- Honest advertising, pricing and labelling.

### Benefits and drawbacks of ethics

| Benefits | Drawbacks |
|---|---|
| Reputation and customer loyalty | Higher costs |
| Easier recruitment, lower staff turnover | Higher prices may lose price-sensitive customers |
| Better supplier relations | Lower short-term profit, which owners may resist |
| Point of difference in marketing | Damage if ethical claims prove untrue |

### Environmental impacts and responses

| Impact | Business response | Main cost |
|---|---|---|
| Traffic congestion | Off-peak deliveries, combined loads, staff travel schemes | Less flexibility, scheme costs |
| Recycling | Recycle waste, use recycled inputs, take-back schemes | Sorting time, collection fees |
| Disposing of waste | Cut waste at source, use approved disposal | Redesign and disposal fees |
| Noise pollution | Quieter machinery, limited hours | Equipment cost, less operating time |
| Air pollution | Cleaner vehicles, filters, renewable energy | Purchase cost |

### How consumers accept responsibility

Choosing products with less packaging, recycling at home, buying second-hand, repairing rather than replacing, and paying more for greener products. Their choices reward businesses that act responsibly.

### Sustainability in one line each

- Global warming: cut fossil-fuel energy and transport miles.
- Scarce resources: use less, reuse, switch to renewable or recycled materials, design products to last.

### The trade-off method

Action → cost or lost sales → benefit gained → net effect on profit → judgement for this business. Check the timing: many costs come now and benefits later.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ethics | Moral principles about what is right and wrong |
| Ethical behaviour | Acting in ways stakeholders consider fair and honest |
| Trade-off | Gaining one thing at the cost of another |
| Fair trade | Buying from producers at a price that gives them fair pay and conditions |
| Supply chain | The businesses involved in getting a product from raw materials to customer |
| Environmental responsibility | Taking account of the effect of decisions on the environment |
| Traffic congestion | Overcrowding of roads, causing delays and pollution |
| Recycling | Processing used materials so they can be used again |
| Sustainability | Meeting present needs without harming future generations' ability to meet theirs |
| Global warming | The rise in average world temperatures linked to gases from burning fossil fuels |
| Scarce resources | Materials in limited supply or slow to replace |

## Exam traps

- **Ethical is not the same as legal.** The law is a minimum.
- **Not always a trade-off.** Some ethical or green choices save money or raise sales.
- **Forgetting consumers.** The specification names both business and consumers.
- **No named impact.** Use the specification's terms: congestion, recycling, waste, noise, air pollution.
- **One-sided answers.** Give the cost and the benefit before judging.
- **No timing.** Say whether the effect on profit is short term or long term.
- **No stakeholder.** Ethics is judged by stakeholders, so name who.

## Self-test

1. Complete the specification's definition: ethical behaviour requires businesses to act in ways that stakeholders consider to be...
2. Give two examples of ethical behaviour towards suppliers.
3. State two benefits of behaving ethically.
4. Explain why ethical behaviour might reduce profit.
5. Name the four environmental impacts listed in the specification.
6. Give one way a business can reduce its impact on traffic congestion.
7. Give two ways consumers can accept environmental responsibility.
8. Name the two sustainability areas in the specification.
9. A firm spends £12,000 on solar panels that save £3,000 a year in electricity. How long until the cost is recovered?
10. Why might a sustainability decision lower profit in the short run but raise it in the long run?
11. Give one situation where acting ethically involves no real trade-off with profit.

### Answers

1. Both fair and honest.
2. Paying a fair price; paying invoices on time.
3. Any two: better reputation, customer loyalty, easier recruitment, better supplier relations, a marketing advantage.
4. It often raises costs, such as paying more for fairly produced inputs, so the profit margin falls unless prices rise, and higher prices may cut sales.
5. Traffic congestion, recycling, disposing of waste, noise and air pollution.
6. For example: schedule deliveries outside rush hours, or combine loads to make fewer journeys.
7. Any two: choosing less packaging, recycling at home, buying second-hand, repairing goods, paying more for greener products.
8. Global warming and using scarce resources.
9. £12,000 ÷ £3,000 = 4 years.
10. The investment costs money now, but lower running costs or extra sales from a better image build up over later years.
11. For example: paying suppliers on time costs little and builds goodwill.
