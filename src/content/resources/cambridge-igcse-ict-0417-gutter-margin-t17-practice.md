## Section A: short answers

**1.** State the purpose of a gutter margin. **[1]**

**2.** Which **one** of the following documents is most likely to need a gutter margin at the **top** of the page?

A: a stapled booklet

B: a letter sent in an envelope

C: a wall planner bound with a wire along its upper edge

D: a report in a ring binder

**[1]**

**3.** A4 portrait paper is 21.0 cm wide. A document has left and right margins of 2 cm and a 1.5 cm gutter on the left. Calculate the width of the text area. Show your working. **[2]**

**4.** State what *mirror margins* do. **[1]**

**5.** Give **two** ways of binding or filing a document that use space near the edge of the paper. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** A school prints a 24-page handbook on both sides of the paper and staples it down the spine.

**(a)** Explain why a gutter on the left edge of every page is not suitable. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe the page setup settings that should be used. **[2]**

**7.** An A5 booklet (14.8 cm wide) uses mirror margins with inside and outside margins of 1.5 cm and a gutter of 1 cm.

**(a)** Calculate the width of the text area. **[1]**

**(b)** State how far the text starts from the spine on every page. **[1]**

**8.** A single-sided report is hole-punched and kept in a ring binder. No gutter margin has been set and the left margin is small.

Explain **two** problems the reader may have. **[2]**

**9.** A task says: "Set the left margin to 2 cm and add a 1 cm gutter." A student instead sets the left margin to 3 cm with no gutter on a single-sided document.

**(a)** State whether the text starts at the same distance from the left edge in both cases. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the student's method is still not correct. **[2]**

**10.** A desk calendar is printed on A4 landscape paper (21.0 cm high) with top and bottom margins of 1.5 cm and a gutter of 2.5 cm at the top.

**(a)** Calculate the height of the text area. **[1]**

**(b)** State how far below the top edge the text begins. **[1]**

**11.** After a gutter margin is added, a ten-page document becomes eleven pages long.

Explain why this happens and what the student should check. **[2]**

**12.** A company is producing a 200-page printed manual for its engineers, bound like a book.

Explain the purpose of setting a gutter margin for this manual and how it should be set. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** To add extra space on the edge of the page that will be bound, so that text is not hidden or lost in the binding. **[1]**

**2.** C: a wall planner bound with a wire along its upper edge. **[1]**

**3.** 21.0 - 2 - 2 - 1.5 (1) = 15.5 cm (1). **[2]**

**4.** They make the margins of facing pages mirror each other, so the left and right margins become inside and outside margins (and a gutter is added to the inside edge of every page). **[1]**

**5.** Any two: staples (1); stitching or sewing (1); glue (1); comb or spiral wire binding (1); punched holes for a ring binder (1). **[2]**

**6. (a)** On a double-sided document the binding edge is on the left of right-hand pages but on the right of left-hand pages (1), so a left gutter would put the extra space on the outside edge of every even-numbered page, where it is not needed, and leave the bound edge without it (1). **[2]**

**(b)** Turn on mirror margins (inside and outside margins) (1) and set a gutter, which is added to the inside edge of every page (1). **[2]**

**7. (a)** 14.8 - 1.5 - 1.5 - 1 = 10.8 cm. **[1]**

**(b)** 1.5 + 1 = 2.5 cm from the spine. **[1]**

**8.** Any two, 1 mark each: the punched holes may go through text near the left edge, so words are lost (1); the rings and the binder's spine may cover text close to the edge (1); the pages may not turn or lie flat enough for text near the edge to be read easily (1). **[2]**

**9. (a)** Yes, 3 cm from the left edge in both cases. **[1]**

**(b)** The settings do not match the instruction, which a marker will check in the page setup (1); the left margin no longer has its stated value and, if the document is later made double-sided with mirror margins, the extra 1 cm will not move to the inside edge as a gutter would (1). **[2]**

**10. (a)** 21.0 - 1.5 - 1.5 - 2.5 = 15.5 cm. **[1]**

**(b)** 1.5 + 2.5 = 4.0 cm. **[1]**

**11.** The gutter makes the text area narrower, so fewer words fit on each line and page and the text reflows onto an extra page (1); the student should check pagination, for example that headings, tables and page breaks are still in the right places and that there are no widows, orphans or blank pages (1). **[2]**

**12.** Up to 4 marks:
- The manual is bound like a book, so part of each page near the spine is taken by the binding (1).
- A thick book does not open flat, so text near the spine curves into the fold and is hard to read (1).
- The gutter adds extra space on the inside edge so all the text remains visible and readable (1).
- Because the manual is double-sided, mirror margins should be used so the gutter is on the inside edge of every page (left of right-hand pages, right of left-hand pages) (1).
- A thicker document may need a larger gutter (1). **[4]**

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to know a standard size for a gutter?**
No. The syllabus asks for the purpose. In practical tasks, use the value given in the instructions.

**Is a gutter needed for a document only shown on screen?**
No. A gutter is for printed documents that will be bound. A document read only on screen has no binding edge.
