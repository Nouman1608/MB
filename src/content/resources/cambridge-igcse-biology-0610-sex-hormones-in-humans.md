This guide covers sub-topic **16.5 Sex hormones in humans** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Outcomes 1 and 2 are Core. Outcomes 3 and 4, on where oestrogen and progesterone are made and how FSH, LH, oestrogen and progesterone control the cycle and pregnancy, are Supplement content and are marked **Extended only**.

The [reproduction study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-reproduction/) gives a short summary and a hormone table. This page sets out the menstrual cycle as two parallel timelines, explains the hormone sequence as a chain of cause and effect, and shows how to read hormone data. Afterwards, use the [sex hormones revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-sex-hormones-in-humans-revision-notes/) and the [sex hormones practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-sex-hormones-in-humans-practice/).

## Puberty and secondary sexual characteristics (Core)

**Primary** sexual characteristics are the reproductive organs present from birth. **Secondary** sexual characteristics develop at **puberty**, under the control of sex hormones.

| Hormone | Made in | Secondary sexual characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| **Testosterone** | testes | growth of facial, body and pubic hair; voice deepens; muscles develop; penis and testes grow; sperm production begins |
| **Oestrogen** | ovaries | breasts develop; hips widen; growth of pubic and underarm hair; menstrual cycle begins |

The syllabus says "development **and regulation**": the hormones cause the changes at puberty and continue to maintain them afterwards. In an exam, "testosterone causes hair growth" is too vague; say **facial** or **body** hair.

## The menstrual cycle (Core)

The cycle is usually described as **28 days**, with **day 1 as the first day of menstruation**. Real cycles vary in length from person to person. Learn it as two timelines happening at the same time.

| Days (28-day cycle) | In the ovary | In the uterus lining |
|---|---|---|
| 1–5 | a follicle starts to develop, with an egg cell maturing inside it | lining breaks down and is lost through the vagina: **menstruation** |
| 6–13 | the follicle grows and the egg cell matures | lining is **repaired and thickens** |
| about 14 | **ovulation**: the egg cell is released from the follicle into the oviduct | lining is thick |
| 15–28 | the empty follicle becomes the **corpus luteum** | lining is **maintained**, ready for an embryo to implant |
| 28 → day 1 | if no pregnancy, the corpus luteum breaks down | the lining breaks down and the cycle starts again |

In Core answers you can describe the ovary changes as "an egg matures in the ovary and is released at ovulation, about day 14". The names follicle and corpus luteum are most useful for Extended answers.

### Worked example 1: dates in a cycle of a different length

*A woman's cycle is regular and 30 days long. Menstruation starts on 2 June. Assume, as in this question, that ovulation happens 14 days before the next menstruation. Give the day and date of ovulation and of the next menstruation.*

```
Next day 1 = day 31 → 2 June + 30 days = 2 July
Ovulation = 14 days before → day 31 − 14 = day 17
Day 17 = 2 June + 16 days = 18 June
```

**Ovulation on day 17 (18 June); next menstruation on 2 July.** The trap is assuming ovulation is always day 14. In a question that gives a different cycle length, follow the rule the question states.

## Where oestrogen and progesterone are made (Extended only)

| Hormone | In the menstrual cycle | In pregnancy |
|---|---|---|
| Oestrogen | the **ovaries** (cells of the developing follicle) | the **placenta** |
| Progesterone | the **corpus luteum** in the ovary | the corpus luteum at first, then the **placenta** |

FSH and LH are not in outcome 16.5.3, but you need their source for 16.5.4: both come from the **pituitary gland** in the brain.

## How the hormones control the cycle (Extended only)

Treat the cycle as a chain in which each hormone causes the next step.

1. **FSH** from the pituitary gland causes a **follicle to develop** in the ovary, with an egg cell maturing inside. FSH also stimulates the ovary to produce **oestrogen**.
2. **Oestrogen** causes the uterus lining to **repair and thicken**. Rising oestrogen **inhibits FSH**, so no more follicles start to develop, and high oestrogen stimulates the **release of LH**.
3. A surge of **LH** causes **ovulation**. LH also causes the empty follicle to become the **corpus luteum**.
4. The corpus luteum secretes **progesterone**. Progesterone **maintains the thick lining** and **inhibits FSH and LH**, so no new follicle develops and no further ovulation occurs.
5. **No pregnancy:** the corpus luteum breaks down, so progesterone falls. The lining is no longer maintained and breaks down (menstruation). With progesterone low, FSH is no longer inhibited, so FSH rises and a new cycle begins.

### Pregnancy

If an embryo implants, the corpus luteum keeps secreting progesterone at first; later the **placenta** secretes progesterone and oestrogen. Progesterone stays high, so:

- the uterus lining is **maintained**, so there is no menstruation;
- **FSH and LH stay inhibited**, so no follicles develop and there is no ovulation during pregnancy.

### Worked example 2: reading hormone data

The table gives blood concentrations, in arbitrary units, over one cycle. The values are invented for this example.

| Day | 2 | 7 | 12 | 14 | 21 | 27 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FSH | 6 | 5 | 4 | 8 | 2 | 4 |
| Oestrogen | 2 | 5 | 11 | 8 | 6 | 2 |
| LH | 5 | 5 | 9 | 30 | 3 | 4 |
| Progesterone | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 12 | 2 |

**What the data show**

- Oestrogen rises to a peak on day 12, as the follicle grows; the lining is thickening.
- LH peaks sharply on day 14 (30 units, about three times its day 12 value), so **ovulation** happens at about day 14.
- Progesterone is highest on day 21, from the corpus luteum, while FSH and LH are low; this fits progesterone **inhibiting** them.
- By day 27 progesterone has fallen to 2 units, so no pregnancy has occurred and menstruation is about to start.

### Annotated model answer

*Explain why a woman does not ovulate while she is pregnant.* **[3]**

> During pregnancy progesterone stays at a high level, secreted first by the corpus luteum and then by the placenta [1]. Progesterone inhibits the release of FSH and LH from the pituitary gland [1]. Without FSH no follicle develops, and without an LH surge no egg cell is released [1].

## Common mistakes

- Saying testosterone is made in the sperm ducts, or oestrogen in the uterus.
- Calling menstruation "the release of the egg". Menstruation is loss of the uterus lining; **ovulation** releases the egg cell.
- Assuming ovulation is always on day 14, even in a cycle of another length.
- (Extended) Saying FSH or LH is made in the ovary. Both come from the **pituitary gland**.
- (Extended) Saying progesterone "causes ovulation". **LH** does.
- (Extended) Forgetting that in pregnancy the **placenta** takes over making progesterone.
- (Extended) Writing "progesterone stops the cycle" without naming the hormones it inhibits.

## Exam technique

- **Describe** puberty changes: name the hormone, where it is made and at least two specific changes.
- **Describe** the cycle (Core): pair each ovary event with the uterus lining event at the same time.
- **Explain** hormone control (Extended): use cause-and-effect links: "FSH **causes**…", "oestrogen **inhibits**…".
- **Interpret data**: quote days and values, identify peaks, and link each peak to an event.
- **Calculate dates**: count from day 1 and show the subtraction.
