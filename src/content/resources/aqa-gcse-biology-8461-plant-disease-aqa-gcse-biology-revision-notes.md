These notes condense section **4.3.3 Plant disease** (4.3.3.1 and 4.3.3.2) of the AQA GCSE Biology (8461)
specification, for first teaching from 2016. The section is **Biology only**. The signs of disease and the
methods of identification are **Higher tier only**. It is assessed on Paper 1.

For full explanations and worked examples, read the [Plant disease study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-plant-disease-aqa-gcse-biology/).
Then try the [Plant disease practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-plant-disease-aqa-gcse-biology-practice/).
For the rest of Topic 3, use the [Infection and response revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-infection-and-response-revision-notes/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Plant pathogen | A virus, bacterium or fungus that infects plants |
| Pest | An animal, such as an insect, that damages plants |
| Aphid | A small insect that feeds on plant sap; the specification's named insect pest |
| Chlorosis | Yellowing of leaves caused by lack of chlorophyll, from magnesium deficiency |
| Stunted growth | Growth much less than normal; caused by nitrate deficiency (and by some diseases) |
| Fungicide | A chemical that kills fungi |
| Cuticle | The waxy layer on the surface of a leaf |
| Mimicry | A plant resembling something else to trick animals |

## What you must know: the limits

- **Pathogens and pests:** tobacco mosaic virus (viral), black spot (fungal), aphids (insects). Nothing else
  needs recall.
- **Ions:** nitrate and magnesium only.

## The named problems

| Problem | Type | Sign | Effect on the plant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tobacco mosaic virus | Virus | Mosaic discolouration of leaves | Less photosynthesis → less growth |
| Black spot (roses) | Fungus | Purple or black spots; leaves yellow and drop early | Less photosynthesis → less growth |
| Aphids | Insect | Insects visible on stems and leaves | Feed on sap; weaken the plant |

Black spot: spread by **water or wind**; treat with **fungicides** and/or **remove and destroy** affected leaves.

## Ion deficiencies

| Ion | Needed for | Deficiency sign |
|---|---|---|
| Nitrate | Protein synthesis → growth | Stunted growth |
| Magnesium | Making chlorophyll | Chlorosis (yellow leaves) |

Chain for nitrate: no nitrate → no amino acids → no proteins → no new cells → stunted.
Chain for magnesium: no magnesium → less chlorophyll → less light absorbed → less photosynthesis.

## Detection (Higher tier only)

Seven signs: **stunted growth**, **spots on leaves**, **areas of decay (rot)**, **growths**, **malformed stems or
leaves**, **discolouration**, **presence of pests**.

## Identification (Higher tier only)

| Method | Strength | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Gardening manual or website | Quick, free, done at home | Similar signs can have different causes |
| Laboratory testing | Identifies the pathogen reliably | Takes time; plants must be sent away |
| Test kit with monoclonal antibodies | Specific to one pathogen; fast; on site | Detects only the pathogen the kit is made for |

## Defences

| Type | Examples | How it protects |
|---|---|---|
| Physical | Cellulose cell walls | Barrier around each cell |
| Physical | Tough waxy cuticle on leaves | Barrier on the leaf surface |
| Physical | Layers of dead cells on stems (bark) that fall off | Pathogens fall away with the dead cells |
| Chemical | Antibacterial chemicals | Kill bacteria |
| Chemical | Poisons | Deter herbivores |
| Mechanical | Thorns and hairs | Deter animals |
| Mechanical | Leaves that droop or curl when touched | Dislodge insects; deter animals |
| Mechanical | Mimicry | Tricks animals into avoiding the plant |

Physical defences resist **microorganisms**; mechanical adaptations deter **animals**.

## Explanation chains to learn

- **Black spot:** spots on leaves → leaves yellow and fall early → less leaf area → less photosynthesis → less
  glucose → less growth.
- **Test kit (Higher tier only):** monoclonal antibodies specific to one antigen → bind only if that pathogen is
  present → result shows the pathogen is there.
- **Aphids:** feed on phloem sap → sugars removed → less sugar for respiration and growth.
- **Bark:** dead outer layers → pathogens land on them → layers fall off → pathogens removed with them.

## Exam traps

- Nitrate for **protein**, magnesium for **chlorophyll**: swapping them is the commonest error.
- Deficiency is not infection: no pathogen is involved.
- "Stops photosynthesis" is too strong; write "reduces".
- Fungicides do not kill viruses.
- Thorns are mechanical, not physical, in this specification.
- A sign is not an identification: "spots on leaves" detects a problem but does not name the pathogen.
- Give the effect as well as the defence: "waxy cuticle" alone does not explain anything.

## Self-test

1. Name the viral, fungal and insect examples in this section.
2. Why does TMV reduce plant growth?
3. How is black spot spread?
4. Give two ways to treat black spot.
5. What is the sign of nitrate deficiency, and why does it happen?
6. What is chlorosis, and which ion deficiency causes it?
7. (Higher tier only) Give four signs that a plant may be diseased.
8. (Higher tier only) Give three ways a disease can be identified.
9. Name three physical defences of plants.
10. Name two chemical defences.
11. How do thorns protect a plant?
12. Why does bark falling off help protect a tree?

### Answers

1. Tobacco mosaic virus; black spot; aphids.
2. Discoloured leaves have less chlorophyll, so less photosynthesis and less glucose for growth.
3. By water or wind.
4. Spray with fungicide; remove and destroy affected leaves.
5. Stunted growth, because nitrate is needed to make proteins for growth.
6. Yellowing of leaves; magnesium deficiency.
7. Any four: stunted growth; spots on leaves; areas of decay (rot); growths; malformed stems or leaves;
   discolouration; presence of pests.
8. Gardening manual or website; laboratory testing; test kits containing monoclonal antibodies.
9. Cellulose cell walls; waxy cuticle; layers of dead cells (bark) that fall off.
10. Antibacterial chemicals; poisons.
11. They deter animals from eating the plant.
12. Pathogens on the dead cells are removed when the cells fall off.
