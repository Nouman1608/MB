These revision notes cover criterion D (Writing) for IB MYP Language Acquisition, aligned to the International Baccalaureate Organization, Middle Years Programme Subject Brief -- *Language acquisition*, from 2020 (first eAssessment May 2023/November 2023). ## Facts from the brief

| Fact | What the brief says |
|---|---|
| Criterion D name | Writing |
| eAssessment objective | Writing in response to multimodal texts |
| eAssessment marks | 24 (criteria A and B also 24 each) |
| Mark flexibility | Distribution may vary by no more than 3 marks from the blueprint |
| On-screen examination | Three tasks; may include any topic from the language acquisition list |
| Achievement levels | Eight (1–8) in four bands, for each criterion |
| Proficiency levels | Emergent, capable or proficient |
| Weighting | The four criteria are equally weighted |

What criterion D asks for, in brief: language that suits the **audience and purpose** (home, classroom, formal and informal, social and academic language), and use of **language, form, mode, medium and literary concepts** to express ideas, values and opinions creatively and meaningfully.

## Key terms

- **Purpose** -- why the text exists: to inform, persuade, describe, explain, request, complain or reflect.
- **Audience** -- who reads it, and your relationship with them.
- **Form** -- the text type and its conventions (email, blog, article, diary, speech).
- **Mode** -- how meaning is carried: written words, images, layout, sound.
- **Medium** -- the channel: print, screen, social media.
- **Register** -- the level of formality your language shows.
- **Cohesion** -- language that links sentences and paragraphs: connectives and reference words.
- **Coherence** -- whether the ideas make sense in a logical order. A text can use many connectives and still be incoherent.
- **Range** -- variety of vocabulary, tenses and sentence types.
- **Accuracy** -- control of grammar, spelling and word order.

## Method in steps: from prompt to finished text

> 1. **Decode** -- purpose, audience, text type, stimulus (PATS).
> 2. **Choose the register** from the audience. Write it at the top of your plan.
> 3. **Mine the stimulus** -- list 3–4 facts or ideas, including what the image or layout adds.
> 4. **Plan** -- one job per paragraph; include one paragraph that answers the reader's likely doubt.
> 5. **Write** -- topic sentence first in each paragraph; link with connectives.
> 6. **Check** -- verbs, agreement, word order, spelling and accents, register.

## Small worked reminders

**Decoding.** Prompt: "Your cousin is visiting your town for the first time. Write a message suggesting what to do at the weekend." Purpose: to suggest and describe. Audience: a relative of your age. Text type: a message. Register: **informal**, familiar form of address, short friendly sentences.

**Using, not copying, the stimulus.** A leaflet says: "Free entry. Open 9–5. Over 200 species of butterfly." Copying: "It is free entry. Open 9–5." Using: "You won't need to pay anything, and it's open all day, so we could go after lunch and see hundreds of butterflies."

**One paragraph, one job.** Topic sentence → reason → example or detail → link to the next paragraph. If a paragraph has two jobs, split it.

**Answering the reader's doubt.** "You might think this would be expensive. However, ..." This one move shows audience awareness and adds a contrast connective.

## Connectives by function

| Function | English | French | Spanish | German |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Adding | furthermore, also | de plus, aussi | además, también | außerdem, auch |
| Contrasting | however, although | cependant, bien que | sin embargo, aunque | jedoch, obwohl |
| Cause | because | parce que | porque | weil |
| Result | therefore, so | donc | por lo tanto | deshalb |
| Example | for example | par exemple | por ejemplo | zum Beispiel |
| Sequencing | first, finally | d'abord, enfin | primero, por último | zuerst, schließlich |

Grammar flags: French *bien que* takes the subjunctive. German *weil* and *obwohl* send the verb to the end of the clause; *deshalb* at the start puts the verb second. Learn each connective with a full example sentence.

**Reference words** avoid repetition: *this*, *these*, *it*, *such a project*, *the idea*. Use them to point back to a noun you have just used.

## Register markers

| Signal | Formal | Informal |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Dear Mr / Ms + surname | Hi + first name |
| Closing | Yours sincerely / Kind regards | See you soon / Bye |
| Address | Polite form (*vous*, *usted*, *Sie*) | Familiar form (*tu*, *tú*, *du*) |
| Contractions | Avoid | Normal |
| Requests | "Would it be possible to...?" | "Can you...?" |
| Vocabulary | Precise, neutral | Everyday, sometimes slang |
| Punctuation | Few exclamation marks | Exclamations allowed |

Register is a scale: informal → neutral → formal. Neutral suits a school newsletter or an article for a general audience.

## Range upgrades

| Basic | Upgraded | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| It is good. | It is worthwhile because it teaches lasting skills. | Precise adjective + reason |
| I went. It was fun. | When I went last month, I enjoyed it more than I expected. | Subordinate clause + comparison |
| We can do it. | If the school agreed, we could run it in May. | Conditional sentence |
| People throw things away. | Many objects that could be repaired end up in the bin. | Relative clause + precise noun |
| I think... | In my view... / I am convinced that... | Varied opinion phrases |

Rule: use range you can control. An accurate simple sentence beats an inaccurate complex one.

## By proficiency level (a reasonable aim, not IB wording)

- **Emergent** -- short, accurate sentences on familiar topics; basic connectives (*and*, *but*, *because*); present tense secure, some past.
- **Capable** -- clear paragraphs; reasons and examples; more than one time frame; some complex sentences; register mostly consistent.
- **Proficient** -- precise word choice; varied structures; idiom used naturally; register fully controlled; ideas, values and opinions developed.

Check these against the descriptors your teacher gives you.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Purpose vs audience** -- purpose is *why*; audience is *who*. Both decide the register.
- **Register vs tone** -- register is formality; tone is attitude (enthusiastic, critical, sympathetic). A formal letter can be angry.
- **Cohesion vs coherence** -- cohesion is the linking language; coherence is the logic underneath.
- **Using vs copying the stimulus** -- use its ideas and facts in your own words; do not lift whole phrases.
- **Range vs accuracy** -- range is variety; accuracy is control. Aim for both, but never sacrifice meaning.

### Answers

1. Writing in response to multimodal texts.
2. 24 marks.
3. No more than 3 marks.
4. Any four of: language used at home; the language of the classroom; formal exchanges; informal exchanges; social language; academic language.
5. Purpose, audience, text type, stimulus.
6. *However* -- it signals contrast; the others add a point.
7. *Vous* (French), *usted* (Spanish), *Sie* (German).

9. Cohesion is the linking language on the surface; coherence is whether the ideas follow logically.
10. For example: "The event was a real success, because so many people came that we ran out of chairs."
11. It moves to the end of the clause.
12. Eight levels (1–8) in four bands.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Answering the topic but not the task: a description when the prompt asked you to persuade.
- Ignoring the reader: an email to a head of school written as if to a friend.
- Register drift, such as a formal letter that ends "Thanks, bye!"
- Mixing polite and familiar forms of address, including in verb endings and possessives.
- Copying chunks of the stimulus instead of reusing its ideas in your own words.
- Ignoring the image or layout of a multimodal stimulus when it offers a useful idea.
- One block of text with no paragraphs, so the organisation cannot be seen.
- Connectives that contradict the logic, such as *therefore* before an unrelated point.
- Over-ambitious structures full of errors that block meaning.
- No time left to check verbs, agreement and accents.
