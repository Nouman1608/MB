## Definitions (3.1.12.1)

- **Kp:** the equilibrium constant worked out from partial pressures, for a system held at constant
  temperature. You deduce its expression from the equation of a gas-phase reversible reaction.
- **Homogeneous system:** every species is in the same phase. Here, all are gases, so all appear in Kp.
- **Mole fraction:** amount of one gas ÷ total amount of gas. No units; never more than 1.
- **Partial pressure:** the pressure that one gas would produce on its own in the same container.
  Its unit matches the total pressure's.

## Key relationships

| What | Relationship | Check or note |
|---|---|---|
| Mole fraction of A | n(A) ÷ n(total) | all mole fractions sum to 1 |
| Partial pressure of A | mole fraction of A × total pressure | all partial pressures sum to P |
| Kp for wW + xX ⇌ yY + zZ | p(Y)^y × p(Z)^z ÷ (p(W)^w × p(X)^x) | use equilibrium partial pressures |
| Kp units | (pressure unit)^Δn | Δn = product gas moles − reactant gas moles |

## Units at a glance

| Δn | Example equation | Units if pressures are in kPa |
|---|---|---|
| +3 | 2N₂O₅(g) ⇌ 4NO₂(g) + O₂(g) | kPa³ |
| +1 | 2NO₂(g) ⇌ 2NO(g) + O₂(g) | kPa |
| 0 | butane(g) ⇌ 2-methylpropane(g) | none |
| −1 | 2CO(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2CO₂(g) | kPa⁻¹ |
| −2 | CO₂(g) + 4H₂(g) ⇌ CH₄(g) + 2H₂O(g) | kPa⁻² |

**Changing the pressure unit.** 1 kPa = 1000 Pa. Multiply a Kp value by 1000^Δn to go from kPa-based
to Pa-based units. For Δn = −2, a Kp of 3.30 × 10⁻⁵ kPa⁻² becomes 3.30 × 10⁻¹¹ Pa⁻².

## Method in steps: Kp from starting amounts

1. Balanced equation first.
2. Table of amounts: initial, change, equilibrium (all in mol).
3. Change row from the mole ratio in the equation.
4. Total every gas at equilibrium, including any that started at zero.
5. Mole fraction of each gas, then partial pressure (fraction × P).
6. Substitute into Kp. Work out Δn for the units.
7. Round only at the end, to the significant figures the data support.

**Shortcut that keeps accuracy:** key (moles ÷ total) × P in one go for each gas.

## Method in steps: an unknown from Kp

1. Write Kp in terms of partial pressures.
2. Rearrange so the unknown stands alone.
3. Substitute, then take a root if the unknown carries a power.
4. If the question then asks for a total pressure, add all the partial pressures.

**When Δn = 0** you can work in moles directly: the total pressure and the total amount cancel. Use this to save time, but say so in your working so the method is clear.

## Condition changes at a glance

| You change | Equilibrium position | Kp |
|---|---|---|
| Raise total pressure (constant T) | towards fewer moles of gas | unchanged |
| Lower total pressure (constant T) | towards more moles of gas | unchanged |
| Pressure change, Δn = 0 | no shift | unchanged |
| Raise T, forward reaction exothermic | moves left | decreases |
| Raise T, forward reaction endothermic | moves right | increases |
| Add a catalyst | no shift; equilibrium reached sooner | unchanged |

**Why pressure shifts the position but not Kp:** substituting (mole fraction × P) for every p leaves
P^Δn multiplying the mole-fraction terms. A new P therefore forces new mole fractions if Kp is to stay
the same, and that adjustment is the shift.

**Working from data:** a Kp that grows on heating means an endothermic forward reaction; a Kp that
shrinks on heating means an exothermic one.

**Explaining a temperature effect in full:** state the direction the equilibrium moves (towards the
endothermic side), say whether that raises or lowers the product partial pressures relative to the
reactant ones, then state what happens to Kp. For an exothermic forward reaction heated up, that reads:
moves left, product partial pressures fall, Kp decreases.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Position vs value.** Pressure can change the position, never Kp. Temperature changes both.
- **Rate vs extent.** A catalyst changes how quickly equilibrium is reached, not where it lies.
- **Kp vs Kc.** Kp uses p(X) from partial pressures; Kc uses [X] in mol dm⁻³.
- **Mole fraction vs partial pressure.** One is a unitless fraction; the other is a pressure.
- **Initial vs equilibrium amounts.** Only equilibrium amounts give mole fractions for Kp.

## Small worked reminder

Equilibrium partial pressures for 2NO₂(g) ⇌ 2NO(g) + O₂(g) at a fixed temperature are listed below.

```
p(NO2) = 60.0 kPa   p(NO) = 30.0 kPa   p(O2) = 15.0 kPa

Kp = p(NO)^2 x p(O2) / p(NO2)^2
   = 30.0^2 x 15.0 / 60.0^2
   = 13 500 / 3600 = 3.75 kPa       (Δn = 3 − 2 = +1)
```

## Quick self-test

1. A gas mixture holds 0.270 mol of one gas in 0.900 mol of gas in total, at 250 kPa. Calculate the
   mole fraction and the partial pressure of that gas.
2. Write the Kp expression for 2N₂O₅(g) ⇌ 4NO₂(g) + O₂(g) and give its units in MPa.
3. In the worked reminder above, what would Kp be if every pressure were given in Pa?
4. At the same temperature as the worked reminder, p(NO₂) = 40.0 kPa and p(O₂) = 15.0 kPa at
   equilibrium. Calculate p(NO).
5. An equilibrium mixture for butane(g) ⇌ 2-methylpropane(g) contains 0.300 mol butane and 0.750 mol
   2-methylpropane at 120 kPa. Calculate Kp.
6. The total pressure on the mixture in question 5 is doubled at constant temperature. What happens
   to the position of equilibrium?
7. The total pressure on 2N₂O₅(g) ⇌ 4NO₂(g) + O₂(g) is raised while the temperature stays fixed.
   What happens to the equilibrium position, and what happens to Kp?
8. For a gas-phase reaction, Kp increases when the temperature increases. Is the forward reaction
   exothermic or endothermic?
9. State the effect of a catalyst on the time to reach equilibrium and on Kp.
10. Three gases in an equilibrium mixture have partial pressures of 30.0, 45.0 and 75.0 kPa. Give the
    total pressure and the mole fraction of the third gas.

### Answers

1. Mole fraction = 0.270 ÷ 0.900 = **0.300**; partial pressure = 0.300 × 250 = **75.0 kPa**.
2. **Kp = p(NO₂)⁴ × p(O₂) ÷ p(N₂O₅)²**; Δn = 5 − 2 = +3, so the units are **MPa³**.
3. Δn = +1, so multiply by 1000: **3.75 × 10³ Pa**.
4. p(NO)² = Kp × p(NO₂)² ÷ p(O₂) = 3.75 × 1600 ÷ 15.0 = 400, so **p(NO) = 20.0 kPa**.
5. Δn = 0, so Kp = 0.750 ÷ 0.300 = **2.50**, with **no units**. The total pressure is not needed.
6. **No shift**: there is 1 mole of gas on each side, so pressure cancels from Kp.
7. The position moves **left**, towards the 2 moles of gas on the reactant side rather than the 5
   on the product side. **Kp is unchanged.**
8. **Endothermic**: a rise in temperature moved the equilibrium to the right, the endothermic
   direction.
9. Equilibrium is reached **in less time**; **Kp is unchanged**.
10. Total = **150 kPa**; mole fraction = 75.0 ÷ 150 = **0.500**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the starting amounts, not the equilibrium amounts, to find the total moles of gas.
- Missing a product that began at zero when adding up the equilibrium total.
- Using a 1 : 1 change when the equation's ratio is 1 : 4 or 2 : 1.
- Writing square brackets inside a Kp expression.
- Missing a power, such as the 4 on p(NO₂) in the N₂O₅ expression.
- Units with the wrong sign of power when Δn is negative, or any units at all when Δn = 0.
- Mixing Pa and kPa in one substitution.
- Forgetting the square root when the unknown partial pressure is squared.
- Saying Kp changes with pressure, or that a catalyst increases the equilibrium yield.
- Explaining a temperature change without naming the endothermic or exothermic direction.
