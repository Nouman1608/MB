Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Economics (4EC1) introduces students to
the way economies allocate scarce resources, from the decisions of
individual consumers and firms to the policies of governments and the
links between countries, and it is usually taught over two years.
Assessment is by two externally assessed written papers, each lasting 90
minutes, carrying 80 marks and worth 50% of the qualification. The
specification was first taught in September 2017 and first examined in June
2019.

## How the course is organised

The content is divided into two halves, and each half matches one paper.

**Microeconomics and business economics (Paper 1)**

- **1.1 The market system** starts with the economic problem and the
  assumptions economists make, then develops demand, supply and market
  equilibrium, elasticity, the mixed economy and externalities.
- **1.2 Business economics** looks inside the firm and the markets it works
  in: production, productivity and the division of labour, business costs,
  revenues and profit, business competition, the labour market and
  government intervention.

**Macroeconomics and the global economy (Paper 2)**

- **2.1 Government and the economy** covers macroeconomic objectives, the
  policies governments use to pursue them, and the relationships between
  those objectives and policies.
- **2.2 The global economy** covers globalisation, international trade and
  exchange rates.

The numbering makes the structure easy to follow: everything that begins
with 1 belongs to Paper 1, and everything that begins with 2 belongs to
Paper 2.

## How it's assessed

4EC1 is a linear qualification with no tiers. Every candidate sits the same
two papers.

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4EC1/01 | Paper 1: Microeconomics and Business Economics | 90 minutes (1h30) | 80 | 50% |
| 4EC1/02 | Paper 2: Macroeconomics and the Global Economy | 90 minutes (1h30) | 80 | 50% |

Each paper has four compulsory questions worth 20 marks each. Within those
questions the styles are mixed: multiple-choice items, short-answer
questions, data-response tasks and open-ended questions that ask for
analysis and judgement. Both papers are available as paper-based or onscreen
assessment, so check with your centre which format you will sit.

## How the parts fit together

Although the two papers are examined separately, the course is designed as
one argument. The tools built in 1.1 are used everywhere. Demand and supply
diagrams return when the labour market is studied in 1.2, when trade and
tariffs are analysed in 2.2 and when exchange rates are explained as the
price of one currency in terms of another. Elasticity explains why some
taxes raise more revenue than others and why firms respond to price changes
in different ways. Externalities and government intervention in Paper 1
prepare the ground for the wider policy discussion in 2.1.

The second half then asks the bigger question of how well a whole economy is
performing. Macroeconomic objectives, government policies and the
relationships between them form a chain, and the global economy section shows
how decisions made in one country affect another. A student who can move
comfortably between the scale of a single market and the scale of a national
economy is well placed for both papers.

Three skills run across all four content areas: using economic terms
precisely, drawing and explaining diagrams, and weighing up arguments to
reach a supported judgement. Because each 20-mark question mixes question
styles, all three skills are needed in every question rather than in one
section only.

## Planning your study

A two-year plan usually follows the specification order. In the first year,
cover 1.1 The market system thoroughly, since its diagrams and vocabulary
underpin the rest of the course, then move into 1.2 Business economics.
Finishing the whole of Paper 1 content in the first year leaves room to
revise it while new material is being taught.

In the second year, work through 2.1 Government and the economy and then 2.2
The global economy. Keep a short list of current examples, such as recent
changes in prices, interest rates or trade, because data-response and
open-ended questions reward applying theory to a context.

Two simple habits pay off throughout. Keep a glossary of key terms, written
in your own words with a one-line example for each, and add to it every week.
Alongside it, keep a diagram book in which each diagram is drawn neatly,
labelled fully and accompanied by a sentence explaining what a shift in each
curve shows.

Begin practice on full questions as soon as each half of the content is
complete, and move to timed full papers during the final term. Guidance on
how to approach each paper in the exam is kept on the exam-preparation page
so that this overview stays focused on the course itself.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers does Pearson Edexcel IGCSE Economics 4EC1 have?**
There are two papers. Paper 1 is Microeconomics and Business Economics and Paper 2 is Macroeconomics and the Global Economy. Each lasts 90 minutes, carries 80 marks and is worth 50%.

**Is IGCSE Economics 4EC1 tiered?**
No. The qualification is untiered, so all candidates sit the same two papers.

**What types of question are on the 4EC1 papers?**
Each paper has four compulsory questions worth 20 marks each, combining multiple-choice, short-answer, data-response and open-ended question styles.

**Can 4EC1 be taken onscreen?**
Both papers are available as paper-based or onscreen assessment. Your centre decides which format it offers.

**Which topics are on Paper 1 and which are on Paper 2?**
Paper 1 covers 1.1 The market system and 1.2 Business economics. Paper 2 covers 2.1 Government and the economy and 2.2 The global economy.
