## Section A

**1.** Name **two** fresh water stores and **one** salt water store in the water cycle. **[3]**

**2.** Distinguish between through-flow and ground water flow. **[2]**

**3.** Identify the ocean that surrounds Antarctica, and the ocean that lies between Europe and Africa on one side and the Americas on the other. **[2]**

**4.** Define potable water. **[1]**

**5.** State the purpose of sedimentation and of filtration in water treatment. **[2]**

**6.** State how distillation separates salt from seawater. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A description of a water cycle diagram uses six labels.

- P: water vapour rises from the sea surface.
- Q: water vapour is lost from the leaves of trees.
- R: tiny droplets form clouds as moist air cools over mountains.
- S: rain falls on the mountains.
- T: water flows over saturated ground into a river.
- U: water soaks into the soil surface.

Name the transfer shown by each label. **[6]**

**8.** Explain how cutting down a forest in a river's catchment changes interception, infiltration and surface run-off. **[3]**

**9.** The table shows the percentage of people with access to potable water in two countries. The data are invented for practice.

| | Urban areas | Rural areas |
|---|---|---|
| Country A | 98% | 95% |
| Country B | 85% | 45% |

**(a)** Compare access to potable water in Country A and Country B. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest **two** reasons why access is much lower in rural areas of Country B. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how a lack of sewage treatment can reduce the availability of potable water. **[2]**

**10.** A reservoir holds 18 million m³ of water. The city it supplies uses 60,000 m³ a day.

**(a)** Calculate how many days the reservoir could supply the city with no inflow of water. **[1]**

**(b)** During a dry season of 120 days there is no inflow, and evaporation removes a further 15,000 m³ a day. Calculate the volume of water left in the reservoir at the end of the dry season. Show your working. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest **two** ways the city could make its water supply last longer. **[2]**

**11.** A coastal city in a hot desert country plans to build a reverse osmosis desalination plant. Discuss the benefits and limitations of this plan. **[6]**

**12.** A government plans a multipurpose dam that will flood a valley where several villages are located. Discuss the benefits and limitations of the dam for farmers downstream, villagers in the valley and people in a city that will receive its electricity. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Fresh water, any two: ice sheets and glaciers [1]; ground water [1]; atmosphere [1]; lakes and rivers [1]. Salt water: oceans [1].

**2.** Through-flow is the sideways movement of water through the soil [1]; ground water flow is the slow movement of water through permeable rock, deeper underground [1].

**3.** Southern Ocean [1]; Atlantic Ocean [1].

**4.** Water that is safe to drink [1].

**5.** Sedimentation lets heavier suspended particles settle out of water held still in tanks [1]; filtration removes smaller particles by passing water through sand and gravel [1].

**6.** Water is heated until it boils and the salt remains in the liquid [1]; the steam, which is pure water, is cooled and condensed to form potable water [1].

**7.** P: evaporation [1]. Q: transpiration [1]. R: condensation [1]. S: precipitation [1]. T: surface run-off [1]. U: infiltration [1].

**8.** Interception decreases because there are fewer leaves and branches to catch rain [1]; infiltration decreases because rain hits bare, compacted soil and there are fewer roots to open up the soil [1]; surface run-off therefore increases, so water reaches the river faster [1].

**9. (a)** Access is higher in Country A in both urban and rural areas [1]; the gap between urban and rural access is small in Country A (3 percentage points) but large in Country B (40 percentage points) [1].

**(b)** Any two: rural people are spread out, so piped supplies are expensive to build [1]; fewer treatment works serve rural areas [1]; people rely on untreated wells, rivers or rainwater [1]; less government money is spent in rural areas [1].

**(c)** Untreated sewage released into rivers and lakes pollutes them with disease-causing microorganisms [1], so water sources that people use for drinking become unsafe and must be treated more or abandoned [1].

**10. (a)** 18,000,000 ÷ 60,000 = 300 days [1].

**(b)** Total daily loss = 60,000 + 15,000 = 75,000 m³; over 120 days = 75,000 × 120 = 9,000,000 m³ [1]. Water left = 18,000,000 − 9,000,000 = 9,000,000 m³ (9 million m³) [1].

**(c)** Any two: reduce demand through water-saving campaigns or devices [1]; repair leaking pipes [1]; restrict non-essential uses such as watering gardens [1]; develop another source, such as ground water or desalination [1]; reduce evaporation, for example by covering smaller reservoirs [1].

**11.** Levels-based. Credit up to 6 from:
- **Benefits**: the ocean provides an almost unlimited source for a city with little rainfall [1]; supply is reliable even in drought [1]; reduces pressure on scarce ground water and rivers [1]; the city is on the coast, so no long pipelines are needed [1].
- **Limitations**: reverse osmosis needs large amounts of energy to create high pressure, making the water expensive [1]; if the energy comes from fossil fuels, carbon dioxide emissions increase [1]; brine discharged into the sea can harm marine life [1]; high cost of building and maintaining membranes [1].
- **Judgement**: for example, worthwhile if powered by solar energy, which is plentiful in a hot desert, with careful brine disposal [1].

Maximum 6; a one-sided answer scores no more than 3.

**12.** Levels-based. Credit up to 6, with at least one point for each group for full marks:
- **Farmers downstream**: benefit from reliable irrigation water in dry seasons and from flood control [1]; but lose fertile silt that used to be deposited by floods, so they may need more fertiliser [1].
- **Villagers in the valley**: lose homes, farmland and possibly sacred sites when the reservoir floods [1]; may gain jobs in construction, fishing or tourism, or compensation, but resettlement can break up communities [1].
- **City residents**: gain renewable electricity with no emissions in use, and a stored water supply [1]; may pay for the high construction cost through taxes or bills [1].
- **Judgement** that weighs the benefits to many against the costs to the villagers, for example that the dam is justified only with fair compensation and resettlement [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Question 2**: describing both as "water moving underground" without distinguishing soil from rock.
- **Question 7**: writing "evaporation" for Q. Water lost from leaves is transpiration.
- **Question 9(a)**: describing each country separately without comparing, or not using the figures.
- **Question 10(b)**: forgetting to add evaporation to the city's use.
- **Question 12**: discussing the dam in general without considering each group named in the question.
