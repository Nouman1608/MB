## Questions

**1.** A curve has parametric equations x = 2 sec t, y = 3 tan t, where 0 ≤ t < π/2.

**(a)** Show that 9x² − 4y² = 36. **[3]**
**(b)** State the range of values of x on the curve. **[1]**

**2.** A curve has parametric equations x = 2 sin t, y = cos 2t.

**(a)** Find y in terms of x. **[3]**
**(b)** State the range of x. **[1]**

**3.** The curve C has equation 2x² − 3xy + y² = 12. Find the equation of the tangent to C at (4, 2), giving it as ax + by + c = 0 where the coefficients are integers. **[5]**

**4.** The curve C has equation x sin y + y = 1 + π/2 and passes through (1, π/2). Find the equation of the normal to C at this point. **[5]**

**5.** A curve has equation x² − 4xy + 5y² = 4.

**(a)** Show that dy/dx = (x − 2y)/(2x − 5y). **[3]**
**(b)** Find the coordinates of the points where the tangent is parallel to the x-axis. **[3]**

**6.** A curve has parametric equations x = 3t − t³, y = 3t². Find the equation of the tangent and of the normal at the point where t = 2. **[6]**

**7.** A curve has parametric equations x = 2θ − sin 2θ, y = 1 − cos 2θ.

**(a)** Show that dy/dx = cot θ. **[3]**
**(b)** Find, in exact form, the equation of the tangent at the point where θ = π/3. **[3]**

**8.** The curve C has parametric equations x = 2t, y = 3/t, t ≠ 0.

**(a)** Show that C has cartesian equation xy = 6. **[1]**
**(b)** Find dy/dx in terms of t. **[2]**
**(c)** The tangent at the point P where t = p (p > 0) meets the x-axis at A and the y-axis at B. Show that the area of triangle OAB, where O is the origin, does not depend on p. **[6]**

**9.** The mass M kg of a radioactive sample decreases at a rate proportional to M. When M = 60, the mass is decreasing at 1.5 kg per year. Form a differential equation for M in terms of time t years, and find the value of the constant. **[4]**

**10.** A tank has a rectangular base 4 m by 5 m. Water enters at 0.6 m³ per minute and leaves at k√h m³ per minute, where h metres is the depth.

**(a)** Show that dh/dt = (0.6 − k√h)/20. **[2]**
**(b)** The depth stays constant at 9 m. Find k and write the differential equation for h. **[2]**
**(c)** Find dh/dt when h = 4. **[2]**

**11.** Sand falls at 12 cm³ per second onto a flat surface and forms a cone whose height is always twice its base radius r.

**(a)** Find the rate at which r is increasing when the height is 10 cm. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the rate at which the height is increasing at that instant. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** sec t = x/2 and tan t = y/3 [1]. Use 1 + tan²t = sec²t: 1 + y²/9 = x²/4 [1]. Multiply by 36: 36 + 4y² = 9x², so **9x² − 4y² = 36** [1].
**(b)** sec t ≥ 1 for this range, so **x ≥ 2** [1].
*Examiner insight:* in a "show that" question, write the identity you use and the line after multiplying out, so the route to the printed result is complete.

**2. (a)** cos 2t = 1 − 2 sin²t [1]. sin t = x/2, so y = 1 − 2(x/2)² [1], giving **y = 1 − x²/2** [1].
**(b)** −1 ≤ sin t ≤ 1 gives **−2 ≤ x ≤ 2** [1].
*Examiner insight:* the cartesian equation alone describes a whole parabola, so a stated range of x is part of a complete answer when the parameter is limited.

**3.** Differentiate: 4x − 3y − 3x dy/dx + 2y dy/dx = 0 [2], with the product rule applied to the xy term. Rearrange: dy/dx = (4x − 3y)/(3x − 2y) [1]. At (4, 2): (16 − 6)/(12 − 4) = **5/4** [1]. Tangent: y − 2 = (5/4)(x − 4), so **5x − 4y − 12 = 0** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* a coefficient error in the product rule term usually spoils the gradient, so write the xy derivative as its own line before combining.

**4.** Differentiate: sin y + x cos y dy/dx + dy/dx = 0 [2]. At (1, π/2): 1 + 0 + dy/dx = 0, so dy/dx = −1 [1]. Normal gradient = 1 [1]. Normal: y − π/2 = x − 1, so **y = x + π/2 − 1** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* keep cos(π/2) = 0 and sin(π/2) = 1 exact; the question asks for an equation, so a decimal gradient or intercept loses clarity.

**5. (a)** 2x − 4y − 4x dy/dx + 10y dy/dx = 0 [2]. So (10y − 4x) dy/dx = 4y − 2x, and dividing by −2 gives **dy/dx = (x − 2y)/(2x − 5y)** [1].
**(b)** Tangent parallel to the x-axis: x − 2y = 0, so x = 2y [1]. Substitute: 4y² − 8y² + 5y² = 4, so y² = 4 [1]. The points are **(4, 2) and (−4, −2)**; the denominator 2x − 5y is −2 and 2, not zero [1].
*Examiner insight:* set only the numerator to zero, then use the curve equation to get y. Using the gradient formula alone gives x in terms of y, which is not yet a point.

**6.** dx/dt = 3 − 3t² and dy/dt = 6t [2]. dy/dx = 6t/(3 − 3t²). At t = 2: 12/(−9) = −4/3 [1]. Point: x = 6 − 8 = −2, y = 12 [1]. Tangent: y − 12 = −(4/3)(x + 2), so **4x + 3y = 28** [1]. Normal gradient 3/4: y − 12 = (3/4)(x + 2), so **3x − 4y + 54 = 0** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* work out both coordinates of the point from t as well as the gradient, since a correct gradient with a wrong point still gives the wrong line.

**7. (a)** dx/dθ = 2 − 2 cos 2θ and dy/dθ = 2 sin 2θ [1]. dy/dx = sin 2θ/(1 − cos 2θ) [1]. Use sin 2θ = 2 sin θ cos θ and 1 − cos 2θ = 2 sin²θ: dy/dx = 2 sin θ cos θ/(2 sin²θ) = **cot θ** [1].
**(b)** At θ = π/3: gradient = cot(π/3) = 1/√3 [1]. Point: x = 2π/3 − √3/2, y = 1 − (−1/2) = 3/2 [1]. Tangent: **y − 3/2 = (1/√3)(x − 2π/3 + √3/2)** [1].
*Examiner insight:* a "show that" on a trig derivative nearly always needs a double-angle step; name each identity so the simplification can be followed.

**8. (a)** xy = 2t × 3/t = **6** [1].
**(b)** dx/dt = 2 and dy/dt = −3/t² [1], so **dy/dx = −3/(2t²)** [1].
**(c)** At t = p the point is (2p, 3/p) and the gradient is −3/(2p²). Tangent: y − 3/p = −(3/(2p²))(x − 2p) [1]. Put y = 0: −3/p = −(3/(2p²))(x − 2p), so x − 2p = 2p and A = (4p, 0) [2]. Put x = 0: y = 3/p + 3/p, so B = (0, 6/p) [1]. Area = ½ × 4p × 6/p [1] = **12**, which does not depend on p [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* in a "show that" with a general parameter p, keep p symbolic throughout and cancel it visibly at the end.

**9.** Decreasing and proportional to M: **dM/dt = −kM**, k > 0 [2]. Substitute: −1.5 = −k × 60 [1], so **k = 0.025** (per year) [1].
*Examiner insight:* state the sign of k as you define it, since the minus sign for a decrease is separate from the proportionality.

**10. (a)** V = 4 × 5 × h = 20h, so dV/dt = 20 dh/dt [1]. Net rate dV/dt = 0.6 − k√h, so dh/dt = **(0.6 − k√h)/20** [1].
**(b)** Constant depth means dh/dt = 0: 0.6 − 3k = 0 [1], so k = 0.2 and **dh/dt = (3 − √h)/100** [1].
**(c)** At h = 4: (3 − 2)/100 [1] = **0.01 m per minute** (depth rising) [1].
*Examiner insight:* explain the link dV/dt = 20 dh/dt in words or symbols, because connecting volume and depth is the key idea, not just the algebra.

**11. (a)** Height 10 means r = 5. V = (1/3)πr²(2r) = 2πr³/3 [1], so dV/dr = 2πr² [1]. Then 12 = 2π(25) dr/dt [1], so **dr/dt = 6/(25π) ≈ 0.0764 cm s⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** h = 2r, so dh/dt = 2 dr/dt [1] = **12/(25π) ≈ 0.153 cm s⁻¹** [1].
*Examiner insight:* eliminate h before differentiating the volume. Differentiating a formula with both r and h in it makes the chain rule harder to apply.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing dy/dx for a y term but forgetting it on a product such as xy.
- Dividing by dx/dt instead of by dy/dt, or the reverse.
- Using a tangent gradient as the normal gradient, or forgetting to change the sign.
- Finding a gradient but then using the wrong point on the line.
- Giving a numerical answer for an exact-form question.
- Stopping at the cartesian equation without the range of the parameter.
- Forgetting the minus sign for a decreasing quantity.
- Differentiating a volume with two variables before eliminating one.
- Leaving out units on a rate of change.
