These notes condense subtopic **6.1 Photosynthesis** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series: the process, chlorophyll, the equations, what happens to the carbohydrates made, the investigations of what photosynthesis needs, and the factors that affect its rate. The full explanations and worked data are in the [6.1 study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-photosynthesis/). Then try the [6.1 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-photosynthesis-practice/).

## Key facts

| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Photosynthesis | The process by which plants make carbohydrates from raw materials using energy from light |
| Raw materials | Carbon dioxide (from air, through stomata) and water (from roots, through xylem) |
| Chlorophyll | Green pigment in chloroplasts; transfers light energy into chemical energy for making glucose and other carbohydrates |
| Product | Glucose, with oxygen released |

**Word equation:** carbon dioxide + water, in light and with chlorophyll, gives glucose + oxygen.

**Chemical equation:** 6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2 (light, chlorophyll)

## Uses of the carbohydrates

| Substance | Use |
|---|---|
| Glucose | Respiration, to release energy |
| Starch | Energy store (insoluble) |
| Cellulose | Builds cell walls |
| Sucrose | Transport through the plant in the phloem |

## Investigations

| To show the need for | Test | Control | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chlorophyll | Variegated leaf, in light | Green area of the same leaf | White areas stay orange-brown; green areas blue-black |
| Light | Cover part of a leaf with foil | The uncovered part | Covered area stays orange-brown |
| Carbon dioxide | Plant in a jar with potassium hydroxide or soda lime | Plant in a jar with water or hydrogencarbonate | No starch without carbon dioxide |

**Starch test in order:** destarch the plant (dark 24 to 48 hours) before the experiment; boil the leaf to kill it and soften it; heat in hot ethanol in a water bath to remove chlorophyll; rinse in water; add iodine; blue-black means starch.

Safety: ethanol is flammable, so use a water bath and no naked flame.

## Measuring the rate

- Use a submerged aquatic plant such as *Elodea*.
- Count bubbles of oxygen per minute, or collect the gas in a capillary tube.
- Change one factor, keep the others constant, repeat and take a mean.
- Change light intensity by moving the lamp closer or further away.
- Hydrogencarbonate indicator: **yellow** is high carbon dioxide, **orange or red** is atmospheric level, **purple** is low carbon dioxide.

| Observation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Indicator turns purple in light | Plant is using carbon dioxide faster than it makes it |
| Indicator turns yellow in dark | Respiration releases carbon dioxide and no photosynthesis uses it |

## Factors affecting the rate

| Factor | Effect | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Light intensity | Rate rises, then plateaus | More light energy for chlorophyll until another factor is limiting |
| Carbon dioxide | Rate rises, then plateaus | Raw material; more is available until another factor limits |
| Temperature | Rises to an optimum, then falls | Enzymes work faster, then are denatured above the optimum |

### Limiting factors

- The **limiting factor** is the factor in shortest supply, which stops the rate increasing.
- On a rising part of a graph, the factor on the horizontal axis is limiting.
- On a plateau, a different factor is limiting.
- To find the limiting factor on a plateau, find which change raises the plateau.

### Worked data

Distance of lamp 10 cm: 52 bubbles per minute; 20 cm: 31 bubbles per minute. Percentage fall from 10 to 20 cm = (52 - 31) divided by 52 x 100 = 40.4 per cent. Explanation: less light, so less energy for photosynthesis.

## Short model answers

**State the word equation.** Carbon dioxide + water gives glucose + oxygen, in the presence of light and chlorophyll.

**Explain why leaves are tested for starch after being boiled in ethanol.** Boiling in water kills the leaf and softens it. Hot ethanol removes the chlorophyll so that the blue-black colour of starch with iodine can be seen.

**Explain why the rate of photosynthesis stops rising at high light intensity.** Another factor, such as carbon dioxide concentration or temperature, is now limiting, so more light has no effect.

**Explain why photosynthesis slows above 45 degrees Celsius.** The enzymes are denatured, as their shape changes, so the reactions slow down.

## Exam traps

- Chlorophyll **transfers** energy; it does not make it.
- The chemical equation must balance: 6CO2 and 6H2O on the left, one C6H12O6 and 6O2 on the right.
- Use a water bath for ethanol; never a flame.
- Destarch the plant before the investigation.
- On a plateau, the factor on the x-axis is **not** limiting.
- At high temperatures the rate falls because enzymes are **denatured**, not because the plant runs out of light.
- Photosynthesis happens in the light, but plants respire all the time.

## Self-test

1. Define photosynthesis.
2. Name the pigment and where it is found.
3. Write the balanced symbol equation.
4. State two uses of glucose in a plant.
5. Why is sucrose, not starch, moved around the plant?
6. How is a plant destarched?
7. What is the control in the variegated leaf experiment?
8. What does potassium hydroxide do in the carbon dioxide experiment?
9. What colour is hydrogencarbonate indicator at low carbon dioxide?
10. What does a limiting factor mean?
11. Why does the rate fall at a high temperature?
12. A graph of rate against light intensity is flat. What does this tell you?

### Answers

1. The process by which plants make carbohydrates from raw materials (carbon dioxide and water) using energy from light.
2. Chlorophyll, in the chloroplasts.
3. 6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2.
4. Any two: respiration; making starch for storage; making cellulose for cell walls; making sucrose for transport.
5. Sucrose is soluble and can be transported in the phloem; starch is insoluble and is a store.
6. By keeping it in the dark for 24 to 48 hours.
7. The green part of the same leaf (or a plant given the same treatment without the change).
8. It absorbs carbon dioxide.
9. Purple.
10. The factor in shortest supply, which stops the rate from rising further.
11. Enzymes are denatured.
12. A different factor is limiting; light intensity is not.
