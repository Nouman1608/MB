Cambridge International AS & A Level Psychology (9990) introduces
students to the scientific study of behaviour and experience through a
set of core studies, a strong grounding in research methods and, at A
Level, applied specialist options such as clinical and health
psychology. It suits students interested in people, evidence and
argument, and it combines well with both sciences and humanities. The
full A Level is assessed by four written papers of equal size: each
lasts 90 minutes, carries 60 marks and is worth 25%. This overview
describes the 2024-2026 syllabus edition, whose final examination year
is 2026; if you are sitting later, check the syllabus for your year.

## How the course is organised

### AS Level

The AS content has two parts.

- **The core studies** are grouped under four approaches: the
  biological approach, the cognitive approach, the learning approach
  and the social approach. Each approach is explored through named
  studies that show how psychologists from that tradition investigate
  behaviour.
- **Research methodology** covers research methods and methodological
  concepts: how studies are designed, how data are collected and
  analysed, and how the quality of research is judged.

### A Level

The A Level year is built around four specialist options:

- **Clinical Psychology** – schizophrenia; mood (affective) disorders,
  including depressive disorder (unipolar) and bipolar disorder;
  impulse control disorders; anxiety disorders and fear-related
  disorders; and obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD).
- **Consumer Psychology** – the physical environment, the psychological
  environment, consumer decision-making, the product, and advertising.
- **Health Psychology** – the patient–practitioner relationship,
  adherence to medical advice, pain, stress, and health promotion.
- **Organisational Psychology** – motivation to work, leadership and
  management, group behaviour in organisations, organisational work
  conditions, and satisfaction at work.

Check with your teacher which of these options your course covers
before you start planning the A Level year.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | % of A Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Approaches, Issues and Debates | 90 minutes | 60 | 25% |
| Paper 2 | Research Methods | 90 minutes | 60 | 25% |
| Paper 3 | Specialist Options: Approaches, Issues and Debates | 90 minutes | 60 | 25% |
| Paper 4 | Specialist Options: Application and Research Methods | 90 minutes | 60 | 25% |

Papers 1 and 2 form the AS Level, where each is worth 50% of the AS.
Papers 3 and 4 complete the full A Level. All four papers are
externally assessed written exams.

The course is staged, and there are three ways through it:

- take AS Level (Papers 1 and 2) as a standalone qualification;
- take AS Level first and carry the result forward, completing Papers
  3 and 4 in a later series for the full A Level; or
- take all four papers together in the same series.

The 2024-2026 edition is examined in the June and November series,
and also in March in India.

## How the parts fit together

The paper titles reveal the course's design. Two ideas run all the way
through it: **approaches, issues and debates**, and **research
methods**. At AS, Paper 1 applies the first to the core studies and
Paper 2 assesses the second. At A Level, Paper 3 returns to approaches,
issues and debates in the context of the specialist options, and Paper
4 combines application with research methods in that same context.

This means the AS year is not just a first stage to get through. The
four approaches studied in the core studies (biological, cognitive,
learning and social) give you the vocabulary for explaining disorders,
consumer behaviour, health behaviour or workplace behaviour at A Level.
For example, explanations of schizophrenia or depression can be
biological or cognitive; explanations of adherence or motivation often
draw on learning or social ideas.

Research methodology is equally cumulative. The concepts you learn at
AS, such as how a study's design affects what it can show, are what
allow you to evaluate the specialist research and to answer questions
on designing or applying studies in Paper 4. Students who keep their
methods knowledge sharp across both years generally find the A Level
papers far more manageable.

## Planning your study

In the AS year, a practical approach is to study the core studies one
approach at a time while running research methodology alongside, so
each new study becomes a worked example of method. Good habits are to
summarise each study under consistent headings and to note which
issues and debates it raises.

In the A Level year, study your specialist options one at a time,
completing all of one before starting the next. Because Papers 3 and 4
both draw on the same options, revisiting each option for both
evaluation and application is more efficient than treating the two
papers separately.

Start short practice questions on each study or option as soon as it is
taught. Move to longer questions and past-paper practice once a full
approach or option is complete. The detailed approach to each paper and
its timing is set out in the exam preparation guide.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are in Cambridge A Level Psychology 9990?**
Four: Paper 1 Approaches, Issues and Debates; Paper 2 Research Methods; Paper 3 Specialist Options: Approaches, Issues and Debates; and Paper 4 Specialist Options: Application and Research Methods. Each is 90 minutes and 60 marks.

**Which papers make up AS Level Psychology?**
Papers 1 and 2. Each is worth 50% of the AS Level and 25% of the full A Level.

**What are the four approaches in the core studies?**
The biological, cognitive, learning and social approaches.

**What are the specialist options?**
Clinical Psychology, Consumer Psychology, Health Psychology and Organisational Psychology. They are assessed in Papers 3 and 4.

**Can I take all four papers at once?**
Yes. You can take all four papers in the same series, take AS first and complete the A Level later, or take AS Level on its own.
