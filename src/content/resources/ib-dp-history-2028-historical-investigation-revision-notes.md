Revision notes for the **historical investigation** (internal assessment) in IB DP History, first assessment 2028, for SL and HL. See the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-historical-investigation/) and the [practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-historical-investigation-practice/).

## The task in one table

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Task | Individual historical investigation into a topic of your choice |
| Time | About 20 hours |
| Weighting | 30 per cent (SL); 20 per cent (HL) |
| Marks | 24 |
| Length | Maximum 2,200 words, stated at the start; references, headings and bibliography excluded |
| Sources | Maximum seven |
| Document | One document: three sections plus bibliography |
| Marked by | Your teacher; externally moderated by the IB |

## Sections, criteria and marks

| Section | Suggested words | Criterion | Marks | What is assessed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Historical inquiry question | 400 | A | 6 | Formulating an appropriate, conceptual question |
| 2. Sources and perspectives | 400 | B | 6 | Identifying and choosing sources with diverse perspectives |
| 3. Synthesis and evaluation | 1,400 | C | 12 | Synthesising evidence, evaluating sources and reaching a reasoned judgement |

The word split is a suggestion. Criterion C is worth half the marks.

## Criterion A: what to do

1. **State** the inquiry question clearly.
2. **Explain** why it is appropriate for historical inquiry (analytical, not descriptive; invites diverse perspectives; treated historically if contemporary).
3. **Explain** how it is conceptual and how the concept informs the inquiry.

**Checklist for a good question.**

- Focused: fits the word limit.
- Analytical: asks "to what extent", "how far", "why" or "how", and leads to a judgement.
- Conceptual: linked to cause and consequence, continuity and change, perspectives, significance, or another concept.
- Has enough different sources and views.

**Levels at a glance.**

| Marks | Summary |
|---|---|
| 1 to 2 | Question unclear; descriptive; vague or generic reference to a concept |
| 3 to 4 | Question clear; partial explanation of appropriateness; concept referenced but only superficially explained |
| 5 to 6 | Question clear; full explanation of appropriateness; precise, detailed references to an appropriate concept and a clear explanation of how it informs the inquiry |

## Criterion B: what to do

- Explain how your selected sources (up to seven) provide **diverse historical perspectives**.
- Explain how those perspectives will help **answer your question**.
- Organise by perspective, not source by source.
- Make sure the sources match those you use in section 3.

| Marks | Summary |
|---|---|
| 1 to 2 | Sources identified or described; limited reference to how they will be used |
| 3 to 4 | Explains how the sources provide diverse perspectives; partly explains how they help answer the question |
| 5 to 6 | Well-developed explanation of diverse perspectives; clear explanation of how they help answer the question |

## Criterion C: what to do

- **Synthesise** evidence from the sources into an analytical argument.
- **Evaluate** sources as appropriate, using context (author, date, audience, purpose) and linking evaluation to the argument.
- **Judge**: reach a reasoned judgement consistent with the analysis.

| Marks | Summary |
|---|---|
| 1 to 3 | Generalisations and poorly supported assertions; limited use of sources; no clear judgement |
| 4 to 6 | Mostly descriptive with some analysis; some evidence; minimal evaluation; judgement partly consistent |
| 7 to 9 | Mostly analytical; supported by evidence; partial evaluation; consistent judgement |
| 10 to 12 | Sustained analysis focused on the question; fully supported by synthesised evidence; evaluation integrated; reasoned judgement |

## Choosing the topic: rules

- Your choice, with teacher approval before you start.
- No regional limits; no date limits (including before 750 CE).
- Need not link to the syllabus.
- Contemporary (about the past two decades) allowed, but treated historically.
- Access to enough sources is essential.
- Consider ethics (sensitivity, confidentiality).

## Source and evidence

- **Source:** any object historians can use (written, visual, diagrammatic, multimodal, material).
- **Evidence:** the information taken from the source.

## Worked example: improving a question

Draft: "What caused the Great Fire of London?" (a narrow factual question; little room for diverse perspectives).

Improved: "To what extent was the 1666 fire's spread caused by the conditions of the city rather than by the actions of the authorities?" This is a cause and consequence question about actors and conditions. It permits perspectives (diarists, official reports, later historians) and leads to a judgement.

Criterion A sentence: "This question is appropriate for historical inquiry because it asks for a judgement about causation, and it invites comparison of eyewitness accounts with official records and later historians. It is conceptual because it draws on cause and consequence, in particular the interplay of historical actors and conditions, which will guide how I weigh the evidence." (Invented illustration.)

## Process checklist

- Topic approved by teacher.
- Question redrafted at least once.
- No more than seven sources.
- Sources in section 2 match those in section 3.
- Word count stated and within 2,200.
- References accurate; bibliography included.
- One draft submitted for advice.
- Final version is your own work.

## Common mistakes

- Descriptive questions.
- Concept named but not used.
- Source-by-source section 2.
- Over seven sources.
- Narrative in section 3.
- Isolated source evaluation.
- Judgement not following from analysis.

## Self-test

1. How many words may the IA contain?
2. How many marks are available, and how are they split?
3. How many sources may you use?
4. What percentage of the final assessment is the IA at SL and HL?
5. Name two things you must do in section 1.
6. What is the difference between a source and evidence?
7. Is the bibliography assessed?

**Answers.**

1. No more than 2,200 words, excluding references, headings and the bibliography.
2. 24 marks: criterion A 6, criterion B 6, criterion C 12.
3. A maximum of seven.
4. 30 per cent at SL and 20 per cent at HL.
5. Any two of: state the question clearly; explain why it is appropriate for historical inquiry; explain how it is conceptual and how the concept informs the inquiry.
6. A source is any object historians can use; evidence is the information taken from it.
7. No. It is required but not assessed and does not count towards the word limit.
