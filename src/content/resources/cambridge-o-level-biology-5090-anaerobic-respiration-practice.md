## Section B

**6.** A student sprinted for 100 m. Her blood lactic acid concentration was measured at intervals.

| Time | Lactic acid (mmol per dm cubed) |
|---|---|
| Before the sprint | 1.2 |
| Immediately after | 11.2 |
| 15 minutes after | 6.2 |
| 45 minutes after | 2.2 |

**(a)** Calculate the increase in lactic acid concentration during the sprint. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage fall in lactic acid concentration between immediately after the sprint and 45 minutes after. Show your working. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why lactic acid built up during the sprint. **[3]**

**(d)** Explain why her breathing rate stayed high after the sprint. **[3]**

**(e)** Describe the role of the heart and blood in removing lactic acid. **[2]**

**7.** Define the term oxygen debt (Excess Post-exercise Oxygen Consumption). **[2]**

**8.** A baker mixes yeast, sugar and flour with warm water and leaves the dough in a warm place. After an hour, the dough has doubled in volume.

**(a)** Name the gas that makes the dough rise. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the process in the yeast that produces this gas, and give the word equation. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why the bread does not taste of alcohol after baking. **[1]**

**9.** Compare aerobic respiration and anaerobic respiration in human muscle. **[4]**

**10.** A student set up an investigation using yeast in boiled, cooled glucose solution covered with a layer of oil, connected by a tube to limewater.

**(a)** Explain why the glucose solution was boiled. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the oil layer was added. **[1]**

**(c)** State the expected result in the limewater and what it shows. **[2]**

**11.** Explain why a marathon runner is less likely to build up a large amount of lactic acid than a 100 m sprinter. **[3]**

**12.** A person's pulse rate is 72 beats per minute at rest. Immediately after a sprint it is 168 beats per minute, and 10 minutes later it is 96 beats per minute.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in pulse rate caused by the sprint. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the pulse rate had not returned to 72 after 10 minutes. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** C [1]. **[1]**

**3.** The release of a relatively small amount of energy [1] by the breakdown of glucose [1] without using oxygen [1]. **[3]**

**4.** Glucose → lactic acid [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Any one of: bread-making; brewing alcoholic drinks; making biofuel [1]. **[1]**

**6.**

**(a)** 11.2 - 1.2 = 10.0 mmol per dm cubed [1].

**(b)** Fall = 11.2 - 2.2 = 9.0 [1]. 9.0 divided by 11.2 x 100 = 80.4 per cent (accept 80) [1].

**(c)** Her muscles needed energy faster than oxygen could be supplied [1], so they respired anaerobically as well [1]. Lactic acid was made faster than it was removed, so it built up [1].

**(d)** Extra oxygen was needed to break down the lactic acid [1], in the liver [1]. This extra oxygen is the oxygen debt, so deep, fast breathing continued to supply it [1].

**(e)** The heart kept beating fast [1], so the blood carried the lactic acid from the muscles to the liver [1]. **[11]**

**7.** The extra oxygen taken in after exercise [1] that is needed to break down the lactic acid that built up [1]. **[2]**

**8.**

**(a)** Carbon dioxide [1].

**(b)** Anaerobic respiration (fermentation) [1]. Glucose → ethanol [1] + carbon dioxide [1].

**(c)** The ethanol evaporates during baking [1]. **[5]**

**9.** Any four of: aerobic respiration needs oxygen but anaerobic does not [1]; aerobic releases a relatively large amount of energy but anaerobic a small amount [1]; aerobic breaks glucose down completely but anaerobic only partly [1]; aerobic produces carbon dioxide and water but anaerobic produces lactic acid [1]; both release energy from glucose [1]. **[4]**

**10.**

**(a)** To remove dissolved oxygen (and kill other microbes) [1].

**(b)** To keep out oxygen from the air [1].

**(c)** The limewater goes cloudy [1], showing that carbon dioxide is produced without oxygen [1]. **[4]**

**11.** A marathon is run at a steady pace, so the muscles' demand for energy is lower [1]. Oxygen can be supplied fast enough for most respiration to be aerobic [1], so little lactic acid is made, but in a sprint the demand is very high and anaerobic respiration makes lactic acid faster than it is removed [1]. **[3]**

**12.**

**(a)** Increase = 168 - 72 = 96 [1]. 96 divided by 72 x 100 = 133 per cent [1].

**(b)** Lactic acid was still in the muscles and blood [1], and the heart was still beating faster to carry it to the liver to be broken down [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Mixing up the products in humans and yeast.
- Writing that anaerobic respiration produces no energy.
- Forgetting to mention the liver in the removal of lactic acid.
- Describing the oxygen debt as a lack of oxygen rather than extra oxygen needed.
- Adding water as a product of anaerobic respiration.
