Condensed notes on section **3.9.4 Assessing greater use of digital technology** of AQA A-level Business (7132, for first teaching from September 2023). Section 3.9 is A-level only. The warehouse robots, conversion rate and data mining examples are in the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-greater-use-of-digital-technology/); questions are in the [practice set](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-greater-use-of-digital-technology-practice/).

## Specification content

- The pressures to adopt digital technology; digital technology to include automation, e-commerce, big data and data mining.
- The value of digital technology.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Digital technology | Computers, networks, software and data used in business |
| Automation | Machines or software doing tasks with little human input |
| E-commerce | Buying and selling over the internet |
| Big data | Very large, fast-growing data sets from sales, websites and sensors |
| Data mining | Searching data for patterns and relationships |
| Conversion rate | Percentage of website visitors who buy |
| Churn | Percentage of customers lost in a period |
| Cyber security | Protection of systems and data from attack |

## Pressures to adopt

Competition; customer expectations; cost pressure; cheaper and cloud-based technology; remote working and flexible supply chains; new digital entrants; regulatory and customer requirements; shareholder pressure for efficiency.

## The four forms compared

| Form | Main value | Main limits |
|---|---|---|
| Automation | Lower unit cost; consistent quality; 24-hour working; safer | High cost; redundancies; breakdown or attack stops output; inflexible |
| E-commerce | Wider market; lower overheads; customer data | Price comparison; delivery and returns costs; security duties; digital exclusion |
| Big data | Understand customers; target marketing; forecast demand | Cost; data quality; privacy law; security |
| Data mining | Finds patterns: churn, fraud, product links | Needs skills; correlation is not cause; misuse of personal data |

## Useful calculations

- Orders = visitors × conversion rate. Revenue = orders × average spend.
- Customers lost = customers × churn rate.
- Net saving from automation = labour saved − running costs. Payback = cost ÷ net saving.

**Mini-example.** 80,000 visitors, 3% convert, average spend £40: orders = 2,400; revenue = **£96,000**. If conversion rises to 3.5%: orders = 2,800; revenue = **£112,000**; increase **£16,000** (16.7%).

## Impact by function

| Function | Impact |
|---|---|
| Operations | Automation; real-time tracking; predictive maintenance |
| Marketing | E-commerce; targeted advertising; customer data |
| Human resources | Retraining; remote work; redundancies; new roles |
| Finance | Large investment; real-time data; digital payments; fraud risk |

## Assessing a decision to go digital

1. Fit with strategy and objectives.
2. Cost and return (payback, ARR, NPV).
3. Readiness: skills, culture, quality of data.
4. Reaction of customers and employees.
5. Risk: security, system failure, reliance on suppliers, rivals copying.
6. Timing: first mover or follower.

Technology alone rarely gives lasting advantage, because rivals can buy the same tools. Advantage comes from using it with unique skills, data and service.

## Links to other parts of the course

- **Strategic direction:** e-commerce opens new markets; data reveals product gaps.
- **Positioning:** automation supports low cost; personalisation supports differentiation.
- **Competitive environment:** low set-up cost means more entrants; price comparison raises buyer power.
- **Innovation:** connected products and apps are product innovation.
- **Change management:** involve staff, train them and introduce in stages.

## Barriers to successful adoption

Skills gaps; old systems that do not connect; resistance from staff; poor integration and duplicate data; cost overruns; customers who lack access or confidence online.

## Ethical and legal points

- Data protection law limits how personal data can be collected and used.
- Breaches damage trust and can bring fines.
- Automation raises questions about job losses; responsible firms retrain and redeploy.
- Algorithms can make unfair decisions if data is biased.

## Model analysis paragraph

"Automation could cut the warehouse's costs. If nine robots replace workers costing £225,000 a year and have running costs of £45,000, the net saving is £180,000 a year, and a £450,000 investment pays back in 2.5 years. This lowers unit costs and supports a low-cost strategy. But redundancies could harm morale, and a breakdown could stop dispatch. The decision is sound only if demand is stable enough to keep the robots busy and staff are retrained."

## Command words

- **Analyse:** name the form of technology and the function it affects.
- **Evaluate:** weigh cost, risk and people against the gains, and say what the decision depends on.

## Exam traps

- Big data is the data; data mining is the analysis.
- Technology is not automatically beneficial. Weigh cost, risk and people.
- Mention the human effects of automation.
- Link technology to a business objective and a function.
- Include data protection when discussing data.

## Self-test

1. Define "automation".
2. Distinguish big data from data mining.
3. Give two pressures that might lead a retailer to start selling online.
4. A website has 50,000 visitors and a 4% conversion rate. How many orders?
5. Each order averages £30. Calculate revenue.
6. Give two limits of e-commerce.
7. A machine costs £200,000 and saves £80,000 a year net. Calculate payback.
8. Give one human cost of automation.
9. Why must a business be careful when using customer data?
10. Give one reason why technology may not give a lasting advantage.
11. Explain how data mining could help a bank.
12. Give one benefit of automation for operations.

### Answers

1. Using machines or software to carry out tasks with little human input.
2. Big data is the very large data sets themselves; data mining is searching them for patterns.
3. Any two: competitors selling online; customer expectations; lower overheads; wider market.
4. 50,000 × 4% = **2,000** orders.
5. 2,000 × £30 = **£60,000**.
6. Any two: price competition; delivery and returns costs; security and fraud; dependence on the website; digital exclusion.
7. £200,000 ÷ £80,000 = **2.5 years**.
8. Any one: redundancies; lower morale; loss of skills; retraining cost.
9. Data protection law limits its use, and a breach could bring fines and a loss of trust.
10. Rivals can buy the same technology, so the advantage can be copied quickly.
11. It can find patterns that signal fraud, or customers likely to leave, so the bank can act sooner.
12. Any one: lower unit cost; consistent quality; 24-hour production; fewer errors.
