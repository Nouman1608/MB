These questions cover section **3.11.2 Thermodynamics and engines** of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408), part of the A-level only Engineering physics option. Learn the content first in the [thermodynamics and engines study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-thermodynamics-and-engines-aqa-alevel/) and the [thermodynamics and engines revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-thermodynamics-and-engines-aqa-alevel-revision-notes/).

Data: molar gas constant R = 8.31 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹. Engine and power-station figures are invented for practice.

## Section A

**1.** In the equation Q = ΔU + W, state what each of the three symbols represents. **[3]**

**2.** A gas is compressed adiabatically and 180 J of work is done on it. State the values of Q, W and ΔU. **[2]**

**3.** A sealed rigid container of gas receives 450 J by heating. State the work done by the gas and the change in its internal energy. **[2]**

**4.** An engine works between a source at 820 K and a sink at 290 K.

**(a)** Calculate its maximum theoretical efficiency. **[1]**
**(b)** Give two reasons why a practical engine has a lower efficiency. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why a heat engine cannot operate according to the First Law alone. **[2]**

**6.** A heat pump delivers 8.0 kW to a building and uses 2.5 kW of electrical power. Calculate its coefficient of performance and the rate at which it extracts energy from outside. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** 0.12 mol of an ideal gas at 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa and 290 K is heated at constant pressure to 435 K.

**(a)** Calculate the initial volume. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the final volume. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the work done by the gas. **[1]**
**(d)** The internal energy of the gas increases by 217 J. Calculate the energy supplied by heating. **[1]**

**8.** In a diesel engine, air (γ = 1.4) at 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa and 300 K is compressed adiabatically from 5.0 × 10⁻⁴ m³ to 2.5 × 10⁻⁵ m³.

**(a)** Calculate the final pressure. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the final temperature. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why fuel injected at this point ignites without a spark. **[1]**

**9.** A gas is taken round a cycle. A to B: volume constant at 1.0 × 10⁻³ m³ while pressure rises from 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa to 3.0 × 10⁵ Pa. B to C: a straight line on the p–V diagram to 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa and 4.0 × 10⁻³ m³. C to A: pressure constant at 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa back to 1.0 × 10⁻³ m³.

**(a)** Calculate the net work done per cycle. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the work done on the gas from C to A. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the work done by the gas from B to C. **[1]**
**(d)** State, with a reason, whether this cycle represents an engine. **[1]**

**10.** A four-cylinder, four-stroke engine runs at 4200 rpm. The area of each indicator loop is 600 J and the brake torque is 150 N m. The fuel has a calorific value of 4.6 × 10⁷ J kg⁻¹ and is used at 6.0 × 10⁻³ kg s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the indicated power. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the brake power and the friction power. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the overall, thermal and mechanical efficiencies. **[3]**

**11.** Describe and explain two differences between the indicator diagram of a real four-stroke petrol engine and its theoretical cycle. **[4]**

**12.** A ground-source heat pump keeps a house at 308 K (radiator temperature) using ground at 278 K.

**(a)** Calculate the maximum possible coefficient of performance. **[1]**
**(b)** The real heat pump has a COP of 3.5 and must deliver 7.0 kW. Calculate the electrical power it needs and the rate at which it takes energy from the ground. **[2]**

**13.** A power station has an overall efficiency of 0.38 and an electrical output of 500 MW.

**(a)** Calculate the rate at which energy is supplied by the fuel and the rate at which energy is rejected. **[2]**
**(b)** In a combined heat and power scheme, 60% of the rejected energy is used to heat buildings. Calculate the fraction of the fuel's energy that is now used. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Q: energy transferred to the system by heating [1]; ΔU: increase in internal energy of the system [1]; W: work done by the system [1]

**2.** Q = 0 and W = −180 J [1]; ΔU = Q − W = **+180 J** [1]

**3.** W = 0 (no volume change) [1]; ΔU = Q = **450 J** [1]

**4. (a)** (820 − 290)/820 = **0.65** [1]
**(b)** Any two: friction between moving parts; heat lost through the cylinder walls or to the surroundings; combustion and heat transfer take time, so processes are not reversible or ideal [2]

**5.** The First Law would allow all the heat input to be converted into work [1]; the Second Law requires the engine to operate between a hot source and a cold sink, so some energy must always be rejected to the sink [1]

**6.** COP = 8.0/2.5 = **3.2** [1]; energy from outside = 8.0 − 2.5 = **5.5 kW** [1]

**7. (a)** V = nRT/p = 0.12 × 8.31 × 290/1.0 × 10⁵ = **2.89 × 10⁻³ m³** [1]
**(b)** V ∝ T at constant p: 2.89 × 10⁻³ × 435/290 = **4.34 × 10⁻³ m³** [1]
**(c)** W = pΔV = 1.0 × 10⁵ × 1.45 × 10⁻³ = **145 J** [1]
**(d)** Q = ΔU + W = 217 + 145 = **362 J** [1]

**8. (a)** p₂ = p₁(V₁/V₂)^γ = 1.0 × 10⁵ × 20^1.4 [1]; = **6.6 × 10⁶ Pa** [1]
**(b)** T₂ = T₁ × p₂V₂/(p₁V₁) = 300 × (6.63 × 10⁶ × 2.5 × 10⁻⁵)/(1.0 × 10⁵ × 5.0 × 10⁻⁴) [1]; = **990 K** [1]
**(c)** Work done on the air in the adiabatic compression raises its internal energy, so its temperature is above the fuel's ignition temperature [1]

**9. (a)** Loop area = triangle = ½ × (4.0 − 1.0) × 10⁻³ × (3.0 − 1.0) × 10⁵ [1]; = **300 J** [1]
**(b)** pΔV = 1.0 × 10⁵ × 3.0 × 10⁻³ = **300 J** [1]
**(c)** Area under BC (trapezium) = ½ × (3.0 + 1.0) × 10⁵ × 3.0 × 10⁻³ = **600 J** [1]
**(d)** Yes: the loop is traversed clockwise, so the gas does more work expanding than is done on it, giving net work output [1]
*Examiner insight:* Check (a) using (b) and (c): 600 J by the gas minus 300 J on the gas gives 300 J net.

**10. (a)** Cycles per second per cylinder = (4200/60)/2 = 35 [1]; indicated power = 600 × 35 × 4 = **84 kW** [1]
**(b)** ω = 4200 × 2π/60 = 440 rad s⁻¹; brake power = 150 × 440 = **66 kW** [1]; friction power = 84 − 66 = **18 kW** [1]
**(c)** Input power = 4.6 × 10⁷ × 6.0 × 10⁻³ = 276 kW; overall = 66/276 = **0.24** [1]; thermal = 84/276 = **0.30** [1]; mechanical = 66/84 = **0.79** [1]

**11.** Corners are rounded rather than sharp [1]; because valves take time to open and close, and the fuel takes time to burn, so heat is not supplied at constant volume [1]; the loop area (and peak pressure) is smaller [1]; because heat is lost through the cylinder walls and the gas is not ideal; there is also a small pumping loop for induction and exhaust [1]

**12. (a)** T_H/(T_H − T_C) = 308/30 = **10.3** [1]
**(b)** W = 7.0/3.5 = **2.0 kW** [1]; Q_C = 7.0 − 2.0 = **5.0 kW** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Lower radiator temperatures raise the maximum COP; that is why heat pumps suit underfloor heating.

**13. (a)** Fuel input = 500/0.38 = **1.3 × 10³ MW** [1]; rejected = 1316 − 500 = **820 MW** [1]
**(b)** (500 + 0.60 × 816)/1316 = **0.75** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing W = +180 J for work done on the gas.
- Using Celsius in efficiency or COP formulas.
- Using rev s⁻¹ instead of rev s⁻¹ ÷ 2 for cycles per second in a four-stroke engine.
- Missing the number of cylinders in indicated power.
- Describing differences between real and theoretical cycles without the reasons.
