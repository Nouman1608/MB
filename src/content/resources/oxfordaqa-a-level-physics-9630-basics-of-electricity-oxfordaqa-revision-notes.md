## The three equations

| Equation | In words | Rearrangements |
|---|---|---|
| I = ΔQ/Δt | Current is the rate of flow of charge | ΔQ = IΔt; Δt = ΔQ/I |
| V = W/Q | Pd is work done per unit charge | W = QV; Q = W/V |
| R = V/I | Resistance is pd per unit current | V = IR; I = V/R |

Linked forms you will use later: N = ΔQ/e for a number of electrons; W = VIt for energy transferred; W = eV for one electron.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Charge carrier | Charged particle that moves to form a current: electrons in metals, ions in electrolytes |
| Conventional current | Direction positive charge would flow: + to − outside the source |
| Coulomb | Charge passing when 1 A flows for 1 s (1 C = 1 A s) |
| Volt | One joule per coulomb |
| Ohm | One volt per ampere |
| Quantised charge | Charge comes in whole multiples of e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C |
| Ideal ammeter | Zero resistance; placed in series |
| Ideal voltmeter | Infinite resistance; placed in parallel |

## Graph rules

| Graph | Gradient | Area under line |
|---|---|---|
| Charge (vertical) against time | Current | (not used) |
| Current (vertical) against time | (not used) | Charge |

For a current that changes in straight-line sections, split the area into rectangles and triangles. Average current = total charge ÷ total time.

## Units and prefixes

| Prefix | Factor | Example |
|---|---|---|
| m (milli) | 10⁻³ | 250 mA = 0.250 A |
| μ (micro) | 10⁻⁶ | 40 μC = 4.0 × 10⁻⁵ C |
| k (kilo) | 10³ | 2.0 kV = 2000 V |
| M (mega) | 10⁶ | 1.2 MΩ = 1.2 × 10⁶ Ω |

Base units: C = A s; V = kg m² s⁻³ A⁻¹; Ω = kg m² s⁻³ A⁻².

Time: 1 min = 60 s; 1 h = 3600 s.

## Method boxes

**Number of electrons**

1. Find the charge: ΔQ = IΔt (time in s).
2. Divide by e: N = ΔQ / 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹.

**Energy transferred**

1. Find the charge if it is not given.
2. Use W = QV, or W = VIt in one step.

**Resistance at a point**

1. Read V and I at that point (not the gradient).
2. R = V/I, units Ω.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Charge and current.** Charge (C) is an amount; current (A) is how fast that amount passes a point.
- **Current and pd.** Current is measured *through* a component; pd is measured *across* it, between two points.
- **R = V/I and Ohm's law.** R = V/I defines resistance for any component at any point; Ohm's law is the special case where R stays constant.
- **Electron flow and conventional current.** Electrons in a wire drift from − to + outside the source, opposite to the conventional current.

## Worked reminder

The current in a warming thermistor rises steadily from 0.20 A to 0.80 A over 3.0 s while the pd across it is 6.0 V.

1. Charge = area of the trapezium = ½ × (0.20 + 0.80) × 3.0 = 1.5 C.
2. Average current = 1.5 / 3.0 = 0.50 A.
3. Energy transferred = QV = 1.5 × 6.0 = 9.0 J.

## Exam traps

- Current defined as "flow of charge" without "rate".
- Pd defined as "energy" rather than energy per unit charge.
- Time left in minutes or hours.
- One quantity with a prefix converted and the other forgotten.
- Gradient of a curved I-V graph used as resistance or its reciprocal.
- Dividing by e to find charge, instead of multiplying the number of electrons by e.
- Meters drawn the wrong way round: ammeter in parallel, voltmeter in series.

## Quick self-test

1. Define electric current.
2. A current of 250 mA flows for 8.0 minutes. Find the charge.
3. How many electrons carry the charge in Question 2?
4. A 9.0 V supply drives 40 C round a circuit. Find the energy transferred in the external circuit, assuming the pd across it is 9.0 V.
5. Define potential difference.
6. A resistor has 3.6 V across it and carries 0.15 A. Find its resistance.
7. What does the gradient of a charge-time graph represent?
8. A current falls steadily from 2.0 A to zero in 5.0 s. Find the charge that flows.
9. An electron is accelerated through 150 V. Find the energy it gains in joules.
10. Express the volt in SI base units.
11. Why must an ammeter be connected in series, and why should its resistance be very small?
12. The pd across a component is 1.5 V while 5.0 × 10¹⁸ electrons pass through it. Find the energy transferred.

### Answers

1. The rate of flow of charge, I = ΔQ/Δt.
2. Q = 0.250 × 480 = 120 C.
3. N = 120 / 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 7.5 × 10²⁰.
4. W = QV = 40 × 9.0 = 360 J.
5. The work done (energy transferred) per unit charge moving between two points.
6. R = 3.6 / 0.15 = 24 Ω.
7. The current.
8. Area of the triangle = ½ × 2.0 × 5.0 = 5.0 C.
9. W = eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 150 = 2.4 × 10⁻¹⁷ J.
10. J C⁻¹ = (kg m² s⁻²)/(A s) = kg m² s⁻³ A⁻¹.
11. In series the same current passes through it as through the component; a very small resistance means it does not reduce the current it is measuring.
12. Q = 5.0 × 10¹⁸ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 0.80 C; W = QV = 0.80 × 1.5 = 1.2 J.
