This guide covers **Identities**, one of five compulsory themes for
IB Diploma Programme Language B, first assessment 2020. The [full
syllabus guide](/resources/ib-dp-language-b-syllabus-guide/) confirms
all five prescribed themes are compulsory and examined through unseen
material in Paper 2, alongside [Sharing the
Planet](/resources/ib-dp-language-b-sharing-the-planet/).

## Where this fits in the syllabus

Because Paper 2's listening and reading sections use **unseen**
material across all five themes, an unfamiliar text on Identities is
just as likely to appear as one on any other theme. Concentrating
revision only on the themes discussed most in classroom speaking
practice -- Identities is often the first theme taught, and so risks
being under-revised once teaching moves on -- leaves real gaps in
comprehension for the exam's unseen material.

## What Identities covers

Explores the nature of the self and what it means to be human.
Recommended topics include lifestyles, health and well-being, beliefs
and values, subcultures, and language and identity. The theme's
guiding question -- how do language and culture contribute to forming
an identity? -- is a useful lens to apply to any Identities text,
whether written, audio, visual or audio-visual, rather than a fixed
list of facts to memorise.

## Vocabulary and comprehension practice

Because Paper 2's reading and listening sections are worth 50%
combined -- as much as productive writing and the internal oral put
together -- comprehension vocabulary specific to this theme
(describing personality, values, cultural belonging, lifestyle
choices) deserves dedicated practice time, not just the vocabulary
needed to talk about the theme yourself. A student who can discuss
Identities fluently in speaking practice but has not built
comprehension vocabulary for it separately is only prepared for half
of what the exam actually tests.

## Worked example: applying the guiding question to an unseen text

An unseen reading passage discusses how a community's traditional
dress has changed across generations. The surface content simply
describes changes in traditional dress over time. Applying the theme's
guiding question means asking how the described change in dress
reflects a shift in how the community's identity is expressed or
understood across generations. The comprehension skill actually being
tested is connecting a specific detail in the passage -- what changed,
when, why -- to this broader theme question, rather than only
extracting isolated facts. Practising this connection, specific detail
to broader theme question, under timed conditions is more useful
preparation than memorising vocabulary lists in isolation.

## Text types and register

For Paper 1's writing task, if a question falls under Identities,
practise the specific text types the syllabus expects (a blog post, a
formal letter, an article) using vocabulary and register appropriate
to discussing lifestyle, values or personal identity specifically --
format and register are assessed alongside content, so a
well-argued response written in the wrong text-type conventions still
loses marks.

## The individual oral connection

For SL students, the individual oral uses a visual stimulus relevant
to one of the five themes -- if that stimulus relates to Identities,
be ready to discuss lifestyle, values, subcultures or personal
identity using the theme's specific vocabulary rather than generic
conversational language. For HL students, whose oral stimulus comes
from one of their two studied literary works, check early whether
either work engages with identity-related content, since a work that
clearly connects to this theme gives a ready source of textual
reference to draw on if an Identities-related discussion arises.

## Cultural variation within the theme

Because Language B is taught across many different school and cultural
contexts, "identity" itself can be discussed very differently
depending on the target-language culture being studied -- family
structure, social expectations and the language used to discuss
personal identity vary meaningfully between cultures. Paying attention
to how identity is discussed specifically within the culture or
cultures associated with your target language, rather than assuming
your own cultural assumptions transfer directly, strengthens both
comprehension of unseen material and the authenticity of your own
spoken and written responses.

## Common exam pitfalls

Discussing surface content of an Identities text without connecting
it back to the theme's guiding question about language, culture and
identity formation is the most common gap -- a summary of what a
passage says is not, on its own, an answer to a comprehension question
that expects the broader theme applied. Assuming familiarity with a
theme discussed heavily in class guarantees comprehension of
unfamiliar texts on it in the exam is a second common mistake: fluent
speaking practice on Identities does not automatically transfer to
strong listening or reading comprehension of a genuinely unseen text
on the same theme, since the two skills draw on different vocabulary
recognition and processing demands. A third is neglecting register in
the Paper 1 writing task -- a well-argued response about lifestyle or
personal values that ignores the required text-type conventions (a
blog post's informal, first-person voice versus a formal letter's
conventions, for instance) is marked down even where the content
itself is strong.

## A note on assessment weighting

Like the other four themes, Identities is not tested as a separately
named exam section -- it is one of the five themes Paper 2's unseen
material can be drawn from, and one of several possible sources for
Paper 1's writing task and the individual oral's stimulus. Preparation
is most effective when it builds transferable comprehension and
production fluency around the theme's vocabulary and guiding question,
rather than treating Identities as a fixed block of content to recall
verbatim.

## How to approach it

Build vocabulary across all five recommended Identities topics, not
just the one or two discussed most in class. Practise unseen listening
and reading comprehension on Identities specifically, not only
speaking and writing output, since receptive skills carry equal
weighting. For HL, check whether either of your two studied literary
works connects to the Identities theme, since the individual oral
stimulus is drawn from one of them. Treat Identities as a lens applied
fluently to unfamiliar material, rather than a fixed block of content
to recall verbatim.
