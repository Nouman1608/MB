## Section A

**1.** Identify three problems that weakened liberal governments in Italy in 1919-20. **[3]**

**2.** State what the Acerbo Law (1923) provided and explain how it helped Mussolini. **[3]**

**3.** Explain two consequences of the murder of Giacomo Matteotti in 1924. **[4]**

**4.** Identify two fascist institutions and two traditional state institutions that shared power in Italy after 1926. **[4]**

**5.** Identify, with dates, three measures that built the Corporate State. **[3]**

**6.** Give two achievements and one failure of the Battle for Grain. **[3]**

**7.** State three provisions of the Lateran settlement of 1929. **[3]**

**8.** Explain two reasons why Italy moved closer to Germany after 1935. **[4]**

---

## Section B

**9.** "Propaganda was more important than terror in maintaining Mussolini's control of Italy." Assess this view. **[30]**

**10.** Evaluate the success of Mussolini's foreign policy in achieving its aims, 1922-41. **[30]**

**11.** Assess the impact of the Great Depression on Mussolini's economic policies. **[30]**

**12.** "Fascist policies changed little in the lives of Italian women." Assess this view. **[30]**

---

## Answers

**1.** Any three, 1 mark each: inflation and rising unemployment after demobilisation (1); the "mutilated victory" and d'Annunzio's seizure of Fiume, which exposed the state's weakness (1); the Biennio Rosso strikes, land seizures and factory occupations (1); proportional representation producing a parliament dominated by Socialists and Popolari who would not co-operate with the liberals, so cabinets kept falling (1); war debts and the cost of the war (1). **[3]**

**2.** The list with the most votes, if it gained at least 25 per cent, received two-thirds of the seats in the Chamber (1). It encouraged liberals and conservatives to join the fascist-led national list (1), and in April 1924 that list won about two-thirds of the vote, giving Mussolini a secure parliamentary majority (1). **[3]**

**3.** Two consequences, 2 marks each (1 for the consequence, 1 for development): the Aventine Secession -- opposition deputies left parliament, but this removed them from the chamber and the king did not act, so the protest failed (2); Mussolini, pressed by the ras to act decisively, used his speech of 3 January 1925 to accept responsibility and begin building a dictatorship, leading to the laws of 1925-26 (2); press censorship was tightened and opposition newspapers were closed (2). **[4]**

**4.** 1 mark each, two from each group. Fascist: the Duce as Head of Government (1); the PNF (1); the Fascist Grand Council (1); the MVSN militia (1). Traditional: the monarchy, Victor Emmanuel III (1); the army (1); the civil service and prefects (1); the judiciary (1); parliament, until replaced by the Chamber of Fasces and Corporations in 1939 (1). **[4]**

**5.** Any three, 1 mark each: the Palazzo Vidoni Pact, 1925, by which Confindustria and the fascist unions recognised only each other (1); the Rocco Law, 1926, banning strikes and lockouts and creating labour courts (1); the Ministry of Corporations, 1926 (1); the Charter of Labour, 1927 (1); the National Council of Corporations, 1930 (1); the 22 corporations, 1934 (1). **[3]**

**6.** Achievements, 1 mark each, up to 2: wheat output rose and imports of grain fell (1); a propaganda success that presented Mussolini as a farmer-leader (1); prizes and new methods encouraged mechanisation on larger farms (1). Failure, 1 mark: land was switched from more suitable crops such as olives, citrus and vines, and bread became dearer because of tariffs (1). **[3]**

**7.** 1 mark each: Vatican City became an independent state under the Pope (1); a Concordat made Catholicism the state religion, with religious teaching in state schools and church marriage given legal force (1); the Vatican received financial compensation for the territory lost in 1870 (1). **[3]**

**8.** Two reasons, 2 marks each: League sanctions over Abyssinia (1935-36), backed by Britain and France, ended the Stresa Front, while Germany did not apply them (2); joint intervention in the Spanish Civil War from 1936 created co-operation with Germany and hostility with France (2); Mussolini believed Germany was the rising power that could help Italy win Mediterranean dominance, leading to the Axis (1936) and Anti-Comintern Pact (1937) (2). **[4]**

**9.** Indicative content. **For propaganda:** the cult of the Duce and *romanità*; LUCE newsreels, radio and a controlled press; rallies and sporting triumphs (the World Cups of 1934 and 1938); the Ministry of Popular Culture (1937). Consent seemed strong in the early and mid-1930s, especially at the conquest of Abyssinia. **For terror:** OVRA, the Special Tribunal and *confino* removed active opponents; the squads had already destroyed the socialist movement before 1926. **Other factors:** compromise with the elites -- the king, army, Church after 1929, and industrialists through the Corporate State -- may matter more than either; popular leisure through the OND bought acceptance. **Criterion:** "control" means the absence of a serious challenge. Strong answers argue that terror was targeted and limited in scale, propaganda reached townspeople more than the rural south, and the regime's durability rested on elite consent, shown by the elites' removal of Mussolini in July 1943. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**

**10.** Indicative content. **Aims:** national prestige, Mediterranean dominance, African empire, revision of the 1919 settlement. **Successes:** Fiume annexed by treaty (1924); Albania drawn into a protectorate (1926) and annexed (1939); respectability at Locarno (1925) and Stresa (1935); the conquest of Abyssinia (1935-36); Mussolini's role as broker at Munich (1938). **Failures:** the Corfu climb-down (1923) after League and British pressure; Abyssinia cost lives and money, brought sanctions and pushed Italy into dependence on Germany; Austria lost to the Anschluss (1938), once a key Italian interest; intervention in Spain drained resources and brought defeat at Guadalajara (1937); the Greek campaign (1940) and defeats in North Africa (1940-41) forced German rescue. **Judgement:** prestige was won in the short term, especially 1935-36, but the means chosen made Italy a junior partner of Germany and exposed military weakness, so by 1941 the core aim of an independent great-power Italy had failed. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**

**11.** Indicative content. **Impact:** falling exports and prices, rising unemployment and bank failures after 1929. **Responses:** the state rescued banks and industries through IMI (1931) and IRI (1933), making the state a major owner of shipbuilding, steel and engineering; public works and land reclamation provided jobs; the corporations were completed in 1934; wage cuts and short-time working spread the burden to workers. **Continuities:** Quota 90 and the Battle for Grain predated 1929; autarky was driven more by the 1935 sanctions and war plans than by the Depression. **Judgement:** the Depression transformed the state's role in industry -- a lasting change -- but did not change the regime's priorities of prestige and war. Strong answers distinguish short-term crisis measures from long-term structural change. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**

**12.** Indicative content. **For the view:** the birth rate kept falling despite the Battle for Births; economic need kept many women in agriculture, textiles and domestic work; Catholic values on family already shaped lives before fascism, so policy reinforced rather than created them; many rural women had little contact with fascist organisations. **Against the view:** marriage loans, bachelor tax and family allowances; harsher penalties for abortion and a ban on contraception information; employment quotas in public offices in the 1930s; ONMI maternity and child welfare clinics; women's fascist groups and youth organisations drew many girls and women into public life. **Criterion:** distinguish policy intention, legal change and lived experience, and region and class. **Judgement:** policies changed the legal and welfare framework but had limited effect on behaviour, so the view is largely sustainable for everyday lives, though less so for urban middle-class women affected by employment restrictions. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**
