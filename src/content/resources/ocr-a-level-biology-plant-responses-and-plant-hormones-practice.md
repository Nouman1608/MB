## Questions

**1.** Name the type of response shown by each of the following. **(a)** A pea tendril coils round a cane. **(b)** The leaflets of *Mimosa* fold when touched. **(c)** A root grows towards a patch of moist soil. **[3]**

**2.** A seedling is laid on its side in the dark. After two days the shoot curves upwards and the root curves downwards. Explain these observations. **[4]**

**3.** A student investigates phototropism in cress seedlings. Five seedlings were lit from one side for 24 hours. Five others were rotated slowly on a clinostat in the same light. The angles of curvature were: one-sided light 62°, 70°, 58°, 66°, 64°; clinostat 3°, 5°, 2°, 6°, 4°.

**(a)** Calculate the mean angle for the seedlings lit from one side. **[1]**
**(b)** State two variables the student should control. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the purpose of the clinostat treatment. **[2]**

**4.** A plant is deprived of water. Its leaf stomatal conductance falls from 220 to 70 mmol m⁻² s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease in stomatal conductance. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how abscisic acid (ABA) causes stomata to close. **[3]**

**5.** Explain how plant hormones bring about leaf loss in a deciduous tree in autumn, and suggest one advantage of losing leaves. **[5]**

**6.** Barley seeds (50 per treatment) were soaked in different solutions and the number that germinated was recorded. Water: 18. Gibberellin (GA): 47. ABA: 3. GA and ABA together: 21.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage germination with water and with GA. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe what the results show about the effect of ABA. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how gibberellin promotes germination in a barley seed. **[3]**

**7.** An investigator removed the apical bud from pea plants and put agar on the cut stump. Mean lateral shoot growth after 6 days is shown.

| Treatment | Lateral growth / mm |
|---|---|
| Apex intact | 2 |
| Apex removed, plain agar | 22 |
| Apex removed, agar with 0.05% auxin | 15 |
| Apex removed, agar with 0.5% auxin | 7 |
| Apex removed, agar with 5% auxin | 2 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage inhibition caused by 0.5% auxin compared with plain agar. **[2]**
**(b)** What do the data show about the effect of auxin concentration? **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the treatment with plain agar was included. **[2]**

**8.** Three types of pea plant were grown for 12 days with and without gibberellin (GA). Mean stem growth in mm is shown.

| Plant type | No GA | With GA |
|---|---|---|
| Tall | 44 | 47 |
| Dwarf A | 10 | 43 |
| Dwarf B | 9 | 10 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in growth of dwarf A when GA was applied. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain what the results suggest about the cause of dwarfism in A and in B. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest why GA had little effect on the tall plants. **[2]**

**9.** A student investigates the effect of gibberellin (GA) on hypocotyl length in cress seedlings. The stock solution is 1 mg dm⁻³.

**(a)** Describe how to make solutions of 0.1, 0.01 and 0.001 mg dm⁻³ by serial dilution. **[3]**
**(b)** Hypocotyl lengths in mm after 4 days for 8 seedlings each were: control 14, 12, 15, 13, 16, 13, 14, 15; 0.01 mg dm⁻³ GA 18, 20, 17, 21, 19, 18, 22, 19. State a null hypothesis for a t-test and the degrees of freedom. **[2]**
**(c)** The calculated value of t is 7.00 and the critical value at p = 0.05 is 2.14. State the conclusion. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest why the student should plot the concentrations on a logarithmic axis. **[2]**

**10.** Explain how alkaloids and pheromones help plants survive attack by herbivores. **[5]**

**11.** Plant hormones have commercial uses.

**(a)** Explain why a synthetic auxin can be used as a selective weedkiller on a cereal crop. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain the advantage to a grower of dipping cuttings in rooting powder. **[2]**
**(c)** Bananas are picked green and transported cool, then exposed to ethene. Explain why, and why the fruit ripen together once started. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Thigmotropism, a response to touch or contact [1]. **(b)** A nastic response (not directional), since it does not depend on the direction of touch [1]. **(c)** Hydrotropism, a positive response to water [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* For a nastic response, add that the direction of the stimulus does not decide the direction of movement.

**2.** Auxin (IAA) moves to the lower side of both organs [1]. In the shoot, the higher concentration stimulates cell elongation on the lower side, so the shoot curves up [1]. In the root, the same higher concentration inhibits elongation on the lower side [1]. The upper side grows faster, so the root curves down [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Saying auxin simply "makes the root grow down" loses credit; the contrast between stimulation and inhibition is the point.

**3. (a)** (62 + 70 + 58 + 66 + 64) ÷ 5 = 320 ÷ 5 = **64°** [1]. **(b)** Any two from: species, seedling age, light intensity, distance from the light, temperature, duration, moisture [2]. **(c)** Rotation exposes all sides equally to light, so no side is more strongly lit [1]. This shows that the curvature is caused by the one-sided light and not by another factor such as handling or the container [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "Same conditions" is too vague; name each variable and say how it is held constant.

**4. (a)** (220 − 70) ÷ 220 [1] × 100 = **68.2%** [1]. **(b)** Water stress raises ABA, which binds to receptors on the guard cells [1]. Calcium ions enter and channels open so potassium ions leave the guard cells [1]. Water potential of the guard cells rises, water leaves by osmosis, turgor falls and the stomata close [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide the change by the starting value. The osmosis step is the one most often left out.

**5.** Shorter days and cooler weather reduce the auxin produced by the leaf [1]. Ethene stimulates the abscission zone at the base of the leaf stalk [1]. Cells in the separation layer make enzymes that digest cell walls [1]. A protective layer forms on the stem side of the scar, preventing infection [1]. Advantage: less water is lost in winter when water is hard to absorb [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Name both hormones and say which is rising and which is falling; "hormones cause the leaf to fall" is too general.

**6. (a)** Water: 18 ÷ 50 × 100 = **36%** [1]. GA: 47 ÷ 50 × 100 = **94%** [1]. **(b)** ABA reduced germination to 6% compared with 36% in water [1]. With GA present, ABA lowered germination from 94% to 42%, so ABA opposes the effect of GA [1]. **(c)** The embryo makes gibberellin, which diffuses to the aleurone layer [1] and stimulates transcription of the amylase gene [1]. Amylase hydrolyses starch to maltose and then glucose, which the embryo respires to release energy for growth [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote figures from the data when describing results; "it went down" is weaker than 94% to 42%. In (c), name the enzyme and its substrate.

**7. (a)** (22 − 7) ÷ 22 × 100 [1] = **68.2%** [1]. **(b)** Greater auxin concentration gave greater inhibition of lateral growth (22 to 15, 7 and 2 mm) [1]. At 5% the lateral growth was the same as with the apex intact (2 mm), so enough auxin replaces the apex [1]. **(c)** Plain agar shows the effect of removing the apex without hormone [1]. It also shows that agar itself does not inhibit lateral growth, so differences are due to auxin [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Compare each auxin treatment with plain agar, not the intact plant, when asked for inhibition by the hormone.

**8. (a)** (43 − 10) ÷ 10 [1] × 100 = **330%** [1]. **(b)** A grew much taller with GA (10 to 43 mm), so it is short because it makes too little gibberellin [1]. B hardly responded (9 to 10 mm) even with GA applied [1], so its dwarfism is not due to lack of GA but to being unable to respond, for example a fault in receptors or in the response pathway [1]. **(c)** Tall plants already make enough GA [1], so the response is near its maximum and extra GA has little further effect (a 3 mm rise) [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Distinguish "not enough hormone" from "cannot respond"; only the response to added hormone separates them.

**9. (a)** Put 1 cm³ of the 1 mg dm⁻³ stock into 9 cm³ of distilled water and mix to give 0.1 mg dm⁻³ [1]. Take 1 cm³ of that into a fresh 9 cm³ of water to give 0.01 mg dm⁻³, then repeat to give 0.001 mg dm⁻³ [1]. Use a clean pipette each time and discard 1 cm³ from the last tube so every tube holds 9 cm³ [1]. **(b)** Null hypothesis: there is no significant difference in mean hypocotyl length between the control and 0.01 mg dm⁻³ GA [1]. Degrees of freedom = 8 + 8 − 2 = 14 [1]. **(c)** 7.00 is greater than 2.14, so reject the null hypothesis: GA significantly increased hypocotyl length [1]. **(d)** The concentrations differ by factors of ten, so on a linear axis most points crowd near zero [1]. A log axis spaces them evenly [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the null hypothesis as "no difference", and compare t with the critical value in the conclusion.

**10.** Alkaloids such as caffeine or nicotine are bitter or toxic chemicals [1]. They deter or poison herbivores, so less tissue is eaten [1]. Pheromones are airborne signals released by a damaged plant [1]. Neighbouring plants detect them and raise their own defences before being attacked [1]. Less damage means more plants survive and reproduce [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Give a named alkaloid and say who detects the pheromone; "it warns others" is too thin.

**11. (a)** Synthetic auxins are not broken down by the plant like natural IAA [1]. In broad-leaved weeds they persist and cause fast, uncontrolled growth that kills the plant [1]. Cereals (narrow-leaved) are much less affected, so the crop survives [1]. **(b)** Auxin in the powder stimulates roots to form from the cut stem [1], so many genetically identical plants are produced quickly and reliably [1]. **(c)** Unripe fruit bruise less in transport, and ethene at the destination times ripening for sale [1]. Ethene stimulates starch breakdown to sugar and softening of cell walls [1]. Ripening fruit release more ethene, a positive feedback, so nearby fruit ripen together [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* "Kills weeds" does not explain selectivity; say why the crop differs.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating auxin as always stimulating; higher concentrations inhibit roots.
- Describing results without numbers.
- Calculating percentage change from the wrong baseline.
- Leaving out the control, or naming the wrong control.
- Not naming the enzyme (amylase) and the substrate (starch) in germination.
- Saying guard cells "shrink" without water potential, osmosis and turgor.
- Giving vague controlled variables such as "same conditions".
- Omitting degrees of freedom or the null hypothesis.
- Calling a weedkiller a poison rather than a synthetic auxin.
