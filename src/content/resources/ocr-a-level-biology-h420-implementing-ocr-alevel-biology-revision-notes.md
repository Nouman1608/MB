## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Resolution | The smallest change in a quantity that an instrument can show |
| Meniscus | The curved surface of a liquid in a tube |
| Tare (zero) | Setting a balance to read zero with the empty container on it |
| Blank | A sample without the substance being measured, used to set a colorimeter to zero |
| Qualitative | Recorded as a description, such as a colour |
| Quantitative | Recorded as a number with a unit |
| Rate | Change in a quantity divided by the time taken |
| Raw data | Measurements as they were first recorded |
| Calibration | Checking or setting an instrument against a known standard |

## Correct use of apparatus: 1.1.2(a)

| Apparatus | Key point |
|---|---|
| Measuring cylinder, pipette, burette | Eye level with the bottom of the meniscus |
| Syringe | Remove air bubbles; read at the right mark |
| Balance | Tare with the container; blot tissue dry first |
| Thermometer | Bulb in the liquid, wait for a steady reading |
| Water bath | Allow time for tubes to reach the water temperature |
| Stopwatch | Define the start and the end point clearly |
| pH meter | Calibrate with buffers; rinse the probe between samples |
| Colorimeter | Zero with a blank; same cuvette; correct filter |
| Gas syringe | Check for leaks and a free plunger |

**Safe use:** identify the hazard, assess the risk, control it. Examples: eye protection for acids, alkalis and hydrogen peroxide; a cutting tile for blades; careful handling of hot water; washing hands after handling biological material.

## Units: 1.1.2(b)

| Quantity | Unit |
|---|---|
| Length of a cell | μm or mm |
| Volume | cm³ or dm³ |
| Mass | g or mg |
| Time | s or min |
| Concentration | mol dm⁻³ |
| Rate of gas production | cm³ min⁻¹ |
| Temperature | °C |

**Conversion factors**

| From | To | Operation |
|---|---|---|
| mm | μm | × 1000 |
| μm | mm | ÷ 1000 |
| cm³ | mm³ | × 1000 |
| dm³ | cm³ | × 1000 |
| g | mg | × 1000 |
| min | s | × 60 |

**Check:** a smaller unit gives a bigger number.

**Standard form:** 0.0050 mol dm⁻³ = 5.0 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³. Keep the same number of significant figures.

**Rates:**

- Rate = change in quantity ÷ time.
- 28 cm³ in 4.0 min is 7.0 cm³ min⁻¹.
- A rate based on time uses 1 ÷ time. A reaction taking 20 s is faster than one taking 40 s.
- Potometer: bubble moves 36 mm in 3.0 min in a capillary of radius 0.5 mm. Volume = π × 0.5² × 36 = 28.3 mm³, so the rate is 9.4 mm³ min⁻¹ (0.0094 cm³ min⁻¹).

## Recording data: 1.1.2(c)

**Observations**

- Be exact: "brick-red precipitate", not "it changed".
- Record colour at the start and at the end.
- Note the time of any change.

**Tables**

1. Ruled lines.
2. Independent variable in the first column.
3. Quantity and unit in the column headings, not in the cells.
4. Same number of decimal places down a column, matching the instrument.
5. Columns for repeats and for the mean.

| Temperature / °C | Volume of oxygen / cm³ | | | Mean / cm³ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | Repeat 1 | Repeat 2 | Repeat 3 | |
| 20 | 8.0 | 8.5 | 9.0 | 8.5 |
| 30 | 14.5 | 15.0 | 14.5 | 14.7 |

**Decimal places**

- Balance to 0.01 g: write 2.40 g, not 2.4 g.
- Measuring cylinder in 1 cm³ divisions: estimate to 0.5 cm³.
- Means: no more significant figures than the least precise raw data support.

## Quick chains

- 0.25 mm → 250 μm.
- 3.5 cm³ → 3500 mm³.
- 5 mg → 0.005 g.
- 45 s → 0.75 min.
- 2.4 dm³ → 2400 cm³.
- 12 mL = 12 cm³.

## Exam traps

- A number with no unit.
- A unit written in every cell instead of the column heading.
- Reading the top of the meniscus.
- Forgetting to tare a balance or to blot tissue.
- Converting the wrong way round.
- Using time as a rate.
- Writing 2.4 g for a reading of 2.40 g.
- Describing a colour change as "it went darker" without saying what colour.

## Self-test

1. Where should your eye be when reading a liquid in a measuring cylinder?
2. Convert 0.18 mm to μm.
3. Convert 2.5 dm³ to cm³.
4. Convert 90 s to minutes.
5. A balance reads to 0.01 g. A potato cylinder has a mass of 3.10 g. How should this mass be written?
6. 15 cm³ of gas is collected in 2.5 minutes. Calculate the rate and give the unit.
7. Which should be in the first column of a results table: the independent or the dependent variable?
8. Why is a colorimeter set to zero with a blank?
9. Write 0.0040 mol dm⁻³ in standard form.
10. A reaction takes 25 s. State the rate in s⁻¹.

### Answers

1. Level with the bottom of the meniscus.
2. 0.18 × 1000 = 180 μm.
3. 2.5 × 1000 = 2500 cm³.
4. 90 ÷ 60 = 1.5 min.
5. 3.10 g (two decimal places).
6. 15 ÷ 2.5 = 6.0 cm³ min⁻¹.
7. The independent variable.
8. So that absorbance from the solvent or cuvette is not included in the reading.
9. 4.0 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³.
10. 1 ÷ 25 = 0.040 s⁻¹.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to write units in the table cells?**
No. Put the quantity and the unit in the column heading, for example "Mass / g", and write numbers only in the cells.

**How many decimal places should I use for raw data?**
The number the instrument supports. A balance reading to 0.01 g gives two decimal places. A mean can be given to one more decimal place than the raw data.

**What counts as a good observation?**
A specific description of what you see that another person could check, such as "the solution changed from blue to brick-red after 90 s".
