OxfordAQA International A-Level Economics (9640) is modular, with four papers that are **not equally
weighted**. AS Paper 1 (The Operation of Markets, Market Failure and the Role of Government) and AS
Paper 2 (The National Economy in a Global Environment) are each 1 hour 45 minutes, 80 marks and 20% of
the full A-level. A-level Paper 1 (The Economics of Business Behaviour and the Distribution of Income)
and A-level Paper 2 (Economic Development and the Global Economy) are longer -- 2 hours, 90 marks --
and each carries **30% of the full A-level**. AS content contributes 40% of the final qualification
overall and A2 content the remaining 60%. These notes complement the site's guides to
[The Operation of Markets, Market Failure and the Role of Government](/resources/a-level-oxfordaqa-economics-operation-of-markets/)
and
[The Measurement of Macroeconomic Performance](/resources/a-level-oxfordaqa-economics-measurement-of-macroeconomic-performance/).

## The A2 papers are worth half again as much as the AS papers

This is the single most important structural fact about the qualification: each A2 paper (30%) is
worth 1.5 times an AS paper (20%). **Exam-preparation priority**: weight revision time towards the A2
content accordingly, not evenly across all four papers. A candidate who revises all four papers to the
same depth has under-invested in 60% of the final grade relative to 40%.

## A2 papers are also longer and denser -- pacing has to adjust

At 2 hours for 90 marks against the AS papers' 1 hour 45 for 80, the A2 papers demand more sustained
concentration as well as more content. **Exam-preparation priority**: practise complete A2-length
papers specifically, since stamina across the longer sitting is trainable and is not built by AS-length
practice alone.

## Business behaviour and development economics are largely new territory at A2

A-level Paper 1's business behaviour content and A-level Paper 2's development economics content build
on AS foundations but introduce substantially new material -- market structures, growth and development
theory, international trade in more depth. **Exam-preparation priority**: do not assume AS-level
familiarity with markets or macroeconomics is sufficient preparation for A2; the new content needs its
own dedicated revision time, even where it uses familiar diagrams and vocabulary.

## Diagrams remain a fast route to marks, and get more demanding at A2

Cost curves, market structure diagrams, and development indicators appear more frequently and in more
combined forms at A2 than at AS. **Exam-preparation priority**: practise drawing the A2-specific
diagrams -- not just the AS staples -- to speed and accuracy, and practise combining more than one
diagram within a single answer where the question calls for it.

## Essays need a stated criterion and a chain of reasoning, at both AS and A2

Extended questions across all four papers reward linked reasoning that reaches a supported judgement.
**Exam-preparation priority**: practise the same disciplined structure regardless of paper -- define
key terms, build the chain step by step, take the counter-argument seriously, and state what the
conclusion depends on.

## Worked routine: planning an essay under time pressure

The routine below is an original model written for this resource, not a reproduction of any official
past paper or mark scheme.

```
Step 1 - define the key term precisely:
One sentence. Disciplines everything that follows.

Step 2 - state the position and the criterion for judging it:
"On balance yes, judged by long-run growth rather than short-run
stability."

Step 3 - build the main chain of reasoning:
Cause -> mechanism -> effect, each link stated. Insert a diagram
where it does real analytical work.

Step 4 - give the strongest counter-consideration:
Taken seriously, not dismissed in a sentence.

Step 5 - state what the answer depends on, then conclude:
Elasticity, time horizon, magnitude, or an assumption held
constant -- then return to the criterion from step 2.
```

Step 2 -- setting a criterion before arguing -- is what makes step 5's conclusion possible; without it,
a closing paragraph has nothing to be measured against.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exams**: weight revision towards the two A2 papers, which together are 60% of the
  grade against the AS papers' 40%; practise full-length A2 papers specifically; give the new A2
  content (business behaviour, development economics) dedicated revision time rather than assuming AS
  familiarity covers it; drill the A2-specific diagrams.
- **During any paper**: match pacing to the paper's own length and mark total rather than a single
  fixed rule across all four.
- **In every essay**: define terms, state a judging criterion up front, build one clear causal chain,
  take the counter-argument seriously, state what the conclusion depends on, and close against the
  stated criterion.

## Self-test

1. How are the four papers weighted, and what does that imply for revision time?
2. How do the A2 papers differ from the AS papers beyond their weighting?
3. Why is AS-level familiarity with markets insufficient preparation for A2 content?
4. What must be set before an essay's conclusion can be reached?

**Answers:** 1. AS Papers 1 and 2 are 20% each; A-level Papers 1 and 2 are 30% each -- so revision time
should weight towards the two A2 papers, which together carry 60% of the grade. 2. They are longer (2
hours vs 1 hour 45) and carry more marks (90 vs 80), demanding more sustained concentration as well as
more content. 3. Because A2 papers introduce substantially new material -- market structures, growth
and development theory in depth -- that builds on but goes well beyond AS foundations. 4. A stated
criterion for judgement, set out before the argument is built, so the closing paragraph has something
concrete to measure the conclusion against.
