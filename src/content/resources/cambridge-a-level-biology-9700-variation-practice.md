These questions cover section 17.1 (Variation) of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. This is A Level content, examined in Paper 4; Questions 8 to 10 and 12 also practise Paper 5 skills. All data are invented for the exercise. The t-test formula is t = (x̄₁ − x̄₂) ÷ √(s₁²/n₁ + s₂²/n₂). Revise first with the [17.1 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-variation/) and the [17.1 revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-variation-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which is an example of discontinuous variation in humans?

A: Body mass

B: ABO blood group

C: Height

D: Hand span **[1]**

**2.** Identical twins have the same genotype but can differ in body mass. Explain why. **[2]**

**3.** A t-test compares a sample of 9 measurements with a sample of 11 measurements. How many degrees of freedom are there?

A: 18

B: 19

C: 20

D: 99 **[1]**

**4.** State two differences between continuous and discontinuous variation. **[2]**

**5.** State the null hypothesis for a t-test comparing the mean leaf mass of plants from two sites. **[1]**

**6.** Suggest why a histogram of the heights of adult humans is bell-shaped. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** In a plant, height is controlled by two unlinked genes, A and B. Each capital allele adds 4 cm to a base height of 10 cm; lower-case alleles add nothing. Two plants with genotype AaBb are crossed.

**(a)** State the number of different height classes expected among the offspring. **[1]**

**(b)** List the heights of these classes. **[1]**

**(c)** Give the expected ratio of the classes, from shortest to tallest. **[2]**

**(d)** Suggest why real measurements of this plant's height show continuous variation rather than separate classes. **[1]**

**8.** The shell lengths of snails were measured at two sites.

| Site | Shell length / cm | Mean / cm | Standard deviation (s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| X | 4.1, 3.8, 4.5, 4.0, 4.3, 3.9 | 4.10 | 0.261 |
| Y | 4.4, 4.6, 4.2, 4.8, 4.5, 4.3 | 4.47 | 0.216 |

**(a)** Calculate the value of t. Show your working. **[3]**

**(b)** The critical value of t at p = 0.05 for the correct degrees of freedom is 2.23. State the degrees of freedom and what the result shows. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why a t-test, rather than a chi-squared test, is used for these data. **[1]**

**9.** Two populations of a plant species grow at different altitudes. Plants at high altitude are much shorter. Plan an investigation to find out whether this difference is caused by genetic or environmental factors. **[4]**

**10.** Plants from one clone, and plants of three different varieties, were grown with different nitrogen supplies. Mean height after 8 weeks was recorded.

| Plants | Low nitrogen / cm | High nitrogen / cm |
|---|---|---|
| Clone | 22 | 41 |
| Variety P | 18 | 30 |
| Variety Q | 25 | 47 |
| Variety R | 31 | 58 |

**(a)** Use the data to explain that height is affected by environmental factors. **[1]**

**(b)** Use the data to explain that height is also affected by genetic factors. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest why the effect of nitrogen is greater in variety R than in variety P. **[1]**

**11.** Explain, with examples, the genetic basis of discontinuous and of continuous variation. **[5]**

**12.** A student compared the mean mass of seeds from two varieties of bean, with 8 seeds of each, and calculated t = 1.45. The critical value at p = 0.05 is 2.14.

**(a)** State and explain the conclusion. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest one change that would make the comparison more reliable. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]

**2.** Body mass is affected by environmental factors as well as genes [1]. The twins may differ in diet, exercise or illness, so their phenotypes differ even with the same alleles [1].

**3.** A [1]

**4.** Any two: continuous variation shows a range of values, discontinuous shows distinct categories [1]; continuous is controlled by many genes, discontinuous by one or a few [1]; continuous is strongly affected by environment, discontinuous little affected (accept).

**5.** There is no significant difference between the mean leaf masses of plants from the two sites [1].

**6.** Height is controlled by many genes with small additive effects, so most people have a middle number of contributing alleles and few have very many or very few [1]. Environmental effects add further variation, smoothing the distribution into a bell shape [1].

**7.**

**(a)** 5 [1]

**(b)** 10, 14, 18, 22 and 26 cm [1]

**(c)** 1 : 4 : 6 : 4 : 1 [2]

**(d)** Environmental factors such as light, water and nutrients cause variation within each genotype, so the classes overlap and merge [1].

**8.**

**(a)** s₁²/n₁ = 0.261² ÷ 6 = 0.01135; s₂²/n₂ = 0.216² ÷ 6 = 0.00778 [1]. √(0.01135 + 0.00778) = √0.01913 = 0.1383 [1]. t = (4.47 − 4.10) ÷ 0.1383 = **2.7** (accept 2.6 to 2.7) [1].

**(b)** 6 + 6 − 2 = 10 degrees of freedom; t is greater than 2.23 [1]. The difference between the means is significant (p < 0.05): reject the null hypothesis; snails at site Y have significantly longer shells [1].

**(c)** The data are continuous measurements and the test compares two means; chi-squared compares observed and expected counts in categories [1].

**9.** Collect seeds (or cuttings) from plants of both populations [1]. Grow them together in the same conditions, at one altitude or in a controlled environment, with the same soil, water, light and temperature [1]. Measure the height of many plants of each population after the same time and compare the means with a t-test [1]. If the difference remains when the plants are grown together, it is genetic; if it disappears, it was caused by the environment [1].

**10.**

**(a)** The clone plants are genetically identical but are 22 cm tall with low nitrogen and 41 cm with high nitrogen, so the difference must be environmental [1].

**(b)** In the same conditions, the varieties differ in height (for example 18, 25 and 31 cm at low nitrogen), so their different genotypes affect height [1].

**(c)** Variety R has alleles that give a greater potential height, so it can respond more to the extra nitrogen (genes and environment interact) [1].

**11.** Discontinuous variation is controlled by one or a few genes [1]. Their alleles have large effects, so different genotypes give distinct phenotypes with no intermediates, for example ABO blood groups produced by alleles Iᴬ, Iᴮ and Iᴼ [1]. Continuous variation is controlled by many genes (polygenes) at different loci [1]. Each contributing allele has a small additive effect, so many genotype classes are possible [1]. The environment also affects the phenotype, so the classes merge into a range of values, for example human height [1].

**12.**

**(a)** 1.45 is less than 2.14, so the difference between the means is not significant (p > 0.05) [1]. Accept the null hypothesis: the difference could be due to chance, so there is no evidence that the varieties differ in mean seed mass [1].

**(b)** Use larger samples of seeds (for example 30 of each), taken at random [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving height as a purely genetic example.
- Saying continuous variation is caused by "many alleles" instead of many genes.
- Using s instead of s² in the t-test formula.
- Using n₁ + n₂ − 1 as degrees of freedom.
- Writing that a significant result "proves" a cause.
- Missing "in context" in t-test conclusions.
