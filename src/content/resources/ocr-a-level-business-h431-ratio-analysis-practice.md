## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** A business has current assets of £330,000, inventory of £180,000 and current liabilities of £150,000. Calculate the current ratio and the acid test ratio. **[3]**

**2.** Explain what is meant by gearing. **[2]**

**3.** Operating profit is £180,000 and interest paid is £30,000. Calculate interest cover and explain what the result shows. **[3]**

**4.** Which **one** of the following would increase a company's debtor days?

A: Customers paying their invoices faster.

B: Offering customers a longer credit period.

C: Cutting prices to increase sales for cash.

D: Paying suppliers more slowly. **[1]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why price/earnings ratios are used by investors. **[2]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**6.** Kenning Retail plc has 400,000 shares in issue, and a share price of £4.50.

| Income statement | £ | Statement of financial position | £ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 1,800,000 | Non-current assets | 900,000 |
| Cost of sales | 1,170,000 | Inventory | 180,000 |
| Operating expenses | 450,000 | Receivables | 90,000 |
| Interest | 30,000 | Cash | 60,000 |
| Tax (20% of profit before tax) | 30,000 | Payables | 150,000 |
| Dividends | 60,000 | Non-current liabilities | 300,000 |

Net assets are £780,000 and capital employed is £1,080,000.

**(a)** Calculate the gross profit margin and the net profit margin. **[4]**

**(b)** Calculate ROCE and return on equity. **[3]**

**(c)** Calculate debtor days, creditor days and stock turnover. **[4]**

**(d)** Calculate earnings per share, dividend per share, dividend yield and the price/earnings ratio. **[4]**

**7.** The previous year's ratios for Kenning were: current ratio 2.6; acid test 1.4; gearing 33%; ROCE 14.2%; earnings per share 26.0p; creditor days 52. This year's current ratio is 2.2, the acid test is 1.0, gearing is 27.8%, ROCE is 16.7%, EPS is 30.0p and creditor days are 46.8.

Evaluate Kenning Retail's financial position, using the ratios. **[9]**

**8.** Explain **two** reasons why a high gearing ratio might concern a lender. **[4]**

**9.** A retail company's debtor days are 18 and its creditor days 47. Explain how this helps its cash position and evaluate the risks. **[6]**

**10.** Evaluate the usefulness of ratio analysis to a potential investor in a company. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Current ratio = £330,000 ÷ £150,000 = **2.2** [1] [1]. Acid test = (£330,000 − £180,000) ÷ £150,000 = **1.0** [1]. **[3]**

**2.** Gearing is the proportion of a business's capital that is financed by long-term borrowing [1], measured as non-current liabilities divided by capital employed, × 100 [1]. **[2]**

**3.** £180,000 ÷ £30,000 [1] = **6 times** [1]. The business earns six times as much operating profit as it needs to pay its interest, so interest is easily covered [1]. **[3]**

**4.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**5.** The ratio shows how many pounds investors pay for each £1 of earnings [1], so it lets them compare the value of similar companies, or see whether the market expects growth [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Gross profit = £1,800,000 − £1,170,000 = £630,000; margin = 630,000 ÷ 1,800,000 × 100 = **35.0%** [1] [1]. Operating profit = £630,000 − £450,000 = £180,000; profit before tax = £150,000; tax £30,000; profit for the year £120,000. Net margin = 120,000 ÷ 1,800,000 × 100 = **6.7%** [1] [1]. **[4]**

**(b)** ROCE = 180,000 ÷ 1,080,000 × 100 = **16.7%** [1] [1]. Return on equity = 120,000 ÷ 780,000 × 100 = **15.4%** [1]. **[3]**

**(c)** Debtor days = 90,000 ÷ 1,800,000 × 365 = **18.25 days** [1] [1]. Creditor days = 150,000 ÷ 1,170,000 × 365 = **46.8 days** [1]. Stock turnover = 1,170,000 ÷ 180,000 = **6.5 times** [1]. **[4]**

**(d)** EPS = 120,000 ÷ 400,000 = **30.0p** [1]. DPS = 60,000 ÷ 400,000 = **15.0p** [1]. Yield = 15 ÷ 450 × 100 = **3.3%** [1]. P/E = 450 ÷ 30.0 = **15.0** [1]. **[4]**

**7.** Profitability: ROCE rose from 14.2% to 16.7% and EPS from 26.0p to 30.0p (+15%), so returns to the business and shareholders are improving [1] [1].
Solvency: gearing fell from 33% to 27.8%, so the business is less dependent on debt and interest cover of 6.0 is comfortable [1] [1].
Liquidity: the current ratio fell from 2.6 to 2.2 and the acid test from 1.4 to 1.0 [1]. The acid test is now exactly 1.0, which is adequate but leaves little margin, and the trend is downwards [1].
Efficiency: creditor days fell from 52 to 46.8, so Kenning is paying suppliers faster, which uses cash but protects relationships [1]. Debtor days are low at 18 days because it sells mainly for cash [1].
Judgement: financial position is improving in profit and risk, but liquidity should be watched, as it is weakening [1]. The causes (for example investing cash in non-current assets or paying suppliers earlier) should be investigated, and a comparison with competitors would help [1]. (Maximum 9 marks.) **[9]**

**8.** A highly geared company has large fixed interest payments [1], which must be paid whatever the profit, so a fall in sales could make it unable to repay [1]. The lender is also repaid after existing lenders if the business fails, as security may already be pledged [1], so extra lending is riskier [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Customers pay in about 18 days, while the company takes 47 days to pay its suppliers [1], so cash comes in about 29 days before it goes out [1]. This gives the business free credit and reduces the need for borrowing [1].
Risks: stretching creditor days too far may damage relationships with suppliers or lose discounts [1]; if sales fall, the advantage may disappear [1]; relying on supplier credit can be dangerous if suppliers tighten terms [1]. **[6]**

**10.** For: ratios turn accounts into comparable percentages, so the investor can compare the company with rivals [1]; they show trends over time [1]; profitability and shareholder ratios (ROCE, EPS, P/E, dividend yield) show the return [1]; gearing and interest cover show risk [1].
Limits: ratios use historic figures, which may not predict the future [1]; accounting policies differ between companies, so comparisons may mislead [1]; the year-end snapshot may be unusual [1]; ratios ignore non-financial factors such as management quality and the market [1].
Judgement: ratio analysis is a valuable starting point but investors should also consider the industry, future plans and cash flow before investing [1]. (Maximum 8 marks.) **[8]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using revenue instead of cost of sales for creditor days and stock turnover.
- Using profit for the year in ROCE instead of operating profit.
- Forgetting to multiply by 100 or by 365.
- Quoting a ratio without saying what it shows.
- Evaluating with no comparison or judgement.
