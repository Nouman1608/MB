## Section A

**1.** Which two gases are greenhouse gases?

A: carbon dioxide and methane

B: methane and oxygen

C: nitrogen and carbon dioxide

D: oxygen and nitrogen

**[1]**

**2.** Which process in a lake directly reduces the concentration of dissolved oxygen after an algal bloom?

A: photosynthesis by algae

B: respiration by decomposers

C: evaporation of water

D: absorption of nitrate ions

**[1]**

**3.** State what is meant by over-harvesting. **[1]**

**4.** State what is meant by a non-biodegradable material. **[1]**

**5.** State **two** causes of deforestation. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Large areas of tropical forest are cleared for cattle ranching.

**(a)** Explain how deforestation can lead to loss of soil. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain how deforestation can lead to flooding. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how deforestation increases the carbon dioxide concentration in the atmosphere. **[3]**

**(d)** Explain how deforestation can lead to the extinction of species. **[2]**

**7.** Nitrogen-containing fertiliser from a farm drains into a pond. Over the following weeks the water becomes green, and later many fish and insects die.

**(a)** Name the process that has occurred in the pond. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain, in order, how the fertiliser leads to the death of the fish. **[5]**

**(c)** Suggest **two** ways in which the farmer could reduce this problem. **[2]**

**8.** The table shows the mean concentration of carbon dioxide in the air at different times.

| Year | Carbon dioxide (parts per million) |
|---|---|
| 1980 | 339 |
| 2000 | 369 |
| 2020 | 414 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase from 1980 to 2020. **[2]**

**(b)** State **two** human activities that have caused the increase. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe **two** likely effects of global warming. **[2]**

**9.** The grey squirrel was introduced to a country where it did not occur before, and the numbers of native red squirrels there have fallen.

**(a)** Explain why the population of an introduced species may grow very fast. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest **two** ways in which the introduced species could reduce the population of a native species. **[2]**

**10.** An insecticide is sprayed on a field of cotton.

**(a)** State **one** benefit to the farmer. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain how the insecticide could harm species that are not pests. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest how the insecticide could reach organisms in a nearby river. **[1]**

**11.** Describe the harmful effects of non-biodegradable plastic in the sea. **[3]**

**12.** Fishermen take large numbers of cod from a fishing ground every year, so that the cod catch falls each year. Explain what is happening to the cod population and suggest a likely effect on the food web. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** A [1]. **[1]**

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Taking plants or animals from the wild faster than they can reproduce (or replace themselves) [1]. **[1]**

**4.** A material that cannot be broken down by decomposers [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Any two of: clearing land for agriculture; timber; housing; roads [1 each]. **[2]**

**6.**

**(a)** Tree roots hold the soil together [1]. Without trees, rain washes away the topsoil [1].

**(b)** Trees take up water and slow its flow over the ground [1]. Without them, rainwater runs quickly into rivers, which flood [1].

**(c)** There are fewer trees to take in carbon dioxide in photosynthesis [1]. Burning the trees releases carbon dioxide [1]. The decay of dead wood by decomposers releases carbon dioxide in respiration [1].

**(d)** The forest is the habitat of many species [1], so some species that live only there lose their food and shelter and may die out [1]. **[9]**

**7.**

**(a)** Eutrophication [1].

**(b)** The fertiliser increases the nitrate concentration in the water [1]. Algae grow rapidly [1]. The algae and plants die [1], and decomposers feed on the dead material, using more oxygen in aerobic respiration [1]. The dissolved oxygen concentration falls, so the fish cannot respire and die [1].

**(c)** Any two of: use less fertiliser; avoid spreading fertiliser before rain; leave a strip of land beside the water; treat sewage [1 each]. **[8]**

**8.**

**(a)** (414 - 339) divided by 339 x 100 [1] = 22.1 per cent (to one decimal place) [1].

**(b)** Any two of: burning fossil fuels; deforestation [1 each].

**(c)** Any two of: ice melts and sea level rises; changes in weather patterns; more droughts or floods; loss of habitats [1 each]. **[6]**

**9.**

**(a)** There may be no natural predators or diseases in the new area [1], and plenty of food, so more survive and breed [1].

**(b)** Any two of: it competes with the native species for food or space [1]; it may eat the native species [1]; it may bring disease [1]. **[4]**

**10.**

**(a)** It kills pests, so the cotton yield is higher [1].

**(b)** It may kill insects that are not pests, such as bees [1], so it reduces biodiversity [1].

**(c)** It can be washed from the field into the river by rain [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Plastic does not decay because it is non-biodegradable and stays in the sea for a long time [1]. Animals may become entangled in it [1], or swallow it, which can block their digestive systems and cause starvation [1]. **[3]**

**12.** The cod are being removed faster than they can reproduce (over-harvesting) [1], so the population falls [1]. Predators of cod have less food, and the prey of cod may increase [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying that algae use up the oxygen, not the decomposers.
- Leaving gaps in the eutrophication sequence.
- Writing that greenhouse gases cause a hole in the ozone layer.
- Not explaining how a cause leads to the effect.
- Dividing by the later value when calculating a percentage increase.
