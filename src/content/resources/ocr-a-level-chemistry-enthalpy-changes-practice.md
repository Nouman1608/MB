This set covers section 3.2.1, Enthalpy changes, of the OCR A Level Chemistry A (H432) specification, Version 3.1 (May 2026), for first assessment in 2017. The questions test every learning outcome, 3.2.1(a) to (h), from Module 3, Periodic table and energy. H432 is the full A level. Questions 11 and 12 are longer problems.

Revise first with the [Enthalpy changes study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-enthalpy-changes/) and the [Enthalpy changes revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-enthalpy-changes-revision-notes/). Other topics are on the [OCR chemistry hub](/boards/ocr/a-level/chemistry/) and the [printable checklist](/checklists/ocr/a-level/chemistry/).

Use c = 4.18 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹ and take 1.00 cm³ of solution as 1.00 g.

## Questions

**1.** This question is about enthalpy definitions.

**(a)** State the conditions meant by standard conditions. **[1]**
**(b)** Write the equation, with state symbols, for the standard enthalpy change of formation of ethanal, CH₃CHO(l). **[2]**
**(c)** Write the equation, with state symbols, for the standard enthalpy change of combustion of cyclohexane, C₆H₁₂(l). **[2]**

**2.** The reaction N₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2NO(g) is endothermic.

**(a)** Sketch a labelled enthalpy profile diagram for this reaction. Show ∆H and the activation energy. **[3]**
**(b)** Use ideas about the bonds broken and formed to explain why ∆H is positive. **[2]**

**3.** Magnesium ribbon burns exothermically in air, but only after it is held in a flame. Explain why. **[2]**

**4.** A student burns propanone (M = 58.0 g mol⁻¹) to heat 125 g of water. The spirit burner's mass falls by 0.290 g and the water warms by 10.4 K.

**(a)** Calculate the enthalpy change of combustion of propanone from these results. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[3]**
**(b)** The data value is −1790 kJ mol⁻¹. Suggest two reasons why the student's value is less exothermic. **[2]**

**5.** 25.0 cm³ of 0.800 mol dm⁻³ H₂SO₄(aq) is mixed with 50.0 cm³ of 0.800 mol dm⁻³ NaOH(aq). The temperature rises by 7.2 K. Calculate the enthalpy change of neutralisation. **[4]**

**6.** 5.95 g of potassium bromide, KBr (M = 119.0 g mol⁻¹), is dissolved in 50.0 cm³ of water in a polystyrene cup. The temperature falls by 4.8 K.

**(a)** Calculate the enthalpy change for dissolving 1 mol of KBr. Include a sign. **[3]**
**(b)** Suggest why this value is likely to be smaller than the true value. **[1]**

**7.** Methanal burns in oxygen: HCHO(g) + O₂(g) → CO₂(g) + H₂O(g).
Use these values (kJ mol⁻¹): C–H 413; C=O in methanal 736; O=O 498; C=O in CO₂ 805; O–H 464.

**(a)** Calculate ∆H for this reaction using the bond enthalpies. **[3]**
**(b)** The standard enthalpy change of combustion of methanal is −571 kJ mol⁻¹. Give two reasons for the difference. **[2]**

**8.** For Cl₂(g) + F₂(g) → 2ClF(g), ∆H = −101 kJ mol⁻¹. The Cl–Cl bond enthalpy is 243 kJ mol⁻¹ and the F–F bond enthalpy is 158 kJ mol⁻¹. Calculate the Cl–F bond enthalpy. **[3]**

**9.** Use these standard enthalpy changes of combustion (kJ mol⁻¹) to calculate the standard enthalpy change of formation of cyclohexane, C₆H₁₂(l): C(s) −393.5; H₂(g) −285.8; C₆H₁₂(l) −3919.6. **[3]**

**10.** Phosphorus(V) chloride reacts with water: PCl₅(s) + 4H₂O(l) → H₃PO₄(s) + 5HCl(g).
Use these standard enthalpy changes of formation (kJ mol⁻¹): PCl₅(s) −444; H₂O(l) −286; H₃PO₄(s) −1279; HCl(g) −92. Calculate ∆rH. **[3]**

**11.** A student finds ∆H for the thermal decomposition ZnCO₃(s) → ZnO(s) + CO₂(g) indirectly.

Experiment 1: 3.76 g of ZnCO₃ (M = 125.4 g mol⁻¹) reacts with 50.0 cm³ of 2.00 mol dm⁻³ HCl. The temperature rises by 2.9 K.
ZnCO₃(s) + 2HCl(aq) → ZnCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g) ∆H₁

Experiment 2: 2.44 g of ZnO (M = 81.4 g mol⁻¹) reacts with a fresh 50.0 cm³ of the same acid. The temperature rises by 12.8 K.
ZnO(s) + 2HCl(aq) → ZnCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) ∆H₂

**(a)** Suggest why ∆H for the decomposition cannot easily be measured directly. **[1]**
**(b)** Show that the acid is in excess in Experiment 1. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate ∆H₁. **[3]**
**(d)** Calculate ∆H₂. **[2]**
**(e)** Use a Hess cycle to calculate ∆H for the decomposition of ZnCO₃. **[2]**

**12.** 30.0 cm³ of 1.50 mol dm⁻³ HCl in a polystyrene cup stays at 19.6 °C for 3 minutes. At 4 minutes, 30.0 cm³ of 1.60 mol dm⁻³ NaOH at 19.6 °C is added and stirred. Later readings:

| Time / min | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature / °C | 29.4 | 29.0 | 28.6 | 28.2 |

**(a)** Extrapolate the cooling readings back to 4 minutes. State the temperature at 4 minutes and the temperature change. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the enthalpy change of neutralisation. **[3]**
**(c)** State how the answer to (b) would change if the student had used the highest recorded temperature instead. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest two changes to the method that would improve the accuracy of the result. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 100 kPa and a stated temperature, usually 298 K [1]
**(b)** 2C(s) + 2H₂(g) + ½O₂(g) → CH₃CHO(l): correct formulae and balancing for 1 mol of ethanal [1]; correct state symbols [1]
**(c)** C₆H₁₂(l) + 9O₂(g) → 6CO₂(g) + 6H₂O(l): balanced for 1 mol of cyclohexane [1]; state symbols, with water as a liquid [1]
*Examiner insight:* Keep the ½O₂ in (b); doubling the equation to clear the fraction makes 2 mol of ethanal.

**2. (a)** Product level (2NO) drawn above reactant level (N₂ + O₂), with axes labelled [1]; ∆H arrow pointing up from reactants to products [1]; Ea arrow drawn upwards from the reactant line to the peak [1]
**(b)** More energy is taken in breaking bonds than is released making new bonds [1]; breaking the very strong N≡N triple bond needs a large amount of energy [1]
*Examiner insight:* An Ea arrow drawn from the product level, or a ∆H line with no arrowhead, costs credit on a sketch.

**3.** The reaction has an activation energy, a minimum energy needed before it can take place [1]. The flame supplies this energy; once started, the energy released keeps the reaction going [1]
*Examiner insight:* Use the phrase "minimum energy"; it matches the specification's wording.

**4. (a)** q = 125 × 4.18 × 10.4 = 5434 J = 5.434 kJ [1]; n = 0.290 ÷ 58.0 = 0.00500 mol [1]; ∆cH = −5.434 ÷ 0.00500 = −1087, so **−1090 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** Heat lost to the air or absorbed by the can [1]; incomplete combustion, or propanone evaporating from the wick [1]
*Examiner insight:* m is the 125 g of water, not the 0.290 g of fuel.

**5.** n(H₂SO₄) = 0.0250 × 0.800 = 0.0200 mol, so n(H⁺) = 0.0400 mol [1]; n(OH⁻) = 0.0500 × 0.800 = 0.0400 mol, so 0.0400 mol of water forms [1]; q = 75.0 × 4.18 × 7.2 = 2257 J = 2.257 kJ [1]; ∆neutH = −2.257 ÷ 0.0400 = **−56.4 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Dividing by 0.0200 mol of acid gives about double the answer; the definition is per mole of water.

**6. (a)** q = 50.0 × 4.18 × 4.8 = 1003 J = 1.003 kJ [1]; n = 5.95 ÷ 119.0 = 0.0500 mol [1]; temperature falls, so ∆H = +1.003 ÷ 0.0500 = **+20.1 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** Heat flows in from the surroundings, so the measured fall is too small [1]
*Examiner insight:* Write the + sign; 20.1 alone does not show the direction of the change.

**7. (a)** Broken: 2(413) + 736 + 498 = 2060 kJ [1]; made: 2(805) + 2(464) = 2538 kJ [1]; ∆H = 2060 − 2538 = **−478 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** The standard value has H₂O(l), so it includes energy released on condensing [1]; C–H and O–H values are averages, not the actual bonds in these molecules [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Bond enthalpies are not accurate" is too vague; say that they are averages from many compounds.

**8.** Bonds broken: 243 + 158 = 401 kJ [1]; −101 = 401 − 2x [1]; x = 502 ÷ 2 = **251 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Two moles of Cl–F bonds form in the equation; forgetting to halve gives 502.

**9.** Equation: 6C(s) + 6H₂(g) → C₆H₁₂(l); ∆fH = Σ∆cH(reactants) − Σ∆cH(products) [1]; = 6(−393.5) + 6(−285.8) − (−3919.6) = −4075.8 + 3919.6 [1]; = **−156.2 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Multiply both the carbon and hydrogen values by six.

**10.** Σ∆fH(products) = −1279 + 5(−92) = −1739 kJ [1]; Σ∆fH(reactants) = −444 + 4(−286) = −1588 kJ [1]; ∆rH = −1739 − (−1588) = **−151 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Keep brackets around negative values when you subtract.

**11. (a)** It needs strong, continuous heating, so no temperature change can be measured in a calorimeter [1]
**(b)** n(HCl) = 0.0500 × 2.00 = 0.100 mol [1]; n(ZnCO₃) = 3.76 ÷ 125.4 = 0.0300 mol, which needs 0.0600 mol of HCl, less than 0.100 mol [1]
**(c)** q = 50.0 × 4.18 × 2.9 = 606 J = 0.606 kJ [1]; n = 0.0300 mol [1]; ∆H₁ = −0.606 ÷ 0.0300 = **−20.2 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]
**(d)** q = 50.0 × 4.18 × 12.8 = 2675 J and n(ZnO) = 2.44 ÷ 81.4 = 0.0300 mol [1]; ∆H₂ = −2.675 ÷ 0.0300 = **−89.2 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]
**(e)** Both reactions give ZnCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g), so ∆H = ∆H₁ − ∆H₂ [1]; = −20.2 − (−89.2) = **+69.0 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (e), write ∆H₁ − ∆H₂ before substituting, so your method is visible if the arithmetic slips.

**12. (a)** The readings fall by 0.4 °C per minute, so the temperature at 4 minutes is **29.8 °C** [1]; ∆T = 29.8 − 19.6 = **10.2 K** [1]
**(b)** n(HCl) = 0.0300 × 1.50 = 0.0450 mol, n(NaOH) = 0.0480 mol, so 0.0450 mol of water forms [1]; q = 60.0 × 4.18 × 10.2 = 2558 J = 2.558 kJ [1]; ∆neutH = −2.558 ÷ 0.0450 = **−56.8 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** Less exothermic (about −54.6 kJ mol⁻¹), because ∆T of 9.8 K ignores heat lost before the reading [1]
**(d)** Put a lid on the cup to cut heat loss [1]; take readings every 30 seconds, or measure volumes with a pipette [1]
*Examiner insight:* Use the limiting reagent, here HCl, for the moles of water; using NaOH overstates the amount formed.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting the fuel's or solid's mass into q = mc∆T (questions 4 and 6).
- Leaving q in joules when ∆H is wanted in kJ mol⁻¹.
- Dividing by moles of acid for a diprotic acid (question 5).
- Missing the + sign for an endothermic change.
- Doubling a definition equation to remove ½, which breaks the 1 mol rule.
- Running a ∆cH cycle as products minus reactants.
- Writing ∆H₂ − ∆H₁ in an indirect cycle without checking the direction of each arrow.
