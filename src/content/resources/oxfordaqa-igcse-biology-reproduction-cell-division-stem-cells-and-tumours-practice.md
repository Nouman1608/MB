## Questions

**1.** A gardener grows new strawberry plants from runners. A runner is a shoot that grows from the parent plant and takes root.

**(a)** State the type of reproduction involved. **[1]**
**(b)** The new plants are clones. Explain what this means. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why plants grown from runners show less genetic variation than plants grown from seeds made by pollination of two parent plants. **[1]**

**2.** A chimpanzee has 48 chromosomes in each body cell.

**(a)** State the number of pairs of chromosomes in a body cell. **[1]**
**(b)** State the number of chromosomes in a sperm cell. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why a sperm cell has this number and not 48. **[2]**

**3.** A mouse has a gene for fur colour. One allele gives black fur and a different allele gives brown fur.

**(a)** Distinguish between a gene and an allele. **[2]**
**(b)** A student writes: "Each chromosome carries one gene." Correct this statement. **[1]**
**(c)** State where in a cell chromosomes are found. **[1]**

**4.** A plant has 20 chromosomes in each body cell.

**(a)** Copy and complete the table for one cell dividing by each process. **[3]**

| | Mitosis | Meiosis |
|---|---|---|
| Number of divisions | | |
| Number of cells made | | |
| Chromosomes in each new cell | | |

**(b)** State the purpose of each process in the plant. **[2]**

**5.** A hiker cuts her hand. Over the next few days, the skin heals.

**(a)** Explain how mitosis helps the cut to heal. **[2]**
**(b)** In a mature animal, cells that line the gut are replaced much more often than most other cells. Suggest why. **[2]**

**6.** A mouse body cell has 40 chromosomes. A fertilised mouse egg divides repeatedly by mitosis. Assume every cell divides at the same time in each round.

**(a)** Calculate the number of cells after 7 rounds of division. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the smallest number of rounds needed for the number of cells to exceed 500. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the total number of chromosomes in the embryo after 7 rounds and explain why each cell has 40. **[2]**

**7.** Cells differentiate as an organism develops.

**(a)** State what is meant by differentiation. **[1]**
**(b)** A gardener cuts a shoot from a geranium and it grows roots. A limb cut from a mature animal does not grow a new animal. Explain the difference. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why the new geranium plant is genetically identical to the parent. **[2]**

**8.** A laboratory grows stem cells in a culture. The table shows the cell numbers.

| Time in hours | 0 | 20 | 40 | 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Number of cells | 500 | 1000 | 2000 | 4000 |

**(a)** Each cell divides at the same regular interval and none die. Calculate how many times each original cell line divided between 0 and 60 hours. **[1]**
**(b)** Predict the number of cells at 100 hours. Show your working. **[2]**
**(c)** Stem cells can come from an embryo made by therapeutic cloning to carry the patient's genes. Explain why these stem cells are less likely to be rejected by the patient. **[3]**
**(d)** Name one other source of stem cells that the specification mentions. **[1]**

**9.** A patient is paralysed after an accident. A panel must decide between two research treatments.

Treatment A uses stem cells from an embryo made by therapeutic cloning. The embryo is not allowed to develop.
Treatment B uses stem cells from the bone marrow of a volunteer, who gives them in a medical procedure.

Make a judgement about which treatment the panel should support, using the information. Give points for and against each treatment. **[6]**

**10.** Tumours can form in the body.

**(a)** Distinguish between a benign tumour and a malignant tumour. **[2]**
**(b)** A malignant tumour in the lung is followed by tumours in the liver. Explain how this can happen. **[2]**
**(c)** Name one chemical carcinogen and one source of ionising radiation that can cause tumours. **[2]**

**11.** A doctor measures tumours in two patients over 6 months using scans.

| | Diameter at start in mm | Diameter after 6 months in mm | Other findings |
|---|---|---|---|
| Patient P | 12 | 21 | Tumours found in liver |
| Patient Q | 10 | 11 | No tumours found elsewhere |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in diameter for Patient P. **[2]**
**(b)** For each patient, calculate the mean growth in diameter per month, in mm. Give Patient Q's answer to 2 decimal places. **[2]**
**(c)** State which patient is more likely to have a malignant tumour, giving two reasons. **[2]**
**(d)** In an illustrative data set, lung cancer cases per 100 000 people per year are 15 in non-smokers and 120 in heavy smokers. Calculate how many times greater the rate is in heavy smokers and suggest one reason from tobacco smoke for the difference. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Asexual reproduction [1].
**(b)** Each one has exactly the same genes as the parent and as every other runner plant [1].
**(c)** Runners involve one parent with no mixing of genetic information, whereas two parents give a mixture of genes [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The word "genetically" matters in part (b); "look the same" can fail because clones may differ in appearance through the environment.

**2. (a)** 48 ÷ 2 = **24 pairs** [1].
**(b)** 48 ÷ 2 = **24** [1].
**(c)** Gametes are made by meiosis and have a single set of chromosomes [1]. At fertilisation the sperm and egg join, and the 24 plus 24 restores 48 [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the link to fertilisation; stating "half" without saying the full number is restored later is usually only part of the explanation.

**3. (a)** A gene is a section of genetic information that controls a characteristic [1]. An allele is a different form of the same gene, which may produce a different characteristic [1].
**(b)** Each chromosome carries a large number of genes [1].
**(c)** In the nucleus [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition of allele gains credit when it mentions a form of the same gene; "a type of gene" on its own is too loose.

**4. (a)** Mitosis: 1 division, 2 cells, 20 chromosomes [1]. Meiosis: 2 divisions, 4 cells [1], 10 chromosomes each [1].
**(b)** Mitosis: growth or replacement of body cells [1]. Meiosis: making gametes [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In table questions, write the actual numbers, such as 10, rather than "half", because the question gives you the chromosome number to work from.

**5. (a)** Mitosis makes new cells [1] that are genetically identical to the damaged cells, replacing those lost [1].
**(b)** The gut lining is worn away or damaged by food and digestive enzymes [1], so cells must be replaced by division often [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use a cause and an effect; just writing "they divide more" is a restatement of the question.

**6. (a)** 2⁷ = **128 cells** [1].
**(b)** 2⁸ = 256 is not enough [1]; 2⁹ = 512 exceeds 500, so **9 rounds** [1].
**(c)** 128 × 40 = **5120 chromosomes** [1]. Each cell receives an identical copy of the genetic material before the cell divides [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Showing the check at 8 rounds makes it clear why 9 is the answer; a bare number can look like a guess.

**7. (a)** A cell becomes specialised for a particular job [1].
**(b)** Plant cells can still differentiate at any age [1], so cells in the cut shoot can become root cells [1]. Most animal cells specialise early, and a mature animal divides cells largely for repair and replacement [1].
**(c)** The new plant is produced from the parent by mitosis, which makes genetically identical cells [1]. There is no fusion of gametes or mixing of genetic information [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Comparisons gain more when both organisms are mentioned; describing only the plant leaves out half of the contrast.

**8. (a)** The number doubles every 20 hours, so 60 ÷ 20 = **3 divisions** [1].
**(b)** 100 ÷ 20 = 5 doublings [1]; 500 × 2⁵ = **16 000 cells** [1].
**(c)** The embryo carries the patient's genes [1]. The stem cells are therefore genetically matched to the patient's own cells [1], so the immune system does not treat them as foreign and attack them [1].
**(d)** Adult bone marrow [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Whenever a table is given, try the pattern on two intervals first; doubling in each row confirms the regular interval.

**9.** Treatment A, for: cells match the patient's genes, so there is no rejection [1]. Treatment A, against: an embryo is used and not allowed to develop, which some people regard as destroying a human life [1]. Treatment B, for: bone marrow cells come from an adult, so no embryo is used [1]. Treatment B, against: cells from a volunteer may not match the patient, and the donor must go through a procedure [1]. A clear judgement is stated [1], supported by a reason that follows from the points, for example weighing the benefit of treating paralysis against the concerns about the embryo or the donor [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Judgement questions reward a balance of points on both treatments followed by a conclusion that follows from them; either choice can be justified.

**10. (a)** A benign tumour does not invade other tissues [1]; a malignant tumour has cells that invade healthy tissue [1].
**(b)** Some cells from the malignant tumour enter the bloodstream [1] and are carried to the liver, where they form secondary tumours [1].
**(c)** Chemical carcinogen: for example tobacco smoke or asbestos [1]. Ionising radiation: for example UV or X-rays [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Spreads" alone is vague; name the route, which is the bloodstream, and the result, which is secondary tumours.

**11. (a)** Increase = 21 − 12 = 9 mm [1]. 9 ÷ 12 × 100 = **75%** [1].
**(b)** Patient P: 9 ÷ 6 = **1.5 mm per month** [1]. Patient Q: 1 ÷ 6 = **0.17 mm per month** [1].
**(c)** Patient P [1]. The tumour grew faster and tumours were found in the liver, which suggests that cells have spread and formed secondary tumours [1].
**(d)** 120 ÷ 15 = **8 times greater** [1]. Tobacco smoke contains chemical carcinogens that can cause cells to divide in an uncontrolled way [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Take the original value as the denominator in percentage change; using the final value is a common slip. Say what the figures show in words.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "the cell splits into two" for meiosis, which makes four gametes, not two.
- Saying chromosomes "split in half" instead of stating the number of chromosomes in each cell.
- Using "gene" and "allele" as if they mean the same thing.
- Not saying that clones are genetically identical, which is the key idea.
- Giving the percentage change as a fraction of the final value instead of the starting value.
- Explaining rejection without saying the cells have the patient's genes.
- Listing only advantages or only objections in an ethics question.
- Describing a tumour as "cancer" without saying whether it invades other tissues.
- Leaving out units, such as mm per month, in rate answers.
