These questions cover section 3.2.3 The economic climate on businesses of AQA GCSE Business (8132). Revise first with the [Economic climate study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-business-8132-the-economic-climate-on-businesses-aqa-gcse-business/) and the [Economic climate revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-business-8132-the-economic-climate-on-businesses-aqa-gcse-business-revision-notes/). All businesses and figures are invented.

## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Define the term *interest rate*. **[1]**

**2.** State **two** ways in which a rise in interest rates could reduce consumer spending. **[2]**

**3.** A business has an average overdraft of £25,000. The overdraft interest rate rises from 9% to 12%. Calculate the increase in yearly interest. **[2]**

**4.** Explain **one** reason why a business might find recruitment harder when the level of employment is high. **[3]**

**5.** Identify which **one** of the following businesses is likely to be **least** affected by a fall in incomes: a holiday company, a bakery selling bread, a car dealer. Give a reason. **[2]**

**6.** Explain **one** way a fall in interest rates could benefit a business that is planning to expand. **[3]**

### Section B: longer and case-study questions

**7.** Wexcombe Kitchens fits kitchens costing £8,000 to £20,000. Most customers pay with a loan or finance agreement. Wexcombe also has a bank loan of £250,000.

**(a)** The interest rate on Wexcombe's bank loan rises from 5% to 6.5%. Calculate the extra interest Wexcombe will pay each year. **[2]**
**(b)** Analyse how the rise in interest rates could affect Wexcombe's sales. **[6]**

**8.** Brightling Bargains runs discount stores selling household goods at low prices. Analyse why a rise in unemployment might increase demand for its products. **[4]**

**9.** Halesworth Hotel sells 6,500 room-nights a year at an average of £110. Incomes in the region fall and room sales drop by 12%.

**(a)** Calculate the fall in revenue. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain **one** action the hotel could take to reduce the effect of falling incomes. **[2]**

**10.** Explain why a manufacturer of industrial machinery might receive fewer orders when interest rates rise, even if it has no borrowing of its own. **[3]**

**11.** Analyse how an increase in the level of employment could affect the human resources function of a fast-food restaurant chain. **[6]**

**12.** Kingsdown Electrics makes expensive electric bicycles. It has a large overdraft and plans to borrow more to open a second factory. Economists expect interest rates to rise and unemployment to increase over the next year. Evaluate whether Kingsdown Electrics should go ahead with the second factory now. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** The cost of borrowing money (or the reward for saving), shown as a percentage of the amount borrowed or saved [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Any two of: loans and credit cost more, so fewer items are bought on credit [1]; variable-rate mortgage repayments rise, leaving less to spend [1]; saving pays more, so some people save instead. **[2]**

**3.** Old interest = 9% of £25,000 = £2,250; new interest = 12% of £25,000 = £3,000 [1]. Increase = £750 a year [1]. **[2]**

**4.** When employment is high, fewer people are looking for work [1], so each vacancy attracts fewer applicants and other employers are competing for the same workers [1]. The business may need to offer higher pay or better conditions to attract staff, raising its costs [1]. **[3]**

**5.** The bakery selling bread [1], because bread is an essential that people still buy when incomes fall, whereas holidays and cars can be postponed [1]. **[2]**

**6.** A fall in interest rates makes borrowing cheaper [1], so the business pays less interest on a loan to fund the expansion [1], which leaves more profit and makes the investment more likely to be worthwhile [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** 1.5% of £250,000 = £3,750 a year [1] [1].
**(b)** Most customers pay using finance, so a rise in rates increases their monthly repayments [1]. Some customers will delay buying a new kitchen, because it is an expensive item they can put off [1], so Wexcombe's sales are likely to fall [1]. Customers may also choose cheaper kitchens at the £8,000 end of the range to keep repayments down [1], so even if the number of sales holds up, the average revenue per kitchen falls [1]. Higher mortgage payments for households on variable rates would leave less money for home improvements, adding to the fall in demand [1]. **[8]**

**8.** When unemployment rises, more households have lower incomes [1]. These households look for cheaper ways to buy the household goods they still need [1], so some switch from dearer shops to discount stores [1], meaning Brightling Bargains may gain customers and sales even as total spending falls [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** 12% of 6,500 = 780 room-nights [1]. 780 × £110 = £85,800 [1].
**(b)** The hotel could offer lower-priced packages, such as midweek deals [1], to attract customers who are still willing to travel but are watching their spending [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Machinery is expensive, so the manufacturer's business customers often borrow to buy it [1]. When rates rise, the cost of that borrowing increases [1], so customers delay or cancel their purchases and the manufacturer's orders fall [1]. **[3]**

**11.** As employment rises, fewer people are looking for jobs [1], so the chain receives fewer applications for each vacancy [1]. Fast-food work is often low paid, so staff can more easily leave for better-paid jobs elsewhere [1], increasing staff turnover and the cost of recruiting and training replacements [1]. The HR function may need to raise pay or offer benefits such as flexible hours to keep staff [1], which raises costs, although higher employment also means more customers with money to spend, which may help pay for it [1]. **[6]**

**12.** **For going ahead now:** a second factory would increase capacity, so Kingsdown could meet demand and gain economies of scale [1]. If it waits, rates may be even higher when it eventually borrows [1]. **Against:** it already has a large overdraft, so rising rates will increase its interest costs on existing debt [1], and borrowing more would add further interest payments, reducing profit and squeezing cash flow [1]. Its electric bicycles are expensive, so rising rates may lead customers to delay purchases bought on credit [1], and rising unemployment would reduce incomes and demand for non-essential items [1]. A new factory with falling demand could leave it with spare capacity it is paying for [1]. **Judgement:** Kingsdown should delay the second factory [1], because it faces higher costs and falling demand at the same time; it should reconsider when demand is clearer, unless it already has firm orders that the current factory cannot meet [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Strong answers combine the influences. Here interest rates and unemployment both point the same way, which makes the judgement clearer.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing about why interest rates rise instead of their effects.
- Calculating interest on the change in rate without multiplying by the amount borrowed.
- Assuming every business loses when incomes fall.
- Covering only demand, or only recruitment, for employment questions.
- Treating "rates rise" and "unemployment rises" as separate topics in a case that combines them.
- Giving no judgement in an evaluation.
