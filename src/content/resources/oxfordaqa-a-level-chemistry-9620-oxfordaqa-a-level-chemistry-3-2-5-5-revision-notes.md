These notes condense **3.2.5.5 Variable oxidation states** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International A2 content, examined in Unit 3 and able to reappear in Unit 5. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [variable oxidation states study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-2-5-5/); then try the [variable oxidation states practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-2-5-5-practice/).

## The subtopic in five lines

1. 4s and 3d electrons are close in energy, so transition elements have several oxidation states.
2. Zinc in acid reduces vanadate(V) through V(IV) and V(III) to V(II): yellow → blue → green → violet.
3. A redox potential depends on **pH** and on the **ligand**, not only on the metal.
4. Tollens' reagent, [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺, is reduced to silver by aldehydes but not ketones.
5. MnO₄⁻ titrations: 1 : 5 with Fe²⁺, 2 : 5 with C₂O₄²⁻; acidify with dilute sulfuric acid.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Oxidation state | Charge an atom would have if all its bonds were ionic |
| Vanadate(V) | VO₂⁺ in acid solution (often written VO₃⁻ as the solid's anion) |
| Reducing agent | Electron donor; it is oxidised |
| Oxidising agent | Electron acceptor; it is reduced |
| E⦵ | Standard electrode potential: 298 K, 100 kPa, 1.00 mol dm⁻³ ions |
| E⦵cell | E⦵(species reduced) − E⦵(species oxidised); positive means feasible |
| Self-indicating titration | Titrant's own colour shows the end point (MnO₄⁻) |
| Titre | Volume of titrant added at the end point |

## Vanadium

| Oxidation state | Species | Colour | E⦵ of the step down / V |
|---|---|---|---|
| +5 | VO₂⁺ | yellow | +1.00 (to VO²⁺) |
| +4 | VO²⁺ | blue | +0.34 (to V³⁺) |
| +3 | V³⁺ | green | −0.26 (to V²⁺) |
| +2 | V²⁺ | violet | — |

Zn²⁺/Zn is −0.76 V, below all three, so zinc reduces vanadium to V²⁺. A reductant with E⦵ between +0.34 V and −0.26 V (for example Sn²⁺/Sn⁴⁺ at +0.15 V) stops at V³⁺. A green colour can appear briefly between yellow and blue: it is a mixture of the two.

**Half-equations to know:**

```
VO₂⁺ + 2H⁺ + e⁻ → VO²⁺ + H₂O
VO²⁺ + 2H⁺ + e⁻ → V³⁺ + H₂O
V³⁺ + e⁻ → V²⁺
Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻
```

## pH and ligand effects

- H⁺ on the left of a reduction half-equation: more acid → more positive E → stronger oxidising agent (MnO₄⁻ in acid).
- Alkaline conditions make lower oxidation states easier to oxidise: Fe(OH)₂ (green) darkens to brown in air.
- Ligands change E: Ag⁺/Ag +0.80 V but [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺/Ag +0.37 V. Cobalt(II) is oxidised by air in excess ammonia but not as [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺.

## Tollens' reagent

- [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ + e⁻ → Ag + 2NH₃
- RCHO + H₂O → RCOOH + 2H⁺ + 2e⁻
- Aldehyde: silver mirror. Ketone: no change. Warm in a water bath.

## Manganate(VII) titrations

```
MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O            1 : 5
2MnO₄⁻ + 16H⁺ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ → 2Mn²⁺ + 10CO₂ + 8H₂O        2 : 5
2MnO₄⁻ + 6H⁺ + 5H₂O₂ → 2Mn²⁺ + 8H₂O + 5O₂             2 : 5
```

**Method for any redox titration**

1. Write both half-equations; balance electrons; add and cancel.
2. Moles of titrant = concentration × titre ÷ 1000.
3. Use the ratio for moles of analyte in the portion.
4. Scale up to the full flask (× 10 for 25.0 cm³ from 250 cm³).
5. Convert to mass, Mr, percentage or concentration.

**Acid choice:** dilute H₂SO₄. HCl: Cl⁻ oxidised, titre too large. HNO₃: oxidises analyte, titre too small. Weak acid: brown MnO₂ forms.

**Ethanedioate titrations** are carried out warm, because the reaction starts slowly; Mn²⁺ formed then speeds it up.

## Exam traps

- VO₂⁺ is +5 and VO²⁺ is +4: count the oxygens.
- Mixing up 1 : 5 (Fe²⁺) and 2 : 5 (C₂O₄²⁻ and H₂O₂).
- Forgetting the × 10 scale-up from portion to flask.
- Subtracting E⦵ values in the wrong order.
- End point is the first permanent pale pink, not purple.
- "Acid makes it faster" is not the reason for acidifying: H⁺ is a reactant in the half-equation.
- In an iron(II) ethanedioate sample, both ions react with MnO₄⁻: 3 mol MnO₄⁻ per 5 mol FeC₂O₄.

## Self-test

Answer without looking, then check below.

1. Why do transition elements show variable oxidation states?
2. Give the oxidation state of vanadium in VO²⁺ and in VO₂⁺.
3. State the colours of vanadium(V), (IV), (III) and (II) in acid solution.
4. Use E⦵ values to show that zinc can reduce V³⁺ to V²⁺.
5. Why does tin(II) stop at V³⁺?
6. Write the half-equation for the reduction of Tollens' reagent.
7. How does increasing [H⁺] affect the oxidising power of MnO₄⁻? Explain.
8. Why is hydrochloric acid unsuitable for acidifying a manganate(VII) titration?
9. 22.80 cm³ of 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ KMnO₄ reacts with Fe²⁺ in a 25.0 cm³ portion. Find n(Fe²⁺) in the portion.
10. How many moles of MnO₄⁻ react with 2.50 × 10⁻³ mol of C₂O₄²⁻?
11. What brown solid forms if a manganate(VII) titration is not acidified enough?
12. Iron(II) hydroxide is precipitated and left in air. What is seen, and what does it show about oxidation states and pH?

### Answers

1. The 4s and 3d sub-levels are close in energy, so different numbers of electrons can be lost without a large energy jump.
2. VO²⁺: +4. VO₂⁺: +5.
3. Yellow, blue, green, violet.
4. E⦵cell = −0.26 − (−0.76) = +0.50 V; positive, so feasible.
5. For V³⁺ → V²⁺ with Sn²⁺, E⦵cell = −0.26 − 0.15 = −0.41 V; negative, so not feasible.
6. [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ + e⁻ → Ag + 2NH₃.
7. It increases: H⁺ is on the left of MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ ⇌ Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O, so the equilibrium shifts right and E becomes more positive.
8. MnO₄⁻ oxidises Cl⁻ to Cl₂, so extra manganate(VII) is used and the titre is too large.
9. n(MnO₄⁻) = 4.56 × 10⁻⁴ mol; n(Fe²⁺) = 5 × 4.56 × 10⁻⁴ = 2.28 × 10⁻³ mol.
10. 2.50 × 10⁻³ × 2/5 = 1.00 × 10⁻³ mol.
11. Manganese(IV) oxide, MnO₂.
12. The green precipitate darkens to brown as iron(II) is oxidised to iron(III); lower oxidation states are oxidised more easily in alkaline conditions.
