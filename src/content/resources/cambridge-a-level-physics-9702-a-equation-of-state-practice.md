Use R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹, k = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹ and N_A = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹. Assume all gases behave ideally.

---

## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by an ideal gas. **[1]**

**2.** By comparing pV = nRT with pV = NkT, show that k = R/N_A. **[2]**

**3.** Calculate the volume occupied by 0.25 mol of an ideal gas at a pressure of 2.0 × 10⁵ Pa and a temperature of 27 °C. **[2]**

**4.** A sealed rigid can contains air at 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa and 20 °C. It is heated to 300 °C. Calculate the new pressure. **[2]**

**5.** A vacuum chamber contains gas at a pressure of 1.0 × 10⁻⁸ Pa and a temperature of 293 K. Calculate the number of molecules per cubic metre. **[2]**

**6.** A student measures the pressure of a fixed volume of gas at several temperatures in °C and expects the graph of pressure against temperature to pass through the origin. Explain why it does not. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A weather balloon contains 4.0 m³ of helium at 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa and 17 °C at ground level. The molar mass of helium is 4.0 g mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the amount of helium in the balloon. **[2]**

**(b)** At high altitude the pressure is 3.0 × 10⁴ Pa and the temperature is –43 °C. Calculate the new volume of the balloon. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the mass of helium in the balloon. **[1]**

**8.** A cylinder of volume 0.050 m³ contains oxygen (molar mass 32 g mol⁻¹) at 1.2 × 10⁷ Pa and 20 °C. Some time later, after a leak, the pressure is 8.0 × 10⁶ Pa and the temperature is 12 °C.

**(a)** Calculate the initial mass of oxygen. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the final mass of oxygen. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the mass of oxygen that has leaked out. **[1]**

**9.** Flask A, of volume 2.0 × 10⁻³ m³, contains gas at 3.0 × 10⁵ Pa. Flask B, of volume 3.0 × 10⁻³ m³, is evacuated. The flasks are joined by a tube of negligible volume, and a tap between them is opened. The temperature stays constant.

**(a)** Calculate the final pressure. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why pV = constant can be applied to the gas as a whole, even though gas leaves flask A. **[1]**

**10.** A graph of pV against T for a sample of ideal gas is a straight line through the origin with gradient 12.5 J K⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the amount of gas. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of molecules. **[1]**

**(c)** Describe the line for a sample containing half as much gas. **[1]**

**11.** Air is trapped in a sealed syringe at 22 °C. Its volume is 50 cm³ at a pressure of 1.20 × 10⁵ Pa. The plunger is pushed in slowly, so that the temperature stays constant, until the volume is 30 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate the new pressure. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the amount of air in the syringe. **[2]**

**(c)** State which graph of the measurements would give a straight line through the origin. **[1]**

**12.** The density of an ideal gas is given by ρ = pM/RT.

**(a)** Calculate the ratio of the density of air at 0 °C to its density at 30 °C, at the same pressure. **[2]**

**(b)** Use your answer to explain why a hot-air balloon rises. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** A gas for which pV is proportional to the thermodynamic temperature T (for a fixed amount of gas) [1].

**2.** nRT = NkT, so nR = Nk [1]; N = nN_A, so nR = nN_A k, giving k = R/N_A [1].

**3.** T = 300 K; V = nRT/p = 0.25 × 8.31 × 300 ÷ 2.0 × 10⁵ [1] = 3.1 × 10⁻³ m³ [1].

**4.** p₂ = p₁ × T₂/T₁ = 1.0 × 10⁵ × 573 ÷ 293 [1] = 1.96 × 10⁵ Pa [1].

**5.** N/V = p/kT = 1.0 × 10⁻⁸ ÷ (1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 293) [1] = 2.5 × 10¹² m⁻³ [1].

**6.** Pressure is proportional to thermodynamic temperature, not Celsius temperature [1]; the zero of the Celsius scale is not absolute zero, so the line meets the temperature axis at about –273 °C instead [1].

**7. (a)** n = pV/RT = 1.0 × 10⁵ × 4.0 ÷ (8.31 × 290) [1] = 166 mol [1].

**(b)** V₂ = V₁ × (p₁/p₂) × (T₂/T₁) = 4.0 × (1.0 × 10⁵ ÷ 3.0 × 10⁴) × (230 ÷ 290) [1] = 10.6 m³ [1].

**(c)** m = 166 × 0.0040 = 0.66 kg [1].

**8. (a)** T₁ = 293 K [1]; n₁ = p₁V/RT₁ = 1.2 × 10⁷ × 0.050 ÷ (8.31 × 293) = 246 mol [1]; m₁ = 246 × 0.032 = 7.9 kg [1].

**(b)** n₂ = 8.0 × 10⁶ × 0.050 ÷ (8.31 × 285) = 169 mol; m₂ = 5.4 kg [1].

**(c)** 7.9 – 5.4 = 2.5 kg [1].

**9. (a)** pV is constant for the whole gas: 3.0 × 10⁵ × 2.0 × 10⁻³ = p × 5.0 × 10⁻³ [1]; p = 1.2 × 10⁵ Pa [1].

**(b)** No gas enters or leaves the two flasks together, so the total amount n is fixed, and T is constant [1].

**10. (a)** Gradient = nR, so n = 12.5 ÷ 8.31 [1] = 1.5 mol [1].

**(b)** N = 1.50 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 9.1 × 10²³ [1].

**(c)** A straight line through the origin with half the gradient (6.25 J K⁻¹) [1].

**11. (a)** p₂ = 1.20 × 10⁵ × 50 ÷ 30 = 2.0 × 10⁵ Pa [1].

**(b)** n = pV/RT = 1.20 × 10⁵ × 50 × 10⁻⁶ ÷ (8.31 × 295) [1] = 2.4 × 10⁻³ mol [1].

**(c)** Pressure against 1/volume [1].

**12. (a)** ρ ∝ 1/T at constant p, so ratio = 303 ÷ 273 [1] = 1.11 [1].

**(b)** The hot air inside the balloon is less dense than the cooler air outside, so the weight of the air displaced exceeds the weight of the air inside (an upthrust greater than the weight of balloon plus contents) [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Substituting Celsius temperatures.
- Leaving volumes in cm³, dm³ or litres when using R or k.
- Using p₁V₁/T₁ = p₂V₂/T₂ for a cylinder that has leaked.
- Using R with a number of molecules, or k with a number of moles.
- Forgetting that the gradient of pV against T is nR, not R.
- Giving a molecular-model answer when asked to define an ideal gas.
