This guide covers subtopic **1.15 Time** in Topic 1, Number, of Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024, 2025 to 2027 series. Time calculations use bases of 60 and 24 rather than 10, which is why they cause so many slips. The [Number revision notes](/resources/o-level-mathematics-number-revision-notes/) give a brief summary. This page covers the units, the clocks, timetables and time zones, with a safe method for each type of problem.

Companion pages: the [Time revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-time-revision-notes/) and the [Time practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-time-practice/).

## What the syllabus asks

The syllabus lists three outcomes:

1. Calculate with time in seconds (s), minutes (min), hours (h), days, weeks, months and years, including the relationship between the units. The notes state that **1 year = 365 days**.
2. Calculate times in terms of the 24-hour and 12-hour clock. In the 24-hour clock, for example, 3.15 a.m. is written 03 15 and 3.15 p.m. is written 15 15.
3. Read clocks and timetables. This includes problems with time zones, local times and time differences.

## Units of time

| Unit | Equals |
|---|---|
| 1 minute | 60 seconds |
| 1 hour | 60 minutes = 3 600 seconds |
| 1 day | 24 hours = 1 440 minutes = 86 400 seconds |
| 1 week | 7 days |
| 1 year | 365 days (the syllabus value) |

Months vary in length, so a question will say how many days to use. A year is 52 weeks and 1 day, because 365 = 7 × 52 + 1.

### Converting between units

To change to a smaller unit, multiply. To change to a bigger unit, divide. For results with a remainder, divide, then convert the remainder.

**Worked example.** Write 5 400 seconds in hours and minutes.

```
5400 ÷ 60 = 90 minutes
90 min = 1 h 30 min
```

**Worked example.** Write 23 456 seconds in hours, minutes and seconds.

```
23 456 ÷ 3600 = 6 remainder 1856
1856 ÷ 60 = 30 remainder 56
23 456 s = 6 h 30 min 56 s
```

**Worked example.** How many seconds are there in 3 days?

```
3 x 24 x 60 x 60 = 259 200 seconds
```

**Worked example.** How many hours are there in a year of 365 days?

```
365 x 24 = 8 760 hours
```

## Decimals and time

Time is not decimal. 2.5 hours is 2 hours 30 minutes, not 2 hours 50 minutes.

```
2 h 24 min = 2 + 24/60 = 2.4 hours
2.4 hours = 2 h + 0.4 x 60 min = 2 h 24 min
```

This matters when using the speed formula and on the calculator, as the calculator subtopic explains. To change minutes to hours, divide by 60. To change a decimal part of an hour into minutes, multiply by 60.

## The 12-hour and 24-hour clocks

The **12-hour clock** uses a.m. (before noon) and p.m. (after noon). The **24-hour clock** counts from 00 00 at midnight to 23 59.

| 12-hour | 24-hour |
|---|---|
| 12.25 a.m. | 00 25 |
| 3.15 a.m. | 03 15 |
| 12.00 noon | 12 00 |
| 3.15 p.m. | 15 15 |
| 9.40 p.m. | 21 40 |

Rules:

- From 1 a.m. to 12.59 p.m. (noon hour), the hour stays the same. Just add a zero if needed, so 7.30 a.m. becomes 07 30.
- From 1 p.m., add 12 to the hour: 3.15 p.m. is 15 15.
- Just after midnight, 12.25 a.m. becomes 00 25.

To go from 24-hour to 12-hour, subtract 12 from hours 13 to 23 and write p.m.: 21 40 becomes 9.40 p.m.

## Finding a duration

To find how long between two times, count in stages.

**Method 1: count up to the next hour.**

**Worked example.** How long is it from 09 35 to 22 50?

```
09 35 to 10 00  =  25 min
10 00 to 22 00  =  12 h
22 00 to 22 50  =  50 min
total: 12 h + 75 min = 13 h 15 min
```

**Method 2: subtract.** Subtract the hours and minutes separately. If the minutes would be negative, borrow one hour (60 minutes).

```
22 50 - 09 35:  hours 22 - 9 = 13,  minutes 50 - 35 = 15
answer 13 h 15 min
```

**Worked example (across midnight).** A night train leaves at 21 40 and arrives at 03 15 the next day. How long is the journey?

```
21 40 to 24 00  =  2 h 20 min
00 00 to 03 15  =  3 h 15 min
total: 5 h 35 min
```

Do not just subtract 21 40 from 03 15. Always go through midnight when the arrival time is earlier than the departure time.

## Finding an arrival time

Add the duration to the start time. Add hours first, then minutes, then carry if the minutes reach 60 or more.

**Worked example.** A coach leaves at 08 45 and the journey takes 3 hours 40 minutes. When does it arrive?

```
08 45 + 3 h = 11 45
11 45 + 40 min = 12 25
arrival: 12 25
```

**Worked example.** A train leaves at 06 50. The journey takes 2 hours 25 minutes. Find the arrival time.

```
06 50 + 2 h = 08 50
08 50 + 25 min = 09 15
```

## Timetables

A timetable lists times in columns. To read it, find the row for the stop and the column for the service. To work out a journey time, subtract the departure from the arrival.

**Worked example.** A train leaves at 11 45 and arrives at 13 10. How long does the journey take?

```
11 45 to 12 00 = 15 min
12 00 to 13 00 = 1 h
13 00 to 13 10 = 10 min
total: 1 h 25 min
```

When there is a waiting time at a station, add it to the travelling time to find the whole journey time.

## Repeated durations

**Worked example.** A job takes 3 hours 50 minutes and is done on 4 days. Find the total time.

```
4 x 3 h 50 min = 12 h + 200 min
200 min = 3 h 20 min
total: 15 h 20 min
```

## Time zones

Places in different parts of the world use different local times. A time difference tells you how many hours to add or subtract. A place **ahead** of you has a later time on the clock. A place **behind** you has an earlier time.

**Worked example.** A flight leaves at 22 40 local time and takes 9 hours 35 minutes. The destination is 5 hours ahead of the departure point. Find the local arrival time.

```
22 40 + 9 h 35 min = 32 15  →  08 15 the next day (departure time zone)
add the time difference: 08 15 + 5 h = 13 15
arrival: 13 15 the next day, local time
```

An alternative is to add the time difference to the departure time first: 22 40 + 5 h = 03 40 (destination clock), and then add the flight time: 03 40 + 9 h 35 min = 13 15. Both methods agree.

**Worked example.** City A is 3 hours behind City B. A meeting begins at 14 30 in City B. What time is it in City A?

```
14 30 - 3 h = 11 30
```

Sense check: the place that is behind has the earlier time.

## Time in the speed formula

When you use speed = distance ÷ time, express the time in hours (decimal) if the speed is in km/h. For example, 1 h 45 min = 1.75 h. See the rates guide for more.

## Exam technique

- Write the times you add or subtract, to show the working.
- For answers in the 24-hour clock, write four digits, such as 09 15.
- If the question uses a.m. and p.m., give the answer the same way.
- When the arrival is on the next day, say so.
- Remember that there are 60 minutes in an hour. Do not carry at 100.
- For time zones, decide which place is ahead before adding or subtracting.

## Common mistakes

- Treating minutes as decimals, so that 2 h 30 min becomes 2.30 hours.
- Subtracting 21 40 from 03 15 without crossing midnight.
- Forgetting to carry 60 minutes into an hour.
- Reversing the time zone direction (subtracting when the place is ahead).
- Confusing 12.00 a.m. and 12.00 p.m.
- Writing 3.15 p.m. as 3 15 in the 24-hour clock.

## Where this leads

Time connects with speed and rates, with bearings and journeys, and with exchange problems that involve a timetable or time zone. It also appears in questions about interest, where years are counted.
