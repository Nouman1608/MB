## Section A

**1.** Identify two strengths and one weakness of the German Confederation created in 1815. **[3]**

**2.** State two aims of German liberals before 1848. **[2]**

**3.** Give three reasons why nationalist ideas grew in the German states in the 1840s. **[3]**

**4.** Identify three actions of Friedrich Wilhelm IV during 1848-49 that affected the revolution in Prussia. **[3]**

**5.** State two reasons why Austria was weaker in relation to Prussia by 1859 than in 1850. **[2]**

**6.** Identify three outcomes of the Treaty of Prague and the settlement of 1866-67. **[3]**

**7.** Explain two ways in which Bismarck used the Luxemburg crisis of 1867 to his advantage. **[4]**

## Section B

**8.** Explain why the Frankfurt Parliament collapsed in 1849. **[10]**

**9.** "Bismarck had planned war with Austria from the moment he took office." How far do you agree? **[20]**

**10.** Read the two invented sources below. Compare Sources A and B as evidence about liberal attitudes towards Bismarck in 1866.

*Source A (invented)* -- From a speech by a Progressive Party deputy in the Prussian parliament, September 1866. "I will vote against this bill. For four years the government spent the people's money without our consent. Victory at Königgrätz does not make that lawful. If we pardon it now, every future minister will know that success excuses everything."

*Source B (invented)* -- From a letter by a liberal newspaper editor in the Rhineland, August 1866. "We fought the minister over the budget, and we were right. But he has done in seven weeks what our speeches could not do in fifty years: he has driven Austria out of Germany. Let us make peace with him and build the new Germany together, and fight for our freedoms inside it." **[15]**

**11.** Read the two further invented sources below, together with Sources A and B in Question 10. How far do Sources A to D support the view that nationalism mattered more to German liberals than liberalism by 1867?

*Source C (invented)* -- From the programme of a new liberal party, 1867. "We give our support to the government in its national policy, and we shall work to strengthen the North German Confederation until the south joins it. We will not abandon our demand for a responsible ministry and for the full rights of parliament."

*Source D (invented)* -- A description of a cartoon in a satirical Berlin magazine, 1866. A Prussian officer in a spiked helmet holds out a large sausage labelled "Unity". A thin man labelled "the Liberal" drops a book labelled "Constitution" to take the sausage with both hands. **[25]**

**12.** Explain why the southern German states were reluctant to join a Prussian-led Germany before 1870. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** Up to 2 marks for strengths, 1 for a weakness: it preserved the sovereignty of the princes, which made it acceptable to them (1); it kept peace among the German states and provided a common defence framework (1); weakness -- it had no common government, army or economic policy, and was dominated by Austria in the interests of rulers (1). **[3]**

**2.** Any two, 1 mark each: written constitutions limiting rulers (1); elected assemblies with control over taxation (1); freedom of the press, speech and association (1); equality before the law and an end to remaining feudal privileges (1). **[2]**

**3.** Any three, 1 mark each: the Rhine crisis of 1840, when French claims to the Rhine frontier provoked patriotic songs and writing (1); the growth of the Zollverein and railways, which gave businessmen an interest in a single market (1); the spread of newspapers, reading clubs and gymnastic and choral societies (1); the influence of nationalist professors and historians in the universities (1). **[3]**

**4.** Any three, 1 mark each: withdrew troops from Berlin after the March street fighting (1); appeared wearing the black-red-gold colours and promised that Prussia would merge into Germany (1); allowed a Prussian National Assembly to meet (1); sent troops back into Berlin and dissolved the Assembly in late 1848 (1); imposed his own constitution in December 1848 (1); refused the Frankfurt crown in April 1849 (1). **[3]**

**5.** Any two, 1 mark each: Austria had failed to enter the Zollverein, which was renewed without it, while Prussian industry, coal and railways grew fast (1); Austria faced financial strain and lost Lombardy after defeat in Italy in 1859 (1); Austria had isolated itself from Russia by not supporting it in the Crimean War (1). **[2]**

**6.** Any three, 1 mark each: Austria was excluded from German affairs and the old Confederation dissolved (1); Prussia annexed Hanover, Hesse-Kassel, Nassau, Frankfurt and Schleswig-Holstein (1); the North German Confederation was formed under Prussian leadership (1); the southern states remained independent but signed secret military alliances with Prussia (1); Austria paid a modest indemnity and lost no territory to Prussia (1). **[3]**

**7.** Two ways, 2 marks each (1 for the point, 1 for development): he roused German national opinion against Napoleon III's plan to buy Luxemburg, which strengthened northern and southern support for Prussia (2); the London conference of 1867 neutralised Luxemburg and left Napoleon without the compensation he wanted, damaging his prestige at home and making France more hostile and more likely to blunder later (2); he could publish the southern military treaties to show France that Prussia would not stand alone (2). **[4]**

**8.** Indicative content. **No power of its own:** the Parliament had no army, civil service or taxes, so it depended on the princes, above all Prussia. **Schleswig-Holstein:** when Prussia signed the Malmö armistice in August 1848, Frankfurt accepted it, provoking riots that Prussian and Austrian troops put down -- showing where real power lay. **Divisions:** debate over *grossdeutsch* or *kleindeutsch*, and over Austria's non-German lands, delayed the constitution; liberals feared radicals and the workers' movement more than the princes. **Recovery of the conservatives:** Austria retook Vienna in October 1848; Prussian troops returned to Berlin in November. **The crown refused:** when Friedrich Wilhelm IV rejected the crown in April 1849, Austria and Prussia recalled their deputies; the rump moved to Stuttgart and was dispersed in June. **Linking:** dependence on the princes was the root weakness; their recovery and the King's refusal turned it into collapse. **[10]**

**9.** Indicative content. **For:** at Frankfurt in the 1850s Bismarck had concluded that Austria and Prussia could not share Germany; on taking office he told the Austrian ambassador that Austria should shift its centre of gravity to Hungary; he blocked the Congress of Princes (1863); he used the Danish war and the Gastein arrangement to keep a quarrel available; he secured French neutrality at Biarritz and an Italian alliance in April 1866 that only made sense if war came within three months. **Against:** his first priority in 1862 was the constitutional crisis at home; he co-operated with Austria in 1864 and at Gastein accepted a compromise that could have lasted; his letters show he considered several outcomes, including a division of influence along the River Main; he exploited Austrian mistakes such as referring the duchies to the Diet. **Judgement:** strong answers distinguish aims from methods -- he consistently aimed at Prussian dominance in north Germany and was willing to fight for it, but the timing and route to war were improvised from opportunities. **[20]**

**10.** Indicative content. **Similarities:** both accept the government acted unlawfully over the budget; both admit the importance of the 1866 victory. **Differences:** A refuses to pardon the government and fears that success will excuse future illegality; B wants to "make peace" with Bismarck and fight for freedoms inside the new Germany. **Explaining them:** A is a deputy in the parliamentary debate on the Indemnity Bill, speaking for the minority who held to constitutional principle; B is an editor writing privately as the scale of victory became clear, and reflects the mood that produced the National Liberals. Context: the Indemnity Bill passed by a large majority, so B represents the majority view. A judgement on usefulness: B is better evidence of how most liberals responded, A of the principle at stake. **[15]**

**11.** Indicative content. **Support:** B puts the expulsion of Austria ahead of the budget dispute; D shows the liberal abandoning the constitution for "Unity"; C pledges support for the government's national policy. Knowledge: the Indemnity Bill passed; the National Liberals formed in 1867 and became Bismarck's main parliamentary support; the North German constitution gave the Reichstag universal male suffrage but no control over the chancellor. **Challenge:** A shows that some liberals refused; C insists on keeping the demand for a responsible ministry and the full rights of parliament; the Progressive Party survived in opposition. **Provenance:** D is satire, exaggerating for effect; C is a public programme designed to justify a change of course; A and B show the split in real time. **Judgement:** the sources largely support the view, but C suggests many liberals believed they were postponing, not abandoning, their constitutional aims. **[25]**

**12.** Indicative content. **Religion:** Bavaria, Baden, Württemberg and southern Hesse were heavily Catholic and feared domination by Protestant Prussia. **Politics:** their rulers valued their own sovereignty and dynasties; Bavaria had its own ambitions to lead a "third Germany". **Culture and resentment:** many southerners disliked Prussian militarism and conscription; some had fought on Austria's side in 1866. **Economics:** they were already tied to Prussia through the Zollverein without needing political union; the 1868 Zollparlament elections returned many anti-Prussian deputies. **Austria:** many southerners still looked to Austria as part of Germany. **Linking:** only a threat from France was strong enough to override these concerns, which is why the war of 1870 completed unification. **[10]**
