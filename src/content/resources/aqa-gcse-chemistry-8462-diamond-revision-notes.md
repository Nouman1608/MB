These notes condense **4.2.3.1 Diamond** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462) specification (for teaching from
September 2016, version 1.2). It is Paper 1 content for both tiers, with no Higher-only statements and no
required practical. For full explanations, see the [diamond study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-diamond/);
then practise with the [diamond practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-diamond-practice/).

## Core facts

- In diamond, **each carbon atom forms four covalent bonds** with other carbon atoms in a **giant covalent
  structure**.
- So diamond is **very hard**, has a **very high melting point** and **does not conduct electricity**.
- You must be able to **explain the properties of diamond in terms of its structure and bonding**.

## Structure

- A form of the element carbon, formula C.
- A rigid three-dimensional network of atoms.
- Each carbon atom is bonded to four others, using all four of its outer electrons.
- There are no molecules, ions or delocalised electrons.

```
          C
         /|\
        / | \
       C  |  C
        \ | /
         \|/
          C
```

## Property explanations

| Property | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Very hard | Each atom is held by four strong covalent bonds in a rigid 3D network |
| Very high melting point | Many strong covalent bonds must be broken, needing a very large amount of energy |
| No electrical conduction | All outer electrons are used in bonds, so no delocalised electrons or ions are free to move |

## Diamond and graphite

| | Diamond | Graphite |
|---|---|---|
| Bonds per carbon atom | 4 | 3 |
| Structure | 3D network | Layers of hexagonal rings |
| Hard? | Very hard | Soft |
| Conducts? | No | Yes |

Both are forms of carbon with giant covalent structures. The difference is in how the atoms are bonded.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Diamond | A giant covalent form of carbon with four bonds per atom |
| Giant covalent structure | A network of atoms joined by strong covalent bonds |
| Covalent bond | A shared pair of electrons between two atoms |
| Rigid | Not able to be bent or deformed easily |
| Delocalised electron | An electron free to move through a structure |
| Allotrope | A different structural form of the same element (diamond and graphite are allotropes of carbon) |

## Model answers

**Why is diamond hard?** Giant covalent structure; each carbon bonded to four others by strong covalent bonds;
rigid 3D network; atoms cannot slide, and a lot of energy is needed to break the bonds.

**Why does it have a very high melting point?** The strong covalent bonds must be broken, and there are very many
of them, so a very large amount of energy is needed.

**Why does it not conduct?** All four outer electrons of each atom are used in bonds; there are no delocalised
electrons and no ions.

## Counting bonds

If each atom has four bonds and each bond joins two atoms, the number of bonds is atoms × 4 ÷ 2.

| Atoms | Bonds |
|---|---|
| 100 | 200 |
| 500 | 1000 |
| 2000 | 4000 |

## Representing diamond

| Representation | Good for | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Ball and stick | Showing the 3D bonding | Sticks are not real; atoms are not to scale |
| 2D bond diagram | Showing which atoms are bonded | Cannot show the 3D structure; shows only part |
| Space-filling | Showing packing | Hides the bonds |

Check: every carbon atom inside a diamond diagram should have four bonds.

## Six-mark answer plan

1. Giant covalent structure.
2. Each carbon atom forms four covalent bonds.
3. Rigid 3D network, so very hard.
4. Covalent bonds must be broken to melt it.
5. Many strong bonds, so a very large amount of energy.
6. All outer electrons in bonds, so no conduction.

## Diamond and silicon dioxide

| | Diamond | Silicon dioxide |
|---|---|---|
| Elements | Carbon only | Silicon and oxygen |
| Bonding | 4 C–C bonds per atom | Each Si bonded to 4 O; each O to 2 Si |
| Both | Giant covalent, very high melting point, strong covalent bonds to break | Same |

## Exam traps

- **"Intermolecular forces in diamond."** There are no separate molecules.
- **"Strong bonds" with no detail.** Say covalent and say they must be broken.
- **Confusing diamond and graphite.** Diamond has four bonds per atom, graphite three.
- **"Diamond conducts because carbon has four outer electrons."** All four are used in bonding.
- **"Carbon atoms are hard."** Hardness belongs to the structure.
- **Writing "diamond is a compound".** It is an element.

## Quick self-test

1. How many covalent bonds does each carbon atom form in diamond?
2. What type of structure does diamond have?
3. Give three properties of diamond.
4. Why is diamond very hard?
5. Why does diamond have a very high melting point?
6. Why does diamond not conduct electricity?
7. What is the formula of diamond?
8. Name another form of carbon that is a giant covalent structure.
9. Calculate the number of bonds in a crystal of 300 atoms if each atom has four bonds and each bond is shared.
10. Which has more bonds per atom, diamond or graphite?

### Answers

1. Four.
2. Giant covalent.
3. Very hard, very high melting point, does not conduct electricity.
4. Each carbon is held by four strong covalent bonds in a rigid 3D network.
5. Many strong covalent bonds must be broken, needing a very large amount of energy.
6. All outer electrons are used in bonds, so there are no delocalised electrons or ions.
7. C.
8. Graphite.
9. 300 × 4 ÷ 2 = 600.
10. Diamond (4 against 3).
