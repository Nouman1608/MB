## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. In
the 4-mark questions, one mark for each point identified and one for explaining it, up to two marks
for each of the two (question 6: two marks for each term, defined and developed). The 6-mark
questions give four AO1 marks on that pattern plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general,
accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The
12-mark questions are marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

### Section A

**1.** Ghibah [1].

**2.** Al-Amin [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: safety from harm; kindness and gifts such as shared food; help when ill or in need; respect for privacy; patience with minor annoyances; being visited and greeted.

**4.** Any two, [1] each: money or goods left for safekeeping; a secret; a promise; a job or working hours; public office or public money; one's own body, time or talents.

**5.** For each of two reasons: reason identified [1] and explained [1].

- **It was the sin of Iblis** [1]: he refused to obey Allah because he thought himself better than Adam (AS) (7:12) [1].
- **It rejects the truth** [1]: the Prophet (pbuh) defined it as rejecting the truth, so it stops a person accepting guidance [1].
- **It looks down on others** [1]: it denies the equality of all people before Allah [1].
- **It blocks entry to Paradise** [1]: even an atom's weight of arrogance in the heart can prevent it, according to the Hadith in Sahih Muslim [1].

**6.** **Hasad** is wanting another person to lose a blessing Allah has given them [1]; it is forbidden because it resents Allah's decree and leads to hatred and harm [1]. **Ghibtah** is wishing to have the same blessing without wanting the other person to lose it [1]; it is allowed, for example for wealth spent rightly or wisdom used to teach, as the Prophet (pbuh) said (Sahih al-Bukhari) [1].

**7.** For each of two ways: way identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Using working time honestly** [1]: time is paid for by the employer, so wasting it betrays a trust [1].
- **Keeping confidential information** [1]: secrets learned at work are an amanah and must not be shared [1].
- **Looking after the employer's property** [1]: equipment and money are entrusted, so they must not be misused [1].
- **Doing the job well** [1]: the Prophet (pbuh) encouraged doing work with excellence, so careless work is a breach of trust [1].

**8.** For each of two ways: way identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Destroys trust** [1]: people who hear gossip wonder what is said about them too [1].
- **Damages reputations** [1]: a person can lose friends, work or marriage prospects because of talk they cannot answer [1].
- **Breeds division** [1]: groups form and quarrels grow, breaking the brotherhood Islam teaches [1].
- **Spreads falsehood** [1]: talk is often exaggerated and becomes slander [1].

### Section B

**9.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, the reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- **Command of Allah** [1]: returning trusts is a direct instruction in the Qur'an, so it is an act of obedience [1].
- **Sign of faith** [1]: betraying trust is a sign of hypocrisy, so trustworthiness shows true belief [1].
- **Following the Prophet (pbuh)** [1]: he was known as al-Amin even by his enemies [1].
- **Society depends on it** [1]: trade, work and government only function when people can be trusted [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Indeed, Allah commands you to render trusts to whom they are due" (Qur'an 4:58) [2].
- The signs of a hypocrite include that "when he is trusted, he betrays" (Sahih al-Bukhari) [2].

**10.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 9. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 9.

Reasons (AO1):

- **It resents Allah's decree** [1]: Allah gives each person what He wills, so jealousy questions His wisdom [1].
- **It leads to other sins** [1]: backbiting, hatred and harming others often follow jealousy [1].
- **It destroys brotherhood** [1]: the Prophet (pbuh) linked giving up envy with being brothers [1].
- **It harms the jealous person** [1]: it brings unhappiness and stops gratitude for one's own blessings [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Do not wish for that by which Allah has made some of you exceed others" (Qur'an 4:32) [2].
- "Do not envy one another, do not hate one another ... and be, O servants of Allah, brothers" (Sahih Muslim) [2].

**11.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- Faith (iman) in Islam includes **conduct**, not only belief and worship.
- **Neighbours see a person's daily character** in a way others do not, so treatment of them is a true test.
- The Hadith on Jibril (AS) stressing the neighbour shows how central the duty is.
- Qur'an 4:36 places neighbours in the same verse as worship of Allah and kindness to parents.
- **Against the statement:** faith is also shown in Salah, sincerity and love of Allah, which a neighbour cannot see; a person may have difficult neighbours and still be a sincere believer; only Allah knows the strength of anyone's faith.
- **Balanced view:** treatment of neighbours is one clear outward sign of faith, not the only measure.

*Use of the passage*

- "By Allah, he does not believe!", repeated, shows how serious harming a neighbour is.
- The companions' question "Who is that?" shows the surprise that such a link could be made.
- "One whose neighbour does not feel safe" sets the minimum standard: safety, not just politeness.
- "From his evil" covers words and actions, so backbiting and spying are included.

**12.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** the prescribed Hadith makes safety from harm the test of faith; scholars state the principle that preventing harm comes before bringing benefit; backbiting, arrogance and jealousy are all forbidden because they harm others.
- **Against the statement:** Islam also commands active good: gifts to neighbours, charity, visiting the sick; Qur'an 4:36 tells Muslims to "do good" to neighbours, not merely leave them alone; a Muslim who never harms but never helps falls short.
- **Balanced view:** avoiding harm is the foundation; doing good is what builds on it.

*Use of the passage*

- "Does not feel safe from his evil" shows that harm-avoidance is the minimum requirement.
- The strength of "he does not believe" suggests that harming others is worse than failing to help them.
- The passage speaks only of harm, so other sources are needed to show the duty of active kindness.

An answer that explains a range of points, reasons about their implications and uses the passage directly reaches Level 4.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining backbiting as lying.
- Treating amanah as only money.
- Confusing hasad with ghibtah.
- Giving the rule without its social impact.
- In the 12-mark questions, not using the prescribed Hadith or giving only one side.
