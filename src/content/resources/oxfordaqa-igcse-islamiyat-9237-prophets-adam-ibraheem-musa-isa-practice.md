## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** The 1- and 2-mark questions give one mark for each correct answer.
The 4-mark questions give one mark for each point identified and one more for explaining it, up to
two marks for each of the two. The 6-mark questions award those four AO1 marks plus up to two AO2
marks for applying a source of authority: **one** mark for a general, accurate use, **two** marks
for a specific, accurate use applied directly to the question. The 12-mark questions are marked by
levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent use of the passage, applied directly |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

Other correct answers of the same quality receive the same credit.

### Section A

**1.** The Tawrat (Torah) [1].

**2.** Khalilullah [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: created from clay; taught the names of all things; the angels prostrated to him; Iblis refused to prostrate; he lived in the Garden; he ate from the forbidden tree after Shaytan made him slip; he repented and was forgiven; he was sent to live on earth as the first *khalifah*.

**4.** Any two, [1] each (Qur'an 3:49): making a bird from clay that became a living bird; healing a man born blind; healing the leper; bringing the dead to life. Speaking from the cradle may also be credited.

**5.** For each of two roles: role identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Messenger to Pharaoh** [1]: he and Harun (AS) were sent to call Pharaoh to Allah and to demand freedom for the Children of Israel [1].
- **Leader of the Children of Israel** [1]: he led them out of Egypt and through the parted sea to safety [1].
- **Receiver of the Tawrat** [1]: he brought Allah's law to his people as "guidance and light" [1].
- **Kalimullah** [1]: Allah spoke to him directly, which gave his mission special authority [1].

**6.** For each of two lessons: lesson identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Worship Allah alone** [1]: his reasoning that the stars, moon and sun set shows that created things cannot be gods [1].
- **Courage in faith** [1]: he broke the idols and faced the fire, so Muslims should stand by Tawhid even under pressure [1].
- **Truth above family loyalty** [1]: he challenged his own father, showing that belief comes before custom [1].
- **Trust in Allah's protection** [1]: the fire was made "coolness and safety" for him, so Muslims rely on Allah in danger [1].

**7.** For each of two beliefs: belief identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Born without a father** [1]: the angel told Maryam she would have a son though no man had touched her, showing Allah's power [1].
- **Created by Allah's command** [1]: like Adam (AS) he was created by the word "Be" (3:59), so his birth is a miracle but not a sign of divinity [1].
- **He spoke from the cradle** [1]: as a baby he defended his mother and declared that he was Allah's servant and a prophet [1].
- **Maryam was honoured** [1]: she was chosen and purified by Allah, and a surah is named after her [1].

**8.** For each of two ways: way identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Humans are stewards** [1]: Adam was made *khalifah*, so humans are responsible for caring for the earth [1].
- **Humans can repent** [1]: Adam's repentance was accepted (2:37), so Muslims believe people are not born sinful and can always return to Allah [1].
- **Knowledge is a gift** [1]: Adam was taught the names, so learning is part of being human and honoured by Allah [1].
- **All humans are equal** [1]: everyone descends from Adam, so no race or tribe is superior [1].

### Section B

**9.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, the reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- **Example of courage against injustice** [1]: he confronted Pharaoh, so Muslims learn to speak out against oppression in their own societies [1].
- **Example of trust in Allah** [1]: trapped between the sea and Pharaoh's army, he relied on Allah, so Muslims trust Allah in hardship [1].
- **Shows the value of revealed law** [1]: the Tawrat guided his people, so Muslims see that Allah guides through law as well as belief [1].
- **Linked to worship** [1]: Muslims fast on Ashura because Allah saved Musa (AS) on that day [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- When his people feared capture, Musa (AS) said, "Indeed, with me is my Lord; He will guide me" (Qur'an 26:62), so Muslims learn that faith gives strength in danger [2].
- "Allah spoke to Moses with [direct] speech" (Qur'an 4:164), showing the honour of his prophethood [2].

**10.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 9. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 9.

Reasons (AO1):

- **His own words** [1]: from the cradle he said he was the servant of Allah, which is a claim to prophethood, not divinity [1].
- **Miracles came from Allah** [1]: each miracle was "by permission of Allah", so the power was Allah's, not his own [1].
- **His creation was like Adam's** [1]: a birth without a father does not make him divine, because Adam (AS) had neither father nor mother [1].
- **Tawhid** [1]: Allah has no partner or son (Surah 112), so a divine Isa would contradict the oneness of Allah [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Indeed, I am the servant of Allah. He has given me the Scripture and made me a prophet" (Qur'an 19:30), so Isa (AS) himself denies being more than a prophet [2].
- "Indeed, the example of Jesus to Allah is like that of Adam" (Qur'an 3:59) [2].

**11.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- He is the **Seal of the Prophets** (33:40), so his message is final and complete for all people.
- His **Sunnah** is recorded in detail, so Muslims can follow him in worship and daily life in a way not possible for earlier prophets.
- The **Qur'an** revealed to him is preserved, whereas Muslims believe earlier books were not preserved in their original form.
- Against the statement: Muslims "make no distinction" between messengers (2:285) in belief; all brought Tawhid and all must be believed in. Rejecting any one of them is rejecting Islam.
- A balanced view: all prophets are equally believed in, but Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh) is the one Muslims follow in practice.

*Use of the passage*

- "A Messenger from among yourselves" shows he was human, like Adam, Ibraheem, Musa and Isa (AS), so every prophet shares the same human nature.
- "Grievous to him is what you suffer" shows his deep care for his community, a reason Muslims love and follow him.
- "To the believers is kind and merciful" connects him with the quality of mercy that Muslims try to copy.
- "Sufficient for me is Allah ... On Him I have relied" shows that, like Ibraheem (AS) in the fire and Musa (AS) at the sea, he relied on Allah alone, so his importance lies in pointing to Allah.

**12.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- Belief in all the messengers is one of the **key beliefs**; the passage lists it alongside belief in Allah, angels and books.
- Each story gives a **model of character**: repentance (Adam), submission (Ibraheem), courage (Musa), humility (Isa).
- The stories are **lived out in practice**: Hajj and Eid-ul-Adha recall Ibraheem's family; Ashura recalls Musa (AS).
- They show that Islam is **not a new religion** but the same message of Tawhid brought to every people.
- Against the statement: Muslims follow the Shariah brought by Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh), not the laws of earlier prophets, so the stories teach lessons rather than rules.

*Use of the passage*

- "All of them have believed in Allah and His angels and His books and His messengers" shows that believing in the earlier prophets is a condition of faith, not an optional extra.
- "We make no distinction between any of His messengers" shows that Muslims honour all prophets equally in belief, so their stories carry real authority.
- "The Messenger has believed in what was revealed to him" shows that the final prophet himself believed in those who came before him.

An answer that explains a range of points, reasons about their implications and uses the passage directly reaches Level 4.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Matching the wrong book to the wrong prophet.
- Telling a story in a 4- or 6-mark answer without saying what it teaches.
- Giving two points that are really the same point in different words.
- Describing Isa (AS) as crucified or divine.
- In the 12-mark questions, ignoring the passage or giving only one point of significance.
