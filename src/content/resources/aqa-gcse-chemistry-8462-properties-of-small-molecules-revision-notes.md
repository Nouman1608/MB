statements and no required practical. For full explanations, see the
[properties of small molecules study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-properties-of-small-molecules/);
then practise with the

## Core facts

- Substances made of **small molecules** are usually **gases or liquids**, with relatively **low melting points
  and boiling points**.
- There are only **weak forces between the molecules**: **intermolecular forces**.
- When the substance melts or boils, the **intermolecular forces are overcome, not the covalent bonds**.
- Intermolecular forces **increase with the size of the molecules**, so **larger molecules have higher melting
  and boiling points**.
- Small molecules **do not conduct electricity**, because the molecules **have no overall electric charge**.
- Intermolecular forces are **weak compared with covalent bonds**.

## Two kinds of attraction

| | Covalent bond | Intermolecular force |
|---|---|---|
| Where | Between atoms inside a molecule | Between molecules |
| Strength | Strong | Weak |
| Overcome on melting or boiling? | No | Yes |

## Trends to know

| Substance | Boiling point / °C | State at 20 °C |
|---|---|---|
| Chlorine, Cl₂ | -34 | Gas |
| Bromine, Br₂ | 59 | Liquid |
| Iodine, I₂ | 184 | Solid |

| Alkane | Boiling point / °C |
|---|---|
| Methane, CH₄ | -162 |
| Ethane, C₂H₆ | -89 |
| Propane, C₃H₈ | -42 |
| Butane, C₄H₁₀ | -1 |
| Pentane, C₅H₁₂ | 36 |

The boiling points rise as molecular size rises. You do not have to learn the values. You must be able to
describe a trend from data.

## Writing an explanation

1. **Name the particles:** small molecules.
2. **Name the forces overcome:** weak intermolecular forces.
3. **Say the bonds do not break:** the covalent bonds stay intact.
4. **Link to energy:** little energy is needed, so the melting or boiling point is low.
5. **For a comparison:** bigger molecules, stronger intermolecular forces, more energy, higher boiling point.

## Why no conduction

| Needed to conduct | Small molecules have it? |
|---|---|
| Ions free to move | No |
| Delocalised electrons | No |
| Overall electric charge | No, molecules are neutral |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Small molecule | A molecule made of a few atoms joined by covalent bonds |
| Intermolecular force | A weak force of attraction between separate molecules |
| Covalent bond | A strong bond within a molecule, from a shared pair of electrons |
| Boiling point | The temperature at which a liquid boils |
| Volatile | Easily changed to a gas; low boiling point |

## Quick comparison of structure types

| Structure | Particles | Forces overcome on melting | Typical melting point | Conducts? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small molecules | Molecules | Weak intermolecular | Low | No |
| Ionic | Ions | Strong ionic bonds | High | When molten or dissolved |
| Giant covalent | Atoms | Strong covalent bonds | Very high | Usually no |
| Metal | Atoms | Strong metallic bonds | Usually high | Yes |

## Worked examples in brief

**Example 1.** "Explain why oxygen is a gas at room temperature." Oxygen is made of small molecules. There are weak
intermolecular forces between them. Little energy is needed to separate them, so the boiling point (-183 °C) is
far below room temperature.

**Example 2.** "Explain why bromine boils at a higher temperature than chlorine." Bromine molecules are larger than
chlorine molecules. The intermolecular forces are stronger, so more energy is needed to overcome them.

**Example 3.** "Does ethane contain bonds that break when it boils?" The covalent bonds within each ethane molecule
do not break. The intermolecular forces between molecules are overcome.

## Where small molecules appear in other topics

- **Crude oil:** fractional distillation separates hydrocarbons by boiling point. Larger molecules boil at higher
  temperatures.
- **The atmosphere:** nitrogen, oxygen, carbon dioxide and water vapour are small molecules and are gases.
- **Fuels and solvents:** many are small-molecule liquids that evaporate easily.

## Exam traps

- **"The covalent bonds are weak."** Covalent bonds are strong. The weak part is the force **between** molecules.
- **"The bonds break when it boils."** Only the intermolecular forces are overcome.
- **"Small molecules conduct because electrons are shared."** Shared electrons are held between atoms.
- **"Iodine is not a small molecule because it is solid."** Iodine is made of small I₂ molecules and melts
  easily.
- **Forgetting the size link.** If a question gives different sizes, use them.
- **Describing a trend without data.** Quote at least two values if the question gives a table.

## Quick self-test

1. What are the forces between molecules called?
2. Are these forces strong or weak compared with covalent bonds?
3. What is overcome when liquid water boils?
4. What happens to boiling point as molecules get larger?
5. Why do small molecules not conduct electricity?
6. Methane has a boiling point of -162 °C. What state is it at 20 °C?
7. Iodine boils at 184 °C and melts at 114 °C. What state is it at 20 °C?
8. Explain why propane boils at a higher temperature than methane.
9. A liquid has a boiling point of 35 °C and does not conduct. What type of structure does it probably have?
10. Why is the statement "iodine is a solid, so it must be giant" wrong?

### Answers

1. Intermolecular forces.
2. Weak.
3. The intermolecular forces between the water molecules.
4. It rises (larger molecules have higher boiling points).
5. The molecules have no overall electric charge and there are no free ions or electrons.
6. Gas.
7. Solid, because 20 °C is below its melting point.
8. Propane molecules are larger, so the intermolecular forces are stronger and more energy is needed.
9. Small molecules.
10. Iodine is made of small I₂ molecules held by weak forces. It is a solid only because its larger molecules have stronger forces than gases like chlorine.
