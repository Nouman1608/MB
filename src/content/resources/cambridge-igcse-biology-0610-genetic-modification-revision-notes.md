These notes condense section 21.3, Genetic modification, of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Statements 21.3.1 and 21.3.2 are Core. Statements 21.3.3 and 21.3.4 are Supplement ("Extended only"), tested only on Papers 2 and 4. For explanations and a model answer, read the [section 21.3 study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-genetic-modification/); to practise, use the [section 21.3 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-genetic-modification-practice/).

## Tier map

- **Core:** the definition of GM (21.3.1); the four examples (21.3.2).
- **Extended only:** the six-step process for a human protein (21.3.3); advantages and disadvantages of GM crops, including soya, maize and rice (21.3.4).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Genetic modification | Changing the genetic material of an organism by removing, changing or inserting individual genes |
| Gene | Length of DNA that codes for a protein |
| Restriction enzyme | Enzyme that cuts DNA at a particular point, leaving sticky ends |
| Sticky ends | Short single-stranded lengths of DNA left after cutting |
| Complementary sticky ends | Sticky ends whose bases pair with each other, made by cutting with the same restriction enzyme |
| DNA ligase | Enzyme that joins DNA strands together |
| Plasmid | Small circular loop of DNA in bacteria, separate from the main circular DNA |
| Recombinant plasmid | Plasmid containing inserted DNA from another organism |
| Expression | Using a gene to make the protein it codes for |
| Herbicide | Chemical that kills weeds |
| Insecticide | Chemical that kills insects |

## Definition: three parts

1. Changing the **genetic material** of an organism
2. by **removing, changing or inserting**
3. **individual genes**.

## The four examples (Core)

| Gene goes into | New feature | Crop named in the syllabus discussion |
|---|---|---|
| Bacteria | Make a human protein (for example insulin) | — |
| Crop plant | Resistance to herbicides | Soya |
| Crop plant | Resistance to insect pests | Maize |
| Crop plant | Improved nutritional qualities (for example beta-carotene for vitamin A) | Rice |

Spotting the type: *survives spraying* → herbicide resistance; *fewer insects feed on it* → pest resistance; *contains a new nutrient* → nutritional improvement; *bacteria make a human substance* → human protein.

## The six steps (Extended only)

| Step | What happens | Key words for the mark |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | Human gene cut out | **restriction enzymes**, **sticky ends** |
| (b) | Plasmid cut open | **same** restriction enzymes, **complementary** sticky ends |
| (c) | Gene joined into plasmid | **DNA ligase**, **recombinant plasmid** |
| (d) | Plasmid into bacteria | recombinant plasmids **inserted into bacteria** (no detail needed) |
| (e) | Bacteria grow | bacteria **multiply** |
| (f) | Protein made | bacteria **express** the human gene, make the **human protein** |

**Memory line:** cut, cut the same, join with ligase, insert, multiply, express.

**Why the same enzyme?** It leaves sticky ends on the plasmid with bases complementary to those on the gene, so they pair up.

## GM crops (Extended only)

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Herbicide-resistant soya: weeds sprayed, crop survives, less competition, higher yield | GM seed can be expensive; may need buying each season |
| Insect-resistant maize: less crop lost, higher yield | Inserted genes may spread to wild relatives by pollen, giving herbicide-resistant weeds |
| Insect-resistant maize: less insecticide sprayed, lower cost, less harm to other insects | Fewer weeds and insects, so reduced biodiversity |
| Nutritionally improved rice: reduces deficiency where rice is a main food | Pests may become resistant to the toxin over time |
| More food from the same land | Less genetic variation if one variety is grown widely |
| | Some people are concerned about health effects such as allergies (evidence of harm is limited) |

**Structure for a "discuss" answer:** named crop and gene purpose → advantage with reason → disadvantage with reason → repeat → short weighed conclusion.

## Selective breeding versus GM

| Selective breeding | Genetic modification |
|---|---|
| Choose parents with wanted features | Change or insert a specific gene directly |
| Many generations | Within one generation |
| Only within a species, or closely related ones | Gene can come from a different species |

## Exam traps

- **Cut versus join:** restriction enzymes cut; DNA ligase joins.
- **"Same" restriction enzyme:** leave it out and lose the mark.
- **Recombinant plasmid**, not recombinant gene or recombinant bacterium.
- **No nucleus** in bacteria; the gene goes into a plasmid.
- **Herbicide** (weeds) versus **insecticide** (insects).
- **Health risks** are a concern some people raise, not a proven effect.
- **Both sides** are needed when GM crops are discussed.
- **Individual genes** in the definition, not "DNA" alone.

## Quick self-test

1. Define genetic modification.
2. Give the four Core examples of genetic modification.
3. What feature does a herbicide-resistant crop have?
4. Which crop in the syllabus is linked with insect resistance?
5. What is a sticky end? (Extended)
6. Why must the plasmid be cut with the same restriction enzyme as the gene? (Extended)
7. Which enzyme joins the gene into the plasmid? (Extended)
8. What is a recombinant plasmid? (Extended)
9. What does "express the gene" mean? (Extended)
10. Give one advantage of insect-resistant maize. (Extended)
11. Explain how growing herbicide-resistant crops could lead to herbicide-resistant weeds. (Extended)
12. State one way in which GM differs from selective breeding.

### Answers

1. Changing the genetic material of an organism by removing, changing or inserting individual genes.
2. Human genes into bacteria to make human proteins; genes into crops for herbicide resistance; for insect pest resistance; to improve nutritional qualities.
3. It survives being sprayed with herbicide, so weeds can be killed without killing the crop.
4. Maize.
5. A short single-stranded length of DNA left after cutting with a restriction enzyme.
6. So the sticky ends are complementary and pair with those on the gene.
7. DNA ligase.
8. A plasmid that contains DNA from another organism, such as a human gene.
9. The bacterium uses the gene to make the protein it codes for.
10. Less insecticide needed (or: less crop lost to pests, so higher yield).
11. Pollen carrying the resistance gene may fertilise wild relatives, which then inherit the gene and survive spraying.
12. GM changes or inserts a specific gene directly, possibly from another species, within one generation; selective breeding chooses parents over many generations.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving "individual genes" out of the definition.
- Swapping restriction enzyme and ligase.
- Missing "same" or "complementary".
- Describing step (d) in great detail when no detail is required, while leaving out (e) and (f).
- Mixing up herbicide and insecticide.
- One-sided GM crop answers.
