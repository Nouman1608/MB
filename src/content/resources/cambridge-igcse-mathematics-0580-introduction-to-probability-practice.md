These questions cover subtopic 8.1, Introduction to probability, in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. Questions without a label use content common to Core (C8.1) and Extended (E8.1). Parts labelled **(Extended)** use probability notation, which is E8.1 only. All questions can be done without a calculator. Give probabilities as fractions, decimals or percentages. Learn the methods first with the [introduction to probability study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-introduction-to-probability/) and the [introduction to probability revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-introduction-to-probability-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** A letter is chosen at random from the word PROBABILITY.

**(a)** Find the probability that it is the letter I. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the probability that it is not the letter B. **[1]**

**2.** The probability that it rains on a particular day is 0.27. Find the probability that it does not rain on that day. **[1]**

**3.** A fair six-sided dice is rolled once. Write down the probability that it shows

**(a)** a number less than 7, **[1]**

**(b)** the number 0. **[1]**

**4.** A spinner can land on A, B, C or D. P(A) = 0.15 and P(B) = 0.4. Landing on C and landing on D are equally likely. Find the probability of landing on D. **[2]**

**5.** (Extended) For an event A, P(A) = 3/8. Write down P(A′). **[1]**

**6.** A whole number from 1 to 20 is chosen at random. Find the probability that it is

**(a)** a square number, **[1]**

**(b)** a multiple of 3 or a multiple of 5. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** A bar chart shows the shoe sizes of 30 students: size 4, 3 students; size 5, 7 students; size 6, 9 students; size 7, 6 students; size 8, 5 students. One student is chosen at random. Find the probability that the student's shoe size is

**(a)** 6, **[1]**

**(b)** larger than 5, **[2]**

**(c)** not 8. **[1]**

**8.** A circular spinner has four sectors. The angles are A 120°, B 90°, C x° and D 2x°.

**(a)** Find x and the probability of landing on D. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the probability of not landing on A. **[1]**

**9.** 120 students each study exactly one of Art or Music.

| | Art | Music |
|---|---|---|
| Year 10 | 35 | 25 |
| Year 11 | 28 | 32 |

One of the 120 students is chosen at random. Find the probability that the student

**(a)** is in Year 11, **[1]**

**(b)** is a Year 10 student who studies Music, **[1]**

**(c)** studies Art. **[1]**

**10.** A bag contains 8 yellow counters and some green counters only. The probability of taking a yellow counter at random is 2/7.

**(a)** Find the number of green counters. **[2]**

**(b)** More yellow counters are added until the probability of taking a yellow counter is 3/5. How many yellow counters are added? **[3]**

**11.** In a group of 40 people, 12 drink only tea, 7 drink tea and coffee, 15 drink only coffee and the rest drink neither. One person is chosen at random. T is the event that the person drinks tea.

**(a)** Find the probability that the person drinks coffee. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the probability that the person drinks exactly one of the two drinks. **[1]**

**(c)** (Extended) Find P(T′). **[1]**

**12.** A biased four-sided spinner is numbered 1 to 4. The probabilities are P(1) = 0.1, P(2) = 2x, P(3) = x + 0.15 and P(4) = 0.3.

**(a)** Find x, and hence P(2) and P(3). **[3]**

**(b)** Find the probability that the spinner lands on an odd number. **[1]**

**13.** A box contains n balls. 3 are red and the rest are white. Two white balls are removed, and the probability of taking a red ball at random from the box is now 1/4. Find n. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** PROBABILITY has 11 letters, 2 of them I: **2/11** [1]

**(b)** 2 of the letters are B, so 9 are not: **9/11** [1]

**2.** 1 − 0.27 = **0.73** [1]

**3. (a)** **1** (certain) [1]

**(b)** **0** (impossible) [1]

**4.** P(C) + P(D) = 1 − 0.15 − 0.4 = 0.45 [1]; P(D) = 0.45 ÷ 2 = **0.225** [1]

**5.** **5/8** [1]

**6. (a)** 1, 4, 9, 16: 4/20 = **1/5** [1]

**(b)** 3, 5, 6, 9, 10, 12, 15, 18, 20 [1]; **9/20** [1]

*Examiner insight:* 15 is a multiple of both 3 and 5. Counting it twice gives 10/20, the most common wrong answer.

**7. (a)** 9/30 = **3/10** [1]

**(b)** Sizes 6, 7 and 8: 9 + 6 + 5 = 20 [1]; 20/30 = **2/3** [1]

**(c)** 25/30 = **5/6** [1]

**8. (a)** 120 + 90 + 3x = 360, so x = 50 [1]; angle D = 100° [1]; P(D) = 100/360 = **5/18** [1]

**(b)** 240/360 = **2/3** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the four sectors are not equally likely, so an answer of 1/4 for any sector scores nothing.

**9. (a)** 28 + 32 = 60; 60/120 = **1/2** [1]

**(b)** **25/120 = 5/24** [1]

**(c)** 35 + 28 = 63; **63/120 = 21/40** [1]

**10. (a)** 8 counters are 2/7 of the total, so the total is 28 [1]; green = 28 − 8 = **20** [1]

**(b)** Let the new number of yellow be y: y ÷ (y + 20) = 3/5 [1]; 5y = 3y + 60, so y = 30 [1]; counters added = 30 − 8 = **22** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the green counters do not change, but the total does. Writing (8 + k) ÷ 28 = 3/5 forgets that the total increases.

**11. (a)** People who drink coffee: 7 + 15 = 22; **22/40 = 11/20** [1]

**(b)** 12 + 15 = 27; **27/40** [1]

**(c)** Tea drinkers: 12 + 7 = 19, so P(T′) = 1 − 19/40 = **21/40** [1]

**12. (a)** 0.1 + 2x + x + 0.15 + 0.3 = 1, so 3x + 0.55 = 1 [1]; x = 0.15 [1]; **P(2) = 0.3, P(3) = 0.3** [1]

**(b)** P(1) + P(3) = 0.1 + 0.3 = **0.4** [1]

**13.** After removing two white balls there are n − 2 balls, 3 of them red [1]; 3 ÷ (n − 2) = 1/4, so n − 2 = 12 [1]; **n = 14** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing probabilities as "2 out of 11" or as a ratio.
- Treating unequal sectors or groups as equally likely.
- Counting an outcome twice in an "or" question.
- Using the wrong total from a two-way table or Venn diagram.
- Forgetting that adding or removing items changes the total.
