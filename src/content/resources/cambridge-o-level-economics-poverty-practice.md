## Questions

**1.** Define absolute poverty. **[2]**

**2.** Seven households in Country S have these incomes per person per month: 25, 48, 60, 72, 95, 130 and 250. The basic basket costs 55 per person per month. Here the relative poverty line is set at 60% of the median, and a household is poor if it falls below it.

**(a)** State the median income. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the relative poverty line. **[1]**
**(c)** Identify the households in absolute poverty and those in relative poverty. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why a country can have more households below the absolute line than below the relative line. **[2]**

**3.** Explain two causes of poverty, one of which must be illness. **[4]**

**4.** In Country S a household of three has one earner on 1.8 an hour. The absolute poverty line is 70 per person per month. Use 4 weeks to a month.

**(a)** The earner works 35 hours a week. Calculate the household income per person per month and state whether the household is in absolute poverty. **[3]**
**(b)** Hours are cut to 25 a week. Calculate the new income per person and the percentage fall in household income. **[2]**

**5.** In Country T real GDP rises by 6.5% and population by 2.5%.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage rise in real GDP per head (one decimal place). **[2]**
**(b)** The income of the poorest fifth of households rises 3% while the median income rises 6.5%. Explain what happens to relative poverty. **[3]**

**6.** Country U (illustrative) charges a monthly income tax: 0% on the first 300, 10% on income from 300 to 1,000, and 30% on income above 1,000.

**(a)** Calculate the tax paid by households with incomes of 250, 800 and 2,000. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the average tax rate for each and state whether the tax is progressive. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate how many times larger the 2,000 income is than the 250 income before tax and after tax. **[2]**
**(d)** Evaluate the use of this tax to reduce poverty. **[3]**

**7.** A worker on state benefit takes a job and earns 120 more a month, but the benefit falls by 78. Calculate the extra income kept and the withdrawal rate, and explain why a high withdrawal rate may keep people in poverty. **[4]**

**8.** A training course costs 3,000 and raises a worker's earnings by 125 a month.

**(a)** Calculate the payback period in years. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why improved education may not reduce poverty quickly for the poorest households. **[3]**

**9.** The labour market for unskilled workers in Country V (illustrative) has demand for workers = 700 - 100W and supply of workers = 100 + 100W, where W is the hourly wage. A national minimum wage of 3.5 is introduced.

**(a)** Find the equilibrium wage and employment, then employment and the supply of workers at 3.5. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the response of employment to the wage rise, using percentage changes. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the change in the total wage bill of the workers employed, in per cent to one decimal place. **[2]**
**(d)** Discuss whether the minimum wage is a good way to reduce poverty. **[4]**

**10.** In Country W, 600,000 people live below the absolute poverty line. A survey finds that 50% of them are elderly, 30% are in households with an unemployed adult and 20% are in work but low paid. The government can spend on only one of: higher state pensions, an unemployment programme with retraining, or a higher minimum wage. Calculate the number in each group, then discuss which policy to choose. **[8]**

**11.** In Country X the price of the basic basket rises 8% in a year, from 80 per person per month. A household's income per person rises 5% from 82. Calculate the new poverty line and income, and explain how the household can be poorer in real terms even though its income has risen. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Absolute poverty is when a person or household lacks the income to buy the minimum needed to live, such as food, water, shelter and clothing [1], measured against a fixed standard, the cost of a basic basket [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name what is lacking and say the standard is fixed; that is what a good definition contains. "Being very poor" restates the question and gives nothing to credit.

**2. (a)** The income in the middle of seven ranked values is **72** [1].
**(b)** 0.6 x 72 = **43.2** [1].
**(c)** Absolute (below 55): households on 25 and 48 [1]. Relative (below 43.2): only the household on 25 [1].
**(d)** The absolute line is a fixed basket cost, while the relative line depends on the median [1]. If the basket costs more than 60% of the median, as here (55 against 43.2), the absolute line is higher and catches more households [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Rank the incomes before choosing the median, then test each household against each line.

**3.** Illness: treatment costs money and sickness reduces hours worked, so household income falls [1]. Income per person drops below the poverty line, and a sick child may miss school, which lowers later earnings [1]. Second cause, for example age: older people stop working and live on a small pension [1], so their income per person is below the line, especially if prices keep rising [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Take each cause through to lower household income and then to the poverty line. Two names with no chains are a list, not an explanation.

**4. (a)** Monthly income = 1.8 x 35 x 4 = 252 [1]. Per person = 252 / 3 = **84** [1]. This is above 70, so **not in absolute poverty** [1].
**(b)** New income = 1.8 x 25 x 4 = 180, so per person = **60**, below 70 [1]. Percentage fall = (180 - 252) / 252 x 100 = **-28.6%** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the number of people before comparing with a per-person line. Comparing household income with the line is the commonest slip.

**5. (a)** 1.065 / 1.025 - 1 [1] = **3.9%** [1].
**(b)** The poorest fifth's income rises 3%, less than the median's 6.5% [1], so the relative line (a fraction of the median) rises faster than their incomes [1]. More of them fall further below it, so **relative poverty rises** even though their incomes grew, and absolute poverty may fall [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A conclusion about relative poverty must compare growth rates, including that of the median.

**6. (a)** 250: 0 [1]. 800: 0.10 x 500 = **50** [1]. 2,000: 0.10 x 700 + 0.30 x 1,000 = 70 + 300 = **370** [1].
**(b)** Average rates: 0%, 50 / 800 = **6.25%**, 370 / 2,000 = **18.5%** [1]. Richer households lose a larger share of income, so the tax is **progressive** [1].
**(c)** Before tax: 2,000 / 250 = **8.0 times** [1]. After tax: (2,000 - 370) / 250 = 1,630 / 250 = **6.52 times** [1].
**(d)** Valid points: it narrows gaps, from 8.0 to 6.52 times, and raises 420 of revenue to fund benefits and services [1]. Limit: high rates may reduce effort or encourage avoidance, and the effect depends on how the revenue is spent [1]. Judgement: it works best with spending on the poor, since the tax alone does not raise the lowest incomes [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each band's tax separately, then add. An evaluation should end with a reasoned judgement.

**7.** Income kept = 120 - 78 = **42** [1]. Withdrawal rate = 78 / 120 x 100 = **65%** [1]. A worker keeps only 35 of each extra 100 earned [1], so the reward for working more is small, and some may stay on benefit and remain poor [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the rate as a percentage, then interpret it.

**8. (a)** Annual gain = 125 x 12 = 1,500 [1]. Payback = 3,000 / 1,500 = **2 years** [1].
**(b)** The course has a cost, and the poorest may not afford fees, books or transport [1]. Children may need to earn, so they leave school early [1]. Skills take years to earn higher pay, so incomes stay low in the short run [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Education is a long-run policy, so mention timing, and say why the poorest in particular are held back.

**9. (a)** Equilibrium: 700 - 100W = 100 + 100W gives W = **3** and employment = **400** [1]. At 3.5, demand = 700 - 350 = **350** [1] and supply = 100 + 350 = **450**, so 100 workers are without a job [1].
**(b)** Wage change = 0.5 / 3 = 16.7%. Employment change = (350 - 400) / 400 = -12.5% [1]. Response = 12.5 / 16.7 = **0.75** [1].
**(c)** Old bill = 3 x 400 = 1,200; new bill = 3.5 x 350 = 1,225 [1]. Change = 25 / 1,200 = **+2.1%** [1].
**(d)** For: 350 workers earn more, and the bill rises since the response is below 1 [1]. Against: 50 lose their jobs and 100 more seek jobs that are not there [1]. Firms may raise prices or cut hours [1]. Judgement: a modest minimum helps most low-paid workers, but it does not help the unemployed or the elderly, so it works best alongside other policies [1]. **[11]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the figures in the discussion, and say who gains and who loses.

**10.** Elderly: 0.5 x 600,000 = **300,000**; unemployed households: 0.3 x 600,000 = **180,000**; low paid: 0.2 x 600,000 = **120,000** [1]. Pensions target the largest group (half of those in poverty) [1]. Pensions give fast, direct cash and the elderly cannot be retrained [1], but the cost to taxpayers is high and growing as the population ages [1]. The unemployment programme helps 180,000 and tackles the cause, so it breaks the cycle [1], but it is slower and some will not find jobs [1]. The minimum wage helps only the 120,000 in work and may cut jobs [1]. Judgement: pensions, because they reach the biggest group fastest, and add retraining later [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Calculate the group sizes first, then link each policy to the group it fits.

**11.** New line = 80 x 1.08 = **86.4** [1]. New income = 82 x 1.05 = **86.1** [1]. Income rose, but less than the cost of necessities [1], so the household is now below the line (86.1 < 86.4) and can buy fewer necessities than before [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Compare the new income with the new line, not the old one. Real income depends on prices as well as money income.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining poverty as "having no money" with no mention of necessities or a standard.
- Comparing household income with a per-person poverty line.
- Using the mean instead of the median for the relative line.
- Saying equal growth cuts relative poverty.
- Writing causes as one-word lists with no chain to lower income.
- Calculating a tax as one rate on the whole income instead of by band.
- Giving evaluation with no figures and no final judgement.
