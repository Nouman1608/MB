## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. The
4-mark "describe" question gives one mark for each relevant, accurate detail; the 4-mark "explain"
question gives one mark for each point and one for explaining it, up to two marks for each of the
two. The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general,
accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The
12-mark questions are marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

### Section A

**1.** The Age of Ignorance, meaning ignorance of Allah's guidance [1].

**2.** The Quraysh [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: worship of idols; belief in many gods or in intermediaries to Allah; belief in omens and fortune-telling; offerings to stones and trees; a pilgrimage to Makkah mixed with idol worship; belief in Allah as creator.

**4.** Any two, [1] each: baby girls were buried alive in some tribes; women could be inherited as property; women had no fixed share of inheritance; men could marry any number of wives; women had little legal protection.

**5.** Any four, [1] each: society was divided into tribes and clans [1]; there was no central government [1]; each tribe had a chief (sheikh) [1]; the chief was chosen for age, wisdom, bravery and generosity [1]; he ruled by persuasion, with elders [1]; loyalty to the tribe came before everything [1]; people without a tribe had no protection [1].

**6.** For each of two reasons: reason identified [1] and explained [1].

- **No central law** [1]: there was no state to punish crime, so each tribe sought its own justice [1].
- **Duty of revenge** [1]: tribal honour required revenge when a member was harmed, which led to cycles of killing [1].
- **Disputes over resources** [1]: quarrels over wells and pastures in the desert could lead to war [1].
- **Tribal pride** [1]: small insults were treated as attacks on the whole tribe [1].

### Section B

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two problems, the problem identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Problems (AO1):

- **Female infanticide** [1]: some tribes buried baby girls alive from fear of poverty or shame, which showed no respect for life [1].
- **Usury** [1]: lenders charged interest that doubled a debt, trapping the poor [1].
- **Gambling and drinking** [1]: these caused quarrels, wasted money and harmed families [1].
- **Injustice to the weak** [1]: orphans, widows and slaves were cheated or mistreated [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "And do not kill your children for fear of poverty. We provide for them and for you" (Qur'an 17:31) [2].
- "And when the girl who was buried alive is asked for what sin she was killed" (Qur'an 81:8-9) [2].

**8.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** infanticide took innocent life, and its ban shows that every child has the right to live.
- **For the statement:** it raised the status of girls and women and showed that Allah, not wealth, is the provider.
- **Against the statement:** Islam also removed idol worship, usury, injustice to the poor and blood feuds; the call to one God is the foundation of all of them.
- **Against the statement:** tribal pride and class were also challenged by equality before Allah.
- **Balanced view:** the ban on infanticide is a powerful example of Islam's concern for life, but the change in belief about Allah is more basic.

*Use of the passage*

- "do not kill your children" bans the act directly.
- "for fear of poverty" shows that fear was the cause, and that the reason was wrong.
- "We provide for them and for you" shows that Allah is the provider and answers the fear.
- "a great sin" shows the seriousness of the act.

**9.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Reasons (AO1):

- **Loss of income** [1]: pilgrims came to Makkah for the idols, so the Quraysh feared losing trade [1].
- **Loss of status** [1]: they were guardians of the Ka'aba, and worship of one God would reduce their honour [1].
- **Loyalty to ancestors** [1]: they said that they were following the religion of their fathers, and feared the break with tradition [1].
- **Challenge to the social order** [1]: equality before Allah challenged their pride and the position of rich leaders [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "We only worship them that they may bring us nearer to Allah in position" (Qur'an 39:3): the Arabs clung to idols as go-betweens [2].
- "Rather, we will follow that which we found our fathers doing" (Qur'an 2:170) [2].

**10.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** infanticide, usury, gambling, idol worship, blood feuds and the neglect of orphans were serious wrongs that Islam condemned.
- **Against the statement:** the Arabs valued courage, loyalty, generosity, hospitality and keeping promises, and were skilled in poetry and trade.
- **Against the statement:** a few people, the hanifs, kept the faith of Ibrahim (AS), and pacts such as the Hilf al-Fudul defended the wronged.
- **Against the statement:** Jahiliya meant ignorance of guidance, not the absence of all good qualities; Islam built on the good and corrected the harmful.
- **Balanced view:** Arabia had real virtues, but without revelation they were mixed with serious wrongs.

*Use of the passage*

- "do not kill your children" shows a harmful practice that Islam ended.
- "We provide for them and for you" shows that Islam answered the fear that caused it with trust in Allah.
- Even in condemning the act, the passage assumes people loved their families and feared poverty, which are human feelings and not evidence of total wickedness.
