## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following is an example of external (inorganic) growth? **[1]**

A: Launching a new product after research and development

B: Opening an online shop

C: Buying a controlling share in a competitor

D: Selling to customers overseas

**2.** Define the term merger. **[2]**

**3.** Which **two** of the following are internal sources of finance? **[2]**

A: Retained profit

B: Loan capital

C: Selling assets

D: Share capital

E: Stock market flotation

**4.** State **one** risk of research and development for a business. **[1]**

**5.** Explain **one** disadvantage to the original owners of a business becoming a public limited company. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **one** reason why organic growth may be less risky than a takeover. **[3]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** Greystone Games Ltd borrows £250,000 to develop a new game. The total repayment will be £290,000.

**(a)** Calculate the interest on the loan as a percentage. **[2]**

**(b)** The game's sales revenue rises from £1.6 million to £1.84 million in its first year. Calculate the percentage growth in sales revenue. **[2]**

**8.** Brightwell Ices sells ice cream in the UK. Outline **one** way Brightwell Ices could grow by changing its marketing mix to enter a new market. **[2]**

**9.** Kelso Cleaning plc has 2,000,000 shares. It issues 500,000 new shares at £2.40 each.

**(a)** Calculate the money raised. **[1]**

**(b)** Before the issue, the founders owned 1,200,000 shares. Calculate their percentage ownership after the issue. **[2]**

**10.** Harbour Freight, a UK road haulage firm, has agreed to merge with Coastline Logistics, a rival with depots in Scotland. Analyse the impact of the merger on Harbour Freight. **[6]**

**11.** Explain **one** reason why a business might use retained profit rather than loan capital to finance growth. **[3]**

**12.** Lumen Lighting Ltd designs and sells smart light bulbs. It wants to grow and is considering two options.

Option 1: spend £400,000 on research and development of a new range, financed by a bank loan with a total repayment of £460,000. Forecast extra profit: £150,000 a year from year 2.

Option 2: take over a small competitor for £900,000, financed by selling shares that would reduce the founders' ownership from 100% to 70%. Forecast extra profit: £180,000 a year from year 1.

The founders value their independence. Justify which option Lumen Lighting Ltd should choose. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** C [1]. **[1]**

**2.** When two businesses agree [1] to join together to form one business [1]. **[2]**

**3.** A [1]. C [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any one from: the money spent may not lead to a product customers want; it takes time before any revenue is earned; rivals may launch first [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Shares in a plc can be bought by the public [1], so ownership is spread among many shareholders [1]. The original owners may then lose control of decisions, and could even be outvoted or lose the business to a takeover [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Organic growth happens gradually as the business sells more [1], so it can expand at a pace it can manage and fund [1]. A takeover involves spending a large sum at once on a business that may have hidden problems, so if it does not work, the losses are much bigger [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** Interest = £290,000 − £250,000 = £40,000; £40,000 ÷ £250,000 × 100 [1] = **16%** [1]

**(b)** Increase = £1.84m − £1.6m = £0.24m; £0.24m ÷ £1.6m × 100 [1] = **15%** [1] **[4]**

**8.** Brightwell Ices could develop a dairy-free range for customers who avoid milk (product) [1], promoting it to that group so it sells to customers who did not buy its ice cream before, increasing sales [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** 500,000 × £2.40 = **£1,200,000** [1]

**(b)** Total shares = 2,500,000 [1]. 1,200,000 ÷ 2,500,000 × 100 = **48%** [1] **[3]**

**10.** The merger gives Harbour Freight access to Coastline's depots in Scotland [1], so it can offer deliveries across more of the UK and win customers who want one firm for all their deliveries [1]. Joining with a rival also removes a competitor, which may allow it to charge slightly higher prices [1]. Running more lorries could let it buy fuel and tyres in larger amounts at lower prices [1]. However, the two firms may have different working methods and systems, which could cause confusion and lower staff motivation [1], and duplicated office roles may lead to redundancies, which could harm its reputation with staff [1]. **[6]**

**11.** Retained profit does not have to be repaid and has no interest [1]. This means the business does not take on extra fixed repayments [1], so if the growth plan takes longer to bring in revenue, the business is not put under cash-flow pressure by a lender [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Option 1 interest = £460,000 − £400,000 = £60,000, which is 15% of the amount borrowed [1]. Option 2 costs £900,000 but has no repayments [1].

For Option 1: the founders keep 100% ownership, which matters because they value independence [1]. It is organic growth, building on their own design skills [1]. However, R&D may fail, and the extra profit does not start until year 2, while loan repayments start straight away [1].

For Option 2: profit starts sooner, and at £180,000 a year it is £30,000 more than Option 1 [1]. But the founders give up 30% of the company, so 30% of all future profit goes to new shareholders [1], and taking over another business risks a clash of working methods [1].

Judgement: Lumen Lighting should choose Option 1, because it keeps the founders' independence and, after the £60,000 interest, is forecast to earn £150,000 a year with all of it going to the founders [1]. This depends on the R&D succeeding; if the new range is delayed, the loan repayments could strain cash flow. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* The founders' objective of independence is the key piece of context. A strong answer uses it to decide between two options that are close on the figures.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling opening an online shop external growth; it is organic.
- Using the original number of shares instead of the new total in Question 9.
- Writing about mergers in general in Question 10 instead of Harbour Freight's depots and lorries.
- Ignoring the founders' wish for independence in Question 12.
