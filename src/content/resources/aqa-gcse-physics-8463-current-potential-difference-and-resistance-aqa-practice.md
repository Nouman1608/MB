## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State what must be present in a circuit for charge to flow through it. **[2]**

**2.** A current of 1.2 A flows through a motor for 25 s. Calculate the charge that flows. **[2]**

**3.** A heater element has a resistance of 48 Ω. The pd across it is 12 V. Calculate the current. **[2]**

**4.** A charge of 540 C passes through a lamp at a steady current of 0.75 A. Calculate the time taken, in minutes. **[3]**

**5.** A 3.3 kΩ resistor carries a current of 2.0 mA. Calculate the pd across it. **[3]**

**6.** Complete each sentence.

**(a)** The resistance of a thermistor ______ as its temperature increases. **[1]**
**(b)** The resistance of an LDR ______ as light intensity increases. **[1]**
**(c)** A diode has a very ______ resistance in the reverse direction. **[1]**

**7.** A student draws a circuit to measure the resistance of a resistor. She puts the voltmeter in series with the resistor and the ammeter across it. Explain what is wrong and how to correct it. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**8.** The table shows readings for a component.

| pd (V) | 0 | 1.0 | 2.0 | 3.0 | 4.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current (A) | 0 | 0.20 | 0.33 | 0.41 | 0.47 |

**(a)** Calculate the resistance at 1.0 V and at 4.0 V. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest which component this is, and explain your answer using the data. **[3]**

**9.** A student investigates how the resistance of a wire depends on its length (required practical 3).

**(a)** Describe how she should obtain her results. **[4]**
**(b)** Her graph of resistance against length is a straight line that crosses the resistance axis at 0.3 Ω. Suggest a reason for the intercept. **[1]**
**(c)** At 60 cm the readings are 1.50 V and 0.50 A. Calculate the resistance of 1.0 m of the wire, assuming the intercept is removed. **[3]**

**10.** A student investigates the I–V characteristic of a diode (required practical 4).

**(a)** Explain why she includes a fixed resistor in series with the diode. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe how she obtains readings for negative values of pd. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe the shape of the graph she should expect, for both positive and negative pd. **[2]**

**11.** A thermistor is used in a thermostat that controls a heater.

**(a)** Describe how a student could investigate how the thermistor's resistance depends on temperature. **[4]**
**(b)** At 20 °C the thermistor has 6.0 V across it and 1.5 mA through it. Calculate its resistance. **[3]**
**(c)** State and explain what happens to the current through the thermistor, at the same pd, if the room warms. **[2]**

**12.** A torch lamp is connected to a 4.5 V battery and draws 0.30 A. The torch is left on for 40 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the resistance of the lamp when lit. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the charge that flows through the lamp. **[3]**
**(c)** The resistance of the same lamp measured when it is cold is much lower than your answer to (a). Explain why. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** A closed circuit (complete loop) [1]; a source of potential difference, such as a cell or battery [1]

**2.** Q = I t = 1.2 × 25 [1]; Q = **30 C** [1]

**3.** I = V ÷ R = 12 ÷ 48 [1]; I = **0.25 A** [1]

**4.** t = Q ÷ I = 540 ÷ 0.75 [1] = 720 s [1]; 720 ÷ 60 = **12 minutes** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The question asks for minutes; leaving 720 s loses the final mark.

**5.** I = 0.0020 A and R = 3300 Ω [1]; V = I R = 0.0020 × 3300 [1]; V = **6.6 V** [1]

**6. (a)** decreases [1]
**(b)** decreases [1]
**(c)** high [1]

**7.** The voltmeter must be in parallel (across the resistor) and the ammeter in series with it [1]; as drawn, the meters do not measure the current through the resistor and the pd across it, so R = V ÷ I cannot be found from them [1]

**8. (a)** At 1.0 V: R = 1.0 ÷ 0.20 = 5.0 Ω [1]; at 4.0 V: R = 4.0 ÷ 0.47 = 8.5 Ω [1]
**(b)** A filament lamp [1]; equal steps in pd give smaller and smaller increases in current, so the graph is a curve, not a straight line [1]; the resistance rises as the current rises, because the filament gets hotter [1]
*Examiner insight:* The data-based mark needs numbers or a clear pattern from the table, such as the current rising by 0.20 A, then 0.13 A, then 0.08 A.

**9. (a)** Any four from: connect the wire in series with a cell (or supply), switch and ammeter [1]; connect a voltmeter across the length of wire between the crocodile clips [1]; set the length with the clips against a metre rule and record V and I for at least five lengths [1]; calculate R = V ÷ I for each length [1]; switch off between readings or use a small current so the temperature stays constant [1]
**(b)** Extra resistance in the connecting leads or at the crocodile clips (contact resistance), a systematic error [1]
**(c)** R at 60 cm = 1.50 ÷ 0.50 = 3.0 Ω [1]; resistance of the wire alone = 3.0 − 0.3 = 2.7 Ω for 0.60 m [1]; for 1.0 m: 2.7 × (1.0 ÷ 0.60) = **4.5 Ω** [1]

**10. (a)** To limit the current, which would rise very steeply in the forward direction and could damage the diode [1]
**(b)** Reverse the connections to the diode (or to the supply) and repeat the readings [1]
**(c)** For negative pd the current is zero (or almost zero) [1]; for positive pd the current stays near zero until a small pd, then rises steeply [1]

**11. (a)** Any four from: place the thermistor in a beaker of water with a thermometer [1]; connect it in series with an ammeter and a supply, with a voltmeter across it [1]; heat the water and take V and I at a range of temperatures, for example every 10 °C [1]; stir and wait for the reading to settle before each measurement [1]; calculate R = V ÷ I at each temperature and plot R against temperature [1]
**(b)** I = 1.5 mA = 0.0015 A [1]; R = V ÷ I = 6.0 ÷ 0.0015 [1]; R = **4000 Ω** (4.0 kΩ) [1]
**(c)** The current increases [1]; because the thermistor's resistance decreases as its temperature rises, and for the same pd a smaller resistance means a larger current [1]

**12. (a)** R = V ÷ I = 4.5 ÷ 0.30 [1]; R = **15 Ω** [1]
**(b)** t = 40 × 60 = 2400 s [1]; Q = I t = 0.30 × 2400 [1]; Q = **720 C** [1]
**(c)** When lit, the filament is very hot [1]; the resistance of a filament increases as its temperature increases, so the cold filament has a lower resistance [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Because there is more current" is not an explanation; the mark is for linking resistance to the filament's temperature.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the gradient of a curved I–V graph to find resistance instead of V ÷ I at the point.
- Not converting mA to A, kΩ to Ω, or minutes and hours to seconds before substituting.
- Describing a method without saying how temperature is kept constant.
- Giving "fair test" without naming the variable controlled.
- Describing a graph shape without saying whether it passes through the origin.
