## Questions

**1.** *(no calculator)* Rearrange 4y + 3x = 12 to make y the subject, and state the gradient of the line. **[2]**

**2.** *(no calculator)* Line P is perpendicular to the line 4y + 3x = 12 and passes through (3, 2). Find the equation of line P. **[3]**

**3.** *(no calculator)* A is (−2, 5) and B is (6, −1). Find the midpoint of AB and the length of AB. **[3]**

**4.** *(no calculator)* Find the equation of the line through (1, 4) and (5, 12). **[3]**

**5.** *(no calculator)* Show that the lines y = 3x − 2 and 6x − 2y = 7 are parallel. **[2]**

---

## Answers

**1.** 4y = 12 − 3x, so **y = 3 − ¾x** [1]. Gradient = **−¾** [1].

*Tip:* the gradient is the coefficient of x once y is the subject.

*Try the real question next:* Cambridge O Level Mathematics 4024, June 2025, Paper 12, Question 17.

**2.** Gradient of P = 4/3, the negative reciprocal of −3/4 [1]. y − 2 = 4/3(x − 3) [1], so **y = 4/3 x − 2** [1].

*Tip:* for a perpendicular gradient, flip the fraction and change the sign.

*Try the real question next:* Cambridge O Level Mathematics 4024, June 2025, Paper 12, Question 17.

**3.** Midpoint = ((−2 + 6)/2, (5 − 1)/2) = **(2, 2)** [1]. Length = √(8² + 6²) [1] = **10 units** [1].

*Tip:* subtract coordinates carefully when one of them is negative.

*Try the real question next:* Cambridge O Level Mathematics 4024, June 2025, Paper 12, Question 14.

**4.** Gradient = (12 − 4) ÷ (5 − 1) = 2 [1]. y − 4 = 2(x − 1) [1], so **y = 2x + 2** [1].

*Tip:* check your equation with the second point.

*Try the real question next:* Cambridge O Level Mathematics 4024, June 2025, Paper 12, Question 17.

**5.** 6x − 2y = 7 gives y = 3x − 3.5 [1]. Both lines have gradient 3, so they are parallel [1].

*Tip:* parallel lines have equal gradients; say so in your conclusion.

*Try the real question next:* Cambridge O Level Mathematics 4024, June 2025, Paper 12, Question 17.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the reciprocal without changing the sign for a perpendicular gradient.
- Making a sign error when subtracting negative coordinates.
- Leaving y not as the subject when asked.
