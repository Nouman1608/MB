Subtopic 16.1 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702 (2025–2027) asks you to understand internal energy as a function of state, made up of the random kinetic and potential energies of molecules, and to relate a temperature rise to an increase in internal energy. These notes are condensed for recall. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [Internal energy study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-internal-energy/); to apply the ideas, try the [Internal energy practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-internal-energy-practice/).

## The definition you must be able to write

> Internal energy is the **sum** of the **random** distribution of **kinetic** and **potential** energies of the **molecules** of a system.

Five words carry the marks:

| Word | What it rules in or out |
|---|---|
| Sum | A total over all molecules, so it depends on the amount of substance, not just temperature |
| Random | Excludes ordered energy: the kinetic energy or gravitational potential energy of the object as a whole |
| Kinetic | Energy of molecular motion: translation in gases, movement past one another in liquids, vibration in solids |
| Potential | Energy due to intermolecular forces; zero at infinite separation, negative for attracting molecules close together |
| Molecules | It is a microscopic quantity, summed over particles |

## Determined by the state

- The **state** is fixed by quantities such as pressure, volume, temperature, phase and amount of substance.
- Same state means same internal energy, whatever route was taken to reach it.
- Only **changes** in internal energy (ΔU) can be found from energy transfers.
- Around a **closed cycle** (back to the starting state), ΔU = 0.
- Heating and doing work are **processes**; internal energy is a **property**. A body does not "contain heat".

## Kinetic and potential parts in each phase

| Phase | Random kinetic energy | Intermolecular potential energy |
|---|---|---|
| Solid | Vibration about fixed positions | Most negative: molecules close, in deep potential wells |
| Liquid | Molecules move past one another | Less negative: slightly further apart |
| Gas | Free, fast random motion | Close to zero: molecules far apart |
| Ideal gas | All the internal energy | Zero: no intermolecular forces between collisions |

## Temperature and internal energy

- Thermodynamic temperature is a measure of the **mean** random kinetic energy per molecule.
- **Rise in temperature → rise in mean kinetic energy → rise in internal energy.** In solids and liquids the potential energy also rises slightly, because the mean separation increases.
- The reverse does **not** always hold: during melting and boiling, U increases at **constant temperature**. The energy raises the potential energy (molecules separated against attractive forces); mean kinetic energy is unchanged.
- Boiling needs much more energy per kilogram than melting, because the molecules are separated almost completely rather than just loosened from fixed positions.

## Ideal gas equations

```
mean translational KE per molecule = (3/2)kT
monatomic ideal gas:  U = N(3/2)kT = (3/2)nRT = (3/2)pV
ΔU = (3/2)nRΔT
```

- T must be in **kelvin**.
- U ∝ T for a fixed amount of ideal gas: doubling T doubles U.
- Two different ideal gases at the same temperature have the same mean kinetic energy per molecule; the one with more molecules has more internal energy.
- (3/2)pV follows from pV = nRT, and is useful when a question gives p and V instead of T.

## Ordered energy becoming internal energy

When an object slows down because of friction or an inelastic collision, its ordered kinetic energy becomes random molecular energy. Internal energy rises and temperature rises slightly. Examples: a block sliding to rest, a ball of putty hitting the floor, brakes heating up.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Internal energy U | Sum of the random kinetic and potential energies of the molecules of a system |
| Function of state | A quantity fixed by the present state of a system, independent of its history |
| Random kinetic energy | Kinetic energy of molecular motion relative to the centre of mass of the object |
| Intermolecular potential energy | Energy due to forces between molecules; increases (less negative) as they separate |
| Thermodynamic temperature | Temperature in kelvin; proportional to the mean translational kinetic energy of molecules of an ideal gas |
| Ideal gas | A gas obeying pV ∝ T; its molecules have no potential energy, so U is all kinetic |

## Exam traps

- Defining internal energy as "kinetic energy of the molecules" with no potential energy, or as an "average".
- Leaving out the word "random".
- Including the motion or height of the whole object.
- Saying temperature rises while a substance melts.
- Giving an ideal gas potential energy.
- Using °C in (3/2)kT or (3/2)nRT.
- Assuming that equal temperatures mean equal internal energies for objects of different size.

## Self-test

1. Write a definition of internal energy that would earn full marks.
2. A sealed box of gas is lifted 2 m at constant temperature. Does its internal energy change?
3. Why does an ideal gas have no intermolecular potential energy?
4. Calculate the mean translational kinetic energy of a molecule at 400 K.
5. What happens to the mean kinetic energy and the potential energy of molecules while water boils at 100 °C?
6. Two copper blocks at 50 °C have masses 1.0 kg and 4.0 kg. Compare their internal energies and the mean kinetic energies of their molecules.
7. A fixed amount of ideal gas is heated from 200 K to 600 K. By what factor does its internal energy change?
8. Calculate the internal energy of 1.0 mol of monatomic ideal gas at 300 K.
9. A monatomic ideal gas is at 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa in a volume of 4.0 × 10⁻³ m³. Calculate its internal energy.
10. Explain why internal energy is described as a function of state.
11. A gas returns to its starting pressure, volume and temperature after a sequence of changes. What is the total ΔU?
12. In a solid being heated below its melting point, which energy components of the molecules increase?

No: the state (p, V, T) is unchanged; the gain in gravitational potential energy is ordered energy of the whole box. 3. There are no forces between the molecules except during collisions. 4. (3/2)kT = 1.5 × 1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 400 = 8.28 × 10⁻²¹ J. 5. Mean kinetic energy stays constant (temperature constant); potential energy increases as the molecules separate. 6. The 4.0 kg block has four times the internal energy; the mean kinetic energy per molecule is the same, because the temperature is the same. 7. U ∝ T, so it triples. 8.
