These questions cover section 16.2 (The roles of genes in determining the phenotype) of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. This is A Level content, examined in Paper 4. All cross results are invented for the exercise. Critical values for chi-squared at p = 0.05 are 3.84 (1 degree of freedom), 5.99 (2) and 7.82 (3). Revise first with the [16.2 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-the-roles-of-genes-in-determining-the-phenotype-cambridge/) and the [16.2 revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-the-roles-of-genes-in-determining-the-phenotype-cambridge-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** What is the purpose of a test cross?

A: To produce an F2 generation

B: To find out whether an organism showing the dominant phenotype is homozygous or heterozygous

C: To find out whether two alleles are codominant

D: To measure the rate of crossing over between two sister chromatids **[1]**

**2.** Explain the terms *codominant* and *multiple alleles*. **[2]**

**3.** A chi-squared test is used on the results of a cross expected to give four phenotype classes in a 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 ratio. How many degrees of freedom are there?

A: 1

B: 3

C: 4

D: 15 **[1]**

**4.** Explain why pea plants with the genotype lele are dwarf. **[2]**

**5.** A woman who is a carrier of haemophilia has children with a man who has haemophilia. State the probability that one of their sons has haemophilia. **[1]**

**6.** Huntington's disease often appears in every generation of a family. Explain why. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** In a species of flower, red (Cᴿ) and white (Cᵂ) alleles are codominant; heterozygotes are pink. Use a genetic diagram to show the expected results of crossing a pink plant with a white plant. **[4]**

**8.**

**(a)** A woman with blood group AB and a man with blood group O have children. Explain why none of their children can have blood group O. **[2]**

**(b)** State the possible genotypes and blood groups of their children. **[2]**

**9.** A plant heterozygous for two genes, height (T tall, t short) and seed shape (R round, r wrinkled), was crossed with a plant homozygous recessive for both genes. The offspring were:

| Tall, round | Tall, wrinkled | Short, round | Short, wrinkled |
|---|---|---|---|
| 86 | 34 | 31 | 89 |

**(a)** State the ratio expected if the genes are not linked. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the value of χ². **[2]**

**(c)** State the degrees of freedom and what the result of the test shows. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain the results. **[2]**

**10.** In a plant, a dominant allele W prevents any pigment forming, so W_ plants have white fruit. In ww plants, allele Y gives yellow fruit and yy gives green fruit. The genes are on different chromosomes.

**(a)** Explain which gene is epistatic. **[1]**

**(b)** Predict, with working, the phenotypes and ratio of offspring from WwYy × wwyy. **[3]**

**11.**

**(a)** Explain how a mutation in the HBB gene leads to the symptoms of sickle cell anaemia. **[4]**

**(b)** Two parents are both heterozygous for the sickle cell allele. State the probability that their child has sickle cell anaemia. **[1]**

**12.** A woman with normal blood clotting had a father with haemophilia. She has children with a man who has normal blood clotting.

**(a)** Explain what her genotype must be. **[2]**

**(b)** State the probability that their first child is a son with haemophilia. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]

**2.** Codominant: both alleles are expressed in the phenotype of a heterozygote [1]. Multiple alleles: a gene has more than two alleles in the population, though each individual has at most two [1].

**3.** B [1]

**4.** The le allele codes for a non-functional enzyme in the gibberellin synthesis pathway, so little active gibberellin is made [1]. Without enough gibberellin, stem internodes do not elongate, so the plant is short [1].

**5.** 0.5 (or 1 in 2, or 50 %) [1]

**6.** The allele for Huntington's disease is dominant, so one copy causes the disease [1]. Each child of an affected parent has a 50 % chance of inheriting it, so affected people are likely to appear in each generation [1].

**7.** Parental genotypes CᴿCᵂ (pink) × CᵂCᵂ (white) [1]. Gametes Cᴿ and Cᵂ from the pink parent; Cᵂ only from the white parent [1]. Offspring genotypes CᴿCᵂ and CᵂCᵂ [1]. Phenotypes pink and white in a 1 : 1 ratio [1].

**8.**

**(a)** The woman's genotype is IᴬIᴮ, so every egg carries Iᴬ or Iᴮ [1]. Group O requires the genotype IᴼIᴼ, which needs an Iᴼ allele from both parents [1].

**(b)** IᴬIᴼ, blood group A [1]; IᴮIᴼ, blood group B [1].

**9.**

**(a)** 1 : 1 : 1 : 1 [1]

**(b)** E = 240 ÷ 4 = 60 for each class [1]. χ² = (26² + 26² + 29² + 29²) ÷ 60 = 3034 ÷ 60 = **50.6** [1].

**(c)** 3 degrees of freedom; 50.6 is greater than 7.82 [1]. The difference from 1 : 1 : 1 : 1 is significant (p < 0.05), so the results do not fit the hypothesis that the genes are unlinked [1].

**(d)** The genes are linked on the same chromosome, with T and R together on one homologue and t and r on the other, so tall round and short wrinkled are the large parental classes [1]. The small classes (65 offspring, 27 %) are recombinants produced by crossing over between the loci in prophase I [1].

**10.**

**(a)** The W gene, because the dominant allele W masks the effect of the Y gene; Y/y is only expressed in ww plants [1].

**(b)** Gametes from WwYy: WY, Wy, wY, wy; from wwyy: wy only [1]. Offspring: WwYy, Wwyy, wwYy, wwyy [1]. Phenotypes: white, white, yellow, green = **2 white : 1 yellow : 1 green** [1].

**11.**

**(a)** A base substitution in the HBB gene changes one codon, so in the β-globin polypeptide glutamic acid is replaced by valine [1]. The haemoglobin (HbS) molecules stick together and form fibres at low oxygen concentration [1]. Red blood cells become sickle-shaped and carry less oxygen [1]. Sickled cells block capillaries and are destroyed quickly, causing pain and anaemia [1].

**(b)** 0.25 (1 in 4) [1]

**12.**

**(a)** Her father had genotype XʰY and passed his X chromosome, carrying Xʰ, to all his daughters [1]. She has normal clotting, so she also has Xᴴ: her genotype is XᴴXʰ, a carrier [1].

**(b)** 0.25 (1 in 4): half her children are expected to be sons, and half of those to inherit Xʰ [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- No key, or alleles written without X for sex-linked genes.
- Gametes written as pairs of alleles for one gene.
- Degrees of freedom taken from the number of offspring.
- A chi-squared conclusion that says "correct" or "proved" instead of "significant" or "not significant".
- In the HBB chain, missing the amino acid change or the low-oxygen condition.
- Confusing the probability of "a son with haemophilia" with "a son having haemophilia, given the child is a son".
