These notes condense **4.4.2 Reactions of acids** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462) specification (for teaching
from September 2016). Paper 1 content. Higher tier only: the redox view of acid with metals, titration
calculations and strong and weak acids. Titrations are also chemistry only. For explanations use the
[reactions of acids study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-reactions-of-acids/); to test yourself use
the [reactions of acids practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-reactions-of-acids-practice/).

## Core facts

- Acid + metal → **salt + hydrogen** (magnesium, zinc, iron with hydrochloric and sulfuric acid).
- Acid + alkali or base → **salt + water**.
- Acid + metal carbonate → **salt + water + carbon dioxide**.
- Salt name = **positive ion** of the alkali/base/carbonate + **acid used**.
- Acids produce **H⁺(aq)**; alkalis contain **OH⁻(aq)**.
- Neutralisation: **H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l)**.

## Salt families

| Acid | Salt | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Hydrochloric, HCl | chloride | magnesium chloride, MgCl₂ |
| Sulfuric, H₂SO₄ | sulfate | zinc sulfate, ZnSO₄ |
| Nitric, HNO₃ | nitrate | calcium nitrate, Ca(NO₃)₂ |

## Reaction summary

| Reactants | Products | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Mg + HCl | MgCl₂ + H₂ | fizzing, metal disappears, warms |
| Zn + H₂SO₄ | ZnSO₄ + H₂ | slower fizzing |
| Fe + HCl | FeCl₂ + H₂ | slowest of the three |
| CuO + H₂SO₄ | CuSO₄ + H₂O | black solid dissolves, blue solution |
| NaOH + HNO₃ | NaNO₃ + H₂O | no visible change, warms |
| CaCO₃ + 2HCl | CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂ | fizzing, limewater turns milky |

Test for hydrogen: lit splint, squeaky pop. Test for carbon dioxide: limewater turns milky.

## Higher tier only: redox in acid with metal

Mg + 2H⁺ → Mg²⁺ + H₂

- Magnesium **loses** electrons: **oxidised**.
- Hydrogen ions **gain** electrons: **reduced**.
- Anions (Cl⁻, SO₄²⁻) do not change: **spectator ions**.
- OIL RIG: Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain (of electrons).

## Making a pure, dry soluble salt (required practical 1)

1. Warm dilute acid in a beaker.
2. Add **insoluble** metal, oxide or carbonate in small portions until some is left over (**excess**).
3. **Filter** off the excess solid.
4. **Evaporate** to the point of crystallisation, then leave to cool.
5. Filter the crystals and **dry** them.

Why excess solid? All the acid reacts, so the salt is not contaminated with acid, and the leftover solid is
easy to filter off. Why not evaporate to dryness? Heating a salt solution strongly can decompose or spoil the
crystals.

## pH

| pH | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 to 6 | acidic |
| 7 | neutral |
| 8 to 14 | alkaline |

- **Universal indicator** (or wide range indicator): approximate pH from a colour.
- **pH probe**: more accurate numerical value.
- Strong acid into strong alkali: pH rises slowly, then jumps sharply at neutralisation.

## Titration (chemistry only)

Strong acid and strong alkali only (sulfuric, hydrochloric, nitric).

1. **Pipette** a fixed volume of alkali into a conical flask.
2. Add a few drops of **indicator**.
3. Add acid from a **burette**, swirling, until the colour just changes (**end point**).
4. Record the **titre**. Repeat until results are **concordant** (within 0.10 cm³). Mean of concordant results.
5. Use a white tile under the flask so colour changes show clearly. Add acid dropwise near the end.

## Higher tier only: titration calculation

1. Moles of known solution = volume in dm³ × concentration (mol/dm³).
2. Use the **ratio** from the balanced equation.
3. Concentration = moles ÷ volume in dm³.
4. g/dm³ = mol/dm³ × Mr.

Worked: 25.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ NaOH needs 22.5 cm³ of HCl.
Moles NaOH = 0.0250 × 0.100 = 0.00250. Ratio 1 : 1, so moles HCl = 0.00250.
Concentration = 0.00250 ÷ 0.0225 = **0.111 mol/dm³**. Mass concentration = 0.111 × 36.5 = **4.06 g/dm³**.

Ratio 2 : 1 case: 2NaOH + H₂SO₄ → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O. Divide moles NaOH by 2 to get moles H₂SO₄.

## Higher tier only: strong and weak acids

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Strong acid | completely ionised in water (hydrochloric, nitric, sulfuric) |
| Weak acid | partially ionised in water (ethanoic, citric, carbonic) |
| Concentrated | a lot of acid in a small volume |
| Dilute | little acid in a large volume |

- Same concentration: the stronger acid has the **lower pH**.
- Each **fall of one pH unit** means H⁺ concentration **× 10**.
- pH 3 compared with pH 6: 3 units apart, so 10³ = **1000 times** more H⁺.

## Exam traps

- **Wrong product with a carbonate.** It makes water and carbon dioxide, not hydrogen.
- **Wrong salt name.** Check which acid was used before naming the anion.
- **Dilute is not weak.** Dilute is about amount of acid in solution, weak is about ionisation.
- **Volume units.** Divide cm³ by 1000 before using concentration.
- **Missing the ratio.** Sulfuric acid is 1 : 2 with sodium hydroxide, not 1 : 1.
- **Titre taken from an anomalous run.** Use only concordant results.
- **Salt method.** Excess **solid**, not excess acid.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the products when zinc reacts with hydrochloric acid.
2. Name the salt made from nitric acid and copper(II) oxide.
3. Name three products of calcium carbonate with hydrochloric acid.
4. Write the ionic equation for neutralisation.
5. Why is excess insoluble solid used when making a salt?
6. What is the end point of a titration?
7. What does "concordant" mean?
8. Give the colour of phenolphthalein in alkali.
9. (Higher tier only) Which species is oxidised in Mg + 2H⁺ → Mg²⁺ + H₂?
10. (Higher tier only) 25.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol/dm³ sodium hydroxide reacts with 20.0 cm³ of hydrochloric acid. Find the acid concentration.
11. (Higher tier only) How many times greater is the H⁺ concentration at pH 2 than at pH 5?
12. (Higher tier only) Give one example of a concentrated weak acid situation in words.

### Answers

1. Zinc chloride and hydrogen.
2. Copper(II) nitrate.
3. Calcium chloride, water and carbon dioxide.
4. H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l).
5. So that all the acid reacts and none is left in the salt solution; the leftover solid can be filtered off.
6. The point at which the indicator just changes colour, showing exact neutralisation.
7. Results that agree within 0.10 cm³.
8. Pink.
9. Magnesium (it loses electrons).
10. Moles NaOH = 0.0250 × 0.200 = 0.00500; 1 : 1, so moles HCl = 0.00500; 0.00500 ÷ 0.0200 = 0.250 mol/dm³.
11. 1000 times.
12. A large amount of ethanoic acid in a small volume of solution: concentrated, but only partly ionised so weak.
