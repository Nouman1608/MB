## 5.2.1 Detection

- **Background radiation:** ionising radiation always present.
- Main sources: **radon gas** (air), **rocks and buildings**, **food and drink**, **cosmic rays**.
- Measured with a **detector connected to a counter**.
- Count rate = counts ÷ time, in counts/s or counts/minute.
- **Extended only:** corrected count rate = measured count rate − background count rate.

## 5.2.2 The three emissions

- Emission is **spontaneous** and **random in direction**.

| | α | β | γ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nature | Helium nucleus (2p + 2n) | Electron from nucleus | Electromagnetic radiation |
| Charge | +2 | −1 | 0 |
| Ionising | Most | Medium | Least |
| Penetration | Least (paper) | Medium (few mm aluminium) | Most (reduced by thick lead) |
| Electric field (Extended only) | Towards − plate, small bend | Towards + plate, larger bend | Undeflected |
| Magnetic field (Extended only) | Curves one way | Curves the other way, more | Undeflected |

- **Extended only:** α ionises most because of its **large charge** and **large kinetic energy**.
- β always means β⁻ in this syllabus.

## 5.2.3 Radioactive decay

- A change in an **unstable nucleus**; emits α and/or β and/or γ; spontaneous and random.
- α or β decay → nucleus becomes a **different element**.
- **Extended only:** unstable because of **excess neutrons** and/or **too heavy**.
- **Extended only:** decay increases **stability**; β-decay: **neutron → proton + electron**.

**Method in steps: decay equation (Extended only)**

1. Parent nuclide on the left.
2. Emitted particle on the right: ⁴₂He, ⁰₋₁e or γ.
3. Daughter: α → A − 4, Z − 2; β → A same, Z + 1; γ → no change.
4. Check top and bottom rows balance.
5. Name the daughter from its Z.

## 5.2.4 Half-life

- **Half-life:** time for half the nuclei of a particular isotope in any sample to decay.
- Fraction left after n half-lives: (1/2)ⁿ.
- Core calculations: no background.
- **Extended only:** subtract background first, then find the half-life.

**Method in steps: from a decay curve**

1. Read a starting count rate.
2. Find where it has halved; read the time.
3. Repeat from another starting point; average.
4. Draw construction lines on the graph.

## Uses (Extended only)

| Use | Radiation | Half-life |
|---|---|---|
| Smoke alarm | α | Long |
| Irradiating food | γ | Long |
| Sterilising equipment | γ | Long |
| Thickness of paper | β | Long |
| Cancer diagnosis | γ | Short |
| Cancer treatment | γ | Long (external source) |

- Justify every choice with **penetration/absorption** and **half-life**.

## 5.2.5 Safety

- Effects: **cell death**, **mutations**, **cancer**.
- Handling: tongs, at arm's length, pointed away from people, short use, lead-lined box, locked and labelled store.
- **Extended only:** reduce **time**, increase **distance**, use **shielding**.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Ionising vs penetrating:** α ionises most and penetrates least; γ is the reverse.
- **Spontaneous vs random:** spontaneous means conditions such as temperature do not affect it; random means no one can predict which nucleus decays next.
- **Count rate vs corrected count rate (Extended only):** the corrected value has the background removed.
- **α or β decay vs γ emission:** α and β change the element; γ changes only the energy of the nucleus.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Background radiation | Ionising radiation always present from natural and artificial sources |
| Count rate | Counts per second or per minute |
| Spontaneous | Not affected by external conditions |
| Random | Cannot predict which nucleus decays or when |
| Half-life | Time for half the nuclei of an isotope in a sample to decay |
| Corrected count rate (Extended only) | Count rate with background subtracted |

## Exam traps

- γ is **reduced**, not stopped, by lead.
- β bends **more** than α in a field.
- Half-life halves the **count rate**, not the time.
- Extended data with background: subtract it **before** halving.
- In β-decay, Z goes **up** by 1 and A stays the same.
- A use needs both radiation type and half-life.

## Self-test

1. Name four main sources of background radiation.
2. A counter records 900 counts in 3.0 minutes. Find the count rate in counts/s.
3. Which radiation is stopped by a sheet of paper?
4. Which radiation is the most penetrating?
5. What happens to the element when a nucleus emits a β-particle?
6. Define half-life.
7. A sample's count rate falls from 800 to 50 counts/minute in 16 hours. Find the half-life.
8. *(Extended)* A reading of 245 counts/minute includes a background of 25 counts/minute. Find the corrected count rate.
9. *(Extended)* Write the equation for the β-decay of ⁹⁰₃₈Sr to yttrium (Y).
10. *(Extended)* Describe the change inside a nucleus during β-decay.
11. *(Extended)* Why is a γ-emitter with a short half-life chosen as a medical tracer?
12. *(Extended)* Explain why tongs reduce a technician's exposure.

### Answers

1. **Radon gas**, **rocks and buildings**, **food and drink**, **cosmic rays**.
2. 900 ÷ 180 = **5.0 counts/s**.
3. **Alpha**.
4. **Gamma**.
5. It becomes a **different element** (Z increases by 1).
6. The time taken for **half the nuclei** of a particular isotope in any sample to decay.
7. 800 → 400 → 200 → 100 → 50 is 4 half-lives; 16 ÷ 4 = **4.0 hours**.
8. 245 − 25 = **220 counts/minute**.
9. ⁹⁰₃₈Sr → **⁹⁰₃₉Y + ⁰₋₁e**.
10. A **neutron changes into a proton and an electron**; the electron is emitted.
11. γ passes out of the body to the **detector**; the short half-life means the activity **falls quickly**, limiting the dose.
12. They **increase the distance** between the source and the hands.
