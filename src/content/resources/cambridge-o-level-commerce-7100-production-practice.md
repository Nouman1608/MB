## Section A

**1.** State the sector of production for each activity: (a) catching tuna; (b) assembling mobile phones; (c) insuring a warehouse; (d) designing a mobile banking app. **[4]**

**2.** Define *specialisation*. **[1]**

**3.** Distinguish between *in-house production* and *outsourced production*. **[2]**

**4.** State the difference between a *domestic supply chain* and a *global supply chain*. **[2]**

**5.** Identify **two** home trade intermediaries and **two** international trade intermediaries named in the syllabus. **[4]**

**6.** What is meant by *electronic data interchange (EDI)*? **[2]**

**7.** A business pays an agent 2.5% commission on sales of $36,000. Calculate the commission. **[2]**

## Section B

**8.** Explain **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of specialisation for (a) an individual worker and (b) a country. **[8]**

**9.** Bright Threads designs school uniforms and makes them in its own factory. It is considering outsourcing production to a contract manufacturer. Explain **two** benefits of keeping production in-house and **two** benefits of outsourcing. **[8]**

**10.** Explain the difference between an agent and a factor in international trade. **[4]**

**11.** A car-parts factory is replacing manual welding with robots. Explain **two** positive and **two** negative effects of this automation. **[8]**

**12.** Lumina Ltd imports 400 lamps with a total value of $8,000. Its costs are: customs duty at 6% of the value; transport $720; packaging at $0.50 per lamp; bank charges $40.
(a) Calculate the total of these costs.
(b) Calculate the cost per lamp. **[6]**

**13.** Coastal Catch Ltd owns fishing boats, runs a factory that cans fish, sells the cans to wholesalers through its own sales office, and employs a team that built the website and app customers use to track orders. Explain how the business is involved in **four** sectors of production. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) Primary [1]; (b) secondary [1]; (c) tertiary [1]; (d) quaternary [1].

**2.** Concentrating on producing one product, or on one part of a production process [1].

**3.** In-house production uses the business's own staff, premises and equipment [1]; outsourced production means paying another business to make the product or carry out the task [1].

**4.** In a domestic supply chain all stages take place within one country [1]; in a global supply chain some stages, such as sourcing or assembly, take place in other countries [1].

**5.** Home trade: wholesaler [1] and retailer [1]. International trade: agent [1] and factor [1].

**6.** The computer-to-computer exchange of business documents, such as orders and invoices [1], between a business and its suppliers in a standard electronic format [1].

**7.** 2.5% x $36,000 = 0.025 x 36,000 [1] = **$900** [1].

**8.** (a) **Individual.** Advantage: the worker becomes skilled and quick at one task [1], so may earn more as an expert [1]. Disadvantage: repeating the same task is boring [1], which can reduce motivation and care at work [1]. (b) **Country.** Advantage: it can use natural advantages such as climate or minerals to produce more of one product [1] and earn export income from it [1]. Disadvantage: it depends on imports for goods it does not make [1], and its income falls if world demand or prices for its main product fall [1].

**9.** **In-house:** **control on quality**, because managers can inspect uniforms at every stage [1] so faulty stitching is corrected before schools receive them [1]; **control on information**, because designs and school contracts stay inside the business [1], reducing the risk that a rival copies them [1]. **Outsourcing:** **accessing expertise**, since a contract manufacturer may have specialist machinery and skilled staff [1], producing uniforms faster or to a higher standard [1]; **cost savings**, since Bright Threads would not need to buy machines or employ factory staff [1], and a large contractor may produce each uniform more cheaply [1].

**10.** Both act on behalf of another business and are paid a commission [1]. An **agent** finds buyers or sellers and arranges deals [1] but does not take possession of the goods [1]. A **factor** takes possession of the goods and sells them for the owner, without becoming the owner [1].

**11.** **Positive:** robots weld faster and to a consistent standard [1], so output rises and fewer parts are rejected [1]; robots can work around the clock and remove workers from a dangerous task [1], cutting injuries [1]. **Negative:** the robots are expensive to buy and maintain [1], which raises costs in the short term [1]; some welders lose their jobs [1] and the remaining staff need retraining to operate and monitor the robots [1].

**12.** (a) Customs duty = 6% x $8,000 = $480 [1]. Packaging = 400 x $0.50 = $200 [1]. Total = $480 + $720 + $200 + $40 [1] = **$1,440** [1]. (b) $1,440 / 400 [1] = **$3.60** per lamp [1].

**13.** For each sector, 1 mark for naming it and 1 for linking it to Coastal Catch's activity. **Primary**: its boats catch fish, taking a natural resource from the sea [1] [1]. **Secondary**: its factory processes and cans the fish, turning raw materials into finished goods [1] [1]. **Tertiary**: its sales office provides a commercial service, selling the cans to wholesalers and arranging their distribution [1] [1]. **Quaternary**: its team builds and runs the website and app, an activity based on digital technology [1] [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 1: placing insurance in the secondary sector or forgetting the quaternary sector for app design.
- Question 8: giving the same advantage for both contexts instead of one specific to each.
- Question 9: describing outsourcing in general without linking benefits to uniforms and schools.
- Question 10: saying the agent buys the goods.
- Question 12: adding "6%" to dollar amounts, or forgetting to multiply packaging by 400.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much working should I show in a calculation?** Enough that each step can be followed: write each cost as an amount, then the addition, then any division. Method marks are usually available even if the final answer is wrong.

**Why does Question 9 mention schools?** Applying each benefit to the business in the question is what earns the second mark for each point.
