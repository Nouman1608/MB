OCR GCSE (9-1) Gateway Science Physics A (J249) is the separate-science physics GCSE in OCR's Gateway
suite. It is aimed at students taking physics as a full GCSE in its own right, and it covers matter,
forces, electricity, magnetism, waves, radioactivity and energy before finishing with a set of
"global challenges" that apply that physics to transport, power generation and space. Assessment is
by two written papers in either the Foundation or the Higher tier, each lasting 105 minutes, worth
90 marks and carrying 50% of the qualification.

## How the course is organised

The specification content is set out in eight topics, plus practical skills that are taught and
assessed alongside them.

**The first four topics** establish the core ideas. Matter covers the particle model, changes of
state and pressure. Forces covers motion, Newton's laws and forces in action. Electricity covers
static and charge and simple circuits. Magnetism and magnetic fields covers magnets and magnetic
fields and the uses of magnetism.

**The next three topics** widen the picture. Waves in matter covers wave behaviour, the
electromagnetic spectrum and wave interaction. Radioactivity covers radioactive emissions and their
uses and hazards. Energy covers work done, and power and efficiency.

**The final topic, Global challenges**, has three parts: physics on the move, powering Earth, and
beyond Earth. It asks students to apply ideas from across the course to real situations such as
vehicle safety, energy resources and the wider universe.

**Practical skills**, numbered as Topic P9 in OCR's specification, run through the whole course
rather than sitting in one block.

## How it's assessed

| Tier | Paper | Content assessed | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Foundation | J249/01 | Topics P1-P4 and P9 | 105 minutes | 90 | 50% |
| Foundation | J249/02 | Topics P5-P8, assuming P1-P4 and P9 | 105 minutes | 90 | 50% |
| Higher | J249/03 | Topics P1-P4 and P9 | 105 minutes | 90 | 50% |
| Higher | J249/04 | Topics P5-P8, assuming P1-P4 and P9 | 105 minutes | 90 | 50% |

The course is linear: a candidate is entered for one tier and sits both of that tier's papers at the
end of the course. According to the J249 specification, the first paper in each tier assesses Topics
P1 to P4 plus the practical skills in P9, while the second assesses Topics P5 to P8 with assumed
knowledge of P1 to P4 and P9, and includes synoptic questions. Both papers are externally assessed
written exams, and the specification has been examined since 2018.

## How the parts fit together

The paper split is not a clean division of the course into two halves. The second paper assumes
everything in the first, so energy calculations in Topic P7 can draw on forces and motion, and the
global challenges in Topic P8 can draw on any of the earlier physics. Synoptic questions on that
paper deliberately mix topics.

Some links are especially worth noticing. Forces and motion feed straight into stopping distances
and vehicle safety in physics on the move. Electricity and magnetism come together in
electromagnetic induction, which in turn underpins the generation and distribution of electricity in
powering Earth. Wave behaviour and the electromagnetic spectrum connect to the uses and hazards of
radiation. Work done, power and efficiency apply to almost every system in the course.

Practical skills sit across both papers. Planning measurements, recording and processing data,
working with units and interpreting graphs are tested in the written exams, so the habits built
during practical lessons pay off directly.

## Planning your study

A sensible order follows the specification: Matter and Forces first, then Electricity and
Magnetism, so that the content of the first paper is secure early. Waves, Radioactivity and Energy
can follow, with Global challenges taught last because it depends on everything before it. Practical
work should run throughout rather than being saved for the end.

Across the course, keep a single list of the equations and units you meet, and practise rearranging
and using them often; mathematical fluency matters in every topic. When revising the second half of
the course, go back over the first four topics too, since the second paper assumes them.

Once the whole specification has been taught, begin full timed papers in your tier and review where
marks are lost. Tier entry is something to agree with your teacher. The exam-preparation page goes
into detail on handling equations, units and graphs under exam conditions.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in OCR GCSE Physics A?**
Two per tier. Foundation candidates sit Papers 1 and 2, and Higher candidates sit Papers 3 and 4. Each paper is 105 minutes, 90 marks and 50% of the qualification.

**Which topics are on each J249 paper?**
The first paper in each tier assesses Topics P1 to P4 and the practical skills in P9. The second assesses Topics P5 to P8, with assumed knowledge of P1 to P4 and P9.

**Does the second paper include questions on earlier topics?**
Yes. It assumes knowledge of Topics P1 to P4 and P9 and includes synoptic questions that combine ideas from different topics.

**Is there a practical exam in OCR GCSE Physics A?**
There is no separate practical paper in the assessment. Practical skills are part of the specification and are assessed through the written papers.

**What is the Global challenges topic?**
It is the final content topic, made up of physics on the move, powering Earth and beyond Earth, and it applies earlier physics to real-world contexts.
