**These notes condense Section 5 Industrial development of Cambridge IGCSE Pakistan Studies 0448
Paper 2.** You need the key terms, the products, locations and location factors of ten listed
industries, the formal–informal contrast, government support including export processing zones,
and an assessment of call centres and tourism as routes to development.

## Definitions to learn word-perfect

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Primary industry | Obtains raw materials from the land or sea |
| Secondary industry | Processes or manufactures goods |
| Tertiary industry | Provides services |
| Large-scale industry | Big factories, many workers, heavy capital investment |
| Small-scale industry | Small units with few workers and modest capital |
| Cottage industry | Production at home by family members using simple tools |
| Formal sector | Registered, taxed, regular wages and conditions |
| Informal sector | Unregistered, untaxed, irregular work |
| Value-added | Increase in value through processing |
| Infrastructure | Roads, railways, power, water, telecommunications |
| EPZ | Export processing zone: an area with tax and customs concessions for export production |
| GNP / GDP | Value of output of a country's people, including income from abroad (GNP), or produced within the country (GDP) |

## The cotton chain (ginning to clothing)

Raw cotton → **ginning** (separating lint from seed) → **spinning** (yarn) → **weaving** (grey
cloth) → **dyeing and printing** → **garments, towels and bed linen**. Value is added at every
stage. Ginning takes place close to the cotton fields; spinning and weaving cluster in Faisalabad,
Karachi, Lahore and Multan.

## Location factors checklist

**C**apital, **S**ite (flat land), **R**aw materials, **P**ower, **W**ater, **L**abour,
**C**ommunications, **G**overnment policy, plus **M**arket. Use them like a checklist when
explaining any location.

## Informal sector: pros and cons

**Advantages:** many jobs for unskilled workers; little capital needed; cheap goods and services;
flexible; recycles materials.
**Disadvantages:** low, irregular pay; no job security or safety rules; child labour; no tax
revenue; poor quality control.

## Government promotion

- Infrastructure and industrial estates (SITE Karachi, Hattar).
- EPZs (Karachi EPZ; also Sialkot, Gwadar) with duty-free imports and tax concessions.
- Loans and credit for small industries; training; incentives for foreign investment.

## Tourism and call centres

- **Tourism attractions:** natural (Hunza, Swat, Naran–Kaghan, K2, beaches) and cultural (Mohenjo-daro, Taxila, Lahore Fort, Badshahi Mosque).
- **Benefits:** jobs, foreign exchange, rises in GNP and GDP, improved roads.
- **Limits:** access, security concerns, seasonality, environmental damage, lack of hotels in remote areas.
- **Call centres:** need fast internet, stable power, English skills; held back by power cuts and patchy broadband.

## Sources of capital and labour

- **Capital:** private savings and family businesses; bank loans; government development finance;
  foreign direct investment, including investment linked to CPEC; remittances from overseas workers
  that families invest in small workshops.
- **Labour:** a large, young workforce; cheap unskilled labour from rural–urban migrants; skilled
  craft labour passed down in families (Sialkot metalwork, carpet weaving); shortages of trained
  technicians and managers.

## Worked example

**Question:** Explain why sugar mills are located close to the areas where sugar cane is grown. **[3]**

**Model answer:** Sugar cane is bulky and heavy, so it is expensive to transport long distances [1].
Cut cane starts to lose its sugar content within a short time, so it must be crushed quickly [1].
Locating the mill near the fields reduces transport costs and keeps the sugar yield high [1].

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- **Defining the informal sector as "illegal".** It is unregistered, not necessarily illegal.
- **One factor only.** Location questions usually reward several linked factors.
- **Mixing up GNP and GDP.** GNP includes income earned abroad (such as remittances); GDP is output within Pakistan.
- **Generic tourism answers.** Name a natural and a cultural attraction and say why each draws visitors.
- **Forgetting the market.** State whether products go to domestic or export markets when asked.

## How is this section examined?

This content is examined on Cambridge Paper 2, The environment of Pakistan (1 hour 30 minutes, 75 marks, 50% of the qualification). You answer three questions from a choice of five; each is worth 25 marks and has four parts, (a) to (d), built around resources such as maps, graphs, tables and photographs. Part (d) specifically tests analysis and evaluation, so finish every revision topic by practising a judgement: for example, how sustainable a resource or a policy is, with evidence for and against.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which industries are mainly for export?**
Sports goods, surgical instruments and much of the
cotton textile output; cement also has some exports.

**What makes an industrial estate different from an EPZ?**
An industrial estate provides serviced
plots and infrastructure for any industry; an EPZ adds tax and customs concessions for firms that
export their output.
