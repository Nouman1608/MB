## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** What is meant by a generator potential? **[2]**

**2.** Which ion enters the neurone of a Pacinian corpuscle when pressure is applied?

A: Potassium

B: Chloride

C: Sodium

D: Calcium **[1]**

**3.** Explain what is meant by the statement that a receptor responds only to a specific stimulus. **[2]**

**4.** State which type of photoreceptor gives colour vision, and explain why. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**5.** A Pacinian corpuscle has a resting potential of -70 mV. An action potential is produced when the membrane potential reaches -50 mV. The generator potentials and the frequency of action potentials at different pressures are shown.

| Pressure / kPa | Generator potential / mV | Action potentials per second |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | +3 | 0 |
| 1.0 | +8 | 0 |
| 1.5 | +14 | 0 |
| 2.0 | +20 | 5 |
| 2.5 | +26 | 12 |
| 3.0 | +28 | 14 |

**(a)** Calculate the depolarisation required to reach the threshold. **[1]**

**(b)** Use the data to explain why there were no action potentials at 1.5 kPa. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the percentage increase in the generator potential when the pressure increased from 2.0 to 2.5 kPa. **[2]**

**(d)** Describe and explain the effect of the increase in pressure from 2.5 to 3.0 kPa on the generator potential and on the action potentials. **[3]**

**6.** Describe how pressure on a Pacinian corpuscle leads to an action potential in the sensory neurone. **[5]**

**7.** The retina of a person contains 600 rods connected to 20 bipolar neurones and 600 cones, each connected to its own bipolar neurone.

**(a)** Calculate the mean number of rods connected to each bipolar neurone. **[1]**

**(b)** In dim light, each rod produces a generator potential of 1.5 mV. A bipolar neurone needs a total of 12 mV to be depolarised to threshold. Calculate the number of rods that must be stimulated together. **[2]**

**(c)** Use your answers to explain why rods are more sensitive to light than cones. **[3]**

**8.** Explain why visual acuity is greater in the part of the retina that contains mainly cones than in the part that contains mainly rods. **[3]**

**9.** A person looks at a very dim star. They see it better by looking slightly to one side of it than by looking straight at it. Explain this observation. **[3]**

**10.** Explain how the human eye can distinguish red from green light. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** A change in the potential difference (depolarisation) across the membrane of a receptor [1] when it is stimulated, due to the entry of ions through channels in the membrane [1]. **[2]**

**2.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Each type of receptor is sensitive to one kind of stimulus, such as pressure or light [1], and does not produce a generator potential in response to other stimuli [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Cones [1]. There are three types of cone with different pigments, each sensitive to different wavelengths of light, and the brain interprets the combination of signals as a colour [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** -50 - (-70) = +20 mV [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** The generator potential at 1.5 kPa was +14 mV [1], which is below the +20 mV needed to reach the threshold, so no action potential was produced [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** (26 - 20) / 20 [1] x 100 = 30 % [1]. **[2]**

**(d)** The generator potential increased only a little, from +26 to +28 mV [1]. The frequency of action potentials rose from 12 to 14 per second [1]. Nearly all the sodium channels were already open, so the response was close to its maximum [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Pressure deforms the lamellae and stretches the membrane of the sensory neurone ending [1]. Stretch-mediated sodium ion channels widen and open [1]. Sodium ions diffuse into the neurone [1], which depolarises the membrane, producing the generator potential [1]. If the generator potential reaches the threshold, an action potential is produced and travels along the sensory neurone [1]. **[5]**

**7. (a)** 600 / 20 = 30 rods [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** 12 / 1.5 [1] = 8 rods [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** Many rods (30) share one bipolar neurone, so only 8 need be stimulated for the neurone to reach the threshold; the generator potentials add up (summation) [1]. A cone has its own neurone, so a single generator potential of 1.5 mV is too small to reach the threshold [1]. So rods can trigger an impulse in dim light and cones cannot [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Each cone is connected to its own bipolar neurone [1], so light falling on two neighbouring cones produces two separate impulses and the brain sees two points [1]. Many rods share one neurone, so light on neighbouring rods produces one signal, and the two points cannot be distinguished [1]. **[3]**

**9.** The centre of the retina (fovea) has mainly cones, which are not sensitive enough to respond to very dim light [1]. Looking to one side focuses the image on the part of the retina with rods [1], which respond to dim light, because their pigment is broken down by low-intensity light and many rods share one neurone, so their generator potentials add together [1]. **[3]**

**10.** There are three types of cone [1], each with a pigment sensitive to a different range of wavelengths [1]. Red light stimulates the types in a different proportion from green light, and the brain interprets the different combination of impulses as different colours [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "action potential" when the answer is generator potential.
- Describing a stronger stimulus as producing larger action potentials.
- Mixing up sensitivity (rods) and acuity (cones).
- Giving a difference without the cause, which is the pigment or the connection.
- Forgetting to subtract the resting potential when finding the required depolarisation.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these OxfordAQA past paper questions?**
No. They are original questions for section 3.4.2 of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Biology (9610). Use them with the official past papers.

**Does the specification name the optic nerve connections in detail?**
It asks for the connections that the rods and cones make in the optic nerve only in sufficient detail to explain sensitivity, colour and acuity. Learn the one-to-one and many-to-one patterns.
