## Section A

**1.** State the relative charge of:

**(a)** a proton **[1]**
**(b)** a neutron. **[1]**

**2.** Define the proton number of a nucleus. **[1]**

**3.** Calculate the number of neutrons in a nucleus of ²⁰⁷₈₂Pb. **[1]**

**4.** State how an atom becomes a positive ion. **[1]**

**5.** A nucleus of potassium (symbol K) contains 19 protons and 20 neutrons. Write it in nuclide notation. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** This question is about the structure of atoms.

**(a)** Describe the structure of an atom in terms of its nucleus and electrons. **[2]**
**(b)** A neutral atom of oxygen has 8 electrons. State the number of protons in its nucleus and explain your answer. **[2]**

**7.** Lithium has two isotopes, ⁶₃Li and ⁷₃Li.

**(a)** Explain why these two nuclides are isotopes of the same element. **[2]**
**(b)** State the numbers of protons, neutrons and electrons in a neutral atom of each isotope. **[3]**

**8.** Five nuclides are labelled with letters that are not their chemical symbols:

¹²₆A  ¹⁴₆B  ¹⁴₇C  ⁴⁰₁₈D  ⁴⁰₂₀E

**(a)** State which two nuclides are isotopes of the same element. **[1]**
**(b)** Name one pair of nuclides that have the same nucleon number but are different elements. **[1]**
**(c)** State which nuclide has the most neutrons. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why B and C are different elements. **[1]**

**9.** This question is about ions.

**(a)** An atom of ⁴⁰₂₀Ca loses two electrons. State the number of electrons in the ion. **[1]**
**(b)** State whether the nucleus changes when the ion forms. **[1]**
**(c)** An atom of ³⁵₁₇Cl gains one electron. State the charge on the ion and its number of electrons. **[2]**

**10.** *(Extended)* In an alpha-particle scattering experiment, a beam of alpha particles is directed at a very thin metal foil.

**(a)** State **two** features of the apparatus needed for the experiment to work, other than the thin foil. **[2]**
**(b)** For each conclusion below, state the observation that supports it: (i) the nucleus is very small and the atom is mostly empty space; (ii) the nucleus is positively charged; (iii) the nucleus contains most of the mass of the atom. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why the metal foil must be very thin. **[1]**

**11.** *(Extended)* A nucleus of cobalt is written ⁵⁹₂₇Co.

**(a)** State the relative charge of the nucleus. **[1]**
**(b)** State the relative mass of the nucleus. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the relative mass of the whole atom is almost the same as that of its nucleus. **[1]**

**12.** *(Extended)* A nucleus of uranium-235 absorbs a neutron and splits:

²³⁵₉₂U + ¹₀n → ¹⁴⁴₅₅Cs + ⁹⁰₃₇Rb + x ¹₀n

**(a)** Name this process. **[1]**
**(b)** Find x, showing that both rows of the equation balance. **[2]**
**(c)** State how the total mass after the reaction compares with the total mass before, and what this means for energy. **[2]**

**13.** *(Extended)* In the Sun, two helium-3 nuclei join:

³₂He + ³₂He → ⁴₂He + 2 X

**(a)** Name this process. **[1]**
**(b)** Identify particle X, giving its nucleon number and proton number. **[2]**
**(c)** State one difference between this process and the process in Question 12. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **+1** [1].
**(b)** **0** [1]. **[2]**

**2.** The number of **protons** in the nucleus [1]. **[1]**

**3.** 207 − 82 = **125** [1]. **[1]**

**4.** By **losing** one or more electrons [1]. **[1]**

**5.** A = 19 + 20 = 39, so **³⁹₁₉K** [1]. **[1]**

*Common mistake:* writing ¹⁹₃₉K. The nucleon number goes at the top.

**6. (a)** A small, positively charged nucleus at the centre [1], with negatively charged electrons orbiting it [1].
**(b)** **8** protons [1]; a neutral atom has equal numbers of protons and electrons, so the charges cancel [1]. **[4]**

**7. (a)** Both have proton number 3, so they are the same element (lithium) [1]; they have different nucleon numbers, so different numbers of neutrons [1].
**(b)** ⁶₃Li: 3 protons, 3 neutrons, 3 electrons [1]. ⁷₃Li: 3 protons, 4 neutrons [1], 3 electrons [1]. **[5]**

**8. (a)** **A and B** (both Z = 6) [1].
**(b)** **B and C**, or **D and E** [1].
**(c)** **D** (40 − 18 = 22 neutrons; E has 20) [1].
**(d)** They have different proton numbers (6 and 7) [1]. **[4]**

*Common mistake:* choosing B and C as isotopes because they share the number 14. Isotopes share the proton number, not the nucleon number.

**9. (a)** 20 − 2 = **18** [1].
**(b)** **No**; only the number of electrons changes [1].
**(c)** Charge **−1** [1]; 17 + 1 = **18 electrons** [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** Any two: a vacuum (so air does not stop the alpha particles); a narrow beam from an alpha source; a detector that can be moved to different angles around the foil [1 each].
**(b)** (i) Most alpha particles pass straight through or are deflected only slightly [1]; (ii) some are deflected through large angles, repelled by the nucleus [1]; (iii) a very few bounce back towards the source [1].
**(c)** So that the alpha particles pass through rather than being absorbed (and each is likely to interact with only one nucleus) [1]. **[6]**

**11. (a)** **+27** [1].
**(b)** **59** [1].
**(c)** Electrons have a very small mass compared with protons and neutrons, so almost all the mass is in the nucleus [1]. **[3]**

**12. (a)** Nuclear **fission** [1].
**(b)** Top: 235 + 1 = 236 = 144 + 90 + x, so **x = 2** [1]; bottom: 92 + 0 = 55 + 37 + 0 = 92 [1].
**(c)** The total mass after is slightly **less** than before [1]; the lost mass has been released as **energy** [1]. **[5]**

**13. (a)** Nuclear **fusion** [1].
**(b)** Top: 6 = 4 + 2A, so A = 1; bottom: 4 = 2 + 2Z, so Z = 1 [1]; X is ¹₁H, a **proton** (hydrogen nucleus) [1].
**(c)** Fusion joins light nuclei, whereas fission splits a heavy nucleus [1]. **[4]**

*Common mistake:* in (b), forgetting the 2 in front of X and giving X as ²₂He. Divide the remaining numbers between the two particles.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing the proton number at the top of nuclide notation.
- Confusing isotopes (same Z) with nuclides that share a nucleon number.
- Saying ions form by gaining or losing protons.
- Matching the alpha-scattering observations to the wrong conclusions.
- Forgetting the coefficient in front of neutrons or other particles when balancing.
