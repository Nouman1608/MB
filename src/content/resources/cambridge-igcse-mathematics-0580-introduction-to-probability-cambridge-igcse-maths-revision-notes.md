These notes condense subtopic **8.1 Introduction to probability** of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus (examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027). It is in both tiers; lines marked *(Extended)* are in E8.1 only. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [introduction to probability study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-introduction-to-probability-cambridge-igcse-maths/); for exam-style questions, use the [introduction to probability practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-introduction-to-probability-cambridge-igcse-maths-practice/).

## What the syllabus says

- Understand and use the probability scale from 0 to 1.
- *(Extended)* Understand and use probability notation: P(A) is the probability of A; P(A′) is the probability of not A.
- Calculate the probability of a single event.
- Understand that the probability of an event not occurring = 1 − the probability of the event occurring.
- Give probabilities as fractions, decimals or percentages. Information may come from tables, graphs or Venn diagrams (two sets on Core papers).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Outcome | One possible result of a trial |
| Event | One or more outcomes, such as "an odd number" |
| Equally likely | Each outcome has the same probability |
| At random | Chosen so that every item has the same chance |
| Impossible | Probability 0 |
| Certain | Probability 1 |
| Even chance | Probability ½ (0.5 or 50%) |
| Complement (A′) | "Not A"; P(A′) = 1 − P(A) |

## The scale

```
0 ------------- 0.5 ------------- 1
impossible   unlikely   even   likely   certain
```

Any value outside 0 to 1 is wrong. Negative probabilities and values above 1 are common signs of a slip.

## Formulae

```
Equally likely outcomes:
P(event) = number of outcomes in the event ÷ total number of outcomes

Sector of a spinner:
P(sector) = sector angle ÷ 360°

Complement:
P(not A) = 1 − P(A)          (Extended: P(A′) = 1 − P(A))

All outcomes of one trial:
sum of probabilities = 1
```

## Where the numbers come from

| Source | Top of the fraction | Bottom of the fraction |
|---|---|---|
| List of items | items in the event | all items |
| Frequency table or bar chart | frequency of the event | total frequency |
| Two-way table | the cell or cells asked for | grand total |
| Two-set Venn diagram | regions inside the event | total of all regions, including outside the circles |
| Spinner with sectors | angle of the sectors | 360° |

## Working backwards

```
P(red) = 3/10 and there are 9 red  →  total = 9 ÷ 3/10 = 30
Unknowns: write the total in terms of x, set the fraction equal, solve,
          and check x is a whole number.
Adding items changes the total too: recount before finding the new probability.
```

## Two worked reminders

**Systematic list.** A number from 1 to 16 is chosen at random. P(a factor of 16) = 5/16, from the list 1, 2, 4, 8, 16. Writing the factors in pairs (1 × 16, 2 × 8, 4 × 4) makes it hard to miss one, and 4 is counted once only.

**Missing probability.** A biased coin lands heads with probability 0.58. P(tails) = 1 − 0.58 = 0.42. A three-colour spinner has P(red) = 0.25 and P(blue) = 0.4, so P(green) = 1 − 0.65 = 0.35.

## Answer forms

| Acceptable | Not acceptable |
|---|---|
| 3/8, 0.375, 37.5% | 3 out of 8, 3 in 8, 3 : 8, 3 : 5 |

## Exam traps

- Treating outcomes as equally likely when they are not (unequal sectors, different numbers of items).
- Missing an outcome when listing: write the list in order.
- Counting an outcome twice in "A or B" when it belongs to both.
- Using a row total instead of the grand total in a two-way table.
- Forgetting the region outside both circles of a Venn diagram when finding the total.
- Answering in words, such as "unlikely", when a number is asked for.
- *(Extended)* Confusing P(A′) with P(A).

## Self-test

1. A fair ten-sided dice is numbered 1 to 10. Find P(a number greater than 7).
2. The probability that a bus is on time is 0.84. Find the probability that it is not on time.
3. A letter is chosen at random from the word STATISTICS. Find P(T).
4. A spinner has sectors of 160°, 110° and 90°. Find the probability of landing in the 110° sector.
5. A number is chosen at random from 1 to 12. Find P(it is a factor of 12).
6. Four outcomes have probabilities 0.2, 0.35, 0.1 and p. Find p.
7. *(Extended)* P(C) = 0.07. Write down P(C′).
8. A bag has 5 orange and some purple sweets. P(orange) = 1/4. How many purple sweets are there?
9. Why is "2 : 5" not an acceptable answer for a probability?
10. A number from 1 to 20 is chosen at random. Find P(even or a multiple of 5).
11. In a Venn diagram, A only = 9, both = 4, B only = 6, neither = 1. Find P(B).
12. A box holds x red and 12 blue beads. P(red) = 1/3. Find x.

### Answers

1. 8, 9, 10: **3/10**
2. 1 − 0.84 = **0.16**
3. T appears 3 times in 10 letters: **3/10**
4. 110/360 = **11/36**
5. 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12: 6/12 = **1/2**
6. 1 − 0.65 = **0.35**
7. **0.93**
8. Total = 20, so **15 purple**
9. It is a ratio, not a fraction, decimal or percentage (and 2 : 5 would mean 2/7, not 2/5).
10. Evens 10, plus 5 and 15: 12/20 = **3/5**
11. (4 + 6) ÷ 20 = **1/2**
12. x/(x + 12) = 1/3, so 3x = x + 12 and **x = 6**
