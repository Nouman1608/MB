Condensed notes on the Workforce planning content (5.4 on this site) in the area of study The workforce, OCR A Level Business H431, Version 2.1 (January 2026), final assessment summer 2027. Full explanations and two worked examples are in the [workforce planning study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-workforce-planning-ocr-alevel-business/). Test yourself with the [workforce planning practice questions](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-workforce-planning-ocr-alevel-business-practice/). All figures are invented.

## What the specification lists

- What is meant by workforce planning; impact and importance to a business and its stakeholders; recommend and justify a workforce plan.
- Flexible working practices: part-time, flexi-time, compressed hours, home or remote working, annualised hours, term-time working, structured time off in lieu, job sharing and zero hours contracts.
- Evaluate the impact of flexible practices; recommend and justify them.

## Core recall

### Workforce planning: four steps

1. **Forecast demand:** from the business plan, productivity and planned changes.
2. **Forecast supply:** current staff less leavers (turnover, retirement), plus internal moves.
3. **Identify the gap:** shortage (recruit, train, use flexible labour) or surplus (redeploy, stop recruiting, retrain, redundancy as a last resort).
4. **Act and review.**

- Demand for staff = forecast output ÷ output per employee.
- Supply = current staff − expected leavers (± transfers).
- Gap = demand − supply.

### Benefits and limits of planning

| Benefits | Limits |
|---|---|
| Right numbers and skills | Forecasts may be wrong |
| Planned recruitment and training | Takes time and expertise |
| Supports the business strategy | Shocks overturn the plan |
| Controls labour cost | Rigid plans reduce adaptability |
| Fewer sudden redundancies | Small firms may lack expertise |

### Flexible working practices

| Practice | Meaning | Benefit | Drawback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Part-time | Fewer hours than full-time | Cover at busy times | More staff to manage |
| Flexi-time | Choose start and finish within limits; core hours | Morale; fewer late arrivals | Hard to cover every time |
| Compressed hours | Full hours in fewer days | Fewer commutes | Tiredness; cover |
| Home or remote | Work away from the premises | Lower office cost; wider recruitment | Weaker teamwork and supervision |
| Annualised hours | Yearly total of hours spread to match demand | Matches demand; less overtime | Complex; long peak weeks |
| Term-time | Work during school terms | Attracts parents | Holiday cover |
| Structured TOIL | Extra hours taken back as time off | No overtime pay | Absence at awkward times |
| Job sharing | Two people share one job | Cover; two skill sets | Handover problems |
| Zero hours | No guaranteed hours | Lowest cost when quiet | Insecurity; high turnover |

### Choosing a practice

- Predictable seasonal demand: annualised hours.
- Unpredictable demand: part-time, zero hours (with care).
- Office or data work: remote or flexi-time.
- Customer-facing work: rotas and cover matter.
- Staff groups: parents, students, older workers may value flexibility; others want security.

## Worked reminder

Marsden Contact Centre (invented).

- Demand: 1,200,000 ÷ 8,000 = **150**. Leavers: 15% × 138 = 20.7, about 21; supply 138 − 21 = **117**; gap **33**.
- Four internal moves leave **29** external hires at £2,800 each = **£81,200**.
- If volume is 10% lower, demand 135 and gap 18: over-recruiting by 15 costs 15 × £28,000 = **£420,000** a year.
- Dene Garden Centre: 35,520 hours needed (40 weeks × 600 + 12 weeks × 960). Standard contracts £436,800 + overtime £90,720 = **£527,520**; annualised hours 35,520 × £14 = **£497,280**; saving **£30,240**.

## Recommending: what to include

1. Demand, supply, gap with figures.
2. Action: recruit, train, redeploy or use flexible labour.
3. Cost of the action.
4. Risk if forecasts are wrong, and how flexibility reduces it.
5. Effect on stakeholders.
6. Condition: what the plan depends on.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Workforce planning | Forecasting labour needs and supply and closing the gap |
| Labour turnover | Proportion of staff who leave in a period |
| Core hours | Times when all staff must be present under flexi-time |
| Overtime | Hours worked beyond the contract, usually at higher pay |
| Full-time equivalent | Hours worked expressed as the number of full-time staff |
| Time off in lieu | Time off taken instead of pay for extra hours |
| Zero hours contract | A contract with no guaranteed hours |

## Exam traps

- Allow for leavers when forecasting supply.
- A plan must show demand, supply and the gap.
- Flexibility is not always good for staff, for example zero hours contracts.
- Do not confuse job sharing with part-time or flexi-time with compressed hours.
- State the risk that forecasts are wrong.
- Give a judgement that depends on the case.

## Self-test

1. Define workforce planning.
2. Output needed is 90,000 units and each employee makes 1,500 a year. How many employees are needed?
3. There are 55 staff and 10% will leave. How many are expected to remain?
4. What is the gap, and what could the business do?
5. Define annualised hours.
6. Give one drawback of zero hours contracts for employees.
7. Give one benefit of home working for a business.
8. Distinguish between job sharing and part-time working.
9. An overtime hour costs £18 and a standard hour £12. A business needs 500 extra hours. Calculate the saving from using standard annualised hours instead of overtime.
10. Give two reasons why a workforce plan may need to change.

### Answers

1. Forecasting how many people with which skills the business will need, comparing this with expected supply and deciding how to close the gap.
2. 90,000 ÷ 1,500 = **60 employees**.
3. 10% of 55 = 5.5 leavers, so about **49 or 50** remain (55 − 5.5 = 49.5).
4. 60 − 49.5 = about **10 or 11** short. Recruit, train, or use flexible labour.
5. A contract that sets the total hours for the year, spread across the year to match demand.
6. Any one: no guaranteed income; insecurity; difficulty planning finances.
7. Any one: lower office costs; wider choice of recruits; staff satisfaction.
8. Job sharing is two people sharing one job and its responsibilities; part-time is an employee working fewer hours in their own post.
9. Overtime: 500 × £18 = £9,000; standard: 500 × £12 = £6,000; saving **£3,000**.
10. Any two: sales forecasts change; staff leave faster than expected; new technology; law changes; a competitor's actions.
