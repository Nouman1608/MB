These notes condense section **3.2.5.3 Shapes of complex ions** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 1 and Paper 3. Explanations and worked examples are in the [Shapes of complex ions study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-shapes-of-complex-ions/), and exam-style questions are in the [Shapes of complex ions practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-shapes-of-complex-ions-practice/). The wider topic is in the [Transition metals revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-transition-metals-revision-notes/).

## What you must be able to do

- State and draw the **octahedral, tetrahedral, square planar and linear** shapes.
- Describe **cis–trans isomerism** (octahedral with monodentate ligands; square planar).
- Describe **optical isomerism** (octahedral with bidentate ligands).
- State that **cisplatin is the cis isomer** and that Tollens' reagent contains the linear [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Octahedral | Six ligands, 90° |
| Tetrahedral | Four ligands, 109.5° |
| Square planar | Four ligands in a plane, 90° |
| Linear | Two ligands, 180° |
| cis | Identical ligands adjacent (90°) |
| trans | Identical ligands opposite (180°) |
| Optical isomers | Non-superimposable mirror images |
| Bidentate ligand | Forms two co-ordinate bonds |

## Shapes

| Shape | Co-ordination number | Angle | Ligand type | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Octahedral | 6 | 90° | Small (H₂O, NH₃, bidentate) | [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺, [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ |
| Tetrahedral | 4 | 109.5° | Large (Cl⁻) | [CuCl₄]²⁻, [CoCl₄]²⁻ |
| Square planar | 4 | 90° | Pt²⁺ with NH₃ and Cl⁻ | [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] |
| Linear | 2 | 180° | NH₃ with Ag⁺ | [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ |

Why chloride gives four: Cl⁻ is large, so only four fit round the metal ion.

## Cis–trans isomerism

A special case of E–Z isomerism. It needs two different ligands that can be placed next to each other (cis) or opposite (trans).

```
 cis-[Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂]          trans-[Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂]
 Cl      Cl                  Cl      NH₃
   \    /                      \    /
     Pt                          Pt
   /    \                      /    \
 H₃N     NH₃                H₃N     Cl
```

| Complex type | cis–trans? |
|---|---|
| Square planar, two pairs of different ligands (MA₂B₂) | Yes |
| Square planar with three identical ligands (MA₃B) | No |
| Octahedral with MA₄B₂ | Yes: Cl⁻ opposite (trans) or adjacent (cis) |
| Tetrahedral | No |

**Cisplatin** is the cis isomer of [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] and is used as an anti-cancer drug. The trans isomer is not effective in the same way.

## Optical isomerism

- Occurs in octahedral complexes with **three bidentate ligands**, for example [Ni(H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂)₃]²⁺ and [Cr(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻.
- Also in cis-[M(bidentate)₂X₂] complexes, such as cis-[Co(en)₂Cl₂]⁺.
- The two forms are **non-superimposable mirror images** (no plane of symmetry).
- The trans form of [M(en)₂X₂] has a plane of symmetry, so there is no optical isomer.

Drawing tips: draw the first isomer, add a mirror line, and draw the reflection. Show each bidentate ligand as an arc joining two donor atoms (such as N∩N).

## Decision method

1. Count the co-ordinate bonds and decide the shape.
2. Linear or tetrahedral: no stereoisomers.
3. Square planar or octahedral: look for pairs of different ligands (cis–trans).
4. Octahedral with bidentate ligands: check for a plane of symmetry (optical).

## Worked examples

**Shape of [CoCl₄]²⁻.** Four Cl⁻ ligands: tetrahedral, 109.5°, no isomers.

**Isomers of [Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺.** Octahedral. Cl⁻ ligands 180° apart: trans. Cl⁻ ligands 90° apart: cis. Cobalt is +3 (overall +1 = Co + 2 × (–1)).

**[Ni(en)₃]²⁺.** Octahedral with three bidentate ligands: two optical isomers.

## Links between ligand type and shape

| Ligand | Size and charge | Typical complex with Cu²⁺ or Co²⁺ |
|---|---|---|
| H₂O | Small, neutral | Octahedral, [M(H₂O)₆]²⁺ |
| NH₃ | Small, neutral | Octahedral, [M(NH₃)₆]²⁺ (or [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺) |
| Cl⁻ | Larger, 1– | Tetrahedral, [MCl₄]²⁻ |
| en, C₂O₄²⁻ | Bidentate | Octahedral, three ligands per ion |

When the ligand changes from water to chloride, the co-ordination number falls from 6 to 4 and the shape changes from octahedral to tetrahedral.

## Counting isomers: quick reference

| Complex | Shape | Isomerism | Number of isomers |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] | Square planar | cis–trans | 2 |
| [Pt(NH₃)₃Cl]⁺ | Square planar | None | 1 |
| [Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺ | Octahedral | cis–trans | 2 |
| [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ | Octahedral | None | 1 |
| [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ | Octahedral | Optical | 2 |
| trans-[Co(en)₂Cl₂]⁺ | Octahedral | None (plane of symmetry) | 1 |
| cis-[Co(en)₂Cl₂]⁺ | Octahedral | Optical | 2 |
| [CuCl₄]²⁻ | Tetrahedral | None | 1 |

## Naming the key idea in an answer

- For cis–trans: "different arrangements of the same ligands, the same side or opposite sides".
- For optical: "non-superimposable mirror images".
- For shape: name the shape, give the angle, and link to the size of the ligand or the co-ordination number.

## Drawing checklist

- Put the central metal ion in the middle with its symbol.
- Draw one line for each co-ordinate bond, from the donor atom of the ligand to the metal.
- Use wedges and dashes in octahedral and tetrahedral drawings to show bonds towards and away from you.
- Add square brackets and the overall charge.
- Label the shape and bond angle if the question asks.

## Exam traps

- Bond angle of 90° for a tetrahedral complex.
- [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] described as tetrahedral.
- Cisplatin given as the trans isomer.
- "Cis means same side" without naming the ligands.
- Optical isomers drawn that can be superimposed.
- Tetrahedral complexes said to have cis–trans isomers.
- Charge missing from the drawn complex.
- Co-ordination number mixed up with the number of ligands for bidentate complexes.

## Self-test

1. What is the shape of [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺?
2. What is the bond angle in a tetrahedral complex?
3. Why does Cl⁻ form tetrahedral complexes?
4. What shape is the ion in Tollens' reagent?
5. Which isomer is cisplatin?
6. What is the co-ordination number of [Ni(en)₃]²⁺?
7. What kind of isomerism does [Cr(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻ show?
8. What does trans mean for two identical ligands?
9. Does [CoCl₄]²⁻ show cis–trans isomerism?
10. What is the oxidation state of platinum in [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂]?
11. What is meant by non-superimposable mirror images?
12. How many cis–trans isomers does [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] have?

### Answers

1. Octahedral.
2. 109.5°.
3. Chloride ions are large, so only four fit round the metal ion.
4. Linear ([Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺).
5. The cis isomer.
6. 6.
7. Optical isomerism.
8. They are on opposite sides of the central ion (180° apart).
9. No.
10. +2.
11. Two structures that are reflections of each other and cannot be placed exactly on top of each other.
12. Two (cis and trans).
