## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Transversal | A line crossing two or more other lines |
| Vertically opposite angles | The pair of angles opposite each other where two straight lines cross |
| Alternate angles | Between parallel lines on opposite sides of a transversal (Z shape) |
| Corresponding angles | On the same side of the transversal, in matching positions (F shape) |
| Co-interior angles | Between parallel lines on the same side of the transversal (C shape); add to 180° |
| Interior angle | An angle inside a polygon at a vertex |
| Exterior angle | The angle between one side and the next side extended |
| Regular polygon | All sides and all angles equal |
| Rectilinear figure | A shape made of straight lines |

## The facts, with the reasons to write

```
angles at a point                  add up to 360°
angles on a straight line          add up to 180°
vertically opposite angles         are equal
alternate angles                   are equal          (parallel lines)
corresponding angles               are equal          (parallel lines)
co-interior angles                 add up to 180°     (parallel lines)
angles in a triangle               add up to 180°
angles in a quadrilateral          add up to 360°
exterior angle of a triangle       = sum of the two interior opposite angles
```

Write the full fact as the reason. "Z angles" or "F angles" do not earn the reason mark.

## Why the triangle sum is 180°

Draw a line through one vertex parallel to the opposite side. Two alternate pairs copy the other two angles up to that vertex, where the three angles sit on a straight line: 180°.

## Polygon formulae

```
interior angle sum             = (n − 2) × 180°
exterior angle sum             = 360°  (any polygon)
interior + exterior            = 180°  (at each vertex)
regular polygon exterior angle = 360° ÷ n
number of sides                = 360° ÷ exterior angle
```

| Regular polygon | n | Interior sum | Exterior angle | Interior angle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pentagon | 5 | 540° | 72° | 108° |
| Hexagon | 6 | 720° | 60° | 120° |
| Octagon | 8 | 1080° | 45° | 135° |
| Decagon | 10 | 1440° | 36° | 144° |
| Dodecagon | 12 | 1800° | 30° | 150° |

## Problem-solving moves

- **Unknown in an expression:** set up an equation using one fact, solve, then substitute back and check.
- **Bent line between parallels:** draw an extra line parallel to both through the bend; the angle at the bend is the sum of the two alternate angles.
- **Does a regular polygon with interior angle x exist?** Find 180 − x, divide into 360. A whole number of at least 3 means yes.
- **Shapes meeting at a point:** add their angles; they fit with no gap only if the total is exactly 360°.
- **Number of sides from an interior sum:** divide by 180 and add 2.

Reminder: (2x + 15)° and (4x − 25)° are corresponding angles, so 2x + 15 = 4x − 25, x = 20, and each angle is 55°.

Reminder: a regular polygon with interior angle 156° has exterior angle 24°, so 360 ÷ 24 = 15 sides.

Reminder: three angles at a point are 90°, 2y° and 3y°. Then 5y = 270 and y = 54.

## Exam traps

- Assuming lines are parallel because they look parallel.
- Using alternate angles where the angles are actually co-interior (they add to 180°, not equal).
- (n − 2) × 180 confused with n × 180 or 360 ÷ n.
- Dividing 360 by the interior angle.
- Forgetting that the exterior angle sum is 360° for every polygon, not just regular ones.
- No reasons, or a reason for only the last step.
- Finding x but not the angle the question asked for.

## Quick self-test

1. Three angles on a straight line are 48°, x° and 2x°. Find x.
2. Four angles at a point are 90°, 85°, y° and y°. Find y.
3. Two lines cross. One angle is 64°. Write down the other three angles.
4. Parallel lines are crossed by a transversal. One angle is 118°. Write down its alternate angle and its co-interior angle.
5. (3x − 20)° and (x + 40)° are alternate angles. Find x and the size of each angle.
6. Find the sum of the interior angles of a nonagon (9 sides).
7. Find the interior angle of a regular decagon.
8. A regular polygon has an exterior angle of 20°. How many sides has it?
9. Can a regular polygon have an interior angle of 130°? Explain.
10. A triangle has angles 52° and 67°. Find the exterior angle at the third vertex.
11. Do a regular hexagon, two squares and an equilateral triangle fit exactly round a point?
12. A polygon's interior angles add up to 2340°. How many sides has it?

### Answers

1. 48 + 3x = 180, so 3x = 132 and x = 44
2. 175 + 2y = 360, so y = 92.5
3. 116°, 64°, 116° (vertically opposite angles are equal; angles on a line add to 180°)
4. Alternate 118°; co-interior 62°
5. 3x − 20 = x + 40, so x = 30; each angle is 70°
6. 7 × 180° = 1260°
7. 180° − 36° = 144°
8. 360 ÷ 20 = 18 sides
9. No: exterior angle 50°, and 360 ÷ 50 = 7.2 is not a whole number
10. 52° + 67° = 119°
11. 120 + 90 + 90 + 60 = 360, so yes
12. 2340 ÷ 180 = 13, so 13 + 2 = 15 sides
