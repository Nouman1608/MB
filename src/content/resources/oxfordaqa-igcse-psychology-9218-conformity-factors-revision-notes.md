Quick-recall notes for "how social factors (group size, anonymity and task difficulty) and
dispositional factors (personality, expertise) affect conformity to majority influence. Conformity as
investigated by Asch", in **3.2.2 Social influence** of OxfordAQA International GCSE Psychology (9218).
For full explanations and worked answers, use the
[factors affecting conformity study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-psychology-9218-conformity-factors/);
for the basic Asch and Milgram studies, see the
[Conformity and Obedience revision notes](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-psychology-conformity-obedience-revision-notes/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Conformity** | Changing behaviour or belief because of real or imagined group pressure |
| **Majority influence** | Pressure from the larger part of a group |
| **Normative social influence** | Conforming to fit in and be accepted |
| **Informational social influence** | Conforming because you believe the group is right |
| **Compliance** | Agreeing publicly while privately disagreeing |
| **Internalisation** | Genuinely accepting the group's view |
| **Critical trial** | A trial where confederates all give the same wrong answer |
| **Unanimity** | Everyone in the majority agreeing |
| **Anonymity** | Responses cannot be identified by the group |
| **Task difficulty** | How hard or ambiguous the judgement is |
| **Expertise** | Knowledge or skill in the task's area |

## Asch: the baseline

- Male American college students; line-judgement task; answers given aloud.
- 18 trials, 12 critical; confederates answer first.
- About 37% conformity on critical trials; about 75% conformed at least once; about 25% never.
- Alone, participants made almost no errors.
- Mostly **normative**: conformers often knew the answer was wrong.

## Social factors and Asch's variations

| Factor | Variation | Result | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Group size** | 1, 2, 3 or more confederates | About 3%, 13%, 32%; little rise after 3 | Bigger unanimous majority → more normative pressure |
| **Anonymity** | Participant writes answer privately | Conformity falls to about an eighth of trials | No audience → no normative pressure |
| **Task difficulty** | Lines made closer in length | Conformity rises | Uncertainty → informational influence |
| *(Related: unanimity)* | One confederate gives right answer | Conformity falls sharply | Broken agreement reduces pressure |

## Dispositional factors

**Personality** → more conformity with low self-esteem, high need for approval, external locus of
control. Evidence: a quarter of Asch's participants never conformed in the same situation.

**Expertise** → less conformity on tasks in one's own field, because of confidence in one's own
judgement (less informational influence) and feeling entitled to disagree (less normative pressure).
Less expertise than the group → more conformity, especially on hard tasks.

## Factor → influence map

| Factor | Normative | Informational |
|---|---|---|
| Group size | Yes | Slightly |
| Anonymity | Yes | No |
| Task difficulty | Slightly | Yes |
| Personality | Yes | Yes |
| Expertise | Yes | Yes |

## Applying the factors to a scenario

| Detail in the question | Factor | Likely effect | Influence |
|---|---|---|---|
| "All six friends said..." | Group size (and unanimity) | More conformity | Normative |
| "Votes were by show of hands" | No anonymity | More conformity | Normative |
| "She had never seen this kind of problem before" | Task difficulty | More conformity | Informational |
| "He is shy and anxious about what others think" | Personality | More conformity | Normative |
| "She is a qualified pharmacist" (on a medicine question) | Expertise | Less conformity | Informational |
| "One classmate disagreed out loud" | Broken unanimity | Less conformity | Normative |

## One-sentence explanations to adapt

- **Group size:** "As the majority grew, the pressure to fit in increased, so X agreed publicly."
- **Anonymity:** "Because X's answer was private, no one could see X disagree, so normative pressure
  was removed."
- **Task difficulty:** "Because the task was hard, X was unsure and assumed the group was right."
- **Expertise:** "X's knowledge gave X confidence, so X relied less on the group."

## Reducing conformity in practice

- Collect views **privately** first (anonymity).
- Ensure at least **one dissenter** can speak (breaks unanimity).
- Make the task **clear** (reduces informational influence).
- Let people with **expertise** speak first, or ask for written expert judgements.

## Evaluation points

- **For:** controlled variations show cause and effect.
- **Against:** trivial task; 1950s American male students; deception and discomfort.
- **Balance:** situation matters (variations), and so does the person (25% never conformed).

## Exam traps

- **Anonymity reduces** conformity.
- Group size effect **levels off** after about three.
- Expertise and personality are **dispositional**.
- Name **normative** or **informational** influence in every explanation.
- The line task was **easy**, so most of Asch's conformity was normative.

## Self-test

1. Define conformity.
2. Distinguish normative from informational social influence.
3. Name the three social factors in the specification.
4. Name the two dispositional factors.
5. What happened to conformity as group size increased from 1 to 3 confederates?
6. What happened when the majority grew beyond three?
7. What happened when participants wrote their answers privately, and why?
8. How did making the lines more similar affect conformity?
9. Which type of influence explains the task-difficulty effect?
10. Give two personality traits linked to higher conformity.
11. Why is an expert less likely to conform on a task in their field?
12. Give one limitation of Asch's research as evidence about conformity.

**Answers:**
1. Changing behaviour or belief because of real or imagined pressure from a group.
2. Normative: conforming to fit in and be accepted. Informational: conforming because you believe the
   group is right.
3. Group size, anonymity, task difficulty.
4. Personality, expertise.
5. It rose from about 3% to about 32%.
6. There was little further increase.
7. It fell, because no one could see the participant disagree, so normative pressure was removed.
8. Conformity increased.
9. Informational social influence.
10. Any two: low self-esteem; high need for social approval; external locus of control.
11. They are confident in their own judgement, so they rely less on the group and feel able to
    disagree.
12. Any one: the task was trivial with no consequences; the sample was American male students in the
    1950s; participants were deceived.
