These questions cover sections 6.03a and 6.03b of the OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics specification, Version 2.1 (August 2026): solving linear and quadratic equations. Questions 1 to 6 suit both tiers. Questions 7 to 12 are labelled "(Higher tier only)" because they use the quadratic formula, rearranging, completing the square or algebraic fractions. Learn the methods from the [study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-solving-linear-and-quadratic-equations/) or the [revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-solving-linear-and-quadratic-equations-revision-notes/). ## Questions

**1.** Solve each equation.

**(a)** 5x + 7 = 32 **[2]**
**(b)** 8 − 3x = −10 **[2]**
**(c)** 4(x − 3) = 2x + 9 **[3]**

**2.** Solve each equation. Show your working.

**(a)** 7x − 4 = 3(x + 8) **[3]**
**(b)** 2(3x + 1) − 4(x − 2) = 20 **[3]**

**3.** Mia is x years old. Her brother is 5 years older than Mia. Their father is three times as old as Mia. The three ages add up to 80.

**(a)** Show that 5x + 5 = 80. **[2]**
**(b)** Work out the age of Mia's father. **[2]**

**4.** Firm P hires out a bicycle for £15 plus £6 per hour. Firm Q charges £9 per hour with no other charge.

**(a)** Form and solve an equation to find the number of hours for which the two firms charge the same. **[3]**
**(b)** Tom wants a bicycle for 4 hours and Jo wants one for 7 hours. Which firm is cheaper for each of them? Show your calculations. **[2]**

**5.** Solve each equation by factorising.

**(a)** x² − 7x + 10 = 0 **[3]**
**(b)** x² + 2x − 24 = 0 **[3]**
**(c)** x² − 81 = 0 **[2]**
**(d)** x² − 5x = 0 **[3]**

**6.** A rectangle has width x cm and length (x + 4) cm. Its area is 96 cm².

**(a)** Show that x² + 4x − 96 = 0. **[2]**
**(b)** Solve the equation to find the value of x. Explain why one solution is rejected. **[3]**
**(c)** Work out the perimeter of the rectangle. **[1]**

**7.** (Higher tier only) Solve (x + 3)(x − 2) = 24. **[4]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) Solve 2x² − 5x − 12 = 0 by factorising. **[4]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) Solve 2x² − 9x + 5 = 0. Give your answers correct to 3 significant figures. **[4]**

**10.** (Higher tier only)

**(a)** Complete the square for x² − 12x + 13, giving it as (x − a)² − b. **[2]**
**(b)** Use your result to solve x² − 12x + 13 = 0. Write the solutions as p ± √q. **[2]**

**11.** (Higher tier only) Solve 6/x − 2/(x + 2) = 1. Give your answers correct to 3 significant figures. **[6]**

**12.** (Higher tier only) Solve (3x − 1)/4 − (x + 2)/3 = 1. **[4]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 5x = 25 [1]; **x = 5** [1]
**(b)** −3x = −18 [1]; **x = 6** [1]
**(c)** 4x − 12 = 2x + 9 [1]; 2x = 21 [1]; **x = 10.5** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the expanded line in part (c). Subtracting 2x from both sides before moving the 12 keeps the working short and makes slips easy to spot.

**2. (a)** 7x − 4 = 3x + 24 [1]; 4x = 28 [1]; **x = 7** [1]
**(b)** 6x + 2 − 4x + 8 = 20 [1]; 2x + 10 = 20 [1]; **x = 5** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (b) the second bracket is multiplied by −4, so both terms change sign. A wrong sign here usually makes the final answer wrong, so check by substituting.

**3. (a)** Brother is x + 5 and father is 3x, so x + (x + 5) + 3x = 80 [1]; collecting terms gives 5x + 5 = 80 [1]
**(b)** 5x = 75, so x = 15 [1]; father is 3 × 15 = **45 years** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* On a "show that" question, write the unsimplified equation first and then the simplified one. Jumping straight to 5x + 5 = 80 leaves no working to assess.

**4. (a)** 15 + 6h = 9h [1]; 15 = 3h [1]; **h = 5 hours** [1]
**(b)** Tom, 4 hours: P costs £39 and Q costs £36, so **Q is cheaper** [1]. Jo, 7 hours: P costs £57 and Q costs £63, so **P is cheaper** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The answer 5 hours is where the costs are equal, not where one firm wins. State a conclusion for each person, with both costs shown.

**5. (a)** (x − 2)(x − 5) = 0 [1]; x = 2 [1]; x = 5 [1]
**(b)** (x + 6)(x − 4) = 0 [1]; x = −6 [1]; x = 4 [1]
**(c)** x² = 81 or (x − 9)(x + 9) = 0 [1]; **x = 9 or x = −9** [1]
**(d)** x(x − 5) = 0 [1]; x = 0 [1]; x = 5 [1] **[11]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (c) has two solutions, and part (d) has the solution x = 0. Dividing by x in part (d) removes it, so factorise instead.

**6. (a)** Area = x(x + 4) = 96 [1]; expanding gives x² + 4x = 96, so x² + 4x − 96 = 0 [1]
**(b)** (x + 12)(x − 8) = 0 [1]; x = −12 or x = 8 [1]; a width cannot be negative, so **x = 8** [1]
**(c)** The sides are 8 cm and 12 cm, so the perimeter is **40 cm** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the reason for rejecting −12 in words that refer to the context. "It is negative" alone is weaker than "a width cannot be negative".

**7.** Expand: x² + x − 6 = 24 [1]; rearrange: x² + x − 30 = 0 [1]; (x + 6)(x − 5) = 0 [1]; **x = −6 or x = 5** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A common slip is to set each bracket equal to 24. The product must equal 0 before you split it, so rearrange first.

**8.** The numbers with product −24 and sum −5 are −8 and +3, so 2x² − 8x + 3x − 12 = 0 [1]; 2x(x − 4) + 3(x − 4) = 0 [1]; (2x + 3)(x − 4) = 0 [1]; **x = −3/2 or x = 4** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Expand your brackets back out to check them. It takes a few seconds and confirms the middle term.

**9.** a = 2, b = −9, c = 5, so x = (9 ± √(81 − 40)) / 4 [1]; b² − 4ac = 41 [1]; x = (9 ± √41) / 4 [1]; **x = 3.85 or x = 0.649** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the formula and the substituted values before evaluating. Keep the brackets around −9 when squaring so the sign cannot be lost.

**10. (a)** (x − 6)² − 36 + 13 [1]; **(x − 6)² − 23** [1]
**(b)** (x − 6)² = 23, so x − 6 = ±√23 [1]; **x = 6 ± √23** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep ± when you take the square root, and write the solutions in the form requested. Since 23 has no square factor, √23 cannot be simplified.

**11.** Multiply every term by x(x + 2), with x not 0 or −2 [1]; 6(x + 2) − 2x = x(x + 2) [1]; 4x + 12 = x² + 2x [1]; x² − 2x − 12 = 0 [1]; x = (2 ± √52) / 2 = 1 ± √13 [1]; **x = 4.61 or x = −2.61** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Multiply the right-hand side 1 by the common denominator too. Leaving it out gives an equation that no longer matches the original.

**12.** Multiply every term by 12: 3(3x − 1) − 4(x + 2) = 12 [1]; 9x − 3 − 4x − 8 = 12 [1]; 5x = 23 [1]; **x = 23/5** (4.6) [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The minus sign in front of the second fraction applies to the whole bracket (x + 2). Write the bracket explicitly before expanding.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Expanding brackets with a negative multiplier and changing only the first term.
- Giving only one root of a quadratic, or losing x = 0 after dividing by x.
- Not setting the right-hand side to 0 before factorising.
- Leaving a negative length as an answer, or failing to say why it is rejected.
- Stopping at the value that makes two costs equal without answering the question.
- Writing the quadratic formula with the wrong sign on −b.
- Rounding before the end, or writing a decimal where an exact form was requested.
- Forgetting to multiply the whole-number term by the common denominator.
- Not substituting the answer back into the original equation to catch a slip, especially when negative numbers are involved.
- Mixing up the signs in a bracket and its root, for example writing x = 2 from the bracket (x + 2).
- Ignoring the accuracy instruction in the question: exact form, fraction, or 3 significant figures.
