These notes condense content item 2.5.2, Effective recruitment, from Theme 2 Topic 2.5 Making human resource decisions in the Pearson Edexcel GCSE (9-1) Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022. The [effective recruitment study guide](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-effective-recruitment/) has the worked examples, and the [effective recruitment practice questions](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-effective-recruitment-practice/) let you test yourself. All businesses mentioned are invented.

## The item at a glance

- **Job roles and responsibilities:** directors, senior managers, supervisors/team leaders, operational staff, support staff.
- **Documents:** person specification, job description, application form, CV.
- **Methods:** internal and external recruitment, chosen to meet different business needs.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Recruitment | Finding and attracting suitable people to apply for a vacancy and choosing between them |
| Vacancy | A job that needs filling, because it is new or someone has left |
| Director | Sets long-term aims and direction for the whole business; answers to the owners |
| Senior manager | Runs a function or area of the business, turning directors' plans into action |
| Supervisor/team leader | Organises and checks the daily work of a small team |
| Operational staff | Make the product or provide the service |
| Support staff | Help others to work, such as IT, cleaning, reception or payroll |
| Job description | Describes the job: title, duties, reporting line, hours, location |
| Person specification | Describes the ideal person: skills, qualifications, experience, qualities |
| Application form | A form designed by the business that asks every candidate the same questions |
| CV | A summary of education, experience and skills written by the candidate |
| Internal recruitment | Filling a vacancy with an existing employee |
| External recruitment | Filling a vacancy with someone from outside the business |
| Shortlisting | Choosing which applicants go on to the next stage by comparing them with the person specification |

## Roles: one-line recall

- **Directors** → whole business, long term.
- **Senior managers** → one department or region, medium term.
- **Supervisors/team leaders** → one team, day to day.
- **Operational staff** → do the core work.
- **Support staff** → help everyone else do theirs.

As a business grows, the owner's many jobs are split into separate roles, and each split creates a vacancy.

## Documents: the order they are used

1. Business writes the **job description** (what the job is).
2. Business writes the **person specification** (who can do it), often with essential and desirable criteria.
3. Business advertises internally or externally.
4. Candidates send an **application form** or a **CV**.
5. Business shortlists by checking each candidate against the person specification.

**Application form vs CV**

| | Application form | CV |
|---|---|---|
| Who designs it | Business | Candidate |
| Strength | Easy, fair comparison; the business asks for exactly what it needs | Shows the candidate's own presentation; quick for candidates |
| Weakness | Takes candidates longer, may put some off | Hard to compare; information may be missing |

## Internal vs external

| | Internal | External |
|---|---|---|
| Examples of methods | Staff noticeboard, staff email, internal website | Job websites, agencies, newspapers, social media, job centre |
| Cost and speed | Cheaper and quicker | Dearer and slower |
| Candidate knowledge | Ability already known | Ability less certain |
| Effect on staff | Motivates through promotion | Existing staff may feel overlooked |
| New skills and ideas | None brought in | New skills and fresh ideas |
| Knock-on effect | Leaves another vacancy to fill | No extra vacancy created |

Match to need: speed, low cost, loyalty → internal. New skills, many posts, specialist → external.

## Useful calculations (standard methods)

- Cost per suitable applicant = cost of the method ÷ number of suitable applicants.
- Agency fee = fee percentage × salary.
- Percentage shortlisted = shortlisted ÷ applicants × 100.

## Exam traps

- Job description = **job**; person specification = **person**. Do not swap them.
- An application form is designed by the business, not the candidate.
- Internal recruitment is rarely free: the promoted person's old job must be filled.
- A desirable criterion cannot make up for a missing essential one when shortlisting.
- "Advertise externally" is too vague in a Justify answer; name a method and its cost or reach.
- When identifying a role in a case, use the scope of the responsibility, not the job title alone.

## Self-test

1. Which role sets the long-term direction of the whole business?
2. A worker on a café till serves customers. Which role is this?
3. Which document lists the duties of the job?
4. Which document is used to judge candidates when shortlisting?
5. Give one advantage of an application form over a CV for the business.
6. Give two methods of external recruitment.
7. Give one reason internal recruitment can motivate staff.
8. Why is internal recruitment not always cheaper overall?
9. When is external recruitment most appropriate?
10. An agency charges 12% of a £30,000 salary. Calculate the fee.
11. A £420 advert attracts 6 suitable applicants. Calculate the cost per suitable applicant.
12. Name the role that answers staff questions during a shift and checks their work.

**Answers**

1. Directors.
2. Operational staff.
3. The job description.
4. The person specification.
5. Every candidate answers the same questions, so they are easy to compare.
6. Any two: job websites, recruitment agencies, local newspapers, social media, job centres.
7. It shows staff that promotion is possible, so they have a goal to work towards.
8. Promoting someone leaves their old job empty, which must also be filled.
9. When the business needs skills or ideas it does not have, or has many posts to fill.
10. 0.12 × £30,000 = **£3,600**.
11. £420 ÷ 6 = **£70**.
12. Supervisor or team leader.
