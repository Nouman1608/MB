## The atom

- Small, dense, **positive nucleus** at the centre.
- **Negative electrons** in orbit around it.
- Mostly empty space.
- Neutral atom: number of electrons = number of protons.

| Particle | Location | Relative charge | Relative mass |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proton | Nucleus | +1 | 1 |
| Neutron | Nucleus | 0 | 1 |
| Electron | Orbits | −1 | About 1/2000 |

## Alpha-particle scattering

**Set-up:** beam of alpha particles → very thin gold foil → movable
detector, all in a vacuum (alpha particles are absorbed by a few cm of
air).

| Observation | Shows |
|---|---|
| Most passed straight through | Atom mostly empty space; nucleus very small |
| A few deflected through large angles | Nucleus positively charged (repels positive alpha particles) |
| Very few bounced back | Nucleus contains most of the mass |

Small fraction strongly deflected → only alpha particles passing very close
to the tiny nucleus are affected → nucleus very small.

## The nucleus

- Made of **protons** and **neutrons** (nucleons).
- **Proton number Z** = number of protons = atomic number. Fixes the
  element.
- **Nucleon number A** = protons + neutrons = mass number.
- **Neutrons N = A − Z.**

**Nuclide notation:** ᴬ_Z X — nucleon number on top, proton number below,
before the symbol. Example: ⁴⁰₁₈Ar has 18 protons and 22 neutrons.

**Nuclide:** a particular kind of nucleus with a stated number of protons
and neutrons.

## Ions

| Change | Result | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Atom loses electrons | Positive ion | Magnesium (Z = 12) loses 2 → 12 p, 10 e, charge +2 |
| Atom gains electrons | Negative ion | Oxygen (Z = 8) gains 2 → 8 p, 10 e, charge −2 |

The nucleus (Z and A) does not change when an ion forms.

## Isotopes

- Same element → same **proton number**.
- Different numbers of **neutrons** → different **nucleon numbers**.
- Same chemical properties; different masses.
- An element may have more than one isotope; some isotopes are
  radioactive.

| Isotope | p | n |
|---|---|---|
| ¹²₆C | 6 | 6 |
| ¹⁴₆C | 6 | 8 |
| ²³⁵₉₂U | 92 | 143 |
| ²³⁸₉₂U | 92 | 146 |

## Counting particles — method

1. Protons = bottom number (Z).
2. Neutrons = top − bottom (A − Z).
3. Electrons = Z for a neutral atom; subtract the + charge or add the −
   charge for an ion.

Example: ²⁷₁₃Al³⁺ → 13 protons, 14 neutrons, 13 − 3 = 10 electrons.

## What changes, and what stays the same

| Change | Protons (Z) | Neutrons | Nucleon number (A) | Electrons | Same element? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Atom becomes a positive ion | Same | Same | Same | Fewer | Yes |
| Atom becomes a negative ion | Same | Same | Same | More | Yes |
| A different isotope | Same | Different | Different | Same (neutral atom) | Yes |
| Different proton number | Different | Any | Any | Any | No |

Rule of thumb: **Z decides the element; electrons decide the charge;
neutrons decide the isotope.**

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Nucleus | Small, dense, positively charged centre of the atom, containing protons and neutrons |
| Proton number (Z) | Number of protons in the nucleus |
| Nucleon number (A) | Total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus |
| Nucleon | A proton or a neutron |
| Nuclide | A particular type of nucleus with stated proton and nucleon numbers |
| Isotopes | Atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons |
| Ion | An atom that has gained or lost electrons and so is charged |

## Exam traps

- A is protons **plus** neutrons, not neutrons alone.
- Larger number on top in nuclide notation.
- Isotopes differ in **neutrons**, never protons.
- Positive ion = electrons **lost**. Nothing happens to the nucleus.
- "Most went straight through" → empty space, **not** positive charge.
- "Bounced back" → most of the mass (and charge) concentrated in the
  nucleus.
- Electrons cannot deflect alpha particles significantly — they are far
  too light.
- Gold foil must be thin; the apparatus must be evacuated.

## Self-test

1. Where is most of the mass of an atom?
2. Why is a neutral atom uncharged?
3. What did the large-angle deflections in the scattering experiment show?
4. What did the fact that most alpha particles passed straight through
   show?
5. Why was the scattering apparatus evacuated?
6. Define nucleon number.
7. How many neutrons are in ³¹₁₅P?
8. Write the nuclide notation for a nucleus with 20 protons and 20
   neutrons, using the symbol Ca.
9. A lithium atom (Z = 3) loses one electron. State the number of
   electrons and the charge of the ion.
10. Define isotopes.
11. Two nuclei both have 6 protons; one has 6 neutrons and the other 8.
    Are they the same element?
12. How many electrons are in ³²₁₆S²⁻?

**Answers**

1. In the nucleus.
2. It has equal numbers of protons and electrons, so the charges cancel.
3. The nucleus is positively charged (it repelled the positive alpha
   particles).
4. The atom is mostly empty space and the nucleus is very small.
5. Alpha particles are absorbed by a few centimetres of air.
6. The total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus.
7. 31 − 15 = **16**.
8. ⁴⁰₂₀Ca.
9. **2 electrons**; charge **+1**.
10. Atoms of the same element (same proton number) with different numbers
    of neutrons.
11. Yes — same proton number; they are isotopes of carbon.
12. 16 + 2 = **18**.
