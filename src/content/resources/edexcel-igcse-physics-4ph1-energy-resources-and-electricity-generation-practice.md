## Section A

**1.** Name two renewable and two non-renewable energy resources used to generate electricity. **[2]**

**2.** State the energy store of the fuel used in a coal-fired power station. **[1]**

**3.** Name the device in a power station that transfers energy electrically when it is turned by the turbine. **[1]**

**4.** A weather station on a remote mountain is powered by solar cells. Give one reason why solar cells suit this use. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why tidal power is more reliable than wind power. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Describe the energy transfers involved in generating electricity in a gas-fired power station. **[4]**

**7.** Some countries generate electricity from geothermal resources.

**(a)** Describe how a geothermal power station generates electricity. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why many countries cannot use this resource. **[1]**

**8.** An oil-fired power station has a total energy output of 1800 MJ each second. It transfers 630 MJ each second electrically to the grid.

**(a)** Calculate the efficiency of the power station. **[2]**
**(b)** State where most of the wasted energy goes. **[1]**
**(c)** Give one environmental disadvantage of generating electricity from oil. **[1]**

**9.** A house has a solar heating system on its roof and solar cells on a shed.

**(a)** Describe the energy transfer in the solar heating system. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the energy transfer in the solar cells. **[1]**
**(c)** Give one disadvantage that applies to both systems. **[1]**

**10.** A hydroelectric power station uses water stored in a reservoir behind a dam high in the mountains.

**(a)** Describe the energy transfers involved in generating electricity in this power station. **[3]**
**(b)** Give two disadvantages of building a hydroelectric power station. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why hydroelectric power stations are useful when demand for electricity suddenly rises. **[1]**

**11.** A country with a long, windy coastline and many sunny days generates most of its electricity in coal-fired power stations. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of replacing these with wind turbines and solar cells. **[6]**

**12.** Compare a nuclear power station with a coal-fired power station in terms of the carbon dioxide they release and the waste they produce. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two renewable: wind, hydroelectric, tidal, wave, geothermal, solar [1]. Any two non-renewable: coal, oil, gas, nuclear [1].

**2.** Chemical store [1].

**3.** The generator [1].

**4.** Any one: no fuel needs to be delivered; no moving parts, so little maintenance; no need to connect to the grid [1].

**5.** Tides rise and fall at predictable times every day [1], whereas wind speed depends on the weather and can drop to zero, so wind output cannot be guaranteed [1].

**6.** Energy in the chemical store of the gas is transferred by burning, heating water [1]. This transfers energy to the thermal store of the water, turning it to high-pressure steam [1]. The steam turns the turbine, transferring energy to its kinetic store [1]. The turbine turns the generator, which transfers energy electrically to the grid (with energy dissipated by heating the surroundings along the way) [1].

**7. (a)** Cold water is pumped down to hot rocks deep underground, transferring energy from the thermal store of the rocks to the water by heating [1]. The water returns to the surface as steam (or very hot water that makes steam) [1]. The steam drives a turbine, which turns a generator that transfers energy electrically [1].
**(b)** It needs hot rocks close enough to the surface to drill to, which are found only in some (often volcanic) regions [1].

**8. (a)** efficiency = 630 ÷ 1800 × 100% [1] = 35% [1].
**(b)** To the thermal store of the surroundings, mostly through the cooling water and air [1].
**(c)** Any one: burning oil releases carbon dioxide, a greenhouse gas linked to climate change; oil is non-renewable; oil spills during transport [1].

**9. (a)** Radiation from the Sun is absorbed by the dark collector panels [1], transferring energy by heating to the thermal store of the water flowing through them [1].
**(b)** Radiation (light) is transferred electrically by the cells, directly [1].
**(c)** Any one: no output at night; less output in cloudy weather; large area of panels needed for a large output [1].

**10. (a)** Water in the high reservoir has energy in its gravitational store [1]. As it flows down through pipes, this is transferred to the kinetic store of the water [1]. The moving water turns turbines, which turn generators that transfer energy electrically [1].
**(b)** Any two: flooding a valley to make the reservoir destroys habitats; people may have to move; building the dam is very expensive; only possible in suitable mountainous areas with enough rainfall [1] [1].
**(c)** Water can be released from the reservoir almost immediately, so the output can be increased very quickly [1].

**11.** One mark for each point made, to a maximum of six. A full answer gives advantages and disadvantages and a conclusion:
Wind and solar are renewable, so they will not run out, unlike coal [1].
They release no carbon dioxide while generating, so they would reduce the country's contribution to climate change; coal also releases sulfur dioxide, which causes acid rain [1].
There are no fuel costs once the turbines and panels are built [1].
Wind output depends on the wind and solar output stops at night and falls in cloud, so neither is reliable on its own [1].
Coal-fired stations can be controlled to match demand, so the country would need some controllable backup (for example gas or hydroelectric) or storage [1].
Many turbines and large areas of solar panels are needed to replace a large power station, with visual impact on the coast and landscape; conclusion: the switch is worthwhile for the environment and fuel supply, provided reliable backup is kept [1].

**12.** Coal: burning coal releases carbon dioxide [1], which is a greenhouse gas linked to climate change [1]. Nuclear: no carbon dioxide is released while generating, but it produces radioactive waste [1], which must be stored safely for a very long time (coal produces ash and gases instead, not radioactive waste) [1].

## Frequently asked questions

**How should I answer a six-mark "discuss" question on energy resources?**
Make six separate, developed points that cover both advantages and disadvantages, compare on the same criteria, and
finish with a conclusion that follows from what you have written.
