## Section A: short skills questions

Read the scene below, then answer Questions 1 to 6.

> *A railway platform at night. A bench. A single lamp. ROSA sits with a suitcase at her feet. TOM
> enters quickly, out of breath. He stops at the edge of the light.*
>
> TOM: The last train's gone.
>
> ROSA: *(not turning)* I know. I watched it go.
>
> TOM: Then why are you still here?
>
> ROSA: Because I watched it go.
>
> *TOM steps into the light. He sits at the far end of the bench. Neither speaks. A distant whistle.*
>
> TOM: I didn't think you'd wait.
>
> ROSA: *(picking up the suitcase and holding it on her knees)* Neither did I.

**1.** What does the opening stage direction establish about the setting? Give two details. **[2]**

**2.** Explain the effect of Tom stopping "at the edge of the light" before stepping into it. **[3]**

**3.** How does the repetition of "I watched it go" reveal Rosa's feelings? **[4]**

**4.** Explain how the stage direction "Neither speaks" could be used to create tension for an
audience. **[3]**

**5.** Rosa picks up the suitcase in the final line. Suggest two different interpretations of this
action. **[4]**

**6.** Write one staging note for Tom's line "I didn't think you'd wait", explaining how it could be
delivered and why. **[3]**

**7.** Explain the difference between dramatic irony and suspense, using a brief example of your own.
**[3]**

## Section B: exam-style questions (use your own studied play)

**8.** How does the playwright present power, and the struggle for it, in your studied play? **[30]**

**9.** Explore how the playwright uses the final scene of your studied play to shape the audience's
response to its central ideas. **[30]**

**10.** Choose a short exchange of about a page from the middle of your studied play. Explore how the
playwright creates tension in this exchange and how it connects to the play as a whole. **[30]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** Any two: night-time; a railway platform; a single lamp, suggesting isolation or a small pool of
light; a bench; Rosa alone with a suitcase, suggesting travel or departure [1 each, maximum 2].

**2.** Up to three marks. Tom is visually separated from Rosa at first [1]; staying at the edge of
the light suggests hesitation, as if he is unsure of his welcome [1]; stepping into it later marks a
decision to commit to the conversation, so the lighting shows the change in their relationship [1].

**3.** Up to four marks. Rosa's first use of the phrase sounds like a plain fact [1]. Repeating it
as her reason for staying turns it into an admission: she let the train go on purpose [1]. The
repetition, together with "not turning", suggests she is guarding her feelings and will not explain
them directly [1]. The audience must infer that she was waiting for Tom, which makes the moment more
powerful than a direct statement [1].

**4.** Up to three marks. Silence forces the audience to watch both characters' body language [1].
The distance on the bench keeps them physically apart, so the audience waits to see who will break
the silence [1]. The distant whistle reminds the audience of the missed train and of time passing,
heightening the tension [1].

**5.** Two marks for each developed interpretation. For example: she is preparing to leave after
all, so the action shows the relationship is still uncertain [2]; or she is holding the suitcase like
something precious or protective, so she is not ready to let go of her plan or her independence [2].
Credit any reasonable reading supported by the text.

**6.** Award one mark for a delivery suggestion, one for a reason linked to the text and one for
effect on the audience. Example: Tom could say it quietly, looking at the ground [1], because his
earlier hesitation suggests guilt or uncertainty [1], so the audience senses that he expected to have
lost her [1].

**7.** Dramatic irony: the audience knows something a character does not [1]. Suspense: uncertainty
about what will happen next [1]. A valid original example of either, such as an audience who has seen
a letter hidden while a character searches for it [1].

### Section B (approach: marks depend on your studied play)

These questions are marked holistically against level descriptors rather than point by point. The
guidance below describes a high-level response.

**8. Power.** A strong answer:

- defines what kind of power matters in the play (family, social, political, emotional) and states a
  clear argument about it (AO1, AO3);
- tracks how power shifts between characters at several points, such as the opening, the climax and
  the ending (AO1);
- analyses dramatic methods: who speaks most, interruptions, commands and questions, entrances and
  exits, and stage directions about position and movement (AO2);
- links to context where the play reflects the social structures of its time or setting (AO1);
- judges whether the play endorses or challenges the power it shows (AO3).

**9. The final scene.** A strong answer:

- identifies the central ideas the ending addresses and argues how the scene shapes the audience's
  response;
- analyses the final stage picture, last lines, exits and any silence (AO2);
- connects the ending back to the opening or to the climax, showing structural awareness (AO2, AO1);
- considers how different audiences might respond, or whether the ending is open (AO3).

**10. Tension in an exchange.** A strong answer:

- analyses the chosen exchange closely: short lines, interruptions, pauses, stage directions and
  dramatic irony (AO2);
- explains how tension is built and released for an audience, not just what the characters argue
  about;
- connects the exchange to earlier or later moments, showing why the tension matters to the play's
  structure and ideas (AO1);
- gives a personal judgement on the exchange's importance (AO3).

Answers that treat the play as prose, or that ignore the staging entirely, are unlikely to reach the
top levels.

## How to use this page

Spend about 25 minutes on Section A. Then answer one Section B question in 45 minutes from memory,
as Paper 1 is closed book. Afterwards, underline each place where you discussed staging; if there are
fewer than three, rewrite one paragraph to include a staging note.
