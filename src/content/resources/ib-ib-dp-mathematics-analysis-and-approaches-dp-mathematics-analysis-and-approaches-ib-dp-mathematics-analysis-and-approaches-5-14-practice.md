These questions cover **section 5.14 of IB DP Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches: implicit differentiation, related rates of change and optimisation**. It is AHL content, for HL students. Questions are labelled "(calculator-free)" or "(calculator allowed)". Give answers exactly or to 3 significant figures, with units.

Learn the methods first in the [section 5.14 study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-5-14/) and the [section 5.14 revision notes](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-5-14-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** (calculator-free) Find the gradient of the curve x²y + y² = 6 at the point (1, 2). **[4]**

**2.** (calculator-free) Find the gradient of the curve ln y + xy = 2 at the point (2, 1). **[4]**

**3.** (calculator-free) Let y = arctan x. By writing tan y = x, show that dy/dx = 1/(1 + x²). **[3]**

**4.** (calculator-free) The radius of a sphere increases at 0.1 cm s⁻¹. Find the rate of increase of its surface area when the radius is 5 cm. **[3]**

**5.** (calculator-free) The area of a square decreases at 6 cm² s⁻¹. Find the rate of change of its side length when the side is 3 cm. **[3]**

**6.** (calculator-free) A point moves on the circle x² + y² = 169. When it is at (5, 12), dx/dt = 3. Find dy/dt at that instant. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** (calculator-free) A curve has equation x² + xy + y² = 3.

**(a)** Show that dy/dx = −(2x + y)/(x + 2y). **[3]**
**(b)** Find the coordinates of the points where the tangent is parallel to the y-axis. **[4]**
**(c)** Find the value of d²y/dx² at the point (1, 1). **[4]**

**8.** (calculator-free) A 5 m ladder leans against a vertical wall. Its foot slides away from the wall at 0.4 m s⁻¹. Let x m be the distance of the foot from the wall, y m the height of the top, and θ the angle between the ladder and the ground.

**(a)** Find the rate at which the top slides down the wall when x = 3. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the rate of change of θ at that instant. **[3]**

**9.** (calculator allowed) A tank is a cone with its vertex at the bottom, of height 3 m and top radius 1 m. Water leaks out at 0.1 m³ per minute. Find the rate of change of the depth of water when the depth is 1.5 m. **[5]**

**10.** (calculator-free) Two ships leave a port at the same time. Ship A sails due north at 15 km h⁻¹ and ship B sails due east at 20 km h⁻¹. Find the rate at which the distance between them is increasing two hours later. **[5]**

**11.** (calculator allowed) A wire of length 20 cm is cut into two pieces. A piece of length x cm is bent into a square and the rest into a circle, where 0 ≤ x ≤ 20.

**(a)** Show that the total area enclosed is A(x) = x²/16 + (20 − x)²/(4π). **[3]**
**(b)** Find the value of x that minimises A, and the minimum area. **[4]**
**(c)** Find the maximum total area, and state how the wire should be used to obtain it. **[3]**

**12.** (calculator-free) A right circular cone has slant height 6 cm, so that r² + h² = 36.

**(a)** Show that its volume is V = (π/3)(36h − h³). **[2]**
**(b)** Find the exact maximum volume, justifying that it is a maximum. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** 2xy + x² dy/dx + 2y dy/dx = 0 [1] [1]; at (1, 2): 4 + dy/dx + 4 dy/dx = 0 [1]; **dy/dx = −4/5** [1] **[4]**

**2.** (1/y) dy/dx + y + x dy/dx = 0 [1] [1]; at (2, 1): dy/dx + 1 + 2 dy/dx = 0 [1]; **dy/dx = −1/3** [1] **[4]**

**3.** sec²y dy/dx = 1 [1]; dy/dx = 1/sec²y = 1/(1 + tan²y) [1] = 1/(1 + x²) [1] **[3]**

**4.** S = 4πr², dS/dt = 8πr dr/dt [1]; = 8π(5)(0.1) [1] = **4π cm² s⁻¹** (≈ 12.6) [1] **[3]**

**5.** A = s², dA/dt = 2s ds/dt [1]; −6 = 6 ds/dt [1]; **ds/dt = −1 cm s⁻¹** (decreasing at 1 cm s⁻¹) [1] **[3]**

**6.** 2x dx/dt + 2y dy/dt = 0 [1]; 5(3) + 12 dy/dt = 0 [1]; **dy/dt = −5/4** [1] **[3]**

**7. (a)** 2x + y + x dy/dx + 2y dy/dx = 0 [1] [1]; dy/dx(x + 2y) = −(2x + y), giving the result [1]
**(b)** Parallel to the y-axis when x + 2y = 0, so x = −2y [1]; 4y² − 2y² + y² = 3 [1], y = ±1 [1]; **(−2, 1) and (2, −1)** [1]
**(c)** At (1, 1), dy/dx = −3/3 = −1 [1]; differentiate 2x + y + x y′ + 2y y′ = 0 again: 2 + 2y′ + x y″ + 2(y′)² + 2y y″ = 0 [1]; at (1, 1): 2 − 2 + y″ + 2 + 2y″ = 0 [1]; **d²y/dx² = −2/3** [1] **[11]**
*Examiner insight:* Differentiating the unrearranged equation a second time is usually less error-prone than applying the quotient rule to the expression for dy/dx.

**8. (a)** x² + y² = 25, so 2x dx/dt + 2y dy/dt = 0 [1]; at x = 3, y = 4 [1]; dy/dt = −3(0.4)/4 = −0.3, so the top slides down at **0.3 m s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** cos θ = x/5, so −sin θ dθ/dt = (1/5) dx/dt [1]; sin θ = 4/5 [1]; dθ/dt = −(0.4/5)/(4/5) = **−0.1 rad s⁻¹** [1] **[6]**

**9.** Similar triangles: r = h/3 [1]; V = (1/3)π(h/3)²h = πh³/27 [1]; dV/dt = (πh²/9) dh/dt [1]; −0.1 = (π(2.25)/9) dh/dt [1]; dh/dt = **−0.127 m per minute** (the depth falls at 0.127 m per minute) [1] **[5]**

**10.** After t hours, y = 15t, x = 20t and D² = x² + y² [1]; 2D dD/dt = 2x dx/dt + 2y dy/dt [1]; at t = 2: x = 40, y = 30, D = 50 [1]; 50 dD/dt = 40(20) + 30(15) = 1250 [1]; **dD/dt = 25 km h⁻¹** [1] **[5]**

**11. (a)** Square side x/4, area x²/16 [1]; circle circumference 20 − x, so radius (20 − x)/(2π) [1]; area π((20 − x)/(2π))² = (20 − x)²/(4π) [1]
**(b)** A′(x) = x/8 − (20 − x)/(2π) = 0 [1]; πx = 4(20 − x), x = 80/(π + 4) [1] = **11.2 cm** (11.201…) [1]; minimum area **14.0 cm²** (14.002…) [1]
**(c)** End points: A(0) = 400/(4π) = 31.8 and A(20) = 25 [1] [1]; maximum **31.8 cm²**, when all of the wire is used for the circle (x = 0) [1] **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* The maximum here is an end point where A′ ≠ 0; solving A′ = 0 alone finds only the minimum.

**12. (a)** r² = 36 − h² [1]; V = (1/3)πr²h = (π/3)(36 − h²)h = (π/3)(36h − h³) [1]
**(b)** dV/dh = (π/3)(36 − 3h²) [1] = 0, so h² = 12, h = 2√3 [1]; d²V/dh² = −2πh < 0, so a maximum [1]; r² = 24 [1]; V = (π/3)(24)(2√3) = **16√3π cm³** (≈ 87.1) [1] **[7]**
