## Section A

**1.** Write down the next two terms of each sequence.

**(a)** 2, 8, 14, 20, … **[1]**

**(b)** 2, 10, 50, 250, … **[1]**

**(c)** 4, 6, 10, 16, 24, … **[1]**

**2.** Write down the term-to-term rule for the sequence 38, 31, 24, 17, … **[1]**

**3.** Find an expression for the nth term of 2, 8, 14, 20, … **[2]**

**4.** Find an expression for the nth term of 38, 31, 24, 17, … **[2]**

**5.** The nth term of a sequence is n³ + n. Write down the first three terms. **[2]**

**6.** The nth term of a sequence is 6n − 4. Show that 300 is not a term of the sequence. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Find an expression for the nth term of 5, 14, 27, 44, 65, … **[3]**

**8.** Find an expression for the nth term of 4, 6, 10, 16, 24, … **[3]**

**9.** The first five terms of a sequence are 9, 16, 21, 24, 25.

**(a)** Find an expression for the nth term. **[3]**

**(b)** Write down the greatest term in the sequence and explain why no later term is larger. **[2]**

**10.** Find an expression for the nth term of 3, 6, 11, 20, 37, … by comparing it with the powers of 2. **[2]**

**11.** The nth term of sequence A is 4n + 1. The terms of sequence B are 25, 81, 169, …, where each term of B is the square of the term of A in the same position.

**(a)** Write down an expression for the nth term of B. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the 4th term of B. **[1]**

**12.** Pattern 1 is a row of 1 square made from 4 matchsticks. Pattern 2 is a row of 2 squares made from 7 matchsticks, and Pattern 3 is a row of 3 squares made from 10 matchsticks.

**(a)** Find an expression for the number of matchsticks in Pattern n. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain, using the patterns, why your expression works. **[1]**

**(c)** Can a pattern be made from exactly 100 matchsticks? Give a reason. **[2]**

**13.** The nth term of a sequence is (2n − 1)/n².

**(a)** Write down the first four terms as fractions. **[2]**

**(b)** Which term of the sequence is 19/100? **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **26, 32** [1]

**(b)** **1250, 6250** [1]

**(c)** **34, 46** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* In (c) the differences are 2, 4, 6, 8, so the next differences are 10 and 12.

**2.** **Subtract 7** [1] **[1]**

**3.** Common difference 6, so 6n + c [1]; 6 + c = 2, c = −4: **6n − 4** [1] **[2]**

**4.** Common difference −7, so −7n + c [1]; −7 + c = 38, c = 45: **45 − 7n** [1] **[2]**

**5.** 1 + 1 = 2; 8 + 2 = 10; 27 + 3 = 30 [1]: **2, 10, 30** [1] **[2]**

**6.** 6n − 4 = 300 gives 6n = 304, n = 50.67 (50⅔) [1]. This is not a whole number, so 300 is not a term [1]. **[2]**

**7.** First differences 9, 13, 17, 21; second difference 4, so a = 2 [1]. Term − 2n²: 3, 6, 9, 12, which is 3n [1]. **Tₙ = 2n² + 3n** [1] **[3]**

**8.** First differences 2, 4, 6, 8; second difference 2, so a = 1 [1]. Term − n²: 3, 2, 1, 0, which is 4 − n [1]. **Tₙ = n² − n + 4** [1] **[3]**

**9. (a)** First differences 7, 5, 3, 1; second difference −2, so a = −1 [1]. Term + n²: 10, 20, 30, 40, which is 10n [1]. **Tₙ = 10n − n²** [1]

**(b)** **25** (the 5th term) [1]. The first differences keep falling by 2, so after 1 they become −1, −3, …, and every later term is smaller [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* A negative second difference gives a negative coefficient of n². 10n − n² = 25 − (n − 5)², which also shows the greatest value is 25 when n = 5.

**10.** Powers of 2: 2, 4, 8, 16, 32. The differences between the terms and these are 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 [1]. **Tₙ = 2ⁿ + n** [1] **[2]**

**11. (a)** **(4n + 1)²** [1]

**(b)** (4 × 4 + 1)² = 17² = **289** [1] **[2]**

**12. (a)** 4, 7, 10, … has common difference 3: **3n + 1** [1] [1]

**(b)** The first square needs 1 extra matchstick on the left side, and each square then adds 3 matchsticks [1].

**(c)** 3n + 1 = 100 gives n = 33 [1]. Yes, **Pattern 33** uses exactly 100 matchsticks [1]. **[5]**

**13. (a)** **1, 3/4, 5/9, 7/16** [2] (1 mark for three correct)

**(b)** Numerator 2n − 1 = 19 gives n = 10, and the denominator n² = 100 also matches [1]. It is the **10th term** [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Both the numerator and the denominator must fit the same n. Here 2(10) − 1 = 19 and 10² = 100.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing 7n + 45 or n − 7 for a decreasing sequence (Question 4).
- Forgetting to halve the second difference (Questions 7 to 9).
- Missing the negative sign of a in Question 9.
- Saying 300 is "nearly a term" or giving n = 51 in Question 6.
- Using differences in Question 10 instead of spotting the powers of 2.
- In Question 12(b), restating the formula rather than linking it to the matchsticks.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I know whether a sequence is quadratic or exponential?**
Check the second differences and the ratios. Constant second differences mean quadratic; a constant ratio between terms means exponential.

**Do I need to use subscript notation in answers?**
No. You may write "nth term = 3n + 1" or Tₙ = 3n + 1. The syllabus says subscript notation may be used in questions, so you need to understand it.

**What does "simple combinations" of sequences mean?**
Sequences built from familiar ones, such as 2ⁿ + n or n³ + 2, or the product of two linear sequences such as n(n + 1).
