These questions cover section **3.3.3.3 Ozone depletion** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2. Learn the ideas first in the [Ozone depletion study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-ozone-depletion/) and the [Ozone depletion revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-ozone-depletion-revision-notes/). Use Aᵣ values C = 12.0, F = 19.0, O = 16.0, Cl = 35.5.

## Questions

**1.** State why ozone in the upper atmosphere is beneficial. **[1]**

**2.** Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) were used as refrigerants. State two properties of CFCs that made them suitable for this use. **[2]**

**3.** CCl₂F₂ is a CFC.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction that forms chlorine atoms from CCl₂F₂ in the upper atmosphere. **[2]**

**(b)** Name the type of bond fission in this reaction. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why a C–Cl bond is broken and a C–F bond is not. **[2]**

**4.** Chlorine atoms catalyse the decomposition of ozone.

**(a)** Write two equations to show how this happens. **[2]**

**(b)** Write the overall equation for the decomposition of ozone. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why chlorine atoms are described as a catalyst. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why CFCs are able to reach the upper atmosphere before they react. **[2]**

**6.** A chlorine atom can be removed from the cycle in a termination step. Write an equation for a termination step involving a chlorine atom and a methyl radical. **[1]**

**7.** Explain how the work of different research groups led to legislation banning CFCs. **[3]**

**8.** A refrigerant is replaced by CH₂FCF₃.

**(a)** Explain why this compound does not deplete the ozone layer by the mechanism in question 4. **[2]**

**(b)** State one way in which the development of this compound illustrates the role of chemists. **[1]**

**9.** Assume that 6.05 g of CCl₂F₂ (Mᵣ = 121.0) releases one chlorine atom per molecule, and that each chlorine atom takes part in 4000 complete cycles. Each cycle destroys two ozone molecules.

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in mol, of CCl₂F₂. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the amount of ozone destroyed. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the mass of ozone destroyed (Mᵣ of O₃ = 48.0). **[1]**

**10.** Explain, using equations, how the release of chlorine atoms from CCl₃F results in the destruction of many ozone molecules. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** It absorbs harmful ultraviolet radiation from the Sun [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Any two from: chemically unreactive; non-toxic; non-flammable; easily liquefied or volatile [2]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** CCl₂F₂ → •CClF₂ + Cl• [1]; ultraviolet radiation as the condition [1]

**(b)** Homolytic fission [1]

**(c)** The C–Cl bond is weaker than the C–F bond [1]; so it needs less energy to break and the ultraviolet radiation can break it, while the stronger C–F bond remains [1]. **[5]**

**4. (a)** Cl• + O₃ → ClO• + O₂ [1]; ClO• + O₃ → 2O₂ + Cl• [1]

**(b)** 2O₃ → 3O₂ [1]

**(c)** Chlorine is used in the first step and regenerated in the second [1]; it is not used up overall and it speeds up the decomposition by providing an alternative route [1]. **[5]**

**5.** CFCs are chemically unreactive (stable) in the lower atmosphere [1]; so they are not broken down or removed there, and they slowly reach the upper atmosphere where ultraviolet radiation is strong enough to break the C–Cl bond [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The harm comes from stability in the lower atmosphere, not from reactivity.

**6.** Cl• + •CH₃ → CH₃Cl [1]. **[1]**

**7.** Evidence from research groups showed that ozone levels were falling [1]; results from independent groups, using different methods, agreed that the cause was chlorine from CFCs [1]; the consistent evidence gave governments enough confidence to agree to legislation to ban or phase out CFCs [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** It contains no chlorine and no C–Cl bonds, so ultraviolet radiation cannot release chlorine atoms [1]; without chlorine radicals there is no catalyst to break down ozone [1]

**(b)** Chemists developed chlorine-free alternatives to CFCs [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** n = 6.05 ÷ 121.0 = **0.0500 mol** [1]

**(b)** n(cycles) = 0.0500 × 4000 = 200 mol [1]; n(O₃) = 2 × 200 = **400 mol** [1]

**(c)** mass = 400 × 48.0 = **19 200 g** (19.2 kg) [1]. **[4]**

**10.** CCl₃F → •CCl₂F + Cl• by ultraviolet radiation [1]; Cl• + O₃ → ClO• + O₂ [1]; ClO• + O₃ → 2O₂ + Cl• [1]; the chlorine atom is regenerated, so it acts as a catalyst [1]; one chlorine atom can therefore destroy many ozone molecules, until a termination step removes it [1]. **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Omitting the dot from Cl• or •CClF₂.
- Writing that CFCs react directly with ozone.
- Breaking the C–F bond in the initiation step.
- Leaving ultraviolet radiation out of the initiation step.
- Not showing that chlorine is regenerated.
- Referring to ozone depletion as the cause of global warming.
