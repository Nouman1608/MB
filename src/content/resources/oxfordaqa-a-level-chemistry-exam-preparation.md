OxfordAQA A-Level Chemistry (9620) is a modular qualification of five externally assessed written papers: AS Paper 1 (Unit 1, Inorganic 1 and Physical 1, 90 minutes, 70 marks, 20%), AS Paper 2 (Unit 2, Organic 1 and Physical 1, 90 minutes, 70 marks, 20%), A-level Paper 1 (Unit 3, Inorganic 2 and Physical 2, 90 minutes, 80 marks, 21%), A-level Paper 2 (Unit 4, Organic 2 and Physical 2, 90 minutes, 80 marks, 21%) and A-level Paper 3 (Unit 5, Practical and Synoptic Paper, 85 minutes, 60 marks, 18%). The percentages are shares of the full A-level and sum to 100%. Use this page with the topic notes on [acids and bases](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-acids-and-bases-revision-notes/), [alcohols](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-alcohols-revision-notes/) and [alkenes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-alkenes-revision-notes/).

## AS Papers 1 and 2

The two AS papers are each 90 minutes and 70 marks, and each is worth 20% of the full A-level. Paper 1 pairs inorganic with physical chemistry, and Paper 2 pairs organic with physical chemistry, and physical chemistry therefore appears on both. The AS content builds the foundation (for example atomic structure, amount of substance, bonding, energetics, kinetics, equilibria and introductory organic chemistry), so it is revised again for the later papers.

Preparation tips:

- Make a table showing which specification topics sit under Unit 1 and which under Unit 2, and note where physical chemistry topics are shared, so that you cover both papers.
- Practise calculations in every revision session, because physical chemistry runs through both papers.
- For organic content, learn reactions as reagent, conditions, product and mechanism type, and practise writing them from memory.

## A-level Papers 1 and 2

The two A2 papers are 90 minutes each, but they carry 80 marks and 21% each. Paper 1 (Unit 3) covers Inorganic 2 and Physical 2, and Paper 2 (Unit 4) covers Organic 2 and Physical 2. Topics such as thermodynamics, electrode potentials, rate equations, transition metals, aromatic chemistry, polymers and organic synthesis belong to the second year of the course, though the record does not say which of the two papers each falls under, so confirm that from the specification. These papers are also where AS ideas return in more demanding forms, so do not leave the first year behind.

Preparation tips:

- Link physical chemistry ideas to inorganic or organic examples, such as using equilibrium constants in a context.
- Practise multi-step organic synthesis routes, writing each intermediate and reagent.
- Allow time for longer calculations that combine several concepts.

## A-level Paper 3: Practical and Synoptic

Paper 3 is the shortest paper at 85 minutes and 60 marks, worth 18%. It is a written examination, even though the course has ten required practicals: those practicals are assessed through the written papers and not through a hands-on exam. Expect questions that test practical technique, planning, data handling and error analysis, and also questions that draw content together from across the course.

Preparation tips:

- For each required practical, know the purpose, apparatus, method, measurements, expected observations and sources of error.
- Practise processing data: tables, graphs, uncertainties and percentage errors.
- Practise a synoptic question each week mixing topics, such as a titration followed by a structure question.

## Timing and marks-per-minute

- AS papers: 90 / 70 = 1.29 minutes per mark.
- A-level Papers 1 and 2: 90 / 80 = 1.125 minutes per mark.
- A-level Paper 3: 85 / 60 = 1.42 minutes per mark.
- Total marks: 70 + 70 + 80 + 80 + 60 = 360.

Paper 3 therefore gives more time per mark than the A2 content papers, which suits the reading, planning and calculation involved. On the A2 papers you have only about 1.1 minutes per mark, so a 6-mark question should take around 6 x 1.125 = 6.75 minutes. Note that a student taking the AS alone would sit only the two AS papers.

## Command words and question styles

- **State** or **name**: short recall, no explanation.
- **Calculate**: show the working in steps, with units and correct significant figures.
- **Explain**: give the chemistry reason, naming the particles or bonds involved.
- **Suggest**: apply your knowledge to an unfamiliar situation.
- **Deduce**: draw a conclusion from data or evidence.
- **Evaluate**: judge a method or result using its strengths and limitations.

## Worked practice scenario: pH of a weak acid

Question (original): "Ethanoic acid has Ka = 1.8 x 10^-5 mol dm^-3 at 298 K. Calculate the pH of a 0.100 mol dm^-3 solution, stating any assumption."

```
Ka = [H+][CH3COO-] / [CH3COOH]
Assume [H+] = [CH3COO-] and that [CH3COOH] stays at 0.100 mol dm^-3
(dissociation is very small).

[H+]^2 = Ka x [CH3COOH] = 1.8 x 10^-5 x 0.100 = 1.8 x 10^-6
[H+]   = sqrt(1.8 x 10^-6) = 1.34 x 10^-3 mol dm^-3
pH     = -log10(1.34 x 10^-3) = 2.87
```

Check of the assumption: 1.34 x 10^-3 is about 1.3% of 0.100, so treating the acid concentration as unchanged is reasonable. Writing the assumptions explicitly earns marks and also protects you from unfair loss on method.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Identify which paper you are sitting by its unit title and know its content.
- Take a calculator, a data source if permitted, and spare pens.
- Write a time plan using the minutes-per-mark rates above.
- Show each stage of a calculation with units.
- Check the number of marks to judge the length of your answer.
- In practical questions, state what you would measure, how and why.
- Keep five minutes to check significant figures and units.
- If stuck, move on and return, since every paper has questions at different levels.

## Self-test

1. How many papers does OxfordAQA A-Level Chemistry 9620 have, and is it linear or modular?
2. What is the weighting of the Practical and Synoptic paper?
3. Work out the minutes per mark for A-level Paper 1.
4. How are the ten required practicals assessed?
5. In the worked scenario, why is [H+] set equal to [CH3COO-]?

**Answers:** 1. Five papers, in a modular qualification. 2. 18%. 3. 90 / 80 = 1.125 minutes per mark. 4. Through the written papers, not through a hands-on exam. 5. Because each ethanoic acid molecule that dissociates produces one H+ ion and one ethanoate ion, ignoring the tiny amount from water.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in OxfordAQA A-Level Chemistry 9620?**
There are five written papers: two AS papers, two A-level papers on Units 3 and 4, and a Practical and Synoptic paper (Unit 5).

**How are the marks and weightings split?**
The AS papers are 70 marks and 20% each, the A-level Papers 1 and 2 are 80 marks and 21% each, and Paper 3 is 60 marks and 18%. The weightings sum to 100%.

**How long is each paper?**
The four content papers last 90 minutes and Paper 3 lasts 85 minutes.

**Is there a practical exam?**
No hands-on practical exam. Ten required practicals are assessed through the written papers, so Paper 3 is a written paper.

**When was it first examined?**
First teaching was in September 2019 and the first AS and A-level exams were in May/June 2020.
