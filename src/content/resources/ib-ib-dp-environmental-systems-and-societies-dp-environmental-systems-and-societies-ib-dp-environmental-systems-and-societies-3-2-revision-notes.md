## Condensed recall

### Two layers of cause

- **Direct drivers** act physically on organisms or habitats.
- **Indirect drivers** (population, consumption, trade, technology, governance, value systems) make the direct drivers happen.
- IPBES 2019 global ranking of direct drivers, largest first: land and sea-use change; direct exploitation; climate change; pollution; invasive alien species.
- Land-use change leads on land; direct exploitation leads in the oceans.
- **Leakage**: protecting one area while demand stays the same shifts the damage elsewhere.

### Habitat change in three steps

1. **Area falls**: smaller populations, more exposed to chance events (fire, disease, a failed breeding season).
2. **Isolation rises**: barriers block movement, gene flow stops, inbreeding and genetic drift reduce genetic diversity, and recolonisation after local extinction cannot happen.
3. **Remaining habitat degrades**: edge effects, access roads bring hunters and invasive species, pollution spreads inwards.

**Extinction debt**: species still present in a fragment that is now too small to support them in the long term. Their extinctions are already committed.

### Extinction rates

- **Background rate**: the rate of extinction without catastrophes or human influence, estimated from fossils.
- Unit: **extinctions per million species-years (E/MSY)**.
- Method: rate = extinctions ÷ (number of species × number of years) × 1 000 000.
- Compare the result with the background rate as a ratio ("× times background").

Short reminder: 6 extinctions among 2000 species over 100 years gives 6 ÷ 200 000 × 1 000 000 = **30 E/MSY**.

**Why the figures are uncertain**: most species are undescribed; extinction is declared only after long failed searches; fossils record hard-bodied groups best; extinction debt delays the losses.

### Vulnerable ecosystems

| Ecosystem | Feature that makes it vulnerable |
|---|---|
| Oceanic islands | High endemism; species evolved without mammal predators |
| Tropical rainforest | Nutrients in biomass, so slow recovery after clearance |
| Coral reefs | Narrow temperature and chemistry tolerance |
| Mangroves, lowland grassland | Accessible, valuable land converted first |
| Deep sea, polar | Slow growth and late reproduction |

### Case studies in one line each

- **Passenger pigeon (extinct)**: commercial hunting aided by railways and the telegraph, plus forest clearance; colonial breeder, so scattered survivors bred poorly; last individual died 1914.
- **Vaquita (Critically Endangered)**: porpoise of the northern Gulf of California; drowned as bycatch in gillnets, including the illegal totoaba fishery; tiny range and small population.
- **Brown tree snake on Guam (invasive)**: arrived in cargo around the end of the Second World War; native birds had no defence against snakes; most native forest bird species lost.
- **Dodo (extinct)**: flightless Mauritius bird; hunting and introduced rats, pigs and monkeys; gone by the late seventeenth century.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Direct driver | A human activity that physically changes organisms or habitats |
| Indirect driver | A social or economic cause behind a direct driver |
| Fragmentation | Breaking a continuous habitat into smaller, isolated patches |
| Gene flow | Movement of alleles between populations through migration and breeding |
| Genetic drift | Random change in allele frequencies, strongest in small populations |
| Extinction debt | Future extinctions already made inevitable by past habitat loss |
| Background extinction rate | The natural rate of extinction between mass extinctions |
| E/MSY | Extinctions per million species-years |
| Bycatch | Non-target organisms caught and usually killed by fishing gear |
| Endemic | Found naturally in only one place |
| Invasive species | A non-native species that spreads and causes harm |
| Leakage | Displacement of damage to another area when only a direct driver is controlled |

## Exam traps

- "Population growth causes extinction." Not on its own: link it to a direct driver.
- "Climate change is the main cause of loss." At present, land-use change and exploitation rank higher globally; climate change is rising in importance.
- Forgetting the 1 000 000 multiplier, or dividing by years only, when calculating E/MSY.
- Writing "non-native" when the question asks about "invasive". Invasive species must also spread and cause harm.
- Calling a fragment "safe" because a species still lives there. Mention extinction debt.
- Case studies with no mechanism. Name the species or place, the driver and the chain of effects.
- In evaluate questions, listing drivers without ranking them or reaching a judgement.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish between a direct and an indirect driver of biodiversity loss.
2. State the two highest-ranked direct drivers of biodiversity loss in the 2019 IPBES global assessment.
3. Which direct driver ranks highest in the oceans?
4. Give three ways fragmentation reduces biodiversity, other than reducing area.
5. Define extinction debt.
6. Calculate the extinction rate in E/MSY for 9 extinctions among 3000 species over 50 years.
7. Give two reasons why recorded extinction rates are likely to underestimate true rates.
8. Why are island species particularly vulnerable to introduced predators?
9. State the main threat to the vaquita.
10. Explain why stopping logging in one forest may not reduce global forest loss.
11. Why does genetic diversity fall in small, isolated populations?
12. Give one feature that makes coral reefs vulnerable to human impact.

### Answers

1. A direct driver physically changes organisms or habitats (e.g. clearing forest); an indirect driver is the social or economic cause behind it (e.g. rising demand for beef).
2. Changes in land and sea use; direct exploitation of organisms.
3. Direct exploitation (fishing).
4. Any three: isolation stops gene flow; inbreeding and genetic drift reduce genetic diversity; recolonisation after local extinction is prevented; edge effects degrade habitat; access roads bring hunters and invasive species.
5. Extinctions that will happen in the future because habitat has already been reduced below what the species needs, even though the species are still present.
6. 9 ÷ (3000 × 50) × 1 000 000 = 9 ÷ 150 000 × 1 000 000 = **60 E/MSY**.
7. Any two: most species are undescribed; extinction is declared only after long searches; extinction debt delays losses; poorly studied regions are under-sampled.
8. They evolved without such predators, so they lack defensive behaviour (many are flightless, ground-nesting or tame).
9. Drowning as bycatch in gillnets.
10. Demand for timber or farmland is unchanged, so clearance moves to other forests (leakage).
11. Little or no gene flow brings in new alleles, while inbreeding and genetic drift remove alleles at random.
12. Narrow tolerance of temperature and water chemistry, so warming or pollution causes bleaching and mass mortality.
