AQA GCSE Mathematics (8300) covers the number, algebra, geometry,
proportional reasoning, probability and statistics that underpin
further study and everyday decisions. It is offered at two tiers,
Foundation and Higher, and each tier is assessed entirely by three
written papers of 1 hour 30 minutes and 80 marks each, giving 240 marks
per tier. There is no coursework, controlled assessment or non-exam
assessment.

## How the course is organised

The subject content is divided into six areas. Two of them are broken
into named sub-areas; the other four are single areas in their own
right.

- **Number.** Structure and calculation; fractions, decimals and
  percentages; and measures and accuracy. This is the toolkit of exact
  and approximate calculation that every other area uses.
- **Algebra.** Notation, vocabulary and manipulation; graphs; solving
  equations and inequalities; and sequences. Algebra is where the
  course moves from working with particular numbers to working with
  general relationships.
- **Ratio, proportion and rates of change.** Comparing quantities,
  scaling and working with changing amounts. It draws heavily on number
  skills and links naturally to graphs.
- **Geometry and measures.** Properties of shapes, measurement and
  spatial reasoning.
- **Probability.** Describing and calculating how likely events are.
- **Statistics.** Collecting, presenting and interpreting data.

The same six areas are studied by everyone. What differs between the
tiers is the depth and difficulty at which they are assessed.

## How it's assessed

The qualification is linear, so all three papers are sat at the end of
the course, and it is tiered. Foundation tier leads to grades 1 to 5;
Higher tier leads to grades 4 to 9. Whichever tier you are entered for,
the paper pattern is the same.

| Paper | Tier codes | Calculator | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | 8300/1F, 8300/1H | Not allowed | 1 hour 30 minutes | 80 | 33.33% |
| Paper 2 | 8300/2F, 8300/2H | Allowed | 1 hour 30 minutes | 80 | 33.33% |
| Paper 3 | 8300/3F, 8300/3H | Allowed | 1 hour 30 minutes | 80 | 33.33% |

Each paper is worth one third of the GCSE, so the three are equally
important. All of the assessment is by written examination. The
specification was first taught in September 2015 and first examined in
June 2017.

Tier choice is usually made with your teacher based on how you are
progressing. Because grades 4 and 5 are available on both tiers, the
decision is about which set of papers will let you show your best
work, not a fixed ceiling decided at the start of the course.

## How the parts fit together

The content areas are listed separately, but the papers do not test
them in isolation. A single question can start with a ratio, require an
equation to be formed and solved, and finish with an interpretation of
the answer. Number underpins all of this: fractions, percentages and
accuracy appear in geometry, probability and statistics questions as
often as in number questions themselves.

The non-calculator paper is where this is most visible. Without a
calculator, fluency with fractions, decimals, percentages and
estimation becomes essential, and weaknesses in written methods show
up quickly. The two calculator papers give you a tool but
do not remove the need to choose the right method, set out working
clearly and check that an answer is sensible.

Algebra and graphs also connect several areas. Rates of change in the
ratio and proportion strand are often shown graphically, sequences
rely on algebraic notation, and many geometry problems are solved by
setting up and solving an equation.

## Planning your study

GCSE Mathematics is cumulative, so the order matters more than in many
subjects.

1. **Start with number.** Structure and calculation, fractions, decimals
   and percentages, and measures and accuracy give you the fluency that
   everything else relies on.
2. **Introduce algebra steadily.** Notation and manipulation first, then
   solving equations and inequalities, sequences and graphs.
3. **Interleave the other areas.** Ratio and proportion, geometry and
   measures, probability and statistics can be taught in blocks
   alongside algebra, revisiting earlier skills each time.
4. **Keep non-calculator skills sharp.** Short regular practice without
   a calculator helps throughout, not just before Paper 1.
5. **Mix topics in the final stage.** Mixed practice is closer to how
   the papers actually look than revising one area at a time.

Begin topic-by-topic practice from the start. Full papers, timed
conditions and the paper-by-paper exam preparation page belong in the
last few months, once all six areas have been taught at your tier.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in AQA GCSE Maths?**
Each tier has three written papers. Each paper lasts 1 hour 30 minutes, is worth 80 marks and carries 33.33% of the GCSE, giving 240 marks per tier.

**Which AQA GCSE Maths paper is non-calculator?**
Paper 1 is the non-calculator paper. A calculator is allowed on Papers 2 and 3.

**What grades can I get on Foundation and Higher tier?**
Foundation tier covers grades 1 to 5 and Higher tier covers grades 4 to 9.

**Is there coursework in GCSE Mathematics 8300?**
No. The qualification is assessed entirely by written examination, with no coursework, controlled assessment or non-exam assessment.

**When was AQA GCSE Mathematics 8300 first examined?**
It was first taught from September 2015 and first examined in June 2017.
