These questions cover section 2.3 of the Cambridge O Level Statistics (4040) syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027: "The purpose and use of various forms of representation, their advantages and disadvantages". Revise with the [study guide on choosing representations](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-purpose-use-representation-advantages-disadvantages/) or the [revision notes on choosing representations](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-purpose-use-representation-advantages-disadvantages-revision-notes/). Answers show model wording; other well-reasoned answers can also earn credit.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** State the main purpose of a pie chart. **[1]**

**2.** Give one disadvantage of a pictogram compared with a bar chart. **[1]**

**3.** Give one advantage of a stem-and-leaf diagram over a grouped frequency table. **[1]**

**4.** A box-and-whisker diagram is drawn for the reaction times of 40 drivers. Name two pieces of information about the data that the diagram does not show. **[2]**

**5.** A sectional bar chart shows the numbers of boys and girls in each of four clubs. Explain why it is harder to compare the numbers of girls than the numbers of boys between clubs, when boys form the bottom section. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why comparative pie charts are drawn with circles of different sizes. **[1]**

**7.** Give one situation in which a table is more useful than any diagram. **[1]**

## Section B: longer questions

**8.** A town council has data on how residents of two districts travel to work (walk, bus, car, cycle). District P has 1800 working residents and District Q has 600.

**(a)** Explain why a dual bar chart of the numbers in each category could give a misleading comparison. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest a more suitable diagram and justify your choice. **[2]**

**(c)** Give one disadvantage of the diagram you chose in (b). **[1]**

**9.** The heights of 18 seedlings in each of two trays are to be compared.

**(a)** Name a diagram that would show every height from both trays together, and give one advantage of it. **[2]**

**(b)** Later, the heights of 400 seedlings from each of six trays are to be compared. Suggest a suitable diagram and give a reason. **[2]**

**10.** A company's annual report shows profit as a bar chart. The vertical axis begins at 80 (thousand dollars). Profits were 84 thousand in 2024 and 92 thousand in 2025.

**(a)** By what factor does the 2025 bar appear taller than the 2024 bar? **[1]**

**(b)** By what percentage did profit actually rise? **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the chart is misleading and how it should be redrawn. **[2]**

**11.** A poster shows the number of trees planted by a charity. A picture of a tree 2 cm tall represents 500 trees in Year 1. For Year 2, when 1000 trees were planted, a tree picture 4 cm tall and twice as wide is drawn.

**(a)** By what factor is the area of the Year 2 picture larger? **[1]**

**(b)** Explain the false impression this gives and how a pictogram should show the data. **[2]**

**12.** A survey records, for 120 pupils, whether they play in the orchestra, sing in the choir, or both.

**(a)** Name a suitable diagram for these data and give a reason. **[2]**

**(b)** A pupil suggests a pie chart with sectors "orchestra" and "choir". Explain why this is not appropriate. **[1]**

**(c)** Name another form of representation, from section 2.1, that could show the same information. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** To show how a whole is divided into parts, as shares of the total [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Any one: part-symbols make values approximate and hard to read; drawing equal symbols is slow; values cannot be read from a scale [1]. **[1]**

**3.** It keeps the individual values, so exact measures such as the median can be found [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Any two, for [1] each: the individual values; the mean; the number of drivers; the shape of the data within each quarter. **[2]**

**5.** The girls' sections sit on top of the boys' sections, so they start at different heights [1]; there is no common baseline, so lengths must be compared by subtraction rather than by eye [1]. **[2]**

**6.** So that the area of each circle is proportional to the total it represents, showing which total is larger [1]. **[1]**

**7.** When exact values are needed for further calculation, for example to find a mean or a percentage [1]. **[1]**

**8. (a)** District P has three times as many working residents [1], so its bars will be taller in almost every category, hiding whether the pattern of travel differs [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** A percentage bar chart, one bar per district [1], because it compares the proportion using each method when the totals differ (comparative pie charts, with the same reason, are also acceptable) [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** The actual numbers in each district are not shown (for a percentage bar chart); or small differences in angle are hard to judge (for comparative pie charts) [1]. **[1]**

**9. (a)** A back-to-back stem-and-leaf diagram [1]; it keeps every height while showing the shape of both distributions side by side [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Box-and-whisker diagrams on a common scale [1], because there are too many values for stem-and-leaf diagrams and six medians and spreads can be compared at a glance [1]. **[2]**

**10. (a)** Bars of 92 − 80 = 12 and 84 − 80 = 4 units: 3 times as tall [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** (92 − 84)/84 × 100 = 9.5% [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** The axis does not start at zero, so a 9.5% rise looks like a threefold increase [1]. Redraw with the axis starting at zero, or mark a clear break in the axis [1]. **[2]**

**11. (a)** 2 × 2 = 4 [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** Readers judge by area, so planting appears to have quadrupled when it only doubled [1]. A pictogram should use identical tree symbols with a key (one tree = 500), showing 1 symbol for Year 1 and 2 for Year 2 [1]. **[2]**

**12. (a)** A Venn diagram (two overlapping circles in a rectangle of 120 pupils) [1], because some pupils do both, and it shows orchestra only, choir only, both and neither [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Sectors cannot overlap, so pupils in both cannot be shown correctly and the shares would not make one whole [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** A two-way table (orchestra yes/no against choir yes/no) [1]. **[1]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reasons such as "it is clearer" with no link to the data.
- Ignoring different group sizes when choosing between counts and proportions.
- Naming a disadvantage that applies to the other diagram.
- Saying a diagram is misleading without stating the effect on the reader.
- Missing the area effect of pictures enlarged in two directions.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are there alternative acceptable answers?**
Yes. Where two forms suit the aim equally, either earns credit with a reason that fits the data.

**How long should a written answer be?**
About one sentence per mark, each making a separate point linked to the context.
