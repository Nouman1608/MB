## Source notes (original)

Some questions use these notes, as if taken from a Section A source pack on a plan to close a town's
central market on Sundays.

- **Council report:** Sunday trading costs the council for cleaning and security; stallholders'
  takings on Sundays are lower than on Saturdays.
- **Stallholder's blog:** "Sunday is the only day families have time to talk to us. Close it, and you
  close the friendliest hour in town."
- **Survey poster:** "Have your say: Sunday market, yes or no?"

## Section A — Short questions

**1.** Identify every audience and every purpose in this task: "Write a letter to the town council,
which will also be printed in the local newspaper, explaining why the Sunday market matters and
persuading councillors and readers to keep it open." **[4]**

**2.** For the task in Question 1, explain which audience should set the register, and why. **[2]**

**3.** Explain the difference between a multiple audience and a diverse audience, using the market as
an example. **[2]**

**4.** For a task that asks you to "inform and persuade", explain why informing usually comes first.
**[2]**

**5.** Rewrite this sentence so that it addresses a sceptical audience fairly: "Anyone who wants to
close the market is wrong." **[2]**

## Section B — Longer questions

**6.** Plan the letter in Question 1. Include the audiences, purposes, register, an opening idea, four
paragraph topics and an ending. Use at least two ideas from the source notes, reworked. **[6]**

**7.** Write the **opening paragraph** of the letter. **[4]**

**8.** Write a paragraph that addresses councillors' concern about cost, using the council report's
point without copying it. **[5]**

**9.** Write **two** short passages of direct address, one to market-goers and one to councillors,
that show segmented address. **[4]**

**10.** A student's article for "students and teachers" uses slang throughout. Explain why this fails
the task and suggest how to fix it. **[3]**

**11.** Plan a leaflet for "young people and their parents" that **explains** a new summer reading
scheme at the town library and **encourages** families to join. Show how you would use sub-headings
for the two audiences. **[5]**

**12.** Write the complete letter from Question 1. Use the checklist in the answers to assess your
work. **[16]**

## Answers

**How these are marked.** Short questions are point-marked as shown. For writing tasks, the bracketed
credits show what a strong response includes; in the exam, writing is marked as a whole against the
writing objectives.

**1.** Audiences [2]: the town council or councillors [1]; newspaper readers [1]. Purposes [2]: explain
why the market matters [1]; persuade them to keep it open [1].

**2.** The council, as the primary and most powerful audience [1], so the register should be formal,
with clear explanation that general readers can also follow [1].

**3.** A multiple audience is more than one group, such as councillors and newspaper readers [1]; a
diverse audience has different interests, such as councillors worried about cost and stallholders
worried about their income [1].

**4.** Readers need to understand what is proposed or what is at stake [1] before they can be persuaded
to support it, so explanation prepares the ground for persuasion [1].

**5.** A rewritten sentence that acknowledges the other view [1] and answers it [1], for example: "Those
who want to close the market have a fair point about cost, but they may be overlooking what Sunday
brings to the town's community."

**6.** Credit up to 6: audiences and purposes named, with the hardest audience identified [1]; a formal
register set by the council [1]; an engaging opening idea [1]; four logical paragraph topics that
include explanation before persuasion [1]; at least two reworked ideas from the notes, such as the
families' Sunday visits and the cleaning costs [1]; an ending with a clear request or call to action
[1].

**7.** Credit up to 4: a correct letter opening and greeting for a formal audience [1]; a clear
statement of purpose [1]; a hook that engages both councillors and readers [1]; a formal but readable
register [1].

**8.** Credit up to 5: acknowledges the cost concern fairly [1]; reworks the report's point in the
writer's own words [1]; offers an answer, such as a shared cleaning scheme or a shorter Sunday [1];
uses a realistic example without invented figures [1]; links the answer back to the purpose of
keeping the market open [1].

**9.** Market-goers [2]: direct address with an inclusive, warm tone [1] and a reason that matters to
them [1]. Councillors [2]: respectful direct address [1] and a reason that matters to them, such as
the town's reputation or footfall for nearby shops [1].

**10.** Slang suits neither teachers nor a school publication [1], so the register fails one of the two
named audiences [1]; fix it by using a semi-formal register with occasional inclusive, friendly
phrases for students [1].

**11.** Credit up to 5: purposes in order, explanation then encouragement [1]; a title and
introduction addressing both audiences [1]; a sub-heading and content for young people, such as
"What's in it for you?" [1]; a sub-heading and content for parents, such as "How it helps your
child" [1]; a closing call to action for families [1].

**12.** Credit up to 16, as a guide: both audiences addressed and the sceptic answered [3]; purposes
sequenced, with explanation before persuasion [3]; formal letter conventions and consistent register
[2]; reworked source material, not copied [2]; developed paragraphs with a clear structure [2];
varied, controlled sentences [2]; accurate spelling and punctuation [2].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing only for councillors or only for readers.
- Explaining nothing before persuading.
- Copying the stallholder's words instead of reworking them.
- Listing audiences in the greeting and then ignoring them.

## Frequently asked questions

**Must every Paper 2 task have more than one audience?**
No. The specification says audience and purpose "could be" multiple and diverse, so some tasks
will be simpler. Practise both kinds.
