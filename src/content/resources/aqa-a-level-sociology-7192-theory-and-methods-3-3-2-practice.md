This set covers the theory areas of section 3.3.2, which Paper 3 assesses with 30 marks of extended
writing. Section A uses short recall questions for practice. Section B uses 10-mark "outline and
explain two" questions and 20-mark evaluation questions, two of them with items invented for
practice.

## Section A

**1.** Distinguish between consensus and conflict theories. **[2]**

**2.** Explain what Giddens meant by the *duality of structure*. **[2]**

**3.** Outline **two** features of modernity. **[4]**

**4.** Explain why Popper argued that falsification, not verification, is the basis of science.
**[3]**

**5.** Outline Weber's view of the place of values in sociological research. **[3]**

**6.** Outline **two** constraints that limit the influence of sociological research on social
policy. **[4]**

## Section B

**7.** Outline and explain **two** differences between postmodernist and late modernist accounts of
contemporary society. **[10]**

**8.** Outline and explain **two** reasons why some sociologists argue that sociology cannot be a
science in the same way as physics. **[10]**

**9.** Outline and explain **two** reasons why sociologists may not be able to keep their values out
of research. **[10]**

**10.** *Item A (invented for practice)*

*Some sociologists argue that society is not an external structure but is produced by people as
they interpret situations and act on their interpretations. Labels, meanings and everyday
negotiation are central. Critics reply that this approach ignores the wider inequalities of power
that shape which meanings count.*

Applying material from **Item A** and your knowledge, evaluate the contribution of social action
theories to our understanding of society. **[20]**

**11.** *Item B (invented for practice)*

*Governments often turn to research to decide how to reduce problems such as crime. Some
sociologists believe their work should provide objective evidence for such decisions. Others argue
that research should challenge the powerful, while some doubt that sociology can claim the
authority to guide policy at all.*

Applying material from **Item B** and your knowledge, evaluate views of the relationship between
sociology and social policy. **[20]**

**12.** Evaluate the contribution of feminist theories to sociological understanding of society.
**[20]**

## Answers

**1.** Consensus theories see society as based on shared norms and values that bind people together
[1]; conflict theories see society as based on competing interests between groups with unequal
power, such as classes or genders [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Structures (rules and resources) make action possible [1], and action in turn reproduces or
changes those structures, so each depends on the other [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Any two, each identified [1] and briefly explained [1]: **rationality and science** — belief
that reason produces reliable knowledge and progress [1] [1]; **industrial capitalism** — production
organised in factories for profit [1] [1]; **the nation state** — centralised political authority over
a territory [1] [1]; **stable identities** — identity rooted in class, occupation and locality [1]
[1]. **[4]**

**4.** No number of confirming observations can prove a universal claim true [1], but a single
contrary observation can show it is false, such as one black swan against "all swans are white" [1].
So scientific theories must be open to falsification and survive attempts to disprove them [1].
**[3]**

**5.** Values legitimately influence the choice of topic, which Weber called value relevance [1].
Once a topic is chosen, data collection and analysis should be as objective as possible [1]. Values
re-enter when findings are interpreted and used, so the researcher should be clear about them [1].
**[3]**

**6.** Any two, each identified [1] and explained [1]: **funding** — those who pay may set the
questions or ignore unwelcome findings [1] [1]; **electoral pressures** — governments may reject
evidence that conflicts with what voters want [1] [1]; **ideology** — governments favour research
that fits their political outlook [1] [1]; **cost** — recommended policies may be too expensive [1]
[1]. **[4]**

**7.** Marked in levels; two clearly distinct, developed differences. Indicative content:
- **Break or continuity.** Postmodernists such as Lyotard and Baudrillard claim a new era in which
  meta-narratives have collapsed; late modernists such as Giddens and Beck argue modernity has
  intensified rather than ended. Develop: so late modernists keep the possibility of rational
  knowledge and planned improvement.
- **Knowledge and action.** For postmodernists, no account of society can claim authority, so
  sociology cannot guide change; for Giddens, reflexivity means knowledge feeds back into how
  individuals and institutions organise themselves. Develop: Beck's risk society shows modern
  science both creating and identifying risks.
- Also creditable: hyperreality versus manufactured risk; Jock Young's late-modern account of
  exclusion and crime. **[10]**

**8.** Marked in levels; two developed reasons. Indicative content:
- **Meaningful action.** Humans act on interpretations and have free will, so behaviour cannot be
  explained by external causal laws in the way matter can. Develop: interpretivists therefore seek
  verstehen rather than laws.
- **Open systems and reactivity.** Sociologists cannot control variables as in a laboratory, and
  people change their behaviour when studied or when findings are published. Develop: realists
  accept the open-system point but argue that meteorology faces it too and is still a science.
- **No single paradigm (Kuhn).** Sociology is divided into competing perspectives, so normal science
  cannot develop. Develop: this may simply reflect sociology's age.
- Also creditable: socially constructed data, as Cicourel showed for juvenile justice statistics.
  **[10]**

**9.** Marked in levels; two developed reasons. Indicative content:
- **Choice of topic and funding.** Values and funders' interests decide what is studied. Develop:
  Gouldner argued value-free claims turned sociologists into "spiritless technicians".
- **Interpretation and taking sides.** Becker argued that all research is told from someone's point
  of view; left realists sided with victims and labelling theorists with the labelled. Develop: the
  same crime data can be read in opposite ways depending on whose side the researcher takes.
- **Career and publication pressures.** Researchers need to publish findings that funders and
  journals value.
- Analysis: Weber's position suggests partial objectivity is still possible in data collection.
  **[10]**

**10.** Marked in levels using AO1, AO2 and AO3. Indicative content:
- **AO2 application of Item A:** "produced by people as they interpret situations" = social action
  theory (Weber, Mead, Blumer); "labels" = labelling theory; "everyday negotiation" =
  ethnomethodology (Garfinkel); "ignores the wider inequalities of power" = the Marxist and feminist
  critique.
- **AO1 knowledge:** Weber's verstehen and types of action; symbolic interactionism and the self;
  phenomenology (Schutz) and typifications; Garfinkel's account of how members make sense of
  situations; Becker and Cicourel on deviance.
- **AO3 evaluation, strengths:** explain meaning and change; show that official statistics are
  constructed; avoid structural determinism.
- **AO3 evaluation, weaknesses:** neglect power and large-scale inequality; focus on small-scale
  settings; ethnomethodology criticised as describing rather than explaining; Weber himself linked
  action to structure.
- **Judgement:** for example, that social action theories are essential for understanding meaning
  but need structural theory or Giddens' structuration to explain why some definitions prevail.
  **[20]**

**11.** Marked in levels using AO1, AO2 and AO3. Indicative content:
- **AO2 application of Item B:** "objective evidence" = positivist and functionalist views and
  piecemeal social engineering; "challenge the powerful" = Marxist, feminist and critical views;
  "doubt that sociology can claim the authority" = postmodernism.
- **AO1 knowledge:** social and sociological problems; social democratic research aimed at reducing
  inequality; feminist influence on domestic violence policy; right realism and "broken windows"
  (Wilson and Kelling, 1982) as research that shaped policing; Marxist view that policy serves
  capitalism.
- **AO3 evaluation:** influence depends on funding, elections and ideology; governments may use
  research selectively; Marxists may underestimate genuine reforms; postmodern scepticism leaves no
  basis for action.
- **Judgement:** for example, that research influences policy most when it matches the government's
  existing priorities, which supports the conflict view more than the positivist one. **[20]**

**12.** Marked in levels using AO1 and AO3 (no item). Indicative content:
- **AO1 knowledge:** liberal feminism (gradual reform through law and changing attitudes); radical
  feminism (patriarchy as the root of inequality); Marxist feminism (gender inequality linked to
  capitalism); difference and postmodern feminism (women's experiences vary by class, ethnicity and
  sexuality); Walby's patriarchal structures; Heidensohn on the social control of women as an
  explanation of lower female offending.
- **AO3 evaluation, strengths:** made gender visible across sociology; challenged "malestream"
  research; influenced methods (Oakley on interviewing) and policy.
- **AO3 evaluation, weaknesses:** disagreement between feminisms; radical feminism criticised for
  treating women as one group; difference feminists' critique of white middle-class assumptions;
  postmodern feminism may lose the shared basis for political action.
- **Judgement:** for example, that feminism's contribution is substantial, but strongest where it
  links gender to other inequalities. **[20]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing theories without evaluating them against each other.
- Mentioning an item phrase without developing what it shows.
- Treating postmodernism and late modernism as interchangeable.
- Reaching no judgement in a 20-mark answer.
