OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Mathematics (9660) is a modular
course in pure mathematics with applied work in statistics and
mechanics. It suits students heading for mathematics, science,
engineering, economics or computing, and anyone who enjoys solving
problems with precise reasoning. The specification was first taught in
September 2017, with first AS exams in May/June 2018 and first A-level
exams in May/June 2019. The full A-level has four units: two AS units,
P1 and PSM1, each worth 20%; a compulsory A-level pure unit, P2, worth
37.5%; and a final unit chosen between Statistics (S2) and Mechanics
(M2), worth 22.5%.

## How the course is organised

**International AS: Unit P1, Pure Maths.** Algebra, coordinate geometry,
differentiation, integration, and sequences and series. This is the
foundation of the whole course.

**International AS: Unit PSM1.** A combined unit in three parts:

- PP1, Pure Maths: circles, trigonometry, and exponentials and
  logarithms.
- S1, Statistics: further probability, discrete random variables, and
  Bernoulli and binomial distributions.
- M1, Mechanics: motion in a straight line with constant and with
  variable acceleration, forces and Newton's laws, and momentum and
  impulse.

**International A-level: Unit P2, Pure Maths.** Algebra and functions,
sequences and series, coordinate geometry in the (x, y) plane,
trigonometry, exponentials and logarithms, differentiation, integration,
differential equations, numerical methods and vectors.

**International A-level: Unit S2, Statistics (option).** The Poisson
distribution, continuous random variables, the exponential distribution,
the normal distribution, estimation and hypothesis testing.

**International A-level: Unit M2, Mechanics (option).** Mathematical
modelling, kinematics, statics and forces, Newton's laws of motion,
projectiles, work and energy, and uniform circular motion.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Unit | Time | Marks | Weighting (A-level) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AS Paper 1 | P1: Pure Maths | 90 minutes | 80 | 20% |
| AS Paper 2 | PSM1: Pure Maths, Statistics and Mechanics | 90 minutes | 80 | 20% |
| A-level Paper 1 | P2: Pure Maths | 150 minutes | 120 | 37.5% |
| A-level Paper 2 (Option A) | S2: Statistics | 90 minutes | 80 | 22.5% |
| A-level Paper 2 (Option B) | M2: Mechanics | 90 minutes | 80 | 22.5% |

You take either S2 or M2, not both, so the full A-level is four papers.
All are externally assessed written exams, and a calculator is allowed in
each. Each AS unit is worth 50% of the International AS, which can be
certificated on its own after the first year. Units can be resat any
number of times, with the best result counting.

## How the parts fit together

Pure mathematics is the spine of the course. P1 and the PP1 section of
PSM1 together give you the algebra, calculus, trigonometry and
exponential work that P2 then extends. P2 is the single largest unit, at
37.5% of the A-level, and it depends heavily on fluent first-year
technique; weaknesses in algebra or differentiation at AS show up again
in the second year.

The applied strands grow from PSM1. S1 introduces probability and the
binomial distribution, which leads into the Poisson, normal and
continuous distributions and hypothesis testing in S2. M1 introduces
straight-line motion, forces and momentum, which leads into projectiles,
energy and circular motion in M2. Whichever option you take, its first
steps are in PSM1, so both applied sections of PSM1 matter for AS even
if you will drop one at A-level.

Pure mathematics also supports the applications directly. Integration is
used for continuous random variables in S2 and for variable acceleration
in mechanics; vectors and trigonometry are used to resolve forces. Clear,
logical working is rewarded in every unit.

## Planning your study

In the first year, start with P1, since PSM1 builds on its algebra and
calculus. Pure topics from PP1 can be woven in once the P1 basics are
secure, with statistics and mechanics from PSM1 introduced alongside.
Practise every week: short, regular sets of questions build accuracy far
better than occasional long sessions.

Before the second year begins, find out whether your school takes S2 or
M2. Review your PSM1 notes for that strand, and revisit P1 algebra and
calculus before starting P2. Within P2, functions and algebra make a
natural starting point, with differential equations, numerical methods
and vectors later on.

Use past-paper questions on each topic as you finish it, then full timed
papers in the run-up to each exam series. Because units can be resat, it
is worth discussing with your teacher how resits fit your plans. The
course's exam-preparation page explains how the weightings and the
statistics-or-mechanics choice affect revision, so use it for paper
strategy.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many units are in OxfordAQA A-level Mathematics 9660?**
Four: P1 and PSM1 at AS, each worth 20% of the A-level; P2 at A-level, worth 37.5%; and a final unit, either S2 Statistics or M2 Mechanics, worth 22.5%.

**Do I take both statistics and mechanics at A-level?**
No. At AS, PSM1 includes both S1 and M1. At A-level you take either S2 or M2, which are equally weighted alternatives.

**Can I use a calculator in the 9660 exams?**
Yes. A calculator is allowed in every paper.

**Can I resit units in 9660?**
Yes. Units can be resat any number of times, and the best result counts.

**How long is the P2 paper?**
A-level Paper 1, Unit P2: Pure Maths, is 150 minutes long and worth 120 marks.
