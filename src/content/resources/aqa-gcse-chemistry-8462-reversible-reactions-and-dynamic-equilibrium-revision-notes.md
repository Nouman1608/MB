specification (for teaching from September 2016). Paper 2 content. The effect of changing conditions on
equilibrium (4.6.2.4 to 4.6.2.7) is Higher tier only. For explanations use the
[reversible reactions and dynamic equilibrium study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-reversible-reactions-and-dynamic-equilibrium/);
to test yourself use the
[practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-reversible-reactions-and-dynamic-equilibrium-practice/).

## Core facts

- **Reversible reaction**: products can react to form the original reactants. Shown by **⇌**.
- Direction can be changed by changing the **conditions**.
- If exothermic one way, it is **endothermic the other way**, with the **same amount of energy**.
- **Equilibrium** is reached in a **closed system** when the **forward and reverse rates are equal**.
- At equilibrium the **concentrations are constant** (not necessarily equal), and both reactions continue.

## Reversible examples

| Reaction | Forward | Reverse |
|---|---|---|
| CuSO₄·5H₂O ⇌ CuSO₄ + 5H₂O | Heat blue crystals: white powder, water driven off (endothermic) | Add water: blue again, gets hot (exothermic) |
| NH₄Cl ⇌ NH₃ + HCl | Heat: decomposes to gases (endothermic) | Cool: re-forms solid at top of tube (exothermic) |
| N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ | Makes ammonia (exothermic) | Breaks ammonia down (endothermic) |

## Building equilibrium

| Stage | Forward rate | Reverse rate | Concentrations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Start | Fastest | Zero | Reactants high |
| Middle | Falling | Rising | Changing |
| Equilibrium | Equal | Equal | Constant |

- **Dynamic**: reactions continue, but there is no overall change.
- **Closed system**: nothing escapes. In an open container, gas escapes and the reaction goes to completion.
- The position may lie to the **left** (mostly reactants) or the **right** (mostly products).

## Higher tier only: Le Chatelier's Principle

If conditions of a system at equilibrium are changed, the system **counteracts the change**.

| Change | Shift |
|---|---|
| Increase reactant concentration | Right (more products) |
| Remove a product | Right (more products) |
| Increase product concentration | Left |
| Increase pressure | Towards fewer gas molecules |
| Decrease pressure | Towards more gas molecules |
| Increase temperature | Towards the endothermic direction |
| Decrease temperature | Towards the exothermic direction |

Temperature in short form:

| | Increase T | Decrease T |
|---|---|---|
| Forward reaction **endothermic** | more products | fewer products |
| Forward reaction **exothermic** | fewer products | more products |

## Applying it: ammonia

N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), forward reaction exothermic.

- 4 gas molecules on the left, 2 on the right: **high pressure** favours ammonia.
- Exothermic forward: **low temperature** favours ammonia (but the rate is slow, so a compromise is used).
- Removing ammonia as it forms shifts the position to the right.

## Applying it: nitrogen dioxide

2NO₂(g) ⇌ N₂O₄(g), brown ⇌ colourless.

- 2 molecules to 1: **higher pressure** shifts to N₂O₄, so the brown colour fades.
- Lower pressure shifts to NO₂, so the mixture is browner.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Reversible reaction | Reaction in which products can re-form the reactants |
| Equilibrium | Forward and reverse reactions occur at the same rate |
| Dynamic | Both reactions still going on |
| Closed system | Nothing can enter or leave |
| Position of equilibrium | Relative amounts of reactants and products at equilibrium |
| Le Chatelier's Principle | System at equilibrium counteracts a change |

## Exam traps

- **"The reaction stops."** Both reactions continue at equilibrium.
- **"Equal concentrations."** They are constant, not necessarily equal.
- **Open systems.** Equilibrium needs a closed system.
- **Wrong direction for temperature.** Check whether the forward reaction is exothermic or endothermic.
- **Counting liquids or solids for pressure.** Count gas molecules only.
- **Catalyst changes the position.** It does not; it only speeds both directions equally.
- **No effect named.** State the shift and the effect on the amount of product.

## Quick self-test

1. What symbol shows a reversible reaction?
2. Which colour change happens when water is added to anhydrous copper(II) sulfate?
3. If a reaction is exothermic forwards, what is it backwards?
4. State two conditions needed for equilibrium.
5. Compare the rates of the forward and reverse reactions at equilibrium.
6. What happens to the concentrations at equilibrium?
7. Why is equilibrium not reached in an open container where a gas escapes?
8. What is the name for equilibrium where both reactions continue?
9. (Higher tier only) State Le Chatelier's Principle.
10. (Higher tier only) For H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g), what is the effect of increasing the pressure? Explain.
11. (Higher tier only) For 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃ (forward exothermic), what is the effect on yield of SO₃ of a higher temperature and of a higher pressure?
12. (Higher tier only) A product is removed continuously. What happens to the position of equilibrium?

### Answers

1. ⇌
2. White to blue (and it gets hot).
3. Endothermic, with the same amount of energy transferred.
4. A reversible reaction and a closed system (nothing escapes).
5. They are equal.
6. They stay constant.
7. The products cannot react to re-form the reactants, so the reverse reaction cannot occur.
8. Dynamic equilibrium.
9. If a system at equilibrium is changed, it responds to counteract the change.
10. No effect on the position, because there are two gas molecules on each side.
11. Higher temperature: less SO₃ (opposes the rise by favouring the endothermic reverse reaction). Higher pressure: more SO₃ (3 gas molecules on the left, 2 on the right).
12. It shifts to the right to replace the product, so more product forms.

## Worked answers to model

**Explain what is meant by dynamic equilibrium.** "In a closed system, the forward and reverse reactions happen at
the same rate, so the concentrations of reactants and products stay constant, but both reactions are still
going on."

**Predict the effect of removing water vapour from CuSO₄·5H₂O ⇌ CuSO₄ + 5H₂O (Higher tier only).** "The
equilibrium shifts to the right to replace the water, so more anhydrous copper sulfate forms."

**Predict the effect of pressure on 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃ (Higher tier only).** "There are 3 gas molecules on the left
and 2 on the right. Increasing the pressure shifts the position to the right, giving more SO₃."

## Method for any prediction

1. Write the balanced equation with state symbols.
2. Decide whether the forward reaction is exothermic or endothermic.
3. For pressure, count gas molecules on each side.
4. State the direction of shift and the effect on the amount of product.
