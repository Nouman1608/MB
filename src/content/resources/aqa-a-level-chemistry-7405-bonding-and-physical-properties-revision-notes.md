These notes condense section **3.1.3.4 Bonding and physical properties** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Papers 1 and 2 (and Paper 3). The [Bonding and physical properties study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-bonding-and-physical-properties/) explains each structure with worked examples, and the [practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-bonding-and-physical-properties-practice/) give exam-style practice.

## What the specification asks

- The **four crystal types**: ionic, metallic, macromolecular (giant covalent) and molecular.
- The structures of **diamond, graphite, ice, iodine, magnesium and sodium chloride**.
- **Relate melting point and conductivity** to the structure and bonding.
- **Explain the energy changes** at changes of state.
- **Draw diagrams** of these structures with specified numbers of particles.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Giant lattice | Regular structure with bonding throughout the crystal |
| Macromolecular | Giant covalent structure of atoms joined by covalent bonds |
| Molecular | Small molecules held by intermolecular forces |
| Delocalised | Not attached to one atom; free to move |
| Intermolecular force | Force between molecules (London, dipole-dipole, hydrogen bonding) |
| Sublimation | Solid to gas without becoming liquid |

## Summary table

| Type | Example | Particles | Bonding or forces | Melting point | Conducts solid | Conducts liquid |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ionic | NaCl | Ions | Electrostatic attraction | High | No | Yes |
| Metallic | Mg | Ions and delocalised electrons | Electrostatic attraction | High | Yes | Yes |
| Macromolecular | Diamond, graphite | Atoms | Covalent bonds | Very high | No (graphite yes) | No |
| Molecular | Ice, iodine | Molecules | Intermolecular forces | Low | No | No |

## The six structures

| Structure | Key features |
|---|---|
| Sodium chloride | Cubic lattice of Na⁺ and Cl⁻; each ion has 6 neighbours of opposite charge |
| Magnesium | Mg²⁺ ions in a sea of delocalised electrons; metallic |
| Diamond | Each C has 4 covalent bonds, tetrahedral; no free electrons; very hard |
| Graphite | Each C has 3 covalent bonds in hexagonal layers; one delocalised electron per C; weak London forces between layers; conducts along layers; soft |
| Ice | Hydrogen-bonded molecular lattice; each molecule has 4 hydrogen bonds; open structure; less dense than water |
| Iodine | I₂ molecules in a lattice with London forces; sublimes easily; non-conductor |

## What is overcome when each substance melts

| Substance | Overcome on melting |
|---|---|
| NaCl | Electrostatic attraction between ions |
| Mg | Metallic bonding (attraction between ions and delocalised electrons) |
| Diamond | Covalent bonds between carbon atoms |
| Graphite | Covalent bonds within layers (it sublimes at very high temperature) |
| Ice | Hydrogen bonds between water molecules |
| Iodine | London forces between I₂ molecules |

For molecular substances the covalent bonds within molecules **stay intact**.

## Energy at changes of state

- Energy supplied during melting or boiling is used to overcome forces between particles, not to raise the temperature.
- The stronger the forces, the more energy needed and the higher the melting or boiling point.
- Melting frees particles from fixed positions; boiling separates them completely.
- Freezing and condensing release the same energy as forces re-form.

## Answer structure for "explain" questions

1. Type of structure.
2. Particles present.
3. Forces holding them together, strong or weak.
4. What must be overcome (or what is free to move).
5. Link to the property.

## Worked reminders

**Graphite conducts but diamond does not.** In graphite each carbon uses three outer electrons, so the fourth is delocalised in the layer. In diamond all four outer electrons are in bonds.

**Silicon dioxide has a very high melting point.** It is a giant covalent lattice. Strong covalent bonds must be broken throughout the lattice.

**Iodine sublimes easily.** The I₂ molecules are held by weak London forces. Little energy is needed to separate them.

**Ice is less dense than water.** Hydrogen bonds hold the molecules in an open tetrahedral lattice with gaps. When it melts the lattice collapses and the molecules pack closer together.

**Identify an unknown.** A solid melts at 1000 °C, does not conduct when solid, but conducts when molten. It is ionic: the high melting point shows strong forces, and mobile ions appear only on melting.

## Drawing checklist

- NaCl: cube with alternating labelled ions.
- Mg: three rows of ions with charges, electrons between.
- Diamond: a tetrahedral unit extended outwards.
- Graphite: joined hexagons, second layer, weak forces between layers.
- Ice: central molecule with four hydrogen bonds.
- Iodine: pairs of atoms, regularly arranged.

## Common mistakes

- Saying covalent bonds break when ice or iodine melts or when water boils.
- Missing out the type of structure at the start of an answer.
- Calling the intermolecular forces in graphite "bonds".
- Forgetting that graphite conducts only along the layers.
- Writing "molecules" for sodium chloride or diamond.
- Explaining ice's low density without hydrogen bonds or the open lattice.

## Self-test

1. Name the four types of crystal structure.
2. State the number of covalent bonds formed by each carbon atom in diamond and in graphite.
3. Explain why graphite conducts electricity.
4. Explain why graphite is soft.
5. Explain why diamond has a very high melting point.
6. State what is overcome when iodine melts.
7. Explain why ice is less dense than liquid water.
8. A solid does not conduct when solid or molten, and melts at 1700 °C. Identify the structure type.
9. State two differences between the structures of magnesium and sodium chloride.
10. Why does sodium chloride conduct when dissolved in water?
11. Explain why iodine has a much lower melting point than sodium chloride.
12. Draw a labelled sketch of the sodium chloride lattice using at least eight ions.

### Answers

1. Ionic, metallic, macromolecular (giant covalent) and molecular.
2. Four in diamond; three in graphite.
3. One outer electron per carbon is delocalised within each layer, so it is free to move and carry charge.
4. The layers are held only by weak London forces, so they slide over each other easily.
5. Each carbon atom is joined to four others by strong covalent bonds in a giant lattice; much energy is needed to break these bonds.
6. The weak London forces between I₂ molecules (the I–I covalent bonds stay intact).
7. In ice, hydrogen bonds hold the molecules in an open lattice, which holds them further apart than in liquid water.
8. Macromolecular (giant covalent). The very high melting point shows strong covalent bonds, and there are no ions or delocalised electrons.
9. Magnesium has delocalised electrons, conducts when solid and has only positive ions; sodium chloride has fixed ions of both charges and conducts only when molten or dissolved.
10. The ions are separated and free to move in the solution.
11. Iodine is molecular, with weak London forces between molecules. Sodium chloride is ionic, with strong electrostatic attraction between ions in all directions.
12. A cube with alternating Na⁺ and Cl⁻ labelled at the corners; eight ions, four of each kind.
