Revision notes for **sub-theme 2.3** of Theme 2 in Pearson Edexcel A Level Urdu (9UR0): the media (میڈیا), which is the research subject for this theme. For the wider theme, read the [Theme 2 study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-theme-2-study-guide/).

## Research rules

- Theme 2 research must concern **one country or area**: Pakistan, or one area of India where Urdu is an official language. Check which you chose.
- Present it in Urdu, keep a file of named, dated, checked sources, and compare at least two viewpoints on each point.

## Areas to explore

- Print media (newspapers and magazines) and broadcast media (radio and television); the growth of private television channels.
- **Online and social media** and how people get news and entertainment.
- The **influence** (اثر) of the media on opinion, language and behaviour.
- **Freedom and responsibility**: regulation, censorship, accuracy and rumours.
- Urdu in the media: how register changes between news, drama and advertising.

## Issues and perspectives

The formal Urdu term for 'the media' is **ذرائع ابلاغ** (literally 'means of communication'); **میڈیا** is used just as widely. Using both shows range.

**From state broadcasting to many voices (if your research is on Pakistan).** For decades, radio and television were dominated by the state broadcasters, Radio Pakistan and Pakistan Television (PTV). In the early 2000s the airwaves were opened to private companies, and a regulator, the Pakistan Electronic Media Regulatory Authority (PEMRA), was set up to license and oversee them. Many private news and entertainment channels followed, alongside long-established Urdu newspapers such as روزنامہ جنگ. Supporters say this gave viewers choice and made politicians more accountable; critics point to sensational **بریکنگ نیوز**, the race for ratings and noisy talk shows. If your research is on an area of India instead, build the equivalent picture for that one area from your own sources.

**The rise of digital media.** Many young people now get news on their phones through social media and video platforms rather than from a newspaper or a television bulletin. This allows **شہری صحافت** (citizen journalism), where ordinary people record and share events, and gives a voice to those ignored by mainstream outlets. It also allows **افواہیں** (rumours) and **جعلی خبریں** (fake news) to spread very quickly, before anyone checks them.

**Freedom and responsibility.** **آزادیٔ اظہار** (freedom of expression) is essential for democracy, but most people accept some limits, for example on hate speech or invasion of privacy. The debate is about who sets the limits and whether regulation protects the public or silences criticism (**سنسرشپ**). Journalists' safety and independence are part of this debate; use reported cases from reliable sources rather than general claims.

**Influence on society and language.** Television drama, advertising and online content shape fashion, consumer habits and attitudes, for instance to marriage or women's work. The media also shapes Urdu itself: news uses formal, Persian- and Arabic-derived vocabulary, drama uses everyday speech, and advertising often mixes Urdu with English. This register point is a good way to link your research to the language you are studying.

## Useful sentences with grammar notes

1. **سوشل میڈیا پر افواہیں بہت تیزی سے پھیلتی ہیں۔** (Rumours spread very quickly on social media.) The verb agrees with افواہیں (feminine plural): پھیلتی ہیں.
2. **نجی ٹی وی چینلوں کے آنے سے ناظرین کو زیادہ انتخاب ملا ہے۔** (With the arrival of private TV channels, viewers have more choice.) **ملنا** takes the receiver with کو and agrees with what is received, انتخاب (masculine): ملا ہے.
3. **صحافیوں کی ذمہ داری ہے کہ وہ خبر کی تصدیق کریں۔** (It is journalists' responsibility to verify the news.) After a statement of duty, کہ is followed by a subjunctive: کریں.
4. **اخبار میں چھپنے والی ہر بات سچ نہیں ہوتی۔** (Not everything printed in a newspaper is true.) The participle چھپنے والی and the verb ہوتی both agree with بات (feminine).
5. **آزادیٔ اظہار اہم ہے، مگر اس کے ساتھ ذمہ داری بھی ضروری ہے۔** (Freedom of expression is important, but responsibility is also needed alongside it.) Note the hamza in آزادیٔ, which links the two words of the Persian construction.

## Model paragraph

گزشتہ دو دہائیوں میں پاکستان کے ذرائع ابلاغ میں بڑی تبدیلی آئی ہے۔ میری تحقیق کے مطابق نجی ٹی وی چینلوں کے آنے سے ناظرین کو خبروں اور تفریح کے لیے زیادہ انتخاب ملا ہے، اور اب بہت سے نوجوان خبریں ٹی وی کے بجائے موبائل فون پر دیکھتے ہیں۔ اس کا ایک مثبت پہلو یہ ہے کہ عام شہری بھی اپنی آواز دوسروں تک پہنچا سکتے ہیں۔ اس کے برعکس سوشل میڈیا پر بغیر تصدیق کے خبریں اور افواہیں تیزی سے پھیلتی ہیں، جس سے رائے عامہ گمراہ ہو سکتی ہے۔ میرے خیال میں میڈیا کی آزادی ضروری ہے، لیکن صحافیوں اور صارفین دونوں کو خبر کی تصدیق کی عادت ڈالنی چاہیے۔

**Translation:** Over the past two decades, Pakistan's media have changed greatly. According to my research, the arrival of private TV channels has given viewers more choice of news and entertainment, and many young people now watch the news on mobile phones instead of television. One positive aspect is that ordinary citizens can also make their voices heard. In contrast, unverified news and rumours spread quickly on social media, which can mislead public opinion. In my view media freedom is necessary, but both journalists and users should get into the habit of checking the news.

**Why it works:** a clear statement of change, a research-based point, a benefit and a risk linked by **اس کے برعکس**, and a balanced recommendation. Note **عادت ڈالنی چاہیے**: the infinitive agrees with عادت (feminine), so ڈالنی, not ڈالنا.

## Discussion and essay prompts

- «سوشل میڈیا نے اخبارات کی جگہ لے لی ہے۔» (Social media has taken the place of newspapers.) To what extent is this true in the place you researched?
- «میڈیا پر پابندیاں عوام کے مفاد میں ہوتی ہیں۔» (Restrictions on the media are in the public interest.) Discuss.
- Does television drama reflect society, or shape it?
- How should young people protect themselves from fake news?

## Exam traps

- Basing media research on several countries instead of one.
- Quoting a figure you cannot trace.
- Discussing only benefits or only risks; compare both.
- Not separating your research from the source text in a Section C answer.
- Treating میڈیا as feminine: it normally takes masculine agreement (میڈیا اثر ڈالتا ہے).
- Writing خبر as if it were masculine; it is feminine (خبر چھپی، خبریں پھیلتی ہیں).

9. Give the Urdu for 'viewers', 'readers' and 'listeners'.
