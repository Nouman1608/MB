## Section A

**1.** State why alkanes are described as saturated hydrocarbons. **[2]**

**2.** Which equation shows a substitution reaction?

A: C₂H₄ + H₂ → C₂H₆

B: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O

C: C₂H₆ + Cl₂ → C₂H₅Cl + HCl

D: C₂H₄ + Br₂ → C₂H₄Br₂

**[1]**

**3.** Define the term *substitution reaction*. **[1]**

**4.** Name the condition needed for an alkane to react with chlorine. **[1]**

**5.** Name the products of the complete combustion of an alkane. **[2]**

**6.** Hexane is shaken with aqueous bromine in normal laboratory conditions. State what is observed. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Methane reacts with chlorine.

**(a)** Write a balanced equation for the reaction, showing monosubstitution. **[2]**

**(b)** Name the organic product. **[1]**

**(c)** The reaction does not take place in the dark. Explain why. **[2]**

**8.** Write a balanced equation for the complete combustion of:

**(a)** propane **[2]**

**(b)** butane **[2]**

**9.** Propane reacts with chlorine in ultraviolet light to give two different monosubstituted products.

**(a)** Draw the displayed formulae of both products. **[2]**

**(b)** Name both products. **[2]**

**(c)** State the relationship between the two products. **[1]**

**10.** 50 cm³ of methane is burned completely in excess oxygen. All volumes are measured at room temperature and pressure.

**(a)** Calculate the volume of oxygen that reacts. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide formed. **[1]**

**11.** A student writes this equation for the reaction of ethane with chlorine:

C₂H₆ + Cl₂ → C₂H₅Cl + H₂

**(a)** Identify the error and write the correct equation. **[2]**

**(b)** Draw the displayed formula of the organic product. **[1]**

**12.** Alkanes are described as generally unreactive.

**(a)** Explain why alkanes are generally unreactive. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe **one** test result that shows an alkane is less reactive than an alkene. **[2]**

**(c)** Despite being generally unreactive, alkanes are widely used as fuels. Explain why. **[1]**

**13.** Butane reacts with chlorine in ultraviolet light.

**(a)** Write the structural formulae of the **two** monosubstituted products. **[2]**

**(b)** A student suggests a third product, CH₃CHClCH₂CH₃. Explain why this is not a different product. **[1]**

**(c)** State the molecular formula of the monosubstituted products. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Hydrocarbons: they contain hydrogen and carbon only [1]. Saturated: all carbon–carbon bonds are single bonds [1]. **[2]**

**2.** C [1]. **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* A and D are addition reactions (one product); B is combustion.

**3.** A reaction in which one atom or group of atoms is replaced by another atom or group of atoms [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Ultraviolet (UV) light [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Carbon dioxide [1]; water [1]. **[2]**

**6.** No change / the orange colour remains [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl: correct formulae [1]; balanced [1]

**(b)** Chloromethane [1]

**(c)** The reaction needs ultraviolet light to provide the activation energy [1]; in the dark, colliding particles do not have enough energy (the activation energy) to react [1].

**[5]**

**8. (a)** C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O: correct products [1]; balanced [1]

**(b)** 2C₄H₁₀ + 13O₂ → 8CO₂ + 10H₂O: correct products [1]; balanced [1]

**[4]**

*Examiner insight:* C₄H₁₀ + 6½O₂ → 4CO₂ + 5H₂O is balanced, but most mark schemes expect whole numbers. Double it.

**9. (a)** Chlorine on an end carbon [1]; chlorine on the middle carbon [1], each with all atoms and bonds shown:

```
    H   H   H                 H   Cl  H
    |   |   |                 |   |   |
H - C - C - C - Cl        H - C - C - C - H
    |   |   |                 |   |   |
    H   H   H                 H   H   H
```

**(b)** 1-chloropropane [1]; 2-chloropropane [1]

**(c)** They are structural isomers (same molecular formula, C₃H₇Cl, different structural formulae) [1].

**[5]**

**10. (a)** CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O, so 2 × 50 = 100 cm³ [1]

**(b)** 1 × 50 = 50 cm³ [1]

**[2]**

*Examiner insight:* the volume ratio comes straight from the balanced equation, because equal volumes of gases at the same temperature and pressure contain equal numbers of moles. Do not include the water, which condenses to a liquid at room temperature.

**11. (a)** The second product should be hydrogen chloride, not hydrogen [1]; C₂H₆ + Cl₂ → C₂H₅Cl + HCl [1]

**(b)** Two carbons, five H atoms and one Cl atom, all bonds shown, each carbon with four bonds [1]:

```
    H   H
    |   |
H - C - C - Cl
    |   |
    H   H
```

**[3]**

**12. (a)** They have only strong single C–C and C–H bonds / no functional group (no C=C double bond) [1]

**(b)** An alkene decolourises aqueous bromine (orange to colourless) [1]; an alkane leaves it orange [1]

**(c)** Their combustion releases a large amount of energy (is highly exothermic) [1]

**[4]**

**13. (a)** CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂Cl [1]; CH₃CH₂CHClCH₃ [1]

**(b)** It is the same as CH₃CH₂CHClCH₃ (2-chlorobutane) numbered from the other end; the chlorine is still on the second carbon of a four-carbon chain [1]

**(c)** C₄H₉Cl [1]

**[4]**

*Examiner insight:* to check whether two structures are the same, number the chain from both ends and take the lower number for the chlorine. If both drawings give the same name, they are the same compound.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving H₂ instead of HCl as the second product of substitution.
- Writing "light" or "heat" instead of ultraviolet light, or not linking UV light to activation energy.
- Leaving halves in combustion equations, or miscounting oxygen atoms.
- Drawing more than one chlorine atom for a monosubstitution product.
- Treating a redrawn or renumbered chain as a new isomer.
