## Questions

**1.** An atom of lead has the nuclear symbol ²⁰⁷₈₂Pb. It is electrically neutral. State the number of protons, the number of neutrons and the number of electrons. **[3]**

**2.** Four nuclei are written below.

²⁸₁₄Si   ³⁰₁₄Si   ³⁰₁₅P   ³²₁₆S

**(a)** Name the two nuclei that are isotopes of the same element. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why ³⁰₁₄Si and ³⁰₁₅P are not isotopes of each other, even though their top numbers match. **[2]**

**3.** The nucleus of a sodium atom has a relative charge of +11. One isotope has 12 neutrons.

**(a)** State which particles are in the nucleus and which of them gives it its charge. **[2]**
**(b)** Write the nuclear symbol for this isotope of sodium. **[2]**

**4.** Describe each of the four emissions from unstable nuclei: alpha particle, beta particle, neutron and gamma ray. **[4]**

**5.** Copy and complete the table to show how the nucleus changes when each emission leaves it. **[4]**

| Emission | Change in mass number | Change in atomic number |
|---|---|---|
| Alpha | | |
| Beta | | |
| Neutron | | |
| Gamma | | |

**6.** A nucleus of thorium, ²³⁰₉₀Th, decays by emitting an alpha particle. Write a balanced equation and name the new element. **[3]**

**7.** Potassium-42 has the symbol ⁴²₁₉K. It decays by beta emission. Write the balanced equation and name the new element. **[3]**

**8.** A nucleus of calcium has 20 protons and 25 neutrons. It emits a single neutron.

**(a)** State the number of protons, the number of neutrons and the mass number of the nucleus that is left. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain whether the nucleus that is left is the same element as the original, and say how the two nuclei are related. **[2]**

**9.** Caesium-137, ¹³⁷₅₅Cs, decays by beta emission to an excited nucleus of barium. The barium nucleus then emits a gamma ray.

**(a)** Write the equation for the beta emission. **[2]**
**(b)** Write the equation for the gamma emission. **[2]**
**(c)** State how many neutrons the caesium nucleus and the barium nucleus each contain. **[1]**

**10.** A nucleus of thorium-232, ²³²₉₀Th, goes through a series of decays and ends as lead-208, ²⁰⁸₈₂Pb. Only alpha and beta emissions occur.

**(a)** Work out how many alpha particles are emitted. **[3]**
**(b)** Work out how many beta particles are emitted. **[3]**
**(c)** State why the number of beta particles cannot be found from the mass numbers alone. **[1]**

**11.** A student writes this equation for the decay of lead-214.

²¹⁴₈₂Pb → ²¹⁴₈₂Bi + ⁰₋₁e

**(a)** Explain what is wrong with it. **[2]**
**(b)** Write the correct equation. **[2]**

**12.** Two isotopes of neon are written ²⁰₁₀Ne and ²²₁₀Ne. Compare the two nuclei in terms of their identities, their charges and their masses. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Protons = 82 (the bottom number) [1]. Neutrons = 207 - 82 = **125** [1]. An uncharged atom has as many electrons as protons, so electrons = **82** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the subtraction for the neutrons. A wrong answer with no working leaves nothing to credit.

**2. (a)** ²⁸₁₄Si and ³⁰₁₄Si, which are both silicon with 14 protons [1].
**(b)** They have different numbers of protons, 14 and 15 [1], so they are different elements, and isotopes must be the same element [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The explanation has to refer to protons. Saying only "different symbols" restates the question and gives no reason.

**3. (a)** The nucleus contains protons and neutrons [1]. The protons give it its positive charge, because neutrons have no charge [1].
**(b)** A = 11 + 12 = 23 [1], so the symbol is **²³₁₁Na** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A symbol needs both numbers in the right places. Writing 11 on top and 23 below gives a different nucleus.

**4.** Alpha: two protons and two neutrons, the same as a helium nucleus [1]. Beta: a fast electron emitted from the nucleus [1]. Neutron: a single neutron with no charge [1]. Gamma: electromagnetic radiation with no mass and no charge [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Be specific about the beta particle. "An electron" alone may not show that it comes from the nucleus, so add where it is emitted from.

**5.** Alpha: A down by 4, Z down by 2 [1]. Beta: A no change, Z up by 1 [1]. Neutron: A down by 1, Z no change [1]. Gamma: A no change, Z no change [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give direction and size, such as "down by 4". A bare 4 does not say whether the number rises or falls.

**6.** Mass number 230 - 4 = 226 [1]. Atomic number 90 - 2 = 88, which is radium [1]. **²³⁰₉₀Th → ²²⁶₈₈Ra + ⁴₂He** [1]. Check: 226 + 4 = 230 and 88 + 2 = 90. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the alpha particle with both of its numbers, ⁴₂He. Then the balance can be checked on both lines.

**7.** The mass number stays at 42 [1]. Atomic number 19 + 1 = 20, which is calcium [1]. **⁴²₁₉K → ⁴²₂₀Ca + ⁰₋₁e** [1]. Check: 20 + (-1) = 19. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The charge line is where beta equations usually go wrong. Show 20 + (-1) = 19 so the working is visible.

**8. (a)** Protons = 20 (unchanged) [1]. Neutrons = 24 and mass number = 44 [1].
**(b)** It is still calcium, because it has 20 protons [1]. It is a different isotope of calcium, with one fewer neutron [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Start from the original nucleus: 20 + 25 = 45. Then lose 1 from the mass number and none from the protons.

**9. (a)** Mass number stays at 137 and Z = 55 + 1 = 56, which is barium [1]. **¹³⁷₅₅Cs → ¹³⁷₅₆Ba (excited) + ⁰₋₁e** [1].
**(b)** **¹³⁷₅₆Ba (excited) → ¹³⁷₅₆Ba + γ** [1]. Both numbers are unchanged and gamma has none [1].
**(c)** Caesium-137: 137 - 55 = 82 neutrons. Barium: 137 - 56 = 81 neutrons [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In a gamma equation keep the nuclear symbol the same on both sides. Changing the element there suggests a particle was emitted.

**10. (a)** The mass number falls from 232 to 208, a drop of 24 [1]. Only alpha emission changes the mass number, by 4 each time [1]. 24 ÷ 4 = **6 alpha particles** [1].
**(b)** Six alpha particles lower Z by 12, so Z would be 90 - 12 = 78 [1]. Lead has Z = 82, which is 4 higher [1]. Each beta raises Z by 1, so **4 beta particles** [1].
**(c)** Beta emission does not change the mass number [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Set the working out as two separate stories, one for mass number and one for charge. Mixing them in one line is where slips happen.

**11. (a)** A beta particle has a charge of -1, so the product must have a charge one higher [1]. The student left the atomic number at 82, so the charge does not balance (82 on the left, 81 on the right) [1].
**(b)** The atomic number rises to 83, which is bismuth [1]. **²¹⁴₈₂Pb → ²¹⁴₈₃Bi + ⁰₋₁e** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* State the numbers that fail to match. Saying "it does not balance" gives less than saying 82 on the left and 81 on the right.

**12.** Identities: both are neon, with 10 protons [1]. Charge: both nuclei are +10 [1]. Mass: neon-22 has a larger mass number, 22 against 20 [1]. It has 12 neutrons against 10, so two more [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the three words the question gives you as headings. Each one needs its own statement for its own mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reading the symbol the wrong way round, giving the atomic number as the top number.
- Giving the neutron number as the mass number rather than A - Z.
- Saying isotopes differ in protons or electrons when they differ only in neutrons.
- Treating the same mass number as proof of the same element.
- Using +1 or a mass of 1 for the beta particle in a balance.
- Failing to raise Z by 1 in beta emission, or lowering it.
- Not naming the new element after finding its atomic number.
- Changing the mass number in a gamma equation.
- Counting alpha and beta emissions together instead of taking mass number first, then charge.
