**These notes condense topic 3.2.2 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Pakistan Studies 9236, The
People and Economy of Pakistan, examined in Paper 2 Section B.** Learn the definitions, the reasons
behind population change and migration, how the economic sectors have shifted, the development
indicators, trade patterns and the opportunities and challenges of urban growth.

## Syllabus coverage

**9236 topic 3.2.2 (Version 1.9)**
- **3.2.2.1** Population structure, growth, density, distribution, migration
- **3.2.2.2** Sector changes over 100 years; agriculture, manufacturing and services location and employment
- **3.2.2.3** GDP, HDI, FDI, trade; imports, exports, balance of trade, remittances; partners; trade blocs and the WTO
- **3.2.2.4** Urbanisation trends; Karachi and Lahore; opportunities, challenges and strategies

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Birth rate / death rate | Births / deaths per 1,000 people per year |
| Infant mortality rate | Deaths of babies under one year per 1,000 live births |
| Life expectancy | Average number of years a person is expected to live |
| Natural increase | Birth rate minus death rate |
| Population density | People per km² |
| Push / pull factors | Reasons that drive people away / attract them |
| Remittances | Money sent home by workers abroad |
| Primary / secondary / tertiary / quaternary | Raw materials / manufacturing / services / knowledge work |
| GDP | Value of all goods and services produced in a year |
| HDI | Human Development Index: health, education and income combined |
| FDI | Foreign direct investment by overseas companies |
| Balance of trade | Exports minus imports |
| Trade bloc | Group of countries with reduced trade barriers between them |
| Megacity | City of more than 10 million people |
| Urbanisation | Increase in the share of people living in towns and cities |

## Population: key points

- About **241 million** people (2023 census); youthful structure.
- Death rate fell faster than birth rate → rapid natural increase.
- Birth rate falling with girls' education, later marriage, family planning, urbanisation.
- **Dense:** Indus Plain, Punjab and Sindh, big cities. **Sparse:** northern mountains, Balochistan, deserts.

## Migration: key points

| Type | Push | Pull | Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rural–urban | Low farm incomes, mechanisation, floods | Jobs, schools, hospitals | City growth; katchi abadis; rural labour shortage |
| International | Unemployment, low wages | Gulf jobs; UK, Europe, North America | Remittances; brain drain; cultural links |
| Refugees into Pakistan | War in Afghanistan | Safety | Pressure on services; cultural exchange |

## Economic sectors

- **Primary:** falling share of GDP; still employs more than a third of workers; irrigated Punjab and Sindh.
- **Secondary:** textiles (Faisalabad, Karachi, Lahore), cement, fertiliser, sugar, cars, sports goods and surgical instruments (Sialkot).
- **Tertiary:** largest share of GDP; retail, transport, banking, telecoms, health, education.
- **Quaternary:** IT and software exports, research; Lahore, Karachi, Islamabad.
- **Location factors:** raw materials, power, labour, transport, ports, markets, government policy.

## Trade

- **Exports:** textiles and clothing, rice, leather, sports goods, surgical instruments, fruit, fish.
- **Imports:** oil and LNG, machinery, chemicals, palm oil, steel, vehicles.
- **Trade deficit** usually; remittances help close the gap.
- **Partners:** China, USA, UAE, Saudi Arabia, UK and EU.
- **Blocs and bodies:** EU GSP+, China FTA, ECO, SAARC, WTO.

## Development indicators: strengths and limits

| Indicator | Use | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| GDP | Size of the whole economy | Large population means low GDP per person |
| GDP per capita | Average income | Hides inequality between provinces and classes |
| HDI | Health, education and income together | National average hides rural–urban gaps |
| FDI | Confidence of foreign investors | Profits may leave; can add to debt |
| Trade balance | Strength of exports against imports | Affected by oil prices and exchange rates |

## Urbanisation

- About two-fifths of people urban; rising.
- **Karachi:** largest city and port; **Lahore:** Punjab capital; both megacities.
- **Opportunities:** jobs, universities, innovation, investment, transport projects.
- **Challenges:** informal jobs, housing shortage, katchi abadis, congestion, smog, sewage.
- **Strategies:** low-cost housing; settlement upgrading (Orangi Pilot Project); mass transit (Lahore Orange Line, bus rapid transit); pollution controls.

## Worked example

**Question:** Explain why the tertiary sector has grown in Pakistan. **[4]**

**Model answer:** As incomes rise and cities grow, demand for shops, transport, banking and health
care increases [1]. Mechanisation has reduced farm jobs, so workers move into urban service work [1].
Better education and internet access have created IT and call-centre jobs [1]. Government spending
on education, health and administration also employs many people [1].

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- **Saying agriculture is "no longer important".** Its share of GDP has fallen, but it still employs the largest single share of workers.
- **Confusing GDP and HDI.** HDI includes health and education, not just income.
- **Listing push and pull factors without impacts.** The specification asks about impacts on culture, economy and society.
- **Ignoring remittances in balance-of-trade answers.** They are named in the specification.

## How is this topic examined?

This topic is examined in OxfordAQA 9236 Paper 2, Section B, worth 28 of the paper's 77 marks; each paper lasts 1 hour 30 minutes. The section mixes multiple-choice, short and extended response questions on this compulsory topic. Short items reward precise named facts, so learn the dates, places and people in the tables above; the extended responses reward a clear line of argument, two or three developed points with evidence, and a judgement that answers the question.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why are Karachi and Lahore so large?** Karachi has the main seaport and industry; Lahore is a
historic capital with industry, services and universities. Both attract migrants from across
Pakistan.

**What is GSP+?** An EU trade scheme that removes or lowers tariffs on many Pakistani exports, in
return for commitments on human rights, labour and environmental standards.
