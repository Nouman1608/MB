This set covers sections 9.1 and 9.2 of the Cambridge O Level Statistics (4040) syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027: correlation, semi-average lines of best fit, y = mx + c, and prediction. For an odd number of pairs, leave out the middle pair and say so.

## Questions

**1.** State whether you would expect positive correlation, negative correlation or no correlation between each pair of variables, for a group of adults.

**(a)** The age of a used car and its selling price. **[1]**
**(b)** The number of hours of sunshine in a day and the number of sunglasses sold. **[1]**
**(c)** The height of the adult and the number of letters in the surname. **[1]**
**(d)** The height above sea level of a town and the average air temperature there. **[1]**

**2.** Eight people record their weekly television time in hours (x) and a general knowledge score (y).

| x | 2 | 5 | 8 | 10 | 12 | 15 | 18 | 20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 70 | 55 | 75 | 58 | 68 | 52 | 66 | 57 |

Describe the correlation between x and y. **[3]**

**3.** A line of best fit for the fuel left in a lorry tank (y litres) after travelling x hundred kilometres passes through the points (2, 52) and (6, 22).

**(a)** Work out the equation of the line, written as y = mx + c. **[3]**
**(b)** Say what the gradient tells you about the fuel use. **[1]**
**(c)** Use the line to estimate the fuel left after 500 km. **[1]**

**4.** The height of a seedling (y mm) is measured x days after sowing.

| x | 3 | 5 | 7 | 9 | 11 | 13 | 15 | 17 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 12 | 15 | 17 | 22 | 23 | 28 | 29 | 34 |

**(a)** Plot the data as a scatter diagram on graph paper. **[2]**
**(b)** Use the method of semi-averages to find the equation of the line of best fit. **[5]**
**(c)** Estimate the height after 10 days. **[1]**
**(d)** A pupil uses the line to estimate the height after 40 days. Comment on this estimate. **[2]**

**5.** A shop records its monthly advertising spend (x, in thousands of rupees) and the number of new customers (y).

| x | 40 | 10 | 70 | 25 | 55 | 85 | 20 | 60 | 35 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 45 | 10 | 80 | 24 | 60 | 88 | 16 | 68 | 30 |

Find the equation of the line of best fit by the method of semi-averages. **[6]**

**6.** For a block of flats, daily outdoor temperature (x °C) and heating units used (y) were recorded for days with temperatures from 2 °C to 16 °C. The line of best fit is y = 48 − 2.5x.

**(a)** Estimate the units used when the temperature is 10 °C. **[1]**
**(b)** Estimate the units used when the temperature is 25 °C, and comment on your answer. **[2]**
**(c)** Estimate the temperature on a day when 30 units are used. **[2]**

**7.** The mean of the x values in a data set is 12 and the mean of the y values is 40. A line of best fit passes through the mean point and through the point (20, 56).

**(a)** Find the equation of the line. **[3]**
**(b)** Estimate y when x = 15. **[1]**

**8.** The weekly number of book loans (y, in hundreds) at a library is recorded for weeks x = 1 to 9.

| x | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 11 | 13 | 16 | 18 | 40 | 23 | 26 | 28 | 31 |

**(a)** Identify the outlier and suggest one possible reason for it. **[2]**
**(b)** Ignore the outlier. Find the equation of the line of best fit by semi-averages for the other eight points. **[5]**
**(c)** Use your line to estimate y for week 5. **[1]**

**9.** A café varies the price of a sandwich (x, in rupees) over ten weeks and records the number sold (y).

| x | 5 | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 | 30 | 35 | 40 | 45 | 50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 98 | 90 | 85 | 77 | 70 | 66 | 57 | 50 | 44 | 38 |

**(a)** Describe the correlation. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the equation of the line of best fit by semi-averages. **[5]**
**(c)** Estimate the number sold at a price of 28 rupees. **[1]**
**(d)** Estimate the number sold at a price of 80 rupees, and comment on the result. **[2]**
**(e)** Interpret the gradient in this context. **[1]**

**10.** Fire brigade records show strong positive correlation between the number of fire engines sent to a fire and the cost of the damage. Explain why this does not mean that sending more engines causes more damage. **[3]**

**11.** Six pairs of values are given: (1, 8), (2, 12), (3, k), (4, 22), (5, 28), (6, 31). The line of best fit found by the method of semi-averages is y = 5x + 2. Find the value of k. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) Negative correlation [1]. (b) Positive correlation [1]. (c) No correlation [1]. (d) Negative correlation [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* give one clear type for each pair, since offering two options may not be credited.

**2.** The points slope downwards slightly, so it is negative [1]. They are widely scattered, so it is weak [1]. People who watch more television tend to score slightly lower [1]. **Weak negative correlation.** **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* give type, strength and a statement in context; two words alone may leave marks unclaimed.

**3.** (a) Gradient = (22 − 52)/(6 − 2) = −30/4 [1] = −7.5 [1]. Using (2, 52): 52 = −7.5 × 2 + c, so c = 67, giving **y = −7.5x + 67** [1].
**(b)** The lorry uses 7.5 litres of fuel for every 100 km [1].
**(c)** 500 km is x = 5, so y = −37.5 + 67 = **29.5 litres** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* x is in hundreds of kilometres, so substituting 500 instead of 5 gives a meaningless answer.

**4.** (a) Points plotted correctly [1], with axes labelled and scales suitable [1].
**(b)** Lower half x = 3, 5, 7, 9: mean x = 24/4 = 6 and mean y = 66/4 = 16.5 [1]. Upper half x = 11, 13, 15, 17: mean x = 56/4 = 14 and mean y = 114/4 = 28.5 [1]. m = (28.5 − 16.5)/(14 − 6) = 12/8 = 1.5 [1]. 16.5 = 1.5 × 6 + c, so c = 7.5 [1]. **y = 1.5x + 7.5** [1].
**(c)** y = 15 + 7.5 = **22.5 mm** [1].
**(d)** Day 40 is far outside the data, which stop at day 17, so this is extrapolation [1]. The line gives 67.5 mm, but growth is unlikely to stay linear, so the estimate is unreliable [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* name the problem (extrapolation) and give a reason in context; "it is not accurate" says little.

**5.** Order by x: 10, 20, 25, 35, 40, 55, 60, 70, 85 [1]. Nine pairs, so leave out the middle pair (40, 45) [1]. Lower half: x = 10, 20, 25, 35 gives mean x = 22.5, and y = 10, 16, 24, 30 gives mean y = 80/4 = 20 [1]. Upper half: x = 55, 60, 70, 85 gives mean x = 67.5, and y = 60, 68, 80, 88 gives mean y = 296/4 = 74 [1]. m = (74 − 20)/(67.5 − 22.5) = 54/45 = 1.2 [1]. 20 = 1.2 × 22.5 + c, so c = −7, giving **y = 1.2x − 7** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* keep each y value paired with its x value when you re-order; a slip spoils every later step.

**6.** (a) y = 48 − 25 = **23 units** [1].
**(b)** y = 48 − 62.5 = **−14.5** [1]. Negative use is impossible, and 25 °C is outside the data range (2 to 16) [1].
**(c)** 30 = 48 − 2.5x [1], so x = 18/2.5 = **7.2 °C** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* to find x from y, rearrange before you calculate, and give the answer with its unit.

**7.** (a) The line passes through (12, 40) and (20, 56). m = (56 − 40)/(20 − 12) [1] = 16/8 = 2 [1]. 40 = 2 × 12 + c, so c = 16, giving **y = 2x + 16** [1].
**(b)** y = 30 + 16 = **46** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* write the mean point as a pair of coordinates before you start the gradient.

**8.** (a) The outlier is (5, 40) [1]. A possible reason: a recording error, or a book fair that week [1].
**(b)** Lower half x = 1, 2, 3, 4: mean x = 2.5 and mean y = 58/4 = 14.5 [1]. Upper half x = 6, 7, 8, 9: mean x = 7.5 and mean y = 108/4 = 27 [1]. m = (27 − 14.5)/(7.5 − 2.5) = 12.5/5 = 2.5 [1]. 14.5 = 2.5 × 2.5 + c, so c = 8.25 [1]. **y = 2.5x + 8.25** [1].
**(c)** y = 12.5 + 8.25 = **20.75** (about 2,075 loans) [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* if you exclude a point, say so and say why; the remaining pairs must still split into equal halves.

**9.** (a) As the price rises, the number sold falls, so the correlation is negative [1]. The points lie very close to a straight line, so it is strong [1].
**(b)** Lower half x = 5 to 25: mean x = 15 and mean y = 420/5 = 84 [1]. Upper half x = 30 to 50: mean x = 40 and mean y = 255/5 = 51 [1]. m = (51 − 84)/(40 − 15) = −33/25 = −1.32 [1]. 84 = −1.32 × 15 + c, so c = 103.8 [1]. **y = −1.32x + 103.8** [1].
**(c)** y = −36.96 + 103.8 = 66.84, so about **67 sandwiches** [1].
**(d)** y = −105.6 + 103.8 = −1.8 [1]. A negative number sold is impossible, and 80 rupees is outside the range 5 to 50, so the line should not be used [1].
**(e)** Each extra rupee in price reduces sales by about 1.32 sandwiches [1]. **[11]**
*Examiner insight:* interpret a negative gradient as a fall, in the units of the question.

**10.** The correlation shows an association only, not cause [1]. Large fires cause both more engines to be sent and more damage, so the size of the fire is a lurking variable [1]. The engines do not make the fire bigger [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* name the third factor and say how it affects both variables.

**11.** Six pairs, so each half has 3 pairs: x = 1, 2, 3 and x = 4, 5, 6 [1]. The line passes through both semi-average points. Lower mean x = 2, so the lower mean y = 5 × 2 + 2 = 12 [1]. Lower mean y = (8 + 12 + k)/3 [1], so (20 + k)/3 = 12 and k = 36 − 20 = **16** [1]. Upper half check: mean y = (22 + 28 + 31)/3 = 27 = 5 × 5 + 2 [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* checking the other half confirms your working and shows the method.

## Where marks are usually lost

* Describing correlation in one word without strength or context.
* Splitting into halves before putting the pairs in order of x.
* Subtracting in different orders on the top and bottom of the gradient.
* Giving a prediction outside the data range with no comment on reliability.
* Accepting an impossible value, such as negative sales, without comment.
* Saying that correlation proves cause.
