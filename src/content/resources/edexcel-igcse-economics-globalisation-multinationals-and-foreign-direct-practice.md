All countries, firms and figures are invented. ## Questions

**1.** What is meant by globalisation? **[2]**

**2.** State two reasons for globalisation. **[2]**

**3.** Country K sells tiles to Country L. The factory price is 20.00 a tile, transport is 4.00 and Country L charges a tariff of 10% of the factory price. Later, transport falls by 25% and the tariff is removed.

**(a)** Calculate the landed cost before the changes. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the landed cost after the changes. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage fall in landed cost, to 1 decimal place. **[1]**

**4.** Explain one way that lower communication costs help an MNC to grow. **[3]**

**5.** **(a)** What is meant by foreign direct investment? **[2]**
**(b)** FDI into Country M rose from 40 million to 52 million. Calculate the percentage increase. **[2]**

**6.** A firm compares sites for a new plant. Unit costs (illustrative):

| Site | Materials | Labour | Delivery to customers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6 | 5 | 4 |
| 2 | 5 | 3 | 3 |
| 3 | 4 | 4 | 8 |

**(a)** Calculate the total unit cost at each site. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain one reason, other than cost, why the firm might still choose Site 3. **[3]**

**7.** A manufacturer has fixed costs of 48 million and variable costs of 30 a unit.

**(a)** Calculate average cost at output of 1.2 million units and at 4.8 million units. **[4]**
**(b)** Calculate, to 1 decimal place, the percentage by which average cost falls. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how economies of scale may encourage a firm to become an MNC. **[3]**

**8.** A town has a labour force of 15,000 and 4% unemployment. Cheaper imports close a footwear industry employing 900. An MNC call centre opens and employs 400.

**(a)** Calculate the net change in jobs. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the new unemployment rate, to 2 decimal places, assuming the labour force is unchanged. **[3]**
**(c)** Analyse one reason why the closure may harm workers for longer than the first few months. **[4]**

**9.** A subsidiary of an MNC earns 30 million profit in Host H, which taxes profit at 25%. It then pays an 18 million fee to a group company in another country.

**(a)** Calculate the tax paid in H before the fee. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the tax paid in H after the fee. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the fall in H's tax revenue. **[1]**
**(d)** What is meant by tax avoidance? **[2]**

**10.** Analyse two disadvantages of an MNC setting up in a host country. **[6]**

**11.** A car-parts MNC invests in Country A, a developing country. It employs 3,000 workers, trains them, builds a road to its plant and pays 15% tax on profit. It sends most of its profit abroad, and a river near the plant becomes polluted. Evaluate whether this FDI benefits Country A. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** Increased integration [1] and interdependence of economies [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Write both ideas, integration and interdependence. An answer about trade flows alone covers only part of what the question asks.

**2.** Any two of: fewer tariffs and quotas; lower transport costs; lower communication costs; more significant MNCs. First reason [1]. Second reason [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A State answer needs no explanation. Do not write more than a sentence, and do not give three in the hope that one is right.

**3. (a)** Tariff = 0.10 × 20.00 = 2.00 [1]. Landed cost = 20.00 + 4.00 + 2.00 = **26.00** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Transport = 4.00 × 0.75 = 3.00 [1]. Landed cost = 20.00 + 3.00 + 0 = **23.00** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** (26 − 23) ÷ 26 × 100 = **11.5%** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each part of the landed cost on its own line. The specification's command-word table says workings should be given on Calculate questions.

**4.** Lower communication costs make it cheaper to manage operations in several countries [1], for example by video link instead of travel [1], so the MNC can run more plants and sell to more markets [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A strong Explain answer states a point and then expands it twice, as the specification's table describes. Name the cost that falls and the result for the firm.

**5. (a)** Investment by a firm [1] in business assets in another country [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** (52 − 40) ÷ 40 × 100 [1] = **30%** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the original figure, not the new one. A bare "12" is a change, not a percentage.

**6. (a)** Site 1: 6 + 5 + 4 = 15 [1]. Site 2: 5 + 3 + 3 = 11 [1]. Site 3: 4 + 4 + 8 = 16 [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Stable rules and political conditions at Site 3 reduce risk [1], so the firm is less likely to face sudden costs or closure [1], which protects long-run investment even though unit costs are higher [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Pick a factor that is not on the cost table, such as skills, infrastructure or access to a market. Repeating labour cost adds nothing new.

**7. (a)** At 1.2 million: total cost = 48m + (30 × 1.2m) = 84m [1]. Average = 84m ÷ 1.2m = **70** [1]. At 4.8 million: total = 48m + 144m = 192m [1]. Average = 192m ÷ 4.8m = **40** [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** (70 − 40) ÷ 70 × 100 = **42.9%** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Serving more markets raises output [1], which spreads fixed costs over more units and lowers average cost [1], and one way to reach those markets is to invest abroad as an MNC [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the lower average cost to the decision to locate abroad. Saying only "costs fall" stops short of the question.

**8. (a)** 400 − 900 = **−500** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Unemployed before = 0.04 × 15,000 = 600 [1]. After = 600 + 500 = 1,100 [1]. Rate = 1,100 ÷ 15,000 × 100 = **7.33%** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** Footwear workers may have skills that suit few other jobs [1], so they may stay unemployed or take lower-paid work [1]. Their lost income reduces spending in local shops [1], which can cost further jobs in the town [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Work out the new number of unemployed before finding the rate. In (c), a chain such as lost wages, lower spending, fewer local jobs scores better than the word "unemployment" alone.

**9. (a)** 0.25 × 30 = **7.5 million** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Taxable profit = 30 − 18 = 12 [1]. Tax = 0.25 × 12 = **3 million** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** 7.5 − 3 = **4.5 million** [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** Using legal rules [1] to reduce the amount of tax paid [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Define avoidance as legal. If you mention evasion, state that it is illegal, so the two terms are not mixed.

**10.** Suggested points: environmental damage and profits moved abroad. Disadvantage one: the plant may pollute air or water [1], especially where rules are weak [1], harming health and farming, so the host bears the cost [1]. Disadvantage two: profits are sent to owners overseas [1], so less income stays in the host economy [1], which limits the gain in spending and tax [1]. Credit any two of the specification's disadvantages, such as avoiding paying taxes. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* For Analyse, push each disadvantage through a chain and keep to the host country. Two half-developed points usually do worse than two chains.

**11.** The marks below are a suggested way to self-mark, not the board's scheme.
Benefits (3 marks): jobs and wages raise spending [1]; training develops skills so workers can earn more later [1]; the road is infrastructure other firms can use, and the 15% tax adds to government revenue [1].
Costs (3 marks): most profit leaves, so less income stays in Country A [1]; river pollution harms health and fishing, and the damage may cost more than the gain [1]; the firm may move if costs rise elsewhere, ending the jobs [1].
Judgement (3 marks): weigh the two sets using the case [1]; name a deciding factor, such as whether the tax and pollution rules are enforced and how much profit is kept [1]; reach a supported conclusion, for example that the FDI helps if rules are enforced and workers move into skilled posts, but not if profits leave and the river stays polluted [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* An Evaluate answer needs a conclusion that follows from your own weighing. Use the facts given (3,000 workers, 15% tax, pollution), not general statements.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining globalisation as "trade between countries" only.
- Showing only the final figure on a Calculate question, where the table says workings should be given.
- Using the later figure as the base when working out a percentage change.
- Adding the tariff to the transport cost instead of taking a percentage of the factory price.
- Writing "jobs" as an advantage with no link to wages and spending.
- Mixing up tax avoidance and tax evasion.
- Describing the problem for the home country when the question asks about the host.
- Concluding "it depends" without naming what it depends on.
