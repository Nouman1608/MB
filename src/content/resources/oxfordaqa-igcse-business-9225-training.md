This guide covers section **3.4.4 Training** of OxfordAQA International GCSE Business (9225), part of Topic 4, Human resources. The specification asks you to explain the **benefits of training employees**, including **increased productivity, the ability to deal with changes in technology, increased motivation, staff retention, the production of high-quality goods and good customer service**; to understand the methods of training, **induction, on-the-job and off-the-job training**, and the benefits of each; and to **analyse the benefits and costs** of on-the-job and off-the-job training and **evaluate which is most appropriate** for a variety of businesses. All businesses and figures in this guide are invented.

For the whole topic, see the [Human Resources study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-human-resources/). Condensed notes are in the [revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-9225-training-revision-notes/) and questions are in the [practice set](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-9225-training-practice/).

## What is training?

**Training** is the process of teaching employees the skills and knowledge they need to do their job well, now or in the future. It is different from **recruitment**, which is about choosing people. Even a skilled recruit needs training in how this business works, uses its equipment and treats its customers.

## Why train employees? The benefits

| Benefit | How training helps |
|---|---|
| Increased productivity | Trained staff work faster and make fewer mistakes, so more output per worker |
| Dealing with changes in technology | Staff learn new machines, software and methods, so the business can adopt them quickly |
| Increased motivation | Training shows the business values its employees and gives them confidence and a chance to progress |
| Staff retention | Motivated, skilled staff are less likely to leave, which cuts recruitment costs |
| High-quality goods | Staff who understand the process make fewer faults, so less waste and fewer returns |
| Good customer service | Staff learn how to deal with customers, answer questions and solve problems |
| Safety | Staff learn safe methods, which reduces accidents (see also the legislation guide) |

Training also helps a business to be flexible, since staff with several skills can cover for absent colleagues.

### Costs and risks of training

Training is an investment, so it has costs.

- **Direct costs:** course fees, trainers, materials and equipment.
- **Lost output:** trainees are not doing their normal job while they train, and trainers also spend time away from their own work.
- **The risk of losing trained staff:** a trained employee may leave for a rival and take the skills with them.
- **Uncertain benefit:** the improvement may be less than hoped.

A business should compare the cost of training with the gain in productivity and quality.

## Methods of training

### Induction training

**Induction** is the introduction of new employees to the business. It usually takes place in the first days or weeks. It covers:

- a tour of the premises and meeting colleagues
- the business's rules, hours, pay and holidays
- health and safety, such as fire exits and emergency procedures
- an explanation of the job and who to ask for help
- the business's aims, products and values.

| Benefits | Drawbacks |
|---|---|
| New staff settle in more quickly and feel welcome | Takes time away from productive work |
| Reduces mistakes and accidents | Needs staff to plan and deliver it |
| Helps staff to stay, as many leave in the first weeks if they are not supported | Information overload if too much is covered at once |

### On-the-job training

In **on-the-job training**, employees learn while doing their normal job, usually by watching and working alongside an experienced colleague (sometimes called shadowing), or being coached by a supervisor.

| Benefits | Drawbacks |
|---|---|
| Cheaper: no course fees or travel | The trainer may not be a good teacher, or may pass on bad habits |
| Trainee produces something while learning | Trainer's own output falls, and the trainee works slowly at first |
| Training is specific to the business and its equipment | Mistakes can damage equipment or upset customers |
| Training can start immediately | Limited to the skills the business already has |

### Off-the-job training

In **off-the-job training**, employees learn away from their normal workplace, for example at a college, a training centre, or on a specialist course. It may take place in the business's own training room, if it is separate from normal work.

| Benefits | Drawbacks |
|---|---|
| Specialist trainers can teach new skills and wider knowledge | More expensive: fees and travel |
| Trainees can concentrate without work interruptions | Output is lost while staff are away |
| Staff may gain qualifications that motivate them and help the business's reputation | Skills may not match the business's own methods |
| Brings in new ideas from outside | Trained staff are more attractive to rivals, so may leave |

## Comparing on-the-job and off-the-job training

**Worked example 1: costs.** A bakery chain has 8 new bakers. Each baker normally produces $120 of output a day.

- **Off-the-job course:** a fee of $600 per baker, and each baker is away from work for 3 days.
- **On-the-job training:** each baker is trained for 5 days by an experienced baker earning $110 a day, and the trainee works at half speed in this period.

```
Off-the-job: fees 8 x $600 = $4,800
             lost output 3 days x 8 bakers x $120 = $2,880
             total = $4,800 + $2,880 = $7,680

On-the-job: trainer cost per baker = 5 x $110 = $550
            lost output per baker = 5 days x half of $120 = 5 x $60 = $300
            per baker = $550 + $300 = $850
            total for 8 = 8 x $850 = $6,800
```

On-the-job training is cheaper by $880 ($7,680 - $6,800). However, the off-the-job course may teach skills that the chain's experienced bakers do not have.

**Worked example 2: is training worth the cost?** Suppose that training raises each baker's productivity by 10%, and bakers work 230 days a year.

```
Extra output per baker per year = 10% x $120 x 230 = $2,760
Extra output for 8 bakers       = 8 x $2,760 = $22,080 a year
Payback of off-the-job course   = $7,680 / $22,080 x 12 months = about 4.2 months
Payback of on-the-job training  = $6,800 / $22,080 x 12 months = about 3.7 months
```

Both methods pay for themselves within five months on these figures. The result depends on whether the 10% improvement is achieved, and on trained staff staying with the business.

## Which method is most appropriate?

There is no single best method. Consider:

| Factor | Points to consider |
|---|---|
| The skill being taught | Practical skills suit on-the-job training. Technical, legal or safety knowledge may need specialist off-the-job teaching |
| Size of business | A small business may lack experienced staff to train others, or the money for courses |
| Number of employees | A large group can be trained together, and the cost per person falls |
| Budget and time | On-the-job training is usually cheaper and quicker to start |
| Risk | If mistakes are dangerous, as with a pilot or surgeon, training away from the real situation is safer |
| Need for qualifications | Some jobs need recognised certificates, which require external courses |

Many businesses use a mixture. A new shop assistant might have induction on day one, then on-the-job training on the till, with an off-the-job course on customer care.

| Business | Likely best method | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Fast-food restaurant | Induction, then on-the-job | Simple tasks learned quickly while working |
| Small building firm | On-the-job, plus off-the-job for safety certificates | Practical skills plus legal requirements |
| Bank introducing new software | Off-the-job course for all cashiers | Everyone needs the same technical knowledge |
| Airline | Off-the-job simulators, then supervised flying | Mistakes in real life are too dangerous |

## Common mistakes

- Confusing induction with all training. Induction is for new employees only.
- Saying that on-the-job training is "free". The trainer's time and the trainee's lower output have a cost.
- Stating that off-the-job training is always better because it uses experts.
- Listing benefits without linking them to productivity, quality or service.
- Forgetting the risk that trained staff leave for a rival.
- Giving a general answer where the question asks you to choose a method for a named business.

## Exam technique for this section

For **state** questions, name the type of training. For **explain** questions, give a benefit with a reason: "off-the-job training teaches staff to use new software, so the business can adopt new technology faster". For **calculate** questions, show fees, lost output and totals. For **analyse** questions, trace the effect from training to productivity, quality or retention, then to profit. For **evaluate** questions, compare the methods against the business's size, budget and the skill being taught, and give a reasoned recommendation.
