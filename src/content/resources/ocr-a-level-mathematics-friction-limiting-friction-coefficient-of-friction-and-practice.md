## Questions

**1.** The normal contact force on a block resting on a rough surface is 40 N. The coefficient of friction is 0.35. Find the greatest possible frictional force. **[2]**

**2.** A particle of mass 1.5 kg moves on a horizontal surface, with i and j perpendicular unit vectors in the surface. The applied force is (10i + 9j) N. The friction force has magnitude 5 N and acts in the direction of −(4i + 3j). Find the acceleration as a vector, and its magnitude. **[3]**

**3.** The contact force between a crate and rough ground has a normal component of 68 N and a frictional component of 21 N.

**(a)** Find the magnitude of the contact force. **[2]**
**(b)** The crate is on the point of slipping. Find the coefficient of friction. **[2]**

**4.** A sledge of mass 18 kg is on rough horizontal snow with coefficient of friction 0.2. A rope pulls it with tension 35 N at 35° above the horizontal. Show that the sledge does not move, and state the frictional force. **[5]**

**5.** A block of mass 9 kg is on rough horizontal ground with coefficient of friction 0.4. A force P acts on it at 20° below the horizontal, pushing it. The block is on the point of sliding.

**(a)** Find P. **[5]**
**(b)** Find the normal contact force. **[1]**

**6.** A block of mass 5 kg is on a rough plane inclined at 28° to the horizontal. The coefficient of friction is 0.6.

**(a)** Show that the block stays at rest, and find the friction force. **[4]**
**(b)** The plane is slowly tilted. Find the greatest inclination at which the block stays at rest. **[2]**

**7.** A block of mass 8 kg is on a rough plane inclined at 25° to the horizontal. The coefficient of friction is 0.3. A force P acts up a line of greatest slope.

**(a)** Show that the block would slide down if P were zero. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the least P for which the block stays at rest. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the greatest P for which the block stays at rest. **[3]**

**8.** A body of mass 12 kg is at rest on rough horizontal ground, with coefficient of friction 0.3. A horizontal force of 50 N acts for 4 s and is then removed. Find the total distance moved until the body stops. **[7]**

**9.** A body of mass 2 kg is sent down the line of greatest slope on a rough plane at 15° to the horizontal, starting at 5 m s⁻¹. The coefficient of friction is 0.35.

**(a)** Show that the body decelerates, and find the deceleration. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the distance travelled before it stops. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain whether it then remains at rest. **[2]**

**10.** A block of mass 6 kg is on a rough plane inclined at 20° to the horizontal, with coefficient of friction 0.2. A force of 45 N acts up a line of greatest slope, and the block starts from rest.

**(a)** Find the acceleration. **[4]**
**(b)** After 2 s the force is removed. Find the further distance the block moves up the plane. **[3]**
**(c)** Show that the block then slides back down. **[2]**

**11.** A block of mass 7 kg is pulled across rough horizontal ground by a force of 40 N at 30° above the horizontal. The block accelerates at 1.1 m s⁻².

**(a)** Find the coefficient of friction. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the greatest force at this angle for which the block stays in contact with the ground. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Greatest friction = μR = 0.35 × 40 [1] = **14 N** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* write μR explicitly; a bare 14 gives nothing to credit if it is wrong.

**2.** Friction = −(4i + 3j) N, since |4i + 3j| = 5. Resultant = (10 − 4)i + (9 − 3)j = (6i + 6j) N [1]. a = (6i + 6j) ÷ 1.5 = **(4i + 4j) m s⁻²** [1]. |a| = 4√2 = **5.66 m s⁻²** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* turning "magnitude 5 N in a given direction" into a vector by dividing by the length of the direction vector is the step to show clearly.

**3. (a)** S = √(68² + 21²) [1] = √5065 = **71.2 N** [1]
**(b)** Limiting: F = μR, so μ = 21 ÷ 68 [1] = **0.309** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* the contact force is the resultant of two perpendicular components, so adding 68 and 21 loses the method.

**4.** Vertical: R = 18 × 9.8 − 35 sin 35° = 176.4 − 20.08 = 156.3 N [1] [1]. Maximum friction = 0.2 × 156.3 = 31.3 N [1]. Horizontal pull = 35 cos 35° = 28.7 N, which is less than 31.3 N, so no motion [1]. Friction balances the pull: **F = 28.7 N** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* in a "show that" you must compare the two forces explicitly and conclude. Quoting 31.3 N as the friction is a common error.

**5. (a)** R = 88.2 + P sin 20° [1]. Limiting: P cos 20° = 0.4R [1]. P cos 20° = 35.28 + 0.4P sin 20° [1]. P(0.9397 − 0.1368) = 35.28 [1]. **P = 43.9 N** [1]
**(b)** R = 88.2 + 43.94 sin 20° = **103 N** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* the push adds to R. If your R is below the weight, the sign of the P sin 20° term is wrong.

**6. (a)** Along the plane the friction needed is 5 × 9.8 × sin 28° = 23.0 N [1]. R = 5 × 9.8 × cos 28° = 43.3 N [1]. Maximum friction = 0.6 × 43.3 = 26.0 N [1]. Since 23.0 < 26.0, the block stays at rest, with **F = 23.0 N** [1]
**(b)** Limiting: mg sin θ = μ mg cos θ [1], so tan θ = 0.6 and **θ = 31.0°** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* the friction is 23.0 N, not 26.0 N. State which number is the actual friction.

**7. (a)** R = 8 × 9.8 × cos 25° = 71.05 N, so μR = 21.3 N [1]. Weight component down the slope = 8 × 9.8 × sin 25° = 33.1 N, which exceeds 21.3 N, so it would slide [1]
**(b)** About to slide down, friction up the slope: P + μR = mg sin 25° [1]. P = 33.13 − 21.32 [1] = **11.8 N** [1]
**(c)** About to slide up, friction down the slope: P = mg sin 25° + μR [1] = 33.13 + 21.32 [1] = **54.4 N** [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* draw a separate diagram for each limiting case. The friction arrow reverses between (b) and (c).

**8.** R = 117.6 N, so F = 0.3 × 117.6 = 35.28 N [1]. 12a = 50 − 35.28 [1], a = 1.227 m s⁻². After 4 s, v = 4.907 m s⁻¹ [1]. s₁ = ½ × 1.227 × 16 = 9.81 m [1]. After removal, deceleration = 0.3 × 9.8 = 2.94 m s⁻² [1]. s₂ = 4.907² ÷ (2 × 2.94) = 4.09 m [1]. Total = **13.9 m** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* carry unrounded values from stage 1 into stage 2. Early rounding of v can shift the third significant figure.

**9. (a)** R = 2 × 9.8 × cos 15° = 18.93 N, F = 0.35R = 6.63 N [1]. Weight component down the slope = 2 × 9.8 × sin 15° = 5.07 N, so 2a = 5.07 − 6.63 [1]. a = −0.777, so it **decelerates at 0.777 m s⁻²** [1]
**(b)** 0 = 5² − 2(0.777)s [1], **s = 16.1 m** [1]
**(c)** tan 15° = 0.268 < 0.35 (or 5.07 N < 6.63 N) [1], so friction can balance the weight component and it **stays at rest** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* "explain" needs the comparison and a conclusion. Saying "friction is big enough" without figures is weaker.

**10. (a)** R = 6 × 9.8 × cos 20° = 55.25 N [1]. F = 0.2R = 11.05 N [1]. Weight component = 6 × 9.8 × sin 20° = 20.11 N. 6a = 45 − 20.11 − 11.05 [1]. **a = 2.31 m s⁻²** [1]
**(b)** v = 2 × 2.306 = 4.61 m s⁻¹ [1]. Deceleration = 9.8(sin 20° + 0.2 cos 20°) = 5.19 m s⁻² [1]. s = 4.613² ÷ (2 × 5.194) = **2.05 m** [1]
**(c)** tan 20° = 0.364 > 0.2 [1], so the weight component exceeds the maximum friction and it slides back [1] **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* moving up the slope, friction and the weight component both act down it. Using g sin 20° − 0.2g cos 20° in (b) is the classic slip.

**11. (a)** R = 7 × 9.8 − 40 sin 30° = 68.6 − 20 = 48.6 N [1]. Horizontal: 40 cos 30° − F = 7 × 1.1 [1]. F = 34.64 − 7.7 = 26.94 N [1]. μ = 26.94 ÷ 48.6 = **0.554** [1]
**(b)** Contact is lost when R = 0: P sin 30° = 68.6 [1], **P = 137 N** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* find R first, from the vertical forces including the rope's vertical component, before using F = μR.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using R = mg when a rope or push has a vertical component, or on a slope.
- Taking F = μR for a stationary body that is not close to slipping.
- Reversing the friction direction wrongly between the least and greatest force cases.
- Adding the normal and frictional components of the contact force.
- Not concluding in words after comparing forces in a "show that" or "explain".
- Rounding intermediate values and losing accuracy at the end.
- Using the up-slope deceleration formula for motion down the slope, or the reverse.
- Treating the stopped body as sliding back without comparing tan α with μ.
