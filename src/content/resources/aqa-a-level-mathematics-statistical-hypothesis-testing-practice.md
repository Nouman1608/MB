These questions cover **Section O: Statistical hypothesis testing** (O1 to O3) of the **AQA A-level
Mathematics (7357) specification**, version 1.3, for A-level exams from June 2018 onwards. Section O
is listed under Paper 3. A calculator is required in every 7357 paper, so every question here assumes
you have one with binomial, Normal and inverse Normal functions. Give probabilities to 3 significant
figures unless a question says otherwise.

## Questions

**1.** In the context of a hypothesis test:

**(a)** State what is meant by the critical region. **[1]**
**(b)** State what is meant by a p-value. **[1]**

**2.** A games designer states that a reward box contains a rare item with probability 0.08. A
player suspects that the probability is lower than this. Define a suitable parameter and write down
the hypotheses for a test of the player's suspicion. **[2]**

**3.** In a town, 40% of adults used to cycle to work. After a new car park opens, a random sample
of 30 adults finds that 7 cycle to work. Test at the 5% significance level whether the proportion
who cycle to work has decreased. **[5]**

**4.** A seed supplier states that 15% of its seeds fail to germinate. A gardener plants a random
sample of 20 seeds and suspects that the proportion failing is higher than stated.

**(a)** Using a 5% significance level, find the critical region for the number of seeds that fail
to germinate. **[3]**
**(b)** State the probability that the test incorrectly rejects the null hypothesis. **[1]**

**5.** A biased coin is thought to land heads with probability 0.3. It is thrown 50 times to test
whether this probability is correct, using a 5% significance level.

**(a)** Find the critical region for X, the number of heads. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the actual significance level of the test. **[1]**
**(c)** The coin lands heads 21 times. State the conclusion of the test in context. **[1]**

**6.** For a random sample of 15 cars, the product moment correlation coefficient between age and
resale value is r = −0.47. The critical value for a 1-tail test at the 5% level with a sample of 15
is 0.4409.

**(a)** Write down hypotheses to test for negative correlation in the population. **[1]**
**(b)** Carry out the test at the 5% level. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe what the value r = −0.47 shows about the scatter diagram of the data. **[1]**

**7.** For 40 randomly chosen days, the correlation coefficient between daily rainfall and the number
of umbrellas sold in a shop is r = 0.29. For a test of H₀: ρ = 0 against H₁: ρ ≠ 0, the p-value is
0.069.

**(a)** Carry out the test at the 5% significance level. **[2]**
**(b)** State, with a reason, whether the conclusion would be different at the 10% level. **[1]**

**8.** A phone battery gives a video playback time that is Normally distributed with mean 250 minutes
and standard deviation 12 minutes. After a software update, a random sample of 20 phones has a mean
playback time of 244.8 minutes. Assuming the standard deviation is unchanged, test at the 5% level
whether the mean playback time has decreased. **[6]**

**9.** The lengths of a species of lizard are Normally distributed with standard deviation 2.4 cm. A
field guide states the mean length is 18.5 cm. A random sample of 36 lizards from one island is
measured to test whether the mean length on the island is different from 18.5 cm.

**(a)** Find the critical region for the sample mean X̄ at the 5% significance level. **[4]**
**(b)** The sample mean is 19.4 cm. State, with a reason, the conclusion of the test in context.
**[2]**

**10.** A website records that 12% of visitors click on a particular advert. After a redesign, 9 out
of a random sample of 40 visitors click on the advert.

**(a)** Test at the 5% significance level whether the proportion who click has increased. **[5]**
**(b)** Find the critical region for this test at the 1% significance level. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain what the 1% significance level means in the context of this test. **[1]**

**11.** A machine makes metal pins whose lengths are Normally distributed with mean 4.2 cm and
standard deviation 0.5 cm. An engineer suspects the mean length has increased. A random sample of 25
pins has mean length 4.37 cm.

**(a)** Assuming the standard deviation is unchanged, test the engineer's suspicion at the 2.5%
significance level. **[5]**
**(b)** The same sample mean, 4.37 cm, is obtained from a larger random sample of size n. Find the
smallest value of n for which the result would be significant at the 2.5% level. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The set of values of the test statistic that lead to the null hypothesis being rejected
[1].
**(b)** The probability, assuming H₀ is true, of obtaining a result at least as extreme as the one
observed [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* the p-value definition needs "assuming H₀ is true" and "at least as extreme".

**2.** Let p be the probability that a reward box contains the rare item [1].
**H₀: p = 0.08, H₁: p < 0.08** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* an undefined letter in the hypotheses loses the definition mark.

**3.** Let p = proportion of all adults in the town who cycle to work. H₀: p = 0.4, H₁: p < 0.4 [1].
Under H₀, X ~ B(30, 0.4) [1].
p-value = P(X ≤ 7) [1] = **0.0435** [1].
0.0435 < 0.05, so reject H₀. There is sufficient evidence at the 5% level to suggest that the
proportion of adults who cycle to work has decreased [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* the final mark needs a comparison with 0.05 and a non-definite conclusion in
context.

**4. (a)** Under H₀, X ~ B(20, 0.15), H₁: p > 0.15 [1].
P(X ≥ 7) = 0.0219 and P(X ≥ 6) = 0.0673 [1].
Critical region **X ≥ 7** [1].
**(b)** P(X ≥ 7) = **0.0219** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* quote the probabilities either side of the boundary to justify the critical
value.

**5. (a)** Under H₀, X ~ B(50, 0.3); 2-tail, so 2.5% in each tail [1].
Lower: P(X ≤ 8) = 0.0183, P(X ≤ 9) = 0.0402, so X ≤ 8 [1].
Upper: P(X ≥ 23) = 0.0123, P(X ≥ 22) = 0.0251, so X ≥ 23 [1].
Critical region **X ≤ 8 or X ≥ 23** [1].
**(b)** 0.0183 + 0.0123 = **0.0305** (3 s.f.) [1].
**(c)** 21 is not in the critical region, so do not reject H₀: there is insufficient evidence to
suggest the probability of heads is not 0.3 [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* P(X ≥ 22) = 0.0251 is just above 0.025, so X ≥ 22 is wrong; comparing each tail
with 0.05 instead of 0.025 gives the wrong region.

**6. (a)** H₀: ρ = 0, H₁: ρ < 0 [1].
**(b)** Compare r = −0.47 with −0.4409: −0.47 < −0.4409 [1].
Reject H₀: there is sufficient evidence at the 5% level of negative correlation between age and
resale value of cars in the population [1].
**(c)** The points lie moderately close to a straight line sloping downwards [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* hypotheses must use ρ, not r; writing H₀: r = 0 loses the mark even if the
comparison is correct.

**7. (a)** 0.069 > 0.05 [1], so do not reject H₀: there is insufficient evidence at the 5% level of
correlation between rainfall and umbrella sales [1].
**(b)** Yes: 0.069 < 0.10, so H₀ would be rejected at 10% [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* a conclusion such as "rainfall causes umbrella sales" is not credited; the test
only gives evidence about correlation.

**8.** Let μ = mean playback time after the update. H₀: μ = 250, H₁: μ < 250 [1].
Under H₀, X̄ ~ N(250, 12²/20) [1].
z = (244.8 − 250)/(12/√20) [1] = **−1.94** (3 s.f.), or P(X̄ ≤ 244.8) = **0.0263** [1].
−1.94 < −1.645 (or 0.0263 < 0.05) [1].
Reject H₀: there is sufficient evidence at the 5% level to suggest the mean playback time has
decreased [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* using 12 instead of 12/√20 gives z = −0.433 and the wrong conclusion; the
distribution mark depends on the variance σ²/n.

**9. (a)** H₀: μ = 18.5, H₁: μ ≠ 18.5; under H₀, X̄ ~ N(18.5, 2.4²/36), so σ/√n = 0.4 [1].
Critical z values ±1.960 [1].
18.5 ± 1.960 × 0.4 = 17.72 and 19.28 (2 d.p.) [1].
Critical region **X̄ < 17.72 or X̄ > 19.28** [1].
**(b)** 19.4 > 19.28, so 19.4 is in the critical region [1].
Reject H₀: there is sufficient evidence at the 5% level to suggest the mean length of lizards on
the island is different from 18.5 cm [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* in a 2-tail test the z value is 1.960, not 1.645; using 1.645 gives a region that
is too wide and loses the accuracy mark.

**10. (a)** Let p = proportion of all visitors who click after the redesign. H₀: p = 0.12,
H₁: p > 0.12 [1].
Under H₀, X ~ B(40, 0.12) [1].
p-value = P(X ≥ 9) = 1 − P(X ≤ 8) [1] = **0.0443** [1].
0.0443 < 0.05, so reject H₀: there is sufficient evidence at the 5% level to suggest the proportion
who click has increased [1].
**(b)** P(X ≥ 11) = 0.00615 and P(X ≥ 10) = 0.0175 [1], so the critical region is **X ≥ 11** [1].
**(c)** The probability of concluding that the proportion has increased when it is still 0.12 is
at most 1% [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* using 1 − P(X ≤ 9) for P(X ≥ 9) gives 0.0175 and loses the accuracy mark.

**11. (a)** Let μ = mean length of pins. H₀: μ = 4.2, H₁: μ > 4.2 [1].
Under H₀, X̄ ~ N(4.2, 0.5²/25) [1].
z = (4.37 − 4.2)/0.1 = **1.7** [1].
Critical value z = 1.960 at 2.5%; 1.7 < 1.960 [1].
Do not reject H₀: there is insufficient evidence at the 2.5% level to suggest the mean length has
increased [1].
**(b)** Need (4.37 − 4.2)/(0.5/√n) > 1.960 [1].
√n > 1.960 × 0.5/0.17 = 5.765, so n > 33.2 [1].
Smallest n = **34** [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* the final answer must be an integer that satisfies the inequality; rounding
33.2 down to 33 loses the accuracy mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Hypotheses written in terms of r, x̄ or a sample proportion instead of ρ, μ or p.
- The parameter is never defined, so the hypotheses have no context.
- P(X = k) given as the p-value instead of a cumulative tail probability.
- P(X ≥ k) found as 1 − P(X ≤ k).
- In a 2-tail test, each tail compared with the full significance level.
- Critical regions stated with no probabilities either side of the boundary.
- σ used in place of σ/√n for the sample mean.
- Conclusions written as certainties, or with no context.
