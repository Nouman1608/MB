## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** State the role of DNA and the role of RNA in living cells. **[2]**

**2.** Name the three components of an RNA nucleotide. **[2]**

**3.** Give two structural differences between a DNA molecule and an RNA molecule. **[2]**

**4.** One DNA strand has the sequence C A T G G T A C.

**(a)** Write the sequence of the complementary DNA strand. **[1]**

**(b)** Write the RNA sequence that is complementary to the original strand. **[1]**

**5.** Ribosomes are sometimes described as "protein structures". Explain why this description is incomplete. **[1]**

**6.** Explain why the hydrogen bonds between bases, rather than the phosphodiester bonds, are broken during DNA replication. **[2]**

**7.** A student writes: "DNA polymerase forms hydrogen bonds between the new nucleotides and the template strand." Correct the student's statement. **[2]**

**8.** Calculate how many different base sequences are possible for a DNA strand 6 bases long. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**9.** Describe the process of semi-conservative DNA replication. **[5]**

**10.** A drug binds to DNA helicase and stops it working.

**(a)** Suggest and explain the effect of the drug on DNA replication. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest why such a drug might slow the growth of a tumour. **[1]**

**11.** Bacteria were grown for many generations in a medium containing only light nitrogen (¹⁴N). They were then moved to a medium containing only heavy nitrogen (¹⁵N). Samples were taken after one and two generations, and the DNA was separated by density.

**(a)** Predict the band or bands after one generation if replication is semi-conservative. **[1]**

**(b)** After one generation, a single intermediate band was seen. Explain which model of replication this rules out. **[2]**

**(c)** Predict the bands after two generations if replication is semi-conservative, and the proportion of DNA in each. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain how the result after two generations could distinguish the semi-conservative model from the dispersive model. **[2]**

**12.** You are told that a C–G base pair is held by three hydrogen bonds and an A–T pair by two. Two DNA fragments of the same length are heated slowly. Fragment P is 30% guanine and fragment Q is 15% guanine.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of C–G base pairs in each fragment. **[2]**

**(b)** Predict which fragment separates into single strands at the higher temperature. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**13.** In the early twentieth century, many scientists thought proteins, not DNA, carried genetic information. Explain why, and explain how the structure of DNA can in fact store large amounts of information. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** DNA holds genetic information [1]; RNA transfers genetic information from DNA to the ribosomes [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Ribose and a phosphate group [1]; a nitrogen-containing organic base, one of adenine, cytosine, guanine or uracil [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Any two: DNA has two polynucleotide chains, RNA one [1]; DNA is very long, RNA relatively short [1]; DNA contains deoxyribose, RNA ribose; DNA contains thymine, RNA uracil. **[2]**

**4. (a)** G T A C C A T G [1].

**(b)** G U A C C A U G [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Check that no T appears in an RNA answer. It is the most common slip.

**5.** Ribosomes are formed from RNA as well as proteins [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Hydrogen bonds are weak, so the two strands can be separated easily by an enzyme [1], while the strong phosphodiester bonds keep each strand's backbone and base sequence intact so it can act as a template [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Hydrogen bonds form by complementary base pairing between new nucleotides and the template, without DNA polymerase [1]; DNA polymerase catalyses condensation reactions joining adjacent nucleotides, forming phosphodiester bonds [1]. **[2]**

**8.** 4⁶ = **4,096** [1]. **[1]**

**9.** DNA helicase unwinds the double helix [1] and breaks the hydrogen bonds between complementary bases, separating the strands [1]. Free DNA nucleotides are attracted to exposed bases on each template strand and pair with them by complementary base pairing (A with T, C with G) [1]. DNA polymerase joins adjacent nucleotides in condensation reactions, forming phosphodiester bonds [1]. Each new molecule contains one original strand and one new strand, so replication is semi-conservative [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Name each enzyme with its exact job. Answers that give DNA polymerase the job of separating strands lose credit.

**10. (a)** The double helix is not unwound and hydrogen bonds between strands are not broken [1], so no template strands are exposed and new nucleotides cannot pair; DNA is not replicated [1].

**(b)** Cells must replicate their DNA before they divide, so tumour cells could not divide as often [1]. **[3]**

**11. (a)** One intermediate band [1].

**(b)** The conservative model predicts two bands: one light (the original molecules) and one heavy (entirely new molecules) [1]. A single intermediate band means every molecule contains both light and heavy DNA, so the conservative model is ruled out [1].

**(c)** Two bands: intermediate and heavy [1], half of the DNA in each [1].

**(d)** The dispersive model predicts one band, between intermediate and heavy, because every strand would be a mixture [1]; seeing two separate bands rules out dispersive replication and fits the semi-conservative model [1]. **[7]**

*Examiner insight:* Explain each prediction from the model, then say which result rules which model out. That is what "evaluate the work of scientists" asks for.

**12. (a)** P: G = C = 30%, so C–G pairs are 30% + 30% = **60%** of bases, which is 60% of base pairs [1]. Q: 15% + 15% = **30%** of base pairs [1].

**(b)** Fragment P [1], because it has a higher proportion of C–G pairs, each held by three hydrogen bonds, so more hydrogen bonds in total must be broken to separate the strands, needing more energy [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* In (a), each C–G pair contains one G and one C, so the percentage of bases that are G or C equals the percentage of pairs that are C–G.

**13.** DNA seemed too chemically simple [1]: a repeating sugar-phosphate backbone with only four kinds of base, whereas proteins are made from twenty different amino acids and seemed more variable [1]. Information is carried by the sequence of bases [1]; the number of possible sequences is 4ⁿ for n bases, and DNA molecules are very long, so the number of different sequences is enormous [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Thymine in an RNA sequence.
- Giving DNA polymerase the role of breaking or forming hydrogen bonds.
- Forgetting that ribosomes contain RNA.
- Not explaining which model each result rules out.
- Confusing the percentage of G with the percentage of C–G pairs.
- Saying DNA is "simple, so it cannot carry information" without the idea of sequence.

**What should a description of DNA replication include?**
Unwinding and breaking of hydrogen bonds by helicase, complementary pairing of free nucleotides, condensation by DNA polymerase, and the semi-conservative result.
