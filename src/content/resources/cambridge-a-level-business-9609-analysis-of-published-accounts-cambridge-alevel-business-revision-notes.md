These notes condense **section 10.2 Analysis of published accounts** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Business 9609 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. The [analysis of published accounts study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-business-9609-analysis-of-published-accounts-cambridge-alevel-business/) interprets one company's ratios over two years; the [analysis of published accounts practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-business-9609-analysis-of-published-accounts-cambridge-alevel-business-practice/) test the skills. The statements behind the ratios are covered in the [financial statements revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-business-9609-financial-statements-cambridge-alevel-business-revision-notes/).

## Formulas and units (as given in the syllabus)

| Ratio | Formula | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Current ratio | Current assets ÷ current liabilities | Ratio, e.g. 1.5 : 1 |
| Acid test ratio | (Current assets − inventory) ÷ current liabilities | Ratio |
| Gross profit margin | Gross profit ÷ revenue × 100 | % |
| Profit margin | Profit from operations ÷ revenue × 100 | % |
| ROCE | Profit from operations ÷ capital employed × 100 | % |
| Rate of inventory turnover | Cost of sales ÷ average inventory | Times |
| Trade receivables turnover | Trade receivables ÷ credit sales × 365 | Days |
| Trade payables turnover | Trade payables ÷ credit purchases × 365 | Days |
| Gearing | Non-current liabilities ÷ capital employed × 100 | % |
| Dividend yield | Dividend per share ÷ market price per share × 100 | % |
| Dividend cover | Profit for the year ÷ annual dividend | Times |
| P/E ratio | Market price per share ÷ earnings per share | No unit |

**Capital employed** = issued shares + reserves + non-current liabilities.
**Average inventory** = (opening + closing) ÷ 2.

## Meaning and importance

| Group | Meaning | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Liquidity | Ability to pay short-term debts | Profitable firms can fail through lack of cash |
| Profitability | Profit earned from sales and from capital | Owners' return; lenders' security; managers' performance |
| Financial efficiency | How quickly inventory, receivables and payables turn over | Cash tied up cannot be used elsewhere |
| Gearing | Reliance on long-term borrowing | Interest is a fixed commitment; risk rises with gearing |
| Investment | Return to shareholders | Investors decide whether to buy, hold or sell |

## Interpretation: the five steps

1. Calculate with the correct unit.
2. Compare with last year, a competitor or a target.
3. Explain the cause from case evidence.
4. State the consequence for the business or a stakeholder.
5. Judge or recommend.

## Reading the signals

| Change | Possible causes | Possible consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Acid test falls, current ratio steady | Cash replaced by inventory or receivables | Harder to pay suppliers on time |
| Gross margin falls | Price cuts; dearer supplies | Less profit per sale |
| Profit margin falls more than gross margin | Expenses rising faster than sales | Overheads need control |
| ROCE falls | Lower profit, or more capital not yet earning | Investment returns below cost of finance |
| Inventory turnover slows | Over-stocking; slow sellers | Cash tied up; risk of obsolete stock |
| Receivables days rise | Longer credit offered; poor credit control | Cash shortage; bad debts |
| Payables days rise | Delaying suppliers | Helps cash; may damage goodwill |
| Gearing rises | New loans | Higher interest; more risk if profits fall |
| Dividend cover falls | Dividend up or profit down | Less profit retained for growth |
| P/E falls | Investors less optimistic | Harder to raise finance by selling shares |

**Look behind an unchanged ratio.** A steady current ratio can hide a fall in cash.

**ROCE has two drivers.** Profit (numerator) and capital employed (denominator). Name which moved.

## Methods of improving, with trade-offs

| Ratio group | Methods | Possible side-effect |
|---|---|---|
| Liquidity | Sell surplus stock; chase receivables; sale and leaseback; long-term loan to replace overdraft | Lower margins; customer goodwill; higher gearing |
| Profitability | Raise prices; cheaper suppliers; cut expenses; sell unused assets | Lower volume; quality risk; less capacity |
| Efficiency | Just-in-time ordering; tighter credit control; settlement discounts; longer supplier terms | Stock-outs; lost customers; lower margins; supplier goodwill |
| Gearing | Issue shares; retain profit; repay loans | EPS diluted; lower dividends; less cash |
| Investment | Raise profit; raise dividend; build confidence | Less retained for growth; weaker cash |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Liquidity | Ability to meet short-term debts as they fall due |
| Capital employed | Issued shares + reserves + non-current liabilities |
| Highly geared | Large share of capital employed from long-term borrowing |
| Dividend cover | Times profit for the year covers the dividend |
| Earnings per share | Profit for the year ÷ number of shares |
| P/E ratio | Share price as a multiple of earnings per share |

## Exam traps

- Revenue instead of cost of sales in inventory turnover.
- Closing inventory instead of average inventory.
- Current ratio written as a percentage; P/E given a unit.
- Total revenue instead of credit sales when they differ.
- A ratio quoted with no comparison or explanation.
- An improvement method with no side-effect considered.

## Self-test

1. What does the acid test exclude, and why?
2. Current assets 900, inventory 360, current liabilities 600. Calculate the acid test.
3. Give the formula for capital employed.
4. Profit from operations 450; capital employed 3,000. Calculate ROCE.
5. Cost of sales 3,600; opening inventory 500; closing inventory 700. Calculate inventory turnover.
6. Trade receivables 300; credit sales 2,400. Calculate receivables days.
7. Non-current liabilities 1,500; capital employed 4,000. Calculate gearing.
8. Dividend per share 12 cents; share price $3.00. Calculate dividend yield.
9. Profit for the year 800; dividends 200. Calculate dividend cover.
10. ROCE fell although profit from operations rose. Give a possible reason.
11. Give one method of improving gearing and its side-effect.
12. Why might a rise in payables days be a concern?

### Answers

1. Inventory, because it is the hardest current asset to turn quickly into cash.
2. (900 − 360) ÷ 600 = **0.9 : 1**.
3. Issued shares + reserves + non-current liabilities.
4. 450 ÷ 3,000 × 100 = **15%**.
5. Average inventory 600; 3,600 ÷ 600 = **6 times**.
6. 300 ÷ 2,400 × 365 = **45.6 days**.
7. 1,500 ÷ 4,000 × 100 = **37.5%**.
8. 0.12 ÷ 3.00 × 100 = **4%**.
9. 800 ÷ 200 = **4 times**.
10. Capital employed rose by proportionally more, for example through new borrowing for an investment not yet earning.
11. Issue shares to repay loans; EPS is diluted and owners' control may be reduced.
12. Suppliers may lose goodwill, refuse credit or withdraw settlement discounts.
