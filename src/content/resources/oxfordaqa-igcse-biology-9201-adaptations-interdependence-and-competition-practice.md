These questions cover section **3.3.2 Adaptations, interdependence and competition** of the OxfordAQA International
GCSE Biology (9201) specification, Version 5.2, for exams in May/June 2018 onwards. The qualification is untiered. All
data are invented for practice. A calculator is useful for questions 5 and 7. Questions 10 and 11 test content that the
specification marks as Biology-only.

## Section A

**1.**

**(a)** Give **two** things that plants compete for. **[2]**
**(b)** Give **two** things that animals compete for. **[2]**

**2.**

**(a)** State what is meant by an adaptation. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the **three** kinds of adaptation in the specification. **[3]**

**3.** Classify each of these adaptations as structural, behavioural or functional. **[4]**

**(a)** Penguins huddling together in cold weather.
**(b)** A streamlined body shape in a fish.
**(c)** A desert animal producing very concentrated urine.
**(d)** Birds migrating to warmer places in winter.

**4.** State what is meant by an extremophile and give **one** example of an extreme condition. **[2]**

## Section B

**5.** A student counted the chicks in five nests in each of two reed beds. Reed bed X has few nesting pairs. Reed bed Y has many.

| Nest | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reed bed X (chicks) | 6 | 5 | 7 | 6 | 6 |
| Reed bed Y (chicks) | 3 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 3 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean number of chicks per nest in each reed bed. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage reduction in the mean number of chicks per nest in Y compared with X. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest an explanation for the difference. **[2]**

**6.** A cactus has a thick stem that stores water, sharp spines instead of leaves, and a wide network of shallow roots. Suggest how each feature helps the cactus to survive in a desert. **[3]**

**7.** A microorganism that lives in very salty water was grown in different salt concentrations. The table shows how quickly it divided.

| Salt concentration (%) | 1 | 5 | 15 | 25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Divisions per hour | 0.2 | 0.6 | 1.8 | 1.4 |

**(a)** State the salt concentration at which the microorganism divided fastest. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times faster it divided at 15 % than at 1 %. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage fall in division rate between 15 % and 25 %. Give your answer to the nearest whole number. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why this microorganism is described as an extremophile. **[1]**

**8.** Describe **two** ways in which organisms in a community depend on one another. **[2]**

**9.** Small tree seedlings growing in the shade of a large tree grow very slowly. Explain why. **[3]**

**10.** (Biology only) A tapeworm lives inside the intestine of a mammal. It has hooks and suckers at its head end and a long, flat body. It has no digestive system of its own. Explain how these features adapt the tapeworm to its way of life. **[3]**

**11.** (Biology only) A flea lives amongst the hair of a mammal. Its body is small and flattened from side to side, and it has strong legs. Suggest how these features help the flea. **[2]**

**12.** The fennec fox lives in a desert. It has very large ears, pale sandy fur, thick fur on the soles of its feet, and it hunts at night while spending the day in a burrow. Explain how **three** of these features adapt it to desert life. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Any two of the following, 1 mark each [2]: light; space; water; nutrients (from the soil)
**(b)** Any two of the following, 1 mark each [2]: food; mates; territory

**2. (a)** A feature that enables an organism to survive in the conditions in which it normally lives [1]
**(b)** Structural [1]; behavioural [1]; functional [1]

**3. (a)** Behavioural [1]
**(b)** Structural [1]
**(c)** Functional [1]
**(d)** Behavioural [1]

**4.** An organism that lives in a very extreme environment [1]; high levels of salt (or high temperatures, or high pressures) [1]

**5. (a)** X: (6 + 5 + 7 + 6 + 6) ÷ 5 = 30 ÷ 5 = **6.0** [1]; Y: (3 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 3) ÷ 5 = 15 ÷ 5 = **3.0** [1]
**(b)** (6.0 − 3.0) ÷ 6.0 × 100 [1] = **50%** [1]
**(c)** In reed bed Y there are more nesting pairs, so there is more competition [1]; for food (or territory), so fewer chicks survive in each nest [1]

**6.** Any three of the following, 1 mark each [3]: the thick stem stores water, so the plant can survive long dry periods; spines have a small surface area compared with leaves, so less water is lost (they also protect against animals); the wide, shallow roots absorb water quickly over a large area when it rains

**7. (a)** 15 % [1]
**(b)** 1.8 ÷ 0.2 = **9 times** [1]
**(c)** (1.8 − 1.4) ÷ 1.8 × 100 [1] = 22.2, so **22%** [1]
**(d)** It lives in (and grows best in) very salty water, which is an extreme environment [1]

**8.** Any two of the following, 1 mark each [2]: animals feed on plants or other animals; plants provide shelter or habitats for animals; animals pollinate flowers or spread seeds; decomposers return nutrients to the soil for plants

**9.** The leaves of the large tree absorb most of the light, so there is less light for photosynthesis [1]; the large tree's roots also take most of the water and mineral ions from the soil [1]; so the seedlings compete unsuccessfully for light, water and nutrients and cannot make enough food for growth [1]

**10.** Hooks and suckers attach the tapeworm to the wall of the intestine so it is not carried away [1]; the long, flat body gives a large surface area for absorbing digested food [1]; as the host has already digested the food, the tapeworm does not need a digestive system and absorbs soluble food directly [1]

**11.** The small, flattened body allows the flea to move easily between the hairs of the host [1]; the strong legs allow it to cling to the host or to jump onto a new host [1]

**12.** Marking points, 1 mark each for the feature and the reason, for three features [6]:
- very large ears: large surface area, so heat can be lost to keep cool
- pale sandy fur: camouflage against sand; reflects sunlight
- thick fur on the soles of the feet: insulates the feet from hot sand (or helps grip loose sand)
- hunting at night: avoids the heat of the day, so less water is lost
- day in a burrow: stays out of the sun and in a cooler place, reducing water loss

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a feature without linking it to the environment.
- Writing that organisms "choose" to adapt.
- Forgetting to name mates and territory when animals compete.
- Confusing structural with functional adaptations.
- Missing the word "concentrated" in a mark about water saving in desert animals.
- Dividing by the wrong mean in a percentage reduction (divide by the starting value).
