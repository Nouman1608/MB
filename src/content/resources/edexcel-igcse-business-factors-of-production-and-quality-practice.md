Revise first with the [study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-factors-of-production-and-quality/) and [revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-factors-of-production-and-quality-revision-notes/) if needed. Every business is invented and amounts are in dollars. Calculators may be used and workings should be shown. Round percentages to 1 decimal place where needed.

## Questions

**1.** Define the term capital-intensive. **[2]**

**2.** Garrick Gardens is a garden-design business. Give two examples of capital it might use. **[2]**

**3.** Farida Garments has yearly costs of wages 45,000, materials 30,000, running costs of machinery 15,000 and rent 10,000.

**(a)** Calculate total costs. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate wages as a percentage of total costs. **[2]**
**(c)** State, using your answers, whether Farida Garments is better described as labour-intensive or capital-intensive. **[1]**

**4.** Explain one way in which a business that closes its high-street shop and sells only online changes its use of land. **[3]**

**5.** Define the term quality control. **[2]**

**6.** Penwith Pies bakes batches of 750 pies. 6% of each batch is defective and each defective pie costs 14 to scrap.

**(a)** Calculate the number of defective pies in a batch. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the cost of defective pies per batch. **[1]**
**(c)** After staff training the defect rate falls to 2%. Calculate the saving per batch. **[2]**

**7.** Hallam Joinery has 9 joiners, each paid 30,000 a year. It buys a cutting machine for 180,000 that will last 5 years, with maintenance of 6,000 a year. After it is installed, the business needs only 5 joiners. Analyse how buying the machine changes the relationship between capital and labour in this business. **[6]**

**8.** Valmont Chairs expects to make 18,000 chairs a year. It has a supermarket contract for two years, after which demand is uncertain. Option A is to hire more workers: yearly costs of 126,000. Option B is to buy an upholstery machine: yearly costs of 122,400, of which the machine accounts for 62,400 (depreciation, finance and maintenance). Justify which option Valmont Chairs should choose. **[8]**

**9.** Seaview Cafe had 30 complaints from 1,250 customers last year. After introducing TQM it had 14 complaints from 1,400 customers this year. Its set lunch costs 9.90 and a rival's costs 9.00.

**(a)** Calculate the complaint rate this year as a percentage of customers. **[2]**
**(b)** Analyse how the higher quality of service may give Seaview Cafe a competitive advantage. **[6]**

**10.** Outline two ways in which total quality management (TQM) differs from quality control. **[4]**

**11.** Tolliver Electronics employs 40 people and makes 5,000 units a month. 5% are defective and each defective unit costs 12 to rework. The owner is considering TQM, which would cost 21,600 in staff training and is expected to cut defects to 2%. Evaluate whether Tolliver Electronics should introduce TQM. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** An activity that uses a high proportion of capital (equipment, machinery) [1] compared with labour, so capital is the main cost [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition that names both capital and labour and compares them is stronger than one that says only that a firm is large or uses machines.

**2.** For example a lawnmower [1] and a van [1]; hedge trimmers, design software or a laptop are other equipment examples. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Give physical or technological equipment; money, staff and the owner are not capital in this sense.

**3. (a)** 45,000 + 30,000 + 15,000 + 10,000 = **100,000** [1].
**(b)** 45,000 ÷ 100,000 × 100 [1] = **45%** [1].
**(c)** **Labour-intensive**, because wages (45%) are far larger than machinery running costs (15%) [1].
*Examiner insight:* Set out the formula before substituting. A correct final answer is easier to credit when the working is visible.

**4.** Less land (shop space) is needed [1]. The business can use a cheaper warehouse or smaller premises in a lower-rent place [1], which lowers its fixed costs and frees money for website and delivery costs [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* For explain questions, state the point, then add two expansion points that follow on from each other.

**5.** Checking products or services against a set standard [1] and removing or correcting those that fail, usually by inspection [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention both the check and the action taken on faulty items.

**6. (a)** 750 × 6 ÷ 100 [1] = **45 pies** [1].
**(b)** 45 × 14 = **630** [1].
**(c)** 2% of 750 = 15 pies, 15 × 14 = 210 [1]; saving 630 − 210 = **420 per batch** [1].
*Examiner insight:* A multi-part calculation is easier to follow, and to credit, if each part's result is labelled and carried into the next.

**7.** Wages fall from 270,000 (9 × 30,000) to 150,000 (5 × 30,000), a drop of 120,000 [1]. Machine costs are 36,000 depreciation (180,000 ÷ 5) plus 6,000 maintenance, 42,000 a year [1]. The net saving is 78,000 a year [1]. Capital has replaced some labour, so the business becomes more capital-intensive [1]. The machine's costs are fixed, so they must be paid even if orders fall, which adds risk for the owner [1]. Four joiners lose jobs or need retraining, and the remaining five may need new skills to run the machine [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* An analysis answer that stays with the numbers given and then follows each effect to its consequence for this business is stronger than one with general statements about machines.

**8.** Cost per chair: A = 126,000 ÷ 18,000 = 7.00 [1]; B = 122,400 ÷ 18,000 = 6.80 [1]. B is cheaper by 3,600 a year [1]. B has 62,400 of machine costs that do not change with output [1], so if demand falls after the contract ends, Valmont still pays them [1]. Option A is more flexible, because workers can be laid off or moved more easily [1]. Recommendation: Option B [1], because the saving of 0.20 a chair is secured for the two-year contract and the machine's cost is spread over a large guaranteed output; it should plan for the risk after the contract [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A justification must pick one option and use the figures given. Quoting the contract details shows you have used the information in the stem.

**9. (a)** 14 ÷ 1,400 × 100 [1] = **1.0%** [1].
**(b)** Complaint rate fell from 2.4% (30 ÷ 1,250) to 1.0% [1], so customers are happier with service [1] and more likely to return and recommend the cafe [1]. Customers may be willing to pay more for good service: the cafe's lunch is 0.90 above its rival, 10% higher [1]. The number of customers rose from 1,250 to 1,400, which suggests quality is helping win custom [1]. Conclusion: better service gives it a way to stand out from rivals, so long as quality is maintained [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the data in the stem, such as the lower complaint rate and higher customer numbers, to build each stage of the analysis.

**10.** First difference: quality control is carried out by inspectors or a quality department, but in TQM everyone is responsible for quality [1]; this makes quality part of every job and not just a final check [1]. Second difference: quality control finds faults, often at the end, whereas TQM aims to prevent faults throughout the process [1]; this reduces waste of materials and labour that detection alone cannot [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* An outline needs two linked points for each difference: the contrast, then its effect on the business.

**11.** Current defect cost: 5,000 × 5% = 250 units × 12 = 3,000 a month [1]. With TQM: 5,000 × 2% = 100 units × 12 = 1,200 [1]. Saving 1,800 a month [1]. Payback 21,600 ÷ 1,800 = 12 months [1]. Benefits beyond money: fewer faulty products reach customers, giving a better reputation and fewer returns [1]. Risks: the 2% target may not be reached, training takes staff time and some staff may resist change, so the saving could be lower [1]. Once payback is complete, the saving continues, and quality could keep improving [1]. Judgement: yes, introduce TQM [1], since the cost is recovered in a year, the gains continue and a small business of 40 staff can train its team; the decision depends on staff commitment and on the target being met [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation reaches a supported conclusion. Weigh the payback figure against the risks and say what the decision depends on.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing capital means money rather than equipment and technology.
- Calling a firm capital-intensive without comparing capital and labour costs.
- Giving a percentage without showing the division.
- Answering "explain" with one point and no expansion.
- Treating quality control and TQM as if they were the same.
- Recommending an option without quoting figures from the stem.
- Ignoring the costs and risks of change when evaluating.
- Finishing an evaluation without a clear judgement.
