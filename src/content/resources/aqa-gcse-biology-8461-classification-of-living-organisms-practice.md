specification, for first teaching from 2016. Every question suits both tiers. The DNA data in question 12 are
invented for practice.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** Write the seven levels of the Linnaean system in order, starting with the largest group. **[2]**

**2.** The brown bear is *Ursus arctos* and the polar bear is *Ursus maritimus*.

**(a)** Give the genus of the polar bear. **[1]**
**(b)** State what the shared name tells you about the two species. **[1]**
**(c)** Give **one** rule for writing a binomial name. **[1]**

**3.**

**(a)** Name the three domains in Woese's system. **[1]**
**(b)** State which domain a mushroom belongs to. **[1]**

**4.** State what an evolutionary tree shows, and name the **two** types of data used to build one. **[3]**

**5.** In Britain, "robin" refers to one bird species. In North America, "robin" refers to a different species
in a different genus. Explain why scientists use binomial names rather than common names. **[2]**

**6.** Describe the organisms placed in the domain Archaea. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** The table shows part of the classification of five invertebrates, A to E.

| Level | A | B | C | D | E |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phylum | Arthropoda | Arthropoda | Arthropoda | Arthropoda | Mollusca |
| Class | Insecta | Insecta | Insecta | Arachnida | Gastropoda |
| Order | Coleoptera | Coleoptera | Lepidoptera | Araneae | (not shown) |
| Family | Carabidae | Carabidae | (not shown) | (not shown) | (not shown) |

A is in the genus *Carabus*; B is in the genus *Pterostichus*.

**(a)** Use the table to identify the two organisms most closely related. Explain your answer. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify the organism least closely related to A. **[1]**
**(c)** State whether A and B are the same species, and give a reason. **[1]**
**(d)** Identify the lowest level that C and D share. **[1]**

**8.** The evolutionary tree shows the relationships between five species. Each + is a branch point. Species N
is extinct.

```
          +------------------- species J
   -------+
          |        +---------- species K
          +--------+
                   |    +----- species L
                   +----+
                        |  +-- species M
                        +--+
                           +-- N (extinct)
```

**(a)** Name the species most closely related to M. **[1]**
**(b)** State whether species L shares a more recent common ancestor with J or with K. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest how scientists were able to place species N on the tree. **[1]**
**(d)** A student writes: "Species M evolved from species J." Explain why this is not shown by the tree. **[2]**

**9.** Describe how developments in biology led scientists to replace a classification based on visible
structure with Woese's three-domain system. **[4]**

**10.** Compare the Linnaean system of classification with the three-domain system. **[4]**

**11.** Scientists discover a new single-celled organism living in water at 90 °C. Its cells have no nucleus.

**(a)** Explain why it would not be placed in the domain Eukaryota. **[1]**
**(b)** Suggest which domain it is most likely to belong to, and give a reason. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how scientists could confirm which domain it belongs to. **[2]**

**12.** Scientists compare the DNA base sequence of one gene in four species, W, X, Y and Z. The table shows the
percentage of bases that differ between each pair.

| Pair | W and X | W and Y | W and Z | X and Y | X and Z | Y and Z |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Difference (%) | 2 | 9 | 15 | 9 | 15 | 15 |

**(a)** Identify the two most closely related species. **[1]**
**(b)** Identify the species least closely related to the other three. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe the evolutionary tree that these data support. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest why a classification based on DNA might differ from one based on appearance. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species [2] (award one mark if only one adjacent pair is
swapped)
*Examiner insight:* Swapping order and family is the most common error.

**2. (a)** *Ursus* [1]
**(b)** They are in the same genus, so they are closely related [1]
**(c)** Any one: genus first with a capital letter; species second in lower case; printed in italics or
underlined when handwritten [1]

**3. (a)** Archaea, bacteria, eukaryota [1]
**(b)** Eukaryota [1]
*Examiner insight:* A mushroom is a fungus; fungi are eukaryotes, so "plants" or "bacteria" score nothing.

**4.** How scientists believe organisms are related (their common ancestors) [1]; current classification data
for living organisms [1]; fossil data for extinct organisms [1]

**5.** Common names differ between regions or languages, and the same name can refer to different species [1];
a binomial name refers to only one species, so scientists everywhere know exactly which organism is meant [1]

**6.** Primitive bacteria [1]; usually living in extreme environments, such as very hot, very salty or high
pressure conditions [1]

**7. (a)** A and B [1]; they share every level shown down to family (Carabidae), which is lower than any other
pair shares [1]
**(b)** E [1]
**(c)** No; they are in different genera, so they are different species [1]
**(d)** Phylum (Arthropoda) [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), "they are both beetles" is not using the table; quote the shared family.

**8. (a)** N [1]
**(b)** K [1]
**(c)** Using fossil data (comparing the features of its fossils with living species) [1]
**(d)** J and M are both at the tips of branches, so they are separate end-point species [1]; the tree shows
only that they share a common ancestor at the first branch point, not that one came from the other [1]

**9.** Linnaeus classified organisms by structure and characteristics that could be seen [1]; improvements in
microscopes revealed internal structures of cells, such as the presence of a nucleus [1]; understanding of
biochemical processes allowed the chemicals in cells to be compared [1]; chemical analysis showed that archaea
were very different from bacteria, so Woese divided organisms into three domains: archaea, bacteria and
eukaryota [1]

**10.** Any four of the following, 1 mark each [4]: the Linnaean system was developed by Linnaeus, the
three-domain system by Woese; Linnaean classification uses structure and characteristics, the three-domain system
uses evidence from chemical analysis; the Linnaean top level is kingdom, whereas domain is a level above kingdom;
the Linnaean system has seven levels from kingdom to species; the three-domain system separates archaea from
bacteria, which look similar
*Examiner insight:* "Compare" needs paired points ("whereas"), not two separate descriptions.

**11. (a)** Eukaryota have cells with a nucleus; this organism has none [1]
**(b)** Archaea [1]; archaea are primitive bacteria that usually live in extreme environments, and 90 °C is
extreme [1]
**(c)** Use chemical analysis [1]; to compare the chemicals in its cells with those of known archaea and
bacteria [1]

**12. (a)** W and X [1]
**(b)** Z [1]
**(c)** Z branches off first (the oldest common ancestor) [1]; Y branches off next, and W and X share the most
recent common ancestor [1]
**(d)** Organisms can look similar without being closely related (or look different but be closely related), and
chemical data reveal differences that cannot be seen [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing the seven levels in the wrong order.
- Answering "use the table" questions from general knowledge rather than quoting a shared level.
- Calling a genus name on its own a species.
- Placing fungi or protists outside Eukaryota.
- Reading closeness on a tree from the position of names, not from branch points.
- Saying one living species on a tree evolved from another.
