Condensed recall for section 3.3.2 of Unit 3. For explanations and worked examples, use the [Business growth study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-business-ybs11-business-growth/), then try the [practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-business-ybs11-business-growth-practice/). Figures are invented.

## Formula sheet

```
Average cost per unit = total cost / output
Takeover premium      = (offer price / market price - 1) x 100
Cost of takeover      = number of shares x offer price
Receivables           = annual credit sales x days allowed / 365
Combined market share = share A + share B (horizontal merger)
```

## Objectives of growth

| Objective | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Economies of scale | Falling average cost as output rises |
| Market power | Stronger bargaining position with customers and suppliers |
| Market share and brand | Bigger presence; easier launches |
| Profitability | More revenue and lower unit costs |

**Internal economies:** purchasing, technical, financial, managerial, marketing. **External economies:** skilled labour pool, specialist suppliers and transport links near a cluster of firms in the industry.

## Organic or inorganic?

| | Organic | Inorganic |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Own resources | Merger or takeover |
| Speed | Slow | Fast |
| Cost and risk | Lower | Higher |
| Control | Kept | Culture clash risk |
| Methods | New products, new outlets, export, franchising | Buy or join with another business |

- **Organic advantages:** low risk, funded from profit, culture kept.
- **Organic disadvantages:** slow; rivals may move first; limited by resources.
- **Inorganic advantages:** immediate share, new skills and markets, removes a rival.
- **Inorganic disadvantages:** costly, job losses, integration problems, regulatory scrutiny.

## Mergers and takeovers

| | Merger | Takeover |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Two firms agree to combine | One firm buys control of another |
| Attitude | Agreed by both | Friendly or hostile |

**Reasons:** market share, remove competition, new products or technology, new markets, economies of scale, spread risk.

| Integration | Link | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Horizontal | Same stage, same industry | Two supermarkets |
| Vertical backward | With a supplier | Bakery buys flour mill |
| Vertical forward | With a distributor or retailer | Brewery buys pubs |
| Conglomerate | Unrelated industries | Food firm buys a hotel group |

**Financial rewards:** extra profit, synergies, stronger market position. **Financial risks:** overpaying, debt and interest, falling share price, failure to achieve savings.

## Problems arising from growth

| Problem | Cause | Possible solution |
|---|---|---|
| Diseconomies of scale | Poor communication, slow decisions, weak motivation, duplication | Delegate, smaller units, better systems |
| Poor internal communication | More staff, layers and sites | Flatter structure, regular briefings, intranet |
| Overtrading | Sales grow faster than finance | Loan, overdraft, factoring, shorter credit terms, slower growth |

- Diseconomies appear when average cost rises with output.
- Overtrading is a cash problem. The business may be profitable but unable to pay bills.
- Mergers add a communication problem: two cultures and two systems.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Organic growth | Growth from the firm's own resources |
| Inorganic growth | Growth through merger or takeover |
| Synergy | Combined business worth more than the sum of its parts |
| Hostile takeover | Bid opposed by the target's board |
| Backward integration | Joining with a supplier |
| Conglomerate | Group of businesses in unrelated industries |
| Overtrading | Growing faster than cash can support |

## Exam traps

- Growth does not guarantee higher profit: costs may rise faster than revenue.
- A takeover is not a merger. Say who buys whom.
- Horizontal means the **same** stage of production; vertical means a **different** stage.
- Overtrading and diseconomies of scale are different problems: cash against average cost.
- A conglomerate does not give economies of scale in marketing or buying, though it spreads risk.
- For **assess**, weigh speed against risk and cost, and judge with the firm's finances in the source.

## Answer frames

- **Reason for growth:** state the objective, give the mechanism (such as bulk discounts) and the result (a lower unit cost and a wider margin).
- **Takeover risk:** purchase price, financing method, interest cost, then the effect on profit and cash.
- **Organic or inorganic?** Compare speed, cost, risk, control and fit with the firm's cash; then give a judgement that depends on one named factor.
- **Problem of growth:** name the problem, link it to a cause in the source and finish with the effect on cost, morale or cash.

## Self-test

1. 5,000 units cost $50,000 and 15,000 units cost $120,000. Calculate average cost at each output.
2. State the term for the change in your answer to question 1.
3. A bidder offers $6.00 a share for a company with market price $5.00 and 2 million shares. Calculate the premium and the cost.
4. Two rivals with market shares of 18% and 12% merge. State the combined share and the type of integration.
5. Name the type of integration when a baker buys a flour mill.
6. Annual credit sales are $240,000 and customers take 45 days to pay. Calculate trade receivables.
7. State one cause of diseconomies of scale.
8. Give one advantage of organic growth over a takeover.
9. Explain why overtrading can cause a profitable business to fail.
10. Name two internal economies of scale.

### Answers

1. 50,000 / 5,000 = **$10.00**; 120,000 / 15,000 = **$8.00**.
2. Economies of scale.
3. Premium = 6 / 5 - 1 = **20%**; cost = 2m x 6 = **$12m**.
4. 18% + 12% = **30%**; horizontal integration.
5. Backward vertical integration.
6. 240,000 x 45 / 365 = **$29,589** (to the nearest dollar).
7. Any one: poor communication, slow decisions, weaker motivation, duplication, co-ordination between sites.
8. Any one: lower risk; funded from profit; keeps the culture; keeps control.
9. Costs rise before customers pay, so cash runs out and bills cannot be paid even though sales and profit look healthy.
10. Any two from: purchasing, technical, financial, managerial, marketing.
