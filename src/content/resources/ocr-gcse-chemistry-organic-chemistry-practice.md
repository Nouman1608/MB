These questions cover sub-topic **C6.2 Organic chemistry** in J248, the OCR GCSE (9–1) Chemistry A (Gateway Science) specification (Version 4.0, August 2026): learning outcomes C6.2a to C6.2q, the mathematical learning outcome CM6.2i and the practical suggestions on models and making nylon. Two questions test C6.2e and C6.2f and carry a **Higher Tier only** label; Foundation and Higher candidates can attempt the rest. Topic C6 sits on Paper 2 at Foundation Tier and on Paper 4 at Higher Tier.

Find other J248 topics through the [chemistry hub for OCR GCSE](/boards/ocr/gcse/chemistry/); each statement appears on a printable [checklist for J248](/checklists/ocr/gcse/chemistry/). Relative atomic masses where needed: H = 1, C = 12, O = 16, F = 19.

## Questions

**1.** Here are four organic compounds: C₃H₆, C₂H₅COOH, C₄H₉OH, C₅H₁₂.

**(a)** Name the homologous series of each compound. **[2]**
**(b)** Give the functional group of the alcohol. **[1]**

**2.** This question is about naming, drawing and shape.

**(a)** Name the compound CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH. **[1]**
**(b)** Draw the displayed formula of propan-1-ol. **[2]**
**(c)** The displayed formula of methane shows H–C–H angles of 90°. State the actual shape and approximate angle of a methane molecule. **[2]**

**3.** Heptane, C₇H₁₆, is a fuel found in petrol.

**(a)** Heptane burns completely in oxygen. Write the balanced symbol equation. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of carbon dioxide formed when 5.00 g of heptane burns completely. **[2]**

**4.** But-1-ene, CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃, reacts with bromine and with hydrogen.

**(a)** Describe what you would see when but-1-ene is shaken with bromine water. **[1]**
**(b)** Give the molecular formula of the product with bromine. **[1]**
**(c)** Name the product with hydrogen, and state the catalyst used. **[2]**
**(d)** Name the type of reaction in (b) and (c). **[1]**

**5.** A student warms butan-1-ol with acidified potassium manganate(VII) solution.

**(a)** State the colour change. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the organic product and give its formula. **[2]**
**(c)** The student predicts that pentan-1-ol will react the same way. Explain why this prediction is reasonable. **[1]**

**6.** Fluoroethene, CH₂=CHF, forms an addition polymer.

**(a)** Draw the repeat unit of poly(fluoroethene), using the [repeat unit]ₙ form. **[2]**
**(b)** A chain of poly(fluoroethene) has Mr 92 000. Calculate the number of monomers in the chain. **[2]**
**(c)** A different polymer has the repeat unit –[CH(CH₃)–CH(CH₃)]–. Give the structural formula and name of its monomer. **[2]**

**7.** **Higher Tier only.** A polyester is made from monomer A, HO–●–OH, and monomer B, HOOC–■–COOH, where the blocks stand for carbon chains.

**(a)** Name the small molecule formed with the polymer. **[1]**
**(b)** Draw a block diagram of the repeat unit. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why monomer A must have two –OH groups, not one. **[1]**
**(d)** State how many small molecules form when 400 molecules of A react with 400 molecules of B to give 400 repeat units. **[1]**

**8.** **Higher Tier only.** Describe how nylon can be made in the laboratory from 1,6-diaminohexane solution and hexanedioyl dichloride solution. Include the small molecule formed and one safety precaution. **[4]**

**9.** Living things contain natural polymers.

**(a)** Name the monomers that make up DNA. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the four bases that identify these monomers. **[1]**
**(c)** Name the type of monomer used to build **(i)** proteins and **(ii)** starch. **[2]**

**10.** Crude oil is separated by fractional distillation.

**(a)** Explain why the fraction collected at the bottom of the column has a higher boiling point than the fraction collected at the top. Refer to molecular size and forces. **[3]**
**(b)** Give the general formula of most compounds in the fractions. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why crude oil is described as a finite resource. **[1]**
**(d)** State **one** use of crude oil other than as a fuel. **[1]**

**11.** The table shows illustrative supply and demand data for a refinery.

| Fraction | Supply from distillation / % | Demand / % |
|---|---|---|
| Refinery gases | 4 | 5 |
| Petrol | 10 | 26 |
| Kerosene | 12 | 9 |
| Diesel | 20 | 25 |
| Fuel oil and bitumen | 54 | 35 |

**(a)** Explain, using figures from the table, why some fuel oil is cracked at this refinery. **[2]**
**(b)** Dodecane is cracked: C₁₂H₂₆ → C₆H₁₄ + C₄H₈ + X. Identify X. **[1]**
**(c)** State the conditions used for catalytic cracking. **[2]**
**(d)** Describe a test to show that the cracked products contain an alkene. **[1]**

**12.** A delivery company is choosing between hydrogen/oxygen fuel cells and rechargeable batteries for its vans.

**(a)** Write the overall equation for the reaction in a hydrogen/oxygen fuel cell. **[1]**
**(b)** Write the half equation at the negative electrode when the electrolyte is acidic. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why a rechargeable battery must be recharged, but a fuel cell does not stop working. **[1]**
**(d)** A van uses 0.60 kg of hydrogen on one trip. Calculate the mass of water produced. **[2]**
**(e)** Evaluate the use of fuel cells for the vans. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** C₃H₆ alkene; C₂H₅COOH carboxylic acid [1]; C₄H₉OH alcohol; C₅H₁₂ alkane [1].
**(b)** **–OH** (hydroxyl) [1]
*Examiner insight:* For the hydrocarbons, test the hydrogen count against 2n and 2n + 2; guessing from molecule size makes C₃H₆ and C₅H₁₂ easy to mix up.

**2. (a)** **Butanoic acid** [1]
**(b)** Three carbons in a chain with all C–H and C–C bonds shown [1]; O–H on the end carbon, drawn as C–O–H with both bonds visible [1].
**(c)** **Tetrahedral** [1]; about **109.5°** [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (b), writing –OH as a group without the O–H bond is a condensed formula, not a displayed one; show every bond.

**3. (a)** Correct formulae of products, CO₂ and H₂O [1]; **C₇H₁₆ + 11O₂ → 7CO₂ + 8H₂O** [1]
**(b)** Mr = 100, so 5.00 ÷ 100 = 0.0500 mol; CO₂ = 7 × 0.0500 = 0.350 mol [1]; 0.350 × 44 = **15.4 g** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Use the 1 : 7 ratio from your equation; a 1 : 1 ratio gives 2.20 g, which shows a working step was missed.

**4. (a)** Orange bromine water turns **colourless** [1].
**(b)** **C₄H₈Br₂** (1,2-dibromobutane) [1]
**(c)** **Butane** [1]; **nickel** catalyst [1].
**(d)** **Addition** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Clear" describes transparency, not colour; write colourless to describe the change.

**5. (a)** **Purple to colourless** [1]
**(b)** **Butanoic acid** [1]; **C₃H₇COOH** [1]
**(c)** Pentan-1-ol has the same –OH functional group, and the functional group decides the reaction [1].
*Examiner insight:* The acid keeps all four carbons; naming propanoic acid suggests a carbon was lost, which oxidation does not do here.

**6. (a)** Single C–C bond between two carbons, with H, H, H and F on them [1]; brackets with bonds through them and n outside [1].
**(b)** Mr of C₂H₃F = 24 + 3 + 19 = 46 [1]; 92 000 ÷ 46 = **2000** [1]
**(c)** **CH₃CH=CHCH₃** [1]; **but-2-ene** [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (c), put the double bond between the two main-chain carbons of the repeat unit; drawing it elsewhere gives a monomer that would make a different polymer.

**7. (a)** **Water** [1]
**(b)** Repeat unit –[O–●–O–OC–■–CO]– [1], with brackets and n [1].
**(c)** One –OH joins the chain on one side and the second joins it on the other; with one, the chain stops [1].
**(d)** Two ester links per repeat unit, so **800** water molecules [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (b), check that each block keeps its own atoms: ● keeps both O atoms and ■ keeps both C=O groups.

**8.** Diamine solution in water in a beaker, with the dichloride solution (in cyclohexane) poured gently on top as a separate layer [1]. Nylon forms at the boundary between the layers [1]; it is lifted with tweezers and wound slowly onto a rod [1]. Small molecule **hydrogen chloride**, plus one precaution: gloves/eye protection (corrosive reagents) or no naked flames (flammable cyclohexane) [1].
*Examiner insight:* Say the layers must not be mixed; describing stirring the two solutions together loses the key step of the method.

**9. (a)** **Nucleotides** [1]
**(b)** **Adenine, thymine, cytosine, guanine** [1]
**(c)** **(i)** Amino acids [1]; **(ii)** sugars, such as glucose [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Bases" alone is not the monomer; the monomers are nucleotides, named after their bases.

**10. (a)** Bottom fraction has larger molecules [1]; larger molecules have stronger intermolecular forces [1]; overcoming them takes more energy, which raises the boiling point [1].
**(b)** **CₙH₂ₙ₊₂** (alkanes) [1]
**(c)** It formed over millions of years and is being used much faster than it forms [1].
**(d)** Any one: feedstock for plastics, solvents, detergents or medicines [1].
*Examiner insight:* Name intermolecular forces; an answer about breaking covalent bonds describes cracking, not boiling.

**11. (a)** Fuel oil and bitumen supply (54%) exceeds demand (35%) [1]; petrol demand (26%) exceeds supply (10%), so cracking turns surplus long chains into shorter, more wanted molecules [1].
**(b)** **C₂H₄** (ethene) [1]
**(c)** Vaporised alkane passed over a hot catalyst [1]; about 500 °C with a zeolite catalyst [1].
**(d)** Shake with bromine water; it turns from orange to colourless [1].
*Examiner insight:* Quote figures from the table for both the surplus and the shortage; a general statement about demand does not use the data.

**12. (a)** **2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O** [1]
**(b)** **H₂ → 2H⁺ + 2e⁻** [1]
**(c)** A battery's reactants are used up, but a fuel cell is supplied with fresh fuel and oxygen continuously [1].
**(d)** 600 ÷ 2 = 300 mol H₂, giving 300 mol H₂O [1]; 300 × 18 = 5400 g = **5.4 kg** [1].
**(e)** For: refuelling is quick and the only product at the van is water [1]. Against: hydrogen is hard to store safely and may be made from fossil fuels, with few filling stations; judgement, e.g. suitable only if the depot has its own hydrogen supply [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (d), convert 0.60 kg to grams before dividing by Mr; 0.60 ÷ 2 gives a value a thousand times too small.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using C₃H₇OH-style formulae where a displayed formula is asked for.
- Leaving the –COOH carbon out of a carboxylic acid's name.
- Combustion equations with the products right but the oxygen unbalanced.
- Writing "clear" instead of colourless for bromine water.
- Forgetting the nickel catalyst for hydrogen addition.
- Keeping a double bond inside an addition polymer's repeat unit.
- (Higher Tier) Saying water forms in the nylon rope trick, when the dichloride gives hydrogen chloride.
- Explaining boiling point with covalent bonds instead of intermolecular forces.
- Giving only "heat" for cracking conditions, without the catalyst.
- Not converting kilograms to grams in a moles calculation.
