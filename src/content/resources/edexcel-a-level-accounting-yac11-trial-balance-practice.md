## Answers

**1.** Any two: to check the arithmetical accuracy of the double entry (total debits equal total credits) [1]; to show that an error exists when the totals disagree, or to provide a summary of balances from which the financial statements are prepared [1].

**2. (a)** Debit [1]

**(b)** Credit [1]

**(c)** Credit [1]

**(d)** Debit [1]

**3.** Payments exceed receipts by 13,415 − 12,860 = **555**, so the balance b/d is on the credit side (an overdraft) [1]; it goes in the **credit** column [1].

**4.** 96,240 − 58,915 [1] = **37,325** (credit) [1].

**5.** When the balances are extracted, the inventory account still holds the opening figure, because closing inventory is valued at the year end [1]; it is recorded when the financial statements are prepared, so it is given as a note to the trial balance and used in cost of sales and the statement of financial position [1].

**6.** Yes: a debit balance of 630 entered in the credit column raises the credit total by 630 and lowers the debit total by 630, a difference of 1,260 [1]. The balance has been wrongly placed in the **credit** column [1].

**9.** Any two limitations, each with an example [1] + [1]:

- Errors that put equal amounts on both sides are not revealed [1], e.g. a purchase of a computer for 1,200 debited to office expenses (error of principle) still balances [1].
- It does not show where an error is [1], e.g. a difference of 90 could be a transposition such as 1,450 posted as 1,540, or a one-sided entry of 90 [1].
- It is prepared before year-end adjustments [1], e.g. electricity owing at the year end is not in the light and heat balance [1].
- It is not a financial statement [1], e.g. it lists revenue and expenses separately but does not calculate profit for the year [1].

**10.** For [1]: agreement shows that total debits equal total credits, so there are no one-sided entries or casting errors in the ledger [1].
Against [1]: some errors leave the trial balance agreeing but change profit, for example an error of principle debiting a new oven to repairs, which overstates expenses and understates profit [1].
Against [1]: the trial balance is drawn up before adjustments such as accruals, prepayments, depreciation and irrecoverable debts, so profit calculated from its unadjusted balances would be wrong [1].
Judgement: the statement is not justified; an agreeing trial balance is a useful first check of arithmetic, but profit is only reliable once postings have been reviewed and year-end adjustments made [1]. (Maximum 6.)

**11.** 86,020 − 74,380 [1] = **11,640**, in the **debit** column [1].

**12. (a)** The trial balance shows amounts paid or recorded during the year, before year-end adjustments [1]; the accruals concept requires the expense for the year, so prepaid amounts are removed and amounts owing are added [1].

**(b)** Insurance 1,150 − 150 = **1,000** [1]; wages 12,400 + 320 = **12,720** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Placing a bank balance by its name rather than the side of its balance b/d (Questions 3 and 8).
- Moving only some of the misplaced items in a redraft, then forcing the totals with a new balancing figure.
- Omitting the heading or writing "for the year ended" on a trial balance.
- Explaining a limitation without an example or consequence, which earns only the identification mark.
- In an evaluation, listing points on one side only and giving no final judgement.

## Approaching trial balance questions

For a full trial balance, sort the list into the debit and credit columns first, total the side without the missing figure, then find the missing figure by subtraction and show that calculation. For a redraft, go through the draft line by line and mark each item as correct or misplaced before writing anything; check your new totals against the old ones by adding and removing the moved balances. For written questions, tie every limitation to a specific error or item and say what goes wrong as a result.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these Edexcel YAC11 trial balance questions from past papers?**
No. They are original questions on outcomes 1.2.1 and 1.2.2, written for revision. Use them alongside the official past papers.

**How should I set out a trial balance in the exam?**
Use a heading with the business name and the date ("at"), two money columns headed debit and credit, one line per account, and totals that agree at the foot.
