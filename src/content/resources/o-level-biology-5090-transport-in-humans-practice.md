## Questions

**1.** Identify each structure of the heart from its description.

**(a)** The wall of muscle between the left and right sides. **[1]**
**(b)** The type of valve between the left atrium and the left ventricle. **[1]**
**(c)** Blood vessels on the outside of the heart that supply its muscle. **[1]**
**(d)** The type of valve at the base of the pulmonary artery. **[1]**

**2.** Humans have a double circulation.

**(a)** Describe what is meant by a double circulation. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why it is an advantage to have a low pressure circulation to the lungs and a high pressure circulation to the body. **[2]**

**3.** Explain why:

**(a)** the wall of the left ventricle is thicker than the wall of the right ventricle **[2]**
**(b)** the walls of the atria are thinner than the walls of the ventricles. **[2]**

**4.** Describe how the contraction of the heart muscle and the action of the valves move blood through the heart in one heartbeat, starting when the atria contract. **[5]**

**5.** A student measured his pulse for 30 s, three times at rest and three times straight after 2 minutes of skipping.

| | Count 1 | Count 2 | Count 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beats in 30 s at rest | 36 | 38 | 37 |
| Beats in 30 s after skipping | 62 | 64 | 63 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean resting heart rate in beats per minute. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in heart rate after skipping. Give your answer to one decimal place. **[2]**
**(c)** After skipping, his heart rate was 126, 104, 88, 74 and 74 beats per minute at 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 minutes. State how long his heart rate took to return to the resting value. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why heart rate increases during physical activity. **[3]**

**6.** On an ECG trace, the peaks of successive heartbeats occur at 0.40 s, 1.15 s, 1.90 s, 2.65 s and 3.40 s.

**(a)** Calculate the heart rate in beats per minute. **[2]**
**(b)** State two other ways of monitoring the activity of the heart. **[2]**

**7.** **(a)** Compare the structure of an artery with the structure of a vein. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how the structure of an artery is related to the pressure of the blood it carries. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how the wall of a capillary suits its function. **[1]**

**8.** Name the blood vessel that:

**(a)** carries blood from the lungs to the heart **[1]**
**(b)** carries blood rich in absorbed amino acids from the small intestine to the liver **[1]**
**(c)** carries blood from the aorta to a kidney **[1]**
**(d)** carries blood from the liver towards the heart. **[1]**

**9.** A stained blood smear is viewed under a light microscope. Cell X has no nucleus. Cell Y has a lobed nucleus and much cytoplasm. Cell Z has a large, round nucleus that almost fills the cell.

**(a)** Identify cells X, Y and Z. **[3]**
**(b)** State the function of cell Y and of cell Z. **[2]**
**(c)** At ×2500, cell Y measures 30 mm on the image. Calculate its actual diameter in µm. **[2]**

**10.** **(a)** Describe the role of platelets when the skin is cut. **[2]**
**(b)** Name four substances, other than proteins, transported in the plasma. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe how glucose passes from the blood in a capillary to a muscle cell, and how carbon dioxide passes the other way. **[4]**

**11.** **(a)** Describe coronary heart disease and explain how it can damage the heart. **[3]**
**(b)** Discuss the role of diet and exercise in reducing the risk of coronary heart disease. **[4]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Septum [1]
**(b)** Atrioventricular valve [1]
**(c)** Coronary arteries [1]
**(d)** Semilunar valve [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the syllabus term "atrioventricular" in (b); in (c), "coronary veins" or "aorta" names the wrong vessel and cannot be credited.

**2. (a)** Blood passes through the heart twice for each complete circuit of the body. [1]
**(b)** Low pressure to the lungs avoids damaging the delicate lung capillaries. [1] High pressure delivers blood quickly to all the body tissues, supplying oxygen and glucose. [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Two circuits" alone is too vague for (a); the idea of passing through the heart twice per circuit is what earns the mark.

**3. (a)** The left ventricle pumps blood all round the body / over a longer distance [1], so it must produce a higher pressure, which needs more muscle; the right ventricle pumps only to the lungs. [1]
**(b)** Atria pump blood only into the ventricles, a short distance, at low pressure [1]; ventricles pump blood out of the heart into the arteries, needing more force. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Pumps more blood" is wrong, because both ventricles pump the same volume; the answer must be about pressure and distance.

**4.** Atria contract, pushing blood into the ventricles through the open atrioventricular valves. [1] Ventricles contract, raising pressure. [1] Atrioventricular valves close to prevent backflow into the atria. [1] Semilunar valves open, so blood is pushed into the aorta and pulmonary artery. [1] When the ventricles relax, the semilunar valves close, preventing backflow into the ventricles. [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Sequence matters in a "describe" answer: each valve must be named and linked to the correct contraction, and "valves stop blood going backwards" without saying which valve and where is likely to earn little.

**5. (a)** Mean = (36 + 38 + 37) ÷ 3 = 37 beats per 30 s [1]; 37 × 2 = **74 beats per minute** [1]
**(b)** After skipping: mean 63 × 2 = 126 beats per minute; increase = 126 − 74 = 52 [1]; 52 ÷ 74 × 100 = **70.3%** [1]
**(c)** **4 minutes** [1]
**(d)** Muscles contract more, so they respire faster to release more energy. [1] They need more oxygen and glucose delivered. [1] More carbon dioxide must be removed, so the heart pumps blood faster. [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* An error carried forward from (a) into (b) is usually allowed, but the percentage must be calculated on the resting value, not the exercise value.

**6. (a)** Time per beat = 1.15 − 0.40 = 0.75 s [1]; heart rate = 60 ÷ 0.75 = **80 beats per minute** [1]
**(b)** Pulse rate [1]; listening to the sounds of the valves closing (with a stethoscope) [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the interval between peaks, not the time of the first peak; 60 ÷ 0.40 gives 150, which scores nothing.

**7. (a)** Artery wall thicker than vein wall [1]; artery has more muscle and elastic tissue [1]; artery has a narrower lumen and no valves, vein a wider lumen with valves [1]
**(b)** Blood is at high pressure, so a thick wall withstands it [1]; elastic tissue stretches and recoils with each surge, keeping blood flowing [1]
**(c)** Wall one cell thick, giving a short diffusion distance for exchange [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In a "compare" question each point needs both vessels, either stated or clearly implied with a comparative word such as "thicker".

**8. (a)** Pulmonary vein [1]
**(b)** Hepatic portal vein [1]
**(c)** Renal artery [1]
**(d)** Hepatic vein [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Spelling that changes the vessel, such as "hepatic artery" for (d), cannot be credited; "vena cava" is wrong for (d) because the question asks for the vessel leaving the liver.

**9. (a)** X red blood cell [1]; Y phagocyte [1]; Z lymphocyte [1]
**(b)** Y engulfs pathogens [1]; Z produces antibodies [1]
**(c)** 30 mm = 30 000 µm [1]; 30 000 ÷ 2500 = **12 µm** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* "White blood cell" alone is not enough for Y or Z; the specific type is required, and the unit µm must be shown in (c).

**10. (a)** Platelets start clotting, in which soluble fibrinogen is converted to insoluble fibrin [1]; the clot prevents blood loss and the entry of pathogens [1]
**(b)** Any four from: ions, glucose, amino acids, hormones, carbon dioxide, urea, vitamins (two correct [1], four correct [1])
**(c)** Plasma leaks out of the capillary to form tissue fluid around the cells [1]; glucose diffuses from the blood into the tissue fluid [1] and then into the muscle cell [1]; carbon dioxide diffuses from the cell into the tissue fluid and then into the blood [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* For (c), saying glucose goes "straight into the cell" misses the tissue fluid step, and "fibrinogen becomes fibrin" needs the soluble/insoluble change to be complete.

**11. (a)** A coronary artery becomes blocked (for example by fatty deposits) [1]; less blood reaches the heart muscle, so it gets too little oxygen and glucose [1]; the muscle cannot respire, so it cannot contract and may die (heart attack) [1]
**(b)** Less saturated fat and cholesterol in the diet reduces fatty deposits in coronary arteries [1]; less salt helps keep blood pressure down [1]; regular exercise strengthens the heart muscle and helps control body mass, blood pressure and cholesterol [1]; but diet and exercise cannot change genetic predisposition, age or gender, so risk is reduced, not removed [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* A "discuss" answer that only lists risk factors scores little; each point needs a reason linked to the coronary arteries or heart, plus some balance.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "two circuits" for a double circulation without "passes through the heart twice".
- Explaining the thicker left ventricle by volume pumped rather than pressure and distance.
- Naming "valves" without saying which ones open or close at each stage.
- Using the time of one ECG peak instead of the interval between peaks.
- Quoting a pulse count per 30 s as the heart rate.
- Calculating a percentage change on the wrong baseline.
- Mixing up the hepatic vein and the hepatic portal vein.
- Writing "white blood cell" when the question needs lymphocyte or phagocyte.
- Leaving out tissue fluid when describing exchange between blood and cells.
