## Section A

**1.** A duck floats on a pond as ripples pass. Describe the duck's motion and explain what this shows about waves. **[2]**

**2.** Define **(a)** frequency, **(b)** wavelength and **(c)** amplitude. **[3]**

**3.** State the difference between a transverse wave and a longitudinal wave, and give **one** example of each. **[4]**

**4.** A wave has a frequency of 40 Hz and a wavelength of 0.075 m. Calculate its speed. **[2]**

**5.** A sound wave of frequency 1360 Hz travels through air at 340 m/s. Calculate its wavelength. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A student counts 10 complete waves passing a post in 4.0 s. The distance from the first crest to the fifth crest is 3.2 m.

**(a)** Calculate the frequency of the waves. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the wavelength. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the wave speed. **[1]**

**7.** Straight water waves in a ripple tank pass from deep water into shallow water over a glass plate.

**(a)** State what happens to the speed, the wavelength and the frequency of the waves. **[3]**
**(b)** The boundary between the deep and shallow regions is at an angle to the wavefronts. Describe what happens to the direction of the waves and explain why. **[2]**

**8.** Straight water waves of wavelength 2.0 cm reach a barrier with a gap. Describe the pattern of the waves beyond the gap when the gap is **(a)** 2.0 cm wide and **(b)** 20 cm wide. **[4]**

**9.** Straight water waves meet a flat barrier. The angle between the direction of the incident waves and the normal is 35°.

**(a)** State the angle of reflection. **[1]**
**(b)** State whether the wavelength of the reflected waves is larger, smaller or the same as that of the incident waves. **[1]**

## Section C

**10.** Sea waves pass the end of a harbour wall. Explain why long-wavelength waves reach further into the calm water behind the wall than short-wavelength ripples do. **[3]**

**11.** An earthquake produces P-waves and S-waves.

**(a)** State which is longitudinal and which is transverse. **[2]**
**(b)** A P-wave travels through rock at 7.0 km/s with a frequency of 2.0 Hz. Calculate its wavelength in metres. **[2]**

**12.** A vibrator in a ripple tank of constant depth produces waves of wavelength 3.0 cm. The frequency of the vibrator is doubled. State what happens to the wave speed and the wavelength, and explain your answer. **[3]**

**13.** Describe how a ripple tank is set up and lit so that the wavefronts can be seen and their wavelength measured. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The duck **bobs up and down** (moves about a fixed position) but does not travel along with the ripples [1]; this shows that waves **transfer energy without transferring matter** [1].

**2. (a)** The number of wavelengths that pass a point **per unit time** [1]. **(b)** The distance between **two consecutive, identical points**, such as two consecutive crests [1]. **(c)** The **maximum distance from the mean position** [1].

**3.** Transverse: vibration **at right angles** to the direction of energy transfer [1]; example: electromagnetic radiation, water surface waves or seismic S-waves [1]. Longitudinal: vibration **parallel** to the direction of energy transfer [1]; example: sound or seismic P-waves [1].

**4.** v = fλ = 40 × 0.075 [1] = **3.0 m/s** [1].

**5.** λ = v ÷ f = 340 ÷ 1360 [1] = **0.25 m** [1].

**6. (a)** f = 10 ÷ 4.0 [1] = **2.5 Hz** [1].
**(b)** First to fifth crest = **4 wavelengths** [1], so λ = 3.2 ÷ 4 = **0.80 m** [1].
**(c)** v = 2.5 × 0.80 = **2.0 m/s** [1].

**7. (a)** Speed **decreases** [1]; wavelength **decreases** [1]; frequency **stays the same** [1].
**(b)** The waves change direction, bending **towards the normal** [1], because the part of each wavefront that reaches the shallow water first **slows down first**, so the wavefront turns [1].

**8. (a)** Gap similar to the wavelength: the waves **spread out widely** [1], emerging as almost **circular (curved) wavefronts** [1].
**(b)** Gap much wider than the wavelength: the waves pass through **mostly straight** [1], with only **slight spreading at the edges** [1]. (In both cases the wavelength is unchanged.)

**9. (a)** **35°** (angle of reflection = angle of incidence) [1].
**(b)** The **same** [1].

**10.** Waves **diffract** (spread) round the end of the wall into the region behind it [1]. The amount of diffraction at an edge **increases with wavelength** [1], so long-wavelength waves bend further into the sheltered region than short ripples [1].

**11. (a)** P-waves are **longitudinal** [1]; S-waves are **transverse** [1].
**(b)** λ = v ÷ f = 7000 ÷ 2.0 [1] = **3500 m** [1].

**12.** The wave speed **stays the same**, because it depends on the **medium (depth)**, which has not changed [1]. Since v = fλ, doubling f must **halve** the wavelength [1], to **1.5 cm** [1].

**13.** A shallow transparent tray of water with a vibrating bar or dipper to make waves [1]; a **lamp above** the tank so the crests focus light into **bright lines on a screen or paper below** [1]; measure the distance across several bright lines and divide by the number of wavelengths (a stroboscope can freeze the pattern) [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Counting crests instead of wavelengths (first to fifth crest is four wavelengths).
- Writing that frequency changes during refraction.
- Measuring amplitude from crest to trough.
- Forgetting to convert km/s into m/s.
- Saying diffraction changes the wavelength.
