This page is a guide to AQA GCSE Sociology (8192) past papers for the June 2026, June 2025 and June 2024 series. It gives the grade thresholds copied from AQA's own grade-boundary documents, and a short account of what examiners reported for June 2025, written by us from AQA's Reports on the Examination. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on AQA's own site, linked below.

## Where to get the official papers

- [AQA GCSE Sociology (8192) specification](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/sociology/gcse/sociology-8192/specification)
- [AQA GCSE Sociology (8192) assessment resources](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/sociology/gcse/sociology-8192/assessment-resources)
- [AQA grade boundaries](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries) and the [grade boundaries archive](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries/archive)

The assessment resources page lists question papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination for each series. Use the three documents together. Sit a paper first, under timed conditions. Mark it strictly with the mark scheme, which shows what each mark needs. Then read the report for that paper: it explains why answers that looked reasonable still lost marks. The notes below are a quick way into the June 2025 reports.

Check which specification a paper was set on. 8192 is a linear, untiered qualification, first taught from September 2017 and first examined in June 2019. It has two written papers, each 1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks and 50% of the GCSE, and no coursework. Paper 1 covers the sociology of families and education, and Paper 2 covers the sociology of crime and deviance and social stratification. Both also draw on social theory and research methods from across the course. Every series on this page was set on that specification.

## June 2025

Examiners reported that entries are rising and that many candidates showed good sociological vocabulary and theory. Extended answers were better structured than before, with alternative perspectives used more often. Studies set in the source material were well known. Research methods were the main concern on Paper 1, and Paper 2 showed improved methods understanding compared with 2024 but still too much confusion between key terms. In both papers, the most common reason for lost marks was a correct idea that was not tied to the context in the question, which caps an answer at half marks.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1, families: patriarchy explained as a list of family roles instead of men holding power and controlling money; family functions named but not developed; a factor behind rising cohabitation not linked to cohabitation, or answers about why people marry less or about divorce; types of family diversity given that the named study did not identify, and the research method of that study missed.
- Paper 1, research methods: evaluation points for official statistics and a small sample written as "accurate" or "flawed" instead of validity, reliability, representativeness or generalisability; ethical issues offered where a practical issue was asked for; non-participant observation confused with covert observation, so the Hawthorne effect was wrongly dismissed; the disadvantage of structured interviews not applied to the group being studied.
- Paper 1, extended answers: the extended family question drifting to the nuclear family, and the question on the family as the main agent of socialisation answered without considering other agents or reaching a conclusion about how far sociologists agree.
- Paper 1, education: anti-school subcultures described as simply not wanting to go to school, cultural and material deprivation confused, and an in-school factor given without linking it to social class and achievement. The gender and education essay stayed on in-school versus out-of-school factors, ignoring the gender element.
- Paper 2, crime and deviance: a mixed-methods question where candidates did not know what mixed methods meant; sampling and ethics answers with no link to deviant subcultures; the media's role in public fear answered without using the source; a labelling answer that did not name the effect or the perspective.
- Paper 2, extended answers: crime essays that relied on one perspective, with weak or missing conclusions, while the stronger ones contrasted functionalist and Marxist views and added feminist ideas.
- Paper 2, social stratification: the reasons for the gender pay gap described rather than explained, quantitative methods praised without reference to the pay gap, and a question about sexuality answered as if it were about gender. The 4-mark source questions lost their final mark when no sociological perspective was named. Official statistics were criticised as outdated even though the data were recent.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find AQA GCSE Sociology 8192 past papers?**
AQA lists question papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination on the 8192 assessment resources page. Grade boundaries are on its grade boundaries page and archive.

**What does "link to the context" mean in the reports?**
Many short questions name a method, group or topic. The June 2025 reports say an answer that gives a correct advantage or disadvantage without applying it to that named situation can earn no more than half marks.

**Which research methods terms should I use?**
The reports ask for precise terms such as validity, reliability, representativeness and generalisability, not words like "accurate". Learn the glossary terms in the specification and practise using each one in an evaluation.

**Do the 12-mark answers need a conclusion?**
The 2025 reports say an introduction is not needed, but a conclusion that says how far sociologists would agree with the statement is. Candidates who covered a second perspective as a counter-argument did best.

**Can grade thresholds tell me what mark I need next time?**
No. Thresholds change every series and should only be used as a rough guide for the series they belong to, never to predict future ones.
