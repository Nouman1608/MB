## Section B: Drama extract (original)

*The scene is a kitchen at night. MARA stands at the sink. OLIVER enters with a coat on.*

> MARA: You're dressed.
> OLIVER: I thought I might walk.
> MARA: It's two in the morning.
> *(Pause. She turns the tap off.)*
> OLIVER: That's when it's quiet.
> MARA: And in here?
> OLIVER: Here I can hear the clock.
> *(MARA dries her hands slowly. OLIVER does not take off his coat.)*

**5.** Analyse how the dramatist uses dialogue, pauses and stage directions to show the relationship between the two characters. **[plan only: 4 points with evidence]**

## Section C: Poem (original)

> **Harbour, Late**
> The boats lean into the dark like old arguments,
> each rope pulled tight and then let go.
> Nobody is here to count them.
> The water keeps its own accounts.

**6.** Comment on how the poem uses imagery and structure to create a mood. **[plan only: 4 points with evidence]**

## Section D: Essay planning (apply to your own set texts)

**7.** Choose your drama set text. Write a one-sentence argument, three pieces of evidence and one named dramatic choice for each, in answer to: "In what ways does the dramatist present a character under pressure?"

**8.** Choose your poetry set text. Plan an answer to: "Discuss how the poet uses form to shape meaning in one poem you have studied."

## Guidance (indicative, not a mark scheme)

**1.** 50 marks [1]; Section A 25 and Section B 25 [1].

**2.** AO1, AO2, AO3 and AO4 [1].

**3.** (a) is an essay question with nothing printed [1]; (b) is based on a passage or poem printed on the paper [1].

**4.** Any two: the set text; a dictionary [1 each].

**5.** Possible points: short exchanges and stichomythia-like rhythm suggest a strained, controlled relationship; MARA's questions are met with oblique answers; the pause after "two in the morning" slows the scene; OLIVER's coat stays on, showing he is half-departed; MARA's slow drying of her hands suggests restraint; the clock detail suggests tension. Credit analysis that names a choice and its effect.

**6.** Possible points: simile ("like old arguments") ties the boats to conflict; the rope "pulled tight and then let go" suggests tension released; the empty harbour ("nobody is here") produces loneliness; the final line personifies the water as someone keeping accounts; the short four-line form is compressed and ends on a note of impersonal record. Credit any supported reading.

**7.** and **8.** Self-marked: check that the argument answers the question, each piece of evidence is specific, each choice is named and its effect explained.
