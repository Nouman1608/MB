These questions cover **4.2.4 Energy transfers** (4.2.4.1 to 4.2.4.3) of AQA GCSE Physics (8463), assessed on
Paper 1 at both tiers. Recall every equation: none of the four is given on the equation sheet.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Write the equation that links power, potential difference and current. **[1]**

**2.** A 230 V toaster draws a current of 4.0 A. Calculate its power. **[2]**

**3.** A current of 2.0 A flows through a 6.0 Ω resistor. Calculate the power transferred. **[2]**

**4.** A 40 W lamp is switched on for 30 minutes. Calculate the energy transferred. **[3]**

**5.** A charge of 1200 C flows through a 3.0 V motor. Calculate the energy transferred. **[2]**

**6.** Describe the useful energy transfer when a battery-powered toy car is running. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A kettle is rated 2.2 kW, 230 V.

**(a)** Calculate the current through the kettle. **[2]**
**(b)** The kettle takes 2.5 minutes to boil some water. Calculate the energy transferred. **[3]**
**(c)** A second kettle is rated 1.1 kW. Explain how long it would take to transfer the same energy, assuming no extra losses. **[2]**

**8.** A 12 V car headlamp draws 4.5 A.

**(a)** Calculate the power of the headlamp. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the charge that flows in 10 minutes. **[3]**
**(c)** Use your answer to (b) to calculate the energy transferred in 10 minutes. **[2]**

**9.** A heating element has a resistance of 30 Ω.

**(a)** Calculate the power when the current is 2.0 A. **[2]**
**(b)** A fault makes the current 3.0 A. Calculate the new power and state the factor by which it has increased. **[3]**

**10.** An electric heater transfers 5.4 MJ in 30 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the power of the heater. **[3]**
**(b)** The heater runs on 230 V. Calculate the current. **[2]**

**11.** A power station supplies 100 MW to the National Grid. The transmission cables have a total resistance of 4.0 Ω.

**(a)** Calculate the current in the cables if the power is sent at 25 kV. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the power wasted heating the cables at this pd. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the power wasted if the power is sent at 400 kV instead. **[3]**
**(d)** Explain why step-down transformers are used before the electricity reaches homes. **[1]**

**12.** Explain why the National Grid is an efficient way to transfer energy from power stations to consumers. Refer to transformers, current and the heating of cables. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** power = potential difference × current (P = VI) [1]

**2.** P = V I = 230 × 4.0 [1]; P = **920 W** [1]

**3.** P = I² R = 2.0² × 6.0 [1]; P = **24 W** [1]

**4.** t = 30 × 60 = 1800 s [1]; E = P t = 40 × 1800 [1]; E = **72 000 J** (72 kJ) [1]

**5.** E = Q V = 1200 × 3.0 [1]; E = **3600 J** [1]

**6.** Energy is transferred from the chemical store of the battery [1]; to the kinetic energy of the motor (and car) [1]

**7. (a)** I = P ÷ V = 2200 ÷ 230 [1]; I = **9.6 A** (2 s.f.) [1]
**(b)** t = 2.5 × 60 = 150 s [1]; E = 2200 × 150 [1]; E = **330 000 J** (330 kJ) [1]
**(c)** It has half the power, so it transfers half as much energy each second [1]; it would take twice as long: 300 s, or 5.0 minutes [1]

**8. (a)** P = 12 × 4.5 [1]; P = **54 W** [1]
**(b)** t = 10 × 60 = 600 s [1]; Q = I t = 4.5 × 600 [1]; Q = **2700 C** [1]
**(c)** E = Q V = 2700 × 12 [1]; E = **32 400 J** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A check with E = Pt gives 54 × 600 = 32 400 J, the same answer.

**9. (a)** P = 2.0² × 30 [1]; P = **120 W** [1]
**(b)** P = 3.0² × 30 = 9.0 × 30 [1]; P = **270 W** [1]; the power has increased by a factor of 270 ÷ 120 = 2.25, because (3.0 ÷ 2.0)² = 2.25 [1]

**10. (a)** E = 5 400 000 J and t = 1800 s [1]; P = E ÷ t = 5 400 000 ÷ 1800 [1]; P = **3000 W** (3.0 kW) [1]
**(b)** I = 3000 ÷ 230 [1]; I = **13 A** (2 s.f.) [1]

**11. (a)** I = P ÷ V = 100 000 000 ÷ 25 000 [1]; I = **4000 A** [1]
**(b)** P = I² R = 4000² × 4.0 [1]; P = **64 000 000 W** (64 MW) [1]
**(c)** I = 100 000 000 ÷ 400 000 = 250 A [1]; P = 250² × 4.0 [1]; P = **250 000 W** (0.25 MW) [1]
**(d)** To decrease the pd to a much lower value that is safe for domestic use [1]
*Examiner insight:* (b) and (c) show why low-pd transmission is not used: at 25 kV most of the power would be wasted, while at 400 kV the loss is a quarter of one per cent.

**12.** Step-up transformers increase the pd for transmission [1]; for the same power, a higher pd means a smaller current in the cables (P = VI) [1]; the heating of the cables depends on the current (P = I²R), so a smaller current heats them much less [1]; less energy is wasted, so more of the energy reaches consumers; step-down transformers then reduce the pd for use in homes [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving time in minutes in E = Pt or Q = It.
- Squaring the resistance instead of the current.
- Writing 2.2 kW as 2.2 W.
- Saying step-up transformers increase the current.
- Explaining efficiency without the link from smaller current to less heating.
