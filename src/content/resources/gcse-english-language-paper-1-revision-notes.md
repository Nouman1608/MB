## Paper 1 at a glance

- One unseen literature fiction extract (19th, 20th or 21st century prose).
- Reading questions move from short, contained tasks to a longer evaluative response.
- One creative writing task, linked in theme to the reading source.
- Reading and writing are equally weighted within the paper.

## Reading question ladder

| Question type | What it rewards |
|---|---|
| Identify/select | Picking out explicit information or ideas directly from the text |
| Language analysis | Explaining the effect of specific word choices, imagery and techniques |
| Structure analysis | Explaining how the writer has shaped and sequenced the whole extract |
| Evaluate | Judging how successfully the writer achieves an effect, using the text as evidence |

## Reading technique

- Quote **precisely** — a single well-chosen word or short phrase beats a long, imprecise quotation.
- Always name the **technique** (metaphor, verb choice, short sentence) before explaining its effect.
- For structure questions, track the extract's **shape**: opening focus, shifts, zooming in/out, and the ending.
- For evaluation, keep returning to the specific statement or focus given in the question — don't drift into general praise.

## Creative writing

- The task is linked to the reading extract's theme, but is your own original piece — not a continuation of the extract.
- Plan before writing: a clear structure (opening hook, development, turning point, ending) outperforms a technically fluent piece with no shape.
- A varied range of sentence structures and precise vocabulary is rewarded more than length.
- Leave time to check punctuation and spelling — technical accuracy is assessed as part of writing.

## Exam traps

- Retelling the plot of the extract instead of analysing language/structure.
- Naming a technique without explaining its effect ("this is a metaphor" with no "because…").
- Writing a continuation of the source text instead of an original creative response.
- Running out of time on the writing task because too long was spent on reading questions.

## Self-test

1. What are the two components tested in Paper 1?
2. Why should you always name a technique before explaining its effect?
3. What is the risk of treating the creative writing task as "continue this story"?
4. Besides content, what else is credited in the writing task?

**Answers:** 1. Reading (an unseen fiction extract) and creative writing (an original piece linked in theme to the extract). 2. Because analysis marks are for the *effect* the technique creates, not simply for spotting that a technique is present — naming it is only the starting point. 3. The task asks for an original piece inspired by the theme, not a literal continuation of the source text, so writing a continuation can miss what the task is actually asking for. 4. Technical accuracy — spelling, punctuation and grammar — alongside a varied, purposeful use of vocabulary and sentence structures.
