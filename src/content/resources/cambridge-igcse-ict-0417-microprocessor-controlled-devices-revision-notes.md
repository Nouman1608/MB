These notes condense subtopic 5.1 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417, the effects of using microprocessor-controlled devices. For full explanations and an annotated model answer, use the [microprocessor-controlled devices study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-microprocessor-controlled-devices/), then test yourself with the [microprocessor-controlled devices practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-microprocessor-controlled-devices-practice/).

## Syllabus coverage

| Setting | What you must weigh |
|---|---|
| Home | Lifestyle, leisure time, physical fitness, security of data, degree of social interaction |
| Transport | Security of data, autonomous vehicles, transport safety |

In both settings you need **positive and negative** effects of microprocessors and smart devices in **monitoring and controlling**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Microprocessor | A small processor inside a device that runs a stored program to read inputs and control outputs |
| Smart device | A microprocessor-controlled device connected to a network, so it can be monitored and controlled remotely |
| Sensor | An input device that measures a physical quantity such as temperature, light or movement |
| Actuator | An output device, such as a motor, valve or heater, that the microprocessor switches on or off |
| Monitoring | Collecting and reporting data without acting on it |
| Controlling | Changing an output automatically in response to data |
| Autonomous vehicle | A vehicle whose steering, braking and acceleration are controlled by a computer system using sensors, cameras and GPS |
| Labour-saving device | A device that does a household chore, such as a dishwasher or robot vacuum cleaner |

## The home: quick recall

| Dimension | Positive effect | Negative effect |
|---|---|---|
| Lifestyle | Chores done automatically; devices such as ovens and heating controlled remotely by app | Dependence on devices; more convenience food; costly repairs |
| Leisure time | Time freed from chores for hobbies and family | Freed time often spent on screens |
| Physical fitness | Fitness trackers encourage exercise | Less walking and standing as devices do tasks; risk of weight gain |
| Security of data | Smart alarms and cameras protect the home and alert the owner | Hackers may view cameras, learn when the home is empty or steal account details |
| Social interaction | Video calls keep families in contact | More time alone at home; fewer shared tasks |

Memory hook for the five home dimensions: **L**ifestyle, **L**eisure, **F**itness, **D**ata, **S**ocial.

### Why connection matters

- A **stand-alone** device saves effort but has little data risk.
- A **connected** device adds remote control and data collection, and also adds the risk of unauthorised access.
- Risks rise with weak or default passwords and with software that is never updated.

## Transport: quick recall

**Where microprocessors are used:** engine management, anti-lock brakes, cruise control, parking sensors, airbags, satellite navigation, smart traffic lights, variable speed-limit signs, train signalling, driverless metro trains, aircraft autopilot.

| Heading | Positive | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| Security of data | Location data helps find stolen cars and guide emergency services; fault data helps garages | Journeys can be tracked if data is intercepted; connected systems may be hacked |
| Autonomous vehicles | Fewer crashes from tiredness, distraction or alcohol; efficient driving saves fuel; independence for people who cannot drive | Expensive; sensors can fail in bad weather or poor road markings; hacking; unclear responsibility after a crash; driver job losses |
| Transport safety | Systems react faster than humans; smart lights reduce queues | Over-reliance by drivers; one software fault can affect many vehicles |

## How to build a top answer

1. Name the **device**.
2. Explain the **mechanism**: what it does that changes behaviour.
3. State the **effect** on the dimension named in the question.
4. Give the **other side**.
5. In discuss or evaluate questions, end with a short **judgement**.

Example chain: robot vacuum cleaner, so no one pushes a vacuum around the house, so less light exercise, so fitness may fall; but the saved time can be used to exercise.

## Exam traps

- Answering about transport when the question is about the home, or the other way round.
- Writing "lazy" or "unhealthy" without the device and the activity it removes.
- Claiming a non-networked device can be hacked remotely.
- Listing only advantages of autonomous vehicles.
- Using "security" to mean only burglar alarms; the syllabus heading is **security of data**.
- Repeating one point under two dimensions, for example "saves time" under both lifestyle and leisure without a different effect for each.

## Self-test

1. Define a smart device.
2. State the difference between monitoring and controlling.
3. Name the five home dimensions in the syllabus.
4. Give one positive effect of smart devices on leisure time.
5. Explain one negative effect of a robot vacuum cleaner on physical fitness.
6. Explain why a smart camera raises a data security issue.
7. Give two ways a householder can reduce the data risk of smart devices.
8. Name three microprocessor-controlled systems found in a car.
9. Give two positive effects of autonomous vehicles.
10. Give two negative effects of autonomous vehicles.
11. Explain one way microprocessors improve transport safety.
12. State one risk of drivers relying on safety systems.

### Answers

1. A microprocessor-controlled device connected to a network so it can be monitored and controlled remotely.
2. Monitoring collects and reports data; controlling changes an output automatically in response to the data.
3. Lifestyle, leisure time, physical fitness, security of data, degree of social interaction.
4. Chores are done automatically, so there is more free time for hobbies or family.
5. It does the vacuuming, so people walk around the house less, losing light exercise.
6. It sends live video over the internet, so a hacker who gains access to the account could watch the home or see when it is empty.
7. Any two: change default passwords to strong ones; update the device software; use a secure home network.
8. Any three: engine management, anti-lock braking, cruise control, parking sensors, airbag control, satellite navigation.
9. Any two: fewer accidents caused by human error; more efficient driving; independent travel for people who cannot drive.
10. Any two: high cost; sensor failure in bad weather; hacking; unclear responsibility after a crash; job losses for drivers.
11. Automatic emergency braking reacts faster than a human driver, so collisions are avoided or less severe.
12. Drivers may stop paying attention, so they cannot react if the system fails.
