These questions cover sub-section 3.7.2 Gravitational fields of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408, AS and A-level exams June 2016 onwards): Newton's law, field strength, potential, and orbits of planets and satellites. It is A-level only and assessed in Paper 2.

Revise from the [gravitational fields study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-gravitational-fields-aqa-alevel/) and [gravitational fields revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-gravitational-fields-aqa-alevel-revision-notes/) first. Mixed section 3.7 questions are in the [Fields and their consequences practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-fields-and-their-consequences-practice/).

Data: G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻²; Earth mass 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg; Earth radius 6.37 × 10⁶ m; g at Earth's surface = 9.81 N kg⁻¹. Planet X (invented): mass 3.2 × 10²³ kg, radius 2.4 × 10⁶ m.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State Newton's law of gravitation. **[2]**

**2.** Estimate the gravitational force between two cars, each of mass 1200 kg, whose centres of mass are 5.0 m apart. **[2]**

**3.** State what is meant by an equipotential surface and the work done moving a mass along one. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why a satellite moved to a higher circular orbit has more total energy but less kinetic energy. **[3]**

**5.** Satellite A orbits a planet at radius r; satellite B orbits the same planet at radius 4r. Calculate the ratio of B's period to A's period. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** This question is about Planet X.

**(a)** Calculate the gravitational field strength at its surface. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the gravitational potential at its surface. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the escape velocity from its surface. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the minimum energy needed for a 15 kg probe to escape from the surface. **[1]**

**7.** A 500 kg satellite orbits Planet X at a height of 600 km above its surface.

**(a)** Calculate its orbital speed. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate its orbital period. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate its kinetic energy, potential energy and total energy. **[3]**

**8.** This question is about Earth's gravitational field.

**(a)** Calculate the gravitational potential difference between Earth's surface and a point at a distance 2R from Earth's centre, where R is Earth's radius. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the work done lifting an 800 kg satellite from the surface to this point. **[1]**
**(c)** A student calculates mgh with g = 9.81 N kg⁻¹ and h = R. Explain why this gives the wrong answer and state whether it is too large or too small. **[2]**

**9.** A student plots lg(T/s) against lg(r/m) for several moons of a planet, where T is orbital period and r is orbital radius. The line has a gradient of 1.50 and passes through lg r = 8.00, lg T = 4.90.

**(a)** Explain why the gradient is 1.50. **[2]**
**(b)** Use the point given to calculate the mass of the planet. **[3]**

**10.** A planet of mass 4.0 × 10²⁴ kg rotates once every 30 hours.

**(a)** Calculate the radius of its synchronous orbit. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why a synchronous satellite must also orbit in the equatorial plane, moving in the direction of rotation, to stay above one point on the surface. **[1]**

**11.** Derive the relationship between orbital period T and orbital radius r for a satellite in a circular orbit of a planet of mass M. **[3]**

**12.** **(a)** Show that the escape velocity from the surface of a planet is √2 times the speed of a satellite in a circular orbit just above the surface. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why escape velocity does not depend on the mass of the escaping object. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Every pair of point masses attracts with a force proportional to the product of their masses [1] and inversely proportional to the square of their separation, F = Gm₁m₂/r² [1].

**2.** F = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 1200 × 1200/5.0² [1] = **4 × 10⁻⁶ N** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Treating cars as point masses is an approximation; for an estimate one significant figure is acceptable.

**3.** A surface on which the gravitational potential is the same everywhere [1]; no work is done moving a mass along it [1].

**4.** E_k = GMm/2r, so E_k decreases as r increases [1]; E_p = −GMm/r becomes less negative, increasing by twice the fall in E_k [1]; so E_total = −GMm/2r increases (becomes less negative) [1].

**5.** T ∝ r^(3/2) [1]; ratio = 4^(3/2) = **8** [1]

**6. (a)** g = GM/R² = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 3.2 × 10²³/(2.4 × 10⁶)² [1] = **3.7 N kg⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** V = −GM/R = **−8.9 × 10⁶ J kg⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** v = √(2GM/R) = √(2 × 8.89 × 10⁶) [1] = **4.2 × 10³ m s⁻¹** [1]
**(d)** E = m|V| = 15 × 8.89 × 10⁶ = **1.3 × 10⁸ J** [1]

**7. (a)** r = 2.4 × 10⁶ + 6.0 × 10⁵ = 3.0 × 10⁶ m [1]; v = √(GM/r) = **2.7 × 10³ m s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** T = 2πr/v = 2π × 3.0 × 10⁶/2667 [1] = **7.1 × 10³ s** (about 2.0 hours) [1]
**(c)** E_k = GMm/2r = **1.8 × 10⁹ J** [1]; E_p = −GMm/r = **−3.6 × 10⁹ J** [1]; E_total = **−1.8 × 10⁹ J** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Using 600 km as r is the most common error; always add the radius.

**8. (a)** ΔV = GM(1/R − 1/2R) = GM/2R [1] = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 5.97 × 10²⁴/(2 × 6.37 × 10⁶) = **3.1 × 10⁷ J kg⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** ΔW = mΔV = 800 × 3.13 × 10⁷ = **2.5 × 10¹⁰ J** [1]
**(c)** g decreases with height (to a quarter at 2R), so assuming a constant 9.81 N kg⁻¹ is wrong [1]; mgh = 5.0 × 10¹⁰ J, which is **too large** (twice the true value) [1].

**9. (a)** T² = (4π²/GM)r³, so lg T = 1.5 lg r + constant [1]; a graph of lg T against lg r is a straight line of gradient 3/2 [1].
**(b)** r = 1.00 × 10⁸ m, T = 10^4.90 = 7.94 × 10⁴ s [1]; M = 4π²r³/(GT²) [1] = 4π² × 10²⁴/(6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × (7.94 × 10⁴)²) = **9.4 × 10²⁵ kg** [1]

**10. (a)** T = 30 × 3600 = 1.08 × 10⁵ s [1]; r³ = GMT²/4π² [1]; r = **4.3 × 10⁷ m** [1]
**(b)** Only an equatorial orbit in the direction of rotation keeps the satellite over the same point; any other plane makes it drift north and south of that point during each orbit [1].

**11.** Gravitational force provides the centripetal force: GMm/r² = mv²/r [1]; substitute v = 2πr/T to get GM/r = 4π²r²/T² [1]; so T² = (4π²/GM)r³, that is T² ∝ r³ [1].

**12. (a)** Orbit just above surface: v_orb² = GM/R [1]; escape: ½v_esc² = GM/R, so v_esc² = 2GM/R = 2v_orb² and v_esc = √2 v_orb [1]
**(b)** Both kinetic energy and the energy needed to escape are proportional to m, so m cancels [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out the planet's radius when a height is given.
- Converting hours or days to seconds incorrectly.
- Mixing up which quantities increase with orbital radius.
- Using mgh far from the surface.
- Leaving out a line of the T² ∝ r³ derivation.
