Cambridge O Level Economics (2281) is not tiered and has two externally assessed papers. Paper 1 Multiple Choice lasts 45 minutes, carries 30 marks and is worth 30%, with all 30 questions compulsory. Paper 2 Structured Questions lasts 2 hours 15 minutes, carries 90 marks and is worth 70%, with one compulsory question plus three from a choice of four. This page turns that structure into a plan, using topic pages such as [costs, revenue and objectives of firms](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-costs-revenue-and-objectives-of-firms/), [economic growth and recession](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-economic-growth-and-recession/) and [the current account of the balance of payments](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-current-account-of-the-balance-of-payments/).

## Paper 1: Multiple Choice

Paper 1 is 45 minutes for 30 marks, so 45 / 30 = 1.5 minutes per question. Because every question is compulsory and the paper is short, it samples broadly across the six syllabus areas: the basic economic problem, the allocation of resources, microeconomic decision makers, government and the macroeconomy, economic development, and international trade and globalisation.

Preparation should combine definitions with application. Many multiple choice questions give a small scenario, a table or a diagram of supply and demand and ask what would happen next. Practise sketching a quick diagram on the question paper to test each option. Eliminate options you can prove wrong, then choose between the remainder. Avoid spending more than two minutes on any one question in the first pass.

## Paper 2: Structured Questions

Paper 2 is the heavyweight: 2 hours 15 minutes, 90 marks and 70% of the qualification. According to the verified record, there is one compulsory question plus three questions chosen from four. You therefore answer four questions in total and leave one unanswered.

That choice should be made deliberately. Spend the first few minutes scanning all the optional questions, mark the three where you can answer the largest number of parts with confidence, and begin with whichever feels most secure. Do not choose on the opening part alone: read through to the final, longest part, because the highest-mark parts usually require analysis or evaluation.

Structured questions typically move from definitions and short explanations to application and judgement. Build answers as a chain: make a point, explain why using an economic idea, then link to the context or consequence. For evaluation parts, consider more than one effect, short-term and long-term effects, or different groups affected, and finish with a justified conclusion.

## Timing and marks per minute

- Paper 1: 45 / 30 = 1.5 minutes per mark.
- Paper 2: 135 / 90 = 1.5 minutes per mark.

The pace is identical on both papers. The weightings are 30% + 70% = 100%, so Paper 2 carries 70 / 30 = about 2.3 times the weight of Paper 1. For Paper 2, a part worth 6 marks deserves about 9 minutes, and a 2-mark definition about 3 minutes. The record does not state how marks are distributed between questions, so read the mark totals printed on the actual paper and divide your time accordingly.

## Command words and question styles

Define needs a precise meaning. Identify or state needs a brief point. Explain needs a reason and a link. Analyse means break an issue into parts and trace causes and effects. Discuss and evaluate need arguments on more than one side and a conclusion. Calculate needs working. Use data given means quote figures from the source. Economic diagrams should be labelled fully: axes, curves, original and new equilibrium.

## Worked practice scenario

The price of a good rises from 8 to 10 and the quantity demanded falls from 400 to 340 units per week. (a) Calculate price elasticity of demand. (b) Describe what the value shows. (c) Explain what happens to total revenue.

**Model answer.**

(a) Percentage change in price = (10 - 8) / 8 x 100 = 25%.
Percentage change in quantity demanded = (340 - 400) / 400 x 100 = -15%.
Price elasticity of demand = -15% / 25% = -0.6.

(b) The value has magnitude 0.6, which is less than 1, so demand is price inelastic: quantity demanded falls proportionately less than the price rises.

(c) Revenue before = 8 x 400 = 3200. Revenue after = 10 x 340 = 3400. Total revenue rises by 200, which is consistent with inelastic demand, because the price rise outweighs the fall in sales. A firm facing inelastic demand may therefore gain revenue from a price increase, although it should consider whether customers have substitutes in the longer run.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Check the series and edition of the syllabus for your examination year.
- Learn the definitions of the key terms in all six topic areas.
- Practise drawing supply and demand diagrams quickly and accurately.
- Rehearse at least two evaluation structures you can use flexibly.
- Time Paper 1 at 1.5 minutes per question.
- In Paper 2, choose your three optional questions in the first five minutes.
- Show every step in calculations and give units or percentages.
- Leave a few minutes at the end for review.

## Self-test

1. How many questions must be answered in Paper 2?
2. What is the weighting of Paper 2?
3. How many minutes per mark does Paper 2 allow?
4. In the worked scenario, is demand elastic or inelastic?
5. Why should you scan all four optional questions before choosing?

**Answers:** 1. Four: the compulsory question plus three from four. 2. 70%. 3. 135 / 90 = 1.5 minutes per mark. 4. Inelastic, because the absolute value 0.6 is below 1. 5. Because the best choice depends on the full question, especially the final high-mark parts, not only the opening part.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge O Level Economics 2281?**
Two: Paper 1 Multiple Choice and Paper 2 Structured Questions. Both are compulsory and externally assessed.

**How long and how many marks is each paper?**
Paper 1 is 45 minutes and 30 marks (30%), and Paper 2 is 2 hours 15 minutes and 90 marks (70%).

**How is Paper 2 structured?**
It has one compulsory question and three questions chosen from a choice of four.

**Is the subject tiered?**
No. It is not tiered, and the grade range is A* to E.

**Which edition does this page refer to?**
The record models the single-year 2026 syllabus edition. Cambridge has also published a 2027-2029 edition, which this page does not describe, so check the syllabus for your examination year.
