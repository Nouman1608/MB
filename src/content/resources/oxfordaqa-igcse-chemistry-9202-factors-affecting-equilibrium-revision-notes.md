## Core statements

- **3.8.2 a:** in a **closed system**, equilibrium is reached when forward and backward reactions go at **exactly the same rate**.
- **3.8.2 b:** the **relative amounts** of substances at equilibrium depend on the conditions.
- **3.8.2 c:** higher temperature → more yield from the **endothermic** reaction; lower temperature → more yield from the **exothermic** reaction.
- **3.8.2 d:** higher pressure → the reaction producing **fewer** gas molecules; lower pressure → the reaction producing **more**.
- **3.8.2 e:** yield and rate together decide **optimum industrial conditions**, as in the Haber process.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Reversible reaction | Products can react to re-form the reactants; shown by ⇌ |
| Closed system | Nothing can enter or leave the reaction vessel |
| Equilibrium | Forward and backward rates equal; amounts constant |
| Dynamic | Both reactions are still happening |
| Yield | Amount of a particular product in the equilibrium mixture |
| Exothermic direction | The direction that transfers energy to the surroundings |
| Endothermic direction | The direction that takes in energy from the surroundings |
| Optimum conditions | Conditions giving the best balance of yield, rate and cost |

## How equilibrium is reached

| Stage | Forward rate | Backward rate | Amounts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Start (reactants only) | High | Zero | Changing |
| Approaching | Falling | Rising | Changing |
| Equilibrium | Equal to backward | Equal to forward | Constant |

## Temperature: four-line method

1. Find which direction is exothermic (the question tells you).
2. The other direction is endothermic.
3. Heating favours endothermic; cooling favours exothermic.
4. Name the substance whose amount increases.

Example: 2NO₂(g) ⇌ N₂O₄(g), forward exothermic. Heating → more brown NO₂. Cooling → more colourless N₂O₄.

## Pressure: count gas molecules

| Reaction | Left | Right | Higher pressure gives |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2NO₂(g) ⇌ N₂O₄(g) | 2 | 1 | more N₂O₄ |
| C₂H₄(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ C₂H₅OH(g) | 2 | 1 | more ethanol |
| 2HI(g) ⇌ H₂(g) + I₂(g) | 2 | 2 | no change in yield |
| CO(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ CO₂(g) + H₂(g) | 2 | 2 | no change in yield |

Use the coefficients in the balanced equation. Count molecules, not atoms.

## Reading data

- Yield falls as temperature rises → **forward exothermic**.
- Yield rises as temperature rises → **forward endothermic**.
- Yield rises with pressure → product side has **fewer** gas molecules.
- Yield falls with pressure → product side has **more** gas molecules.
- Yield unchanged by pressure → **equal** numbers of gas molecules.

## Yield, rate and cost

| Change | Yield of exothermic product | Rate | Cost/safety |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lower temperature | Higher | Slower | Lower energy cost |
| Higher temperature | Lower | Faster | Higher energy cost |
| Higher pressure (fewer molecules on product side) | Higher | Faster | Stronger equipment, more energy, more risk |
| Add catalyst | No change | Faster both ways | Catalyst cost; lets a lower temperature be used |

A **compromise** temperature balances yield against rate.

## Exam traps

- "The reaction stops at equilibrium": it does not.
- "Equal amounts at equilibrium": amounts are constant, not equal.
- Forgetting to check which direction is exothermic before applying the temperature rule.
- Counting atoms, or including a solid or liquid, in the pressure count.
- "A catalyst shifts the equilibrium": it only speeds up reaching it.
- "Pressure has no effect" for equal molecules: it has no effect on **yield**, but it still increases **rate**.
- Answering a data question without quoting data.

## Quick self-test

1. What two conditions define equilibrium?
2. Why must the system be closed?
3. A forward reaction is endothermic. What happens to its yield when the temperature is lowered?
4. 2NO₂(g) ⇌ N₂O₄(g), forward exothermic. Predict the colour change when the mixture is cooled.
5. How many gas molecules are on each side of N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g)?
6. For C₂H₄(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ C₂H₅OH(g), what does increasing pressure do to the yield of ethanol?
7. Why does pressure not change the yield in 2HI(g) ⇌ H₂(g) + I₂(g)?
8. Yield of a product falls when the temperature rises. Is the forward reaction exothermic or endothermic?
9. What effect does a catalyst have on the yield at equilibrium?
10. Why are high temperatures used even when the forward reaction is exothermic?
11. Give two disadvantages of very high pressure in industry.
12. A student says that increasing the pressure has no effect at all on 2HI(g) ⇌ H₂(g) + I₂(g). Correct the statement.

### Answers

1. Forward and backward reactions at the same rate; in a closed system.
2. If a substance escapes, the reverse reaction cannot keep pace, so equilibrium cannot be reached.
3. It decreases.
4. It becomes paler, as more colourless N₂O₄ forms.
5. 4 on the left, 2 on the right.
6. Increases it (2 molecules → 1).
7. There are 2 gas molecules on each side.
8. Exothermic.
9. None; equilibrium is reached faster.
10. A low temperature would make the reaction too slow; a compromise gives a reasonable yield quickly.
11. Expensive, strong equipment; high energy cost for compressing gases; greater safety risk (any two).
12. It has no effect on the yield, but it does increase the rate, so equilibrium is reached sooner.
