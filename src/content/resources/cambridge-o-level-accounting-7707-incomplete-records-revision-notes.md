These notes condense section **5.6 Incomplete records** of the Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus for 2027 to 2029. The section covers why businesses do not keep full double entry records, the advantages and disadvantages, "opening and closing statements of affairs", profit from "changes in capital over time", missing sales, purchases, receivables and payables, preparing the statements, and "mark-up, margin and inventory turnover". The syllabus limits questions to sole trader businesses, not service or manufacturing businesses. The two-method worked example is in the [incomplete records study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-incomplete-records/); test yourself with the [incomplete records practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-incomplete-records-practice/). Figures are invented and in dollars.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Incomplete records | Accounting records that do not use the full double entry system |
| Statement of affairs | A list of a trader's assets and liabilities at a date, with capital as the difference |
| Opening capital | Assets less liabilities at the start of the period |
| Capital introduced | New money or assets the owner puts into the business during the period |
| Drawings | Cash or goods the owner takes out for private use |
| Credit sales | Sales where the customer pays later, found from receipts and changes in trade receivables |
| Mark-up | Gross profit as a percentage of cost of sales |
| Margin | Gross profit as a percentage of revenue |
| Rate of inventory turnover | Cost of sales divided by average inventory |

## Why and why not

- **Why traders skip full records**: time, cost of a book-keeper, lack of skill, a small simple business.
- **Advantages**: quicker, cheaper, simple.
- **Disadvantages**: no trial balance to find errors; profit known only later or estimated; theft and inventory losses may go unseen; weak control of receivables and payables; lenders and tax authorities may question the figures.

## The formulas

**Profit for the year = closing capital - opening capital + drawings - capital introduced.** A negative answer is a loss.

**Credit sales = receipts from credit customers - opening trade receivables + closing trade receivables.**

**Credit purchases = payments to credit suppliers - opening trade payables + closing trade payables.**

**Total sales = credit sales + cash sales.** Cash sales include any cash spent before banking if the question says so.

**Expense for the year = paid - owing at start + owing at end + prepaid at start - prepaid at end.**

**Depreciation (no additions or disposals) = opening book value - closing book value.**

**Missing cash drawings = opening cash + cash takings - cash banked - cash payments - closing cash.** Use the full cash takings, not the amount banked, as cash sales.

## Mark-up and margin

| Mark-up | Margin | Revenue : cost : gross profit |
|---|---|---|
| 20% | 16.67% | 120 : 100 : 20 |
| 25% | 20% | 125 : 100 : 25 |
| 33.33% | 25% | 133.33 : 100 : 33.33 |
| 50% | 33.33% | 150 : 100 : 50 |
| 100% | 50% | 200 : 100 : 100 |

- From revenue with a mark-up: cost of sales = revenue x 100 / (100 + mark-up).
- From revenue with a margin: gross profit = revenue x margin.
- Missing closing inventory = opening inventory + purchases - cost of sales.
- Average inventory = cost of sales / rate of inventory turnover.

## Exam traps

- Drawings are **added** and capital introduced is **deducted** in the profit formula.
- Include every asset and liability in each statement of affairs, including accruals and bank overdrafts.
- Cash received from customers is not the sales figure until receivables are adjusted.
- Goods taken by the owner are drawings and are deducted from purchases.
- Mark-up is on cost; margin is on revenue. Write the 100-based ratio before calculating.
- Check the profit from the capital method against the rebuilt statement of profit or loss when both are possible.
- A missing figure in a cash or bank summary is usually drawings; check the question before assuming it.
- Questions are set on sole traders only, so there is no share capital or appropriation account.

## Self-test

1. What is a statement of affairs?
2. Opening capital $30,000; closing capital $34,500; drawings $15,200; capital introduced $3,000. Calculate the profit.
3. Opening capital $18,000; closing capital $16,400; drawings $9,000. Calculate the profit.
4. Receipts from credit customers $48,300; opening trade receivables $5,100; closing trade receivables $4,600. Calculate credit sales.
5. Payments to suppliers $36,900; opening trade payables $3,200; closing trade payables $4,100. Calculate purchases.
6. Cost of sales is $64,000 and the mark-up is 25%. Calculate gross profit and revenue.
7. Revenue is $90,000 and the margin is 30%. Calculate gross profit and cost of sales.
8. Convert a mark-up of 50% into a margin.
9. Cost of sales is $48,000 and the rate of inventory turnover is 6 times. Calculate average inventory.
10. State two disadvantages of not keeping a full set of accounting records.
11. A trader took goods costing $400 for her own use. State how this is treated.
12. Explain why drawings are added back when calculating profit from changes in capital.

### Answers

1. A list of the assets and liabilities of a business at a stated date, with capital found as assets less liabilities.
2. 34,500 - 30,000 + 15,200 - 3,000 = **$16,700**.
3. 16,400 - 18,000 + 9,000 = **$7,400**.
4. 48,300 - 5,100 + 4,600 = **$47,800**.
5. 36,900 - 3,200 + 4,100 = **$37,800**.
6. Gross profit = 64,000 x 25% = **$16,000**; revenue = **$80,000**.
7. Gross profit = 90,000 x 30% = **$27,000**; cost of sales = **$63,000**.
8. Gross profit 50 on revenue 150 = **33.33%**.
9. 48,000 / 6 = **$8,000**.
10. Any two: no trial balance to check accuracy; profit cannot be found easily; losses of inventory or cash may go unnoticed; poor control of receivables and payables; lenders or tax authorities may not accept the figures.
11. Add $400 to drawings and deduct $400 from purchases.
12. Drawings reduced closing capital, but they are not a cost of running the business, so they must be added back to find the profit earned.
