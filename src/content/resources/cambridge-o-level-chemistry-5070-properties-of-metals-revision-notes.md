Condensed recall for Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 subtopic **9.1 Properties of metals**. Full explanations and worked examples are in the [Properties of metals study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-properties-of-metals/).

## The two outcomes

- **Compare** metals with non-metals: thermal conductivity, electrical conductivity, malleability and ductility, melting and boiling points.
- **Describe** reactions of metals with dilute acids, cold water and steam, and oxygen.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Malleable | Can be hammered or pressed into shape without breaking |
| Ductile | Can be drawn out into wires |
| Brittle | Breaks or shatters when hit (typical of solid non-metals) |
| Thermal conductivity | Ability to transfer heat |
| Electrical conductivity | Ability to carry an electric current |
| Delocalised electrons | Outer electrons free to move through the whole metal lattice |
| Metallic bonding | Electrostatic attraction between positive ions and the sea of delocalised electrons |
| Effervescence | Fizzing caused by a gas being produced |

## Comparison table with reasons

| Property | Metals | Non-metals | Reason (metals) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Electrical conductivity | Good | Poor (graphite conducts) | Delocalised electrons move and carry charge |
| Thermal conductivity | Good | Poor | Mobile electrons transfer energy |
| Malleable / ductile | Yes | Brittle | Layers of ions slide; bonding kept by electron sea |
| Melting / boiling point | Usually high | Usually low | Strong attraction throughout giant lattice |

**Exceptions:** mercury (liquid metal), sodium and potassium (low melting points), graphite (conducting non-metal), diamond (non-metal with very high melting point).

## Reactions: products at a glance

| Reacting with | General products | Example equation |
|---|---|---|
| Dilute acid | Salt + hydrogen | Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂ |
| Cold water | Hydroxide + hydrogen | Ca + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂ |
| Steam | Oxide + hydrogen | Mg + H₂O → MgO + H₂ |
| Oxygen | Oxide | 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO |

## Which metals react with what

| Metal | Cold water | Steam | Dilute HCl | Oxygen (heated) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Potassium, sodium | Vigorous | — (too dangerous) | Violent (not done) | Burns |
| Calcium | Steady fizzing | — | Vigorous | Burns |
| Magnesium | Very slow | Fast, white glow | Fast fizzing | Bright white flame |
| Zinc | No | Reacts when heated | Steady fizzing | Forms oxide |
| Iron | No (rusts slowly) | Reacts when heated | Slow fizzing | Sparks (iron wool) |
| Copper | No | No | No | Black coating of CuO |
| Silver, gold | No | No | No | No |

## Salt formulae from metal + acid

| Metal (ion) | With HCl | With H₂SO₄ |
|---|---|---|
| Magnesium (Mg²⁺) | MgCl₂ | MgSO₄ |
| Zinc (Zn²⁺) | ZnCl₂ | ZnSO₄ |
| Iron (Fe²⁺ with dilute acid) | FeCl₂ | FeSO₄ |
| Calcium (Ca²⁺) | CaCl₂ | CaSO₄ (sparingly soluble, so the reaction soon slows) |

Balance with care: a 2+ metal needs **2HCl** but only **one H₂SO₄**.

## Writing a comparison sentence

Weak: "Metals are malleable."
Strong: "Metals are malleable, whereas solid non-metals are brittle and shatter when hit."

The strong version names both groups and uses a linking word. Repeat the pattern for each property the question asks for.

## Observations to quote

- **Acid:** fizzing; metal disappears; solution warms; gas gives squeaky pop with a lighted splint.
- **Cold water (Ca):** fizzing; calcium sinks then may rise; solution turns cloudy (calcium hydroxide is only slightly soluble).
- **Steam (Mg):** magnesium glows brightly; white solid forms; hydrogen can be lit at the end of the tube.
- **Oxygen:** flame colour (Mg bright white); colour of the product (MgO white, CuO black).

## Exam traps

- **Compare** needs both metals and non-metals in the answer.
- Metal + acid gives **hydrogen**, not water.
- Cold water → **hydroxide**; steam → **oxide**.
- Copper, silver and gold do not react with dilute hydrochloric acid.
- "Electrons move" earns less than "delocalised electrons move through the structure".
- Do not classify an element as a metal from conductivity alone (graphite).
- Name the salt correctly: chloride from hydrochloric acid, sulfate from sulfuric acid.
- Metal oxides are basic; non-metal oxides are generally acidic.

## Self-test

1. Define ductile.
2. Compare the electrical conductivity of metals and non-metals.
3. Explain why metals conduct electricity.
4. Explain why metals are malleable.
5. Name a non-metal that conducts electricity.
6. Give the products of zinc with dilute sulfuric acid.
7. Write a balanced equation for magnesium with steam.
8. Name the product when calcium reacts with cold water.
9. Describe what is seen when copper is heated in air.
10. Describe the test for the gas made when a metal reacts with an acid.
11. Why do most metals have high melting points?
12. Name a metal that does not react with dilute hydrochloric acid.

**Answers:** 1. Can be drawn into wires. 2. Metals are good conductors, whereas most non-metals are poor conductors. 3. They have delocalised electrons that move through the structure and carry charge. 4. The layers of ions can slide over each other while the delocalised electrons keep holding them together. 5. Graphite. 6. Zinc sulfate and hydrogen. 7. Mg + H₂O → MgO + H₂. 8. Calcium hydroxide (and hydrogen). 9. It does not burn; the surface turns black (copper(II) oxide). 10. A lighted splint gives a squeaky pop. 11. Strong electrostatic attraction between positive ions and delocalised electrons throughout the giant lattice needs a lot of energy to overcome. 12. Copper (or silver or gold).
