## The big idea

- Constant speed round a circle still means the velocity is changing, because its direction changes.
- A changing velocity is an acceleration. It points to the centre.
- Newton's second law then needs a resultant force towards the centre: the centripetal force.
- You will not be asked to derive a = v²/r. Learn the result and use it.

## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Radian | Angle at the centre when the arc is as long as the radius; θ = s/r | rad |
| Period T | Time for one complete revolution | s |
| Frequency f | Revolutions per second; f = 1/T | Hz |
| Angular speed ω | Angle turned per unit time | rad s⁻¹ |
| Centripetal acceleration | Acceleration directed towards the centre | m s⁻² |
| Centripetal force | Resultant force towards the centre | N |

## Formulas

| Quantity | Equation | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| Angular speed | ω = v/r = 2πf = 2π/T | Linking speed, radius, frequency and period |
| Linear speed | v = ωr | Finding rim speed from ω |
| Centripetal acceleration | a = v²/r = ω²r | v is given (first form) or f, T, ω is given (second form) |
| Centripetal force | F = mv²/r = mω²r | F is the resultant towards the centre |
| Full turn | 360° = 2π rad | Converting angles |

## Method in steps

1. Draw the real forces on the object. Do not draw a centripetal or centrifugal arrow.
2. Pick the direction towards the centre. Add the forces along it (towards the centre positive).
3. Convert revolutions per minute to revolutions per second, then to ω with ω = 2πf. Convert any angles to radians.
4. Write resultant force = mv²/r or mω²r, whichever fits the data.
5. Rearrange before substituting. Give the answer with units and sensible significant figures.
6. Check by finding the other form of a, or the force from the other equation. They should agree.

## What supplies the force

| Situation | Provider of centripetal force |
|---|---|
| Mass on a string, smooth surface | Tension |
| Object on a spinning disc | Friction |
| Car on a flat bend | Sideways grip of the tyres (friction) |
| Orbiting satellite | Gravitational attraction |
| Rider held against a rotating cylinder wall | Push of the wall (normal contact force) |

## Worked reminders

**Convert and find speed.** A disc turns at 33 revolutions per minute and a point lies 0.15 m from the axis. Then f = 33/60 = 0.55 Hz, ω = 2π × 0.55 = 3.46 rad s⁻¹ and v = 3.456 × 0.15 = 0.52 m s⁻¹.

**Force from speed.** A 0.30 kg object moves at 12 m s⁻¹ on a circle of radius 0.75 m. Then F = 0.30 × 12²/0.75 = **58 N**.

**Rearranging for speed.** The same string can supply at most 24 N to a 1.5 kg mass at radius 0.50 m. The greatest speed is v = √(Fr/m) = √(24 × 0.50/1.5) = √8.0 = 2.8 m s⁻¹.

**Period to force.** A 0.30 kg mass has a period of 0.80 s on a circle of radius 0.55 m. Then ω = 2π/0.80 = 7.85 rad s⁻¹ and F = mω²r = 0.30 × 7.854² × 0.55 = **10 N**.

**Hanging-mass rig.** A mass on a smooth table is held on a string over a pulley by a hanging mass of 0.25 kg. At steady motion the tension is Mg = 0.25 × 9.81 = 2.45 N, and that tension is the centripetal force on the table mass.

**Orbit.** A 600 kg satellite has ω = 1.2 × 10⁻³ rad s⁻¹ at a radius of 7.0 × 10⁶ m. Then a = ω²r = (1.2 × 10⁻³)² × 7.0 × 10⁶ = 10.08, so a ≈ **10 m s⁻²**, and F = ma ≈ **6.0 × 10³ N**.

**Scaling.** If the speed doubles at a fixed radius, the force required rises by a factor of four. If the radius doubles at a fixed speed, the force required halves.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Speed and velocity.** Speed is constant in this unit. Velocity is not.
- **Linear speed and angular speed.** v is in m s⁻¹ and changes with radius. ω is in rad s⁻¹ and is the same for every point on a rigid rotating object.
- **Frequency and angular speed.** f counts revolutions per second. ω = 2πf counts radians per second.
- **Centripetal force and the real forces.** Centripetal force is the resultant of the real forces, taken towards the centre. It does not add to them.
- **Circular path and straight tangent.** If the force stops, the object moves off at constant velocity along the tangent. It does not spiral out radially.
- **Force and work.** The centripetal force acts at 90° to the velocity, so it transfers no energy and the speed stays constant.
- **v²/r and ω²r.** Same quantity, different data. Use whichever you were given.

## Graph reminders

- F against v² for a fixed m and r: straight line through the origin, gradient m/r.
- F against ω² for a fixed m and r: straight line through the origin, gradient mr.
- F against 1/T² for a fixed m and r: straight line through the origin, gradient 4π²mr.
- To reduce the percentage uncertainty in a period, time a large number of revolutions and divide.

## Quick self-test

1. Convert 240° to radians. Give the answer in terms of π and as a decimal to 3 significant figures.
2. Convert 1.8 rad to degrees, to 3 significant figures.
3. A turntable rotates at a frequency of 1.25 Hz. Find its period and angular speed.
4. A point moves at 6.0 m s⁻¹ in a circle of radius 1.5 m. Find its centripetal acceleration.
5. A 0.30 kg ball is on a string of length 0.75 m, travelling at 12 m s⁻¹ in a horizontal circle on a smooth surface. Find the tension.
6. A wheel turns at 10 rad s⁻¹ and a point on the rim is 0.25 m from the axis. Find the frequency, and the speed of the point.
7. A 2.0 kg object travels in a circle of radius 0.30 m at 5.0 rad s⁻¹. Find the resultant force on it.
8. State the direction of the acceleration of an object moving at constant speed in a circle.
9. Explain why the centripetal force transfers no energy to the object.
10. A satellite moves in a circular orbit. What provides its centripetal force?
11. The speed of an object on a circle is doubled and the radius is unchanged. By what factor does the force needed change?
12. A student plots F against ω² for a bung of fixed mass on a string of fixed length and finds the gradient is 0.027 kg m. State what the gradient represents.

### Answers

1. 240 × π/180 = 4π/3 = **4.19 rad**
2. 1.8 × 180/π = **103°**
3. T = 1/1.25 = **0.80 s**; ω = 2π × 1.25 = **7.85 rad s⁻¹**
4. a = 6.0²/1.5 = **24 m s⁻²**
5. T = 0.30 × 12²/0.75 = **58 N** (57.6 N)
6. f = ω/2π = 10/2π = **1.59 Hz**; v = ωr = 10 × 0.25 = **2.5 m s⁻¹**
7. F = mω²r = 2.0 × 5.0² × 0.30 = **15 N**
8. Towards the centre of the circle, perpendicular to the velocity.
9. The force is always at right angles to the displacement along the path, so the work done is zero and the kinetic energy and speed are unchanged.
10. The gravitational attraction towards the body it orbits.
11. F is proportional to v², so it increases by a factor of **4**.
12. The gradient equals mr, the product of the mass and the radius. Dividing by the radius gives the mass.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing the unit of ω as Hz or s⁻¹ with no mention of radians. Say rad s⁻¹.
- Forgetting the 2π when converting frequency to angular speed.
- Using revolutions per minute directly in a formula without converting.
- Using the diameter as the radius.
- Giving "centrifugal force" as the explanation of why an object moves outward. In an exam answer, use the object's own inertia and the real inward force.
- Writing "the speed changes" when explaining why there is an acceleration. The velocity changes; the speed does not.
- Naming the centripetal force as the provider of the motion, instead of naming tension, friction, a normal force or gravity.
- Forgetting to take the square root when rearranging F = mv²/r for v.
- Quoting an unrounded value, or too many significant figures, when the data have two.
