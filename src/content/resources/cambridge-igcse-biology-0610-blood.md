This guide covers sub-topic **9.4 Blood** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028, the last part of topic 9, Transport in animals. Outcomes 1 to 4 are Core. Outcomes 5 to 7 (lymphocytes, phagocytes and the clotting process) are **Extended only**.

The [transport in animals study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-transport-in-animals/) gives the components table. This page goes further: how to identify each cell in a photomicrograph, where each substance in plasma is carried from and to, how to work with cell counts, and how to explain clotting in full. Afterwards, use the [blood revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-blood-revision-notes/) and the [blood practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-blood-practice/).

## 9.4.1 The four components

Blood has a liquid part, **plasma**, and three kinds of cell or cell fragment carried in it: **red blood cells**, **white blood cells** and **platelets**. A "list" question wants exactly these four names.

## 9.4.2 and 9.4.5 Identifying blood cells

| Feature | Red blood cell | White blood cell | Platelet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | **none** | **present**, often large and stained dark | none; a cell fragment |
| Relative size | medium | larger than a red cell | much smaller than either |
| Number in a field of view | very many | few | scattered small dots |
| Appearance | disc, paler in the centre | variable shape; stained nucleus stands out | tiny irregular fragments |

**Extended only.** Two types of white blood cell:

| | Lymphocyte | Phagocyte |
|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | large and round, fills most of the cell | lobed (several connected lobes) |
| Cytoplasm | a thin rim | plenty, surrounding the nucleus |
| Function | **antibody production** | **engulfing pathogens by phagocytosis** |

**Identification routine:** first ask "is there a nucleus?" No nucleus and many of them: red cells. Nucleus present: white cell. Then (Extended) look at the nucleus shape: round and filling the cell means lymphocyte; lobed means phagocyte.

### Worked example 1: a cell count

A student counts the cells in one field of view of a stained blood smear. (Example data written for this guide.)

| Cell | Number counted |
|---|---|
| Red blood cells | 480 |
| White blood cells | 2 |

**Calculate the ratio of red to white blood cells.** 480 ÷ 2 = 240, so the ratio is **240 : 1**.

**Suggest why the student should count several fields of view.** White cells are few, so one field may contain an unusually high or low number by chance; a mean of several fields gives a more representative result.

## 9.4.3 Functions of the components

### Red blood cells

Red blood cells **transport oxygen**. They contain **haemoglobin**, which combines with oxygen where oxygen is plentiful, in the lungs, and releases it where oxygen is scarce, in respiring tissues. Haemoglobin contains iron, which is why iron is needed in the diet.

### White blood cells

White blood cells defend the body against pathogens by **phagocytosis** and **antibody production**. These two functions belong to different white cells, as the Extended outcome makes clear. The defence itself is developed in [diseases and immunity](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-diseases-and-immunity/).

### Platelets

Platelets are involved in **clotting**. At Core, the details are not required.

### Plasma

Plasma is the liquid that carries everything else. The syllabus list is: **blood cells, ions, nutrients, urea, hormones and carbon dioxide**. Knowing where each one is carried from and to helps you answer application questions.

| Substance in plasma | Carried from | Carried to |
|---|---|---|
| Nutrients such as glucose and amino acids | small intestine (absorbed) | all body cells |
| Urea | liver, where it is made | kidneys, where it is excreted |
| Carbon dioxide | respiring cells | lungs |
| Hormones | endocrine glands | target organs |
| Ions | small intestine (absorbed) | cells throughout the body; excess removed by the kidneys |
| Blood cells | carried in the plasma everywhere blood flows | |

Oxygen is **not** on the plasma list. It is carried by haemoglobin in red blood cells.

## 9.4.4 and 9.4.7 Clotting

### The roles of clotting (Core)

When a blood vessel is cut, a clot seals the wound. It has two roles:

1. **Prevents blood loss.**
2. **Prevents the entry of pathogens** through the broken skin.

### The process of clotting (Extended only)

1. A blood vessel is damaged.
2. The soluble plasma protein **fibrinogen** is converted into insoluble **fibrin**.
3. Fibrin forms a **mesh** of fibres across the wound.
4. The mesh traps blood cells, forming a clot that seals the wound.

The syllabus limits you to the fibrinogen-to-fibrin conversion and the mesh. Name both proteins and spell them carefully: fibrin**ogen** comes first and is soluble; fibrin is the insoluble product.

### Model answer: clotting

**Question:** Describe the process of blood clotting. [2]

**Answer:** Fibrinogen, a soluble protein in the plasma, is converted to insoluble fibrin (1). Fibrin forms a mesh that traps blood cells, forming a clot (1).

## Worked example 2: applying the functions

A patient has fewer red blood cells than normal. Suggest and explain one symptom.

- Fewer red cells means less haemoglobin, so **less oxygen is carried** to respiring tissues.
- Less aerobic respiration means **less energy is released**, so the patient may feel **tired** or become breathless on exercise.

A good "suggest" answer goes from the component, to its function, to the effect on cells, to the symptom.

## Common mistakes

- Listing "blood" or "water" as a component of blood instead of the four syllabus names.
- Saying red blood cells have a nucleus, or identifying white cells by colour alone instead of by the nucleus.
- Writing that plasma carries oxygen.
- Saying white blood cells "fight disease" with no mention of phagocytosis or antibodies.
- (Extended) Swapping lymphocyte and phagocyte functions: lymphocytes make antibodies; phagocytes engulf.
- (Extended) Saying fibrin is converted into fibrinogen. It is the other way round.
- Giving "stops bleeding" as the only role of clotting. Pathogen entry is the second role.

## Exam technique for 9.4 command words

- **List**: the four component names, nothing more.
- **Identify**: name the cell and, if asked how, give the feature you used ("no nucleus").
- **State** a function: one precise phrase, such as "transports oxygen using haemoglobin".
- **Describe** clotting: the two proteins and the mesh, in order.
- **Suggest**: link a change in a component to its function and then to an effect.
