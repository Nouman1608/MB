## Questions

**1.** State two ways in which the conditions for catalytic cracking differ from those for thermal
cracking. **[2]**

**2.** Alkane P has six carbon atoms in an unbranched chain. Alkane Q has eighteen. Both are present in
a crude oil sample that is fractionally distilled. State which alkane is collected higher up the column,
and explain why. **[3]**

**3.** Heptadecane, C₁₇H₃₆, is cracked.

**(a)** One molecule of heptadecane forms one molecule of an alkane with eleven carbon atoms, one
molecule of ethene and one molecule of another alkene only. Give the equation. **[1]**
**(b)** State which type of cracking a company would choose if it wanted mainly alkenes. **[1]**
**(c)** Name the catalyst used in catalytic cracking and state the two main types of product it is used
to make. **[2]**

**4.** A refinery produces far more heavy gas oil (long-chain alkanes) than its customers buy, and it
cannot make enough fuel for cars. Explain why the refinery cracks some of its heavy gas oil. **[4]**

**5.** Write balanced equations for:

**(a)** the complete combustion of hexadecane, C₁₆H₃₄ **[1]**
**(b)** the incomplete combustion of propane in which carbon monoxide and carbon form in a 1 : 2 mole
ratio, together with water **[1]**
**(c)** the complete combustion of butan-1-ol, C₄H₉OH. **[1]**

**6.** 15 cm³ of a gaseous alkane burns completely in 120 cm³ of oxygen (an excess). After cooling to
room temperature, so that water is liquid, 82.5 cm³ of gas is left. Sodium hydroxide solution absorbs
the carbon dioxide only, leaving 22.5 cm³ of oxygen. All volumes are measured at the same temperature and pressure. Deduce the
molecular formula of the alkane, showing your working. **[4]**

**7.** A gas burner uses ethane, C₂H₆.

**(a)** Write an equation for the complete combustion of ethane. **[1]**
**(b)** What volume of oxygen, in dm³, does complete combustion of 4.50 g of ethane use at 100 kPa
and 290 K? Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[3]**
**(c)** Assume that air is 20% oxygen by volume. Calculate the volume of air needed, in dm³. **[1]**

**8.** Exhaust gases from a petrol engine contain nitrogen monoxide, carbon monoxide, carbon particles and
unburned hydrocarbons.

**(a)** Explain how nitrogen monoxide forms in the engine. Include an equation. **[2]**
**(b)** Over the converter's catalyst, hexane (C₆H₁₄) that escaped burning is turned by nitrogen
monoxide into CO₂, H₂O and N₂ and nothing else. Give the balanced equation. **[1]**
**(c)** State the condition in the cylinder that leads to carbon monoxide and carbon particles. **[1]**
**(d)** Give the equation for the converter reaction that removes CO and NO together. **[1]**

**9.** A fuel sample is contaminated with a sulfur compound, methanethiol, CH₃SH.

**(a)** Write an equation for the complete combustion of methanethiol to carbon dioxide, sulfur dioxide
and water. **[1]**
**(b)** A power station sprays powdered calcium carbonate into its waste gases to cut SO₂ emissions.
Explain why this works, and include an equation. **[3]**

**10.** Methane and chlorine react in ultraviolet light.

**(a)** Name the type of mechanism and explain the role of the ultraviolet light. **[2]**
**(b)** Write an equation for the initiation step. **[1]**
**(c)** Give the pair of propagation steps leading to chloromethane. **[2]**
**(d)** A trace of 1,2-dichloroethane, CH₂ClCH₂Cl, is found in the product. Write an equation for the
step that forms it. **[1]**
**(e)** Suggest how the proportions of the reactants should be chosen to obtain mainly chloromethane.
Explain your answer. **[2]**

**11.** A company cracks icosane, C₂₀H₄₂, which it obtains from a low-value heavy fraction.

**(a)** Each molecule of icosane forms one molecule of dodecane and some ethene only. Write an equation
for this reaction. **[1]**
**(b)** The company uses thermal cracking. State the conditions. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the maximum mass of ethene, in tonnes, that can be made from 1.00 tonne of icosane.
Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[3]**
**(d)** Write an equation for the complete combustion of dodecane, C₁₂H₂₆. **[1]**
**(e)** Give two economic reasons why the company cracks icosane rather than selling it as it is. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Catalytic cracking uses a slight pressure rather than a high pressure [1]. It uses a zeolite
catalyst; thermal cracking uses no catalyst [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Temperature is high in both types, so "a higher temperature" is not a difference
and is unlikely to gain credit.

**2.** **P** is collected higher up [1]. P has a shorter chain, so fewer electrons and less surface
contact, so weaker van der Waals forces between molecules [1]. Less energy is needed to overcome them,
so P has a lower boiling point and condenses in the cooler upper part of the column [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The force must be named as acting between molecules; an answer that refers to
breaking C–C or C–H bonds contradicts the explanation.

**3. (a)** **C₁₇H₃₆ → C₁₁H₂₄ + C₂H₄ + C₄H₈** [1]
**(b)** **Thermal** cracking [1]
**(c)** **Zeolite** [1]; **motor fuels** and **aromatic hydrocarbons** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a), check the leftover fragment: C₄H₈ fits CₙH₂ₙ, so it is an alkene; writing
C₄H₁₀ leaves hydrogen unbalanced and the equation earns nothing.

**4.** Distillation gives a fixed mix of fractions that does not match demand [1]. There is a surplus of
heavy gas oil and a shortage of lighter fractions such as motor fuel [1]. The lighter fractions are in
higher demand, so they are more valuable; cracking converts the surplus into higher-value products [1].
Cracking also produces alkenes, which are valuable feedstocks for polymers and other chemicals [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each point to the refinery described; a bare list of products does not explain
why cracking pays.

**5. (a)** **C₁₆H₃₄ + 24½O₂ → 16CO₂ + 17H₂O** [1]
**(b)** **C₃H₈ + 2½O₂ → CO + 2C + 4H₂O** [1]
**(c)** **C₄H₉OH + 6O₂ → 4CO₂ + 5H₂O** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c), the products hold 13 oxygen atoms but one is already in the alcohol, so 6 O₂
is needed, not 6½; whole-number multiples of a correct equation are equally acceptable.

**6.** Volume of CO₂ = 82.5 − 22.5 = 60.0 cm³ [1]. 15 cm³ of alkane gives 60 cm³ of CO₂, so each
molecule contains 4 carbon atoms [1]. Volume of O₂ used = 120 − 22.5 = 97.5 cm³, so 6.5 mol O₂ react
per mole of alkane [1]. 13 O atoms: 8 go into 4CO₂ and 5 into H₂O, so there are 10 H atoms: **C₄H₁₀**
[1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Taking 120 cm³ as the oxygen used, without subtracting the 22.5 cm³ left, gives the
wrong hydrogen count.

**7. (a)** **C₂H₆ + 3½O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O** [1]
**(b)** n(C₂H₆) = 4.50 ÷ 30.0 = 0.150 mol [1].
n(O₂) = 3.5 × 0.150 = 0.525 mol [1].
V = nRT ÷ p = 0.525 × 8.31 × 290 ÷ 100 000 = 1.265 × 10⁻² m³ = **12.7 dm³** [1].
**(c)** 12.65 ÷ 0.20 = **63.3 dm³** (63.5 dm³ from the rounded 12.7 is also fine) [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Carry the unrounded volume into (c), and convert m³ to dm³ before giving the
answer.

**8. (a)** At the very high temperature in the cylinder, atmospheric N₂ and O₂ combine [1]:
N₂ + O₂ → 2NO [1].
**(b)** **C₆H₁₄ + 19NO → 6CO₂ + 7H₂O + 9½N₂** [1]
**(c)** A **limited supply of oxygen** (incomplete combustion) [1]
**(d)** **2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* For (a), an answer saying the nitrogen comes from the fuel cannot gain credit,
because alkanes contain no nitrogen.

**9. (a)** **CH₃SH + 3O₂ → CO₂ + SO₂ + 2H₂O** [1]
**(b)** SO₂ is an acidic oxide [1]. Calcium carbonate is a base that reacts with acids, so it
neutralises the SO₂, trapping it as calcium sulfite [1]:
**CaCO₃ + SO₂ → CaSO₃ + CO₂** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "It absorbs the gas" names no chemistry; say SO₂ is acidic and the calcium compound
is basic.

**10. (a)** **Free-radical substitution** [1]. UV light supplies the energy to break the Cl–Cl bond
homolytically, forming radicals [1].
**(b)** **Cl₂ → 2Cl•** [1]
**(c)** **CH₄ + Cl• → HCl + •CH₃** [1]; **•CH₃ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + Cl•** [1]
**(d)** **•CH₂Cl + •CH₂Cl → CH₂ClCH₂Cl** [1]
**(e)** Use an excess of methane [1]. Then a chlorine radical is far more likely to collide with a
methane molecule than with chloromethane, so further substitution is reduced [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A propagation step that forms H• and CH₃Cl, or a dot drawn on the wrong atom, does
not match the accepted mechanism even if the atoms balance.

**11. (a)** **C₂₀H₄₂ → C₁₂H₂₆ + 4C₂H₄** [1]
**(b)** High temperature [1] and high pressure [1].
**(c)** Mr(C₂₀H₄₂) = 282.0, so n = 1.00 × 10⁶ ÷ 282.0 = 3546 mol [1].
n(C₂H₄) = 4 × 3546 = 1.418 × 10⁴ mol [1].
Mass = 1.418 × 10⁴ × 28.0 = 3.97 × 10⁵ g = **0.397 tonnes** [1].
**(d)** **C₁₂H₂₆ + 18½O₂ → 12CO₂ + 13H₂O** [1]
**(e)** Heavy fractions such as icosane are in surplus and have low value, while the smaller products
are in higher demand and worth more [1]. Ethene is an alkene, a valuable feedstock for polymers and
other chemicals [1]. (Also accept: dodecane is a shorter alkane better suited to use as a fuel.) **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c), convert tonnes to grams before dividing by Mr, and back to tonnes at the
end.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming temperature as a difference between thermal and catalytic cracking, when both are high.
- Referring to bonds breaking when explaining boiling points in distillation.
- Writing an alkene fragment as CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ in a cracking equation.
- Explaining cracking with a list of products but no link to surplus, demand and value.
- Not subtracting the oxygen already present in an alcohol.
- Forgetting leftover oxygen in gas-volume data, or leaving pressure in kPa.
- Tracing the N in NOₓ to the fuel instead of to air.
- "Absorbs SO₂" with no acid–base reasoning.
- Radical steps with dots in the wrong place, or termination steps that produce radicals.
