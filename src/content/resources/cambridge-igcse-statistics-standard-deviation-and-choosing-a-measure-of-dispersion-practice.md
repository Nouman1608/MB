## Questions

**1.** Five parcels weigh 20, 24, 25, 29 and 32 kg. Calculate the standard deviation of the weights. **[3]**

**2.** For eight values, Σx = 96 and Σx² = 1250. Calculate the standard deviation. **[3]**

**3.** The table shows the number of passengers in each of 40 cars that stopped at a checkpoint.

| Passengers, x | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency, f | 8 | 14 | 10 | 6 | 2 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean number of passengers. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the standard deviation. **[3]**

**4.** The ages of 50 customers at a café are shown.

| Age, x (years) | 10 ≤ x < 20 | 20 ≤ x < 30 | 30 ≤ x < 40 | 40 ≤ x < 50 | 50 ≤ x < 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 6 | 14 | 20 | 8 | 2 |

**(a)** Calculate an estimate of the mean age. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate an estimate of the standard deviation. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why your answers are estimates. **[1]**

**5.** Four numbers are 2, 6, 7 and x. Their mean is 6. Find x, and then calculate the standard deviation of the four numbers. **[4]**

**6.** A stall records the number of customers on seven days: 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11 and 35.

**(a)** Calculate the range and the interquartile range. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the standard deviation. **[3]**
**(c)** The stall owner wants one measure of how much the daily numbers usually vary. Choose a measure and justify your choice. **[2]**

**7.** A table of waiting times, in minutes, has a final class "60 or more". Explain why the range and the standard deviation cannot be calculated from the table, and name one measure of dispersion that could still be estimated. **[3]**

**8.** Two bus routes have the same mean journey time of 18.0 minutes. Route A has a standard deviation of 2.1 minutes and Route B has a standard deviation of 5.6 minutes.

**(a)** Which route has the more consistent journey time? Give a reason. **[2]**
**(b)** State one advantage of using the standard deviation rather than the range to compare the routes. **[2]**

**9.** A student finds the standard deviation of 2, 4, 7 and 11 using Σx = 24 and Σx² = 190. The student's working is: 190/4 − 24/4 = 47.5 − 6 = 41.5, so the standard deviation is √41.5 = 6.44.

**(a)** Identify the error. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the correct standard deviation. **[3]**

**10.** The number of repeat visits made by 25 customers is summarised below. The frequency k is unknown.

| Visits, x | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 4 | 9 | k | 6 | 3 |

The mean number of visits is 2.8.

**(a)** Find k. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the standard deviation. **[4]**
**(c)** A second survey also has a mean of 2.8 visits and a standard deviation of 1.9. Which survey shows the more consistent number of visits? **[1]**

**11.** **(a)** A data set has a standard deviation of zero. What can you say about its values? **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why a standard deviation can never be negative. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Mean = 130 / 5 = 26 [1]. Squared deviations: 36, 4, 1, 9, 36, which sum to 86 [1]. SD = √(86/5) = √17.2 = **4.15 kg** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Writing the sum Σ(x − x̄)² on its own line makes the method visible, which helps if a later slip occurs.

**2.** Mean = 96 / 8 = 12 [1]. Variance = 1250/8 − 12² = 156.25 − 144 = 12.25 [1]. SD = **3.5** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The subtraction in the second formula is the mean of the squares minus the square of the mean. Subtracting 96/8 without squaring it is a frequent slip.

**3. (a)** Σfx = 8 + 28 + 30 + 24 + 10 = 100 [1]. Mean = 100 / 40 = **2.5** [1]
**(b)** Σfx² = 8 + 56 + 90 + 96 + 50 = 300 [1]. Variance = 300/40 − 2.5² = 7.5 − 6.25 = 1.25 [1]. SD = √1.25 = **1.12** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Build the fx and fx² columns in the table. A long single line of arithmetic is where frequency questions usually go wrong.

**4. (a)** Midpoints 15, 25, 35, 45, 55. Σfx = 90 + 350 + 700 + 360 + 110 = 1610 [1]. Mean = 1610 / 50 = **32.2 years** [1]
**(b)** Σfx² = 1350 + 8750 + 24 500 + 16 200 + 6050 = 56 850 [1]. Variance = 56850/50 − 32.2² = 1137 − 1036.84 = 100.16 [1]. SD = **10.0 years** [1]
**(c)** The exact ages are not known, so each person is treated as if they were at the midpoint of their class [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Estimate" in the question means the midpoint method. Using the class boundary or the class width in place of the midpoint changes every later figure.

**5.** Sum of the four numbers = 4 × 6 = 24, so x = 24 − 15 = **9** [1]. Deviations: −4, 0, 1, 3, with squares 16, 0, 1, 9 summing to 26 [1]. Variance = 26/4 = 6.5 [1]. SD = √6.5 = **2.55** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Use the unrounded value 6.5 under the root. State the value of x clearly before you use it in the next step.

**6. (a)** Range = 35 − 4 = **31** [1]. Lower quartile = 6 and upper quartile = 11 [1]. IQR = 11 − 6 = **5** [1]
**(b)** Σx = 80 and Σx² = 16 + 36 + 49 + 64 + 81 + 121 + 1225 = 1592 [1]. Variance = 1592/7 − (80/7)² = 227.43 − 130.61 = 96.82 [1]. SD = **9.84** [1]
**(c)** The **IQR** [1], because the day with 35 customers is far from the others and inflates the range and the standard deviation, whereas the IQR describes the usual spread of 5 [1]
*Examiner insight:* A choice with no reason scores little. Quote the 35 and the figures 31, 9.84 and 5 to show why the IQR is better here.

**7.** The range needs the largest value, and the final class has no upper limit [1]. The standard deviation needs a midpoint for every class, and an open class has no midpoint [1]. The **interquartile range** could still be estimated, provided both quartiles lie in classes with limits [1]
*Examiner insight:* Give both reasons separately. "The class is open" alone does not say what each measure needs.

**8. (a)** **Route A** [1], because its standard deviation is smaller, so its journey times are closer to the mean [1]
**(b)** The standard deviation uses every journey time [1], whereas the range depends on only the longest and the shortest, so one unusual journey could change it a lot [1]
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both figures (2.1 and 5.6) and a comparative word, not only the name of the route.

**9. (a)** The student subtracted Σx/n (6) instead of its square (6² = 36) [1]
**(b)** Mean = 24/4 = 6 [1]. Variance = 47.5 − 36 = 11.5 [1]. SD = √11.5 = **3.39** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Say exactly what was wrong. "Calculation error" is too vague to be credited.

**10. (a)** Σf = 22 + k and Σfx = 4 + 18 + 3k + 24 + 15 = 61 + 3k [1]. 61 + 3k = 2.8(22 + k) = 61.6 + 2.8k [1]. 0.2k = 0.6, so **k = 3** [1]
**(b)** Σfx² = 4 + 36 + 27 + 96 + 75 = 238 [1]. Σf = 25 and Σfx = 70 [1]. Variance = 238/25 − 2.8² = 9.52 − 7.84 = 1.68 [1]. SD = **1.30** [1]
**(c)** The **first survey** [1], because its standard deviation (1.30) is smaller than 1.9.
*Examiner insight:* Form an equation from the mean and solve it. Setting out the equation shows the method, whereas a bare value of k gives an examiner little to credit.

**11. (a)** All the values are the same [1]
**(b)** The standard deviation is the square root of a mean of squared quantities, which cannot be negative [1]
*Examiner insight:* For part (a), "no spread" is a restatement of the question. State the property of the values themselves.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving the variance and not taking the square root.
- Squaring Σx instead of finding (Σx / n)², or subtracting the mean without squaring it.
- Dividing by the number of classes or table entries, not Σf.
- Using class boundaries or class widths instead of midpoints.
- Rounding the mean or variance early.
- Omitting the word "estimate" when working from a grouped table.
- Naming a measure of dispersion with no reason from the context.
- Judging consistency from the means, when the standard deviations are what differ.
- Writing only the final answer where the question asks you to show working.
