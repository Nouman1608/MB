Use these Ar values: H 1.0, C 12.0, N 14.0, O 16.0, Mg 24.3, S 32.1, Cl 35.5, Fe 55.8, Ba 137.3. Take 1 u = 1.66 × 10⁻²⁴ g. ## Section B

**7.** Strontium has four naturally occurring isotopes.

| Isotope | ⁸⁴Sr | ⁸⁶Sr | ⁸⁷Sr | ⁸⁸Sr |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Abundance / % | 0.56 | 9.86 | 7.00 | 82.58 |

**(a)** Calculate the relative atomic mass of strontium to 3 significant figures, using mass numbers as relative isotopic masses. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. Relative isotopic mass refers to one isotope, so no averaging is involved. *(Total: 1 mark)*

**2.** One twelfth of the mass of one atom of carbon-12 [1]. *(Total: 1 mark)*

**3.** The weighted mean mass of a molecule [1], relative to (divided by) one twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom, the unified atomic mass unit [1]. *(Total: 2 marks)*

**4.** 55.8 + 32.1 + 4(16.0) + 7(18.0) = 277.9 [1]. *(Total: 1 mark)*

**5.** Barium chloride is ionic, so it is made of ions and contains no molecules [1]. *(Total: 1 mark)*

**6.** 1.993 × 10⁻²³ ÷ 1.66 × 10⁻²⁴ = 12.0 [1]; carbon-12 [1]. *(Total: 2 marks)*

**7. (a)** (84 × 0.56 + 86 × 9.86 + 87 × 7.00 + 88 × 82.58) ÷ 100 [1] = 87.7 [1].

**(b)** ⁸⁸Sr is by far the most abundant isotope (82.58 %), so it contributes most to the weighted mean [1]. *(Total: 3 marks)*

**8. (a)** Let the fraction of ¹⁹³Ir be y: 191(1 − y) + 193y = 192.2 [1]. 191 + 2y = 192.2, so y = 0.60 [1]. ¹⁹³Ir 60 %, ¹⁹¹Ir 40 % [1].

**(b)** It is a ratio of two masses (the mean atom's mass divided by 1 u), so the units cancel [1]. *(Total: 4 marks)*

**9. (a)** (62.93 × 69.2 + 64.93 × 30.8) ÷ 100 [1] = 63.55 [1].

**(b)** The masses of protons and neutrons are not exactly 1 u, so an atom's mass is not exactly its mass number in u [1].

**(c)** Carbon-12 [1]; the unified atomic mass unit is defined as one twelfth of its mass, so its relative isotopic mass is exactly 12 by definition [1]. *(Total: 5 marks)*

**10. (a)** 24.3 + 32.1 + 64.0 + 7(18.0) = 246.4 [1].

**(b)** 126.0 ÷ 246.4 × 100 [1] = 51.1 % [1].

**(c)** MgSO₄ = 120.4, so water = 192.4 − 120.4 = 72.0 [1]; x = 72.0 ÷ 18.0 = 4 [1]. *(Total: 5 marks)*

**11. (a)** 137.3 + 2(35.5) + 2(18.0) = 244.3 [1].

**(b)** Fraction of water = 36.0 ÷ 244.3 [1]; 10.0 × 36.0 ÷ 244.3 = 1.47 g [1]. *(Total: 3 marks)*

**12. (a)** 180.0 × 1.66 × 10⁻²⁴ = 2.99 × 10⁻²² g [1].

**(b)** It does not say *weighted mean* mass [1]. It compares with carbon-12 as a whole rather than with *one twelfth* of the mass of one carbon-12 atom [1].

**(c)** Glucose is a simple molecular substance made of discrete C₆H₁₂O₆ molecules [1]. SiO₂ is a giant covalent structure with no discrete molecules, so its formula shows only the ratio of atoms, a formula unit [1]. *(Total: 5 marks)*

## Where marks are usually lost

- Definitions without *weighted mean* or *one twelfth*.
- Treating relative isotopic mass as a weighted mean.
- Units on relative masses.
- Forgetting the 7 or 2 water molecules in a hydrate, or adding only one H₂O.
- Not checking that a calculated Ar lies between the isotopic masses.
- Rounding intermediate values, which shifts four-figure answers.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is "weighted mean" in the definition of relative atomic mass?**
Most elements are mixtures of isotopes with different masses and abundances, so the mean must allow for how common each isotope is.

**How do I find an isotope abundance from a relative atomic mass?**
For two isotopes, call one fraction x and the other 1 − x, write the weighted mean equal to Ar and solve for x.
