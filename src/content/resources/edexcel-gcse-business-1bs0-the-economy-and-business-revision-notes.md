These notes condense content item 1.5.4, The economy and business, in Topic 1.5 (Understanding external influences on business) of the Pearson Edexcel GCSE (9-1) Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022. The [economy study guide](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-the-economy-and-business/) has worked examples for each factor; test yourself with the [economy practice questions](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-the-economy-and-business-practice/). The wider [legislation, economy and external influences revision notes](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-legislation-the-economy-and-external-influences-revision-notes/) also cover 1.5.3 and 1.5.5.

## What the specification lists

The impact of the economic climate on businesses: unemployment, changing levels of consumer income, inflation, changes in interest rates, government taxation, changes in exchange rates.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Economic climate | The overall state of the economy: jobs, incomes, prices, borrowing costs |
| Unemployment | People who want to work but cannot find a job |
| Consumer income | The money people have available to spend |
| Inflation | A general rise in prices over time |
| Interest rate | The cost of borrowing and the reward for saving, as a percentage |
| Taxation | Money collected by government from incomes, profits and spending |
| Exchange rate | The price of one currency in terms of another |
| Import | A good or service bought from another country |
| Export | A good or service sold to another country |

## Effects at a glance

| Change | Typical effect on sales | Typical effect on costs | Who may gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unemployment rises | Spending falls, especially on luxuries | Recruitment easier; less wage pressure | Discount and budget sellers |
| Consumer income rises | Spending rises; trading up | Wage pressure may rise | Luxury and premium sellers |
| Inflation rises | Customers' spending power may fall | Materials, energy and wages rise | Businesses able to raise prices without losing customers |
| Interest rates rise | Less spending, especially on credit purchases | Loan and overdraft costs rise | Businesses with savings |
| Taxes on income rise | Consumer income and spending fall | — | Few; budget sellers may gain |
| Taxes on profits rise | — | Less profit left for owners and reinvestment | — |
| Pound weakens | Exports cheaper abroad, so sales abroad rise | Imports dearer | Exporters |
| Pound strengthens | Exports dearer abroad | Imports cheaper | Importers |

## Questions to ask about the business

1. Luxury or essential?
2. Borrower or saver?
3. Importer, exporter, or both?
4. Are wages a large share of costs?

## Calculations

| Task | Method |
|---|---|
| Pounds to foreign currency | £ × rate |
| Foreign currency to pounds | foreign amount ÷ rate |
| Interest for a year | rate × amount borrowed |
| Percentage change | (new − old) ÷ old × 100 |
| Interest on loans (%) | (total repayment − borrowed amount) ÷ borrowed amount × 100 |

Example: £1 = $1.25 falls to £1 = $1.20. A $500 import cost £400 and now costs £416.67.

## Exam traps

- Inflation is prices **rising**, not prices being high.
- A weak pound is good for exporters and bad for importers.
- Interest rates affect customers' spending as well as the business's own loans.
- Rising unemployment can help a business recruit, so it is not all bad.
- Always say which **type** of business is affected and why.

## Chains to learn

- Unemployment rises → incomes fall → spending on luxuries falls → restaurant sales fall.
- Interest rates rise → loan repayments rise → less profit → expansion delayed.
- Inflation rises → materials cost more → profit per unit falls → price rise considered, but rivals may not follow.
- Pound weakens → imports cost more in pounds → costs rise for importers; exports cheaper abroad → sales rise for exporters.
- Income tax rises → take-home pay falls → customers trade down to cheaper products.
- Interest rates fall → borrowing is cheaper → customers buy more on credit and businesses invest → sales of cars and furniture may rise.

## Self-test

1. List the six economic factors in 1.5.4.
2. Define inflation.
3. Give one way rising unemployment could help a business.
4. Why might a restaurant lose more sales than a supermarket when incomes fall?
5. Give two effects of a rise in interest rates on a business with a large loan.
6. Explain how a rise in income tax affects a clothes shop.
7. A UK firm imports goods costing €600. The rate is £1 = €1.25. Calculate the cost in pounds.
8. The rate in question 7 falls to £1 = €1.20. Calculate the new cost in pounds.
9. Does the change in question 8 help or harm the importer?
10. A UK exporter sells a product for £50. At £1 = $1.30, what is the dollar price?
11. A business has an overdraft of £12,000 at 10% a year. Calculate a year's interest.
12. Give one reason a business with savings may gain from higher interest rates.

### Answers

1. Unemployment; changing levels of consumer income; inflation; changes in interest rates; government taxation; changes in exchange rates.
2. A general rise in prices over time.
3. More people apply for vacancies, so recruitment is easier and there is less pressure to raise wages.
4. Eating out is a luxury customers can cut; food shopping is essential.
5. Any two: higher repayments; less profit; less willing to borrow more to expand; customers may also spend less.
6. Customers have less income left after tax, so they may buy fewer clothes or cheaper ones, reducing sales.
7. €600 ÷ 1.25 = £480.
8. €600 ÷ 1.20 = £500.
9. Harms it: the goods now cost £20 more.
10. £50 × 1.30 = $65.
11. 10% × £12,000 = £1,200.
12. Its savings earn more interest, increasing its income.
