## Questions

**1.** **(a)** Explain why enzymes are described as globular proteins. **[2]**
**(b)** State whether each enzyme is intracellular or extracellular: (i) catalase in mung bean cells; (ii) amylase in the small intestine; (iii) DNA polymerase. **[3]**

**2.** For one reaction, the activation energy is 75 kJ mol⁻¹ without an enzyme and 30 kJ mol⁻¹ with the enzyme. The products have 40 kJ mol⁻¹ less energy than the substrates.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage reduction in activation energy. **[2]**
**(b)** State the overall energy change of the catalysed reaction and explain your answer. **[2]**

**3.** Amylase hydrolyses starch but not cellulose, although both are polymers of glucose. Explain why. **[3]**

**4.** Scientists compared the three-dimensional structure of an enzyme with and without its substrate. Without substrate, the active site was more open. With substrate bound, several R groups in the active site had moved closer around the substrate.

**(a)** State which hypothesis of enzyme action these results support, and explain why. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how this change could help the enzyme lower the activation energy. **[2]**

**5.** Catalase extract from sweet potato was added to hydrogen peroxide. Oxygen was collected in a gas syringe.

| Time / s | 0 | 30 | 60 | 90 | 120 | 150 | 180 | 210 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Volume of O₂ / cm³ | 0 | 18 | 31 | 40 | 46 | 49 | 50 | 50 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of oxygen production in the first 30 s. **[2]**
**(b)** A tangent drawn to the curve at t = 0 passes through (0, 0) and (25, 20). Calculate the initial rate. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the mean rate between 90 s and 150 s, and explain why it is lower than the initial rate. **[3]**
**(d)** Explain why no more oxygen is collected after 180 s. **[1]**

**6.** A student followed starch hydrolysis by amylase, sampling the mixture into iodine solution every 15 s. In three repeats, the iodine first stayed orange-brown at 150 s, 165 s and 180 s.

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of reaction as 1/time. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify two limitations of this method and suggest an improvement for each. **[4]**

**7.** Outline how a colorimeter could be used to follow the disappearance of starch in the reaction in Question 6. **[5]**

**8.** Starch solutions of known concentration were mixed with iodine and their absorbance measured. The calibration line was straight: absorbance = 0.060 × starch concentration (g dm⁻³). Samples from an amylase reaction gave absorbances of 0.60 at 0 min, 0.42 at 3 min and 0.27 at 6 min.

**(a)** Calculate the starch concentration at 3 min and at 6 min. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mean rate of starch disappearance from 0 to 3 min and from 3 to 6 min. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the difference between the two rates. **[1]**

**9.** A student used sweet potato discs as a source of catalase.

**(a)** Explain why all the discs were cut to the same size. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the student set up a tube with boiled discs. **[2]**

**10.** Describe and explain how catalase breaks down hydrogen peroxide, from the arrival of a substrate molecule to the release of products. Include enzyme specificity and the induced-fit hypothesis. **[8]**

**11.** Plan an investigation to compare the catalase activity of extracts of mung beans and potato by measuring the rate of formation of product. **[8]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Its polypeptide folds into a **compact, roughly spherical tertiary structure** [1] with hydrophilic R groups mostly on the outside, so it is **soluble** [1].
**(b)** (i) **intracellular** [1]; (ii) **extracellular** [1]; (iii) **intracellular** [1].
*Examiner insight:* One mark per item in (b). Catalase stays intracellular even when you extract it from tissue; classification is by where the enzyme normally acts.

**2. (a)** (75 − 30) ÷ 75 × 100 [1] = **60%** [1].
**(b)** **40 kJ mol⁻¹ released**, the same as without the enzyme [1]; the enzyme changes only the activation energy, not the energy of the substrates or products [1].
*Examiner insight:* A bare 60% earns both marks only if it is correct; show the subtraction so a slip still earns the method mark.

**3.** Amylase's active site has a shape **complementary** to starch, which has **α-1,4 glycosidic bonds** [1]. Cellulose has **β-1,4 glycosidic bonds**, with alternate glucose units inverted, so its shape is different [1]. Cellulose **cannot fit the active site**, so no enzyme–substrate complex forms [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Same shape" instead of "complementary" usually loses the first mark.

**4. (a)** **Induced fit** [1]: the active site changed shape when the substrate bound, so it is not rigid as in lock-and-key [1].
**(b)** R groups moving around the substrate **put strain on bonds** in it [1], so less energy is needed to break them and the reaction proceeds more easily [1].
*Examiner insight:* Say that the **active site** changes shape. Answers saying the substrate changes shape to fit do not gain the mark.

**5. (a)** 18 ÷ 30 [1] = **0.60 cm³ s⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** 20 ÷ 25 [1] = **0.80 cm³ s⁻¹** [1].
**(c)** (49 − 40) ÷ 60 = **0.15 cm³ s⁻¹** [1]. Hydrogen peroxide is being used up, so **substrate concentration falls** [1] and **fewer enzyme–substrate complexes form per second** [1].
**(d)** All the hydrogen peroxide has been broken down; the volume is limited by the amount of substrate, not the enzyme [1].
*Examiner insight:* Units are required for the accuracy marks in (a) to (c). In (b), the gradient must come from the tangent, not from the data table.

**6. (a)** Mean time = (150 + 165 + 180) ÷ 3 = 165 s [1]; rate = 1 ÷ 165 = **0.0061 s⁻¹** (6.1 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹) [1].
**(b)** The end point is only known to within 15 s [1]; sample more often, for example every 5 s [1].
The colour is judged by eye, which is subjective [1]; use a colorimeter, or compare with a colour standard [1].
Also accept: temperature may not be constant; keep tubes in a thermostatically controlled water bath.
*Examiner insight:* Each improvement must match its limitation. "Repeat more" does not fix sampling-interval uncertainty and usually gains no credit.

**7.** Use a **red filter**, complementary to blue-black [1]. **Zero** the colorimeter with a blank of iodine solution and water [1]. Make a **calibration curve** of absorbance against known starch concentrations [1]. Take samples at **set time intervals**, add each to the **same volume of iodine solution** and read the **absorbance** [1]. Convert absorbance to concentration using the curve and **plot concentration against time** [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Measure the colour" is too vague. Name what is read (absorbance) and how it becomes a concentration.

**8. (a)** 0.42 ÷ 0.060 = **7.0 g dm⁻³** [1]; 0.27 ÷ 0.060 = **4.5 g dm⁻³** [1].
**(b)** (10 − 7.0) ÷ 3 = **1.0 g dm⁻³ min⁻¹** [1]; (7.0 − 4.5) ÷ 3 = **0.83 g dm⁻³ min⁻¹** [1].
**(c)** Starch concentration is lower later, so there are fewer enzyme–substrate collisions per minute [1].
*Examiner insight:* Allow error carried forward in (b) from wrong values in (a). The 0 min value (10 g dm⁻³) comes from 0.60 ÷ 0.060.

**9. (a)** Same size gives the **same surface area** [1], so the same amount of enzyme is exposed to hydrogen peroxide [1].
**(b)** Boiling **denatures** catalase [1], showing that oxygen is produced because of the enzyme and not by hydrogen peroxide breaking down on its own [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Fair test" alone earns nothing. Say which variable is controlled and why it affects the result.

**10.** Catalase is a globular protein whose **active site** has a shape **complementary** to hydrogen peroxide [1].
Hydrogen peroxide collides with and binds to the active site, forming an **enzyme–substrate complex** held by temporary bonds [1].
**Induced fit**: the active site **changes shape slightly** as the substrate binds [1], **straining bonds** in the substrate [1].
This **lowers the activation energy** [1].
The products, **water and oxygen**, are released [1]; the enzyme is **unchanged** and can bind another substrate [1].
**Specificity**: other molecules are not complementary to the active site, so cannot form an enzyme–substrate complex [1].
*Examiner insight:* Real mark schemes often list more points than marks, with a maximum. Extended answers that stop at "the substrate fits the active site" cap well below full credit.

**11.** Blend **equal masses** of mung beans and potato with **equal volumes** of buffer, then filter [1].
Use the **same volume and concentration** of hydrogen peroxide and the **same volume** of each extract [1].
Keep **temperature constant** in a water bath [1] and **pH constant** with a buffer [1].
Collect oxygen in a **gas syringe**, reading the volume at regular intervals [1].
Plot volume against time and find the **initial rate** from a tangent at t = 0 [1].
**Repeat** at least three times and calculate means [1].
**Control** with boiled extract [1].
*Examiner insight:* Planning marks need operational detail: "same amount" earns less than "same mass of tissue in the same volume of buffer".

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "same shape" instead of complementary.
- Saying the substrate changes shape in induced fit.
- Claiming an enzyme changes the overall energy change of a reaction.
- Using a mean rate when the question asks for the gradient of a tangent.
- Leaving out units, or quoting 1/time without s⁻¹.
- Pairing a limitation with an improvement that does not address it.
- Saying a colorimeter "measures colour" without naming absorbance, the filter, the blank or the calibration curve.
- Writing "the enzyme is used up" when a reaction stops; the substrate is used up.
