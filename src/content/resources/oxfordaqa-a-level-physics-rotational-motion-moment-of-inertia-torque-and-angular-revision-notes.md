## Key formulas

| Quantity | Equation | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Moment of inertia, point mass | I = mr² | kg m² |
| Moment of inertia, extended object | I = Σmr² | kg m² |
| Rate of turning (angular speed) | ω = Δθ/Δt | rad s⁻¹ |
| Rate of change of ω (angular acceleration) | α = Δω/Δt | rad s⁻² |
| Torque | T = Fr = Iα | N m |
| Angular momentum | Iω | kg m² s⁻¹ |
| Rotational kinetic energy | Ek(rot) = ½Iω² | J |
| Work done by a torque | W = Tθ | J |
| Power of a torque | P = Tω | W |

The specification says that formulae for the moment of inertia of particular solids are supplied when needed. Learn I = mr² and I = Σmr², and practise using a given expression.

Equations for uniform angular acceleration:

```
ω = ω₀ + αt
θ = (ω₀ + ω)t / 2
θ = ω₀t + ½αt²
ω² = ω₀² + 2αθ
```

## Definitions to know

- **Moment of inertia:** a measure of how hard it is to change the rotation of an object about a given axis. It depends on the mass, how the mass is distributed about the axis, and the position of the axis.
- **Angular displacement:** the angle turned through, in radians.
- **Angular speed:** the rate of change of angle. **Angular velocity** is angular speed with a sense of rotation.
- **Angular acceleration:** the rate of change of angular velocity.
- **Torque:** the turning effect of a force, T = Fr, with r measured at right angles from the axis to the line of the force.
- **Conservation of angular momentum:** the total angular momentum of a system is constant if no external resultant torque acts on it.

## Factors that change I (qualitative)

- More mass gives a larger I.
- Mass further from the axis gives a much larger I, because r is squared.
- A different axis gives a different I for the same object.

For the same mass and radius, a hollow ring has a larger I than a solid disc. It is harder to start and harder to stop, so for the same torque it gains angular speed more slowly.

## Method boxes

**Uniform angular acceleration.**
1. Convert to radians and rad s⁻¹ (multiply rev min⁻¹ by 2π/60).
2. Write the symbols you know: ω₀, ω, α, θ, t.
3. Name the symbol you need and the one you are not given.
4. Pick the equation that leaves out the missing one.
5. Give the sign of α: negative if the body is slowing.

**Torque and acceleration.**
1. Find each torque as force times perpendicular distance.
2. Subtract opposing torques to get the resultant.
3. Find I for the correct axis.
4. α = resultant torque / I.
5. Use the uniform-acceleration equations for ω or θ.

**Conservation of angular momentum.**
1. Check that no external torque acts about the axis.
2. Write Iω before = Iω after.
3. Add the moments of inertia of everything that ends up rotating together.
4. Solve for ω. Compare the kinetic energies if asked about energy.

## Small worked reminders

**Net torque.** A wheel with I = 0.080 kg m² is driven by a torque of 0.75 N m against an opposing torque of 0.15 N m.

```
resultant = 0.75 − 0.15 = 0.60 N m
α = 0.60 / 0.080 = 7.5 rad s⁻²
```

**Changing I.** A diver in the open position has I = 3.6 kg m² and turns at 1.5 rad s⁻¹. In the tucked position I = 1.2 kg m².

```
3.6 × 1.5 = 1.2 × ω
ω = 4.5 rad s⁻¹
Ek: 4.05 J → 12.15 J
```

The kinetic energy rises threefold, because ω rises threefold while I falls by the same factor (Ek = L²/2I with L fixed). The extra energy comes from the work done by the diver's muscles in pulling the body in.

**Ring and disc.** A ring and a disc each have mass 0.40 kg and radius 0.25 m. The ring has I = mr² = 0.025 kg m². The disc is given as ½MR² = 0.0125 kg m². The ring has twice the moment of inertia because all its mass is at the rim.

**Work and energy.** A constant torque of 0.80 N m acts through 15 rad on a rotor of I = 0.060 kg m², starting from rest.

```
W = Tθ = 0.80 × 15 = 12 J
½Iω² = 12  →  ω² = 2 × 12 / 0.060 = 400
ω = 20 rad s⁻¹
```

**Power.** A torque of 50 N m turning at 60 rad s⁻¹ gives P = 50 × 60 = 3000 W.

## Must-know distinctions

| Idea | Be clear about |
|---|---|
| Angular speed and angular velocity | The first has size only; the second also has a sense of rotation |
| Torque and moment of inertia | Torque is the cause; I is the resistance to change |
| Applied torque and resultant torque | T = Iα needs the resultant |
| Mass and moment of inertia | Mass is fixed; I changes with the axis and the distribution |
| Conservation of angular momentum and of energy | The first holds with no external torque; kinetic energy may change |
| W = Tθ and Ek(rot) | The first is energy transferred; the second is energy stored |

| Translational | Rotational |

## Quick self-test

1. Find I for a 0.25 kg mass fixed 0.40 m from an axis on a light arm.
2. A point mass of 0.30 kg and a point mass of 0.10 kg are 0.20 m and 0.50 m from an axis. Find I for the pair.
3. Convert 450 revolutions per minute to rad s⁻¹.
4. A wheel accelerates uniformly from 4.0 rad s⁻¹ to 10.0 rad s⁻¹ in 3.0 s. Find α and θ.
5. A turntable starts from rest and has α = 2.5 rad s⁻². Find its angular speed after it has turned through 20 rad.
6. A force of 15 N acts at right angles to a spanner 0.28 m long. Find the torque.
7. A resultant torque of 4.5 N m acts on a body with I = 0.60 kg m². Find α.
8. A rotor with I = 2.0 kg m² turns at 5.0 rad s⁻¹. Find its angular momentum and its kinetic energy.
9. A spinning object has I = 0.40 kg m² and ω = 6.0 rad s⁻¹. Its I is then halved with no external torque. Find the new ω.
10. A shaft transmits 3.6 kW at 120 rad s⁻¹. Find the torque.
11. State the rotational equivalent of mass, force and momentum.
12. Give two reasons why the same rod has different I about different axes.

### Answers

1. I = mr² = 0.25 × 0.40² = 0.040 kg m².
2. I = 0.30 × 0.20² + 0.10 × 0.50² = 0.012 + 0.025 = 0.037 kg m².
3. 450 × 2π / 60 = 47 rad s⁻¹.
4. α = (10.0 − 4.0)/3.0 = 2.0 rad s⁻². θ = (4.0 + 10.0) × 3.0 / 2 = 21 rad.
5. ω² = 0 + 2 × 2.5 × 20 = 100, so ω = 10 rad s⁻¹.
6. T = 15 × 0.28 = 4.2 N m.
7. α = 4.5 / 0.60 = 7.5 rad s⁻².
8. Iω = 2.0 × 5.0 = 10 kg m² s⁻¹. Ek(rot) = ½ × 2.0 × 5.0² = 25 J.
9. Iω is constant: 0.40 × 6.0 = 0.20 × ω, so ω = 12 rad s⁻¹.
10. T = P/ω = 3600 / 120 = 30 N m.
11. Moment of inertia, torque, angular momentum (Iω is the equivalent of mv).
12. The distances of the mass elements from the axis change, and I depends on r², so the sum Σmr² changes. The total mass stays the same.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using rev min⁻¹ or degrees directly, instead of converting to rad s⁻¹ and radians.
- Writing I = mr² with r measured to the wrong point, or forgetting to square r.
- Adding the masses and using one r for an extended object, instead of summing each mr².
- Using the applied torque in T = Iα when a frictional or opposing torque is present.
- Saying angular momentum is conserved without stating that no external resultant torque acts.
- Claiming kinetic energy is conserved when two spinning bodies lock together, or forgetting that the energy has gone to thermal energy.
- Giving a numerical answer to a "describe the effect" question without saying which way the quantity changes and why.
- Omitting units, or mixing N m with J as if they were interchangeable. Torque is in N m; work and energy are in J.
- Using a value of I for one axis when the object turns about another.
