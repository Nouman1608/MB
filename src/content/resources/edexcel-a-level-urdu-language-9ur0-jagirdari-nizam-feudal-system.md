This guide covers **sub-theme 4.2, the feudal system (جاگیرداری نظام)**, of Theme 4 in Pearson Edexcel A Level Urdu (9UR0). The specification names three aspects:

- **پاکستانی دیہاتی معاشرے میں اِس کے سیاسی اثرات**: its political effects in Pakistani rural society
- **دیہاتی علاقوں میں اِس کے سماجی اثرات**: its social effects in rural areas
- **شہری اور دیہاتی علاقوں کی سیاست میں تفرق و مماثلت**: differences and similarities between urban and rural politics

The [feudal system revision notes](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-feudal-system-revision-notes/) explain where the system came from, how tenancy and sharecropping work, and the land reforms of 1959 and 1972. This guide develops the three aspects, especially the third, which asks you to compare and which the revision notes do not cover. Theme 4 is set in Pakistan. The sub-theme is not a research subject, so it appears in translation, reading and Paper 3. Practise with the [feudal system practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-9ur0-jagirdari-nizam-feudal-system-practice/).

## Aspect 1: political effects in rural society

The core idea is that **land is power**. Where a few families own most of the land, economic dependence can become political dependence. Develop these mechanisms:

- **Influence over votes.** Tenants and labourers who depend on a landlord for land, loans, water and work may be expected to vote as he advises. Critics describe this as a vote that is not truly free.
- **Electables.** Pakistani political commentary often uses the term **الیکٹ ایبلز** (electables) for candidates, frequently from landed families, who can win a constituency whichever party they join. Parties court them because of their local base, which can weaken party programmes and ideology.
- **Patronage (سرپرستی).** The landlord acts as an intermediary between villagers and the state: securing a job, a school posting, a road, or help with a case. The phrase **تھانہ کچہری کی سیاست** (police-station and court politics) describes politics centred on such personal favours.
- **Dynasties.** Seats may pass within families from one generation to the next, at local, provincial and national level.
- **Resistance to reform.** Critics argue that landed representatives in assemblies have slowed land reform, agricultural taxation and tenancy protection.

**Balance:** not every landowner behaves this way, and many villages now have more independent voters because of education, migration, mobile phones and media. Also note **برادری** (clan, kinship group) politics: in parts of Punjab especially, clan loyalty can matter as much as land.

## Aspect 2: social effects in rural areas

Social effects concern daily life, status and opportunity:

- **Hierarchy and dignity.** A rigid order of landlord, tenant and labourer can shape who sits where, who speaks first and who is listened to.
- **Education.** Critics argue that some powerful landlords have little interest in schools that might make tenants independent; reports of non-functioning village schools are often discussed in this context. Present this as a claim made by critics, supported by sources, not as a fact about all landlords.
- **Justice outside the courts.** Disputes may be settled by a **جرگہ** or **پنچایت** (traditional councils) presided over by influential men. Supporters value speed and local knowledge; critics point to decisions that ignore the law, particularly in matters involving women.
- **Debt and bonded labour.** Loans from the landlord can tie families to the land for generations, the most serious form being **بندھوا مزدوری** (bonded labour), outlawed in 1992 but still reported.
- **Women.** Rural women's work in fields and with livestock is vital but often unpaid; inheritance of land by women can be resisted where land equals power.
- **Change.** Migration to cities and abroad, remittances, roads, mobile phones and education are gradually loosening these structures.

## Aspect 3: differences and similarities between urban and rural politics

**تفرق** means difference, **مماثلت** similarity. Prepare both: an answer that lists only differences misses half the aspect.

**Differences:**

| Rural politics | Urban politics |
|---|---|
| Personal loyalty to landlords and clans weighs heavily | Issues such as inflation, jobs and services weigh more heavily |
| Voters may depend economically on local notables | Voters are more economically independent |
| Information travels through local networks | Television, newspapers and social media shape opinion |
| Fewer organised associations | Trade unions, professional bodies and student groups are active |

**Similarities:**

- **Patronage exists in both.** In cities, ward-level strongmen and party workers also trade favours for votes; patronage changes form rather than disappearing.
- **Kinship matters in both.** Biradari networks follow migrants into cities.
- **Dynasties in both.** Family succession is found among urban as well as rural politicians.
- **Shared national concerns.** Both care about prices, security, water and the economy; national issues can override local loyalties at certain elections.

**Analytical point:** the line between urban and rural is blurring. Small towns, commuting, migration and phones carry urban political culture into villages, and rural networks into cities.

## Worked example: language for comparison

The third aspect demands comparison, so learn a small toolkit and use each item accurately:

- **جبکہ** (whereas): **دیہات میں ووٹر اکثر مقامی بااثر افراد کی رائے دیکھتے ہیں، جبکہ شہروں میں مہنگائی اور روزگار جیسے مسائل فیصلہ کن ہوتے ہیں۔**
- **کے برعکس** (in contrast to): **دیہی سیاست کے برعکس شہری سیاست میں میڈیا کا کردار زیادہ نمایاں ہے۔**
- **دونوں میں قدرِ مشترک یہ ہے کہ** (what the two have in common is that): **دونوں میں قدرِ مشترک یہ ہے کہ سرپرستی کی سیاست کسی نہ کسی شکل میں موجود ہے۔**
- **جس طرح ... اسی طرح** (just as ... so too): **جس طرح دیہات میں برادری اہم ہے، اسی طرح شہروں کے کئی حلقوں میں بھی برادری ووٹ پر اثر ڈالتی ہے۔**

## Worked example: translating into English

Original passage, written for this guide:

> انتخابات قریب آتے ہی چوہدری صاحب کی حویلی میں رونق بڑھ جاتی ہے۔ گاؤں کے لوگ اپنے مسائل لے کر آتے ہیں: کسی کو بیٹے کی نوکری چاہیے، کسی کو تھانے سے رہائی۔ چوہدری صاحب سب کی بات تحمل سے سنتے ہیں، اور سب جانتے ہیں کہ اس مہربانی کا بدلہ ووٹ کی صورت میں دینا ہوگا۔

**Model translation:** As soon as the elections draw near, Chaudhry Sahib's mansion becomes busier. The village people come with their problems: one needs a job for his son, another needs someone released from the police station. Chaudhry Sahib listens patiently to everyone, and everyone knows that this kindness will have to be repaid in the form of a vote.

**Points to notice:** **قریب آتے ہی** = "as soon as ... draw near"; **رونق بڑھ جاتی ہے** = "becomes busier, livelier"; the repeated **کسی کو ... کسی کو** = "one ... another"; **دینا ہوگا** expresses future obligation, "will have to be repaid". The passage's irony lies in **مہربانی** (kindness): a strong reading answer would notice that the favour is really a transaction.

## Common mistakes

- Treating **جاگیردار** and **زمیندار** as identical.
- Answering the third aspect with differences only.
- Writing that all landlords oppress tenants; examiners reward nuance and evidence.
- Confusing **جرگہ** (council) with a court (**عدالت**).
- Agreement with **سیاست** (feminine): **دیہی سیاست بدل رہی ہے**.

## Exam technique

- Reading texts on this sub-theme often use irony or understatement, as in the passage above. Ask what the writer really thinks, not only what is said.
- For Paper 3 evaluation, weigh views from different positions: a landowner, a tenant, an urban commentator. Ask whose interests each view serves.
- Back claims about effects with a mechanism (dependence, patronage, kinship) rather than general statements.
