These notes cover section 3.1.2.2, Tropical rainforests, of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Geography (9230) specification, Version 4.3. Key ideas: "Tropical rainforest ecosystems have a range of distinctive characteristics"; "Deforestation has economic and environmental impacts"; "Tropical rainforests need to be managed to be sustainable." For full explanations, read the [tropical rainforests study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-geography-9230-tropical-rainforests/). For the whole unit, see the [Living with the Physical Environment study guide](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-geography-living-with-the-physical-environment/).

## Specification checklist

- **Physical characteristics**; **interdependence** of climate, water, soils, plants, animals, people.
- **Adaptations**; **biodiversity** issues.
- **Changing rates** of deforestation.
- **Case study:** causes and impacts of deforestation.
- **Value** to people and the environment.
- **Sustainable management** strategies.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Biodiversity | Variety of living things |
| Emergent | Tallest trees above the canopy |
| Canopy | Roof of treetops |
| Interdependence | Parts relying on each other |
| Leaching | Nutrients washed down through soil |
| Deforestation | Permanent removal of forest |
| Selective logging | Cutting only some mature trees |
| Ecotourism | Low-impact tourism that benefits local people |
| Debt-for-nature swap | Debt reduced in return for protecting forest |

## Characteristics at a glance

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Location | Within about 10° of the equator: Amazon, Congo, South-East Asia |
| Temperature | About 26-28°C all year |
| Rainfall | Over 2 000 mm a year, daily convectional storms |
| Cause of climate | Rising air at the equator (low pressure) |
| Layers | Emergent (40-60 m), canopy, understorey, forest floor |
| Soil | Deep but infertile; nutrients are in the plants, leached by rain |
| Nutrient cycle | Fast: rapid decay, rapid uptake by shallow roots |

## Interdependence

- Heat + rain = year-round growth.
- Plants recycle water (transpiration), so more rain.
- Fast decomposition releases nutrients that plants use at once.
- Canopy and roots protect soil.
- Animals pollinate plants and spread seeds; plants feed and shelter animals.
- Indigenous people use the forest at low impact.
- Remove one part and others suffer.

## Adaptations

| Plant or animal feature | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Drip tips, waxy leaves | Shed heavy rain |
| Buttress roots | Support in shallow soil |
| Lianas, epiphytes | Reach light |
| Emergent height | Reach sunlight |
| Camouflage (sloth) | Avoid predators |
| Warning colours (poison dart frog) | Warn off predators |
| Prehensile tails (monkeys) | Move through canopy |

## Biodiversity

- Small share of land, very large share of species; many found nowhere else.
- Importance: medicines, food crops, resilience, ethical value.
- Threats: habitat loss, fragmentation, hunting, climate change.

## Rates of deforestation

- Vary over time and between places.
- Brazilian Amazon (approximate): about 27 000 km² a year in 2004; under 5 000 km² by 2012; about 13 000 km² in 2021.
- Rates depend on government policy, enforcement, commodity prices and demand.
- Use a date with every figure.

## Case study: the Amazon, Brazil

| Causes | Detail |
|---|---|
| Commercial farming | Cattle ranching (largest cause), soya for export |
| Subsistence farming | Slash and burn plots |
| Logging | Hardwoods such as mahogany; logging tracks give access |
| Road building | Trans-Amazonian highway (1970s) opened the interior |
| Mineral extraction | Carajás iron ore; illegal gold mining |
| Energy development | Dams such as Belo Monte on the Xingu |
| Settlement, population growth | Migration into the forest for land |

| Impacts | Detail |
|---|---|
| Economic development | Jobs and exports, but short-term if the soil is exhausted |
| Soil erosion | Rain washes away unprotected soil; rivers silt up |
| Loss of biodiversity | Habitat destroyed |
| Climate change | Carbon released by burning and rotting; less carbon stored |
| People | Indigenous land and culture lost |

## Value of rainforests

- **People:** timber, food, medicines, jobs, tourism, indigenous homes.
- **Environment:** habitats, carbon store, water recycling, soil protection, influence on rainfall.

## Sustainable management

| Strategy | Advantage | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Selective logging and replanting | Forest cover maintained | Needs enforcement; machines damage forest |
| Conservation and education | Protected areas; people understand the value | Costly to police |
| Ecotourism | Income for local people | Can damage if too many visitors |
| Agreements on tropical hardwoods | Legal, certified timber (for example FSC) | Illegal logging continues |
| Debt reduction | Debt cut for forest protection | Small scale |

## Exam traps

- Tropical rainforest soil is **not** fertile; the nutrients are in the biomass.
- A case-study answer needs the **named place** and details.
- Do not list causes without saying how they lead to clearance.
- Selective logging is not the same as clear-felling.
- Give a limitation as well as a benefit for a management strategy.
- Date every figure and use approximate wording.

## Self-test

1. State the temperature and rainfall range of a tropical rainforest.
2. Why are rainforest soils infertile despite lush growth?
3. Name **two** adaptations of plants to heavy rain.
4. Explain why biodiversity is high in rainforests.
5. Name **three** causes of deforestation in a rainforest you have studied.
6. Explain how deforestation can increase soil erosion.
7. How does deforestation contribute to climate change?
8. What is ecotourism?
9. Give **one** advantage and **one** limitation of selective logging.
10. What is a debt-for-nature swap?

**Answers**

1. About 26-28°C all year; over 2 000 mm of rain a year.
2. Nutrients are quickly taken up by plants and stored in the biomass; rain leaches what is left in the soil.
3. Any two: drip tips; waxy leaves; buttress roots; shallow roots.
4. Constant warmth, moisture and light at the top give year-round growth, creating many habitats and food sources in the layers of the forest.
5. For example (Amazon): cattle ranching; logging; road building; mining; dams.
6. Without trees, rain hits the bare soil directly and roots no longer bind it, so soil is washed away.
7. Burning and rotting trees release carbon dioxide, and there are fewer trees to absorb it.
8. Low-impact tourism that benefits local people and protects the environment.
9. Advantage: the forest keeps its cover and wildlife. Limitation: heavy machinery and tracks still damage the forest, and illegal cutting is hard to control.
10. A country's debt is reduced in return for a promise to protect part of its forest.
