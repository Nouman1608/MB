Cambridge IGCSE History (0470) combines a broad core of nineteenth- or
twentieth-century international history with a detailed depth study of
one country or conflict. It suits students who enjoy explaining why
events happened, arguing about responsibility and working with
historical documents. Every candidate takes three components: Paper 1
Structured Questions (40%), Paper 2 Document Questions (30%), and
either Component 3 Coursework or Paper 4 Alternative to Coursework
(30%). This overview follows the 2024-2026 syllabus, which covers the
June, November and March (India) series.

## How the course is organised

The content falls into two layers: core content and depth studies.

**Core content** is offered as two options, each organised around key
questions rather than a plain list of events.

- **Option A, the nineteenth century: the development of modern nation
  states, 1848–1914**, asks whether the revolutions of 1848 were
  important, how Italy and Germany were unified, why there was a civil
  war in the United States, why nations expanded their overseas
  empires, and what caused the First World War.
- **Option B, the twentieth century: international relations since
  1919**, asks whether the Treaty of Versailles was fair, how far the
  League of Nations succeeded, how far Hitler's foreign policy was to
  blame for war in 1939, who was to blame for the Cold War, how
  effectively the United States contained communism, and how secure
  the USSR's control over Eastern Europe was from 1948 to c.1989.

**Depth studies** look closely at a shorter period:

- Depth Study A: The First World War, 1914–18
- Depth Study B: Germany, 1918–45
- Depth Study C: Russia, 1905–41
- Depth Study D: The United States, 1919–41
- Depth Study E: The Second World War in Europe and the Asia-Pacific,
  1939–c.1945

Your school decides which core option and which depth study you
follow, so confirm both at the start of the course.

## How it's assessed

| Component | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paper 1 | Structured Questions | 2 hours | 60 | 40% |
| Paper 2 | Document Questions | 1 hour 45 minutes | 40 | 30% |
| Component 3 | Coursework | – | 40 | 30% |
| Paper 4 | Alternative to Coursework | 1 hour | 40 | 30% |

All candidates sit Papers 1 and 2. For the final 30% they take
**either** Component 3 **or** Paper 4, never both.

- **Paper 1** asks for answers to two core-content questions and one
  depth-study question, so it draws on both layers of the course.
- **Paper 2** is built around historical documents and tests the
  ability to interpret and evaluate them.
- **Component 3 Coursework** is one piece of extended writing. It is
  internally assessed by the school and externally moderated.
- **Paper 4 Alternative to Coursework** is a written paper assessed
  externally, for centres that do not run coursework.

Cambridge has also published a later 2027-2028 edition of the syllabus.
If your exams fall in 2027 or later, check that edition with your
teacher, as this overview describes the 2024-2026 version.

## How the parts fit together

The core and the depth study are not separate halves. Paper 1 tests
both in one sitting, which means the habit of building a clear,
well-supported explanation has to work equally for a broad core
question and a narrower depth-study question.

There are also natural overlaps in content. A student taking Option A
finishes with the causes of the First World War, which leads directly
into Depth Study A. Option B begins with the peace settlement after
1919, which provides context for Depth Study B on Germany and for the
interwar United States in Depth Study D. Depth Study E builds on the
question of Hitler's foreign policy in Option B. Where the depth study
connects with the core option, knowledge gained in one supports the
other, so it is worth mapping those links early.

Source skills run through the whole course. The document work in
Paper 2 trains you to ask where a source came from, what it was for and
how far it can be trusted. The same critical approach strengthens the
use of evidence in structured answers for Paper 1 and in the extended
writing for Component 3 or Paper 4.

## Planning your study

A balanced two-year plan might run as follows:

- **Early in the course:** begin with the core option, working through
  its key questions in order. Each one gives practice in explaining
  causes and consequences and reaching a judgement.
- **Middle of the course:** start the depth study, linking it back to
  the core where the periods overlap. Introduce document work alongside
  content from the start rather than leaving it until the end.
- **Coursework or its alternative:** if your school runs Component 3,
  your teacher will set the timetable for the extended writing. If you
  are taking Paper 4, build in regular practice on its question style.
- **Final months:** consolidate content and move to timed writing.

Once you have covered the whole of the core option and depth study,
start full past papers, and use the exam-preparation page for advice on
timing and approach for each component.

## Frequently asked questions

**What papers are in Cambridge IGCSE History 0470?**
All candidates take Paper 1 Structured Questions (40%) and Paper 2 Document Questions (30%). The final 30% is either Component 3 Coursework or Paper 4 Alternative to Coursework.

**Is there coursework in IGCSE History 0470?**
There can be. Component 3 is one piece of extended writing, internally assessed and externally moderated. Schools that do not offer it enter candidates for Paper 4 Alternative to Coursework instead.

**How long is IGCSE History Paper 1?**
Paper 1 lasts 2 hours and is worth 60 marks. Candidates answer two core-content questions and one depth-study question.

**How many depth studies are there in 0470?**
There are five, labelled A to E: the First World War, Germany 1918–45, Russia 1905–41, the United States 1919–41 and the Second World War in Europe and the Asia-Pacific.

**Which exam series is IGCSE History 0470 available in?**
The 2024-2026 syllabus is examined in the June, November and March (India) series.
