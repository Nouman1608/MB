**Syllabus:** Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, **AS Level** subtopic 18.2
Esters: production by condensation of an alcohol and a carboxylic acid with concentrated H₂SO₄; hydrolysis
by dilute acid and by dilute alkali and heat.

## Naming in three steps

1. Find the **C=O**.
2. The C=O carbon and every carbon on its side make the **acid part**: name it **-oate**.
3. The group on the other side of the single-bonded O is the **alcohol part**: name it **-yl**, and say
   it **first**.

| Ester | Made from | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| methyl ethanoate | ethanoic acid + methanol | CH₃COOCH₃ |
| ethyl methanoate | methanoic acid + ethanol | HCOOCH₂CH₃ |
| propyl butanoate | butanoic acid + propan-1-ol | CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₂CH₂CH₃ |
| pentyl ethanoate | ethanoic acid + pentan-1-ol | CH₃COO(CH₂)₄CH₃ |
| methyl hexanoate | hexanoic acid + methanol | CH₃(CH₂)₄COOCH₃ |

Syllabus limit for naming: up to **six carbons in each part**, **straight chains only**.

## Esterification

- **Reagents:** carboxylic acid + alcohol.
- **Conditions:** heat (under reflux) with **concentrated H₂SO₄** as catalyst.
- **Type:** condensation (water eliminated); **reversible**.
- **Yield:** limited by equilibrium; an excess of one reactant improves it.
- **Water** is formed from the acid's –OH and the alcohol's –OH hydrogen.

General equation: RCOOH + R′OH ⇌ RCOOR′ + H₂O

## Hydrolysis compared

| | With dilute acid | With dilute alkali |
|---|---|---|
| Conditions | heat under reflux with dilute HCl or H₂SO₄ | heat under reflux with dilute NaOH(aq) |
| Role of reagent | catalyst | reactant |
| Reversible? | yes | no: goes to completion |
| Products | RCOOH + R′OH | RCOO⁻Na⁺ + R′OH |
| To get RCOOH | nothing more | acidify with a strong acid (e.g. HCl) |

Examples with ethyl propanoate:

    CH₃CH₂COOCH₂CH₃ + H₂O ⇌ CH₃CH₂COOH + CH₃CH₂OH      (dilute acid, heat)
    CH₃CH₂COOCH₂CH₃ + NaOH → CH₃CH₂COONa + CH₃CH₂OH     (dilute alkali, heat)

## Ester isomers to recognise

| Molecular formula | Straight-chain esters | Acid isomer |
|---|---|---|
| C₂H₄O₂ | methyl methanoate | ethanoic acid |
| C₃H₆O₂ | methyl ethanoate, ethyl methanoate | propanoic acid |
| C₄H₈O₂ | methyl propanoate, ethyl ethanoate, propyl methanoate | butanoic acid |

Ester and carboxylic acid with the same formula are **functional group isomers**. Only the acid fizzes
with sodium carbonate.

## Equation-writing drill

| Acid + alcohol | Ester formed | Equation |
|---|---|---|
| methanoic acid + methanol | methyl methanoate | HCOOH + CH₃OH ⇌ HCOOCH₃ + H₂O |
| ethanoic acid + propan-1-ol | propyl ethanoate | CH₃COOH + C₃H₇OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₃H₇ + H₂O |
| propanoic acid + ethanol | ethyl propanoate | C₂H₅COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ C₂H₅COOC₂H₅ + H₂O |
| butanoic acid + methanol | methyl butanoate | C₃H₇COOH + CH₃OH ⇌ C₃H₇COOCH₃ + H₂O |

Check each equation: the ester's molecular formula equals acid + alcohol − H₂O.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ester | compound containing the –COO– group joining an acyl part and an alkyl part, RCOOR′ |
| Esterification | formation of an ester from a carboxylic acid and an alcohol |
| Condensation | two molecules join with the loss of a small molecule such as water |
| Hydrolysis | splitting of a compound by reaction with water |
| Carboxylate salt | ionic salt RCOO⁻M⁺, formed in alkaline hydrolysis |
| Reflux | continuous heating with a condenser so vapours return to the flask |

## Exam traps

- Alcohol part is named **first** but written **last** in RCOOR′.
- HCOOCH₃ is **methyl methanoate**; CH₃COOCH₃ is **methyl ethanoate**: count the acid carbons including
  the C=O carbon.
- Esterification: **concentrated** H₂SO₄. Hydrolysis: **dilute** acid or alkali.
- Alkaline hydrolysis gives the **salt**, not the acid; the alkali is **not** a catalyst.
- Hydrolysis needs **heat**.
- Do not put water on the left of an esterification equation.

## Self-test

1. Name CH₃CH₂COOCH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃.
2. Give the formula of ethyl butanoate.
3. Name the acid and the alcohol used to make methyl pentanoate.
4. State the conditions for making an ester from an acid and an alcohol.
5. Why is esterification described as a condensation reaction?
6. Give one way to increase the yield of an esterification.
7. Write the equation for the hydrolysis of methyl methanoate with dilute acid.
8. Write the equation for the hydrolysis of propyl ethanoate with NaOH(aq).
9. Explain why alkaline hydrolysis goes to completion but acid hydrolysis does not.
10. Name the three straight-chain esters with the formula C₄H₈O₂.
11. An ester is hydrolysed by NaOH(aq) to give sodium methanoate and butan-1-ol. Name the ester.
12. What must be added after alkaline hydrolysis to obtain the free carboxylic acid?

**Answers:**

1. Butyl propanoate.
2. CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₂CH₃.
3. Pentanoic acid and methanol.
4. Heat (under reflux) with concentrated sulfuric acid as catalyst.
5. The acid and alcohol join together with the elimination of a water molecule.
6. Use an excess of one reactant (for example the alcohol).
7. HCOOCH₃ + H₂O ⇌ HCOOH + CH₃OH.
8. CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₃ + NaOH → CH₃COONa + CH₃CH₂CH₂OH.
9. In alkali, the acid formed is converted into its carboxylate ion, which does not react with the
   alcohol, so the reverse reaction cannot occur; acid hydrolysis is the reverse of esterification and
   reaches equilibrium.
10. Methyl propanoate, ethyl ethanoate and propyl methanoate.
11. Butyl methanoate, HCOOCH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃.
12. A strong acid, such as dilute hydrochloric acid.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to name branched esters for Cambridge 9701?**
No. The naming outcome limits esters to straight chains, with up to six carbons in each part.

**Do I need to know the mechanism of esterification?**
No mechanism is required for 18.2: you need the reaction, its conditions and the two hydrolysis reactions.
