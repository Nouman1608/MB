## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. In the 4-mark questions, one mark for each point identified and one for explaining it. The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks on that pattern plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general, accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The 12-mark question is marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

**1.** Riba [1].

**2.** One-third [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: law based on the Qur'an and Sunnah; justice and equality before the law; consultation (shura); trustworthy leaders; protection of life, property and faith, including minorities; social welfare through Zakah; defence and security; accountability of rulers.

**4. (a)** Any one [1]: give testimony truthfully, even against yourselves (4:135); do not conceal testimony (2:283); do not testify to falsehood (25:72); bring two just men to witness (65:2).

**(b)** One reason identified [1] and explained [2].

- **Justice depends on it** [1]: judges decide cases on evidence [1], so a false witness can cause an innocent person to be punished [1].
- **It is a trust before Allah** [1]: the Qur'an says that whoever conceals testimony has a sinful heart (2:283) [1], so evidence is a duty [1].
- **It protects society** [1]: honest witnesses discourage crime and settle disputes [1], and people can rely on the courts [1].

**5.** For each of two reasons: reason identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Preparing for death** [1]: the Prophet (pbuh) said a Muslim should not let two nights pass without his will written [1].
- **Avoiding disputes** [1]: a clear will prevents arguments in the family over property [1].
- **Leaving charity** [1]: up to one-third can go to good causes or relatives who do not inherit [1].
- **Fairness** [1]: it makes sure debts are paid and the fixed shares are given as the Qur'an commands [1].

**6. (a)** £90,000 ÷ 3 = **£30,000** [1].

**(b)** Remainder: £90,000 − £30,000 = £60,000 [1]; wife: £60,000 ÷ 8 = **£7,500** [1].

**(c)** Left for the sons: £60,000 − £7,500 = £52,500; each son: £52,500 ÷ 2 = **£26,250** [1]. Accept follow-through from an incorrect answer to (b).

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- **Exploitation** [1]: the lender gains a fixed profit while the borrower, who is often poor, takes the whole risk [1].
- **Debt** [1]: interest can multiply a debt until it cannot be repaid [1].
- **Unfairness** [1]: interest makes money from money alone, while honest profit comes from work and shared risk [1].
- **Wealth moves from poor to rich** [1]: this widens the gap and harms the community [1].
- **Command of Allah** [1]: Muslims obey the command even when they do not see every reason [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- Qur'an 2:275: "Allah has permitted trade and has forbidden interest" [2].
- Qur'an 2:276: Allah destroys interest and gives increase for charities [2].
- The Hadith that the one who takes, pays, records and witnesses interest are all cursed [2].

**8.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Reasons (AO1):

- **Protects the weak** [1]: courts stop the strong from taking what belongs to others [1].
- **Ends disputes peacefully** [1]: a decision replaces revenge and feuds [1].
- **Equality** [1]: the same rule applies to the rich and the poor [1], as the Prophet (pbuh) showed when he refused to excuse a noble woman who stole.
- **Accountability to Allah** [1]: judges answer for their rulings, so justice is a form of worship [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- Qur'an 4:58: when you judge between people, judge with justice [2].
- Qur'an 4:135: stand firm in justice, even against yourselves, parents and relatives [2].
- The Hadith about the judge who strives and is right receiving two rewards, and one reward if mistaken [2].

**9.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** most modern banking, mortgages and savings use interest; Muslims who avoid it may have fewer choices; the passage warns strongly, so a Muslim who wishes to obey must accept limits; borrowing for a home or business can be harder.
- **Against the statement:** the passage says trade is permitted, so business is open; Islamic finance offers alternatives such as mudarabah, musharakah, murabaha and ijarah; profit-sharing can be fairer; the ban protects borrowers from heavy debt; Muslims have long taken part in trade and business.
- **Balanced view:** the ban requires care and creates challenges, but it is a command of Allah, and Islamic finance has grown to offer ways to take part in business without interest.

*Use of the passage*

- "Allah has permitted trade and has forbidden interest" gives the rule and shows that business is lawful.
- "Trade is [just] like interest" records the argument of those who defend interest, and the Qur'an rejects it.
- "Whoever has received an admonition ... and desists may have what is past" shows the way back for those who stop.
- "Companions of the Fire" shows the seriousness of returning to it.

Level 4 answers use the passage directly, argue both sides with examples, and reach a reasoned judgement.
