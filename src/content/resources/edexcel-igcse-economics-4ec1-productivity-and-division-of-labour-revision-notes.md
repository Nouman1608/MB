These notes condense section 1.2.2, Productivity and division of labour, of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Economics (4EC1) specification, Issue 3, February 2026, examined in Paper 1 with a single tier of entry. All figures are invented. For explanations and worked cases, read the [productivity and division of labour study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-economics-4ec1-productivity-and-division-of-labour/); to practise, use the [productivity and division of labour practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-economics-4ec1-productivity-and-division-of-labour-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Productivity | Output per unit of input over a period of time |
| Production | The total amount of output |
| Labour productivity | Output per worker or per hour worked |
| Land productivity | Output per hectare (yield) |
| Human capital | The knowledge, skills and experience of workers |
| Fertiliser | Substance adding nutrients to soil |
| Drainage | Removing excess water from land |
| Irrigation | Supplying water to land artificially |
| Reclamation | Turning unusable land into usable land |
| Division of labour | Splitting production into separate tasks, each done by a specialised worker |
| Labour cost per unit | Wage cost ÷ output |

## Formulae

```
productivity          = output ÷ input
output per hour       = output ÷ total hours worked
labour cost per unit  = wage per hour ÷ output per hour
percentage change     = (new − old) ÷ old × 100
```

## Factors affecting productivity

| Factor | Specification methods | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Land | Fertiliser | More nutrients, higher yield per hectare |
| | Drainage | Waterlogged land becomes usable |
| | Irrigation | Water in dry periods; higher, more reliable yields; extra harvests |
| | Reclamation | Unusable land brought into use |
| Labour | Quality of labour | Skills, health and motivation raise output per hour |
| | Education and training | Raise human capital |
| | Migration | Skilled immigration fills shortages; lower-skilled immigration may cut the average at first; emigration of skilled workers lowers it at home |
| Capital | Increased quantity | More machines or tools per worker |
| | Technological advances | Faster, more accurate equipment |

## Evaluation points for each

- **Fertiliser:** costs money; run-off can pollute rivers.
- **Irrigation and drainage:** expensive to install; need maintenance.
- **Reclamation:** adds land, but the new land may be poorer, so average yield may fall.
- **Training:** fees and lost output; trained staff may leave for rivals.
- **Migration:** effect depends on migrants' skills and how well they match jobs.
- **Capital:** purchase cost; training needed; may replace workers.

## Production versus productivity

| Change | Production | Productivity |
|---|---|---|
| Hire one more worker with the same tools | Rises | May stay the same or fall |
| Train existing workers | Rises | Rises |
| Workers given faster machines | Rises | Rises |

*Drill (invented):* 8 workers make 1 200 units (150 each). A ninth worker is hired and output becomes 1 260. Production rises by 60 units, but productivity falls to 1 260 ÷ 9 = **140 units per worker**.

## Division of labour

| | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Workers | Skilled and quick at one task; less training to start; possibly higher pay | Boredom; low motivation; narrow skills; job at risk if the task is automated |
| Businesses | Higher output per worker; lower unit costs; consistent quality; quick training | Absence or errors at one stage stop the line; high staff turnover; less flexibility |

**Works best when:** the product is standardised, the market is large, and boredom is managed, for example through job rotation.

*Drill (invented):* 5 workers × 40 hours = 200 hours. Whole-product method takes 2.5 hours per unit → 80 units. Division of labour cuts it to 1.6 hours → 125 units, a rise of (125 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100 = **56.25%**.

## Exam traps

- **Production is not productivity.**
- **Check the input:** per worker or per hour?
- **Division of labour is not mechanisation:** it is about splitting tasks among workers.
- **Migration:** say "it depends on skills".
- **Name the group:** workers or businesses, as the question asks.
- **Land methods need a mechanism**, not just a name.
- Treating extra workers as higher productivity.
- Ignoring hours when they differ between workers.
- Listing land methods with no effect on yield.
- Answering about workers when the question asks about businesses.
- One-sided evaluations of division of labour.

## Quick self-test

1. Define productivity.
2. Define division of labour.
3. Give the four ways of raising land productivity in the specification.
4. What is human capital?
5. How can migration lower average productivity at first?
6. Give one way capital raises productivity.
7. A worker paid 20 an hour makes 40 units an hour. Calculate the labour cost per unit.
8. After training, the worker in question 7 makes 50 units an hour. Calculate the new labour cost per unit.
9. 12 workers produce 3 600 units a week. Calculate output per worker.
10. Give one advantage of the division of labour for workers.
11. Give one disadvantage of the division of labour for businesses.
12. Why does division of labour suit standardised products?

### Answers

1. Output per unit of input over a period of time.
2. Splitting production into separate tasks, with each worker specialising in one task.
3. Fertiliser, drainage, irrigation, reclamation.
4. The knowledge, skills and experience of workers.
5. If arriving workers have lower or mismatched skills, output per worker can fall even though total output rises.
6. More machines per worker, or technological advances that make equipment faster or more accurate.
7. 20 ÷ 40 = **0.50**.
8. 20 ÷ 50 = **0.40**.
9. 3 600 ÷ 12 = **300 units**.
10. They become skilled and quick at their task (or: need less training to start).
11. If one worker is absent, the whole process can stop (or: boredom raises absence and staff turnover).
12. The same tasks are repeated many times, so workers can specialise and become fast.
