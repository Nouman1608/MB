Subtopic 24.3 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702 (2025–2027) covers radioactive tracers, β⁺ decay, electron-positron annihilation, the energy of the gamma-ray photons and how their arrival times produce an image. These notes are condensed for recall. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [PET scanning study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-pet-scanning/); to apply the ideas, try the [PET scanning practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-pet-scanning-practice/).

## The chain of events

1. A **tracer** containing a β⁺-emitting isotope is injected.
2. It is **absorbed** by the tissue being studied (more where the tissue is more active).
3. A nucleus decays: proton → neutron + **positron** + neutrino.
4. The positron travels a very short distance (about a millimetre) and slows down.
5. It meets an **electron**; they **annihilate**.
6. Two **gamma-ray photons** of equal energy travel in **opposite directions**.
7. They leave the body and are detected by a **ring of detectors**.
8. **Arrival times** are processed to locate annihilations and map **tracer concentration**.

## Equations

```
each photon: E = mₑc² = 8.20 × 10⁻¹⁴ J ≈ 0.51 MeV   (pair at rest)
λ = hc / E = 2.43 × 10⁻¹² m        p = E / c = 2.73 × 10⁻²² kg m s⁻¹
total energy released = 2mₑc² = 1.64 × 10⁻¹³ J
distance from midpoint: x = cΔt / 2
```

Example β⁺ decay: ¹⁸₉F → ¹⁸₈O + ⁰₊₁e + ν

## Conservation in annihilation

| Conserved quantity | Consequence in PET |
|---|---|
| Mass-energy | Rest-mass energy 2mₑc² becomes photon energy; each photon gets mₑc² |
| Momentum | Pair nearly at rest (zero momentum), so photons travel in opposite directions with equal momenta |
| Charge | +e and −e give total zero; photons are uncharged |

**Why not one photon?** A single photon always has momentum E/c, which cannot equal the zero momentum of the pair.

## Tracer properties

| Property | Why |
|---|---|
| β⁺ emitter | Provides positrons for annihilation |
| Half-life of order an hour or two (fluorine-18: about 110 min) | Long enough to prepare and inject; short enough to limit dose |
| Absorbed by the target tissue | Concentration in the image shows tissue activity |

## Locating an event

- Two detectors fire at (almost) the same time → annihilation lies on the line joining them.
- Photon arrives first at the nearer detector.
- Path difference = 2x, so Δt = 2x/c and x = cΔt/2.
- Light travels 0.30 m per ns: 400 ps difference → 6.0 cm from the midpoint.
- Many events → image of tracer concentration.

## Method for photon questions

1. Each photon energy E = mₑc² (use mₑ = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg, not the proton mass).
2. For MeV divide by 1.60 × 10⁻¹³; for frequency use f = E/h; for wavelength use λ = hc/E.
3. Photon momentum p = E/c; the two momenta are equal and opposite.
4. If the particles had kinetic energy, add it to 2mₑc² before sharing between the photons.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Tracer | Substance containing radioactive nuclei, introduced into the body and absorbed by the tissue studied |
| Positron | Antiparticle of the electron: same mass, charge +e |
| Antiparticle | Particle with the same mass but opposite charge (and other opposite properties) |
| Annihilation | A particle and its antiparticle meet and are converted into photons |
| β⁺ decay | Proton in the nucleus becomes a neutron; positron and neutrino emitted |
| PET | Positron emission tomography: imaging from annihilation photon pairs |

## Exam traps

- Saying the tracer is a gamma emitter.
- Giving each photon 2mₑc² (that is the total).
- Explaining opposite directions without mentioning momentum.
- Forgetting the factor 2 in x = cΔt/2.
- Using the proton mass instead of the electron mass.
- Unbalanced β⁺ equations: check nucleon number and proton number.
- Describing PET images as showing structure rather than tracer concentration (function).

## Self-test

1. What is a tracer?
2. What type of decay must a PET tracer undergo?
3. Write the change that occurs in the nucleus during β⁺ decay.
4. What is annihilation?
5. Which two quantities are conserved in annihilation?
6. Why are two photons produced rather than one?
7. Calculate the energy of each annihilation photon in joules.
8. Calculate the wavelength of each photon.
9. Why must the tracer's half-life be neither very long nor very short?
10. A pair of photons arrives at opposite detectors 250 ps apart. How far from the midpoint did the annihilation occur?
11. How does the scanner know that two detections come from the same annihilation?
12. What does a bright region in a PET image show?

**Answers:** 1. A substance containing radioactive nuclei introduced into the body and absorbed by the tissue being studied. 2. β⁺ decay. 3. A proton changes into a neutron, emitting a positron and a neutrino. 4. A particle and its antiparticle meet and their mass-energy is converted into photons. 5. Mass-energy and momentum (also charge). 6. The pair has nearly zero momentum; two equal photons in opposite directions have zero total momentum, but one photon cannot. 7. E = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × (3.00 × 10⁸)² = 8.20 × 10⁻¹⁴ J. 8. λ = hc/E = 2.43 × 10⁻¹² m. 9. Long enough to prepare and inject it with useful activity remaining; short enough that the patient's dose stays low after the scan. 10. x = 3.00 × 10⁸ × 250 × 10⁻¹² / 2 = 0.0375 m (3.8 cm), nearer the detector reached first. 11. They are detected at almost the same time on opposite sides of the ring. 12. A high concentration of tracer, meaning tissue that is absorbing more of it (for example, more active tissue).
