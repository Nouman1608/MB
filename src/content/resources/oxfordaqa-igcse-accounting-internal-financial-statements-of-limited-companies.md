This guide is aligned to the OxfordAQA International GCSE Accounting (9215) specification, version 1.2, for exams from May/June 2026 onwards. It covers the limited companies row of specification section 3.4, Preparation of financial statements. Content 3 to 5 is the content assessed in Paper 2: Financial Statements, so this topic sits in that paper. The specification does not split the content into tiers.

All amounts on this page are in dollars. Every business and figure is invented.

## What the specification asks for

| Specification wording | What you must be able to do |
|---|---|
| Prepare and comment on the internal financial statements of limited liability companies | Produce an income statement and a statement of financial position, then explain what they show |
| The statements are income statements and a statement of financial position | Know both statements and where each figure goes |
| These statements are for internal use and not for publication | Know the statements here are internal ones |
| Five sub-headings in the statement of financial position | Use non-current assets, current assets, equity, non-current liabilities, current liabilities |
| Distinguish between share capital and loan capital | Explain the difference and show each in the right place |
| Not examined | Statement of changes in equity, preference shares, general reserves, rights issues, bonus issues, revaluation of non-current assets |

The adjustments you already use for sole traders still apply to a company: accruals, prepayments, depreciation, provision for doubtful debts, irrecoverable debts and closing inventory. The company layout is different, but the arithmetic is the same.

## Limited companies in brief

A limited company is owned by shareholders. Its owners' liability is limited, so a shareholder can lose what they paid for their shares but is not personally liable for the company's debts. This is the main advantage over a sole trader or partnership. The company can also raise money by selling shares to more people. The disadvantages are that the owners have less control when there are many shareholders, and a company has more rules to follow than a sole trader.

For this specification the statements are prepared for internal use. They are not the published accounts, so you do not need to follow a publication format. You need clear headings, correct classification and correct totals.

## Share capital and loan capital

Both bring money into the company. They are not the same thing.

| | Share capital | Loan capital |
|---|---|---|
| Who provides it | Shareholders, who are the owners | Lenders, such as a bank or debenture holders, who are not owners |
| Return paid | Dividend, paid from profit and not guaranteed | Interest, paid at a set rate whether or not there is a profit |
| Repayment | Not normally repaid to shareholders while the company trades | Repaid on an agreed date |
| Where it appears | Equity | Non-current liabilities (current liabilities once repayable within a year) |
| Effect on profit | Dividends are not an expense | Interest is an expense that reduces profit for the year |

Dividends are a distribution of profit to owners. They are deducted from retained earnings, not from the income statement expenses. Loan interest is a cost of borrowing, so it appears in the income statement.

Retained earnings are the profits kept in the business over time. They are added to share capital to give equity. The specification does not list this term separately, so use whatever label your teacher uses for the profit kept in the business.

## The income statement

The income statement is built in the same order as a sole trader's: revenue, cost of sales, gross profit, expenses, operating profit. Two company features come after operating profit. Loan interest is deducted to reach profit for the year, and a short working below shows how that profit and any dividend change retained earnings.

## The statement of financial position and its five sub-headings

Group each balance under one of the five sub-headings.

| Sub-heading | What goes in it | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Non-current assets | Assets kept to use for more than a year, shown at cost less accumulated depreciation | Land, equipment, vehicles |
| Current assets | Assets that become cash within a year | Inventory, trade receivables less provision, prepayments, bank balance |
| Equity | The owners' stake | Share capital, retained earnings |
| Non-current liabilities | Debts due after more than one year | Loan repayable in several years |
| Current liabilities | Debts due within one year | Trade payables, accruals, bank overdraft |

The two sides balance: non-current assets plus current assets, less current liabilities and non-current liabilities, equals equity. You can also total assets on one side and equity plus liabilities on the other.

## Worked example: Avenmoor Cycles Ltd

Avenmoor Cycles Ltd sells bicycles. Its year ends on 31 December 2026. The trial balance after the first stage of the books is as follows.

|---|---|---|
| Land | 300,000 | |
| Equipment at cost | 80,000 | |
| Provision for depreciation of equipment | | 24,000 |
| Inventory at 1 January 2026 | 38,000 | |
| Purchases | 412,000 | |
| Revenue | | 690,000 |
| Trade receivables | 46,000 | |
| Trade payables | | 31,000 |
| Bank | 22,000 | |
| Wages | 96,000 | |
| Insurance | 14,400 | |
| General expenses | 33,600 | |
| Loan interest paid | 3,000 | |
| Dividends paid | 20,000 | |
| 10% loan, repayable in 2030 | | 60,000 |
| Ordinary share capital | | 250,000 |
| Provision for doubtful debts | | 1,200 |
| Retained earnings at 1 January 2026 | | 8,800 |
| **Totals** | **1,065,000** | **1,065,000** |

Adjustments at 31 December 2026:

1. Closing inventory is 41,500.
2. Insurance of 1,200 has been paid in advance.
3. Wages of 3,000 are owing.
4. Loan interest of 3,000 is owing for the second half of the year.
5. Equipment is depreciated at 10% a year on cost, straight line. Land is not depreciated.
6. The provision for doubtful debts is to be 5% of trade receivables.

### Step 1: work out each adjustment

- Depreciation: 10% of 80,000 = 8,000. Accumulated depreciation becomes 24,000 + 8,000 = 32,000, so the net book value is 48,000.
- Provision: 5% of 46,000 = 2,300. The old provision was 1,200, so the increase of 1,100 is an expense.
- Insurance: 14,400 less 1,200 prepaid = 13,200 expense.
- Wages: 96,000 plus 3,000 owing = 99,000 expense.
- Loan interest: 3,000 paid plus 3,000 owing = 6,000, which is 10% of 60,000.

### Step 4: comment on the results

Comments need figures and a judgement, not only a description. For Avenmoor:

- Gross profit is 281,500 on revenue of 690,000, a margin of 40.8%. Profit for the year is 120,600, which is 17.5% of revenue.
- Current assets of 108,400 cover current liabilities of 37,000 about 2.9 times, so the company can pay its short-term debts.
- The loan of 60,000 is 14.3% of the loan plus equity, so most of the finance comes from the owners and retained profit. Profit from operations covers the loan interest of 6,000 about 21 times.
- Dividends of 20,000 are a small share of profit for the year, so most profit is kept in the business.

The specification asks you to comment on the statements but does not list ratio formulas for this row, so use only the measures your course teaches. Always compare with a previous year, a target or another business if the question gives one, and say what the figure means for owners, lenders or managers.

## What is not examined

For limited companies, the specification states that these will not be examined: statement of changes in equity, preference shares, general reserves, rights issues, bonus issues and the revaluation of non-current assets. You can leave all of them out of your preparation. A question will therefore use ordinary share capital, and any change in equity comes only through profit for the year and dividends in the retained earnings working.

## Common errors

- Putting dividends in the income statement expenses. They reduce retained earnings only.
- Leaving loan interest out, or using the amount paid and forgetting the amount owing.
- Showing the whole loan as a current liability when it is repayable in 2030. It stays non-current until it falls due within a year.
- Depreciating land.
- Adding the provision for doubtful debts to expenses instead of only the change in it, and showing the full provision as an expense each year.
- Taking the closing inventory off the expenses instead of off the cost of sales.
- Putting accruals in current assets, or prepayments in liabilities.
- Writing a comment that restates a number without saying what it means.
