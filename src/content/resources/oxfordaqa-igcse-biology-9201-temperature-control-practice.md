## Section A

**1.**

**(a)** State what the receptors in the thermoregulatory centre are sensitive to. **[1]**
**(b)** State what the temperature receptors in the skin detect. **[1]**

**2.** Name the part of the body that monitors and controls body temperature. **[1]**

**3.** Give **two** responses of the body when the core temperature is too high. **[2]**

**4.** Give **two** responses of the body when the core temperature is too low. **[2]**

**5.** Explain how sweating helps to cool the body. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Core and skin temperature were measured on a volunteer after 30 minutes at three room temperatures.

| Room temperature (°C) | 5 | 25 | 40 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core temperature (°C) | 37.0 | 37.0 | 37.3 |
| Skin temperature (°C) | 22.0 | 32.0 | 36.4 |

**(a)** Calculate the difference between core and skin temperature at each room temperature. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the difference is smallest at 40 °C. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain what the data show about the control of core temperature. **[2]**

**7.** A player loses 0.8 dm³ of sweat per hour during a three-hour game in hot weather. She drinks 0.3 dm³ of water four times during the game.

**(a)** Calculate the total volume of sweat lost. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate how much more water she lost than she drank. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why she needs to take in fluid. **[1]**

**8.** A student's finger skin temperature was 33.0 °C. It fell to 21.0 °C over 15 minutes in cold water.

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of fall in °C per minute. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how a response of the blood vessels in the skin contributes to the cooling of the finger. **[2]**

**9.** A student writes: "When we are hot, blood vessels move closer to the skin surface." Correct this statement. **[2]**

**10.** Explain why shivering warms the body. **[3]**

**11.** Explain why there are receptors for temperature in both the skin and the brain. **[2]**

**12.** A person stands outside on a freezing day. Describe and explain how the body responds to keep the core temperature steady. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The temperature of the blood flowing through the brain [1]
**(b)** Skin temperature (the temperature at the surface of the body) [1]

**2.** The thermoregulatory centre in the brain [1]

**3.** Blood vessels supplying the skin capillaries dilate [1]; sweat glands release more sweat [1]

**4.** Blood vessels supplying the skin capillaries constrict [1]; muscles may shiver [1]

**5.** The sweat evaporates [1]; this transfers energy from the body, so it cools the body [1]

**6. (a)** 5 °C: 37.0 − 22.0 = **15.0 °C** [1]; 25 °C: 37.0 − 32.0 = **5.0 °C** [1]; 40 °C: 37.3 − 36.4 = **0.9 °C** [1]
**(b)** At 40 °C the blood vessels in the skin are dilated, so more warm blood flows through the skin capillaries [1]; so the skin is nearly as warm as the core [1]
**(c)** The core temperature changed by only 0.3 °C (37.0 to 37.3) while the room temperature changed by 35 °C [1]; so the core temperature is kept relatively constant by the control system [1]

**7. (a)** 0.8 × 3 = **2.4 dm³** [1]
**(b)** drunk = 0.3 × 4 = 1.2 dm³ [1]; shortfall = 2.4 − 1.2 = **1.2 dm³** [1]
**(c)** More water is lost in sweat when it is hot, so more fluid has to be taken in to balance the loss [1]

**8. (a)** fall = 33.0 − 21.0 = 12.0 °C; 12.0 ÷ 15 [1] = **0.8 °C per minute** [1]
**(b)** In the cold, the blood vessels supplying the skin capillaries constrict [1]; less warm blood flows through the skin, so less energy is transferred to the finger and the skin cools (the core is protected) [1]

**9.** Blood vessels do not move [1]; they dilate (widen), so more blood flows through the skin capillaries and more energy is transferred to the environment [1]

**10.** Shivering is the contraction of muscles [1]; contraction needs energy from respiration [1]; respiration transfers some energy as heat, which warms the body [1]

**11.** Skin receptors detect the temperature at the surface of the body [1]; brain receptors detect the temperature of the blood flowing through the brain (core temperature); together they give the centre information about both skin and core temperature [1]

**12.** Marking points, 1 mark each [6]:
- receptors in the skin detect the fall in skin temperature and send impulses to the thermoregulatory centre
- receptors in the brain detect a fall in the temperature of the blood
- the thermoregulatory centre coordinates the response
- blood vessels supplying the skin capillaries constrict
- so less blood flows through the skin and less energy is transferred to the surroundings
- muscles shiver; the contraction needs respiration, which transfers some energy to warm the body

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying that blood vessels move, or that they "open and close".
- Writing that sweat cools because it is cold, with no mention of evaporation.
- Leaving out respiration in shivering answers.
- Mixing up the effects of dilating and constricting.
- Forgetting to say more fluid must be taken in when it is hot.
- Giving only the response without the effect on energy transfer.
