These questions cover sub-topic 1(b), **Variety of living organisms** (specification points 1.2 to 1.4), of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Biology (4BI1) specification, Issue 3. The qualification is untiered and none of these points is bold-referenced, so they can be examined on either paper. The set applies to the June and November series examined under Issue 3. A calculator may be used.

## Questions

**1.** Name the group of organisms that matches each description.

**(a)** Multicellular, cell walls made of cellulose, cells contain chloroplasts. **[1]**
**(b)** Multicellular, no cell walls, usually has nervous co-ordination. **[1]**
**(c)** Single-celled, no nucleus, has a cell wall and plasmids. **[1]**
**(d)** Body made of hyphae, cell walls made of chitin. **[1]**

**2.** The table shows features of four organisms. A tick means the feature is present.

| Organism | Chloroplasts | Cell wall | Nucleus | Number of cells |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P | ✓ | ✓ (cellulose) | ✓ | Many |
| Q | ✗ | ✗ | ✓ | Many |
| R | ✗ | ✓ | ✗ | One |
| S | ✗ | ✓ (chitin) | ✓ | Many |

**(a)** State which organism is a bacterium. Give a reason. **[2]**
**(b)** State which organism is a fungus. Give a reason. **[2]**
**(c)** Name the likely group for organism Q. **[1]**

**3.** *Mucor* is a fungus that grows on bread.

**(a)** Name the thread-like structures that make up the body of *Mucor*. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe how *Mucor* obtains food from bread. **[3]**
**(c)** Give **one** way in which a yeast cell differs from *Mucor* in body structure. **[1]**

**4.** Protoctists show a wide range of features.

**(a)** Give **two** features that all protoctists share. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why *Chlorella* is classified as a protoctist and not as a plant. **[2]**
**(c)** Name a protoctist that is a pathogen and name the disease it causes. **[2]**

**5.** A bacterial cell was drawn and labelled.

**(a)** Name **three** structures found in a bacterial cell. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe where the genetic material is found in a bacterial cell. **[2]**
**(c)** Name a bacterium used to make yoghurt. **[1]**

**6.** The tobacco mosaic virus infects tobacco plants.

**(a)** Explain why a virus is not classed as a living organism. **[3]**
**(b)** State the effect of the tobacco mosaic virus on tobacco leaves. **[2]**
**(c)** State what is meant by the term pathogen. **[1]**

**7.** A bacterium is 3 µm long. A virus particle is 150 nm long.

**(a)** Convert 3 µm into nm. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times longer the bacterium is than the virus particle. **[2]**

**8.** Complete the table by writing the carbohydrate store for each group.

| Group | Carbohydrate store |
|---|---|
| Plants | (a) |
| Animals | (b) |
| Fungi | (c) |

**[3]**

**9.** A student says: "Viruses and bacteria are both single-celled organisms that cause disease." Evaluate this statement. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** Plants [1]
**(b)** Animals [1]
**(c)** Bacteria [1]
**(d)** Fungi [1]
*Examiner insight:* One distinctive feature is usually enough to decide a group. Chitin points to fungi and the absence of a nucleus points to bacteria.

**2.** **(a)** R [1]; it has no nucleus (and is single-celled) [1]
**(b)** S [1]; its cell wall is made of chitin [1]
**(c)** Animal [1]
*Examiner insight:* The reason mark is lost if you simply say "it is single-celled". Single cells also occur in protoctists and yeast, so use the absence of a nucleus.

**3.** **(a)** Hyphae [1]
**(b)** It secretes digestive enzymes onto the bread (extracellular) [1]; the enzymes break down the food into soluble products [1]; the fungus absorbs the products [1]
**(c)** Yeast is single-celled, whereas *Mucor* has a body of hyphae / a mycelium [1]
*Examiner insight:* Say that the enzymes act outside the fungus. "It digests the bread" is too vague for the first mark.

**4.** **(a)** Any two of: microscopic, single-celled, eukaryotic [2]
**(b)** Plants are multicellular but *Chlorella* is single-celled [1]; protoctists are microscopic single-celled organisms (even though *Chlorella* has chloroplasts) [1]
**(c)** *Plasmodium* [1]; malaria [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), having chloroplasts is not enough to make an organism a plant. State the single-celled feature.

**5.** **(a)** Any three of: cell wall, cell membrane, cytoplasm, plasmid(s), circular chromosome [3]
**(b)** A circular chromosome of DNA [1]; lying free in the cytoplasm because there is no nucleus (accept plasmids also in the cytoplasm) [1]
**(c)** *Lactobacillus* (*Lactobacillus bulgaricus*) [1]
*Examiner insight:* A mark scheme will not credit "nucleoid" unless the answer also says that the DNA is free in the cytoplasm.

**6.** **(a)** A virus has no cellular structure [1]; it can reproduce only inside living cells [1]; it cannot carry out the other characteristics of life by itself (accept: it has only a protein coat and nucleic acid) [1]
**(b)** It causes discolouring of the leaves [1]; by preventing the formation of chloroplasts [1]
**(c)** An organism or particle that causes disease [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), "it is too small" is not a reason. Smallness does not decide whether something is living.

**7.** **(a)** 3 × 1000 = 3000 nm [1]
**(b)** 3000 ÷ 150 [1] = **20 times** [1]
*Examiner insight:* If you divide 3 by 150 without converting, you get 0.02 and lose both marks in (b).

**8.** **(a)** Starch (accept sucrose) [1]
**(b)** Glycogen [1]
**(c)** Glycogen [1]
*Examiner insight:* Glycogen is the store in animals and fungi. The specification says animals "often" store it and fungi "may" store it.

**9.** Viruses are not single-celled, because they have no cells [1]; bacteria are single-celled [1]; bacteria are living organisms but viruses are not [1]; both can be pathogens, so the second half of the statement is correct, and the statement as a whole is only partly correct (accept a clear conclusion that the statement is partly right) [1]
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation needs a final judgement. A list of facts with no conclusion loses the last mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a virus as a cell or as living.
- Giving a nucleus to bacteria.
- Confusing the cell wall materials: cellulose in plants and chitin in fungi.
- Not converting units before dividing sizes.
- Saying fungi "eat" food without mentioning extracellular enzymes.
- Choosing "plant" for any organism that has chloroplasts.

**Are viruses covered in this sub-topic?**
Yes. Point 1.4 covers viruses and the idea that pathogens may be fungi, bacteria, protoctists or viruses.
