These notes cover section 3.2.2.1, Global variations in levels of economic development, of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Geography (9230) specification, Version 4.3. Key ideas: "There are global variations in economic development and quality of life"; "Various strategies exist for reducing the global development gap." For full explanations, read the [global variations study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-geography-9230-global-variations-economic-development/).

## Specification checklist

- **Classifying** the world by development.
- **Measures** of development and their **limitations**.
- Link to the **Demographic Transition Model**.
- **Causes** (physical, economic, historical) and **consequences** of uneven development.
- **Strategies** to reduce the gap.
- **Example:** tourism in an LIC or NEE.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| HIC / LIC / NEE | Higher income / lower income / newly emerging economy |
| GNI per head | Total national income divided by population |
| HDI | Score from 0 to 1 combining life expectancy, education and income |
| Infant mortality | Deaths under 1 year per 1 000 live births |
| Development gap | Difference in wealth and quality of life between countries |
| Intermediate technology | Simple, locally suitable tools and methods |
| Microfinance | Very small loans to people without access to banks |
| Remittances | Money sent home by migrants |

## Classifications

- **HIC, NEE, LIC** (main classification).
- **World Bank income groups:** low, lower-middle, upper-middle, high.
- **HDI groups:** low, medium, high, very high (cut-offs about 0.550, 0.700, 0.800).
- **Global North and South** (Brandt line): broad, with exceptions such as Australia.
- **Developed and developing:** commonly used but criticised.

## Measures

| Type | Measure | Pattern in LICs |
|---|---|---|
| Economic | GNI per head | Low |
| Social | Birth rate | High |
| Social | Death rate | Not a clear indicator (young populations) |
| Social | Infant mortality | High |
| Social | Life expectancy | Low |
| Social | People per doctor | High number of people per doctor |
| Social | Literacy rate | Lower, especially for women |
| Social | Access to safe water | Lower |
| Composite | HDI | Low |

## Limitations

- Averages hide **inequality**.
- Prices differ, so incomes in dollars do not show what can be bought.
- The **informal economy** and subsistence farming are not counted.
- Data may be **unreliable**.
- One measure is **not enough**; GNI misses health and education.
- HDI leaves out inequality, the environment and rights.
- Death rate can mislead: a young population has a low death rate.

## Demographic Transition Model link

| Stage | Birth rate | Death rate | Growth | Typical countries |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | High | High | Low | Very few now |
| 2 | High | Falling | Rapid | Many LICs |
| 3 | Falling | Low | Still growing | NEEs |
| 4 | Low | Low | Slow or stable | Many HICs |
| 5 | Very low | Slightly higher | Falling | Some ageing HICs |

Death rates fall first (food, water, medicine). Birth rates fall later (education for women, urban living, contraception, higher income).

## Causes of uneven development

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Physical | Drought, hazards, poor soil, tropical disease, landlocked, few resources |
| Economic | Reliance on raw material exports, price swings, debt, low investment, unfair trade |
| Historical | Colonial economies built for export of raw materials, slavery, colonial borders, conflict |

These combine in a **cycle of poverty**.

## Consequences

- **Wealth gap:** income, housing, services.
- **Health gap:** life expectancy, infant mortality, disease, clean water.
- **International migration:** to find work or safety; remittances, brain drain; strain on services in receiving countries.

## Strategies

| Strategy | Benefit | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Investment | Jobs, infrastructure | Profits may leave; investors may withdraw |
| Industry and tourism | Jobs, exports | Low pay; tourism seasonal and vulnerable |
| Aid | Saves lives, builds schools and clinics | Dependency, conditions, corruption |
| Intermediate technology | Cheap, local, easy to repair | Small scale |
| Fairtrade | Guaranteed price, community premium | Small share of trade |
| Debt relief | Frees money for health and education | Conditions; borrowing may resume |
| Microfinance | Starts small businesses, especially for women | High interest; repayment trouble |

## Example: tourism in Kenya (check your own example)

- **Growth:** safari parks (for example Maasai Mara), coast, wildlife.
- **Benefits:** jobs; foreign currency; multiplier effect on local shops and crafts; fees for conservation; improved roads and airports.
- **Problems:** leakage of profits abroad; seasonal, low-paid work; pressure on water and wildlife; vulnerable to security fears and global crises.
- **Closing the gap:** raises incomes and spreads benefits to rural areas, best with local ownership and protected environment.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain why GNI per head is not a perfect measure of development. [4 marks]

**Model answer:** "GNI per head is an average, so it hides inequality: a few rich people can raise the average while most people stay poor. It does not count the informal economy or subsistence farming, so real incomes may be under-recorded. Prices differ between countries, so the same income buys different amounts. It also measures only income, not health or education, so a social measure such as life expectancy or the HDI is needed too."

How it scores: one mark for each limitation explained, up to the maximum of four.

## Quick recall: five ideas to remember

- Use both economic and social measures.
- DTM Stage 2 fits many LICs; Stage 3 fits NEEs; Stage 4 fits many HICs.
- Causes of uneven development: physical, economic and historical, which reinforce each other.
- Consequences: wealth gap, health gap and international migration.
- Every strategy has a limitation, including aid.

## Exam traps

- Use HIC, NEE, LIC with named examples.
- Use both economic **and** social measures.
- A low death rate does not prove a country is rich.
- Every strategy needs a limitation.
- Aid does not always help; explain the problems.
- Calculate comparisons from data (ratios, differences) and give units.
- Example answers need named places and facts.

## Self-test

1. What do the initials NEE stand for?
2. Name **three** social measures of development.
3. What three things does the HDI combine?
4. Give **two** limitations of GNI per head.
5. Which DTM stage has high birth rates and falling death rates?
6. Give **one** physical, one economic and one historical cause of uneven development.
7. What is meant by brain drain?
8. Explain how Fairtrade helps farmers.
9. Why can aid sometimes fail to help?
10. Describe **two** benefits and **one** problem of tourism in an LIC or NEE.

**Answers**

1. Newly emerging economy.
2. Any three: life expectancy; infant mortality; literacy; people per doctor; access to safe water; birth rate.
3. Life expectancy, education and income.
4. Averages hide inequality; informal economy not counted; prices differ between countries.
5. Stage 2.
6. For example: drought; dependence on exporting raw materials; colonial rule.
7. Skilled people leaving their home country to work abroad.
8. Farmers get a guaranteed minimum price and a premium for community projects, giving more stable and fairer income.
9. It can create dependency, be tied to conditions, or be lost to corruption.
10. Benefits: jobs; foreign income; multiplier effect. Problem: leakage of profits, or seasonal work.
