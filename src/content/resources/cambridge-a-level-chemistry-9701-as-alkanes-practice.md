## Section B

**6.** Ethane reacts with bromine in the presence of ultraviolet light.

**(a)** Name the mechanism of this reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Write an equation for the initiation step and name the type of bond fission involved. **[2]**

**(c)** Write equations for the two propagation steps. **[2]**

**(d)** Write an equation for a termination step that produces a hydrocarbon, and name that
hydrocarbon. **[1]**

**7.** Decane, C₁₀H₂₂, is cracked to give octane and one other product.

**(a)** Give the formula of the other product. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest one use for each product. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why oil refineries crack heavy fractions of crude oil. **[2]**

**8.** 15 cm³ of butane is mixed with 120 cm³ of oxygen and ignited. The butane burns completely. All
volumes are measured at room temperature and pressure.

**(a)** Calculate the volume of oxygen used. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide formed. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the total volume of gas left after the reaction. **[1]**

**9.** 2-methylbutane, (CH₃)₂CHCH₂CH₃, reacts with chlorine in ultraviolet light.

**(a)** State how many different monochloro products can form. **[1]**

**(b)** Name each of the monochloro products. **[4]**

**10.** Car exhausts contain nitrogen monoxide, NO, and carbon monoxide.

**(a)** Explain how nitrogen monoxide forms in a petrol engine. **[2]**

**(b)** Give one environmental consequence of releasing oxides of nitrogen. **[1]**

**(c)** Write an equation for the reaction in a catalytic converter that removes both NO and CO. **[1]**

**(d)** Write an equation for the removal of unburnt octane, C₈H₁₈, in a catalytic converter. **[1]**

**11.** Methane and chlorine do not react in the dark at room temperature, but react rapidly in sunlight.
Explain this observation. **[2]**

**12.** Hydrocarbon Y, C₅H₈, is an open-chain compound. One mole of Y reacts with two moles of hydrogen in
the presence of a nickel catalyst to give pentane.

**(a)** Deduce the number of C=C bonds in Y. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest a structural formula for Y. **[1]**

**13.** A student chlorinates ethane to make chloroethane. The product is found to contain
dichloroethanes and butane as well as chloroethane.

**(a)** Explain how the dichloroethanes form. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain how butane forms. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest how the proportion of chloroethane in the product could be increased. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Heat with an aluminium oxide (Al₂O₃) catalyst [1].

**2.** Hydrogen gas [1]; platinum or nickel catalyst and heat [1].

**3.** A species (atom or group of atoms) with an unpaired electron [1].

**4.** C₇H₁₆ + 11O₂ → 7CO₂ + 8H₂O [1].

**5.** C–H bonds are strong (410 kJ mol⁻¹) [1]; C and H have similar electronegativities, so the bonds
are almost non-polar and there is no δ+ carbon atom for the nucleophile to attack [1].

**6. (a)** Free-radical substitution [1].

**(b)** Br₂ → 2Br• [1]; homolytic fission [1].

**(c)** Br• + C₂H₆ → C₂H₅• + HBr [1]; C₂H₅• + Br₂ → C₂H₅Br + Br• [1].

**(d)** C₂H₅• + C₂H₅• → C₄H₁₀, butane [1].

**7. (a)** C₂H₄ (ethene) [1].

**(b)** Octane: a fuel, for example in petrol [1]; ethene: making poly(ethene) or other chemicals [1].

**(c)** Fractional distillation gives more heavy, long-chain fractions than are needed and too little of
the lighter fractions in high demand [1]; cracking converts the surplus heavy fractions into more
valuable shorter alkanes for fuels and alkenes for the chemical industry [1].

**8. (a)** C₄H₁₀ + 6½O₂ → 4CO₂ + 5H₂O, so oxygen used = 6.5 × 15 = 97.5 cm³ [1].

**(b)** 4 × 15 = 60 cm³ of CO₂ [1].

**(c)** Oxygen left = 120 − 97.5 = 22.5 cm³; water is liquid, so total gas = 60 + 22.5 = 82.5 cm³ [1].

**9. (a)** Four [1].

**(b)** 1-chloro-2-methylbutane [1]; 2-chloro-2-methylbutane [1]; 2-chloro-3-methylbutane [1];
1-chloro-3-methylbutane [1].

**10. (a)** The temperature inside the engine is very high [1], so nitrogen and oxygen from the air
combine: N₂ + O₂ → 2NO [1].

**(b)** Any one: acid rain; photochemical smog [1].

**(c)** 2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂ [1].

**(d)** C₈H₁₈ + 12½O₂ → 8CO₂ + 9H₂O (or 2C₈H₁₈ + 25O₂ → 16CO₂ + 18H₂O) [1].

**11.** Ultraviolet light in sunlight provides the energy to break the Cl–Cl bond homolytically, forming
chlorine radicals that start the reaction [1]. In the dark no radicals form, and chlorine molecules
cannot attack the strong, almost non-polar C–H bonds, so there is no reaction [1].

**12. (a)** Two C=C bonds (one H₂ adds to each) [1].

**(b)** Any open-chain diene with a five-carbon unbranched chain, for example CH₂=CHCH=CHCH₃
(penta-1,3-diene) or CH₂=CHCH₂CH=CH₂ (penta-1,4-diene) [1].

**13. (a)** Chlorine radicals can remove a hydrogen atom from chloroethane already formed [1]; the
resulting radical, such as CH₃CHCl•, then reacts with Cl₂ to give a dichloroethane [1].

**(b)** In a termination step, two ethyl radicals combine: 2C₂H₅• → C₄H₁₀ [1].

**(c)** Use an excess of ethane, so a chlorine radical is more likely to collide with ethane than with
chloroethane [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out UV light, or giving "heat and a catalyst" for free-radical substitution.
- Writing H• or H₂ in a propagation step instead of HCl or HBr.
- Missing radical dots, or adding them to molecules.
- Including the volume of water when calculating the final gas volume at room temperature.
- Naming monochloro isomers with the wrong locants, or counting equivalent hydrogens as different.
- Saying NOx comes from nitrogen in the petrol.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I find the number of monosubstituted products of an alkane?**
Count the different hydrogen environments. Hydrogens on carbons that are equivalent by symmetry, such as
the two CH₃ groups on C2 of 2-methylbutane, give the same product.

**Why does water not count in a gas-volume answer?**
At room temperature and pressure, water condenses to a liquid, and its volume is negligible compared with
the gases. Only CO₂ and any unreacted oxygen remain as gas.
