These notes condense content item 2.5.4, Motivation, from Theme 2 Topic 2.5 Making human resource decisions in the Pearson Edexcel GCSE (9-1) Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022. Worked examples are in the [motivation study guide](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-motivation/), and the [motivation practice questions](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-motivation-practice/) test the content. All businesses mentioned are invented.

## The item at a glance

- **Importance of motivation:** attracting employees, retaining employees, productivity.
- **Financial methods:** remuneration, bonus, commission, promotion, fringe benefits.
- **Non-financial methods:** job rotation, job enrichment, autonomy.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Motivation | The desire to work hard and do the job well |
| Productivity | Output per worker (or per hour) in a period |
| Retention | Keeping employees rather than losing them |
| Remuneration | Payment for work, such as a wage or salary |
| Bonus | An extra payment for reaching a target or for good business results |
| Commission | Pay linked to the value of sales made, usually a percentage |
| Promotion | Moving to a higher job with more responsibility and usually more pay |
| Fringe benefits | Extras on top of pay, such as staff discount, a company car or health cover |
| Job rotation | Moving employees between tasks at the same level |
| Job enrichment | Adding more challenging, responsible tasks to a job |
| Autonomy | Freedom for employees to decide how to do their work |

## Why motivation matters: chains to learn

- Good reputation as an employer → more applicants → better choice of staff (**attracting**).
- Motivated staff stay → lower recruitment and training costs, experience kept (**retaining**).
- Motivated staff work harder and make fewer errors → more output per worker → lower labour cost per unit (**productivity**).

## Methods: suitability and drawback

| Method | Type | Suits | Drawback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Remuneration | Financial | Attracting staff in a competitive job market | Permanent cost increase |
| Bonus | Financial | Rewarding targets, individual or team | Quality may suffer as staff chase the target |
| Commission | Financial | Sales staff | Unstable pay; pushy selling |
| Promotion | Financial | Ambitious staff in a growing business | Few posts; others disappointed |
| Fringe benefits | Financial | Retaining staff without raising basic pay | Not everyone values the same benefit |
| Job rotation | Non-financial | Repetitive work; covering absences | Tasks stay at the same level; training needed |
| Job enrichment | Non-financial | Capable staff who feel under-used | Training; staff may expect higher pay |
| Autonomy | Non-financial | Skilled, experienced staff | Poor decisions by inexperienced staff |

**Rotation vs enrichment:** rotation = different tasks, same level; enrichment = more demanding job.

## Calculations (standard methods)

- Pay with commission = basic pay + (commission rate × sales).
- Pay with bonus = basic pay + bonus.
- Productivity = output ÷ number of workers.
- Labour cost per unit = wage cost ÷ output.
- Percentage change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100.
- Sales at which two pay schemes give equal pay = difference in basic pay ÷ commission rate.

**Quick check:** basic £1,500 a month + 5% commission on £20,000 sales = £1,500 + £1,000 = £2,500.

## Matching method to problem

- Cannot recruit → pay or fringe benefits.
- Losing ambitious staff → promotion or enrichment.
- Boredom and low output → rotation or team bonus.
- Uneven sales results → commission or bonus.
- Skilled staff frustrated by close control → autonomy.

## Applying the notes to a case

Find the problem first: recruitment, staff leaving, or low output. Choose a method that fits that problem and the type of employee, then state the outcome in the specification's words. For example: "job rotation gives packers variety, so boredom falls and output per worker rises, cutting the labour cost of each unit." Where pay figures are given, calculate both options. Finish with a condition, such as whether staff value money or responsibility more, because no method suits every business.

## Exam traps

- Promotion is a **financial** method in the specification.
- A bonus is not commission: bonus for a target, commission for sales value.
- Every method answer needs a link to attracting, retaining or productivity.
- A pay rise is permanent; a bonus can change each year.
- Autonomy for new staff is risky; say who it suits.
- Show workings for pay calculations and include £ signs.

## Self-test

1. Name the three reasons the specification gives for the importance of motivation.
2. Is promotion a financial or non-financial method?
3. What is the difference between job rotation and job enrichment?
4. Give one drawback of paying commission.
5. Give two examples of fringe benefits.
6. Which method suits skilled staff who dislike close supervision?
7. A worker earns £1,700 a month plus 4% commission on £25,000 of sales. Calculate their pay.
8. 15 workers produce 6,000 units a week. Calculate productivity.
9. Output per worker rises from 400 to 450 units. Calculate the percentage increase.
10. Why does higher productivity lower costs?
11. Give one reason a business might choose a bonus rather than a pay rise.
12. A business struggles to fill vacancies because rivals pay more. Suggest a suitable method.

**Answers**

1. Attracting employees, retaining employees, productivity.
2. Financial.
3. Rotation moves staff between tasks at the same level; enrichment makes the job more demanding and responsible.
4. Any one: pay is unstable; it may encourage pushy selling; other duties may be neglected.
5. Any two: staff discount, company car, health cover, free meals, gym membership.
6. Autonomy.
7. £1,700 + (0.04 × £25,000) = £1,700 + £1,000 = **£2,700**.
8. 6,000 ÷ 15 = **400 units per worker**.
9. (450 − 400) ÷ 400 × 100 = **12.5%**.
10. Each worker produces more for the same wage, so the labour cost of each unit falls.
11. A bonus is paid only when targets are met, so it does not permanently raise costs.
12. Higher remuneration or better fringe benefits.
