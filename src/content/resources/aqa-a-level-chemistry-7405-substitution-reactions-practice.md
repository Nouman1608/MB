These questions cover section **3.2.5.2 Substitution reactions** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 1 and Paper 3. Learn the ideas first in the [Substitution reactions study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-substitution-reactions/) and the [Transition metals revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-transition-metals-revision-notes/). Use R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ where needed and ΔG = ΔH – TΔS.

## Questions

**1.** Define the terms monodentate ligand and bidentate ligand, and give one example of each. **[3]**

**2.** Excess aqueous ammonia is added to a solution containing [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** State why the co-ordination number does not change. **[1]**

**3.** Aqueous ammonia is added to a solution containing [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ until the ammonia is in excess.

**(a)** Write the formula of the complex formed. **[1]**

**(b)** State what is meant by incomplete substitution and state the colour change. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why the co-ordination number changes when chloride ions replace water ligands in [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺, but does not change when ammonia replaces water. **[3]**

**5.** Concentrated hydrochloric acid is added to aqueous copper(II) sulfate.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** State the co-ordination number and the shape of the complex formed. **[2]**

**(c)** State what is observed when the solution is then diluted with water. **[1]**

**6.** Write an equation for the reaction of [Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ with concentrated hydrochloric acid and state the change in co-ordination number. **[2]**

**7.** Identify the donor atoms in each ligand and state how many co-ordinate bonds each forms.

**(a)** H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂ **[1]**

**(b)** C₂O₄²⁻ **[1]**

**8.** Write an equation for the reaction of [Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺ with an excess of H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂, using en to represent the ligand. **[2]**

**9.** [Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺ reacts with 1,2-diaminoethane (en): [Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺ + 3en → [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ + 6NH₃. Use the numbers of particles and the bonds formed to explain why the reaction is feasible. **[4]**

**10.** For a ligand substitution reaction at 298 K, ΔH = –5.0 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔS = +150 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. Calculate ΔG and state whether the reaction is feasible. **[3]**

**11.** Haemoglobin contains haem, which is an iron(II) complex.

**(a)** Explain how oxygen is carried in the blood by haemoglobin. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why carbon monoxide is toxic. **[2]**

**12.** EDTA⁴⁻ reacts with [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ to form [Cu(EDTA)]²⁻. Calculate the volume of 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ EDTA solution needed to react completely with 25.0 cm³ of 0.0100 mol dm⁻³ copper(II) solution, assuming the ratio is 1 : 1. **[3]**

**13.** A student says the chelate effect happens because the bonds formed by bidentate ligands are much stronger than those of monodentate ligands. Evaluate this statement. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** A monodentate ligand forms one co-ordinate bond to the metal ion using one lone pair [1]; a bidentate ligand forms two co-ordinate bonds to the metal ion [1]; one correct example of each, such as NH₃ (or H₂O, Cl⁻) and H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂ (or C₂O₄²⁻) [1]. **[3]**

**2. (a)** [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 6NH₃ ⇌ [Co(NH₃)₆]²⁺ + 6H₂O [1]

**(b)** Ammonia and water are similar in size and both are uncharged, so six ammonia molecules can fit round the cobalt ion [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ [1]

**(b)** Only four of the six water ligands are replaced, even with excess ammonia [1]; the colour changes from pale blue to deep blue [1]. **[3]**

**4.** Chloride ions are larger than water molecules and ammonia molecules [1]; only four chloride ions can fit round the metal ion, so the co-ordination number falls from 6 to 4 [1]; ammonia is similar in size to water and uncharged, so six can still fit and the co-ordination number stays at 6 [1]. **[3]**

**5. (a)** [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CuCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O [1]

**(b)** The co-ordination number is 4 [1] and the shape is tetrahedral [1]

**(c)** The colour changes from yellow-green back to blue because the equilibrium shifts to the left [1]. **[4]**

**6.** [Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [FeCl₄]⁻ + 6H₂O [1]; the co-ordination number changes from 6 to 4 [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Two nitrogen atoms, forming two co-ordinate bonds (bidentate) [1]

**(b)** Two oxygen atoms (one on each carboxylate group), forming two co-ordinate bonds [1]. **[2]**

**8.** [Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 3en → [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ + 6H₂O [2: 1 for the correct species, 1 for the balancing]. **[2]**

**9.** There are 4 particles on the left and 7 on the right, so the entropy increases (ΔS is positive) [1]; six Ni–N bonds break and six Ni–N bonds form, so ΔH is approximately zero [1]; ΔG = ΔH – TΔS, so ΔG is negative [1]; the reaction is therefore feasible and the equilibrium lies on the right [1]. **[4]**

**10.** TΔS = 298 × 0.150 = 44.7 kJ mol⁻¹ [1]; ΔG = –5.0 – 44.7 = **–49.7 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]; ΔG is negative, so the reaction is feasible [1]. **[3]**

**11. (a)** Oxygen forms a co-ordinate bond to the Fe(II) in haem [1]; in the lungs the oxygen binds and in the tissues it is released, because the bond is reversible [1]

**(b)** Carbon monoxide forms a co-ordinate bond to Fe(II) that is stronger than that with oxygen [1]; it replaces the oxygen, so the haemoglobin cannot carry oxygen round the body [1]. **[4]**

**12.** n(Cu²⁺) = 0.0100 × 0.0250 = 2.50 × 10⁻⁴ mol [1]; n(EDTA) = 2.50 × 10⁻⁴ mol (1 : 1) [1]; volume = 2.50 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 0.0500 = 5.00 × 10⁻³ dm³ = **5.00 cm³** [1]. **[3]**

**13.** The statement is not correct [1]; the bond enthalpy change is about zero, because the same number and type of bonds are formed, and the main reason is the increase in entropy due to more particles being released [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* "More particles, more disorder, ΔS positive" is the key phrase for the chelate effect. A mark scheme will also look for ΔH being about zero.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying chloride and ammonia both change the co-ordination number.
- Forgetting that copper substitution with ammonia is incomplete.
- Writing equations without balancing the charge.
- Explaining the chelate effect only with bond strength.
- Not saying carbon monoxide binds more strongly than oxygen.
