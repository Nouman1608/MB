## Answers

Multiple-choice questions score 1 mark. "State" questions score 1 mark per valid point. Four-mark
questions score 1 mark per valid, linked point. The 6- and 9-mark questions are marked by levels; the
level descriptions follow the answers. Credit other accurate points.

**1.** **B: 1960** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **C: Ravi** [1]. The Beas and Sutlej were also allocated to India. **[1]**

**3.** **B: Jhelum** [1]. Tarbela is on the Indus. **[1]**

**4.** **A: Karez** [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Two uses, 1 mark each: domestic use for drinking and sanitation [1]; hydel power generation [1].
Also credit: industry such as textiles or sugar; fisheries. **[2]**

**6.** Two causes, 1 mark each: rapid population growth increases demand [1]; leaking pipes and
illegal connections waste supply [1]. Also credit: limited sources, such as Karachi's dependence on
the Indus via Keenjhar Lake and the Hub Dam; slow building of new supply schemes. **[2]**

**7.** Two ways, 1 mark each: drip or sprinkler irrigation [1]; laser land levelling so water spreads
evenly [1]. Also credit: lining watercourses; irrigating at cooler times of day; growing crops that
need less water. **[2]**

**8.** Four linked points, 1 mark each: tube wells pump out more water than rain and canal seepage
replace [1]; the water table falls deeper below the surface [1]; farmers must deepen wells and use
more diesel or electricity to pump [1], which raises costs, and some wells dry up or bring up saltier
water, reducing yields [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Four linked points, 1 mark each: the Indus Waters Treaty (1960) gave the eastern rivers to
India [1], so Pakistan needed to store western-river water to replace their flow in its canals [1];
the dams store summer meltwater and monsoon flow for use in the dry season [1]; they also generate
hydel power and help control floods [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Mark by the Analysis (6) levels. Indicative content:
- *Upstream and downstream interests:* Punjab lies upstream; Sindh, downstream, fears that canals and
  dams in Punjab reduce the flow it receives and the water reaching the delta.
- *Variable supply:* river flow changes greatly between seasons and years, so fixed shares are hard to
  deliver in dry periods, and each province accuses others of taking more than its share.
- *Disputed projects:* proposals such as a dam at Kalabagh have been opposed by downstream provinces,
  delaying storage.
- *Measurement and trust:* without reliable measurement of flows, provinces doubt each other's figures;
  IRSA was created to regulate distribution, but disputes continue. **[6]**

**11.** Mark by the Analysis (6) levels. Indicative content:
- *Disease:* untreated sewage in water supplies spreads diarrhoea and typhoid, which particularly
  affect children; arsenic in some groundwater causes long-term illness.
- *Cost and time:* families buy bottled water, boil water or pay for tankers, and in rural areas women
  and girls spend hours collecting water.
- *Education and work:* illness and water collection keep children from school and adults from work.
- *Inequality:* poorer households in informal settlements have the least access to safe water.
  **[6]**

**12.** Mark by the Evaluation (9) levels. Indicative content:
- *Agree:* Pakistan can store only a small part of its summer flow; new dams such as Diamer-Bhasha and
  Mohmand add storage, generate hydel power and help control floods.
- *Disagree:* dams are expensive, slow to build, displace people and fill with silt; much water is
  wasted through seepage and flood irrigation, so canal lining, efficient irrigation and groundwater
  regulation could save large amounts at lower cost; pollution and urban supply need other solutions.
- *Judgement:* for example, new storage is necessary, but it is not the best single strategy unless
  combined with using existing water more efficiently. **[9]**

**13.** Mark by the Evaluation (9) levels. Indicative content:
- *Economy:* irrigated agriculture and the textile industry depend on water; shortages reduce yields,
  exports and hydel output, and raise import bills.
- *People:* shortages and contamination cause disease; rural households lose time collecting water;
  urban families pay for tankers; shortages can force migration.
- *Judgement:* for example, the two cannot be separated, because the farm economy employs many of the
  people affected, but the threat to health makes the impact on people more immediate. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reversing the eastern and western rivers.
- Describing water problems without linking them to the economy or people.
- Evaluating dams without considering their costs and alternatives.
- Giving no named dam, barrage, city or agreement.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need percentages for water use?**
No figures are required. It is enough to state that agriculture is by far the largest user and to
explain why.

**Is the Kalabagh dam built?**
No. It has been proposed for decades but remains disputed between provinces, which is why it is a
useful example of the difficulty of sharing water.
