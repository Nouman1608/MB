Subtopic 17.1 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702 (2025–2027) covers the language of oscillations, the defining condition for simple harmonic motion, the equations a = −ω²x, x = x₀ sin ωt, v = v₀ cos ωt and v = ±ω√(x₀² − x²), and the graphs of x, v and a. These notes are condensed for recall. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [Simple harmonic oscillations study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-simple-harmonic-oscillations/); to apply the ideas, try the [Simple harmonic oscillations practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-simple-harmonic-oscillations-practice/).

## Definition

> Simple harmonic motion: the acceleration is **proportional to the displacement** from a fixed point and **directed towards** that point (opposite to the displacement).

```
a = −ω²x
```

The minus sign = "opposite direction"; ω² constant = "proportional".

## Equations (radian mode)

```
T = 1/f = 2π/ω        ω = 2πf
x = x₀ sin ωt         (t = 0 at equilibrium, moving in + direction)
x = x₀ cos ωt         (t = 0 at + extreme, released from rest)
v = v₀ cos ωt         v₀ = ωx₀
v = ±ω√(x₀² − x²)
maximum acceleration = ω²x₀
phase difference = (Δt/T) × 2π
```

Paper 4 data sheet: a = −ω²x, v = v₀ cos ωt, v = ±ω√(x₀² − x²). **Recall** x = x₀ sin ωt and ω = 2π/T.

## Testing for SHM

| Acceleration | SHM? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| a = −36x | Yes, ω = 6.0 rad s⁻¹ | Proportional and opposite |
| a = +9x | No | Same direction as x: runaway |
| a = −4x² | No | Not proportional to x |
| a = −5 | No | Independent of x |

Mass on spring: F = −kx → a = −(k/m)x → SHM with ω² = k/m.

## Where things are maximum and zero

| Position | Displacement | Speed | Acceleration (magnitude) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Equilibrium (x = 0) | 0 | Maximum, ωx₀ | 0 |
| Extremes (x = ±x₀) | Maximum, x₀ | 0 | Maximum, ω²x₀ |

## Graph shapes

| Graph | Shape | Key feature |
|---|---|---|
| x against t | Sine | Amplitude x₀, period T |
| v against t | Cosine | Gradient of x–t; leads x by π/2 |
| a against t | Negative sine | Gradient of v–t; antiphase with x (π) |
| a against x | Straight line through origin, negative gradient | Gradient = −ω²; the graphical test for SHM |
| v against x | Ellipse | v = ±ωx₀ at x = 0; v = 0 at x = ±x₀ |

## Reading an x–t graph in four steps

1. **Amplitude:** read the peak height from the time axis, not peak to trough.
2. **Period:** measure across several complete cycles and divide, for precision; then ω = 2π/T.
3. **Velocity at a moment:** draw a tangent; its gradient is v. Steepest at x = 0, flat at the peaks.
4. **Acceleration at a moment:** read x and use a = −ω²x; a is positive whenever x is negative.

## Choosing sin or cos

- Clock starts at equilibrium, moving in the positive direction: x = x₀ sin ωt, v = v₀ cos ωt.
- Clock starts at an extreme, released from rest: x = x₀ cos ωt, v = −v₀ sin ωt.
- If a question gives no start condition, use v = ±ω√(x₀² − x²), which needs no time.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Displacement | Distance from equilibrium in a stated direction |
| Amplitude | Maximum displacement from equilibrium |
| Period | Time for one complete oscillation |
| Frequency | Number of oscillations per unit time |
| Angular frequency | ω = 2πf, in rad s⁻¹ |
| Phase difference | Fraction of a cycle by which one oscillation leads another, as an angle |
| Equilibrium position | The fixed point where the resultant force, and so the acceleration, is zero |

## Exam traps

- Calculator in degrees.
- Leaving out "towards a fixed point" or "proportional to displacement" in the definition.
- Amplitude taken as peak-to-peak.
- Timing from one extreme to the other and calling it the period (it is T/2).
- Acceleration said to be maximum at the centre.
- Using f in place of ω.
- Phase difference given in seconds.
- Forgetting the ± when asked for the velocity (not speed) at a displacement.

## Self-test

1. State the two conditions on acceleration for SHM.
2. An oscillator completes 30 oscillations in 12.0 s. Find T, f and ω.
3. The peak-to-peak displacement is 9.0 cm. What is the amplitude?
4. Which of a = −16x, a = 16x, a = −16x³ describes SHM?
5. For a = −16x, find ω and T.
6. An oscillator has amplitude 0.020 m and ω = 30 rad s⁻¹. Find the maximum speed and maximum acceleration.
7. For Question 6, find the speed at x = 0.012 m.
8. Where in the cycle is the acceleration zero?
9. What is the phase difference between displacement and acceleration?
10. Two oscillations with T = 2.0 s reach maximum displacement 0.50 s apart. Find the phase difference in radians.
11. What does the gradient of an a–x graph for SHM equal?
12. Show that a mass on a spring obeying Hooke's law moves with SHM.

**Answers:** 1. Acceleration proportional to displacement from a fixed point; directed towards that point. 2. T = 12.0/30 = 0.40 s; f = 2.5 Hz; ω = 2π × 2.5 = 15.7 rad s⁻¹. 3. 4.5 cm. 4. Only a = −16x. 5. ω = 4.0 rad s⁻¹; T = 2π/4.0 = 1.6 s. 6. v₀ = 30 × 0.020 = 0.60 m s⁻¹; a_max = 30² × 0.020 = 18 m s⁻². 7. v = 30 × √(0.020² − 0.012²) = 30 × 0.016 = 0.48 m s⁻¹. 8. At equilibrium (x = 0). 9. π rad (antiphase). 10. (0.50/2.0) × 2π = π/2 rad. 11. −ω². 12. F = −kx so a = −(k/m)x; k/m is a positive constant, so this has the form a = −ω²x with ω² = k/m.
