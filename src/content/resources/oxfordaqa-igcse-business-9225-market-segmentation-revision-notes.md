Condensed recall for section 3.5.2 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Business (9225). For full explanations and worked examples, use the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-9225-market-segmentation/). Figures are invented.

## What the specification asks

- Understand **how and why** different businesses use segmentation to target customers: by **gender, age, location and income**.
- **Recommend** the most appropriate marketing methods for given situations.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Market | All actual and potential customers for a product |
| Market segmentation | Dividing a market into groups of customers with similar characteristics or needs |
| Segment | One of these groups |
| Mass market | A large market selling a standard product to many customers |
| Niche market | A small, specialised segment of a market |
| Target market | The segment or segments a business chooses to sell to |
| Marketing mix | Product, price, promotion and place |

## Why segment?

- Target spending on those most likely to buy.
- Design products to meet specific needs.
- Raise sales and profit.
- Find segments rivals ignore.
- Choose suitable marketing methods.
- Spot growing opportunities.

## Types of segmentation

| Type | Examples of difference | Example of use |
|---|---|---|
| Age | Children, teenagers, adults, older people | Toys for children; mobility aids for older people |
| Gender | Needs and tastes may differ | Razors, cosmetics, clothes (avoid stereotypes) |
| Location | Climate, culture, urban or rural, country | Raincoats in a wet region; local radio adverts |
| Income | Higher, middle and lower incomes | Luxury brands for high income; discounts for low income |

## Matching methods to segments

| Segment | Suitable methods |
|---|---|
| Young adults | Social media, influencers, mobile offers, student discounts |
| Families | Family offers, shared packs, TV and online adverts |
| Older customers | Local newspapers, direct mail, in-store advice |
| High income | Premium packaging, exclusive events, quality service |
| Low income | Low prices, discounts, loyalty points |
| Local area | Local radio, posters, regional offers |

Segmentation affects all four Ps: **product** design, **price**, **promotion** and **place**.

## Calculations

- Number of buyers = people in the segment x percentage who buy.
- Segment share (%) = buyers in the segment / total buyers x 100.
- Cost per customer won = campaign cost / customers won.
- Revenue = buyers x average spend.

Example: 20% of 8,000 = 1,600 buyers; 12% of 15,000 = 1,800 buyers. Total buyers 4,510. Share of the 25 to 44 segment = 1,800 / 4,510 x 100 = 39.9%.

Campaign example: $12,000 for 1,500 new members costs $8 each; $12,000 for 500 members costs $24 each.

## Drawbacks of segmentation

- Research costs money.
- Segments can be too small to be profitable.
- Segments overlap and change.
- Targeting one group may put off others.
- Risk of stereotyping.

## Exam traps

- Segmentation is **dividing customers into groups**, not choosing a product.
- Segmentation is not the same as market research (which collects information).
- When recommending a method, say **why** it suits the segment.
- Consider **size** of the segment as well as likelihood to buy.
- Not all people in a segment behave alike.
- Link segmentation to all four elements of the marketing mix.

## Model answer outline: a 6-mark "recommend" question

Question style: "Recommend a marketing method for a gym that wants to attract 18 to 30-year-olds."

1. **Method:** a social media campaign with offers for students.
2. **Reason:** this age group spends a lot of time online and responds to discounts.
3. **Evidence:** the campaign costs $18,000 and is expected to win 1,500 members, or $12 per member.
4. **Consider the alternative:** newspaper adverts would reach older people but win fewer members per dollar.
5. **Mix:** the price (student rates), product (evening classes) and place (online sign-up) should fit this segment too.

## Revision checklist

- I can define market segmentation and give three reasons for using it.
- I can segment by age, gender, location and income and give an example of each.
- I can match marketing methods to different segments.
- I can calculate the number of buyers in a segment and the share of total buyers.
- I can calculate the cost per customer won.
- I can state two drawbacks of segmentation.
- I can explain how segmentation affects the four Ps.

## Command words for this subtopic

- **State:** name the type of segmentation (age, gender, location, income).
- **Explain:** segment, the need of that group, the effect on marketing.
- **Recommend:** a method, a reason linked to the segment, and the result you expect.
- **Evaluate:** compare the segments by size, likelihood to buy and cost of reaching them, then choose and justify.

## Self-test

1. Define market segmentation.
2. State the four types of segmentation in the specification.
3. State two reasons why a business segments its market.
4. Give an example of a product that is segmented by age.
5. Give an example of a product that is segmented by income.
6. Recommend one promotion method for teenagers and explain why.
7. Recommend one promotion method for retired customers and explain why.
8. A town has 5,000 people aged 18 to 30 and 8% buy a product. Calculate the number of buyers.
9. A campaign costs $4,000 and wins 250 customers. Calculate the cost per customer.
10. Buyers are 400 in segment A and 600 in segment B. Calculate segment A as a percentage of all buyers.
11. Give one drawback of segmentation.
12. Explain how segmentation might affect the price of a product.

### Answers

1. Dividing a market into groups of customers with similar characteristics or needs.
2. Age, gender, location, income.
3. Any two: target spending; meet needs more precisely; raise sales; find a segment rivals ignore; choose suitable methods.
4. For example, toys for children, or mobility aids for older people.
5. For example, luxury cars for high-income customers, or discount supermarkets for low-income customers.
6. Social media, because teenagers spend a lot of time online and can be reached cheaply.
7. A local newspaper or direct mail, because many older customers prefer them to social media.
8. 8% x 5,000 = **400** buyers.
9. $4,000 / 250 = **$16** per customer.
10. 400 / 1,000 x 100 = **40%**.
11. For example, research costs money, or a segment may be too small to be profitable.
12. A business might charge low prices to a price-sensitive segment, and higher prices to a high-income segment that values quality.
