These questions cover content item 1.3.1, Business aims and objectives, in Topic 1.3 Putting a business idea into practice of the Pearson Edexcel Level 1/Level 2 GCSE (9-1) in Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022. Calculators may be used. Market share is taken as business sales ÷ total market sales × 100 (a standard formula). Every business and figure is invented and the mark splits are the writer's own.

## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a financial objective named in the specification? **[1]**

A: Challenge

B: Personal satisfaction

C: Financial security

D: Independence and control

**2.** Which **one** of the following is the best example of a business objective? **[1]**

A: To be a well-known business

B: To increase sales revenue by 8% within 12 months

C: To do well in the future

D: To have happy customers

**3.** Define the term business aim. **[1]**

**4.** Give **two** non-financial aims or objectives named in the specification. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why a business's objectives may conflict with each other. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **one** reason why the objectives of a sole trader may differ from those of a business with several investors. **[3]**

### Section B: case-study questions

**7.** *Greenacre Gardening* is a garden maintenance business in its second year. In year 1 its sales revenue was £48,000. The owner, Dafydd, set two objectives for year 2:

- increase sales revenue by 20%;
- achieve a 15% share of the local garden maintenance market, which is worth £400,000 a year.

Actual sales revenue in year 2 was £52,800.

**(a)** Calculate Greenacre's target sales revenue for year 2. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the actual percentage increase in sales revenue from year 1 to year 2. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate Greenacre's market share in year 2. **[2]**

**(d)** Using your answers, state whether each objective was met. **[2]**

**8.** Dafydd says: "I started Greenacre because I love working outdoors and I never want a boss again." Outline **one** non-financial objective Dafydd appears to have. **[2]**

**9.** *Saffron Street Kitchen* is a café that employs young people leaving care and trains them for jobs in catering. It made a small profit last year. Its manager is considering two objectives for next year.

Option 1: increase profit by 25%, by employing fewer trainees and more experienced staff.

Option 2: double the number of trainees from four to eight, while aiming only to break even.

Justify which objective Saffron Street Kitchen should choose. **[9]**

**10.** Explain **one** reason why survival is often the main objective in a business's first year. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** C: Financial security [1]. **[1]**

**2.** B: To increase sales revenue by 8% within 12 months [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Only option B has a figure and a time limit; the others are aims at best.

**3.** A long-term, general goal that describes where the owner wants the business to be [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Any two from: social objectives [1]; personal satisfaction [1]; challenge; independence and control. **[2]**

**5.** Achieving one objective can make another harder [1]. For example, cutting prices to win market share means each sale earns less [1], so profit may fall even as market share rises [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Investors put money into a business because they expect a return [1], so they are likely to press for profit objectives [1]. A sole trader answers to no one else and may put personal satisfaction or independence ahead of making the highest possible profit [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** £48,000 × 1.2 = **£57,600** [1].

**(b)** (£52,800 − £48,000) ÷ £48,000 × 100 [1] = **10%** [1].

**(c)** £52,800 ÷ £400,000 × 100 [1] = **13.2%** [1].

**(d)** The revenue objective was not met, because sales rose by 10% against a 20% target (£4,800 short) [1]. The market share objective was not met, because 13.2% is below 15% [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Sales did rise, but an objective is met only if the target is reached.

**8.** Dafydd wants independence and control, because he "never wants a boss again" [1], so he is likely to keep Greenacre small enough to run himself rather than take on a partner who would share decisions [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Personal satisfaction (enjoying working outdoors) is an equally valid answer if developed in context.

**9.** Option 1: a 25% rise in profit would give the café more money to survive a poor month and invest in the business [1]. However, employing fewer trainees goes against the café's purpose of helping young people leaving care [1], which may lose the support of customers who choose it for that reason [1].

Option 2: doubling trainees from four to eight would greatly increase the café's social impact, which is why it exists [1]. Its customers may see this as a reason to stay loyal, protecting sales [1]. The risk is that aiming only to break even leaves no cushion if sales fall [1], and trainees take longer to serve customers, so service quality could slip [1].

Judgement: Saffron Street Kitchen should choose Option 2, because its main aim is social and it already makes a profit, showing it can cover its costs [1]. It should monitor its finances closely and slow the increase in trainees if it starts to make a loss [1]. **[9]**

**10.** In its first year a business has few regular customers [1], so its sales are uncertain while costs such as rent and equipment must be paid from the start [1]. Making sure it can cover its costs and keep trading is therefore more important than growth or profit, because without survival no other objective can be achieved [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Choosing a vague statement as an example of an objective.
- Using the year 2 figure as the base for the percentage increase.
- Saying an objective was met because the figure rose.
- Calling financial security a non-financial objective.
- Recommending an objective without linking it to the business's purpose.
