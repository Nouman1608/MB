These questions follow OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560, Version 2.1 (August 2026), section 5.02, Direct and inverse proportion (5.02a and 5.02b). Questions 11 to 14 are **Higher tier only** and are labelled; the rest are for both tiers. A calculator may be used. All prices and exchange rates are invented. Section A has short questions; Section B has longer and formal questions.

Revise first with the [5.02 study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-direct-and-inverse-proportion-ocr-gcse-maths/) or the [5.02 revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-direct-and-inverse-proportion-ocr-gcse-maths-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** 7 m of fabric costs £31.50. Find the cost of 12 m of the same fabric. **[2]**

**2.** The exchange rate is £1 = €1.16.

**(a)** Convert £350 to euros. **[1]**

**(b)** Convert €145 to pounds. **[1]**

**3.** Juice is sold in a 500 ml carton for £1.35 and a 1.5 litre bottle for £3.90. Which is better value? Show your working. **[2]**

**4.** At 40 km/h a journey takes 45 minutes. How long does the same journey take at 60 km/h? **[2]**

**5.** State whether each table shows direct proportion, inverse proportion or neither. Give a reason.

**(a)** x: 2, 3, 6 and y: 9, 6, 3 **[1]**

**(b)** x: 4, 10, 15 and y: 6, 15, 22.5 **[1]**

**6.** y is directly proportional to x. When x = 30, y = 12. Find a formula for y in terms of x. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** y is directly proportional to x. When x = 18, y = 45.

**(a)** Find y when x = 26. **[2]**

**(b)** Find x when y = 100. **[1]**

**8.** 8 workers can build a wall in 6 days.

**(a)** How long would 12 workers take? **[2]**

**(b)** How many workers would be needed to build the wall in 2 days? **[1]**

**(c)** State one assumption you have made. **[1]**

**9.** m is inversely proportional to n. When n = 2.5, m = 8.

**(a)** Find a formula for m in terms of n. **[2]**

**(b)** Find m when n = 16. **[1]**

**(c)** Find n when m = 0.4. **[1]**

**10.** The exchange rates are £1 = €1.16 and €1 = $1.10. Convert £500 to dollars. **[2]**

**11.** (Higher tier only) y is directly proportional to the square of x. When x = 4, y = 48.

**(a)** Find a formula for y in terms of x. **[2]**

**(b)** Find y when x = 7. **[1]**

**(c)** Find the positive value of x when y = 300. **[2]**

**12.** (Higher tier only) The intensity I of a light is inversely proportional to the square of the distance d from the light. When d = 2, I = 36.

**(a)** Find I when d = 6. **[3]**

**(b)** The distance from the light is halved. What happens to the intensity? **[1]**

**13.** (Higher tier only) y is directly proportional to √x. When x = 25, y = 15.

**(a)** Find y when x = 64. **[3]**

**(b)** Find x when y = 4.5. **[2]**

**14.** (Higher tier only) The table shows values of x and y.

| x | 2 | 4 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| y | 0.5 | 4 | 13.5 |

y is directly proportional to a power of x. Find a formula for y in terms of x. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** £31.50 ÷ 7 = £4.50 per metre [1]; × 12 = **£54** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Check with ratios: 31.50/7 = 54/12 = 4.5.

**2. (a)** 350 × 1.16 = **€406** [1]

**(b)** 145 ÷ 1.16 = **£125** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* A pound buys more than a euro, so the pound amount in part (b) must be smaller than 145.

**3.** Same measure for both, such as 27p per 100 ml and 26p per 100 ml [1]; **the 1.5 litre bottle** is better value [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Convert 1.5 litres to 1 500 ml first, so both are per 100 ml.

**4.** Distance 40 × 0.75 = 30 km, or 45 × 40 ÷ 60 [1]; **30 minutes** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* A faster speed must give a shorter time. 67.5 minutes would mean direct proportion was used.

**5. (a)** xy = 18 in every pair, so **inverse proportion** [1]

**(b)** y/x = 1.5 in every pair, so **direct proportion** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The reason earns the mark: name the constant product or constant ratio.

**6.** 12 = 30k, so k = 0.4 [1]; **y = 0.4x** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* y = (2/5)x is equally correct.

**7. (a)** k = 45 ÷ 18 = 2.5 [1]; y = 2.5 × 26 = **65** [1]

**(b)** 100 ÷ 2.5 = **40** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Write y = 2.5x before substituting, so both parts use the same equation.

**8. (a)** 8 × 6 = 48 worker-days [1]; 48 ÷ 12 = **4 days** [1]

**(b)** 48 ÷ 2 = **24 workers** [1]

**(c)** For example, all the workers work at the same rate (or the work can be shared equally) [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* More workers means fewer days, so this is inverse proportion.

**9. (a)** 8 = k ÷ 2.5, so k = 20 [1]; **m = 20/n** [1]

**(b)** 20 ÷ 16 = **1.25** [1]

**(c)** 0.4 = 20/n, so n = 20 ÷ 0.4 = **50** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* For inverse proportion k is the product mn, so k = 8 × 2.5 = 20.

**10.** 500 × 1.16 = €580 [1]; 580 × 1.10 = **$638** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Keep the euro value unrounded if it is not exact, and round the final answer to the nearest cent.

**11. (a)** y = kx² and 48 = 16k [1]; **y = 3x²** [1]

**(b)** 3 × 49 = **147** [1]

**(c)** 300 = 3x², so x² = 100 [1]; **x = 10** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* In part (c), divide by 3 first, then take the square root.

**12. (a)** I = k/d² [1]; 36 = k/4, so k = 144 [1]; I = 144/36 = **4** [1]

**(b)** The intensity is **multiplied by 4** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Halving d multiplies d² by 1/4, so I = k/d² is multiplied by 4.

**13. (a)** y = k√x [1]; 15 = 5k, so k = 3 [1]; y = 3 × 8 = **24** [1]

**(b)** √x = 4.5 ÷ 3 = 1.5 [1]; x = 1.5² = **2.25** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* To find x, undo the multiplication by 3, then square to undo the root.

**14.** Doubling x (2 to 4) multiplies y by 8 = 2³, so y ∝ x³ [1]; 0.5 = k × 8, so k = 1/16 [1]; **y = x³/16** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Check with the third pair: 6³ ÷ 16 = 216 ÷ 16 = 13.5.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating an inverse situation as direct, so the answer moves the wrong way.
- Comparing different measures in a best buy.
- Converting currency in the wrong direction.
- Finding k but not writing the equation with k in it.
- Giving a reason without naming what stays constant.
- (Higher tier only) Forgetting to square or square-root at the right step.
- (Higher tier only) Describing a scaling effect with the wrong power.
