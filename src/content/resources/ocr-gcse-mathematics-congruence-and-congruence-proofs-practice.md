## Questions

**1.** Four triangles are described.
Triangle P: sides 8 cm, 10 cm, 12 cm.
Triangle Q: sides 12 cm, 8 cm, 10 cm.
Triangle R: sides 8 cm and 6 cm with a 40° angle between them.
Triangle S: sides 8 cm and 6 cm with a 40° angle opposite the 6 cm side.

**(a)** Show that P and Q are congruent, naming the condition. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain whether R and S can be proved congruent. **[2]**

**2.** Name the condition (SSS, SAS, ASA or RHS) that proves each pair congruent.

**(a)** Both triangles have sides 5 cm, 12 cm and 13 cm. **[1]**
**(b)** Both have sides 6 cm and 9 cm with a 70° angle between them. **[1]**
**(c)** Both are right-angled, with hypotenuse 10 cm and another side 6 cm. **[1]**
**(d)** Both have angles 50° and 60°, and the 8 cm side between those two angles. **[1]**

**3.** Triangle ABC is congruent to triangle ZXY, with A matching Z, B matching X and C matching Y. AB = 12.4 cm, BC = 9.1 cm, angle A = 33° and angle C = 95°.

**(a)** Write down the lengths of ZX and XY. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the sizes of angle Z and angle X. **[2]**

**4.** ABCD is a square. E lies on AB and F lies on BC, with AE = BF. Prove that DE = AF. **[4]**

**5.** PQRS is a parallelogram. The diagonals PR and QS meet at O. Prove that OP = OR. **[5]**

**6.** O is the centre of a circle. AB and CD are chords. M is the foot of the perpendicular from O to AB, and N is the foot of the perpendicular from O to CD. OM = ON. Prove that AM = CN. **[4]**

**7.** In triangle ABC, AB = AC. D lies on AB and E lies on AC with BD = CE. Prove that BE = CD. **[5]**

**8.** **(a)** Rani says two triangles are congruent because both have angles of 50°, 60° and 70°. Explain why she is wrong. **[2]**
**(b)** Tom says two triangles are congruent because both have sides of 5 cm and 7 cm and a 30° angle. What extra information about the 30° angle would make his argument valid? **[1]**

**9.** Triangle ABC is congruent to triangle DEF, with A matching D, B matching E and C matching F. AB = (4x − 3) cm, DE = (2x + 9) cm, BC = (y + 7) cm, EF = (3y − 5) cm, angle C = (2z + 14)° and angle F = (3z − 16)°.

**(a)** Find x and the length of AB. **[2]**
**(b)** Find y and the length of BC. **[2]**
**(c)** Find z and the size of angle C. **[2]**

**10.** ABCD is a rhombus, so all four sides are equal. The diagonal AC is drawn.

**(a)** Prove that triangle ABC is congruent to triangle ADC. **[3]**
**(b)** Hence prove that AC bisects angle DAB. **[2]**
**(c)** Angle ABC = 70°. Find angle BAC, giving a reason. **[3]**

**11.** Triangle PQR has PQ = PR. X lies on PQ and Y lies on PR with QX = RY. The lines QY and RX meet at Z.

**(a)** Prove that triangle XQR is congruent to triangle YRQ. **[4]**
**(b)** Angle XRQ = 35°. Explain why angle YQR = 35°. **[1]**
**(c)** Find angle QZR. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** P has sides 8, 10, 12 and Q has sides 12, 8, 10, so all three pairs of sides are equal [1]. Condition SSS, so P and Q are congruent **[2]** [1]
**(b)** In R the 40° angle is between the 8 cm and 6 cm sides, but in S it is opposite the 6 cm side [1]. The equal angle is in a different position, so the SAS condition is not met and congruence cannot be proved **[2]** [1]
*Examiner insight:* in part (b) say where the angle is in each triangle. A bare "no" gives the reader nothing to credit.

**2. (a)** **SSS** [1]
**(b)** **SAS** [1]
**(c)** **RHS** [1]
**(d)** **ASA** [1]
*Examiner insight:* name the condition with all its letters. For (c), the right angle is part of RHS, so say it is present rather than just writing "H".

**3. (a)** ZX matches AB, so ZX = **12.4 cm** [1]. XY matches BC, so XY = **9.1 cm** [1]
**(b)** Angle Z matches angle A, so angle Z = **33°** [1]. Angle B = 180° − 33° − 95° = 52°, and angle X matches B, so angle X = **52°** [1]
*Examiner insight:* the order of the letters in ABC ≅ ZXY decides the matching. Write the pairs before substituting numbers.

**4.** Triangles DAE and ABF.
DA = AB, because the sides of a square are equal [1]
Angle DAE = angle ABF = 90°, the angles of a square [1]
AE = BF, given [1]
The triangles are congruent (SAS), with the right angle between the equal sides. Corresponding sides are equal, so **DE = AF** **[4]** [1]
*Examiner insight:* each fact needs its reason, and you must state the condition before the conclusion. A correct conclusion on its own does not show the proof.

**5.** Triangles OPQ and ORS.
Angle OPQ = angle ORS, alternate angles, PQ parallel to SR [1]
Angle OQP = angle OSR, alternate angles, PQ parallel to SR [1]
PQ = SR, opposite sides of a parallelogram [1]
The triangles are congruent (ASA) [1]
Corresponding sides are equal, so **OP = OR** **[5]** [1]
*Examiner insight:* the equal side PQ = SR lies between the two equal angles in each triangle, so say that when you quote ASA.

**6.** Triangles OMA and ONC.
Angle OMA = angle ONC = 90°, perpendiculars [1]
OA = OC, both are radii, so the hypotenuses are equal [1]
OM = ON, given [1]
The triangles are congruent (RHS), so **AM = CN** **[4]** [1]
*Examiner insight:* in RHS, label the equal sides as hypotenuses. A right angle alone is not a full reason.

**7.** Triangles DBC and ECB.
BD = CE, given [1]
Angle DBC = angle ECB, base angles of the isosceles triangle ABC [1]
BC = CB, common side [1]
The triangles are congruent (SAS) [1]
Corresponding sides are equal, so **BE = CD** **[5]** [1]
*Examiner insight:* the base angles result may be quoted as a reason here. Make sure the angles you name are the ones between the equal sides.

**8. (a)** Three equal angles mean the triangles have the same shape [1]. They can still be different sizes, for example one with a 4 cm side and one with a 9 cm side, so AAA does not prove congruence **[2]** [1]
**(b)** The 30° angle would have to be **between** the 5 cm and 7 cm sides (SAS) [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* give a concrete case in (a), such as two sizes of triangle. "They might not be the same" is vague.

**9. (a)** 4x − 3 = 2x + 9 [1], so x = 6 and AB = 4(6) − 3 = **21 cm** **[2]** [1]
**(b)** y + 7 = 3y − 5 [1], so y = 6 and BC = 6 + 7 = **13 cm** **[2]** [1]
**(c)** 2z + 14 = 3z − 16 [1], so z = 30 and angle C = 2(30) + 14 = **74°** **[2]** [1]
*Examiner insight:* substitute back into both expressions. If the two values differ, you have set up the wrong equation.

**10. (a)** Triangles ABC and ADC.
AB = AD, sides of a rhombus [1]
BC = DC, sides of a rhombus [1]
AC is common, and the triangles are congruent (SSS) [1]
**(b)** Corresponding angles are equal, so angle BAC = angle DAC [1]. Hence AC bisects angle DAB **[2]** [1]
**(c)** Triangle ABC has AB = BC, so it is isosceles [1]. Base angles are equal, so angle BAC = (180° − 70°) ÷ 2 [1] = **55°** **[3]** [1]
*Examiner insight:* in (b) point to the pair of corresponding angles. For (c), say which two sides are equal so the base angles can be justified.

**11. (a)** Triangles XQR and YRQ.
XQ = YR, given [1]
Angle XQR = angle YRQ, base angles of isosceles triangle PQR [1]
QR is common [1]
The triangles are congruent (SAS) **[4]** [1]
**(b)** Corresponding angles in congruent triangles are equal, so angle YQR = angle XRQ = 35° [1] **[1]**
**(c)** Triangle ZQR has angles YQR = 35° and XRQ = 35° [1]. Angle QZR = 180° − 35° − 35° = **110°** **[2]** [1]
*Examiner insight:* in (c) use the result from (b) rather than starting again.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a claim without a reason, such as "AB = CD" with no reference to the question or a property.
- Naming the wrong condition, or using SSA or AAA.
- Quoting SAS when the angle is not between the two sides.
- Writing the vertices in an order that does not match, so the final deduction names the wrong sides.
- Leaving out "common side" or "vertically opposite angles" when a fact comes from the diagram.
- Ending with "congruent" and not stating the length or angle that follows.
- Setting corresponding quantities equal in the wrong pairs when solving for x, y or z.
- Using an isosceles result without saying which sides are equal.
