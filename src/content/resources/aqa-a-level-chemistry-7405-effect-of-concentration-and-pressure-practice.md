These questions cover section **3.1.5.4 Effect of concentration and pressure** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2 (and Paper 3). Learn the methods in the [Effect of concentration and pressure study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-effect-of-concentration-and-pressure/) and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-effect-of-concentration-and-pressure-revision-notes/). R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.

## Questions

**1.** Explain why increasing the concentration of a reactant in solution increases the rate of reaction. **[3]**

**2.** A student writes: "A higher concentration gives particles more energy, so a greater proportion of collisions are successful." Identify and correct the error. **[2]**

**3.** Explain why increasing the pressure increases the rate of the reaction 2NO(g) + O₂(g) → 2NO₂(g). **[3]**

**4.** Calculate the concentration of a gas in mol dm⁻³ at a pressure of 150 kPa and a temperature of 300 K, using n ÷ V = p ÷ RT. **[3]**

**5.** Which of these reactions would be affected by a change in pressure? Give a reason for each.

**(a)** CaCO₃(s) + 2HCl(aq) → CaCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g) **[1]**

**(b)** N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) → 2NH₃(g) **[1]**

**(c)** NaOH(aq) + HNO₃(aq) → NaNO₃(aq) + H₂O(l) **[1]**

**6.** 40.0 cm³ of 2.50 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid is diluted to 200 cm³ with water. Calculate the new concentration and explain what happens to the rate of reaction with magnesium ribbon. **[3]**

**7.** Marble chips react with an excess of hydrochloric acid and the volume of carbon dioxide is recorded.

| Time / s | 0 | 20 | 40 | 60 | 80 | 100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Volume / cm³ | 0 | 30 | 50 | 63 | 71 | 76 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate over the first 20 s and over the interval from 60 s to 80 s. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the two rates differ. **[2]**

**(c)** State how the rate at exactly 40 s could be found from a graph. **[2]**

**8.** The experiment in question 7 is repeated with a more concentrated acid, also in excess, and the same mass of marble. Describe two ways in which the graph of volume against time differs and one way in which it is the same. **[3]**

**9.** Name an apparatus, other than a gas syringe, that could be used to follow the rate of the reaction between marble chips and hydrochloric acid. Describe what is measured. **[2]**

**10.** Explain why the proportion of molecules with energy equal to or greater than the activation energy is not changed by a change in concentration. **[1]**

**11.** Explain why a reaction between two solids is not affected by a change in the pressure of the surrounding air. **[2]**

**12.** The volume–time graph for the reaction in question 7 levels off at a constant volume. Explain why the rate becomes zero. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** A higher concentration means more particles in the same volume [1]; so the particles collide more frequently [1]; the proportion of collisions with energy equal to or greater than Eₐ is unchanged, but there are more successful collisions per second, so the rate increases [1]. **[3]**

**2.** Concentration does not change the energy of the particles (the temperature is unchanged) [1]; the proportion of successful collisions stays the same, but the frequency of collisions, and so of successful collisions, increases [1]. **[2]**

**3.** A higher pressure puts the gas molecules into a smaller volume, so there are more molecules per unit volume [1]; so the molecules collide more frequently [1]; this increases the frequency of successful collisions, so the rate increases [1]. **[3]**

**4.** T = 300 K, p = 1.50 × 10⁵ Pa [1]; n ÷ V = 1.50 × 10⁵ ÷ (8.31 × 300) = 60.2 mol m⁻³ [1]; = **0.0602 mol dm⁻³** [1]. **[3]**

**5. (a)** Not affected: the reactants are a solid and a solution; the gas produced does not change the reactant collision frequency [1]

**(b)** Affected: the reactants are gases, so pressure changes their concentration [1]

**(c)** Not affected: the reactants are in solution [1]. **[3]**

**6.** c = 2.50 × 40.0 ÷ 200 = **0.500 mol dm⁻³** [1]; the concentration is one fifth, so there are fewer acid particles per unit volume [1]; collisions are less frequent, so the rate decreases [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** 0 to 20 s: 30 ÷ 20 = **1.5 cm³ s⁻¹** [1]; 60 to 80 s: (71 − 63) ÷ 20 = **0.40 cm³ s⁻¹** [1]

**(b)** The acid concentration falls as the reaction proceeds [1]; so collisions are less frequent and the rate is lower later [1]

**(c)** Draw a tangent to the curve at 40 s [1]; calculate its gradient [1]. **[6]**

**8.** The initial gradient is steeper [1]; the curve reaches its plateau sooner [1]; the final volume of gas is the same, because the marble is the limiting reagent [1]. **[3]**

**9.** A balance [1]; the loss in mass is recorded at intervals, as carbon dioxide escapes from the flask [1]. **[2]**

**10.** The temperature, and hence the distribution of energies, is unchanged [1]. **[1]**

**11.** The particles of a solid are already close together and cannot be compressed [1]; so the concentration of the reacting solids is unchanged by pressure [1]. **[2]**

**12.** The limiting reagent (the marble) is used up, so no more carbon dioxide can form [1]; the concentration of the reactant that is used up has fallen to zero, so there are no more successful collisions that give product [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that a higher concentration gives particles more energy.
- Omitting "frequency" or "per unit time".
- Saying that pressure affects the rate of reactions in solution.
- Forgetting that the rate decreases during the reaction.
- Giving the wrong final volume when the limiting reagent is unchanged.
