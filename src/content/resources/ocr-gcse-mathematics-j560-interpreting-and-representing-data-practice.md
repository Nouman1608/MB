These questions follow OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560, Version 2.1 (August 2026), section 12.02, Interpreting and representing data. Questions 1 to 9 use statement 12.02a and are for **both tiers**. Questions 10 to 12 use statement 12.02b and are **Higher tier only**. Section A has short questions; Section B has longer ones. All data are invented.

Learn the methods first in the [12.02 study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-interpreting-and-representing-data/) or the [12.02 revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-interpreting-and-representing-data-revision-notes/). ## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State whether each variable is categorical, discrete or continuous.

**(a)** Shoe size. **[1]**

**(b)** Favourite colour. **[1]**

**(c)** Time taken to run 100 m. **[1]**

**(d)** Which of the three variables could be shown on a vertical line chart? **[1]**

**2.** 60 students say which pet they have: dog 21, cat 15, fish 9, none 15. Work out the angle of each sector of a pie chart. **[3]**

**3.** A pie chart shows how 540 people travel to work. The sector for cycling has an angle of 70°. How many people cycle? **[2]**

**4.** In a pictogram of cinema tickets sold, one symbol represents 12 tickets.

**(a)** Monday shows 2½ symbols. How many tickets were sold? **[1]**

**(b)** Tuesday shows 3¼ symbols. How many tickets were sold? **[1]**

**(c)** On Wednesday 42 tickets were sold. How many symbols should be drawn? **[1]**

**5.** A composite bar chart shows the sport chosen by students. The Year 7 bar has sections football 0 to 28, netball 28 to 47 and tennis 47 to 60. The Year 8 bar has sections football 0 to 31, netball 31 to 42 and tennis 42 to 66.

**(a)** How many Year 7 students chose netball? **[1]**

**(b)** How many Year 7 students are there in total? **[1]**

**(c)** Which year group had more students choosing tennis, and by how many? **[2]**

**6.** A survey asks for age in the groups 10–20, 20–30, 30–40. Give two criticisms of these groups. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** 200 visitors to a city each chose one attraction. 120 of the visitors were adults. 70 visitors chose the museum, and 45 of these were adults. 60 visitors chose the zoo. 38 children chose the aquarium.

**(a)** Draw and complete a two-way table showing this information. **[3]**

**(b)** What fraction of the visitors who chose the zoo were children? **[1]**

**8.** A shop records umbrella sales (in hundreds) each quarter.

| Year | Jan–Mar | Apr–Jun | Jul–Sep | Oct–Dec |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | 34 | 21 | 15 | 38 |
| Year 2 | 37 | 24 | 17 | 42 |
| Year 3 | 40 | 26 | 20 | 45 |

**(a)** Describe the seasonal variation in the sales. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe the trend, giving evidence from the table. **[2]**

**(c)** Predict the sales for January to March in Year 4. Give a reason for your answer. **[2]**

**9.** In Town A, 400 people were surveyed and the pie chart sector for "bus" is 72°. In Town B, 250 people were surveyed and the "bus" sector is 108°. Sam says, "More people in Town B travel by bus."

**(a)** Work out the number of people who travel by bus in each survey. **[2]**

**(b)** Is Sam correct? Explain your answer. **[1]**

**10.** **(Higher tier only)** The finishing times of 120 runners are recorded.

| Time t (min) | 20<t≤25 | 25<t≤30 | 30<t≤35 | 35<t≤40 | 40<t≤50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 8 | 22 | 41 | 30 | 19 |

**(a)** Work out the cumulative frequencies. **[2]**

**(b)** Write down the coordinates of the points you would plot for the cumulative frequency graph. **[1]**

**(c)** A smooth curve through the points passes through (38, 89). Estimate the number of runners who took more than 38 minutes. **[2]**

**11.** **(Higher tier only)** The masses of 100 parcels are recorded.

| Mass m (kg) | 0<m≤1 | 1<m≤2 | 2<m≤4 | 4<m≤8 | 8<m≤10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 15 | 25 | 30 | 24 | 6 |

**(a)** Work out the frequency density of each class, ready to draw a histogram. **[2]**

**(b)** Which class will have the tallest bar? Is it the class with the greatest frequency? Explain. **[2]**

**(c)** Estimate the number of parcels with a mass between 3 kg and 5 kg. **[2]**

**12.** **(Higher tier only)** Part of a histogram shows a bar over 10<x≤15 with frequency density 4.4, and a bar over 15<x≤30 with frequency density 1.2.

**(a)** Find the total frequency of these two classes. **[2]**

**(b)** The next class, 30<x≤40, has frequency 13. Find the height of its bar. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Discrete** (sizes go up in fixed steps) [1]

**(b)** **Categorical** [1]

**(c)** **Continuous** [1]

**(d)** **Shoe size** [1]

Total: 4

**2.** 360 ÷ 60 = 6° per student [1]; dog 21 × 6 = 126°, cat 15 × 6 = 90° [1]; fish 9 × 6 = 54°, none 15 × 6 = 90° [1]

Total: 3

*Examiner insight:* Show the check 126 + 90 + 54 + 90 = 360. A total that is not 360° tells you an angle is wrong.

**3.** 70/360 × 540 [1]; = **105 people** [1]

Total: 2

**4. (a)** 2.5 × 12 = **30 tickets** [1]

**(b)** 3.25 × 12 = **39 tickets** [1]

**(c)** 42 ÷ 12 = **3½ symbols** [1]

Total: 3

**5. (a)** 47 − 28 = **19** [1]

**(b)** **60** (the top of the bar) [1]

**(c)** Year 7 tennis = 60 − 47 = 13; Year 8 tennis = 66 − 42 = 24 [1]; **Year 8**, by **11** students [1]

Total: 4

*Examiner insight:* Reading the top of the tennis sections (60 and 66) gives a difference of 6, which is wrong. Subtract each section's bottom edge.

**6.** The groups **overlap**: ages 20 and 30 each fit two groups [1]. They do **not cover every age**: there is no group for under 10 or over 40 [1]

Total: 2

**7. (a)**

| | Museum | Zoo | Aquarium | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Adults | 45 | 43 | 32 | 120 |
| Children | 25 | 17 | 38 | 80 |
| Total | 70 | 60 | 70 | 200 |

Children total 80 and aquarium total 70 [1]; children museum 25 and children zoo 80 − 25 − 38 = 17 [1]; adults zoo 43 and adults aquarium 32 [1]

**(b)** **17/60** [1]

Total: 4

*Examiner insight:* Check the adults row: 45 + 43 + 32 = 120. In part (b) the denominator is the zoo total, 60, not 200.

**8. (a)** Sales are highest from October to March and lowest from July to September, every year [1]

**(b)** The trend is **upward** [1]: each quarter's sales are higher than the same quarter the year before, for example January to March rises 34, 37, 40 [1]

**(c)** About **43 hundred** (4300) [1], because January to March sales have risen by 3 hundred each year and the pattern is assumed to continue [1]

Total: 5

*Examiner insight:* Comparing 38 with 37 (October to December then January to March) mixes seasons. Use the same quarter each year.

**9. (a)** Town A: 72/360 × 400 = **80**; Town B: 108/360 × 250 = **75** [1] [1]

**(b)** **No.** A greater **proportion** of people in Town B use the bus, but the actual number is smaller (75 compared with 80) [1]

Total: 3

**10. (a)** 8, 30, 71 [1]; 101, 120 [1]

**(b)** (20, 0), (25, 8), (30, 30), (35, 71), (40, 101), (50, 120) [1]

**(c)** 120 − 89 [1]; = **about 31 runners** [1]

Total: 5

*Examiner insight:* The points go at the upper class boundaries. The reading at 38 is the number who took **up to** 38 minutes, so subtract it from the total.

**11. (a)** 15 ÷ 1 = 15, 25 ÷ 1 = 25, 30 ÷ 2 = 15 [1]; 24 ÷ 4 = 6, 6 ÷ 2 = 3 [1]

**(b)** The **1 to 2 kg** class (density 25) has the tallest bar [1]. It is **not** the greatest frequency: 2 to 4 kg has 30 parcels, but its bar is spread over a width of 2, so it is lower [1]

**(c)** 3 to 4 kg: 1 × 15 = 15; 4 to 5 kg: 1 × 6 = 6 [1]; estimate = **21 parcels** [1]

Total: 6

*Examiner insight:* For part (c), multiply density by the part of the width you need. This assumes the parcels are spread evenly within each class.

**12. (a)** 5 × 4.4 = 22 and 15 × 1.2 = 18 [1]; total = **40** [1]

**(b)** 13 ÷ 10 = **1.3** [1]

Total: 3

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reading the top of a composite section instead of subtracting.
- Pie chart angles that do not add to 360°, or a missing check.
- Comparing two pie charts as if they had the same total.
- Describing a trend by comparing different seasons.
- Overlapping or incomplete classes in a designed table.
- Plotting cumulative frequency at class midpoints, or forgetting the starting point (Higher).
- Treating histogram bar height as frequency (Higher).
