## Questions

**1.** A letting agent records the distance from the city centre, x km, and the weekly rent, £y, for eight flats.

| x (km) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y (£) | 310 | 295 | 282 | 270 | 255 | 238 | 231 | 214 |

**(a)** Describe the correlation between distance and rent. **[2]**
**(b)** State which variable is the explanatory variable. **[1]**

**2.** A gardener measures the height, h cm, of seedlings d days after planting, for d from 5 to 30. The regression line is h = 3.5 + 1.8d.

**(a)** Interpret the value 1.8 in context. **[2]**
**(b)** Comment on the value 3.5 in context. **[2]**

**3.** Use the regression line in question 2.

**(a)** Estimate the height after 12 days. **[1]**
**(b)** Estimate the height after 60 days, and comment on the reliability of your estimate. **[3]**

**4.** A report finds strong positive correlation between the number of computers per pupil and the average test score across a sample of schools. A headteacher concludes that buying more computers will raise test scores. Comment on this conclusion. **[3]**

**5.** A surveyor records the floor area, x m², and the annual heating bill, £y, for houses with loft insulation and houses without.

| Area (m²) | 60 | 80 | 100 | 120 | 140 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bill, insulated (£) | 520 | 590 | 670 | 740 | 810 |
| Bill, not insulated (£) | 780 | 880 | 990 | 1090 | 1190 |

All ten points are plotted on one scatter diagram.

**(a)** Describe what the diagram shows. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why one line of best fit through all ten points would be unsuitable. **[2]**
**(c)** State what should be done instead. **[1]**

**6.** A regression line y = 120 − 2.5x is to be added to a scatter diagram whose x values run from 10 to 40.

**(a)** Find the y values at x = 10 and x = 40, so the line can be drawn. **[2]**
**(b)** The means are x̄ = 24 and ȳ = 60. Show that the line passes through the mean point. **[1]**
**(c)** A further point (30, 52) is plotted. State whether it lies above or below the line and by how much. **[2]**

**7.** A researcher wants to know whether the hours of sunshine in a day affect the number of visitors to a beach.

**(a)** State which variable should go on the horizontal axis and give a reason. **[2]**
**(b)** The regression line predicts a negative number of visitors for one value of sunshine. What does this tell you about that value? **[1]**

**8.** A warehouse records the number of items packed per hour, y, by new employees x weeks after they start.

| x (weeks) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 12 | 25 | 35 | 43 | 48 | 51 | 52 | 52.5 |

**(a)** Describe the pattern in the data. **[2]**
**(b)** A straight line y = 14.6 + 5.6x is drawn. Show that it overestimates y at both x = 1 and x = 8. **[2]**
**(c)** Use the line to estimate y at x = 12, and comment. **[2]**

**9.** A shop records weekly advertising spend, x (in £100s), and weekly sales, y (in £100s), for x from 12 to 45. The regression line is y = 15.2 + 0.46x.

**(a)** Interpret the gradient. **[2]**
**(b)** Estimate the weekly sales when the advertising spend is £3000, and comment on reliability. **[2]**
**(c)** The owner wants to spend £8000. Use the line to estimate sales and comment. **[2]**
**(d)** The owner says the data prove advertising causes sales. Comment. **[2]**

**10.** An analyst plots daily mean temperature against hours of sunshine for two weather stations on one scatter diagram. The points from station A form a cluster at high sunshine and high temperature. The points from station B form a separate cluster at low sunshine and low temperature. Within each cluster there is no visible trend.

**(a)** Describe the overall correlation shown by the whole diagram. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why concluding that more sunshine raises temperature at either station would be unsafe. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe how the analyst could use a spreadsheet to investigate further. **[2]**
**(d)** State what you would expect to see in a single cluster if sunshine did affect temperature at that station. **[1]**

**11.** A survey of 200 children aged 4 to 16 finds strong positive correlation between daily hours of television watched and height.

**(a)** Suggest a third variable that could explain this and say how it would produce the correlation. **[2]**
**(b)** Write a one-sentence conclusion suitable for a report. **[2]**
**(c)** Give one other possible explanation of a correlation of this type. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Rent falls as distance from the centre rises, so the correlation is negative [1], and the points lie almost along a line, so it is strong [1].
**(b)** Distance from the city centre [1].
*Examiner insight:* name both variables in context.

**2. (a)** Each extra day after planting is associated with an increase in height of about 1.8 cm [1] on average [1].
**(b)** 3.5 cm is the predicted height at d = 0, when planted [1], but d = 0 is outside the data range of 5 to 30, so this is an extrapolation and not reliable [1].
*Examiner insight:* include the unit and the word "predicted" or "on average" in a gradient interpretation.

**3. (a)** h = 3.5 + 1.8 × 12 = 3.5 + 21.6 = **25.1 cm [1]**.
**(b)** h = 3.5 + 1.8 × 60 = 3.5 + 108 = **111.5 cm [1]**. Day 60 is outside the range 5 to 30, so this is extrapolation [1] and unreliable, because seedlings may stop growing at a steady rate [1].
*Examiner insight:* give the reason the estimate is unreliable, not just the word "unreliable".

**4.** The correlation shows an association between computers and scores [1], but it does not show that computers cause higher scores [1]. A third variable, such as school funding or the wealth of the area, could cause both [1].
*Examiner insight:* a causation comment normally needs a specific, plausible alternative explanation in the context. "Correlation is not causation" alone is thin.

**5. (a)** Both groups show positive correlation: larger houses have higher bills [1]. The points form two separate bands, with the uninsulated houses well above the insulated ones at every area [1].
**(b)** The data come from two distinct sections of the population [1]. A single line would pass between the bands and would predict a bill (about £826 at 100 m²) that fits neither group (£670 or £990) [1].
**(c)** Use a separate line of best fit for each group [1].
*Examiner insight:* quoting figures from the table to support "a single line is unsuitable" makes the explanation specific. A statement that the line is "inaccurate" without evidence is weaker.

**6. (a)** y = 120 − 2.5 × 10 = **95 [1]**. y = 120 − 2.5 × 40 = **20 [1]**.
**(b)** At x = 24: y = 120 − 60 = 60, which is ȳ, so the line passes through (24, 60) [1].
**(c)** On the line at x = 30: y = 120 − 75 = 45 [1]. The point has y = 52, so it is **above the line by 7 [1]**.
*Examiner insight:* when asked to add to a diagram, work out the values you will plot first and show the substitutions.

**7. (a)** Hours of sunshine on the horizontal axis [1], because it is the explanatory variable that may influence the number of visitors (the response) [1].
**(b)** That sunshine value is probably outside the range of the data, so the line should not be used there [1].
*Examiner insight:* explanatory goes on x and response on y.

**8. (a)** Positive correlation, since packing rate rises with weeks [1]. The rise is quick at first and then levels off near 52, so the pattern is curved, not linear [1].
**(b)** At x = 1: 14.6 + 5.6 = 20.2, above the observed 12 [1]. At x = 8: 14.6 + 44.8 = 59.4, above the observed 52.5 [1].
**(c)** y = 14.6 + 5.6 × 12 = 14.6 + 67.2 = **81.8 [1]**. This is extrapolation and the data have levelled off, so the true value is likely to be far lower [1].
*Examiner insight:* a strong correlation can still go with a poor straight-line fit. Mention the curve when you reject the line, rather than just calling the correlation weak.

**9. (a)** Each extra £100 spent on advertising is associated with about £46 more weekly sales [1] (0.46 × £100, with units and "associated with") [1].
**(b)** £3000 is x = 30: y = 15.2 + 0.46 × 30 = 15.2 + 13.8 = 29, so about **£2900 [1]**. 30 lies in the range 12 to 45, so this is interpolation and reasonably reliable [1].
**(c)** £8000 is x = 80: y = 15.2 + 36.8 = 52, so about **£5200 [1]**. 80 is outside the range 12 to 45, so this is extrapolation and may be unreliable [1].
**(d)** The data show an association only [1]. Other factors, such as the season, could affect both advertising and sales, so causation is not proved [1].
*Examiner insight:* convert units at the end. The line works in £100s, so reading y = 29 as £29 is an easy slip.

**10. (a)** Positive correlation overall [1]: warmer days go with more sunshine, because the whole cloud runs from the low cluster to the high cluster [1].
**(b)** The overall pattern is produced by the difference between the two stations [1]. Within each station there is no trend, so the data do not show that sunshine raises temperature at either [1].
**(c)** Add a column for station and colour the points by it [1]. Make a separate scatter chart for each station, and compare the sunshine and temperature ranges of each [1].
**(d)** A clear trend (points rising from left to right) inside that cluster [1].
*Examiner insight:* when a diagram has distinct sections, say explicitly that the groups differ.

**11. (a)** Age [1]: older children are taller and also watch more television, so both rise together [1].
**(b)** The data show an association between television watching and height [1], but watching television does not make children taller, and age is a likely explanation for both [1].
**(c)** Coincidence, or the relationship could run the other way [1].
*Examiner insight:* in a conclusion, report what the data show first, then qualify it.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing correlation without using the names of the variables.
- Writing a gradient interpretation without units or without "on average" or "associated with".
- Judging reliability without comparing x with the range of the data.
- Treating the intercept as meaningful when x = 0 is outside the data.
- Using the word "causes" when the data only show association.
- Making a causation comment with no specific alternative explanation.
- Missing separate groups on a scatter diagram.
- Forgetting to convert units, for example sales in £100s.
