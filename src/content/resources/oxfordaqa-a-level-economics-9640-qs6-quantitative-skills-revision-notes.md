These notes condense quantitative skill **QS6** from section 3.5 of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Economics (9640) specification: calculate cost, revenue and profit, including averages and totals, with marginal values required for A2 assessments. Full explanations and worked tables are in the [QS6 study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-economics-9640-qs6-quantitative-skills/); test yourself with the [QS6 practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-economics-9640-qs6-quantitative-skills-practice/). ## Key terms

| Term | Meaning | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed cost (FC) | Cost that does not vary with output in the short run | FC = AFC x Q |
| Variable cost (VC) | Cost that rises with output | VC = AVC x Q |
| Total cost (TC) | All costs of producing a given output | TC = FC + VC |
| Average cost (AC) | Cost per unit | AC = TC / Q = AFC + AVC |
| Total revenue (TR) | Receipts from sales | TR = P x Q |
| Average revenue (AR) | Revenue per unit; equals price when all units sell at one price | AR = TR / Q |
| Profit | Revenue minus cost | TR - TC = (AR - AC) x Q |
| Normal profit | Minimum return to keep the entrepreneur in the industry; counted as a cost | Profit of zero when included in TC |
| Supernormal profit | Profit above normal profit | TR - TC when TC includes normal profit |
| Marginal cost (MC), A2 | Change in TC from one more unit | change in TC / change in Q |
| Marginal revenue (MR), A2 | Change in TR from one more unit | change in TR / change in Q |

## Condensed recall

**Moving between totals and averages**

- Multiply an average by output to get its total; divide a total by output to get its average.
- FC is the same at every output, so AFC halves when output doubles.
- AC = AFC + AVC is the check on every row of a cost table.
- AC and AVC get closer together as output rises, because the gap between them is AFC.

**Profit**

- Profit per unit = AR - AC. Total profit = profit per unit x Q.
- A higher price can raise profit even when revenue falls, because each unit not sold saves its variable cost.
- If AR = AC, the firm earns normal profit (when normal profit is in costs).

**Marginal values (A2)**

- TC at any output = FC + the sum of every MC up to that output.
- TR = the sum of every MR.
- When output changes by more than one unit, divide the change in the total by the change in output.
- MC below AC pulls AC down; MC above AC pulls AC up; MC crosses AC at its lowest point.
- If price must fall to sell more, MR is below price, because the lower price applies to all units.
- Profit is maximised at the output where MR = MC, or in a table, the last unit for which MR is at least MC.

## Worked reminders

- FC 240, output 60, VC 360: TC = 600, AFC = 4, AVC = 6, AC = 10.
- AC is 8 at 50 units: TC = 8 x 50 = 400.
- Price 15, sales 40, AC 11: TR = 600, profit per unit = 4, profit = 160.
- (A2) TC rises from 820 to 900 as output rises from 40 to 50: MC = 80 / 10 = 8 per unit.
- (A2) Sales of 30 at 15.00 (TR 450) rise to 32 at 14.50 (TR 464): MR = 14 / 2 = 7 per unit, well below the price of 14.50.
- (A2) FC 100 and MCs of 30, 25 and 28 for the first three units: TC at 3 units = 100 + 30 + 25 + 28 = 183.

## Reading a cost and revenue table quickly

1. Write the formula for each blank column at the top before you fill any cell.
2. Fill fixed cost first, since it is the same everywhere.
3. Work across each row using whatever the question gives (TC, AC or AVC).
4. Check one row with AC = AFC + AVC.
5. For A2, add MC and MR columns between rows, labelled "per unit over the step".
6. Find the highest profit, then explain it with the marginal values.

## Exam traps

- Calling profit per unit "profit".
- Recalculating fixed cost at each output.
- Using TC / Q for marginal cost.
- Forgetting to divide by the size of the output step.
- Leaving fixed cost out when building TC from MC.
- Assuming MR equals price for a firm that has to cut price to sell more.
- Choosing the option with the highest revenue without checking costs.
- Treating TR = TC as a loss when normal profit is already counted.
- Rounding averages to whole numbers before multiplying back up.

## Self-test

1. Fixed cost is 450. Find AFC at 90 units.
2. AVC is 7.20 at 250 units and FC is 500. Find TC and AC.
3. A firm sells 400 units at 9 and TC is 3,000. Find profit and profit per unit.
4. AC is 12.50 at 80 units. Find TC.
5. Profit per unit is 3 and output is 1,200. Find total profit.
6. A café's owner could earn 25,000 elsewhere. Revenue is 130,000 and other costs are 98,000. Find supernormal profit.
7. Why does AFC always fall as output rises?
8. (A2) TC is 1,240 at 60 units and 1,330 at 70 units. Find MC per unit.
9. (A2) TR is 600 at 40 units and 630 at 45 units. Find MR per unit.
10. (A2) FC is 60. The first three units have MC of 15, 12 and 14. Find TC at three units and AC at three units.
11. (A2) MR of the next unit is 18 and MC is 21. Should the firm produce it? Why?
12. (A2) MC is 9 and AC is 11 at the current output. What happens to AC if output rises by one unit?

### Answers

1. 450 / 90 = 5.
2. VC = 7.20 x 250 = 1,800; TC = 2,300; AC = 2,300 / 250 = 9.20.
3. TR = 3,600; profit = 600; per unit = 600 / 400 = 1.50.
4. 12.50 x 80 = 1,000.
5. 3 x 1,200 = 3,600.
6. 130,000 - 98,000 - 25,000 = 7,000.
7. The same fixed cost is spread over more units.
8. 90 / 10 = 9 per unit.
9. 30 / 5 = 6 per unit.
10. TC = 60 + 15 + 12 + 14 = 101; AC = 101 / 3 = 33.67.
11. No: it adds 21 to cost but only 18 to revenue, so profit falls by 3.
12. AC falls, because the extra unit costs less than the current average.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is average revenue always equal to price?**
Yes when every unit sells at the same price, because TR divided by quantity is then the price. Average revenue is therefore the demand curve the firm faces.

**What is the quickest check on a cost table?**
Add AFC and AVC on any row; the sum must equal AC. If it does not, a total has been divided by the wrong output.
