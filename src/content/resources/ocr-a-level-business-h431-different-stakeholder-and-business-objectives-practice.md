These questions cover the OCR A Level Business (H431) content "Different stakeholder and business objectives" (Version 2.1, January 2026; final assessment summer 2027). Every business named is invented. Start with the [study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-different-stakeholder-and-business-objectives/) and [revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-different-stakeholder-and-business-objectives-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Distinguish between an organisational aim and a corporate objective. **[2]**

**2.** Tarnside Tiles Ltd is a national retailer with 80 stores. Classify each objective as corporate, strategic, tactical or operational.

**(a)** Raise group profit by 10% within three years. **[1]**

**(b)** Open a trade-only warehouse division by the end of year two. **[1]**

**(c)** The marketing department will increase email sign-ups by 20% in six months. **[1]**

**(d)** Each delivery driver completes a vehicle check every morning. **[1]**

**3.** Brindle Toys Ltd has sales revenue of £3.2 million and runs at 82% of its production capacity. Its directors propose to raise sales revenue by 25% within one year. Assume output must rise by the same percentage as revenue.

**(a)** Calculate the target sales revenue. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the capacity utilisation needed to meet the target. **[2]**

**(c)** State which element of SMART the proposal fails, and why. **[1]**

**4.** Explain **one** reason why SMART objectives are important to a bank that lends to a business. **[2]**

**5.** Explain how operating in the third sector is likely to affect the objectives of a charity that runs food banks. **[3]**

**6.** State **two** methods a business with staff on 30 sites could use to communicate a new objective. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Wayfarer Breaks Ltd sells package holidays. Demand fell sharply after a rise in fuel prices. Explain **two** reasons why Wayfarer's objectives may need to change. **[4]**

**8.** Hesketh Foods Ltd has the corporate objective of raising profit by 8% this year. Its sales team has been set the target "increase orders by 15%" and its production department "cut overtime hours by 20%". Analyse how these targets could conflict, and the effect on the corporate objective. **[4]**

**9.** Marrick Hotels Ltd told its reception staff, in a single email, to "maximise revenue from every guest". Staff began pressing guests to pay for room upgrades at check-in. Over six months its average online review score fell from 4.5 to 3.9 out of 5, and two receptionists resigned. Evaluate the consequences for Marrick and its stakeholders of mis-communicating this objective. **[8]**

**10.** Ashby Distribution Ltd runs a warehouse around the clock with three shifts of staff. Evaluate **two** ways in which it could communicate its objectives better. **[6]**

**11.** Pennock Studios is a two-person graphic design start-up. Evaluate the importance of setting aims and objectives for Pennock Studios. **[8]**

**12.** Fernhill Cycles is a family-owned bike shop with annual revenue of £900,000: £600,000 from the shop and £300,000 online. Shop sales are falling by 5% a year; online sales are growing by 30% a year. Its current objective is "grow shop sales". Recommend and justify an objective for Fernhill for the next three years, and how the change should be implemented. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** An organisational aim is a broad statement of long-term purpose, usually without a number or deadline [1]; a corporate objective is a measurable target for the whole business, such as a profit or revenue figure, that helps achieve the aim [1]. **[2]**

**2. (a)** Corporate [1]. **(b)** Strategic [1]. **(c)** Tactical [1]. **(d)** Operational [1]. **[4]**
*Note:* (b) is strategic because it changes the direction of the whole business and commits large resources for years; (c) belongs to one department over months.

**3. (a)** £3.2m × 1.25 = **£4.0 million** [1].

**(b)** 82% × 1.25 [1] = **102.5%** [1].

**(c)** Achievable (or realistic) [1]: the business cannot produce more than 100% of capacity without investment. **[4]**

**4.** The bank can measure the business's progress against clear figures and dates [1], so it can judge whether the business is likely to generate the cash to repay the loan, and act early if targets are missed [1]. **[2]**

**5.** A charity exists for a social purpose rather than to make a profit for owners [1], so its objectives are likely to focus on the people it helps, such as the number of families supported each month [1]; any surplus is reinvested, so financial objectives are about covering costs and keeping donor trust rather than maximising profit [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Any two: company intranet [1]; video briefing [1]; email [1]; managers cascading the message through site briefings [1]; newsletters or noticeboards at each site [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Any two reasons, 2 marks each (reason plus link to Wayfarer):
- A fall in demand is an external change [1], so a growth objective may be unachievable and survival or cost control becomes the priority [1].
- The business may have clearly missed its existing targets [1], so continuing with them would demotivate staff and mislead investors; new targets based on lower demand are needed [1].
- Higher fuel prices raise costs [1], so a profit-margin objective may need to change, or the business may shift to destinations or products less affected by fuel costs [1]. **[4]**

**8.** Winning 15% more orders is likely to need more production hours [1], but production has been told to cut overtime by 20%, so it may refuse or delay extra work [1]. Orders may then be late or lost, damaging customer goodwill [1], and either outcome threatens the 8% profit objective: lost sales cut revenue, while meeting the orders with overtime raises costs, so the departments' targets need to be reconciled through the hierarchy of objectives [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Points (1 mark each, up to 6), then judgement [2]:
- The one-line email gave no explanation of how to raise revenue, so staff read it as "push upgrades" [1].
- Customers felt pressured, so review scores fell by 0.6 points, which may deter future bookings [1].
- Lower reviews may reduce future revenue, the opposite of the intended objective [1].
- Two resignations suggest falling morale; recruiting and training replacements adds cost [1].
- Remaining staff may trust later messages from managers less [1].
- Shareholders may question managers' competence if revenue and reputation fall [1].
- However, upgrade revenue may have risen in the short term, so the financial effect may be mixed [1].
Judgement: the long-term damage to reputation and staff is likely to outweigh any short-term revenue gain, because a hotel's future bookings depend heavily on reviews [1]; Marrick should restate the objective with a clear explanation and a customer-satisfaction target alongside revenue [1]. **[8]**

**10.** Two ways, each with development and evaluation (up to 3 marks each):
- **Short briefings at the start of every shift** [1]: every shift hears the same message and can ask questions, so understanding can be checked [1]; but it relies on three supervisors giving consistent messages, and takes time from each shift [1].
- **A written summary on the intranet and noticeboards** [1]: staff on all shifts can read it at any time and refer back to it [1]; however, written messages may be skimmed or ignored, so it works best alongside face-to-face briefings [1].
Other valid ways: monthly team meetings that rotate across shifts; linking objectives to appraisal targets. **[6]**

**11.** Points (1 mark each, up to 6), then judgement [2]:
- Clear objectives give the two founders a shared direction, so they make consistent decisions about which clients to take [1].
- A target such as monthly revenue lets them check they are covering costs, important to a start-up's survival [1].
- Objectives may be needed to persuade a bank or investor to provide finance [1].
- However, with only two people, communication is easy and informal, so formal written objectives matter less [1].
- A start-up's market is uncertain, so detailed long-term targets may quickly become out of date [1].
- Setting and reviewing objectives takes time the founders could spend on paid work [1].
Judgement: a few simple, short-term objectives (especially on survival and cash) are important, but a detailed hierarchy is unnecessary at this size [1]; their importance will grow if the studio takes on staff [1]. **[8]**

**12.** Up to 10 marks:
- Calculation: next year shop sales £600,000 × 0.95 = £570,000 [1]; online £300,000 × 1.30 = £390,000, so online will be about 41% of £960,000 total revenue [1].
- Recommendation: replace "grow shop sales" with a SMART objective such as "raise online revenue to £650,000 within three years while keeping total revenue growing by at least 5% a year" [1].
- Justification: the trend shows customers moving online, so the current objective works against the market [1]; online growth more than offsets the shop's decline [1].
- The shop still provides 60% of revenue now and supports online sales (servicing, collection), so it should be kept with a supporting tactical objective, such as raising servicing income [1].
- Implementation: explain the reason for the change to family members and staff [1]; set tactical targets for website sales and operational targets for dispatch times [1]; review progress every quarter [1].
- Risk and judgement: online competition is intense and growth of 30% may not continue, so Fernhill should review the target annually; overall, the change is justified because it follows where revenue is growing [1]. **[10]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Classifying objectives by their wording, not by who sets them, scope and time span.
- Calculating the target but not testing it against capacity.
- Saying a charity "has no financial objectives".
- Listing communication methods without evaluating them for the business in the question.
- Recommending an objective without a SMART form, evidence, implementation and a risk.
