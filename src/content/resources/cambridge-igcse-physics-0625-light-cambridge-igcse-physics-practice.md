## Section A

**1.** A girl stands 2.5 m in front of a plane mirror.

**(a)** State the distance between the girl and her image. **[1]**
**(b)** State whether her image is real or virtual, and give a reason. **[2]**

**2.** A ray of light strikes a plane mirror at an angle of 20° to the mirror surface.

**(a)** State the angle of incidence. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the angle between the incident ray and the reflected ray. **[1]**

**3.** State what is meant by the critical angle of a material. **[1]**

**4.** List the seven colours of the visible spectrum in order of increasing frequency. **[2]**

**5.** State one difference between a real image and a virtual image. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** A student investigates refraction using a rectangular glass block and a ray box.

**(a)** Describe how the student finds the angle of incidence and the angle of refraction for one ray. **[4]**
**(b)** State how the direction of the ray leaving the block compares with the direction of the ray entering it. **[1]**

**7.** Two rays of light travel from air into a glass block. Ray X travels along the normal; ray Y meets the block at an angle of incidence of 40°.

**(a)** State what happens to the direction of ray X as it enters the glass. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why ray Y bends towards the normal as it enters the glass. **[2]**

**8.** *(Extended)* A ray of light in air enters water at an angle of incidence of 60°. The refractive index of water is 1.33.

**(a)** Calculate the angle of refraction. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the speed of light in the water. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the critical angle for water. **[2]**

**9.** *(Extended)* A student aims a ray through the curved side of a semicircular glass block towards the centre of its flat side. She finds that the refracted ray just runs along the flat surface when the angle of incidence inside the glass is 41°.

**(a)** Calculate the refractive index of the glass. **[2]**
**(b)** The angle of incidence inside the glass is reduced to 30°. Calculate the angle of refraction in the air. **[2]**
**(c)** State what happens to the light when the angle of incidence inside the glass is increased to 50°. **[1]**

**10.** *(Extended)* This question is about optical fibres.

**(a)** Explain how light travels along an optical fibre, even when the fibre is curved. **[3]**
**(b)** State one use of optical fibres in telecommunications. **[1]**

**11.** An object 2.0 cm tall stands upright on the principal axis, 18 cm from a thin converging lens of focal length 6.0 cm.

**(a)** Describe two rays you would draw from the top of the object to locate the image, and how each is refracted by the lens. **[2]**
**(b)** A correct scale drawing is made. State the distance of the image from the lens and the height of the image. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe the image using the three standard describing terms. **[3]**

**12.** *(Extended)* A student uses a converging lens of focal length 4.0 cm as a magnifying glass to read small print.

**(a)** State where the print must be placed relative to the lens. **[1]**
**(b)** The print is 3.0 cm from the lens. A ray diagram shows the image 12 cm from the lens. State which side of the lens the image is on, and describe the image. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why this image cannot be formed on a screen. **[1]**
**(d)** A short-sighted student cannot see distant objects clearly. State the type of lens that corrects short sight and explain how it helps. **[2]**

**13.** A narrow beam of white light passes through a triangular glass prism and falls on a white screen.

**(a)** Describe what is seen on the screen. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why this happens. **[2]**
**(c)** *(Extended)* The white light is replaced by monochromatic red light. State how the pattern on the screen changes, and give a reason. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The image is 2.5 m behind the mirror, so **5.0 m** [1].
**(b)** **Virtual** [1]; the reflected rays only appear to come from behind the mirror / it cannot be formed on a screen [1]. **[3]**

*Common mistake:* giving 2.5 m in (a). That is the distance to the mirror, not to the image.

**2. (a)** 90° − 20° = **70°** [1].
**(b)** 70° + 70° = **140°** [1]. **[2]**

*Common mistake:* using 20° as the angle of incidence. Angles are always measured from the normal.

**3.** The angle of incidence (in the denser material) for which the angle of refraction is 90° [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet, all seven in the correct order [2] (one pair swapped scores 1 mark). **[2]**

**5.** A real image can be formed on a screen (rays actually meet there); a virtual image cannot (rays only appear to come from it) [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** Draw round the block on paper and shine a narrow ray at one side at an angle [1]. Mark the incident and emerging rays with crosses, remove the block and join the points through the block [1]. Draw the normal at the point where the ray enters [1]. Measure the angles between each ray and the normal with a protractor [1].
**(b)** The emerging ray is **parallel** to the incident ray (displaced sideways) [1]. **[5]**

*Common mistake:* measuring the angles from the edge of the block. Both angles are measured from the normal.

**7. (a)** It does not change direction (it passes straight through along the normal) [1].
**(b)** Light slows down as it enters the glass [1]; one side of the beam slows first, so the ray changes direction towards the normal [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** sin r = sin 60° / 1.33 = 0.866 / 1.33 = 0.651 [1]; r = **40.6°** (41°) [1].
**(b)** speed = 3.0 × 10⁸ / 1.33 [1] = **2.3 × 10⁸ m/s** (2.26 × 10⁸) [1].
**(c)** sin c = 1 / 1.33 = 0.752 [1]; c = **48.8°** (49°) [1]. **[6]**

*Common mistake:* writing sin r = 1.33 × sin 60°, which is greater than 1 and has no angle.

**9. (a)** 41° is the critical angle [1]; n = 1 / sin 41° = 1 / 0.656 = **1.52** [1].
**(b)** For light leaving the glass, sin r = n × sin 30° = 1.52 × 0.500 = 0.76 [1]; r = **49.5°** (accept 49° to 50°) [1].
**(c)** **Total internal reflection**: all the light is reflected back into the glass and none refracts out [1]. **[5]**

*Common mistake:* in (b), dividing by n. The ray goes from glass into air, so the larger angle is in the air.

**10. (a)** Light enters the fibre and meets its wall at an angle of incidence greater than the critical angle [1]. It is totally internally reflected [1], again and again along the fibre, so it follows the fibre round bends until it emerges at the far end [1].
**(b)** Any one: carrying telephone or internet (broadband) data; cable television signals [1]. **[4]**

**11. (a)** A ray parallel to the principal axis, refracted through the principal focus on the far side [1]; a ray through the centre of the lens, which passes straight on undeviated (or a ray through the near principal focus, which emerges parallel to the axis) [1].
**(b)** **9.0 cm** from the lens, on the opposite side to the object [1]; **1.0 cm** tall [1] (allow ± 0.5 cm for a drawing).
**(c)** **Real** [1], **inverted** [1], **diminished** [1]. **[7]**

*Common mistake:* calling the image "smaller and upside down" without stating real. Each describing term earns its own mark.

**12. (a)** Closer to the lens than the principal focus (less than 4.0 cm away) [1].
**(b)** On the **same side** of the lens as the print [1]; **virtual** and **upright** [1]; **enlarged** (four times the height of the print) [1].
**(c)** The rays leaving the lens diverge and never actually meet; they only appear to come from the image [1].
**(d)** A **diverging** lens [1]; it spreads the light out before it enters the eye, so the eye focuses it on the retina instead of in front of it [1]. **[7]**

**13. (a)** A band of colours (a spectrum) [1], from red at one end to violet at the other, with red deviated least [1].
**(b)** The different colours (frequencies) of light are refracted by different amounts [1]; violet is refracted most and red least, so the colours separate (dispersion) [1].
**(c)** *(Extended)* Only a single red patch (no spectrum) appears; it is still deviated [1], because monochromatic light has a single frequency, so there is nothing to separate [1]. **[6]**

*Common mistake:* saying the prism adds colour to the light. The colours were already in the white light; the prism separates them.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Measuring angles from the mirror or block surface instead of the normal.
- Inverting n = sin i / sin r when light leaves glass rather than enters it.
- Stating that some light still refracts out above the critical angle.
- Describing images with fewer than three terms.
- Using solid lines for virtual rays or drawing rays without arrows.
- Saying red light is refracted most by a prism.
