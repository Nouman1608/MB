## Questions

**1.** Define the term "mission statement". **[2]**

**2.** Explain **two** purposes of a mission statement for a business. **[4]**

**3.** Explain what is meant by corporate social responsibility (CSR). **[3]**

**4.** Kestrel Valley Dairy has sales revenue of £9.6 million and operating profit of £1.8 million. It plans to give 1.5% of sales revenue each year to local school food projects.

**(a)** Calculate the annual cost of the donation. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the donation as a percentage of operating profit, to one decimal place. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **two** ways in which a CSR policy might conflict with a business's profit or other objectives. **[6]**

**6.** Aldersgate Tiles employs 140 people making hand-finished tiles for the restoration of old buildings. It sells mainly to architects and builders, and exports 30% of its output. The owners have never had a mission statement.

Recommend and justify a suitable mission statement for Aldersgate Tiles. **[8]**

**7.** Fenwick & Rowe is a consultancy with 60 staff that advises small manufacturers. The founders are considering writing a mission statement.

Evaluate the advantages and disadvantages to Fenwick & Rowe of having a mission statement. **[8]**

**8.** Fenwick & Rowe has decided to change its mission statement to focus on advising firms on sustainable manufacturing. The change will cost £40,000 (new materials, staff training and a website rewrite). The directors expect the change to raise contribution by £12,500 a year. About a quarter of its current clients are in traditional manufacturing.

**(a)** Calculate the payback period for the change. **[2]**
**(b)** Evaluate the impact of the change in mission statement on Fenwick & Rowe and its stakeholders. **[6]**

**9.** Kestrel Valley Dairy buys 12 million litres of milk a year from 40 local farms and sells mostly to supermarkets under their own labels. It is writing a CSR policy that will pay farmers a premium of 2p per litre, switch to recyclable bottles, and publish its results. Operating profit is currently £1.8 million. Some shareholders want the money paid as dividends.

Evaluate the impact and importance of a CSR policy to Kestrel Valley Dairy and its stakeholders. **[10]**

**10.** Aldersgate Tiles is considering a £90,000 investment in solar panels for its kilns building. This is expected to save £15,000 a year in energy costs. It has £400,000 in cash reserves and wants a better reputation with architects.

**(a)** Calculate the payback period. **[2]**
**(b)** Justify whether Aldersgate Tiles should go ahead with the investment. **[6]**

**11.** Kestrel Valley Dairy's premium to farmers of 2p per litre on 12 million litres is paid out of operating profit. Sales revenue is £9.6 million. Each extra pound of sales brings 30p of contribution.

**(a)** Calculate the extra sales revenue needed to cover the premium, and express it as a percentage of current sales revenue, to one decimal place. **[3]**
**(b)** Recommend and justify how Kestrel Valley Dairy could improve its CSR profile further, taking account of the conflict between CSR and profit. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** A short, public statement of what a business is for [1], covering what it does, who it serves and why (its purpose and values) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition tends to need purpose plus one further feature. Confusing it with a numerical target (an objective) risks losing credit.

**2.** Purpose 1: it gives direction and guides decisions [1], because managers can test a proposal against it and reject those that do not fit [1]. Purpose 2: it communicates values to stakeholders [1], so customers, staff and investors know what the business stands for and may be more willing to deal with it [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each purpose needs a reason attached; a bare list of two headings tends to gain less.

**3.** CSR means a business taking responsibility for the effects of its decisions on society and the environment [1], and acting ethically towards its stakeholders, such as employees, suppliers and the community [1], going beyond what the law requires, usually by choice [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Charity" alone is too narrow; mention stakeholders and going beyond the law.

**4. (a)** 1.5% × £9,600,000 [1] = **£144,000** a year [1].
**(b)** 144,000 ÷ 1,800,000 × 100 [1] = **8.0%** [1].
**[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the method. Dividing by sales revenue instead of operating profit is a typical slip.

**5.** Conflict 1: profit. CSR can raise costs, for example paying suppliers more or using greener materials [1], which lowers the profit margin unless prices rise or sales increase [1], and shareholders may expect higher dividends [1]. Conflict 2: growth or cost leadership. Refusing business with a supplier or customer that fails ethical standards can slow expansion [1], or higher costs reduce the ability to compete on price [1], leaving it weaker in price-sensitive markets [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Two distinct conflicts, each with a chain from action to cost to effect on the objective, tend to score better than one point repeated.

**6.** Statement: "To make the finest hand-finished tiles for the care of historic buildings, supplying architects and builders who value craft." [1]
Justification:
- It names the product and customers, so it fits what Aldersgate does [1] and tells staff what they are building [1].
- It stresses craft and restoration, which matches the needs of architects, its main buyers, and builds a clear reputation [1] and differentiates from mass-produced tiles [1].
- It guides decisions: a proposal to move into cheap, mass-produced tiles would contradict it [1] and could be rejected [1].
Limitation: "finest" cannot be measured, so objectives (such as defect rates or delivery times) are needed to turn it into action [1].
**[8]**
*Examiner insight:* State the actual wording first, then tie each reason to the case. Generic reasons that would suit any firm tend to earn less credit.

**7.** Advantage 1: direction [1]. A shared statement helps the founders and 60 staff agree which clients and projects to pursue [1]. Advantage 2: reputation [1]. A clear purpose can reassure small manufacturers about what Fenwick & Rowe stands for and help it stand out from rivals [1]. Disadvantage: it may be ignored or restrict flexibility [1]. A narrow statement could discourage moves into new advice areas, and a vague one gives no guidance [1]. Judgement: for a small business whose reputation depends on trust, the benefits are likely to outweigh the costs provided the statement is specific and actually used in decisions [1], and reviewed as the business grows [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A judgement should state the condition on which it depends.

**8. (a)** 40,000 ÷ 12,500 [1] = **3.2 years** [1].
**(b)** Impact on the business: 3.2 years is a fairly quick payback if the extra contribution holds [1], but it is an expectation and not certain, and the cost is paid immediately [1]. A quarter of clients are in traditional manufacturing, who might feel the firm has moved away from them and look elsewhere [1]. Impact on stakeholders: staff need retraining, which costs time but may open new work [1]. Founders and owners accept short-run cost for long-run growth [1]. Judgement: it is likely to be worthwhile if the existing clients are kept informed and the new market is real, because payback is short compared with the likely life of the strategy [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the data in the stem. Quoting the payback result and the one-quarter figure shows application; a general answer about disruption does not.

**9.** The impact on the business: the premium costs 2p × 12,000,000 = £240,000 a year [1], which is 13.3% of operating profit (240,000 ÷ 1,800,000) [1]. Recyclable bottles may add cost too [1]. Benefits: supermarkets selling own-label products may value ethical sourcing, helping retain contracts and reducing the risk of bad publicity [1], and farmers may supply more reliably [1]. Impact on stakeholders: farmers gain higher incomes and security [1]; shareholders may receive lower dividends in the short run, as some already say [1]; consumers and the environment benefit from recyclable packaging, though prices may rise [1]. Importance: high for a food business that relies on trust and on a small number of local suppliers [1]. Judgement: the policy is probably important for long-run reputation and supply security, provided the benefits (retention of contracts, reduced risk) can be shown to exceed a cost of that size, and published results make the claims credible [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Weighing stakeholders against each other, such as farmers against shareholders, shows more analysis than a list.

**10. (a)** 90,000 ÷ 15,000 [1] = **6 years** [1].
**(b)** For going ahead: it improves the reputation with architects, who it wants to impress [1], and reduces future exposure to rising energy costs [1]. Cash reserves of £400,000 mean the cost can be met without borrowing [1]. After year 6 the savings are extra profit [1]. Against: 6 years is a long payback for a small business and the savings are an estimate [1]. Judgement: go ahead, provided the panels are expected to last well beyond 6 years and the reputation gains lead to sales, because the business can afford it and the aim is also to improve CSR standing [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A justified decision gives a clear choice, then reasons, then a condition or risk.

**11. (a)** Premium = 0.02 × 12,000,000 = £240,000 [1]. Extra sales needed = 240,000 ÷ 0.30 = **£800,000** [1]. As a percentage of £9.6 million = **8.3%** [1].
**(b)** Recommendation 1: publish independent audits of farm standards and the results [1]. This makes the claims credible to supermarkets and consumers [1] and costs little compared with the premium [1]. Recommendation 2: a staged programme, such as raising the premium only as sales grow [1], because extra sales of £800,000 (8.3%) are needed to cover the current premium, so the profit conflict is real [1] and phasing protects dividends [1]. Farmers, shareholders and supermarkets gain different things, so communicating the plan is essential [1]. Conflict with profit: the cost is certain, while the benefits (reputation, retention) are uncertain and slow [1]. Judgement: audits first, premium phased, because they build credibility while limiting the hit to profit [1]. **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* Explain why the choice suits this business and how it handles the stated conflict; a list of ideas with no choice tends to score lower.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining a mission statement as a target with figures; that is an objective.
- Defining CSR only as charity, without mentioning stakeholders or going beyond the law.
- Giving advantages and disadvantages without applying them to the named business.
- Using the wrong base figure for a percentage.
- Writing "CSR costs money" with no figure.
- Ignoring the time period.
