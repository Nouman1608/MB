These notes condense subtopic 6.10 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417, recognition systems. For full explanations, including the ANPR steps and a comparison of biometric methods, read the [recognition systems study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-recognition-systems/); then test yourself with the [recognition systems practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-recognition-systems-practice/).

## Syllabus coverage

| System | Named uses |
|---|---|
| OMR | School registers, multiple-choice examination papers, barcode, QR code |
| OCR | Automated number plate recognition (ANPR) |
| RFID | Tracking stock, passports, automobiles, contactless payment |
| NFC | Payment using a smartphone |
| Biometric recognition | Face, iris, retina, finger, thumb, hand, voice |

For each: characteristics, uses, advantages and disadvantages.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| OMR | Optical Mark Recognition: detects the position of marks on a form |
| OCR | Optical Character Recognition: converts an image of text into editable text |
| ANPR | Automated number plate recognition: uses OCR to read vehicle registration plates |
| QR code | Two-dimensional grid of squares holding data such as a web address |
| RFID | Radio Frequency Identification Device: tag and reader exchange data by radio waves |
| Passive tag | RFID tag with no battery, powered by the reader; short range |
| Active tag | RFID tag with its own battery; longer range |
| NFC | Near Field Communication: very short-range, two-way form of RFID |
| Biometrics | Identifying a person by a unique physical or behavioural feature |
| False rejection | A genuine user is not recognised |

## OMR

- Light shone on form; shaded marks reflect less light; **position** of each mark gives its meaning.
- Uses: school registers; multiple-choice exam papers.
- **Plus:** fast; accurate; no typing; simple forms.
- **Minus:** marks must be in the right place; faint, double or crossed-out marks misread; damaged sheets; only fixed choices.

**Barcodes:** dark and light bars representing a number; read by laser or camera; used on products, books, luggage.
**QR codes:** 2D grid; much more data; read by phone camera from any angle; still works if partly damaged. **Risk:** fake QR stickers leading to malicious websites.

## OCR and ANPR

- OCR: image of text captured, characters recognised, editable text produced.
- **ANPR steps:** vehicle detected; camera captures image; plate located and image adjusted; OCR reads characters; number checked against database; action taken (barrier, toll, time recorded, alert).
- Uses: car parks, toll roads, congestion and low-emission zones, traffic enforcement, stolen vehicles.
- **Plus:** no staff; continuous; fast; accurate time records.
- **Minus:** dirty or damaged plates and weather cause errors; costly; privacy concerns; cloned plates.

## RFID

- Tag = microchip + aerial; reader sends radio waves; tag replies.
- **No line of sight needed**; many tags read at once; read through packaging.
- Uses: stock tracking; e-passports (holder's details and photo on chip); automobiles (factory tracking, toll tags); contactless cards.
- **Plus:** fast; reads many at once; more data than barcodes; some rewritable.
- **Minus:** dearer than barcodes; interference from metal and liquids; can be read without the owner knowing; tags removed or damaged.

## NFC

- Very short range (a few centimetres); **two-way**.
- Smartphone payment: card in app; phone unlocked by fingerprint, face or PIN; held near reader.
- **Plus:** quick; hard to intercept; owner must unlock phone; no card needed.
- **Minus:** phone must be charged and compatible; risk if phone stolen while unlocked; not accepted everywhere.

## Biometrics compared

| Method | Key strength | Key weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Face | No contact, quick | Lighting, glasses, masks, ageing |
| Iris | Very accurate; works with glasses | Expensive |
| Retina | Very accurate; hard to fake | Intrusive and slow |
| Finger, thumb | Cheap, quick | Cuts, dirt, worn skin |
| Hand | Easy to use | Large scanner; less unique |
| Voice | Works over the phone | Colds, noise, recordings |

**General plus:** cannot be lost, forgotten or lent; hard to copy. **General minus:** cost; false rejections; sensitive data that cannot be changed if stolen; some find it intrusive.

## Which system? Quick decisions

| Need | Best choice | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Mark 5,000 answer sheets | OMR | Positions of marks read quickly |
| Digitise old printed letters | OCR | Turns text images into editable text |
| Count a whole pallet in seconds | RFID | No line of sight; many tags at once |
| Pay with a phone | NFC | Short-range, two-way, owner unlocks phone |
| Stop learners lending ID cards | Biometrics | Features cannot be lent |
| Open a car park barrier automatically | ANPR | Reads the plate and checks the database |

## Exam traps

- Swapping OMR and OCR.
- "RFID needs to be in line of sight". Barcodes do; RFID does not.
- Describing ANPR without OCR or the database check.
- Treating NFC as unrelated to RFID.
- Giving biometric advantages only; mention privacy and false rejections.

## Self-test

1. How does an OMR reader detect a shaded mark?
2. Give two uses of OMR named in the syllabus.
3. Give two advantages of a QR code over a barcode.
4. State one risk of scanning QR codes.
5. What does OCR produce from a scanned page?
6. List the steps of ANPR at a car park.
7. Give one disadvantage of ANPR.
8. State the difference between a passive and an active RFID tag.
9. Why is RFID faster than barcodes for stock checks?
10. Give one difference between NFC and RFID.
11. Give one weakness of fingerprint recognition and one of voice recognition.
12. Why is stolen biometric data a bigger problem than a stolen password?

### Answers

1. Shaded marks reflect less light than blank paper, so the reader detects the positions of the marks.
2. School registers; multiple-choice examination papers.
3. Any two: holds more data; read from any angle; read by a phone camera; still works if partly damaged; can link to a website.
4. A fake code may lead to a malicious website.
5. Editable, searchable text.
6. Vehicle detected; image captured; plate located; OCR reads characters; number checked against database; barrier opens and time recorded.
7. Any one: misreads of dirty or damaged plates; cost; privacy concerns; cloned plates.
8. Passive tags have no battery and are powered by the reader (short range); active tags have a battery (longer range).
9. No line of sight is needed and many tags are read at once.
10. NFC works only over a few centimetres and allows two-way communication.
11. Fingerprint: cuts or dirt cause rejections. Voice: colds or background noise affect it.
12. A biometric feature cannot be changed, whereas a password can be replaced.
