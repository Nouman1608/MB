## Core recall

- **Homeostasis:** the maintenance of a constant internal environment.
- **Insulin decreases blood glucose concentration.**

## Negative feedback (Extended only)

```
set point → factor changes → change detected → response reverses change → back to set point
```

- **Set point:** the normal value maintained (for example about 37 °C body temperature).
- **Negative:** the response opposes the change.
- The factor fluctuates slightly around the set point.

## Blood glucose control (Extended only)

| Blood glucose | Pancreas secretes | Liver | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Too high | insulin | glucose → glycogen (stored) | falls |
| Too low | glucagon | glycogen → glucose (released) | rises |

**Spelling check:** glucag**on** = hormone; glycog**en** = store.

## Type 1 diabetes (Extended only)

- **Cause (outline):** pancreas produces little or no insulin, so blood glucose stays high after meals.
- **Treatment:** insulin injections; regular blood glucose monitoring; control of diet (carbohydrate); allowing for exercise.

## Skin structures (Extended only)

Hairs · hair erector muscles · sweat glands · receptors · sensory neurones · blood vessels · fatty tissue.

## Temperature control (Extended only)

**Brain:** monitors blood temperature; receives impulses from skin temperature receptors; coordinates responses.

| | Too hot | Too cold |
|---|---|---|
| Sweat | more; evaporation transfers heat away | little or none |
| Arterioles to skin surface capillaries | **vasodilation**: widen → more blood near surface → more heat lost | **vasoconstriction**: narrow → less blood near surface → less heat lost |
| Shivering | no | yes; respiration in muscles releases heat |
| Hairs | lie flat (erector muscles relax) | raised (erector muscles contract), trap insulating air |
| Fatty tissue | insulation always | insulation always |

## Worked reminders

**After a sugary drink.** Glucose absorbed → blood glucose rises → pancreas secretes insulin → liver converts glucose to glycogen → blood glucose falls → insulin secretion falls.

**During a long walk without food.** Glucose used in respiration → blood glucose falls → pancreas secretes glucagon → liver converts glycogen to glucose → blood glucose rises.

**Hot day.** Brain detects rise → vasodilation + more sweating → more heat lost → temperature falls to set point.

**Percentage change.** Blood glucose 4.5 → 7.2 arbitrary units: (7.2 − 4.5) ÷ 4.5 × 100 = **60%** increase.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Insulin vs glucagon (Extended):** insulin lowers blood glucose (glucose to glycogen); glucagon raises it (glycogen to glucose). Both come from the pancreas; the liver responds.
- **Vasodilation vs vasoconstriction (Extended):** arterioles widen (more heat lost) compared with arterioles narrow (less heat lost).
- **Sweating vs shivering (Extended):** sweating loses heat by evaporation; shivering gains heat by respiration in muscles.
- **Detecting vs responding (Extended):** the brain and skin receptors detect; sweat glands, arterioles, muscles and hair erector muscles respond.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Homeostasis | Maintenance of a constant internal environment |
| Set point | Normal value of a controlled factor (Extended) |
| Negative feedback | Response that reverses a change from the set point (Extended) |
| Insulin | Hormone that decreases blood glucose |
| Glucagon | Hormone that increases blood glucose (Extended) |
| Glycogen | Storage carbohydrate made from glucose in the liver (Extended) |
| Type 1 diabetes | Condition in which the pancreas makes little or no insulin (Extended) |
| Vasodilation | Widening of arterioles supplying skin surface capillaries (Extended) |
| Vasoconstriction | Narrowing of arterioles supplying skin surface capillaries (Extended) |
| Shivering | Rapid muscle contractions releasing heat by respiration (Extended) |

## Exam traps

- Glucagon and glycogen confused.
- Pancreas said to store glycogen.
- "Insulin turns glucose into energy."
- Type 1 treatment without insulin.
- Capillaries said to dilate, constrict or move.
- Sweat "is cold"; it is **evaporation** that transfers heat.
- Brain missing from temperature answers.
- Hairs said to "keep you warm" without "trap a layer of air".

## Command words

- **Describe** homeostasis: one sentence, syllabus wording.
- **Explain** negative feedback (Extended): set point, detect, reverse.
- **Describe** glucose control (Extended): hormone + liver + result, for both directions.
- **Outline** treatment (Extended): three or four short points.
- **Identify** skin structures (Extended): exact names.

## Self-test

1. Describe homeostasis.
2. State the effect of insulin on blood glucose concentration.
3. (Extended) What is a set point?
4. (Extended) Why is it called negative feedback?
5. (Extended) Which hormone is secreted when blood glucose falls?
6. (Extended) What does the liver do in response to insulin?
7. (Extended) Outline the treatment of Type 1 diabetes.
8. (Extended) Name the structure in the skin that secretes sweat.
9. (Extended) How does sweating cool the body?
10. (Extended) Describe vasoconstriction and its effect.
11. (Extended) How does shivering help when the body is cold?
12. (Extended) State two roles of the brain in temperature control.

### Answers

1. The maintenance of a constant internal environment.
2. It decreases it.
3. The normal value at which a factor is maintained.
4. The response reverses the change from the set point.
5. Glucagon.
6. Converts glucose into glycogen and stores it.
7. Insulin injections, regular monitoring of blood glucose, controlling diet (carbohydrate) and allowing for exercise.
8. Sweat gland.
9. Water in sweat evaporates from the skin, transferring heat away.
10. Arterioles supplying skin surface capillaries narrow, so less blood flows near the surface and less heat is lost.
11. Rapid muscle contractions increase respiration, which releases heat.
12. It monitors blood temperature; it receives impulses from skin receptors; it coordinates responses (any two).
