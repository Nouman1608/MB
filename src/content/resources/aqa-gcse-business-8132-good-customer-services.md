## The sales process

The sales process is the series of stages a customer goes through when buying, and the business's role at each one. Good customer service is needed at every stage, not only at the till.

1. **Before the sale:** the customer looks for information. The business makes it easy to find out about products, prices and opening times, in person, by phone or online.
2. **Finding out what the customer needs:** staff ask questions and listen, so they can recommend the right product rather than the most expensive one.
3. **Helping the customer choose:** staff use product knowledge to explain features, compare options and answer questions.
4. **Making the sale:** buying is quick and easy, with clear prices, convenient payment and accurate orders.
5. **After the sale:** the business supports the customer through delivery, set-up, help, repairs and handling complaints.

A business that does stages 1 to 4 well but ignores stage 5 may win one sale and lose the customer.

## Methods of good service

### Product knowledge

Staff who know the products can answer questions, explain differences between models, and match a product to the customer's needs. This builds trust, because the customer feels advised rather than pushed. It also reduces returns, because customers buy the right product first time.

Example: a staff member in a running shop who watches a customer jog on a treadmill and recommends a shoe suited to the way they run.

Achieved through: training, product guides, letting staff try products, and keeping staff informed when new products arrive.

### Customer engagement

The specification describes this as "creating a positive experience for the customer". It covers how customers are treated: a friendly greeting, attention without pressure, remembering regular customers, a pleasant environment, short queues and an apology that sounds sincere when things go wrong.

Example: a hairdresser who remembers a regular client's usual style and asks about their holiday.

Achieved through: recruiting friendly staff, training, enough staff at busy times, and empowering staff to fix problems on the spot.

### Post-sales services

Support after the customer has paid. The specification gives three examples:

- **User training:** showing customers how to use the product, such as a session on setting up new software, or a demonstration of a sewing machine.
- **Help lines:** a phone, chat or email service customers can contact with questions or problems.
- **Servicing:** maintenance and repairs, such as an annual boiler check or a car service.

Post-sales services are especially important for complex or expensive products, where customers worry about what happens if something goes wrong. They can also earn extra revenue, for example from paid servicing plans.

## Benefits of good customer service

The specification lists four, and they form a chain:

**Customer satisfaction** → **customer loyalty** → **increased spend** → **profitability**

- Satisfied customers feel the business met or beat their expectations.
- Loyal customers return rather than shop around, and recommend the business to others.
- Loyal customers often spend more over time, buying extra products and upgrades.
- Higher sales from existing customers, without spending as much on advertising to win new ones, raise profit.

## Dangers of poor customer service

These also form a chain:

**Dissatisfied customers** → **poor reputation via word of mouth** → **reduction in revenue**

- Dissatisfied customers may complain, ask for refunds, or simply not return.
- They tell friends and family, and increasingly post reviews online, so one bad experience can put off many potential customers.
- Fewer customers and lower spending mean less revenue.

## Worked example: the value of a loyal customer

Figures invented. Ashgrove Pet Supplies has 1,500 regular customers who each spend an average of £40 a month.

- Monthly revenue from regulars = 1,500 × £40 = £60,000.

A survey shows that slow service at the till is the main complaint. Ashgrove estimates that, without changes, 8% of regulars will leave over the next year.

- Customers lost = 8% of 1,500 = 120.
- Monthly revenue lost once they have gone = 120 × £40 = £4,800, or £57,600 a year.

Employing an extra part-time worker at busy times would cost £11,000 a year. If it keeps most of those customers, it pays for itself several times over. Ashgrove could go further and train staff in product knowledge, so customers spend more on advice-led purchases such as specialist food. The judgement depends on how reliable the 8% estimate is, but the figures show why good service is a source of profit, not just a cost.

## How advances in ICT have changed customer service

| ICT | How it has improved service | New risks |
|---|---|---|
| Websites | Product information, prices, stock levels, FAQs, order tracking and online help available at any time | Customers expect information to be accurate and up to date; a confusing site loses sales |
| E-commerce | Customers can buy at any hour from anywhere, compare products easily, and choose delivery options; past orders and preferences can be remembered | Customers expect fast delivery and easy returns; there is no face-to-face help |
| Social media | Quick two-way contact; complaints answered publicly; businesses can share news and tips | Complaints are visible to many people; a slow or poor reply damages reputation quickly |

ICT has raised customers' expectations. They now expect fast answers, at any time, through the channel they prefer. A business that answers social media messages within an hour can impress customers; one that ignores them is judged publicly.

## Analysing techniques: an annotated answer

Question: *Analyse how a mobile phone retailer could use post-sales services to improve customer service.*

"The retailer could offer free set-up, transferring contacts and photos to the new phone before the customer leaves (user training). Customers who struggle with technology would find the new phone easier to use, so they are more satisfied with their purchase (benefit: satisfaction). Satisfied customers are more likely to return to the same shop for their next upgrade and accessories (benefit: loyalty and increased spend), which raises the retailer's revenue without extra spending on advertising (profitability)."

The answer names a specific technique, applies it to a phone retailer, and follows the specification's chain of benefits.

## Exam technique

- **Identify or state:** name a method, benefit or danger from the specification.
- **Explain:** method → effect on the customer → effect on the business.
- **Analyse:** use the satisfaction-loyalty-spend-profit chain, or the dissatisfaction-reputation-revenue chain, applied to the business.
- **Evaluate:** weigh the cost of better service against the benefits, considering the type of product and customer, and decide.

## Common mistakes

- Describing good service as "being polite". Name specific methods.
- Forgetting post-sales services, especially for complex products.
- Listing the four benefits without linking them.
- Treating ICT only as positive: social media also spreads complaints.
- Ignoring the cost of providing good service.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the methods of good customer service in AQA GCSE Business?**
Product knowledge, customer engagement (creating a positive experience for the customer), and post-sales services such as user training, help lines and servicing.

**What are the dangers of poor customer service?**
Dissatisfied customers, a poor reputation via word of mouth and a reduction in revenue.

**How has social media changed customer service?**
It allows quick, two-way contact between customers and businesses, but complaints and replies are visible to many people, so poor service damages reputation quickly.
