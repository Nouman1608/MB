**Key question 2.5 asks how effectively the United States contained
the spread of communism, and Cambridge examines it through exactly
three case studies: Korea (1950–53), Cuba (1959–62) and Vietnam
(1955–75).** For each you need the American reaction, what happened,
and a judgement on whether containment worked. The strongest answers
judge each case on its own terms and then compare them.

## Syllabus coverage

The syllabus specifies case studies of:

- American reactions to North Korea's invasion of South Korea, the
  involvement of the UN, and the course of the war to 1953
- American reactions to the Cuban Revolution, including the Missile
  Crisis and its aftermath
- American involvement in the Vietnam War, including reasons for
  involvement, tactics and strategy, and reasons for withdrawal

## What did containment mean?

Containment meant stopping communism spreading to new countries
rather than trying to overthrow it where it already ruled. It began
with the **Truman Doctrine** (March 1947). The **domino theory**,
stated by President Eisenhower in 1954, added the fear that if one
country fell to communism its neighbours would follow.

## Korea, 1950–53: containment by the UN

- **Background:** Korea was divided at the 38th parallel in 1945 —
  communist North under Kim Il Sung, US-backed South under Syngman
  Rhee.
- **Invasion:** North Korean forces crossed the parallel on
  **25 June 1950** and took most of the South.
- **American reaction:** Truman saw a test of containment. The UN
  Security Council, which the Soviet Union was boycotting, called on
  members to help South Korea. A UN force, mostly American and
  commanded by **General Douglas MacArthur**, was sent.
- **Course of the war:** the **Inchon landings** (September 1950)
  drove the North back; UN forces crossed the 38th parallel and
  approached the Yalu River, so **China entered the war** in
  October–November 1950 and pushed them south again. Truman sacked
  MacArthur in April 1951 for publicly demanding war on China. The
  front settled near the 38th parallel, and an **armistice was signed
  at Panmunjom on 27 July 1953**.
- **How effective?** Containment succeeded in its original aim: South
  Korea stayed non-communist. But the attempt to unite Korea failed,
  China became a lasting enemy, and the cost was high (about 36,000
  American dead and far more Korean and Chinese deaths).

## Cuba, 1959–62: containment next door

- **Revolution:** Fidel Castro overthrew the US-backed dictator
  Fulgencio Batista in **January 1959**. He nationalised US-owned
  businesses, and the USA responded with a trade embargo and broke
  off diplomatic relations in January 1961. Cuba turned to the USSR
  for trade and aid.
- **Bay of Pigs (April 1961):** about 1,400 CIA-trained Cuban exiles
  landed and were defeated within days. It humiliated President
  Kennedy and pushed Castro closer to Moscow.
- **The Missile Crisis (October 1962):** a US U-2 spy plane
  photographed Soviet missile sites on 14 October. Kennedy rejected an
  air strike and announced a naval **"quarantine"** on 22 October. On
  27 October an American U-2 was shot down over Cuba. On 28 October
  **Khrushchev agreed to remove the missiles** in return for a public
  US promise not to invade Cuba; the USA also secretly agreed to
  remove its Jupiter missiles from Turkey.
- **Aftermath:** a telephone "hotline" between Washington and Moscow
  (1963) and the **Partial Test Ban Treaty** (August 1963) followed.
- **How effective?** Containment failed to stop Cuba becoming
  communist, and the Bay of Pigs was a disaster. But Kennedy forced the
  missiles out without war, so the further spread of Soviet military
  power in the Americas was contained.

## Vietnam, 1955–75: containment fails

- **Reasons for involvement:** after the French defeat at Dien Bien
  Phu, the **Geneva Accords (1954)** divided Vietnam at the 17th
  parallel, with elections planned for 1956. The USA backed Ngo Dinh
  Diem's government in the South, which refused to hold the elections,
  because it feared Ho Chi Minh would win and the "dominoes" of
  South-East Asia would fall.
- **Escalation:** Eisenhower and Kennedy sent money and military
  advisers (about 16,000 by 1963). After the **Gulf of Tonkin
  incident (August 1964)**, Congress gave President Johnson power to
  use force. Bombing of the North (**Operation Rolling Thunder**) and
  the first combat troops followed in March 1965; US troop numbers
  peaked at over 500,000 in 1968–69.
- **Tactics and strategy:** the USA relied on bombing, firepower,
  "search and destroy" missions, napalm and the chemical defoliant
  Agent Orange, and (from 1962) the strategic hamlet programme. The Viet Cong used guerrilla
  warfare, tunnels and supply lines along the Ho Chi Minh Trail, and
  had support from North Vietnam, China and the USSR.
- **Reasons for withdrawal:** the **Tet Offensive (January 1968)**
  showed the war was far from won; news of the **My Lai massacre**
  (1968) and the rising death toll fuelled the anti-war movement;
  President Nixon's **Vietnamisation** handed fighting to the South
  Vietnamese army. The **Paris Peace Accords (January 1973)** removed
  US combat troops, and **Saigon fell on 30 April 1975**.
- **How effective?** A clear failure: all of Vietnam became communist,
  and Laos and Cambodia also fell to communists in 1975. About 58,000
  Americans died, and US prestige at home and abroad was badly damaged.

## How effective was containment overall?

| Case | Aim | Outcome | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Korea | Defend South Korea | South stayed non-communist; stalemate at 38th parallel | Largely successful |
| Cuba | Remove Castro; keep Soviet missiles out | Castro stayed; missiles removed | Mixed |
| Vietnam | Keep South Vietnam non-communist | Whole country communist by 1975 | Failure |

A strong conclusion might argue that containment worked best where the
USA had broad international support and a clear front line (Korea),
and worst where it backed an unpopular government against a
nationalist guerrilla movement (Vietnam).

## Frequently asked questions

**What was containment?**
The US policy, from the Truman Doctrine of
1947, of stopping communism from spreading to further countries.

**Was the Korean War a success for containment?**
Mostly yes: South
Korea remained non-communist, although the war ended in stalemate near
the original border in 1953.

**Who won the Cuban Missile Crisis?**
Both sides could claim
something: the Soviet missiles left Cuba, but the USA promised not to
invade and secretly removed its missiles from Turkey.

**Why did the USA withdraw from Vietnam?**
The war could not be won at
an acceptable cost; after the Tet Offensive public opinion turned
against it, and Nixon withdrew troops through Vietnamisation and the
1973 Paris Peace Accords.
