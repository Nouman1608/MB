These notes condense heading 12 *The influence of religion* from Cambridge International A Level
Sociology (9699) Paper 4. Three debates: is religion declining (12.1)? Is religion patriarchal, and
is that changing (12.2)? Is religion being transformed rather than lost in post-modern societies
(12.3)? Each essay needs a clear judgement backed by evidence from more than one society.

Full explanations: [secularisation, feminism and post-modern religion study guide](/resources/a-level-cambridge-sociology-influence-of-religion/).

## Syllabus coverage

9699 (2024–2026) Paper 4 Section C, heading 12: **12.1** The secularisation debate; **12.2** Gender,
feminism and religion; **12.3** Religion and post-modernity.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Secularisation** | Religion losing social significance (Wilson, 1966) |
| **Disenchantment** | Decline of magical and supernatural explanations (Weber) |
| **Religious pluralism** | Many faiths coexisting; removes the "sacred canopy" (Berger, 1967) |
| **Believing without belonging** | Belief persisting without attendance (Davie, 1994) |
| **Religious market theory** | Constant demand; competition revitalises religion (Stark and Bainbridge, 1985) |
| **Existential security** | Feeling of safety that reduces need for religion (Norris and Inglehart, 2004) |
| **Deprivatisation** | Religion re-entering public life (Casanova, 1994) |
| **Privatised religion** | Personal, home-based belief |
| **Spiritual shopping** | Picking and mixing beliefs as a consumer (Lyon, 2000) |
| **Resacralisation** | Revival of the sacred |
| **NRM** | New religious movement; world-rejecting, -accommodating or -affirming (Wallis, 1984) |
| **Fundamentalism** | Return to the literal basics of a faith, often in reaction to modernity |

## 12.1 Secularisation

**For decline:** falling attendance, membership and religious ceremonies in Western Europe; fewer
believers among the young; generational decline (Bruce, *God is Dead*, 2002); disengagement of
religion from state, education and welfare; rationalisation and disenchantment (Weber); pluralism
removes the sacred canopy (Berger, 1967).

**Against decline:** believing without belonging (Davie, 1994); religious market theory (Stark and
Bainbridge, 1985) — the USA stays religious; existential security (Norris and Inglehart, 2004) —
global religiosity rising; deprivatisation (Casanova, 1994); Berger's later desecularisation view
(1999); growth of NRMs, New Age, Pentecostalism; a past "golden age" may be a myth.

## 12.2 Gender and religion

| Issue | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Religion as patriarchal | de Beauvoir (1949); male leadership; male interpretation of texts |
| Patriarchal interpretation, not faith itself | El Saadawi (1980) |
| Historical change | Armstrong (1993) — earlier female deities |
| Moves to equality | Church of England women priests (1994), first woman bishop (2015); women rabbis in Reform Judaism |
| Benefits to women | Watson (1994) — veiling as choice; Woodhead — religious forms of feminism; community and support |

## 12.3 Post-modernity

- **Privatised religion** — belief at home, personal spirituality.
- **Spiritual shopping** — Lyon (2000); Hervieu-Léger's pilgrims and converts.
- **Spiritual revolution?** — Heelas and Woodhead (2005), Kendal Project: holistic spirituality
  growing, but not replacing congregational religion.
- **Resacralisation** — NRMs, New Age, charismatic growth, fundamentalism.
- **NRMs** — Wallis (1984) typology.
- **Fundamentalism explanations:** cultural defence (Bruce, 1996); cultural transition;
  disengagement; marginality (theodicy of disprivilege); religious revival. Giddens (1999):
  reaction to globalisation.

## Worked essay plan

*Practice question:* "Religion is changing rather than declining." Evaluate this view. [35]

1. Define secularisation and post-modern change.
2. Evidence of decline (Wilson, Bruce, attendance data).
3. Evidence of change: believing without belonging (Davie); spiritual shopping (Lyon); spiritual
   revolution (Heelas and Woodhead); NRMs (Wallis).
4. Global perspective: existential security (Norris and Inglehart); fundamentalism; religious market
   theory.
5. Evaluate the evidence: Kendal Project shows spirituality growing but too small to offset decline.
6. Judgement: in Western Europe decline and change coexist; globally, religion is changing far more
   than it is declining.

## Model evaluation paragraph

*On the claim that religion has declined:* "Evidence of falling attendance in Western Europe
supports Wilson's secularisation thesis, but attendance is only one indicator of religiosity. Davie's
idea of believing without belonging suggests private belief may outlast public practice, while
Norris and Inglehart show that religiosity remains high where existential security is low. The
debate therefore turns on definition and location: if religion is defined substantively and
measured by attendance in affluent societies, decline is clear; if it is defined functionally and
measured globally, religion appears to be changing form rather than disappearing."

This paragraph earns AO3 credit because it weighs evidence, names the measurement problem and
reaches a conditional judgement rather than simply listing studies.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- Treating secularisation as proved by attendance figures alone.
- Forgetting **social significance** — decline of belief is not the same as decline of influence.
- Feminism answers that only criticise religion; 12.2 asks for moves to equality and benefits too.
- Listing fundamentalism explanations without examples or evaluation.
- Using only Western evidence.

## Quick self-test

1. Who defined secularisation in 1966?
2. What is "believing without belonging"?
3. Give two of Wallis's three NRM types.
4. What did Heelas and Woodhead find in Kendal?
5. Explain "cultural transition" as a cause of fundamentalism.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the USA evidence against secularisation?** It is the main example used by religious market
theorists, although critics note that American religion has also become more consumer-oriented.

**Can I combine 12.1 and 12.3 in one essay?** Yes — post-modern evidence is one of the strongest
challenges to the secularisation thesis.

**Which feminist position is strongest?** None by default; judge using evidence of change in
particular faiths and societies.
