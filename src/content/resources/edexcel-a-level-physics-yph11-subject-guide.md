Pearson Edexcel International A Level Physics (YPH11) is a modular course
for students who want to understand the physical world through models,
measurement and mathematics, from mechanics and electricity to fields,
nuclear physics and cosmology. The qualification has six externally
assessed units: three at the International AS stage (Units 1 to 3) and three
at the A2 stage (Units 4 to 6). Four content units are each worth 20%, and
two practical skills units are each worth 10%. The specification was first
taught in September 2018 and first examined in January 2019.

## How the course is organised

Each stage has two content units followed by a practical skills unit.

**AS stage**

- **Unit 1: Mechanics and Materials** covers mechanics and the physics of
  materials.
- **Unit 2: Waves and Electricity** covers waves and the particle nature of
  light, and electric circuits.
- **Unit 3: Practical Skills in Physics I** covers planning an experiment,
  implementation and measurements, and processing results.

**A2 stage**

- **Unit 4: Further Mechanics, Fields and Particles** covers further
  mechanics, electric and magnetic fields, and nuclear and particle physics.
- **Unit 5: Thermodynamics, Radiation, Oscillations and Cosmology** covers
  thermodynamics, nuclear decay, oscillations, and astrophysics and
  cosmology.
- **Unit 6: Practical Skills in Physics II** covers planning,
  implementation and measurements, and analysis.

## How it's assessed

Every unit is an externally assessed written paper.

| Unit | Paper code | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unit 1: Mechanics and Materials | WPH11/01 | 90 minutes (1h30) | 80 | 20% |
| Unit 2: Waves and Electricity | WPH12/01 | 90 minutes (1h30) | 80 | 20% |
| Unit 3: Practical Skills in Physics I | WPH13/01 | 80 minutes (1h20) | 50 | 10% |
| Unit 4: Further Mechanics, Fields and Particles | WPH14/01 | 105 minutes (1h45) | 90 | 20% |
| Unit 5: Thermodynamics, Radiation, Oscillations and Cosmology | WPH15/01 | 105 minutes (1h45) | 90 | 20% |
| Unit 6: Practical Skills in Physics II | WPH16/01 | 80 minutes (1h20) | 50 | 10% |

Units 3 and 6 are worth noting. Although they assess practical skills, they
are written examinations rather than hands-on practical tests: practical
competence is assessed through questions about planning, measurement and
analysis. The specification also states that a minimum of 40% of the marks
across the papers overall will target mathematics at Level 2 or above, so
quantitative work is central to the whole qualification.

## How the parts fit together

The A2 units build directly on the AS units. Further mechanics in Unit 4
extends the mechanics of Unit 1, and electric and magnetic fields in Unit 4
develop ideas from the electric circuits work in Unit 2. Nuclear and
particle physics in Unit 4 prepares the ground for nuclear decay in Unit 5,
and the waves content of Unit 2 supports the study of oscillations and of
astrophysics and cosmology.

The practical skills units are not a separate strand to be crammed at the
end of each year. The planning, measurement and data-processing skills that
Units 3 and 6 examine are the same skills you use whenever you carry out an
experiment in class, so regular practical work is the best preparation. Unit 6 extends the
skills of Unit 3, moving from processing results towards fuller analysis.

Mathematics links everything. Rearranging equations, working with units and
significant figures, handling graphs, and estimating uncertainties are used
in every unit. Given the minimum of 40% of marks targeting mathematics, steady
practice with calculations is one of the most valuable habits to build.

## Planning your study

A natural route follows the unit order. In the first year, teach Unit 1 and
Unit 2, with practical work running alongside to build the skills tested in
Unit 3. In the second year, teach Unit 4 and Unit 5, again with practical
work feeding into Unit 6. Ask your teacher which series each unit will be
entered for, as modular courses can be timetabled in different ways.

Keep a running record of every practical you carry out: the aim, the
apparatus, the measurements taken, sources of uncertainty and how the results
were processed. This record becomes revision material for Units 3 and 6.
Maintain a single equation and definitions sheet that you add to as each unit
introduces new quantities.

Begin past-paper practice for each unit once its content is taught, and move
to full timed papers in the final weeks before the series. The
exam-preparation page covers how to approach each paper in detail.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many units are in Pearson Edexcel International A Level Physics YPH11?**
There are six units. Units 1 to 3 form the International AS stage and Units 4 to 6 the A2 stage. All are externally assessed.

**How are the units weighted?**
Units 1, 2, 4 and 5 are each worth 20%. Units 3 and 6, the practical skills units, are each worth 10%.

**Is there a hands-on practical exam in YPH11?**
No. Units 3 and 6 assess practical skills through written examinations rather than hands-on practical tests.

**How long is each YPH11 paper?**
Units 1 and 2 last 90 minutes (80 marks), Units 3 and 6 last 80 minutes (50 marks), and Units 4 and 5 last 105 minutes (90 marks).

**How much mathematics is in International A Level Physics?**
The specification states that a minimum of 40% of the marks across the papers overall will target mathematics at Level 2 or above.
