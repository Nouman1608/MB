This is a **higher level (HL) only** page. Emergence of Modern China (1910 to 1949) is one of the twelve regional studies in the Asia and Oceania option, examined on Paper 3, the 2-hour essay paper that standard level students do not sit. This guide follows the IB *History guide*, first assessment 2028. It covers the early republic and warlords, the effects of the First World War, Guomindang rule, the rise of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP), the war with Japan and the Civil War of 1946 to 1949.

Use it with the [revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-emergence-of-modern-china-revision-notes/) and the [practice set](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-emergence-of-modern-china-practice/).

## Rise of national identity in China

**The Republic (1912).** The Qing fell in 1912, and Sun Yixian briefly became provisional president before handing the office to **Yuan Shikai**, the commander of the strongest army. Yuan was a strong man who did not value parliament. He banned the Guomindang (GMD) in 1913 after a failed "Second Revolution", dissolved the assembly, and in 1915 to 1916 tried to make himself emperor. He met resistance and died in June 1916.

**Warlordism.** After Yuan, no one controlled the whole country. Military governors in the provinces, backed by their own armies, ruled as **warlords**, and rival cliques fought for Beijing (Zhili, Anhui and Fengtian groups). Taxation rose to pay armies, banditry spread, and the central government had little authority or income. Warlordism left China weak against foreign powers.

**Sun Yixian and the Three Principles.** Sun, leader of the revolutionary movement, based himself at Guangzhou (Canton). His Three Principles of the People were nationalism, democracy and people's livelihood. He planned a three-stage path: military rule, a period of political tutelage, then constitutional government. Reorganised in 1919, the GMD was a small party without a strong army.

**The New Culture Movement.** From about 1915, intellectuals around Peking University and the journal *New Youth* (edited by Chen Duxiu) attacked Confucian tradition and called for "science and democracy". Hu Shi promoted writing in everyday language and Lu Xun wrote critical fiction. The movement spread new ideas widely among students and set the stage for 1919.

## Impact of the First World War on nationalism

**Chinese involvement.** China entered the war on the Allied side in 1917. It sent about 140,000 labourers to France and hoped that the peace settlement would end foreign privileges.

**Twenty-One Demands (1915).** Japan, seizing the chance while Europe was at war, presented demands that would have given it great control over Manchuria, Shandong and China's economy. Yuan accepted most of them after an ultimatum, and the humiliation fed nationalism.

**Versailles and the League.** At the Paris Peace Conference the Allies gave the former German rights in Shandong to Japan. Chinese delegates refused to sign. This betrayal of Wilson's promise of self-determination shocked Chinese opinion.

**The May Fourth Movement (1919).** On 4 May, thousands of students in Beijing demonstrated against the decision and attacked officials seen as pro-Japanese. Strikes and boycotts spread to Shanghai and other cities, and the government released the arrested students and dismissed three officials. The movement was a turning point. It showed popular nationalism, made many young intellectuals turn from liberalism towards Marxism, and led to the founding of the **CCP** in Shanghai in 1921, with Chen Duxiu and Li Dazhao among its leaders. Mao Zedong attended the First Congress.

## Impact of Guomindang rule

**The First United Front.** Disappointed by the Western powers, Sun accepted Soviet aid. The **Sun–Joffe Manifesto** (January 1923) pledged Soviet help to China's unification. In 1924 the GMD allowed Communists to join as individuals (the First United Front), reorganised on a Leninist model, and founded the Whampoa Military Academy under **Jiang Jieshi** (Chiang Kai-shek). Sun died in March 1925.

**Jiang Jieshi and the Northern Expedition.** Jiang became the party's military leader. Between 1926 and 1928 the GMD army marched north to defeat the warlords, and by 1928 the Nanjing government claimed national authority. Many warlords had simply switched allegiance, so control remained partial.

**Ideology and the Nanjing decade (1927 to 1937).** The GMD's ideology was Sun's Three Principles, with Jiang adding Confucian moral themes in the New Life Movement (1934). The government gained tariff control, introduced a new currency (1935), built roads and railways, and developed some modern industry, with German advisers helping to train the army. But the regime depended on the army and bankers, did little for rural poverty, did not carry out land reform, and was marked by corruption and one-party rule. It suppressed liberals as well as Communists.

**Manchuria (1931).** In September 1931 Japanese army officers staged the Mukden Incident and occupied Manchuria, setting up the puppet state of Manchukuo in 1932. Jiang followed a policy of "internal pacification before external resistance", putting the Communists first. This was unpopular and lost the GMD some support among students and patriots.

## Reasons for the rise of communism in China (to 1936)

**The break of 1927.** In April 1927 Jiang turned on his allies, and soldiers and gangsters killed thousands of Communists and union members in the **Shanghai massacre**. The First United Front collapsed. The CCP's urban strategy failed, and its leaders retreated to rural bases.

**The Jiangxi Soviet.** Mao Zedong and Zhu De built a base in the mountains of Jiangxi, establishing the Chinese Soviet Republic (1931). They redistributed land, trained the Red Army and used guerrilla tactics. The GMD mounted five **Encirclement Campaigns** (1930 to 1934). The first four failed against guerrilla methods. In the fifth, Jiang used a strategy of blockhouses and economic blockade, with German military advice, and the CCP's leaders, who had chosen conventional fighting, were forced out.

**The Long March (1934 to 1935).** About 86,000 people broke out of Jiangxi in October 1934 and marched thousands of kilometres to Shaanxi in the north-west, crossing rivers, mountains and marshes. Only a fraction, probably under ten thousand, reached the end. At the **Zunyi Conference** (January 1935) Mao criticised the earlier military leadership and emerged as the dominant figure. The march became a founding story of the party, though it was a retreat.

**Yan'an.** From 1936 the CCP based itself at Yan'an. Mao developed his theory that the peasants could be the main revolutionary force. The Yan'an Soviet used moderate land policies (rent reduction), village schools, and a "mass line" of listening to the peasants, along with rectification campaigns to enforce party discipline.

## Impact of the Second Sino-Japanese War (1937 to 1945)

**Political impact.** In December 1936, in the **Xi'an Incident**, two generals kidnapped Jiang to force him to fight Japan rather than the Communists. After talks involving Zhou Enlai, he agreed to the **Second United Front**. Full war began in July 1937 with the Marco Polo Bridge Incident. Japan took Shanghai, then Nanjing in December 1937 where its troops committed mass killing and rape (the Nanjing massacre, with estimates in the hundreds of thousands, which are disputed in detail).

**Japanese military rule.** In occupied China, Japan set up puppet governments, including that of Wang Jingwei. In northern China its forces used what Chinese call the **Three Alls Policy** ("kill all, burn all, loot all") against villages that supported guerrillas, especially after the CCP's Hundred Regiments Offensive of 1940.

**Strategy.** The GMD, pushed back to Chongqing, traded space for time, hoping that the Allies would defeat Japan. The CCP expanded in the countryside behind enemy lines through guerrilla warfare and political organisation. CCP membership rose from tens of thousands to over a million by 1945.

**Economic and social impact.** The GMD lost its coastal industrial bases. It paid for the war by printing money, which led to **hyperinflation**, and ordinary people, especially officials, teachers and city dwellers, suffered. Famine in Henan (1942 to 1943), the destruction of the Yellow River dikes in 1938 (to slow Japan) and millions of refugees marked the war. Corruption and conscription abuses undermined the GMD.

## Reasons for the outcome of the Chinese Civil War (1946 to 1949)

- **Military strategies.** After Japan's surrender, both sides raced to seize territory. The GMD had more men and American equipment, but it spread its forces out and tried to hold cities and railways in Manchuria. The Communist PLA used guerrilla warfare, then moved to conventional campaigns. The Liaoshen, Huaihai and Pingjin campaigns (autumn 1948 to January 1949) destroyed the GMD's best armies. The PLA crossed the Yangtze in April 1949.
- **Peasant support.** Land reform in CCP areas gave land to the poor and secured recruits and supplies. The GMD's local officials were corrupt.
- **Leaders.** Mao led a disciplined party with talented commanders such as Lin Biao and Zhu De. Jiang kept personal control of military decisions and mistrusted generals.
- **The USA and USSR.** The USA gave billions in aid but would not send troops. The Marshall mission (1945 to 1947) tried to mediate and failed. The Soviet Union occupied Manchuria in 1945 and allowed the CCP to take Japanese arms.
- **Economic collapse.** Hyperinflation destroyed the middle class's trust in the GMD.

Mao proclaimed the People's Republic on 1 October 1949. Jiang's government withdrew to Taiwan.

## Perspectives and historiography

- Chalmers Johnson argued that Japanese invasion mobilised "peasant nationalism" for the CCP.
- Others, such as Mark Selden, stressed social revolution and land reform in Yan'an.
- Odd Arne Westad and others stress GMD mistakes, as well as CCP strengths, in the Civil War.
- Rana Mitter and Hans van de Ven have restored attention to the GMD's wartime effort.

## A worked example

**Question: "To what extent was the Second Sino-Japanese War the main reason for the communist victory in 1949?"** Argue that the war weakened the GMD (economy, army, legitimacy) and allowed the CCP to expand, so it was a necessary condition. But the CCP's land policy and organisation, GMD mistakes after 1945, and Soviet help explain the timing and result. Rank them in the conclusion.

## Common mistakes

- Writing the whole Long March as a victory; it was a costly retreat that became a symbol.
- Treating the GMD as only corrupt, without noting its achievements.
- Ignoring the May Fourth Movement's links to the CCP.
- Forgetting the role of the Soviet Union.
