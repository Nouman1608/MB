These questions cover section **4.5.2 The human nervous system** (4.5.2.1 to 4.5.2.4, with required practical
activity 7) of the AQA GCSE Biology (8461) specification, for first teaching from 2016. Questions on the brain,
the eye and body temperature are Biology only. Questions or parts marked **Higher tier only** cover (HT only)
content. All data are invented for practice.

## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.**

**(a)** Name the type of neurone that carries impulses from the CNS to an effector. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the **two** types of effector. **[1]**
**(c)** Name the **two** parts of the CNS. **[1]**

**2.**

**(a)** A patient's cerebellum is damaged. Suggest **one** effect on the patient. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the region of the brain responsible for memory and language. **[1]**
**(c)** Name the region of the brain that controls breathing. **[1]**

**3.** Name the part of the eye described.

**(a)** The tough white outer layer **[1]**
**(b)** The part that carries impulses to the brain **[1]**
**(c)** The layer that contains the light receptors **[1]**

**4.** You blink automatically when a speck of dust flies towards your eye. Explain why this reflex is
important. **[2]**

**5.** A person walks from a dark room into bright sunlight. Describe and explain the change in the size of
their pupils. **[2]**

### Section B: longer and structured questions

**6.** A student tests whether practice changes reaction time. Each day for five days, she does the ruler-drop
test three times with her right hand.

| Day | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mean distance ruler fell (cm) | ? | 17.5 | 15.0 | 13.5 | 13.0 |

On day 1 her three distances were 21 cm, 19 cm and 23 cm.

**(a)** Calculate the mean distance for day 1. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the trend in the results. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest a reason for the trend. **[1]**
**(d)** Give **two** variables she should keep the same. **[2]**

**7.** A person's hand touches a cactus spine and is pulled away. These events are in the wrong order.

- A -- impulse passes along the motor neurone
- B -- receptors in the skin detect the spine
- C -- the muscle in the arm contracts
- D -- impulse passes along the sensory neurone
- E -- impulse passes along the relay neurone in the spinal cord

**(a)** Put the events in the correct order. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the response happens so quickly. **[2]**

**8.** A student reading a book looks up at a bus in the distance. Describe the changes in the eye that keep the
bus in focus. **[4]**

**9.** In one person's eye, light rays from a distant object meet in front of the retina.

**(a)** Name this defect. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the type of spectacle lens that corrects it. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how the spectacle lens corrects the defect. **[2]**
**(d)** Give **one** other treatment for this defect. **[1]**

**10.** (Higher tier only)

**(a)** Give **two** methods neuroscientists have used to map regions of the brain to their functions. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why it is difficult to treat damage to the brain. **[2]**

**11.** A marathon runner competes on a hot day.

**(a)** Describe **two** responses in her skin that help to stop her body temperature rising too high. **[2]**
**(b)** (Higher tier only) Explain how each response lowers body temperature. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest why a runner who drinks no water during the race may overheat. **[2]**

**12.** Plan an investigation into the effect of caffeine on human reaction time using the ruler-drop test. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Motor neurone [1]
**(b)** Muscles and glands (both needed) [1]
**(c)** Brain and spinal cord (both needed) [1]

**2. (a)** Any one: poor balance; poorly coordinated movement [1]
**(b)** Cerebral cortex [1]
**(c)** Medulla [1]

**3. (a)** Sclera [1]
**(b)** Optic nerve [1]
**(c)** Retina [1]

**4.** The reflex is automatic and rapid [1]; so the eye is protected from damage before the conscious brain could react [1]

**5.** The pupils become smaller [1]; so less light enters the eye, protecting the retina [1]

**6. (a)** (21 + 19 + 23) ÷ 3 = **21.0 cm** [1]
**(b)** The mean distance decreases each day, from 21.0 cm to 13.0 cm [1]; the decrease gets smaller by days 4 and 5 (it levels off) [1]
**(c)** She improves with practice, so her reaction time gets shorter [1]
**(d)** Any two: same person dropping the ruler; same hand; same starting position of the ruler; same ruler; same time of day [2]
*Examiner insight:* One mark for each control variable in (d); "keep it fair" is not a variable.

**7. (a)** B, D, E [1]; A, C [1]
**(b)** It is a reflex, so it is automatic [1]; the conscious part of the brain is not involved, so no time is spent deciding [1]

**8.** One mark for each point:
- the ciliary muscles relax [1]
- the suspensory ligaments are pulled tight [1]
- the lens is pulled thin [1]
- so light is refracted only slightly and focuses on the retina [1]
*Examiner insight:* Writing "the muscles contract" reverses the change for a distant object and loses the first mark.

**9. (a)** Myopia (short sightedness) [1]
**(b)** Concave lens [1]
**(c)** The lens refracts the light rays (spreads them out) before they enter the eye [1]; so they focus on the retina [1]
**(d)** Any one: contact lenses; laser surgery to change the shape of the cornea; a replacement lens [1]

**10. (a)** Any two: studying patients with brain damage; electrically stimulating different parts of the brain; MRI scanning [2]
**(b)** The brain is complex and delicate [1]; so treatment may damage other regions, and damage may be permanent [1]
*Examiner insight:* One mark for each method in (a).

**11. (a)** Blood vessels in the skin dilate (vasodilation) [1]; sweat glands produce sweat [1]
**(b)** Vasodilation brings more blood close to the skin surface, so more energy is transferred to the environment [1]; sweat evaporating from the skin transfers energy from the skin [1]
**(c)** She loses water in sweat and does not replace it [1]; so she may produce less sweat, and less energy is transferred from the skin [1]

**12.** One mark for each point:
- independent variable: caffeine (for example, a caffeinated drink compared with the same drink without caffeine) [1]
- dependent variable: the distance the ruler falls before it is caught [1]
- method: partner holds the ruler with zero level with the thumb and finger and drops it without warning [1]
- control variables: same person dropping, same hand, same ruler and starting position, same time after drinking [1]
- repeat several times in each condition, discard anomalies and calculate a mean [1]
- allow for practice, for example by practising first or alternating conditions; compare the means [1]
*Examiner insight:* A plan that compares two different people, one with caffeine and one without, cannot isolate the effect of caffeine.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reversing the ciliary muscle changes for near and distant objects.
- Using a convex lens for myopia.
- Missing the relay neurone, or placing it outside the CNS.
- Describing a trend without the values.
- Plans that change more than one variable at a time.
