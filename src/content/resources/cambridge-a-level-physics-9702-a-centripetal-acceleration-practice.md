## Section A

**1.** Explain why a force of constant magnitude that is always perpendicular to the velocity of an object makes the object move in a circle at constant speed. **[3]**

**2.** A turntable rotates at 45 revolutions per minute. Calculate its angular speed in rad s⁻¹. **[2]**

**3.** For the turntable in Question 2, calculate the centripetal acceleration of a point 0.12 m from the axis, and state the centripetal acceleration of a point 0.06 m from the axis. **[2]**

**4.** A car of mass 1200 kg travels round a bend of radius 50 m at a constant speed of 15 m s⁻¹. Calculate the resultant force on the car. **[2]**

**5.** A student writes: "When the string breaks, the ball flies radially outwards because the centripetal force has gone." Correct the student's statement. **[2]**

**6.** State what supplies the centripetal force in each case:

**(a)** the Moon orbiting the Earth

**(b)** an electron moving at right angles to a uniform magnetic field

**(c)** wet clothes pressed against the drum of a spin dryer. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** A puck of mass 0.15 kg on a horizontal frictionless table is attached to a fixed pin by a string. It moves in a circle of radius 0.40 m. The tension in the string is 2.4 N.

**(a)** Calculate the speed of the puck. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the angular speed and the period. **[2]**

**(c)** The string breaks if the tension exceeds 6.0 N. Calculate the maximum speed of the puck at this radius. **[2]**

**8.** A ball of mass 0.50 kg hangs from a string of length 1.2 m. It moves in a horizontal circle with the string at 25° to the vertical.

**(a)** Calculate the tension in the string. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the radius of the circle. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the angular speed of the ball. **[2]**

**(d)** Calculate the period. **[1]**

**(e)** Explain why the string can never be exactly horizontal, however fast the ball moves. **[2]**

**9.** In a fairground rotor, riders stand against the inside wall of a vertical cylinder of radius 2.5 m. The cylinder turns once every 2.0 s and then the floor is lowered. A rider has mass 60 kg.

**(a)** Calculate the angular speed of the rotor. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the normal contact force exerted by the wall on the rider. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how the rider remains at the same height after the floor is lowered. **[2]**

**(d)** Calculate the ratio of the normal contact force to the rider's weight. **[1]**

**10.** The Moon moves in an approximately circular orbit of radius 3.84 × 10⁸ m with a period of 27.3 days.

**(a)** Calculate the centripetal acceleration of the Moon. **[3]**

**(b)** State the direction of this acceleration. **[1]**

**11.** A laboratory centrifuge is designed so that a sample 0.10 m from the axis has a centripetal acceleration of 5000g. Calculate the required angular speed and the corresponding number of revolutions per minute. **[3]**

**12.** A small stone of mass 0.020 kg is lodged in the tread of a car tyre of radius 0.30 m. The car travels at 8.0 m s⁻¹ without the tyre slipping, so the tread moves at 8.0 m s⁻¹ relative to the axle.

**(a)** Calculate the resultant force needed to keep the stone moving in a circle about the axle. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the stone is more likely to be thrown off at a higher speed. **[2]**

**13.** A student whirls a rubber bung of mass 0.12 kg in a horizontal circle of constant radius 0.75 m and measures the resultant force on it at several speeds. She plots a graph of force F against v².

**(a)** State the expression for the gradient of the graph. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the value of the gradient, with its unit. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** The force has no component along the velocity, so it does no work and the speed is constant [1]. It changes the direction of the velocity continuously, so the object accelerates towards the centre [1]. Because the force has constant magnitude, the direction changes by equal angles in equal times (constant angular speed), so the path has constant curvature: a circle [1].

**2.** f = 45 ÷ 60 = 0.75 rev s⁻¹ [1]; ω = 2π × 0.75 = 4.7 rad s⁻¹ [1].

**3.** a = rω² = 0.12 × 4.71² = 2.7 m s⁻² [1]. Same ω, so a ∝ r: at 0.06 m, a = 1.3 m s⁻² [1].

**4.** F = mv²/r = 1200 × 15² ÷ 50 [1] = 5.4 × 10³ N [1].

**5.** The ball moves off along the tangent to the circle at the point where the string broke [1], because no resultant horizontal force now acts and it continues with the velocity it had at that instant (Newton's first law) [1].

**6. (a)** The gravitational force of the Earth on the Moon [1].

**(b)** The magnetic force on the moving electron [1].

**(c)** The normal contact force from the drum wall [1].

**7. (a)** T = mv²/r, so v² = Tr/m = 2.4 × 0.40 ÷ 0.15 = 6.4 [1]; v = 2.5 m s⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** ω = v/r = 2.53 ÷ 0.40 = 6.3 rad s⁻¹ [1]; period = 2π/ω = 0.99 s [1].

**(c)** v² = 6.0 × 0.40 ÷ 0.15 = 16 [1]; v = 4.0 m s⁻¹ [1].

**8. (a)** Vertical equilibrium: T cos 25° = mg [1]; T = 0.50 × 9.81 ÷ 0.906 = 5.4 N [1].

**(b)** r = 1.2 sin 25° = 0.51 m [1].

**(c)** T sin 25° = mrω², so ω² = (5.41 × 0.423) ÷ (0.50 × 0.507) = 9.0 [1]; ω = 3.0 rad s⁻¹ [1]. (Or ω² = g/(L cos θ) = 9.81 ÷ (1.2 × 0.906).)

**(d)** Period = 2π ÷ 3.0 = 2.1 s [1].

**(e)** The weight acts vertically, so the tension must have a vertical component equal to mg [1]; a horizontal string has no vertical component, so θ can approach 90° only as T becomes very large, and never reaches it [1].

**9. (a)** ω = 2π ÷ 2.0 = 3.1 rad s⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** N = mrω² = 60 × 2.5 × 3.14² [1] = 1.5 × 10³ N [1].

**(c)** Friction between the rider and the wall acts vertically upwards [1] and is equal to the rider's weight, so there is no resultant vertical force [1].

**(d)** N ÷ mg = 1480 ÷ 589 = 2.5 [1].

**10. (a)** T = 27.3 × 24 × 3600 = 2.36 × 10⁶ s [1]; ω = 2π/T = 2.66 × 10⁻⁶ rad s⁻¹ [1]; a = rω² = 3.84 × 10⁸ × (2.66 × 10⁻⁶)² = 2.7 × 10⁻³ m s⁻² [1].

**(b)** Towards the centre of the Earth (the centre of the orbit) [1].

**11.** a = 5000 × 9.81 = 4.9 × 10⁴ m s⁻² [1]; ω = √(a/r) = √(4.9 × 10⁵) = 7.0 × 10² rad s⁻¹ [1]; revolutions per minute = 700 × 60 ÷ 2π = 6.7 × 10³ rev min⁻¹ [1].

**12. (a)** F = mv²/r = 0.020 × 8.0² ÷ 0.30 [1] = 4.3 N [1].

**(b)** The force needed is proportional to v² [1], and the grip of the tread on the stone can supply only a limited force, so above some speed the force required exceeds it and the stone leaves along a tangent [1].

**13. (a)** F = (m/r)v², so the gradient is m/r [1].

**(b)** 0.12 ÷ 0.75 = 0.16 kg m⁻¹ [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving ω in revolutions per second or per minute.
- Using the string length as the radius of a conical pendulum, or the whole tension as the centripetal force.
- Describing the motion after a string breaks as "outwards" rather than along the tangent.
- Naming "centripetal force" as what supplies the centripetal force; the mark is for the real force.
- Forgetting that a ∝ r applies only when ω is fixed, and a ∝ 1/r only when v is fixed.
- Converting days to seconds incorrectly in orbit questions.
