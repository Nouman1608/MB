## What you must be able to do

- (a) Explain why a provision for irrecoverable debts is necessary.
- (b) Distinguish an irrecoverable debt from a provision for an irrecoverable debt.
- (c) Calculate and record both in the books of account.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Trade receivables | Customers who owe the business for credit sales |
| Irrecoverable debt | A named customer's debt that will not be collected; written off |
| Write-off | Removing the debt from the customer's account and charging it as an expense |
| Provision for irrecoverable debts | Year-end estimate of possible losses on the remaining receivables |
| Increase in provision | Extra amount needed this year; an expense |
| Decrease in provision | Amount no longer needed; income (reduces total expenses) |
| Allowance for doubtful debt | The glossary's newer name for the provision |

## Why a provision is needed

1. Prudence: do not overstate assets or profit.
2. Accruals: match the expected loss to the year of the sales.
3. Realistic receivables for users judging liquidity.
4. Stops the owner withdrawing profit that will never turn into cash.

## Distinguish in one line

An irrecoverable debt is a **known** loss on a **named** customer, whose account is closed; a provision is an **estimate** of **possible** losses on the remaining customers, whose accounts are unchanged.

## Calculation order

1. Receivables − write-offs.
2. × percentage = required provision.
3. Required − opening provision = change.
4. Positive: increase (expense). Negative: decrease (income).

Quick figures: receivables 22,600, write-off 600, rate 4%. Required provision 22,000 × 4% = 880. Opening 1,000. Decrease 120 (income).

## Double entries

| Event | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Write off a debt | Irrecoverable debts | Customer (trade receivables) |
| Year-end transfer of write-offs | Income statement | Irrecoverable debts |
| First provision or increase | Income statement | Provision for irrecoverable debts |
| Decrease in provision | Provision for irrecoverable debts | Income statement |

## Where it appears

| Item | Income statement | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|
| Irrecoverable debts written off | Expense | Already removed from receivables |
| Increase in provision | Expense | |
| Decrease in provision | Income (added after gross profit) | |
| Provision balance | | Deducted from trade receivables in current assets |

Layout in current assets:

```
Trade receivables                         22,000
Less provision for irrecoverable debts      (880)
                                          21,120
```

## Effect on profit: a three-year pattern

| Year | Receivables after write-offs (rate 5%) | Required provision | Change | Effect on profit |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| 1 (first provision) | 16,000 | 800 | Increase 800 | Reduced by 800 |
| 2 | 19,000 | 950 | Increase 150 | Reduced by 150 |
| 3 | 17,400 | 870 | Decrease 80 | Increased by 80 |

Write-offs in each year reduce profit separately, in full.

## Exam traps

- Percentage applied before deducting write-offs.
- Whole provision charged instead of the change.
- Decrease treated as an expense.
- Provision shown as a liability or deducted from payables.
- Customer's account credited for the provision.
- Provision calculated on other receivables (it relates only to trade receivables).
- Forgetting to bring down the new provision balance.

## Quick self-test

1. Distinguish an irrecoverable debt from a provision for irrecoverable debts.
2. Give two reasons why a provision is necessary.
3. Give the journal entry to write off a debt of 340 owed by R. Burns.
4. Receivables before write-offs 18,500; write-offs 500; provision 5%; no opening provision. What is the charge to the income statement for the provision?
5. In question 4, what is the total charge for irrecoverable debts and the provision together?
6. Opening provision 1,200; required provision 950. What is the entry?
7. Where is the provision balance shown in the statement of financial position?
8. Does making a provision change any customer's account?
9. Debts under 60 days total 14,000 (provision 2%) and debts over 60 days total 3,000 (provision 10%). Calculate the provision.
10. Why is a decrease in the provision added to profit?
11. Which concept is mainly applied by writing off a debt now rather than waiting?
12. What is the balance carried down on the provision account at the year end?

### Answers

1. An irrecoverable debt is a known loss on a named customer, written off; a provision is an estimate of possible losses on the remaining receivables, with no customer named.
2. Any two: prudence; accruals (matching); realistic receivables for users; avoids overstated profit being withdrawn.
3. Dr irrecoverable debts 340, Cr R. Burns 340, narrative "debt written off as irrecoverable".
4. 18,000 × 5% = 900, charged in full as there is no opening provision.
5. 500 + 900 = 1,400.
6. Decrease of 250: Dr provision for irrecoverable debts 250, Cr income statement 250.
7. Deducted from trade receivables under current assets.
8. No. Customer accounts are only changed by a write-off.
9. 14,000 × 2% = 280; 3,000 × 10% = 300; total 580.
10. Less was needed than had already been charged in earlier years, so part of the earlier expense is reversed.
11. Prudence: a known loss is recognised at once.
12. The required provision for that year end.

## Frequently asked questions

**What happens if the required provision equals the opening balance?**
No entry is needed in the income statement; the balance is simply carried down.

**Is the provision calculated on receivables before or after write-offs?**
After. Debts already written off are no longer in trade receivables.
