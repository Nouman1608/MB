**These notes condense content area 3.2.1.9 of 9237: the events and importance of Badr, Uhud, the Trench, Khaibar and Hunayn; Aam ul wafood; the Treaty of Hudaibiyah; Fath-e-Makkah and the purification of the Kaaba; and the final victory, including the farewell pilgrimage.** The full explanation is in the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-islamiyat-9237-battles-fought-by-hazrat-muhammad/).

Army sizes are traditional and approximate. Know the order and the scale.

## Badr

- **Cause:** the Muslims went to meet a Makkan caravan; it escaped; the Quraysh army came instead.
- **Event:** the Prophet (pbuh) consulted; the Ansar promised to follow him anywhere; the Muslims won at the wells.
- **After:** captives treated well; literate prisoners could earn release by teaching children.
- **Importance:** Qur'an 8:41 calls it the day of criterion; confidence and status of the community rose.

## Uhud

- **Cause:** revenge for Badr.
- **Event:** Abdullah ibn Ubayy withdrew with about 300; archers on the hill left for spoils; Khalid ibn al-Walid attacked from behind.
- **Cost:** about seventy Muslims martyred, including Hazrat Hamza (RA).
- **Lessons:** obey commands; spoils must not distract; patience after defeat (Qur'an 3:139).

## The Trench

- **Cause:** an alliance of about 10,000 formed against Madinah.
- **Event:** Salman al-Farsi (RA) proposed the trench; the Prophet (pbuh) dug with the companions; siege, cold wind and mistrust broke the alliance.
- **Importance:** planning and shura; the Quraysh never attacked in force again.

## The Treaty of Hudaibiyah

- **Setting:** 6 AH; the Prophet (pbuh) and about 1,400 companions set out for Umrah and were stopped at Hudaibiyah.
- **Pledge of Ridwan:** after a rumour that Hazrat Uthman (RA) was killed, the companions pledged loyalty under a tree (Qur'an 48:18).
- **Terms:** (1) ten-year truce; (2) return next year for three days; (3) Quraysh who joined the Muslims without permission returned, Muslims who left not returned; (4) tribes free to ally with either side.
- **Why disliked:** clause 3 seemed one-sided; "Messenger of Allah" was left out of the text.
- **Why important:** Qur'an 48:1 "clear conquest"; recognition; peace allowed Islam to spread; letters to rulers.
- **Breach:** the Quraysh's ally Banu Bakr attacked Muslim ally Khuza'a, which led to Makkah.

## Khaibar

- Fortified Jewish settlements north of Madinah, some of whose leaders had helped build the alliance against Madinah.
- Taken fortress by fortress; Hazrat Ali (RA) is remembered for the final assault.
- People kept their land in return for part of the produce. Importance: security and resources.

## Fath-e-Makkah and the Kaaba

- 8 AH: about 10,000 Muslims marched after the treaty was broken.
- Orders: do not fight unless attacked. Safety offered to those who stayed indoors, in the sanctuary or in Abu Sufyan's house.
- The idols around the Kaaba were removed: "Truth has come, and falsehood has departed" (Qur'an 17:81).
- Bilal (RA) gave the adhan at the Kaaba.
- **General amnesty:** the best example of forgiveness from strength. Many Makkans accepted Islam.

## Hunayn, Aam ul wafood and the farewell

- **Hunayn:** about 12,000 Muslims; Hawazin and Thaqif ambushed them in a valley; the Prophet (pbuh) stood firm and rallied them. Qur'an 9:25 warns that numbers "did not avail you at all".
- **Aam ul wafood (9 AH):** the Year of Delegations; tribes sent representatives to Madinah.
- **Farewell Pilgrimage (10 AH):** the Final Sermon at Arafat and Qur'an 5:3, "This day I have perfected for you your religion". The Prophet (pbuh) died in Madinah in 11 AH.

## The prescribed passage: Qur'an 3:121-127

| Phrase | Use it to show |
|---|---|
| "post the believers at their stations" | Uhud began with careful planning |
| "victory at Badr while you were weak" | Success was not because of numbers |
| "three thousand angels" then "five thousand" | Support was conditional on patience and awareness of Allah |
| "good tidings for you and to reassure your hearts" | Angels were a comfort, not the cause of victory |
| "victory is not except from Allah" | Allah decides the outcome |

## Rules of war

No harm to women, children, the elderly or worshippers; no needless destruction of crops or trees; humane treatment of captives; accept peace when offered (Qur'an 8:61).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ghazwah | A campaign in which the Prophet (pbuh) took part |
| Furqan | The criterion between truth and falsehood; a name for Badr |
| Ahzab | The confederates, who besieged Madinah |
| Bay'at al-Ridwan | The pledge of allegiance under the tree at Hudaibiyah |
| Fath | Conquest or opening |
| Aam ul wafood | The Year of Delegations |

## Exam traps

- Do not describe Hudaibiyah as a defeat or a failure.
- Makkah was entered almost without fighting; focus on the amnesty and the Kaaba.
- Uhud was lost by disobedience, not simply because the Quraysh were stronger.
- A 6-mark question needs two reasons plus a source; a 12-mark question on 3.2.1.9 will use Qur'an 3:121-127.

## Self-test

1. At which battle did the archers leave their hill?
2. Who suggested digging the trench?
3. Give two terms of the Treaty of Hudaibiyah.
4. What was the Pledge of Ridwan?
5. Which two tribes fought the Muslims at Hunayn?
6. What happened to the idols around the Kaaba in 8 AH?
7. What does Aam ul wafood mean?
8. Which verse describes the treaty's result as a "clear conquest"?
9. Name two rules of conduct in war.
10. Where did the Prophet (pbuh) give the Final Sermon?

**Answers:** 1. Uhud. 2. Salman al-Farsi (RA). 3. Any two: a ten-year truce; return the next year for three days; Quraysh who joined the Muslims without permission returned; tribes free to ally with either side. 4. The pledge made by the companions under a tree when it was rumoured that Uthman (RA) had been killed. 5. Hawazin and Thaqif. 6. They were removed. 7. The Year of Delegations. 8. Qur'an 48:1. 9. Any two: do not harm women, children or the elderly; do not destroy crops without need; treat prisoners kindly; accept peace. 10. At Arafat.
