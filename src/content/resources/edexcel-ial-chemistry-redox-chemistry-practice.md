This practice set works through Topic 8A: Redox chemistry of the Pearson Edexcel International Advanced Subsidiary/Advanced Level in Chemistry specification (YCH11), Issue 1, September 2017, outcomes 8.1 to 8.10. Topic 8A belongs to Unit 2: Energetics, Group Chemistry, Halogenoalkanes and Alcohols, an IAS unit. Learn the content first from the [redox study guide](/resources/edexcel-ial-chemistry-redox-chemistry/) and the [redox revision notes](/resources/edexcel-ial-chemistry-redox-chemistry-revision-notes/), and track progress on the [printable checklist](/checklists/edexcel/a-level/chemistry/). More resources are on the [course hub](/boards/edexcel/a-level/chemistry/).

Data for this set: H = 1.0, O = 16.0, Na = 23.0, S = 32.1, Br = 79.9, Sr = 87.6. Molar volume of a gas = 24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹ at room temperature and pressure.

## Questions

**1.** Give the oxidation number of the element named in each part.

**(a)** N in NO₂⁺ **[1]**
**(b)** S in SF₄ **[1]**
**(c)** H in NaAlH₄ **[1]**
**(d)** O in Rb₂O₂ **[1]**

**2.** This question is about names and formulae.

**(a)** Name N₂O and K₂SO₄, using Roman numerals to show oxidation numbers. **[2]**
**(b)** Write the formulae of calcium chlorate(I), lithium nitrate(III) and aluminium sulfate(VI). **[3]**

**3.** Define these terms.

**(a)** Oxidation, in terms of electrons and in terms of oxidation number. **[2]**
**(b)** Oxidising agent. **[1]**
**(c)** Disproportionation. **[1]**

**4.** Four reactions are shown.

- Reaction W: BaCO₃ → BaO + CO₂
- Reaction X: 2KNO₃ → 2KNO₂ + O₂
- Reaction Y: 3HNO₂ → HNO₃ + 2NO + H₂O
- Reaction Z: Na₂O + H₂O → 2NaOH

**(a)** Classify each reaction as not redox, redox, or disproportionation. Give oxidation number evidence for each. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why reaction X is not a disproportionation, even though only one reactant is involved. **[1]**

**5.** In acidic solution, zinc reduces nitrate(V) ions to ammonium ions.

**(a)** State the change in oxidation number of nitrogen. **[1]**
**(b)** Write the half-equation for the reduction of NO₃⁻ to NH₄⁺. **[2]**
**(c)** Write the half-equation for the oxidation of zinc. **[1]**
**(d)** Combine your half-equations into a full ionic equation. **[2]**

**6.** In acid, 0.00480 mol of sulfate(IV) ions, SO₃²⁻, exactly reduces 0.00192 mol of bromate(V) ions, BrO₃⁻. Each SO₃²⁻ is oxidised to SO₄²⁻.

**(a)** Write the half-equation for the oxidation of SO₃²⁻. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the amount, in mol, of electrons released by the sulfate(IV) ions. **[1]**
**(c)** Deduce the number of electrons gained by each BrO₃⁻ ion. **[1]**
**(d)** Deduce the oxidation number of bromine in the product, and identify the product. **[2]**
**(e)** Write the full ionic equation for the reaction. **[2]**

**7.** White phosphorus reacts with hot aqueous sodium hydroxide:

P₄ + 3OH⁻ + 3H₂O → PH₃ + 3H₂PO₂⁻

**(a)** Give the oxidation number of phosphorus in PH₃ and in H₂PO₂⁻. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why this is a disproportionation reaction. **[2]**

**8.** Potassium reacts with sulfur to form potassium sulfide, K₂S.

**(a)** Explain, in terms of electrons and oxidation numbers, which element is oxidised and which is reduced. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why metals generally form positive ions and non-metals generally form negative ions. **[2]**

**9.** Sodium hydride reacts with water: NaH + H₂O → NaOH + H₂

**(a)** Give the oxidation number of hydrogen in NaH and in H₂O. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify the oxidising agent and the reducing agent. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why this is not a disproportionation reaction. **[1]**
**(d)** Write two half-equations for the reaction, one for each hydrogen-containing reactant. **[2]**
**(e)** Calculate the mass of sodium hydroxide formed from 0.300 g of sodium hydride. **[2]**

**10.** Strontium reacts with cold water.

**(a)** Write an ionic equation for the reaction, with state symbols. **[2]**
**(b)** Write the oxidation and reduction half-equations. **[2]**
**(c)** Identify the oxidising agent, using oxidation numbers. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the volume of hydrogen, in cm³, formed at room temperature and pressure from 0.657 g of strontium. **[3]**

**11.** In one step of extracting bromine from seawater, bromine vapour is absorbed into water with sulfur dioxide.

**(a)** Write the two half-equations, for bromine and for sulfur dioxide in water. **[2]**
**(b)** Combine them into a full ionic equation. **[2]**
**(c)** State the oxidation number changes and identify the oxidising agent. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the mass of sulfur dioxide that reacts with 4.00 g of bromine. Answer to three significant figures. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** x + 2(−2) = +1, so **+5** [1]
**(b)** F is −1, so **+4** [1]
**(c)** Metal hydride, so **−1** [1]
**(d)** Peroxide, so **−1** [1]
*Examiner insight:* An oxidation number without its sign, or written as "5+", is unlikely to be accepted; write the sign first.

**2. (a)** **nitrogen(I) oxide** [1]; **potassium sulfate(VI)** [1]
**(b)** **Ca(ClO)₂** [1]; **LiNO₂** [1]; **Al₂(SO₄)₃** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Check each formula by adding up the charges; brackets are needed whenever more than one polyatomic ion is present.

**3. (a)** Loss of electrons [1]; increase in oxidation number [1]
**(b)** A species that gains electrons (and is itself reduced) [1]
**(c)** The same element in a single species is simultaneously oxidised and reduced [1]
*Examiner insight:* A disproportionation definition that leaves out "single species" or "same element" describes ordinary redox and is unlikely to score.

**4. (a)** W: no changes (Ba +2, C +4, O −2 throughout), **not redox** [1]. X: N +5 → +3 and O −2 → 0, **redox** [1]. Y: N +3 → +5 in HNO₃ and +3 → +2 in NO, **disproportionation** [1]. Z: Na +1, O −2, H +1 throughout, **not redox** [1].
**(b)** Two different elements change (nitrogen is reduced, oxygen is oxidised); disproportionation needs one element to go both up and down [1].
*Examiner insight:* Each classification needs supporting oxidation numbers; a bare label with no numbers gives the examiner nothing to credit.

**5. (a)** **+5 to −3** (a decrease of 8) [1]
**(b)** NO₃⁻ to NH₄⁺, with 10H⁺ and 3H₂O [1]; 8e⁻ giving charge balance: **NO₃⁻ + 10H⁺ + 8e⁻ → NH₄⁺ + 3H₂O** [1]
**(c)** **Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻** [1]
**(d)** Zinc half-equation × 4 [1]; **4Zn + NO₃⁻ + 10H⁺ → 4Zn²⁺ + NH₄⁺ + 3H₂O** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The electron count in (b) must equal the fall in oxidation number; checking that 8 = 5 − (−3) catches most errors.

**6. (a)** **SO₃²⁻ + H₂O → SO₄²⁻ + 2H⁺ + 2e⁻** [1]
**(b)** 2 × 0.00480 = **0.00960 mol** [1]
**(c)** 0.00960 ÷ 0.00192 = **5** [1]
**(d)** +5 − 5 = **0** [1]; the product is **bromine, Br₂** [1]
**(e)** Correct species and ratio 2BrO₃⁻ : 5SO₃²⁻ [1]; **2BrO₃⁻ + 5SO₃²⁻ + 2H⁺ → Br₂ + 5SO₄²⁻ + H₂O** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Set out each step of a ratio deduction; a correct product stated without the electron arithmetic leaves the "deduce" command unanswered.

**7. (a)** PH₃: **−3** [1]; H₂PO₂⁻: **+1** [1]
**(b)** Phosphorus in one species, P₄ (0), is reduced to −3 [1] and oxidised to +1 at the same time [1].
*Examiner insight:* Quote the starting oxidation number of 0 for P₄; the explanation needs both directions from that value.

**8. (a)** Each K atom loses one electron, 0 → +1, so potassium is oxidised [1]. Each S atom gains two electrons, 0 → −2, so sulfur is reduced [1]. Two K atoms supply the two electrons one S atom gains [1].
**(b)** Metal atoms have few outer-shell electrons, which they lose to form positive ions [1]; non-metal atoms have nearly full outer shells and gain electrons to form negative ions [1].
*Examiner insight:* Link the electron change to the direction of the oxidation number change for each element; one element alone gives an incomplete answer.

**9. (a)** NaH: **−1** [1]; H₂O: **+1** [1]
**(b)** Oxidising agent: **H₂O** (H +1 → 0) [1]; reducing agent: **NaH** (H −1 → 0) [1]
**(c)** The hydrogen that changes comes from two different species, so it is not one species being both oxidised and reduced [1].
**(d)** **2H⁻ → H₂ + 2e⁻** [1]; **2H₂O + 2e⁻ → H₂ + 2OH⁻** [1]
**(e)** n(NaH) = 0.300 ÷ 24.0 = 0.0125 mol [1]; mass NaOH = 0.0125 × 40.0 = **0.500 g** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Questions like (c) test the "single species" part of the definition; quoting the formulae of both species makes the point clear.

**10. (a)** **Sr(s) + 2H₂O(l) → Sr²⁺(aq) + 2OH⁻(aq) + H₂(g)**: correct balanced species [1]; state symbols [1]
**(b)** **Sr → Sr²⁺ + 2e⁻** [1]; **2H₂O + 2e⁻ → H₂ + 2OH⁻** [1]
**(c)** **Water** is the oxidising agent [1], because H goes from +1 to 0 (reduced), while Sr goes from 0 to +2 [1].
**(d)** n(Sr) = 0.657 ÷ 87.6 = 0.00750 mol [1]; n(H₂) = 0.00750 mol (1 : 1) [1]; V = 0.00750 × 24.0 = 0.180 dm³ = **180 cm³** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The question asks for cm³; an answer left as 0.180 with no unit conversion does not answer what was asked.

**11. (a)** **Br₂ + 2e⁻ → 2Br⁻** [1]; **SO₂ + 2H₂O → SO₄²⁻ + 4H⁺ + 2e⁻** [1]
**(b)** Electrons already equal, add and cancel [1]: **Br₂ + SO₂ + 2H₂O → 2Br⁻ + SO₄²⁻ + 4H⁺** [1]
**(c)** Br 0 → −1 and S +4 → +6 [1]; **Br₂** is the oxidising agent [1]
**(d)** n(Br₂) = 4.00 ÷ 159.8 = 0.02503 mol [1]; n(SO₂) = 0.02503 mol (1 : 1) [1]; mass = 0.02503 × 64.1 = **1.60 g** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Use the molar mass of Br₂ (159.8), not Br (79.9); halving or doubling the amount here is a common slip.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing oxidation numbers without a sign, or as ionic charges.
- Missing the hydride exception in NaH or NaAlH₄, or the peroxide exception in Rb₂O₂.
- Leaving brackets out of formulae such as Ca(ClO)₂ and Al₂(SO₄)₃.
- Calling any reaction with only one reactant a disproportionation without checking which elements change.
- Writing "the oxidising agent is oxidised".
- Balancing atoms but not charge in a half-equation.
- Combining half-equations before equalising electrons, or leaving electrons in the final equation.
- Using the molar mass of a halogen atom instead of the diatomic molecule.
- Forgetting to convert dm³ to cm³ when the question asks for cm³.
