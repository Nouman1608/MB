These notes condense section **3.3.13.5 Action of anticancer drugs** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 2 and Paper 3. Explanations and worked examples are in the [Action of anticancer drugs study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-action-of-anticancer-drugs/), and exam-style questions are in the [Action of anticancer drugs practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-action-of-anticancer-drugs-practice/). The previous sub-topic is in the [DNA revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-dna-revision-notes/).

## What you must be able to do

- State that the **Pt(II) complex cisplatin** is used as an **anticancer drug**.
- Describe how it **prevents DNA replication** by **ligand replacement**, forming a bond between **platinum and a nitrogen atom on guanine**.
- **Explain** why cisplatin prevents DNA replication and why such drugs have **adverse effects**.
- **Appreciate** that society must assess the **balance of benefits and adverse effects**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cisplatin | [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] with the like ligands on the same side |
| Complex | A central metal ion with ligands bonded by dative covalent bonds |
| Ligand | A species that donates a lone pair to a metal ion |
| Ligand replacement | One ligand is replaced by another |
| Square planar | Four ligands in a plane, 90° apart |
| Cross-link | A bond joining two parts of DNA through platinum |
| Adverse effect | An unwanted harmful effect of a drug |

## The drug

- **[Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂]**; platinum in oxidation state **+2**.
- **Square planar**; neutral overall.
- **cis**: both Cl on the same side (and both NH₃).
- The trans isomer is not the anticancer drug.

## The reaction with DNA

```
[Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] + 2 N(guanine) → [Pt(NH₃)₂(N-guanine)₂]²⁺ + 2Cl⁻
```

- **Cl⁻ ligands are replaced** by nitrogen atoms of guanine.
- A **lone pair on N of guanine** forms a **dative covalent bond** to Pt.
- **NH₃ ligands remain**.
- The two Cl⁻ are cis, so Pt can bond to **two nearby N atoms** (cross-link).

## Why replication stops

1. Pt bonded to guanine bases distorts the DNA and cross-links it.
2. The strands cannot separate and unwind properly.
3. DNA cannot be copied before cell division.
4. The cell cannot divide, so the tumour stops growing.

Cancer cells divide fast, so they are particularly sensitive.

## Adverse effects

| Cause | Result |
|---|---|
| Cisplatin is **not selective** | It reacts with DNA in healthy cells as well |
| Rapidly dividing healthy cells are affected | Hair loss, nausea (gut lining), reduced blood cell production (bone marrow) |
| The drug and products affect organs | Possible kidney damage |

## Benefits against risks

| For | Against |
|---|---|
| Can treat or cure some cancers | Serious side effects |
| Extends life where other options are limited | Harm to healthy cells |
| Allows treatment where surgery is not possible | Cost, monitoring, patient distress |

The assessment depends on the seriousness of the illness, the likelihood of success and the severity of side effects. Researchers work to make drugs more selective.

## A model answer

"Cisplatin is a square planar Pt(II) complex. The two chloride ligands are replaced by nitrogen atoms of guanine bases in DNA, forming dative covalent bonds between platinum and nitrogen. This distorts the DNA and stops the strands separating, so DNA cannot be replicated and the cell cannot divide. Because cisplatin also affects healthy cells that divide quickly, such as hair follicle cells and gut lining cells, it has adverse effects."

## Worked examples to copy

**Example 1.** Explain why cisplatin has adverse effects.

1. Cisplatin bonds to guanine in the DNA of any cell it reaches.
2. It stops DNA replication in cells that divide rapidly, including healthy ones.
3. Hair follicle cells, gut lining cells and bone marrow cells divide quickly.
4. So hair loss, nausea and reduced blood cell production can occur.

**Example 2.** Write the equation for cisplatin bonding to two guanine nitrogen atoms.

1. Start with [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] and two N(guanine) donors.
2. Both Cl⁻ ligands are replaced, and the NH₃ ligands stay.
3. [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] + 2 N(guanine) → [Pt(NH₃)₂(N-guanine)₂]²⁺ + 2Cl⁻.
4. Check the charges: the left side is 0 and the right side is 2+ plus 2−, which is 0.

## Cisplatin and transition metal ideas

| Idea | In cisplatin |
|---|---|
| Complex ion | A central metal ion with ligands |
| Coordination number | 4 |
| Shape | Square planar |
| Ligand replacement | Cl⁻ replaced by N of guanine |
| Isomerism | cis and trans geometric isomers |

## Calculations

- Mᵣ of [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] = 195.1 + 2(17.0) + 2(35.5) = 300.1.
- Mass fraction of Pt = 195.1 ÷ 300.1 = 0.650.
- n = mass ÷ Mᵣ.

Example: 30.0 mg of cisplatin contains 30.0 × 0.650 = 19.5 mg of platinum, and 0.0300 ÷ 300.1 = 1.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol of the complex.

## Exam traps

- Saying cisplatin "kills" the cancer without the mechanism.
- Bond from Pt to phosphate or sugar.
- Describing the bond as ionic or hydrogen bonding.
- Replaced ligand given as NH₃.
- Tetrahedral shape.
- Saying the drug targets cancer cells only.
- One-sided assessment.
- Mixing the cis isomer with the trans isomer.

## Self-test

1. Give the formula of cisplatin.
2. What is the oxidation state of platinum in cisplatin?
3. State the shape of the complex.
4. Which ligands are replaced when it reacts with DNA?
5. To which atom in guanine does platinum bond?
6. What type of bond is formed?
7. Why does the cis isomer allow a cross-link?
8. How does cisplatin stop cancer cells dividing?
9. Why does it affect healthy cells?
10. Name two adverse effects.
11. Why must society assess benefits and adverse effects?
12. Calculate the percentage by mass of platinum in cisplatin (Mᵣ 300.1, Pt 195.1).

### Answers

1. [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂].
2. +2.
3. Square planar.
4. The chloride ligands.
5. A nitrogen atom.
6. A dative covalent bond.
7. The two Cl⁻ are next to each other, so Pt can bond to two nearby nitrogen atoms.
8. It binds to guanine in DNA and prevents DNA replication.
9. It is not selective, so it reacts with DNA in any cell, especially rapidly dividing ones.
10. Hair loss and nausea (also reduced blood cells or kidney damage).
11. Because the drug can save lives but also cause harm, a decision must balance the two.
12. 195.1 ÷ 300.1 × 100 = 65.0 %.
