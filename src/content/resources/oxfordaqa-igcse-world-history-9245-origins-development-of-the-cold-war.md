**This guide covers the whole of the Section B option The origins and development of the Cold War, 1945-1960 in OxfordAQA International GCSE History (9245).** The specification divides it into two parts: "Rivalry between the USA and USSR, 1945-1949" and "The development of the Cold War, 1949-1960". Section B is assessed through a 3-mark knowledge question, a 6-mark question explaining one cause or consequence, a 9-mark question explaining two, and a 12-mark question that ends in a judgement. The [Paper 1 Section B study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-world-history-9245-paper-1-section-b/) explains the question types for all four options. Follow up with the [origins and development of the Cold War revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-world-history-9245-origins-development-of-the-cold-war-revision-notes/) and the [origins and development of the Cold War practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-world-history-9245-origins-development-of-the-cold-war-practice/).

## What the specification says

The option "considers the clash between capitalist and communist states during this time and their impact on East-West rivalry" and "focuses on the causes and development of the Cold War". It asks "how and why conflict occurred and why it proved difficult to resolve the tensions which arose".

| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| Rivalry between the USA and USSR, 1945-1949 | The end of the Second World War (leaders, ideologies, Yalta and Potsdam, the atomic bomb, the division of Germany); the Iron Curtain and East-West tensions (Czechoslovakia 1948, the Truman Doctrine, the Marshall Plan, Cominform and Comecon, Tito); the significance of the division of Germany (Berlin Blockade and Airlift, the two German states, NATO, atomic weapons) |
| The development of the Cold War, 1949-1960 | The nuclear arms and space races; events in Asia (China, Vietnam, Korea); the Thaw (Stalin's death, Austria, peaceful co-existence, Hungary 1956) |

## Rivalry between the USA and USSR, 1945-1949

### The end of the Second World War

The USA and USSR had fought as allies against Germany, but they had very different **ideologies**. The USA believed in capitalism, democracy and free trade. The USSR was a communist one-party state with a planned economy. The wartime alliance depended on a common enemy, and the leaders changed as the war ended. Roosevelt died in April 1945 and was replaced by Truman, who was more suspicious of Stalin. Churchill lost the election of July 1945 and Attlee replaced him.

At the **Yalta Conference** (February 1945) the Big Three agreed to divide Germany into zones, to hold free elections in liberated Europe and to set up the United Nations. At the **Potsdam Conference** (July-August 1945) the new leaders disagreed about reparations and the future of Germany and Poland. Stalin wanted a buffer zone of friendly states in eastern Europe after being invaded twice from the west; the West saw this as expansion. Germany and Berlin were each divided into four zones, with Berlin lying inside the Soviet zone.

The USA dropped atomic bombs on **Hiroshima** (6 August 1945) and **Nagasaki** (9 August), killing tens of thousands at once and many more later. Japan surrendered. The bombs ended the war, but they also showed the USSR that the USA had a weapon it did not have, which increased Stalin's suspicion and started a nuclear arms race.

### The Iron Curtain

Soviet forces occupied eastern Europe, and between 1945 and 1948 communist governments, backed by Moscow, took power in Poland, Hungary, Romania, Bulgaria and elsewhere. In March 1946 Churchill said that "an iron curtain has descended across the continent", and the phrase came to describe the division of Europe. In February 1948 the communists took control of **Czechoslovakia**, the last democracy in the east, which shocked the West.

The USA decided on a policy of **containment**: stopping the spread of communism. The **Truman Doctrine** (March 1947) promised American aid to any country threatened by communism, starting with Greece, where there was a civil war, and Turkey. The **Marshall Plan** (June 1947) offered economic aid to rebuild Europe, on the belief that poverty helped communism. Sixteen countries accepted. The USSR rejected it for itself and its satellites, seeing it as American dollar imperialism. It responded with **Cominform** (1947), which linked communist parties under Soviet leadership, and **Comecon** (1949), which coordinated the economies of the eastern bloc. When Yugoslavia's leader **Tito** refused to follow Stalin's orders, the USSR expelled him from Cominform in 1948; Yugoslavia remained communist but independent, showing that the communist bloc was not united.

### The division of Germany

In 1948 the western powers merged their zones and introduced a new currency, the Deutsche Mark. Stalin responded by blockading West Berlin in June 1948, cutting land routes. The western powers organised the **Berlin Airlift**, flying in food and fuel until the Soviets lifted the Blockade in May 1949. The Blockade failed and boosted western resolve.

In 1949 two German states were created: the **Federal Republic** in the west (May) and the **German Democratic Republic** in the east (October). In April 1949 twelve countries formed **NATO**, a defensive alliance in which an attack on one member would be treated as an attack on all. The USSR exploded its first atomic bomb in 1949, which ended the American monopoly.

## The development of the Cold War, 1949-1960

### The nuclear and space races

Both sides developed more powerful weapons. The USA tested a **hydrogen bomb** in 1952 and the USSR in 1953. In May 1955, after West Germany joined NATO, the USSR formed the **Warsaw Pact** with its satellite states. The USSR developed **early ICBMs**, able to hit the USA, and in October 1957 launched **Sputnik 1**, the first artificial satellite. This worried Americans, who believed they had fallen behind, and linked the space race to the arms race.

### Events in Asia

In 1949 Mao Zedong's communists won the civil war in China. The USSR supported Mao, and the USA was shocked at the "loss" of China. In Vietnam the communist Viet Minh fought French colonial rule, and after the French defeat at Dien Bien Phu the **Geneva Agreement** (1954) divided the country at the 17th parallel, with the USA supporting the south.

The **Korean War** (1950-53) began when communist North Korea invaded South Korea. The USA took the matter to the United Nations, which sent a force dominated by the USA, led by General **MacArthur**, because of the policy of containment. UN forces pushed the North Koreans back to the Chinese border. China then entered the war and drove them back. The war reached stalemate near the 38th parallel. The USSR and China supported North Korea. In 1951 Truman sacked MacArthur, who wanted to widen the war. An armistice in July 1953 left Korea divided. The war cost millions of lives, increased American military spending and confirmed that the Cold War had spread to Asia.

### The Thaw

After **Stalin's death** in March 1953, Khrushchev emerged as leader and in 1956 criticised Stalin's rule. He promoted **peaceful co-existence**, the idea that communism and capitalism could compete without war. In 1955 the great powers agreed that **Austria** would be neutral and the occupation forces withdrew. A summit at Geneva in 1955 raised hopes.

But the Thaw had limits. In **Hungary** in October 1956 a revolt demanded reform, and the new prime minister, Imre Nagy, promised free elections and withdrawal from the Warsaw Pact. The USSR could not accept the loss of a satellite and sent tanks on 4 November, crushing the rising with thousands dead and many refugees. The West protested but did not intervene, because it did not want nuclear war and was distracted by the Suez Crisis. The invasion showed that the USSR would use force within its sphere of influence, and relations worsened by 1960.

## Writing the answers

**Worked example: a 9-mark answer.**

*Explain two reasons why the Marshall Plan made East-West relations worse.* **[9]**

> **First, it divided Europe into two economic blocs.** In 1947 the USA offered aid to rebuild Europe, and sixteen western countries accepted. Stalin forced the satellites to refuse, so western Europe recovered with American money while the east remained poorer. The two sides were now on separate paths.
>
> **Second, it led the USSR to set up its own organisations.** Stalin saw the plan as an attempt to buy influence in eastern Europe, and he responded with Cominform and later Comecon to tighten Soviet control. This hardened the division and increased mistrust, because each side saw the other as aggressive.

**The 12-mark judgement.** For "The Berlin Blockade was the main reason for the Cold War becoming more serious by 1949", cover the Blockade (the cause in the new currency, the Airlift, the creation of two Germanys and NATO), then other factors (the Truman Doctrine, the Marshall Plan, Czechoslovakia, the atomic bomb), compare them, and conclude.

## Common mistakes

- Dating the Truman Doctrine and the Marshall Plan in 1949. Both were in 1947.
- Saying NATO was set up in response to the Warsaw Pact. NATO (1949) came first; the Warsaw Pact (1955) followed West Germany joining NATO.
- Describing the Korean War as a war between the USA and USSR. The USSR supported North Korea but did not send official troops.
- Forgetting that the Hungarian rising was crushed in November 1956.
- Writing a list of events without saying how each increased tension.
