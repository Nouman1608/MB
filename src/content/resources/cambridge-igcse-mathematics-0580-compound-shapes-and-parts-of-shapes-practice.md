These questions cover subtopic 5.5, Compound shapes and parts of shapes, in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. Question 11, on a frustum, is labelled **(Extended)** because the frustum is the Extended example in the syllabus; every other question suits both tiers. Each question is labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator). Sketch each described shape before you start.

For explanations, read the [compound shapes study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-compound-shapes-and-parts-of-shapes/); for a summary, use the [compound shapes revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-compound-shapes-and-parts-of-shapes-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** (non-calculator) A semicircle is cut out of one 6 cm end of a rectangle measuring 14 cm by 6 cm, with the diameter of the semicircle along that end. Give each answer in terms of π.

**(a)** Find the area of the remaining shape. **[2]**

**(b)** Find its perimeter. **[2]**

**2.** (non-calculator) A T-shape is made from a rectangle 12 cm by 3 cm (the top bar) and a rectangle 4 cm by 8 cm (the stem) joined at the middle of the bar's lower edge.

**(a)** Find the area of the T-shape. **[2]**

**(b)** Find its perimeter. **[2]**

**3.** (calculator) A circular flower bed of radius 4.5 m is surrounded by a path 1.2 m wide. Calculate the area of the path. **[3]**

**4.** (non-calculator) A solid hemisphere has radius 6 cm. Give each answer in terms of π.

**(a)** Find its volume. **[2]**

**(b)** Find its total surface area. **[2]**

**5.** (calculator) A quarter circle has radius 8 cm. Calculate the area of the segment between its arc and the chord joining the ends of the arc. **[3]**

## Section B

**6.** (calculator) A window is a rectangle 1.2 m wide and 1.5 m high with a semicircle on top, whose diameter is the top edge of the rectangle.

**(a)** Calculate the area of glass. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the length of the window's outside edge. **[3]**

**7.** (calculator) A solid is a cylinder of radius 2.5 cm and height 8 cm with a cone of the same radius and height 6 cm on top.

**(a)** Calculate the volume of the solid. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the total surface area of the solid. **[4]**

**8.** (calculator) A metal pipe is 2 m long. Its outer diameter is 12 cm and its inner diameter is 10 cm. Calculate the volume of metal in cm³. **[3]**

**9.** (non-calculator) A cuboid measures 20 cm by 10 cm by 10 cm. A cylindrical hole of radius 3 cm is drilled through it along its 20 cm length. Find the volume of the remaining solid in terms of π. **[3]**

**10.** (non-calculator) A hemispherical bowl of internal radius 9 cm is full of water. All the water is poured into an empty cylinder of internal radius 6 cm. Find the depth of the water in the cylinder. **[3]**

**11.** (Extended) (calculator) A lampshade is a frustum of a cone. Its top radius is 5 cm, its bottom radius is 15 cm and its height is 20 cm. It is the bottom part of a cone whose top part has been removed.

**(a)** Show that the height of the full cone is 30 cm. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the volume of the frustum. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the area of the curved surface of the frustum, to the nearest square centimetre. **[3]**

**12.** (non-calculator) A semicircle is drawn outward on each side of a square of side 4 cm, making a four-leaf shape. Find, in terms of π,

**(a)** the area of the shape, **[2]**

**(b)** the perimeter of the shape. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Rectangle 84 cm²; semicircle ½ × π × 3² = 4.5π [1]; area = **(84 − 4.5π) cm²** [1]

**(b)** Straight edges 14 + 14 + 6 = 34; arc ½ × 2π × 3 = 3π [1]; perimeter = **(34 + 3π) cm** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the cut edge is now the arc, so the 6 cm end it replaced is not in the perimeter, but the opposite 6 cm end is.

**2. (a)** 12 × 3 + 4 × 8 [1] = **68 cm²** [1]

**(b)** Edges: 12 + 3 + 3 + 4 + 4 + 8 + 8 + 4 [1] = **46 cm** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the parts of the bar's lower edge either side of the stem are each (12 − 4) ÷ 2 = 4 cm. The 4 cm where the stem joins is inside the shape.

**3.** Outer radius = 4.5 + 1.2 = 5.7 m [1]; area = π × 5.7² − π × 4.5² [1] = **38.5 m²** (3 s.f.) [1]

**4. (a)** ½ × (4/3)π × 216 [1] = **144π cm³** [1]

**(b)** Curved 2π × 36 = 72π; flat π × 36 = 36π [1]; total = **108π cm²** [1]

**5.** Sector = ¼ × π × 64 = 16π [1]; triangle = ½ × 8 × 8 = 32 [1]; segment = 16π − 32 = **18.3 cm²** (3 s.f.) [1]

**6. (a)** Rectangle 1.2 × 1.5 = 1.8 m² [1]; semicircle ½ × π × 0.6² = 0.18π [1]; total = **2.37 m²** (3 s.f.) [1]

**(b)** Straight edges 1.5 + 1.5 + 1.2 = 4.2 m [1]; arc ½ × 2π × 0.6 = 0.6π [1]; total = **6.08 m** (3 s.f.) [1]

**7. (a)** Cylinder π × 2.5² × 8 = 50π [1]; cone (1/3)π × 2.5² × 6 = 12.5π [1]; total 62.5π = **196 cm³** (3 s.f.) [1]

**(b)** Slant height = √(2.5² + 6²) = 6.5 cm [1]; base π × 2.5² = 6.25π and cylinder curved 2π × 2.5 × 8 = 40π [1]; cone curved π × 2.5 × 6.5 = 16.25π [1]; total = 62.5π = **196 cm²** (3 s.f.) [1]

*Examiner insight:* there is no top circle on the cylinder and no base on the cone: that is where they join.

**8.** Radii 6 cm and 5 cm, and 2 m = 200 cm [1]; cross-section = π(36 − 25) = 11π [1]; volume = 11π × 200 = 2200π = **6910 cm³** (3 s.f.) [1]

**9.** Cuboid 20 × 10 × 10 = 2000 [1]; hole π × 3² × 20 = 180π [1]; remaining = **(2000 − 180π) cm³** [1]

**10.** Bowl = ½ × (4/3)π × 729 = 486π cm³ [1]; π × 36 × d = 486π [1]; d = 486 ÷ 36 = **13.5 cm** [1]

**11. (a)** The removed cone is similar to the full cone, with radii 5 : 15 = 1 : 3. If the full height is H, the removed cone has height H − 20 [1]; (H − 20)/H = 1/3, so 3H − 60 = H and H = 30 cm [1]

**(b)** (1/3)π × 15² × 30 − (1/3)π × 5² × 10 [1] = 6500π/3 = **6810 cm³** (3 s.f.) [1]

**(c)** Large slant = √(15² + 30²) = √1125; small slant = √(5² + 10²) = √125 [1]; curved area = π × 15 × √1125 − π × 5 × √125 [1] = **1405 cm²** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the curved surface of a frustum is the difference of two cone curved surfaces; it is not the average radius multiplied by anything.

**12. (a)** Square 16 cm²; four semicircles of radius 2 make two full circles: 2 × π × 2² = 8π [1]; area = **(16 + 8π) cm²** [1]

**(b)** Four semicircular arcs make two full circumferences: 2 × 2π × 2 [1] = **8π cm** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the sides of the square are inside the shape, so they do not appear in the perimeter.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Including internal edges, or the edge where two pieces join, in a perimeter.
- Using the diameter of a semicircle as its radius.
- Missing the flat face of a hemisphere or including hidden joins in a surface area.
- Forgetting to convert 2 m to 200 cm before multiplying by an area in cm².
- In a frustum, guessing the height of the removed cone instead of using similarity.
- Rounding a slant height before the final step.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I find the area of a shape with a hole in it?**
Find the area of the whole outer shape and subtract the area of the hole. The same idea gives the volume of a solid with a hole drilled through it.

**What is the total surface area of a solid hemisphere?**
The curved surface 2πr² plus the flat circular face πr², which is 3πr² in total.
