## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which statement describes a gene? **[1]**

A: an alternative form of an allele

B: a length of DNA that codes for a protein

C: a pair of chromosomes in a nucleus

D: a protein that controls a feature

**2.** Which list is in order of size, from largest to smallest? **[1]**

A: chromosome, gene, nucleus

B: gene, chromosome, nucleus

C: nucleus, chromosome, gene

D: nucleus, gene, chromosome

**3.** In pea plants, one gene affects seed colour. One form of this gene gives yellow seeds and another form gives green seeds.

**(a)** State the term used for each form of the gene. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why "yellow" and "green" are not two separate genes. **[1]**

**4.** State the sex chromosome or chromosomes found in:

**(a)** a human egg cell **[1]**

**(b)** human sperm cells. **[1]**

**5.** *(Extended)* State where in a cell:

**(a)** mRNA is made **[1]**

**(b)** amino acids are assembled into proteins. **[1]**

**6.** *(Extended)* State the number of chromosomes in:

**(a)** a human liver cell **[1]**

**(b)** a human sperm cell. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A couple are expecting a baby.

**(a)** Draw a genetic diagram to show how the sex of their child is inherited. Include the parents' sex chromosomes, the gametes and the possible offspring. **[4]**

**(b)** State the probability that the child will be a girl. **[1]**

**8.** *(Extended)* These statements describe how a protein is made, but they are in the wrong order.

P: the mRNA passes through a ribosome

Q: mRNA is made in the nucleus as a copy of the gene

R: the ribosome assembles amino acids into a protein

S: the mRNA moves out of the nucleus into the cytoplasm

**(a)** Write the letters in the correct order. **[2]**

**(b)** State what determines the order in which the amino acids are joined. **[1]**

**9.** *(Extended)* A change in the base sequence of a gene means that an enzyme no longer breaks down its substrate.

**(a)** Explain how the change in base sequence stops the enzyme working. **[3]**

**(b)** Enzymes are one type of protein whose production is controlled by DNA. Name **two** other types of protein named in the syllabus. **[2]**

**10.** *(Extended)* Three cells, A, B and C, were taken from different organs of the same animal. Each was tested for four genes and for the proteins those genes code for.

| | Cell A | Cell B | Cell C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Genes 1, 2, 3 and 4 present? | all four | all four | all four |
| Proteins made | 1, 2, 4 | 1, 3, 4 | 1, 4 |

**(a)** Using the table, state which gene is expressed only in cell A. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why cell B does not make protein 2, even though it has gene 2. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest why all three cells contain the same genes. **[1]**

**11.** *(Extended)* The body cells of a species of plant contain 16 chromosomes.

**(a)** State how many pairs of chromosomes there are in a body cell. **[1]**

**(b)** State the number of chromosomes in one of its gametes. **[1]**

**(c)** Describe what is meant by a diploid nucleus. **[1]**

**12.** *(Extended)* Explain why a cell needs mRNA in order to make a protein. **[2]**

**13.** A student writes: "Chromosomes are made of genes, and genes are made of alleles." Write a corrected version of this statement. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]

**2.** **C** [1]

**3. (a)** **Allele** [1]

**(b)** They are **alternative forms of the same gene** (for seed colour) [1].

**4. (a)** **X** [1]

**(b)** **X or Y**: half of sperm carry X and half carry Y [1].

**5. (a)** In the **nucleus** [1]

**(b)** At the **ribosomes** in the cytoplasm [1]

**6. (a)** **46** [1]

**(b)** **23** [1]

**7. (a)** Parents' sex chromosomes **XX (mother) and XY (father)** [1]. Gametes: **X and X** from the mother; **X and Y** from the father [1]. Punnett square or lines **combining gametes correctly** [1]. Offspring **XX, XX, XY, XY**, identified as female, female, male, male [1].

**(b)** **1/2 / 0.5 / 50%** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Gametes must each carry one sex chromosome; writing "XY" as a sperm gamete loses the gamete mark.

**8. (a)** Q first and S second [1]; then P, then R [1]: **Q, S, P, R**.

**(b)** The **sequence of bases in the mRNA** [1].

**9. (a)** The new base sequence gives a **different sequence of amino acids** [1]. This gives the enzyme a **different shape** [1], so its **active site no longer fits / is no longer complementary to** the substrate [1].

**(b)** **Membrane carriers** [1]; **receptors for neurotransmitters** [1].

**10. (a)** **Gene 2** [1]

**(b)** Gene 2 is **not expressed** in cell B [1], because each cell **only makes the specific proteins it needs** [1].

**(c)** All the cells came from **one zygote**, by **mitosis**, which produces genetically identical cells [1].

**11. (a)** **8** [1]

**(b)** **8** [1]

**(c)** A nucleus containing **two sets of chromosomes** [1].

*Examiner insight:* (a) and (b) have the same number for different reasons: eight pairs in a body cell, and eight single chromosomes, one set, in a gamete.

**12.** The **gene stays in the nucleus**, but proteins are assembled at **ribosomes in the cytoplasm** [1]. **mRNA is a copy of the gene** that carries its base sequence to the ribosomes [1].

**13.** Chromosomes are **made of DNA**, and **genes are lengths of that DNA** [1]. **Alleles are alternative forms of a gene**, not parts of it [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing gene and allele.
- Writing a sperm gamete as "XY".
- Saying the mother decides the sex of the child.
- (Extended) Placing mRNA production in the cytoplasm.
- (Extended) Saying different cells contain different genes.
- (Extended) Stopping at "different amino acids" without linking to shape and the active site.
