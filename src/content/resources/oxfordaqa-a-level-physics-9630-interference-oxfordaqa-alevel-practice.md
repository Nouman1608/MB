These questions cover section **3.5.6 Interference** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630) specification, including Young's double-slit experiment, white-light fringes, laser safety, interference with sound and electromagnetic waves, and Required practical 5. Marks are shown for each part. All data are invented for practice.

Revise first with the [interference study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-interference-oxfordaqa-alevel/) and the [interference revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-interference-oxfordaqa-alevel-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by path difference. **[1]**

**2.** Which statement defines coherent sources?

A: They have the same amplitude.

B: They have the same frequency and a constant phase difference.

C: They are always exactly in phase.

D: They emit light of more than one wavelength. **[1]**

**3.** Green laser light of wavelength 532 nm passes through two slits 0.20 mm apart. The screen is 3.0 m away. Calculate the fringe spacing. **[2]**

**4.** Twelve bright fringes are seen, and the distance from the centre of the first to the centre of the twelfth is 33 mm. Calculate the fringe spacing. **[1]**

**5.** In a Young's slits experiment, the slit separation is halved and everything else is unchanged. What happens to the fringe spacing?

A: It halves.

B: It stays the same.

C: It doubles.

D: It quadruples. **[1]**

**6.** Explain why the light from the two headlamps of a car does not produce a visible interference pattern on a wall. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Two loudspeakers A and B are connected to the same signal generator and emit sound in phase at 850 Hz. The speed of sound is 340 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the wavelength. **[1]**

**(b)** Point R is 2.60 m from A and 3.20 m from B. Point S is 3.00 m from A and 3.80 m from B. Determine whether the sound is loud or quiet at each point. **[3]**

**(c)** The connections to speaker B are reversed, so the speakers emit in antiphase. State the effect on the sound at R and at S. **[2]**

**8.** In Required practical 5 a student keeps the slit separation fixed at 0.30 mm and varies D. The results are:

| D / m | 1.00 | 1.50 | 2.00 | 2.50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| w / mm | 2.1 | 3.2 | 4.2 | 5.3 |

**(a)** Explain why a graph of w against D should be a straight line through the origin. **[2]**

**(b)** Use the first and last results to find the gradient, and hence calculate the wavelength. **[3]**

**(c)** Suggest why the student measured across several fringes to find each value of w. **[1]**

**9.** The laser in a double-slit experiment is replaced by a white light source with a single slit in front of the double slit. Describe and explain the pattern seen on the screen. **[4]**

**10.** Microwaves of wavelength 3.0 cm are directed at a metal plate with two narrow slits whose centres are 6.0 cm apart. A detector is moved along an arc centred on the slits.

**(a)** Calculate the angle from the centre line at which the first maximum is detected. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why no second-order maximum can be detected. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the equation w = λD/s should not be used here. **[1]**

**11.** Describe three precautions that should be taken when using a laser in a school laboratory, and explain the hazard they reduce. **[4]**

**12.** In a double-slit experiment, s = 0.50 ± 0.02 mm and D = 1.800 ± 0.002 m. The distance across six fringe spacings is 13.2 ± 0.5 mm.

**(a)** Calculate the wavelength. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage uncertainty in the wavelength and give the result with its absolute uncertainty. **[3]**

**(c)** State which measurement should be improved first, and how. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** The difference in the distances travelled by two waves from their sources to a given point [1].

**2.** **B** [1]

**3.** w = λD/s = 532 × 10⁻⁹ × 3.0/0.20 × 10⁻³ [1] = **8.0 × 10⁻³ m (8.0 mm)** [1]

**4.** Twelve fringes contain eleven spacings: w = 33/11 = **3.0 mm** [1]

**5.** **C** [1]

**6.** The two lamps are separate sources that each emit light in random, short bursts [1], so they are not coherent: their phase difference changes continually and any pattern averages out to even illumination [1].

**7.**

**(a)** λ = 340/850 = **0.40 m** [1]

**(b)** At R, path difference = 3.20 − 2.60 = 0.60 m = 1.5λ [1], so the waves arrive in antiphase and the sound is **quiet** [1]. At S, path difference = 0.80 m = 2.0λ, so they arrive in phase and the sound is **loud** [1].

**(c)** R becomes **loud** [1] and S becomes **quiet** [1], because the extra half-cycle at the source swaps every condition.

**8.**

**(a)** w = (λ/s) × D [1]; λ and s are constant, so w is proportional to D, giving a straight line through the origin with gradient λ/s [1].

**(b)** Gradient = (5.3 − 2.1) × 10⁻³/(2.50 − 1.00) = 2.13 × 10⁻³ [1]; λ = gradient × s = 2.13 × 10⁻³ × 0.30 × 10⁻³ [1] = **6.4 × 10⁻⁷ m** [1]

**(c)** Measuring across several spacings and dividing reduces the percentage uncertainty in w [1].

**9.** The central fringe is **white** [1], because the path difference there is zero for every wavelength, so all colours have a maximum together [1]. The side fringes are **coloured**, with violet or blue nearest the centre and red furthest out [1], because w is proportional to λ; only a few fringes are seen before the colours overlap and the pattern fades [1].

**10.**

**(a)** Path difference s sin θ = λ, so sin θ = 3.0/6.0 = 0.50 [1]; θ = **30°** [1]

**(b)** A second-order maximum needs sin θ = 2λ/s = 1.0 [1], which is θ = 90°, along the plate itself, so it cannot be reached by a detector beyond the plate [1].

**(c)** It relies on a small-angle approximation, which needs the slit separation to be many wavelengths; here s is only two wavelengths, so the angles are large [1].

**11.** Never look directly into the beam [1]; remove or avoid reflective surfaces such as rings and watches, and stop the beam with a matt, non-reflecting screen [1]; keep the beam below eye level [1]. Also accept using a low-power laser as one of the precautions. Hazard: the narrow, intense beam can be focused by the eye onto a small spot on the retina and damage it permanently [1].

**12.**

**(a)** w = 13.2/6 = 2.2 mm [1]; λ = ws/D = 2.2 × 10⁻³ × 0.50 × 10⁻³/1.800 = **6.1 × 10⁻⁷ m** [1]

**(b)** % uncertainties: s 4.0%, D 0.11%, six spacings 3.8% [1]; total = 7.9% [1]; λ = **(6.1 ± 0.5) × 10⁻⁷ m** [1]

**(c)** The slit separation, which has the largest percentage uncertainty; measure it with a travelling microscope [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing by the number of fringes rather than the number of spacings (Question 4).
- Applying in-phase conditions to antiphase sources (Question 7).
- Forgetting to convert millimetres to metres before using λ = ws/D (Questions 8 and 12).
- Describing white-light fringes without explaining why the centre is white (Question 9).
- Using w = λD/s with microwaves when the angles are large (Question 10).
- Giving safety precautions without the reason (Question 11).
- Adding absolute instead of percentage uncertainties for a quotient (Question 12).
