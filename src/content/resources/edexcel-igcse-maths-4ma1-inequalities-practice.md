These questions cover section **2.8 Inequalities** in Topic 2 (Equations, formulae and identities) of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) specification, Issue 2 (November 2017), for the January and June series examined on it. Questions 1–7 use Foundation statements 2.8A–E and are for both tiers. Questions 8–12 are marked **(Higher)**: they use the Higher tier only statements on quadratic inequalities and harder regions. A calculator may be used on every 4MA1 paper, but show your working, because most marks here are for method.

Learn the content first in the [inequalities study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-maths-4ma1-inequalities/) and the [inequalities revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-maths-4ma1-inequalities-revision-notes/). For factorising practice, which questions 9–11 rely on, use the [algebraic manipulation practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-algebraic-manipulation-practice/). ## Questions

**1.** (Both tiers)

**(a)** n is an integer and −2 < n ≤ 4. List all the possible values of n. **[2]**
**(b)** A number line shows an open circle at −1 and a closed circle at 3, joined by a line. Write down the inequality shown. **[2]**

**2.** (Both tiers)

**(a)** Solve 7x − 4 ≤ 3x + 10. **[2]**
**(b)** Solve 11 − 4x > 23. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe how your answer to (b) is shown on a number line. **[1]**

**3.** (Both tiers)

**(a)** Solve −5 < 3x + 4 ≤ 13. **[3]**
**(b)** Write down the integers that satisfy −5 < 3x + 4 ≤ 13. **[1]**

**4.** (Both tiers)

**(a)** Solve (2x − 1)/5 > (x + 3)/4. **[3]**
**(b)** Write down the smallest integer that satisfies the inequality. **[1]**

**5.** (Both tiers) A rectangle has length (x + 3) cm and width (2x − 1) cm. Its perimeter is at most 40 cm.

**(a)** Show that 6x + 4 ≤ 40. **[2]**
**(b)** The width must be greater than 0. Find the range of possible values of x, giving your answer as a double-ended inequality. **[3]**

**6.** (Both tiers) Use a coordinate grid with x and y from 0 to 8.

**(a)** Draw the lines x = 2, y = 1 and x + y = 7. **[2]**
**(b)** Show the region R that satisfies x ≥ 2, y ≥ 1 and x + y ≤ 7. Label it R. **[1]**
**(c)** Write down the coordinates of the vertices of R. **[2]**
**(d)** Decide whether each of the points (3, 3) and (4, 4) lies in R. Show your working. **[2]**

**7.** (Both tiers) A triangular region R has vertices (1, 1), (5, 1) and (5, 5). Its sides lie on the lines y = 1, x = 5 and y = x.

**(a)** Write down the three inequalities that define R. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the point (3, 4) does not lie in R. **[1]**

**8.** (Higher)

**(a)** Solve x² ≤ 49. **[2]**
**(b)** Solve 4x² > 81. **[2]**

**9.** (Higher)

**(a)** Solve 3x² ≥ 10x + 8. **[4]**
**(b)** Describe how your answer to (a) is shown on a number line. **[1]**

**10.** (Higher)

**(a)** Solve x² − 6x + 4 < 0, giving your answer in exact form. **[3]**
**(b)** Write down all the integers that satisfy x² − 6x + 4 < 0. **[2]**

**11.** (Higher) A rectangle has length (x + 5) cm and width x cm. Its area is greater than 24 cm² and its perimeter is less than 30 cm.

**(a)** Show that x² + 5x − 24 > 0. **[1]**
**(b)** Solve x² + 5x − 24 > 0. **[3]**
**(c)** Use the perimeter to write down and solve a second inequality in x. **[2]**
**(d)** Hence find the range of possible values of x. **[1]**

**12.** (Higher) The region R is defined by the inequalities y < 2x + 2, x + 2y ≤ 14 and y ≥ 1.

**(a)** Find the coordinates of the three vertices of R. **[4]**
**(b)** Calculate the area of R. **[2]**
**(c)** Decide whether each of the points (6, 4) and (0, 3) lies in R. Give a reason for each. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** At least four correct values, with at most one wrong value [1]; **−1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4** [1]
**(b)** Limits −1 and 3 [1]; **−1 < x ≤ 3** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), including −2 or leaving out 0 costs the accuracy mark; a list with one wrong value usually keeps only the method mark.

**2. (a)** 4x ≤ 14 [1]; **x ≤ 3.5** [1]
**(b)** −4x > 12 [1]; **x < −3** [1] (dividing by −4 reverses the sign)
**(c)** **Open circle at −3 with an arrow pointing left** [1]
*Examiner insight:* An answer of x > −3 in (b) loses the accuracy mark but keeps the method mark for −4x > 12; follow-through is then allowed in (c) only for the circle type and direction matching that answer.

**3. (a)** Subtract 4 from all three parts: −9 < 3x ≤ 9 [1]; divide all three parts by 3 [1]; **−3 < x ≤ 3** [1]
**(b)** **−2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Two separate answers, x > −3 and x ≤ 3, are acceptable for the method marks, but a single wrong symbol (≤ in place of <) loses the final accuracy mark.

**4. (a)** Multiply both sides by 20: 4(2x − 1) > 5(x + 3) [1]; 8x − 4 > 5x + 15 [1]; **x > 19/3** (or x > 6.33…) [1]
**(b)** **7** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The first method mark needs both numerators multiplied correctly; writing 2x − 1 > x + 3 after "cross-multiplying" earns nothing.

**5. (a)** Perimeter = 2(x + 3) + 2(2x − 1) [1] = 2x + 6 + 4x − 2 = 6x + 4, so 6x + 4 ≤ 40 [1]
**(b)** 6x ≤ 36, so x ≤ 6 [1]; 2x − 1 > 0, so x > 1/2 [1]; **1/2 < x ≤ 6** [1]
*Examiner insight:* On a "show that" the expanded perimeter 6x + 4 must appear before the given inequality; quoting 6x + 4 ≤ 40 with no expression for the perimeter scores 0.

**6. (a)** x = 2 and y = 1 drawn correctly [1]; x + y = 7 drawn through (0, 7) and (7, 0) [1]
**(b)** **The triangle to the right of x = 2, above y = 1 and below x + y = 7, labelled R** [1]
**(c)** Any two correct vertices [1]; **(2, 1), (2, 5) and (6, 1)** [1]
**(d)** (3, 3): 3 ≥ 2, 3 ≥ 1 and 3 + 3 = 6 ≤ 7, so **(3, 3) is in R** [1]; (4, 4): 4 + 4 = 8, which is not ≤ 7, so **(4, 4) is not in R** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The region mark in (b) needs the correct region clearly identified as the question instructs; the specification says boundary conventions (solid or dashed lines) are not required at this level.

**7. (a)** **y ≥ 1** [1]; **x ≤ 5** [1]; **y ≤ x** [1]
**(b)** At (3, 4), y = 4 is greater than x = 3, so **y ≤ x is not satisfied** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each inequality earns its own mark, so one wrong symbol loses one mark, not three; check each by substituting a point inside the triangle such as (4, 2).

**8. (a)** Critical values ±7 [1]; **−7 ≤ x ≤ 7** [1]
**(b)** x² > 81/4, critical values ±9/2 [1]; **x < −4.5 or x > 4.5** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Writing x ≤ ±7 or x > ±4.5 shows the critical values and usually earns the method mark only; the accuracy mark needs the correct inside or outside form.

**9. (a)** 3x² − 10x − 8 ≥ 0 [1]; (3x + 2)(x − 4) ≥ 0 [1]; critical values −2/3 and 4 [1]; **x ≤ −2/3 or x ≥ 4** [1]
**(b)** **Closed circle at −2/3 with an arrow left, and a closed circle at 4 with an arrow right** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Finding the critical values earns the method marks, but the final mark needs the correct region; writing −2/3 ≤ x ≤ 4 (inside instead of outside) loses it.

**10. (a)** (x − 3)² − 5 < 0, or the quadratic formula with a = 1, b = −6, c = 4 [1]; critical values x = 3 ± √5 [1]; **3 − √5 < x < 3 + √5** [1]
**(b)** 3 − √5 ≈ 0.76 and 3 + √5 ≈ 5.24 [1]; **1, 2, 3, 4, 5** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Exact form" means surds; a final answer of 0.764 < x < 5.24 loses the accuracy mark in (a), although the decimals are the right tool in (b).

**11. (a)** x(x + 5) > 24, so x² + 5x > 24, so x² + 5x − 24 > 0 [1]
**(b)** (x + 8)(x − 3) > 0 [1]; critical values −8 and 3 [1]; **x < −8 or x > 3** [1]
**(c)** 2(x + 5) + 2x < 30 [1]; 4x + 10 < 30, so **x < 5** [1]
**(d)** x is a length, so x > 0 and x < −8 is rejected: **3 < x < 5** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The final mark in (d) needs the negative solution rejected using the context; carrying x < −8 into the answer loses it even if (b) and (c) are correct.

**12. (a)** y = 1 and y = 2x + 2: 2x + 2 = 1, so **(−0.5, 1)** [1]; y = 1 and x + 2y = 14: **(12, 1)** [1]; x + 2(2x + 2) = 14 [1]; 5x = 10, so **(2, 6)** [1]
**(b)** Base = 12 − (−0.5) = 12.5 and height = 6 − 1 = 5 [1]; area = ½ × 12.5 × 5 = **31.25 square units** [1]
**(c)** (6, 4): 4 < 14, 6 + 8 = 14 ≤ 14 and 4 ≥ 1, so **(6, 4) is in R** (on the boundary x + 2y = 14, which is included) [1]; (0, 3): 2(0) + 2 = 2 and 3 < 2 is false, so **(0, 3) is not in R** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The method mark for the third vertex is for substituting one equation into the other; a vertex read from a rough sketch with no working gets no credit if it is inaccurate.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not reversing the sign after dividing by a negative coefficient, as in 2(b).
- Applying an operation to only two of the three parts of a double-ended inequality.
- Clearing fractions by multiplying only one side, or forgetting the brackets around a numerator.
- Using the wrong circle type: closed for < or >, open for ≤ or ≥.
- Listing integers that include an excluded end value, or missing 0.
- Drawing x = 2 as a horizontal line, or x + y = 7 through the wrong intercepts.
- Leaving a "show that" without the expression it starts from.
- (Higher) Stopping at critical values, or choosing "inside" when the inequality needs "outside".
- (Higher) Giving decimals when the question asks for exact form.
- (Higher) Keeping a negative length in a context answer.
