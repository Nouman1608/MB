## 1. The bank statement

A **bank statement** is the bank's copy of the business's account, listing every payment into and out of it with a running balance. Its uses are:

- to show items the business could not know about until the bank recorded them, such as charges and interest;
- to confirm that receipts and payments recorded in the cash book really passed through the account;
- to give the balance according to the bank, so that it can be compared with the cash book.

**Read it from the bank's side.** Money the business pays in is a debt the bank owes the business, so it appears as a **credit** on the statement, while in the cash book it is a debit. An overdrawn balance is shown as a debit on the statement (often marked "OD" or "Dr") but is a credit balance in the cash book.

## 2. Why the two balances differ

| Group | Items | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| On the statement but not yet in the cash book | Bank charges, interest paid, interest received, credit transfers, direct debits, standing orders | Update the cash book |
| Errors in the cash book | For example, a cheque recorded at the wrong amount | Correct in the cash book |
| In the cash book but not yet on the statement | Uncredited deposits (paid in but not yet credited by the bank); unpresented cheques (written but not yet paid by the bank) | Show in the bank reconciliation statement |
| Errors by the bank | For example, a cheque charged to the wrong customer's account | Show in the bank reconciliation statement; ask the bank to correct it |

The first two groups are the business's own omissions or mistakes, so the cash book is put right. The last two are timing differences or the bank's mistakes, so the cash book is already correct.

## 3. Worked example: Orchid Lane Opticians, April 2027

At 30 April 2027 the bank column of the cash book shows an overdraft of **1,240** (a credit balance). Comparing the cash book with the bank statement shows:

- bank charges of 45 and interest on the overdraft of 28, on the statement only;
- a direct debit for insurance, 160, and a standing order for rent, 750, on the statement only;
- a credit transfer of 920 from a customer, H Dube, on the statement only;
- a cheque to a supplier for 389, recorded in the cash book as 398;
- cheques to suppliers of 612 and 455, entered in the cash book but not on the statement;
- takings of 1,380 paid in on 30 April, in the cash book but not on the statement;
- a cheque for 210 drawn by another customer of the bank, wrongly charged to Orchid Lane's account.

### Step 1: update the cash book

```
Cash book (bank columns only)
Date     Details                    $   Date     Details                    $
2027                                    2027
30 Apr   H Dube (credit transfer) 920   30 Apr   Balance b/d            1,240
30 Apr   Supplier (error            9   30 Apr   Bank charges              45
           correction)                  30 Apr   Interest paid             28
30 Apr   Balance c/d            1,294   30 Apr   Insurance (direct debit) 160
                                        30 Apr   Rent (standing order)    750
                              -----                                     -----
                              2,223                                     2,223
                              =====                                     =====
                                        1 May    Balance b/d            1,294
```

The supplier was paid 389 but the cash book showed 398, so 9 too much was deducted; debiting 9 puts it back. The updated overdraft is **1,294**: this is the figure for the statement of financial position, shown as a current liability.

### Step 2: the bank reconciliation statement

Treat an overdraft as a negative figure and keep the brackets.

```
Orchid Lane Opticians
Bank reconciliation statement at 30 April 2027
                                                          $          $
Balance as per updated cash book (overdraft)                    (1,294)
Add unpresented cheques: 612 + 455                                1,067
                                                                 ------
                                                                   (227)
Less uncredited deposit                                1,380
Less cheque wrongly charged by the bank                  210
                                                                  1,590
                                                                 ------
Balance as per bank statement (overdraft)                       (1,817)
                                                                 ======
```

**Why each line moves the way it does.** The unpresented cheques have reduced the cash book balance but not yet the bank's, so adding them back moves towards the bank figure. The deposit has increased the cash book balance but not yet the bank's, so it is subtracted. The bank error has reduced the bank's balance only, so it is also subtracted. Check: 1,294 - 1,067 + 1,380 + 210 = 1,817 overdrawn on the bank's records.

The statement can also be prepared the other way, starting with the bank statement balance and reversing every sign, to end at the updated cash book balance. Either direction is accepted unless the question specifies one.

## 4. Purpose of a bank reconciliation statement

- To explain the difference between the cash book and bank statement balances at a date.
- To find errors, whether made by the business or by the bank, and items not yet recorded.
- To confirm the correct bank balance for the statement of financial position.
- To spot cheques that have been unpresented for a long time, deposits that never arrive, or payments nobody authorised, which may point to loss or fraud.

## 5. The impact of digital business transactions

| Change | Effect on reconciliation |
|---|---|
| Online statements are available at any time | Reconciliation can be done weekly or daily instead of monthly, so errors are caught sooner |
| More customers pay by card and transfer | More receipts appear on the statement first, so updating the cash book becomes the main task |
| More payments are made by direct debit and standing order | More items must be entered from the statement |
| Fewer cheques are written and paid in | Fewer unpresented cheques and uncredited deposits; differences are smaller and shorter-lived |
| Accounting software can import the bank feed and suggest matches | Faster matching, but each suggested match must still be checked and errors in categorising remain possible |

Timing differences do not disappear. Card takings may reach the account a day or more after the sale, so the cash book can still show receipts not yet on the statement at the month end.

## Common mistakes

- Entering bank charges, direct debits or credit transfers in the reconciliation statement instead of the cash book.
- Putting unpresented cheques or uncredited deposits in the cash book.
- Reading the bank statement's debit and credit columns as if they were the cash book's.
- Losing track of an overdraft: dropping the brackets, or adding when the balance is negative.
- Using the bank statement balance in the statement of financial position instead of the updated cash book balance.

## Exam technique

- **Prepare** the updated cash book first, balanced and brought down, then the reconciliation statement with a full heading.
- Label every line ("Add unpresented cheques", "Less uncredited deposit") and finish with the balance "as per bank statement", writing "overdraft" where relevant.
- When asked to **explain** a difference, name the type (timing difference, bank error, item not yet recorded) and say which record is incomplete.
- In discussion questions on digital banking, give an effect on the reconciliation, not a general benefit of online banking.
