Condensed notes on section **6.2 Environmental and ethical issues** of Cambridge O Level Business Studies 7115 (for examination in 2026). The full teaching and an annotated model answer are in the [environmental and ethical issues study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-business-7115-environmental-and-ethical-issues/); exam-style questions are in the [environmental and ethical issues practice set](/resources/cambridge-o-level-business-7115-environmental-and-ethical-issues-practice/).

## The syllabus heading

"Environmental concerns and ethical issues as both **opportunities and constraints** for businesses". Every answer should keep both words in mind.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Global warming | Rise in the Earth's temperature linked to gases released by burning fossil fuels |
| Externality | A cost or benefit of a business decision that falls on third parties |
| External cost | A harmful effect on third parties, such as pollution affecting residents |
| External benefit | A helpful effect on third parties, such as jobs for the local community |
| Private cost | A cost paid by the business itself, such as wages or materials |
| Sustainable development | Meeting present needs without reducing the ability of future generations to meet theirs |
| Pressure group | An organisation that tries to influence businesses or government without standing for election |
| Consumer boycott | Customers refusing to buy from a business as a protest |
| Pollution controls | Legal limits on what a business may release into air, water or land |
| Ethical issue | A question of whether a business decision is morally right |
| Child labour | Employing children in work that harms their health or education |
| Fair price | A price for a supplier's product that allows a decent living |

## Environmental impacts of business

- Global warming from burning fossil fuels (factories, vehicles, power).
- Air, water, land and noise pollution.
- Using up non-renewable resources.
- Habitat damage from clearing land.
- Feedback: extreme weather and scarce resources raise business costs too.

## Externalities method

1. List effects on the **business** (private).
2. List who **else** is affected.
3. Label each as external **cost** or external **benefit**.

Why it matters: businesses ignore external costs they do not pay, so pressure groups and laws push them back onto the business.

## Ways business contributes to sustainable development

- Renewable energy, efficient machines, less waste, recycling.
- Materials from managed sources.
- Longer-lasting, repairable products; less packaging.
- Fuller vehicles, shorter routes, local sourcing.

Many of these also cut costs.

## Responding to pressure

**Why respond:** protect sales and reputation; meet green demand; obey the law; save costs; attract staff and investors.
**Why not:** high cost; higher prices may lose customers; small firms may not afford it.

**A pressure group is more likely to succeed when:**

- the business depends heavily on its public image
- the campaign wins wide support and publicity
- the change is cheap compared with the likely lost sales
- the law is moving the same way.

## Legal controls

- Limits on pollution; rules on waste disposal; permission needed for harmful activities.
- Penalties: fines, orders to stop.
- Effects: higher costs; equal rules for all firms; pressure to innovate.

## Ethical issues and responses

| Issue | Conflict | Possible responses |
|---|---|---|
| Child labour in supply chain | Cheap supplies vs harm to children | Supplier checks, code of conduct, deadline to remove it, end contract |
| Fair prices to suppliers | Higher margins vs suppliers' living standards | Guaranteed minimum price, certified schemes, long-term contracts |
| Honest advertising | Higher sales vs misleading customers | Accurate claims, clear labelling |
| Working conditions | Lower costs vs staff safety | Safety training, fair hours |

Benefits of acting ethically: reputation, customer loyalty, possible higher price, motivated staff. Costs: higher inputs, checking costs, possibly higher prices.

## Exam traps

- A cost the business pays is **private**, not external.
- Global warming is one example of environmental impact, not the only one.
- "Be more ethical" earns little; name the action and its cost.
- Legal controls also protect responsible firms from being undercut.
- A "consider" answer needs both sides and a judgement.

## Self-test

1. Define an externality.
2. A factory's smoke damages washing on lines nearby. Is this a private or external cost?
3. A new supermarket brings more visitors to nearby cafes. What type of externality is this?
4. Give two ways a delivery firm could contribute to sustainable development.
5. State two methods a pressure group might use.
6. Give two reasons why a business that sells directly to consumers is more likely to respond to a pressure group than one that sells to other businesses.
7. Explain one way pollution controls can benefit a business.
8. Give one example of a conflict between profit and ethics.
9. State two ways a business could respond to finding child labour at a supplier.
10. Why might paying fair prices to suppliers benefit a business?
11. Give one reason why a small business might not respond to environmental pressure.
12. How can an environmental concern be an opportunity for a business?

### Answers

1. A cost or benefit of a business decision that falls on third parties outside the business and its customers.
2. An external cost, borne by residents.
3. An external benefit to the cafe owners.
4. Any two: fuller vehicles; shorter or better-planned routes; electric or more efficient vehicles; less packaging.
5. Any two: publicity, petitions, protests, social media campaigns, calls for boycotts.
6. Its brand is visible to the public; consumers can switch quickly; bad publicity reaches its customers directly (any two).
7. All firms must meet the same rules, so a careful business is not undercut by a careless rival (or it pushes the firm to cleaner, cheaper methods).
8. Any one: buying from a cheap supplier that uses child labour; paying very low prices to farmers; misleading advertising that raises sales.
9. Any two: inspect and audit the supplier; set a deadline to remove it; end the contract; help the children into school.
10. It secures reliable supplies, can attract customers who value ethical products, and may support a higher price.
11. Changes cost money it may not have, and higher prices could lose customers to cheaper rivals.
12. Customers who care about the issue may prefer the business, allowing more sales or a higher price; saving energy and materials can also cut costs.
