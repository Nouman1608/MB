Subtopic 15.2 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702 (2025–2027) covers the definition of an ideal gas, the equation of state in molar and molecular form, and the Boltzmann constant. These notes are condensed for recall. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [Equation of state study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-equation-of-state/); to apply the ideas, try the [Equation of state practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-equation-of-state-practice/).

## Definition

An **ideal gas** is a gas that obeys **pV ∝ T**, where T is the **thermodynamic temperature** (for a fixed amount of gas).

## The equations

```
pV = nRT        n in mol,   R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹
pV = NkT        N = number of molecules,   k = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹
k = R / N_A     N = nN_A
fixed amount:   p₁V₁ / T₁ = p₂V₂ / T₂
density:        ρ = pM / RT
```

- p in **Pa**, V in **m³**, T in **K**.
- 1 cm³ = 10⁻⁶ m³; 1 dm³ (litre) = 10⁻³ m³; 1 kPa = 10³ Pa.
- In p₁V₁/T₁ = p₂V₂/T₂, p and V units only need to match each other, but T must be in kelvin.
- k = gas constant **per molecule**; R = gas constant **per mole**.

## Special cases of pV ∝ T

| Constant | Law | Straight line through origin |
|---|---|---|
| T | p ∝ 1/V | p against 1/V |
| p | V ∝ T | V against T/K |
| V | p ∝ T | p against T/K |

Against θ/°C, V or p is a straight line meeting the axis at about –273 °C.

## Graphs and gradients

| Graph | Shape | Gradient |
|---|---|---|
| pV against T | Straight line through origin | nR (= Nk) |
| p against 1/V (constant T) | Straight line through origin | nRT |
| p against V (constant T) | Curve; higher T further from the axes | — |

## When real gases are close to ideal

- **Low pressure** and temperature **well above** liquefaction: molecules far apart, attractions negligible.
- Departures from ideal behaviour appear at **high pressure** or **low temperature**.

## Decision guide

1. Is the amount of gas fixed? Yes: use p₁V₁/T₁ = p₂V₂/T₂. No: find n (or N) for each state.
2. Does the question give or ask for molecules? Use pV = NkT. Moles or mass? Use pV = nRT (with n = m/M).
3. Convert T to kelvin and V to m³ before substituting.

## Values worth recognising

| Situation | Result |
|---|---|
| Air in a 60 m³ room, 1.01 × 10⁵ Pa, 20 °C | 1.5 × 10²⁷ molecules (2.5 × 10³ mol) |
| Air density at 1.01 × 10⁵ Pa, 20 °C | 1.2 kg m⁻³ |
| 1.0 dm³ of gas at 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa, 300 K | 2.4 × 10²² molecules |
| k = 8.31 ÷ 6.02 × 10²³ | 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹ |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ideal gas | Gas obeying pV ∝ T (thermodynamic temperature) |
| Equation of state | pV = nRT or pV = NkT |
| Molar gas constant R | 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ |
| Boltzmann constant k | R/N_A = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹ |
| Isothermal | At constant temperature |
| Amount of substance n | Number of moles |

## Exam traps

- Celsius instead of kelvin.
- cm³ or litres instead of m³ with R or k.
- Using the fixed-amount ratio when gas leaks or is added.
- n with k, or N with R.
- Defining an ideal gas by molecular assumptions instead of pV ∝ T.
- Forgetting that pV has units of joules.
- Assuming p against θ/°C passes through the origin.

## Self-test

1. Define an ideal gas.
2. State the relationship between k, R and N_A.
3. Calculate k from R and N_A.
4. 0.50 mol of gas at 300 K occupies 0.012 m³. Calculate its pressure.
5. A tyre at 2.4 × 10⁵ Pa and 10 °C warms to 40 °C at constant volume. Find the new pressure.
6. Gas in a syringe at 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa is compressed from 60 cm³ to 20 cm³ at constant temperature. Find the new pressure.
7. State the gradient of a graph of pV against T for 0.50 mol of an ideal gas.
8. How many molecules are in 1.0 × 10⁻³ m³ of gas at 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa and 300 K?
9. Why can p₁V₁/T₁ = p₂V₂/T₂ not be used for a leaking cylinder?
10. Under what conditions does a real gas behave most like an ideal gas?
11. Show that the unit of pV is the joule.
12. Calculate the mass of helium (M = 4.0 g mol⁻¹) in a balloon of volume 0.10 m³ at 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa and 20 °C.

**Answers:** 1. A gas for which pV is proportional to the thermodynamic temperature. 2. k = R/N_A. 3. 8.31 ÷ 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹. 4. p = nRT/V = 0.50 × 8.31 × 300 ÷ 0.012 = 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa. 5. p₂ = 2.4 × 10⁵ × 313.15 ÷ 283.15 = 2.65 × 10⁵ Pa. 6. pV constant: 1.0 × 10⁵ × 60 ÷ 20 = 3.0 × 10⁵ Pa. 7. nR = 0.50 × 8.31 = 4.2 J K⁻¹. 8. N = pV/kT = 1.0 × 10⁵ × 1.0 × 10⁻³ ÷ (1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 300) = 2.4 × 10²² molecules. 9. The amount of gas, n, changes. 10. Low pressure and temperature well above the temperature at which it liquefies. 11. Pa × m³ = N m⁻² × m³ = N m = J. 12. n = pV/RT = 1.0 × 10⁵ × 0.10 ÷ (8.31 × 293.15) = 4.1 mol; m = 4.1 × 0.0040 = 0.016 kg.
