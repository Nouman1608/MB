## The task in brief

- One compulsory question, 25 marks, half of Paper 3.
- A transcript of a child or children aged 0-8, possibly with other speakers.
- Analyse how **the speakers** use language, with specific detail, linked to wider study.
- AO1, AO4, AO5.

## Stages (ages approximate)

- **Cooing** (0-4 months) then **babbling** (about 6-12 months): reduplicated "mamama", then variegated "madaba".
- **Holophrastic** (about 12-18 months): one word, whole meaning.
- **Two-word** (about 18-24 months): "doggy bark", consistent word order.
- **Telegraphic** (about 24-36 months): content words only, "baby want milk".
- **Post-telegraphic** (3 years on): function words, inflections, inverted questions, subordinate clauses.
- **Ages 4-8**: complex sentences, narrative sequencing, pragmatic control, influence of schooling and reading.

## Grammar to spot

- **Overgeneralisation** (virtuous error): "runned", "foots", "goodest".
- **Pronoun case**: "me want", "him did it".
- **Negation path**: "no sleep" to "I no sleep" to "I don't want to sleep".
- **Question path**: rising intonation ("Mummy go?") to "wh-" without inversion ("Where mummy go?") to adult form.
- **Coordination to subordination**: "and... and... and" to "because", "when", "so that".

## Phonology to spot

| Process | Example |
|---|---|
| Final consonant deletion | "bi" for "bib" |
| Unstressed syllable deletion | "raffe" for "giraffe" |
| Consonant cluster reduction | "tar" for "star" |
| Substitution (e.g. gliding) | "yeyo" for "yellow" |
| Assimilation | "gock" for "sock" |
| Reduplication | "wawa" for "water" |

## Semantics to spot

- **Overextension**: one word for too wide a category ("dog" for all four-legged animals).
- **Underextension**: too narrow ("cat" only for the family pet).
- **Early lexis**: names for people, food, toys, animals; social words ("bye-bye").

## Halliday's seven functions

| Function | Key idea | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Instrumental | I want | "More apple." |
| Regulatory | Do as I say | "Go away!" |
| Interactional | Me and you | "Hello, grandma." |
| Personal | Here I am | "I'm tired." |
| Representational | Telling you | "Car is red." |
| Heuristic | Tell me why | "Why moon there?" |
| Imaginative | Let's pretend | "This is my rocket." |

## Theories: claim, for, against

| Theory | Claim | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| Imitation and reinforcement (Skinner) | Learning by copying and reward | Accent, routines, vocabulary | Overgeneralisations are not copied |
| LAD (Chomsky) | Innate grammatical capacity | Overgeneralisation; Berko's "wug" test (1958) | Underplays interaction; no identified physical device |
| LASS (Bruner) | Caregivers scaffold language | Routines, book reading, turn-structuring | Language develops where child-directed interaction is limited |
| Cognitive development (Piaget) | Concepts before words | "Allgone" after object permanence | Language and cognition can develop separately |
| Child-directed speech | Adults adapt speech to support learning | Recasts, expansions, questions | Varies across cultures; children learn without it |

## Adult speech features

- **Recast**: correct form returned without correction ("Me goed" / "You went? Where did you go?").
- **Expansion**: adult adds detail ("Doggy!" / "Yes, a big brown doggy").
- **Prompting questions**: hand the child the turn.
- **Repetition, high pitch, exaggerated intonation, simplified lexis**.

## Transcript notation (always check the key)

- (.) micro-pause; (2) timed pause
- // or [ overlap
- bold or underline: stress
- arrows: rising or falling intonation
- /slashes/: phonemic transcription
- [brackets] or italics: context

## Paragraph formula

1. **Quote** the exact form, with notation if relevant: "cow eated" or "/wed/".
2. **Name** the feature and the level: past-tense overgeneralisation (grammar); substitution (phonology).
3. **Interpret**: what the child can do and what is still developing.
4. **Theorise**: which theory the evidence supports or challenges, and why.
5. **Qualify**: one transcript is limited evidence, so use "suggests" or "may indicate".

Example chain: *"Want ball ↗" (rising intonation) is a telegraphic utterance functioning as a question; the child
signals interrogative meaning through intonation before using auxiliaries or inversion, which suggests pragmatic
intent develops ahead of grammatical form.*

## Exam traps

- Calling forms "mistakes" instead of explaining the rule.
- Treating ages as fixed.
- Ignoring the adult or the notation.
- Leading with theory and adding data afterwards.
- Stating a theory without a limitation.
- Overclaiming from one transcript.

## Self-test

1. Name the four stages listed in the syllabus.
2. What is a virtuous error?
3. Give an example of consonant cluster reduction.
4. What is the difference between overextension and underextension?
5. Which Halliday function is "Why is it raining?"
6. Which function is "Give me that!"?
7. What did Berko's "wug" test show?
8. What is the key difference between Chomsky's LAD and Bruner's LASS?
9. Give one criticism of imitation and reinforcement theory.
10. What is a recast?
11. What does (2) mean in a transcript?
12. Why should you analyse the adult in a transcript?

**Answers:** 1. Babbling, holophrastic, telegraphic and post-telegraphic. 2. An error that shows the child applying a
grammatical rule, such as "goed". 3. "poon" for "spoon" (or similar). 4. Overextension uses a word for too wide a
category; underextension uses it for too narrow a category. 5. Heuristic. 6. Regulatory (it may also be
instrumental). 7. That children apply plural endings to invented words, so they have learned a rule rather than
copied forms. 8. The LAD is an innate capacity in the child; the LASS is the support provided by caregivers through
interaction. 9. Children produce forms they have never heard, such as "mouses", which imitation cannot explain.
10. An adult repeating a child's utterance in its adult form, without explicit correction. 11. A pause of about two
seconds. 12. Because the question asks about the speakers, and adult responses are evidence for or against
interactionist theories.
