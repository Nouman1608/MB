## How should you use these questions?

Answer each question fully before checking. Link every structural feature to its function, and name the stage of
division precisely (for example "anaphase I", not just "anaphase").

## Questions

**1.** Describe what happens during the G1, S and G2 phases of the cell cycle. **[3]**

**2.** Outline how checkpoints control the cell cycle. **[3]**

**3.** Describe the events of prophase and anaphase of mitosis in an animal cell. **[4]**

**4.** Explain how cytokinesis differs between animal and plant cells. **[2]**

**5.** A student examined a stained squash of an onion root tip and counted 250 cells. 215 were in interphase, 20 in
prophase, 6 in metaphase, 4 in anaphase and 5 in telophase.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of cells in mitosis. **[1]**
**(b)** The whole cell cycle in these cells takes 16 hours. Estimate the time spent in prophase, in minutes. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the root tip was used and why it was soaked in hydrochloric acid. **[2]**

**6.** A cell has a diploid number of 8 chromosomes.

**(a)** State the number of chromosomes in each cell produced by mitosis and by meiosis. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of possible combinations of chromosomes in its gametes as a result of independent
assortment alone. **[1]**

**7.** Explain how meiosis produces genetic variation. **[4]**

**8.** Compare anaphase I and anaphase II of meiosis. **[2]**

**9.** Give **two** reasons why mitosis is important in a multicellular animal, and **one** example of mitosis in
asexual reproduction. **[3]**

**10.** Explain how **(a)** an erythrocyte and **(b)** a neutrophil are adapted to their functions. **[4]**

**11.** Explain how guard cells open a stoma. **[2]**

**12.** The trachea contains ciliated epithelium and cartilage. Describe each tissue and its role. **[4]**

**13.** Explain how stem cells in bone marrow can give rise to both erythrocytes and neutrophils. **[3]**

**14.** Describe how xylem vessels are produced from meristem cells. **[3]**

**15.** Discuss **two** potential uses of stem cells in medicine and **one** issue that must be considered. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** G1: the cell grows and makes proteins and organelles [1]. S: DNA replicates (semi-conservatively) [1]. G2: the
cell continues to grow, makes proteins needed for division and checks the replicated DNA [1].

**2.** Checkpoints are points in the cycle where the cell checks conditions before continuing [1]. G1 checkpoint:
cell size, nutrients, growth factors, DNA damage [1]. G2 checkpoint: DNA replicated correctly / undamaged; spindle
assembly checkpoint: all chromosomes attached to spindle before anaphase [1].

**3.** Prophase: chromosomes condense, visible as two sister chromatids joined at a centromere [1]; nuclear envelope
breaks down; centrioles move to opposite poles and spindle fibres form [1]. Anaphase: centromeres divide [1];
spindle fibres shorten, pulling sister chromatids to opposite poles [1].

**4.** Animal cells: the cell-surface membrane is pulled inwards to form a cleavage furrow, which pinches the cell in
two [1]. Plant cells: a cell plate forms across the middle and becomes the new cell wall [1].

**5. (a)** Cells in mitosis = 20 + 6 + 4 + 5 = 35; 35 / 250 x 100 = **14%** [1].
**(b)** 20 / 250 = 0.08 [1]; 0.08 x 16 hours = 1.28 hours = **76.8 minutes** (about 77 minutes) [1].
**(c)** The tip contains meristem, where cells divide [1]; the acid separates the cells so they can be squashed into
a single layer (and stops division) [1].

**6. (a)** Mitosis: 8 [1]. Meiosis: 4 [1].
**(b)** Haploid number n = 4, so 2 to the power 4 = **16** combinations [1].

**7.** Crossing over in prophase I [1]: non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes exchange sections of DNA,
giving new combinations of alleles [1]. Independent assortment in metaphase I [1]: homologous pairs line up randomly,
so gametes receive different combinations of maternal and paternal chromosomes (also of chromatids in metaphase II)
[1].

**8.** Anaphase I: homologous chromosomes are pulled to opposite poles, and chromatids stay joined [1]. Anaphase II:
centromeres divide and sister chromatids are pulled apart [1].

**9.** Any two [1 each]: growth; tissue repair / replacing worn-out cells; producing genetically identical cells.
Example [1]: budding in Hydra, runners in plants, or budding in yeast.

**10. (a)** Biconcave disc gives a large surface area for oxygen diffusion [1]; no nucleus, so more room for
haemoglobin / flexible to pass through capillaries [1]. **(b)** Multi-lobed nucleus allows it to squeeze through
gaps to sites of infection [1]; many lysosomes containing enzymes to digest engulfed pathogens [1].

**11.** Guard cells take in water and become turgid [1]; their inner walls are thicker / less flexible than their
outer walls, so the cells bend apart and the stoma opens [1].

**12.** Ciliated epithelium: ciliated cells with goblet cells [1]; goblet cells secrete mucus that traps particles
and cilia beat to move the mucus away from the lungs [1]. Cartilage: chondrocytes in a matrix of collagen and
elastin [1]; C-shaped rings support the trachea and stop it collapsing [1].

**13.** Bone marrow stem cells are undifferentiated / multipotent and divide by mitosis [1]. Different genes are
expressed in different daughter cells, so they differentiate [1]: erythrocytes lose their nucleus and fill with
haemoglobin, while neutrophils develop a lobed nucleus and many lysosomes [1].

**14.** Meristem (cambium) cells divide and differentiate [1]; the cells elongate and lay down lignin in their walls
[1]; the end walls break down and the cells die, forming continuous hollow tubes [1].

**15.** Uses [1 each, two needed]: repairing damaged tissue; replacing nerve cells in neurological conditions;
research into developmental biology. Issue [1]: ethical concerns about embryonic stem cells (embryo destroyed), risk
of rejection, risk of uncontrolled division / tumours, or cost.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Placing DNA replication in G2 instead of S.
- Writing "chromosomes separate" in anaphase II instead of "sister chromatids separate".
- Using the diploid number instead of the haploid number in the 2 to the power n calculation.
- Naming a feature of a specialised cell without explaining how it helps.
- Mixing up tissues and organs.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I calculate possible chromosome combinations from independent assortment?**
Use 2 to the power n, where n is the haploid number (the number of homologous pairs).

**Do I need to name the stages within prophase I?**
No. OCR states that no details of the names of the stages within prophase I are required.

**Do I need to name neurological conditions?**
No. The specification says learners are not required to recall specific neurological conditions.
