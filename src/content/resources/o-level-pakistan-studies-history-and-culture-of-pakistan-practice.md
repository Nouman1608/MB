## Section A

**1.** Identify **two** internal factors and **two** external factors that contributed to the decline of the Mughal Empire. **[4]**

**2.** State the year the All-India Muslim League was founded. **[1]**

## Section B

**3.** "The decline of the Mughal Empire was caused mainly by internal weakness rather than external pressure." How far do you agree? **[10]**

**4.** Explain the importance of Shah Waliullah's work in the religious revival of the subcontinent. **[7]**

---

## Answers

**1.** Internal (any two): Aurangzeb's policies (e.g. reversing tolerant policies, prolonged costly wars) [1] [1]; weak and incompetent successors after Aurangzeb, unable to hold the empire together [1] [1]. External (any two): foreign invasions weakening central authority [1] [1]; the East India Company's growing political and economic expansion into Mughal territory [1] [1]. (Award any 4 of the above, one mark each.)

**2. 1906** [1].

**3.** A strong answer explains **internal factors** in depth — Aurangzeb's policies, weak successors, court intrigue and loss of central control [3] — and **external factors** in equal depth — foreign invasions and the East India Company's expansion, which exploited the internal weakness already present [3] — before reaching a **substantiated judgement**: the strongest answers argue the two were **interdependent**, with internal weakness creating the conditions that allowed external pressure to succeed, rather than treating either as sufficient alone [4].

**4.** Shah Waliullah's significance should be explained through **specific actions and their impact**: his Persian translation of the Qur'an made scripture more accessible [2], his efforts sought to unify Muslims around a shared understanding of Islam at a time of political fragmentation [3], and his work laid groundwork that later reformers (such as Syed Ahmad Barelvi) built upon [2].

---

## Where marks are usually lost

- Listing internal and external causes separately without explaining how they interacted.
- Naming a reformer or event without explaining *why* it mattered (significance, not just identification).
- "How far do you agree" answers that describe both sides but never reach an actual judgement.
- Confusing dates or figures from the emergence period (1.2) with the earlier background period (1.1).
