This guide covers section **7.3 Technology and sustainability**. The syllabus asks you to understand "the use of digital applications for all or part of the accounting records", "the need to store accounting data safely and sustainably", "the risks associated with not storing accounting data safely and sustainably", the different types of storage systems (**manual, data storage devices, cloud services, other digital services**) and their advantages and disadvantages. It also notes that you do not need knowledge of, or access to, any specific accounting software package. The [concepts, ethics and technology guide](/resources/igcse-accounting-accounting-concepts-ethics-technology-and-sustainabilit/) gives an overview; this page goes into each point in more depth and practises applying it to a business. Use it with the [technology and sustainability revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-technology-and-sustainability-revision-notes/) and the [technology and sustainability practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-technology-and-sustainability-practice/). All businesses and figures are invented; amounts are in dollars.

## 1. Digital applications for all or part of the records

A digital application is any computer program or online service used to record or process accounting data: a spreadsheet, an accounting program, a till system that records sales, or an online banking service. A business can use them for:

- **all of its records**, from sales invoices to the trial balance and financial statements, or
- **part of its records**, keeping some books digitally and others by hand.

Corbel Café shows a part-digital system. Its till records every sale and produces a daily total, which the owner types into a spreadsheet sales book. Supplier invoices are filed on paper and entered in a handwritten purchases journal. Petty cash is kept in a handwritten petty cash book under the imprest system. The bank's online service provides the bank statement.

### What digital applications do well

- **Automatic double entry**: one input can post both sides, so debits always equal credits.
- **Accurate arithmetic**: totals, balances and ratios are calculated without adding-up errors.
- **Speed**: ledgers, a trial balance and financial statements can be produced quickly.
- **Up-to-date information**: balances such as trade receivables can be checked at any time, helping credit control.
- **Easy copying**: back-up copies can be made and stored elsewhere.

### Their limitations

- **Cost** of equipment, programs, subscriptions and training.
- **Input errors spread**: an amount keyed wrongly appears in every report, and errors such as omission or principle are not detected automatically.
- **Dependence** on power, equipment and, for online services, an internet connection.
- **Security risks** such as hacking, viruses and unauthorised access.
- **Reliance on staff** who know how to use the system.

Why might Corbel keep part of its records by hand? Petty cash entries are small and frequent, and a handwritten book needs no device. The owner may also be more confident with paper for purchases. The trade-off is that totals from the paper books must be entered separately, which adds time and a chance of error.

## 2. Storing accounting data safely

Accounting data must be kept because it is needed:

- to prepare the financial statements and the trial balance
- to collect debts and pay suppliers correctly
- to support decisions by owners, managers and lenders
- as evidence for tax authorities and in disputes with customers or suppliers
- to check work, for example in a bank reconciliation

**Safe** storage means the data stays **complete**, **accurate**, **available** when needed, and **protected** from loss and from people who should not see it. Safe storage supports the confidentiality principle in 7.2: a business that lets customer or payroll records leak breaches the trust placed in it.

## 3. Storing accounting data sustainably

**Sustainable** storage keeps records in a way that limits harm to the environment and avoids waste of resources, now and in the future. Every storage method has some environmental cost:

- paper, ink and printing; filing cabinets and floor space
- electricity used by computers and by the remote servers behind cloud services
- devices that are replaced and thrown away, creating electronic waste

### Worked example: sending statements of account

Hartwell Wholesale posts a printed statement of account to each of 400 credit customers every month. Postage is 0.80 per letter, and each statement uses 2 sheets of paper.

| | Posted | Emailed |
|---|---|---|
| Statements a year | 400 × 12 = 4,800 | 4,800 |
| Sheets of paper | 4,800 × 2 = 9,600 | 0 |
| Postage | 4,800 × 0.80 = 3,840 | 0 |

Emailing saves 9,600 sheets and 3,840 a year. The statements are still kept, digitally, so the records are complete. Hartwell must then make sure the digital copies are stored safely, which is the link between the two halves of this section.

## 4. Risks of not storing data safely and sustainably

| Risk | Example | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Physical loss | Fire, flood or theft of files or computers | Records cannot be rebuilt; statements cannot be prepared |
| Equipment failure or accidental deletion | A hard drive stops working; a file is deleted | Recent entries lost; time spent re-entering |
| Unauthorised access | Hacking, viruses, or an employee viewing payroll files | Confidential data exposed; records altered or fraud hidden |
| Loss of trust and penalties | Customer details leaked; tax records missing | Customers leave; tax authority may impose penalties |
| Out-of-date formats | Old devices that can no longer be read | Records exist but cannot be opened |
| Waste and environmental harm | Unnecessary printing; devices dumped | Higher costs; damage to the environment; harm to the business's reputation |

Protective measures include regular back-ups kept at a different place, passwords and limited access, up-to-date security software, fire-resistant storage for paper, keeping records in readable formats, printing only what is needed, and wiping and recycling old devices responsibly.

## 5. The four types of storage system

| Type | What it means | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Manual | Paper books, files and documents kept in the business | No power or internet needed; cannot be hacked online; low set-up cost | Slow to search and copy; uses paper and space; one fire can destroy it all; easy to misfile |
| Data storage devices | Data held on the business's own computers, external drives, USB flash drives or memory cards | Fast access; large capacity; the business controls the data; no continuing charge | Devices fail, are lost or stolen; small devices are easy to misplace; copies must be made deliberately; electronic waste |
| Cloud services | Data stored on a provider's remote servers and reached through the internet | Access from anywhere; off-site, so safe from a fire at the business; provider usually handles back-ups; easy to share with an accountant | Needs a reliable internet connection; continuing charges; the business depends on the provider; data held by a third party raises confidentiality concerns |
| Other digital services | Records created and kept by services the business uses, such as online banking, email and digital payment or invoicing services | Records produced automatically; little extra effort; reduce paper | Data spread across many services; may be kept only for a limited time; the business has less control over format and access |

Most businesses combine types. A small business might keep its main records in a cloud service, with a copy on an external drive kept at the owner's home, and paper copies only of documents that must be kept on paper.

## Annotated model answer

*Question: Laila keeps all her shop's accounting records on one office computer. Advise her whether to move them to a cloud service.* [6 marks]

> At present all the records are in one place, so a fire, theft or computer failure could destroy them **(risk of current method)**. A cloud service would store the data on remote servers, so it would be safe from a disaster at the shop **(advantage)**, and Laila could reach it from anywhere **(advantage)**. However, she would pay a continuing charge **(disadvantage)** and need a reliable internet connection, and she would depend on the provider keeping the data confidential **(disadvantage)**. I advise her to use the cloud service but also keep a regular back-up on an external drive stored away from the shop, so she is not relying on one method **(justified recommendation)**.

## Common mistakes

- Naming a specific software product. The syllabus does not require it, and examiners want the general features.
- Listing only advantages when asked to discuss or evaluate a storage type.
- Treating "safely" and "sustainably" as the same idea.
- Saying digital records are always error-free. Input errors and errors of principle still occur.
- Giving vague risks such as "it might go wrong" instead of naming the cause and the consequence.

## Exam technique

- **State** or **Identify** a storage type, risk or advantage: a short phrase.
- **Explain** a risk: the cause, what happens to the data, and the effect on the business.
- **Discuss** or **Evaluate** a storage system: advantages and disadvantages, applied to the business in the question, then a judgement.
- **Advise** or **Suggest**: a clear recommendation with a reason, often a combination of methods.
