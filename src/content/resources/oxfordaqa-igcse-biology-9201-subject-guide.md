OxfordAQA International GCSE Biology (9201) is a separate-science biology qualification designed to
be taken over two years. It covers how living things are organised, how they obtain and use energy,
how they interact with each other and their environment, and how characteristics are inherited and
change over time. Assessment is linear: two written papers at the end of the course, each lasting 90
minutes, worth 90 marks and carrying 50% of the grade. Any part of the specification may be assessed
on either paper, and there is no separate practical exam.

## How the course is organised

The specification has six content areas. They can be thought of in three pairs.

**How organisms are built and powered.** *Organisation* sets out how living things are structured,
and *Bioenergetics* explains how they obtain and use energy and materials. Bioenergetics is the most
detailed area in the specification, covering photosynthesis, exchange and transport in plants,
circulation in humans, digestion, breathing and respiration.

**How organisms live together.** *Ecology* looks at living things in their communities and
ecosystems, and *Organisms' interaction with the environment* looks at how organisms respond to the
conditions around them.

**How life continues and changes.** *Inheritance* deals with how characteristics are passed from one
generation to the next, and *Variation and evolution* deals with how differences arise and how
populations change over time.

Practical work is part of the course throughout. OxfordAQA states that practical knowledge is
assessed through the written papers.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Coverage | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Any part of the specification may be assessed | 90 minutes | 90 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | Any part of the specification may be assessed | 90 minutes | 90 | 50% |

The qualification is linear and untiered, so every student sits the same two papers at the end of
the course. Both are externally assessed written exams. First teaching was in September 2016 and
the qualification was first examined in May/June 2018.

One entry rule is worth knowing. OxfordAQA applies a Prohibited Combinations rule across its four
International GCSE Science specifications: a centre cannot enter a student for both a separate
science, such as Biology, and Combined Science in the same series.

## How the parts fit together

Because neither paper is tied to particular topics, the six areas have to be learned as one subject
rather than as two halves. That suits biology, where ideas constantly reappear.

Bioenergetics sits at the centre. Photosynthesis connects to ecology, because it is the source of
energy for food chains, and to the carbon cycle. Digestion, breathing, circulation and respiration
together explain how cells receive what they need to release energy. Organisation underpins all of
this, since every system depends on how cells, tissues and organs are arranged.

Following one substance through the course shows how connected the areas are. Glucose is made in
photosynthesis, which depends on the exchange of gases and the transport of water in plants. In
animals, food is broken down in digestion, absorbed, and carried by the circulation to cells, where
respiration releases energy; breathing supplies the oxygen and removes the carbon dioxide. At the
level of the ecosystem, the energy fixed by plants passes along food chains, and the way organisms
respond to light, temperature and other conditions affects how much of it is captured. A question
that starts with one of these steps can easily ask about the next, so it pays to revise them as a
chain rather than as separate facts.

Inheritance and variation and evolution are tightly linked: evolution depends on inherited
variation. They also connect back to ecology, because the environment shapes which variations are
advantageous. Practical skills form another link, since questions about methods, data and
conclusions can be set in the context of any content area.

## Planning your study

A sensible order across the two years starts with Organisation, which gives the vocabulary and
structures needed elsewhere, followed by Bioenergetics. Bioenergetics is large, so break it into
plant topics (photosynthesis, exchange and transport in plants) and human topics (circulation,
digestion, breathing and respiration).

In the second year, move on to Ecology and Organisms' interaction with the environment, then finish
with Inheritance and Variation and evolution. Keep revisiting earlier areas, since either paper can
ask about them. Practical work should run throughout: note the method, variables and typical results
for each practical as you do it.

Diagrams are worth extra attention in this subject. Being able to draw and label the heart, a leaf
in cross-section or a food chain from memory makes it much easier to explain processes clearly, and
many questions give a diagram and ask you to interpret it.

When the whole specification has been taught, start full timed papers and treat each as a test of
the entire course. The exam-preparation page explains how to approach two papers that can each
assess anything.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in OxfordAQA International GCSE Biology?**
Two. Paper 1 and Paper 2 are each 90 minutes, 90 marks and 50% of the grade.

**Which topics are on each 9201 paper?**
Neither paper is limited to particular topics. Any part of the specification may be assessed on either paper.

**Is there a practical exam?**
No. There is no separate practical exam; practical knowledge is assessed through the written papers.

**Can I take Biology and Combined Science together?**
Not in the same series. OxfordAQA's Prohibited Combinations rule means a centre cannot enter a student for both a separate science and Combined Science in the same series.

**Is IGCSE Biology tiered?**
No. All students sit the same two papers.
