## Questions

### Section A: short answer

**1.** Define the term **quality**. **[2]**

**2.** Identify two reasons why quality is important to a business. **[2]**

**3.** Define the term **quality control**. **[2]**

**4.** State two methods a business could use as part of quality assurance. **[2]**

**5.** An inspector checks 700 shirts and finds 21 faulty. Calculate the fault rate. **[2]**

**6.** Outline one difference between quality control and quality assurance. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Wexcombe Juice introduced quality assurance at the start of Month 2, training staff and giving each stage of the bottling process a checklist.

| | Month 1 | Month 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Bottles produced | 40,000 | 42,000 |
| Bottles rejected | 1,600 | 1,050 |

**(a)** Calculate the reject rate in each month. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate how many bottles would have been rejected in Month 2 if the reject rate had stayed the same as in Month 1. **[1]**

**(c)** Each rejected bottle costs Wexcombe $0.50 in wasted juice and labour. Using your answer to part (b), calculate the saving in Month 2 from the lower reject rate. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain one reason why one month's data may not prove that quality assurance has worked. **[2]**

**8.** Explain why quality assurance is often more suitable than quality control for a hair salon. **[3]**

**9.** Coombe Pottery is a small business with two employees. It makes about 50 hand-painted mugs a week. Explain two advantages to Coombe Pottery of using quality control. **[4]**

**10.** Brightwater Hotel has received many complaints about dirty rooms and slow check-in. Explain two methods of quality assurance the hotel could use. **[4]**

**11.** Cardew Labs makes medicines. At present an inspector tests a sample of each day's output at the end of production. The managing director is considering introducing quality assurance across the whole factory, which would need training for all 120 employees.

Recommend and justify whether Cardew Labs should introduce quality assurance or continue with quality control. **[6]**

**12.** Explain why poor quality can increase a business's costs. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Meeting the expectations of customers [1], for example a product that is fit for purpose, free from faults and reliable [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any two from: repeat customers and recommendations; a good reputation or brand; less waste and lower costs; fewer refunds and complaints; an advantage over competitors. One mark for each of two different reasons [1] [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Checking goods or services, usually after they are produced [1], to find and remove those with faults [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Training employees to follow set procedures [1]; choosing and checking suppliers so that materials meet standards [1]. Also accept: checklists at each stage; employees checking their own work. **[2]**

**5.** 21 / 700 x 100 [1] = **3%** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Quality control finds faults after products are made, usually by inspectors [1], whereas quality assurance aims to prevent faults at every stage, with all employees responsible [1]. **[2]**

**7.** **(a)** Month 1: 1,600 / 40,000 x 100 = **4%** [1]. Month 2: 1,050 / 42,000 x 100 = **2.5%** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** 4 / 100 x 42,000 = **1,680** bottles [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** 1,680 - 1,050 = 630 fewer rejects [1]; 630 x 0.50 = **$315** [1]. **[2]**

**(d)** The fall could be caused by something else, such as a better supply of bottles that month [1], so Wexcombe needs data over several months to see whether the lower rate lasts [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Part (b) uses the Month 1 rate on the Month 2 output. Comparing 1,600 with 1,050 directly ignores the fact that output rose.

**8.** A haircut is produced while the customer is in the chair, so a mistake cannot be removed before the customer sees it [1]. Quality control would find the fault only after it has happened, when the customer is already unhappy [1]. Quality assurance, such as training stylists and agreeing the style with the customer before cutting, prevents mistakes in the first place [1]. **[3]**

**9.** Quality control is simple and cheap to set up [1], which suits a two-person business that cannot afford a large training programme [1]. With only 50 mugs a week, every mug can be checked before sale [1], so chipped or badly painted mugs do not reach customers and damage Coombe's reputation [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Train reception and cleaning staff to set standards [1], so that every employee knows what a clean room and a quick check-in look like and is responsible for achieving it [1]. Introduce a checklist for each room that cleaners complete and sign [1], so that every stage of cleaning is done the same way each time and dirty rooms are prevented rather than found by guests [1]. Also accept other suitable methods, such as an online check-in system, with explanation. **[4]**

**11.** For quality assurance: a fault in a medicine could harm patients, so preventing faults at every stage is more important than finding them at the end [1]; a sample test may miss faulty medicines, which could lead to recalls, legal action and lasting damage to Cardew's reputation [1]. Against quality assurance: training 120 employees costs a lot of money and time [1], and results take time to appear, while production must continue [1]. Recommendation: Cardew should introduce quality assurance [1], because the risk to patients and to its reputation from a faulty medicine is far greater than the cost of training, and it can keep its end-of-line tests as an extra check during the change [1]. **[6]**

**12.** Faulty items waste the materials and labour used to make them [1]. They must be scrapped or reworked, which adds further costs [1], and customers who receive faulty goods may need refunds or replacements, and staff time is spent handling complaints [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining quality as "expensive" or "the best".
- Saying quality control prevents faults.
- Comparing numbers of rejects without allowing for a change in output.
- Recommending an approach without linking the reason to the product or business in the question.
- Giving only advantages of the chosen approach, with no drawback.
