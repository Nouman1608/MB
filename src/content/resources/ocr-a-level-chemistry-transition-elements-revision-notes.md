## Configurations and definition: (a) and (b)

- 4s fills before 3d; 4s empties first when ions form.
- Exceptions: Cr = [Ar]3d⁵4s¹ and Cu = [Ar]3d¹⁰4s¹. Write the full form (1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p⁶...) when asked for sub-shell notation.
- **Transition element:** a d-block element that forms an ion with an incomplete d-sub-shell. In Period 4 that is Ti to Cu.
- Sc³⁺ has 3d⁰ and Zn²⁺ has 3d¹⁰, so Sc and Zn are d-block but not transition elements.

**Method: ion configuration**
1. Write the atom (watch Cr and Cu).
2. Remove 4s electrons first.
3. Then remove 3d electrons for any remaining charge.

Quick reminder: Mn (Z = 25) is [Ar]3d⁵4s², so Mn²⁺ is [Ar]3d⁵ and Mn³⁺ is [Ar]3d⁴.

## Properties: (c)

Use at least two elements for each property. No explanation of colour is required.

| Element | Oxidation states with species and colour |
|---|---|
| Fe | +2 Fe²⁺ pale green; +3 Fe³⁺ yellow to orange-brown |
| Cr | +3 Cr³⁺ green (hexaaqua ion violet); +6 Cr₂O₇²⁻ orange, CrO₄²⁻ yellow |
| Mn | +2 Mn²⁺ very pale pink; +4 MnO₂ black-brown solid; +7 MnO₄⁻ purple |
| Cu | +1 Cu⁺ compounds such as CuI white; +2 Cu²⁺ blue |

**Catalysis** (no process detail required):
- Cu²⁺ speeds up Zn + acid.
- MnO₂ speeds up 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂.
- Industry: Fe (Haber process), V₂O₅ (Contact process). Catalysts lower the temperature needed and so cut energy use (3.2.2(d)). HSW9 risk: many transition metals are toxic.

## Ligands, complexes, shapes: (d) and (e)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ligand | Molecule or ion that donates an electron pair to a metal ion or atom, forming a coordinate (dative covalent) bond |
| Monodentate | One coordinate bond: H₂O, Cl⁻, NH₃ |
| Bidentate | Two coordinate bonds: NH₂CH₂CH₂NH₂ (en), through both N atoms |
| Complex ion | Central metal ion surrounded by coordinately bonded ligands |
| Coordination number | Number of coordinate bonds to the central metal ion |

| Coordination number | Shape | Angle | Specification examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | octahedral | 90° | [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺, [Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ |
| 4 | tetrahedral | 109.5° | [CuCl₄]²⁻, [CoCl₄]²⁻ |
| 4 | square planar | 90° | Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂ |

**Method: oxidation number in a complex**
1. Overall charge = metal oxidation number + sum of ligand charges.
2. Neutral ligands (H₂O, NH₃, en) add 0; Cl⁻ and OH⁻ add −1 each.

Quick reminder: in [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻, Fe = −4 − (6 × −1) = +2.

## Isomerism and cis-platin: (f) and (g)

- **Cis–trans:** square planar [MA₂B₂] (e.g. Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂) or octahedral [MA₄B₂]. Cis = same pair at 90°; trans = at 180°.
- **Optical:** no plane of symmetry, so non-superimposable mirror images. Key example: [Ni(en)₃]²⁺. The cis form of [M(en)₂X₂] is optical too; the trans form is not.
- Tetrahedral complexes do not show cis–trans isomerism.
- Draw in 3-D: wedge and dash bonds, with the en ligand drawn as a curved link spanning two adjacent positions.

**Cis-platin:** cis-Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂. Cl⁻ ligands are replaced inside the cell; Pt binds to nitrogen atoms on DNA bases (often two adjacent guanines). The DNA is distorted, so it cannot be copied and the cell cannot divide. HSW9: benefit of chemotherapy against side effects such as nausea, hair loss and kidney damage.

## Ligand substitution and haemoglobin: (h) and (i)

| Change | Equation (complexed formulae) | Colour | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cu²⁺ + excess NH₃ | [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4NH₃ → [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ + 4H₂O | pale blue → deep blue | stays octahedral |
| Cu²⁺ + conc. HCl | [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CuCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O | blue → (green) → yellow | octahedral → tetrahedral |
| Cr³⁺ + excess NH₃ | [Cr(H₂O)₆]³⁺ + 6NH₃ → [Cr(NH₃)₆]³⁺ + 6H₂O | violet (or green) → purple | stays octahedral |

**Haemoglobin:** Fe²⁺, octahedral. Four N from the haem ring, one N from the globin protein, sixth site for O₂. CO substitutes for O₂, binds far more strongly and is not easily released, so less oxygen is carried.

## Precipitation: (j)

| Ion | First drops of either | More NaOH | More NH₃ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cu²⁺ | pale blue ppt | no change | deep blue solution |
| Fe²⁺ | green ppt (browns in air) | no change | no change |
| Fe³⁺ | orange-brown ppt | no change | no change |
| Mn²⁺ | light brown ppt (darkens in air) | no change | no change |
| Cr³⁺ | grey-green ppt | dark green solution, [Cr(OH)₆]³⁻ | purple solution, [Cr(NH₃)₆]³⁺ |

- Excess NaOH redissolves just one hydroxide, Cr(OH)₃. Excess NH₃ redissolves two: Cr(OH)₃ and Cu(OH)₂.
- Non-complexed or complexed formulae are both acceptable for precipitation, e.g. Mn²⁺(aq) + 2OH⁻(aq) → Mn(OH)₂(s).
- NH₃(aq) gives precipitates because NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻.

## Redox: (k)

| Change | Reagent | Colour change | Equation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ | H⁺/MnO₄⁻ | purple decolourised; pale green → yellow | MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O |
| Fe³⁺ → Fe²⁺ | I⁻ | yellow → brown (I₂) | 2Fe³⁺ + 2I⁻ → 2Fe²⁺ + I₂ |
| Cr³⁺ → CrO₄²⁻ | H₂O₂/OH⁻, warm | green → yellow | 2Cr³⁺ + 3H₂O₂ + 10OH⁻ → 2CrO₄²⁻ + 8H₂O |
| CrO₄²⁻ → Cr₂O₇²⁻ | acid | yellow → orange | 2CrO₄²⁻ + 2H⁺ ⇌ Cr₂O₇²⁻ + H₂O |
| Cr₂O₇²⁻ → Cr³⁺ | Zn/H⁺ | orange → green | Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 3Zn → 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O + 3Zn²⁺ |
| Cu²⁺ → Cu⁺ | I⁻ | white ppt (CuI) in brown solution | 2Cu²⁺ + 4I⁻ → 2CuI + I₂ |
| Cu⁺ → Cu²⁺ + Cu | water (disproportionation) | blue solution, red-brown solid | 2Cu⁺ → Cu²⁺ + Cu |

You need not recall these equations, but you must be able to build them.

**Method: combining half-equations**
1. Write each half-equation; balance O with H₂O, H with H⁺, charge with e⁻.
2. Multiply so electrons lost = electrons gained.
3. Add, cancel species on both sides, check atoms and charge.

**Worked reminder: copper by iodine release.** Cu²⁺ reacts with excess I⁻, and the iodine is titrated with thiosulfate: I₂ + 2S₂O₃²⁻ → 2I⁻ + S₄O₆²⁻. Since 2Cu²⁺ gives one I₂, and one I₂ uses 2S₂O₃²⁻, the overall ratio Cu²⁺ : S₂O₃²⁻ is 1 : 1. So a titre containing 3.00 × 10⁻³ mol thiosulfate means 3.00 × 10⁻³ mol Cu²⁺ was present.

**Worked reminder: disproportionation.** In 2Cu⁺ → Cu²⁺ + Cu, copper goes from +1 to +2 (oxidised) and from +1 to 0 (reduced). Copper is oxidised and reduced at once, which is what disproportionation means.

## Unfamiliar reactions: (l)

- Oxidation number unchanged → substitution or precipitation.
- Oxidation number changed → redox: use half-equations.
- Neutral hydroxide needs as many OH⁻ as the ion's charge.
- Bidentate ligand replaces two monodentate ligands.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Ligands vs coordination number:** [Cu(en)₂(H₂O)₂]²⁺ has four ligands, coordination number 6.
- **Substitution vs redox:** CuCl₄²⁻ formation keeps Cu at +2; CuI formation reduces Cu to +1.
- **Precipitate vs solution:** "deep blue precipitate" is wrong; the Cu-ammonia complex is a solution.
- **Cis–trans vs optical:** trans-[M(en)₂X₂] has a mirror plane; cis does not.

## Quick self-test

1. Write the full sub-shell configuration of Co²⁺ (Z = 27).
2. Why is scandium not a transition element?
3. Give the coordination number and shape of [CoCl₄]²⁻.
4. State the oxidation number of Cr in [Cr(OH)₆]³⁻.
5. Which hydroxides dissolve in excess aqueous ammonia?
6. State the colour change when excess concentrated HCl is added to aqueous copper(II) sulfate.
7. How many moles of Fe²⁺ are oxidised by 2.40 × 10⁻⁴ mol MnO₄⁻ in acid?
8. 3.00 × 10⁻³ mol Cu²⁺ reacts with excess I⁻. How many moles of I₂ form?
9. Name the type of isomerism shown by [Ni(en)₃]²⁺.
10. Why is CO toxic?
11. 0.0180 mol Fe³⁺ reacts completely with excess I⁻. How many moles of I₂ form?
12. Give the observation when Mn²⁺(aq) is treated with excess aqueous sodium hydroxide and left to stand.

### Answers

1. 1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p⁶3d⁷
2. Its only ion, Sc³⁺, has no d electrons, so no ion has an incomplete d-sub-shell.
3. 4; tetrahedral.
4. +3
5. Cu(OH)₂ and Cr(OH)₃.
6. Blue → yellow (green when both complexes are present).
7. 5 × 2.40 × 10⁻⁴ = 1.20 × 10⁻³ mol
8. 3.00 × 10⁻³ ÷ 2 = 1.50 × 10⁻³ mol
9. Optical isomerism.
10. CO replaces O₂ on the Fe²⁺ in haemoglobin, binds much more strongly and is not easily released, so less O₂ is carried.
11. 2Fe³⁺ gives one I₂, so 0.0180 ÷ 2 = 9.00 × 10⁻³ mol
12. A light brown precipitate that does not dissolve in excess, and darkens on standing in air.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Taking 3d electrons off before 4s, giving Fe²⁺ as [Ar]3d⁴4s².
- Writing the Cr or Cu atom as 3d⁴4s² or 3d⁹4s².
- Quoting the number of en ligands as the coordination number.
- Forgetting the shape change to tetrahedral in [CuCl₄]²⁻.
- Saying Cu(OH)₂ dissolves in excess NaOH.
- Using uncomplexed formulae in ligand substitution equations, which the guidance says should be complexed.
- Describing Fe(OH)₂ as orange-brown straight away; it starts green.
- Leaving out the acidification step when turning chromate into dichromate.
- Missing the white CuI precipitate and describing only the brown colour.
