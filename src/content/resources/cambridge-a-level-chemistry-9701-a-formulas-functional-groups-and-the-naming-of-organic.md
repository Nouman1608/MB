This guide covers subtopic **29.1, Formulas, functional groups and the naming of organic compounds**, from
Topic 29, An introduction to A Level organic chemistry, of Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701,
2025–2027 series. It is A Level content. The overview page
[A Level Organic Chemistry: Naming, Mechanisms and Aromatic Shape](/resources/a-organic-chemistry-naming-mechanisms-and-aromatic-shape/)
lists the new functional groups; this page teaches you how to **name and draw** them reliably, including the
aromatic and cyclic cases that cause most errors. Afterwards, use the
[naming revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-chemistry-9701-a-formulas-functional-groups-and-the-naming-of-organic-revision-notes/)
and the [naming practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-chemistry-9701-a-formulas-functional-groups-and-the-naming-of-organic-practice/).

## What the syllabus asks for

1. understand that the compounds in the A Level functional-group table **contain a functional group which
   dictates their physical and chemical properties**;
2. interpret and use the **general, structural, displayed and skeletal formulas** of those classes;
3. use **systematic nomenclature of simple aliphatic molecules**, including cyclic compounds with a single
   ring of up to six carbon atoms, up to six carbon atoms (six plus six for esters and amides; straight
   chains only for esters and nitriles);
4. use **systematic nomenclature of simple aromatic molecules** with one benzene ring and one or more simple
   substituents, for example 3-nitrobenzoic acid or 2,4,6-tribromophenol.

The A Level table adds: **arene, halogenoarene, phenol, acyl chloride, secondary and tertiary amines, amides
(primary, secondary and tertiary) and amino acids**. Everything from the AS table still applies. Naming of
secondary and tertiary **amines** is not required; you must still recognise and draw them.

## Functional groups dictate properties

A functional group is the atom or group of atoms responsible for the characteristic reactions of a
homologous series. Members of a series share the same chemical reactions and show a gradual trend in
physical properties. The syllabus wants you to connect the group to the behaviour:

| Class | Functional group | A property it causes |
|---|---|---|
| acyl chloride | –COCl | hydrolysed rapidly by cold water, giving steamy fumes of HCl in moist air |
| phenol | –OH on a benzene ring | weakly acidic: reacts with NaOH(aq) |
| amide | –CONH₂, –CONHR, –CONR₂ | primary amides form many hydrogen bonds; ethanamide is a solid at room temperature |
| amine | –NH₂, –NHR, –NR₂ | lone pair on N makes them bases |
| amino acid | –NH₂ and –COOH | both acidic and basic properties in one molecule |
| halogenoarene | halogen on a benzene ring | far less reactive to nucleophiles than a halogenoalkane |

## Four kinds of formula

Take propanoyl chloride as an example.

- **General formula** (for the homologous series): acyl chlorides CₙH₂ₙ₋₁OCl; primary amides CₙH₂ₙ₊₁NO;
  2-amino acids with one –NH₂ and one –COOH, CₙH₂ₙ₊₁NO₂.
- **Structural formula**: the minimum detail that shows the arrangement unambiguously: CH₃CH₂COCl.
- **Displayed formula**: every atom and every bond drawn. Where a benzene ring is part of the molecule, a
  displayed formula is **not expected**; draw the ring as a hexagon with a circle.
- **Skeletal formula**: carbon atoms at line ends and junctions, hydrogens on carbon omitted, functional
  groups drawn in full.

**Counting atoms from a skeletal formula** is a frequent first step in a question. For a benzene ring, each
corner is a carbon; a corner with no substituent carries one hydrogen. So phenylethanone, C₆H₅COCH₃, is
**C₈H₈O**: six ring carbons with five H, one C=O carbon, and one CH₃.

## Naming aliphatic compounds: the new classes

The same steps from AS Level apply: find the longest chain containing the principal functional group, number
it so the principal group has the lowest locant, add prefixes in alphabetical order.

**Acyl chlorides.** Replace the "-oic acid" of the parent acid by **"-oyl chloride"**. The C of –COCl is
carbon 1.

| Structure | Name |
|---|---|
| CH₃COCl | ethanoyl chloride |
| CH₃CH₂CH₂COCl | butanoyl chloride |
| (CH₃)₂CHCOCl | 2-methylpropanoyl chloride |

**Amides.** Replace "-oic acid" by **"-amide"**. Groups on the nitrogen are shown with the locant **N**.

| Structure | Type | Name |
|---|---|---|
| CH₃CH₂CONH₂ | primary | propanamide |
| CH₃CONHCH₃ | secondary | N-methylethanamide |
| CH₃CON(CH₃)₂ | tertiary | N,N-dimethylethanamide |

"Six plus six" means up to six carbons in the acyl part and six in the part attached to N (or, for esters, to
O). Hexyl hexanoate is therefore within scope.

**Amino acids.** The –COOH carbon is carbon 1 and gives the suffix; the amino group is a prefix:
H₂NCH₂COOH is 2-aminoethanoic acid; CH₃CH(NH₂)COOH is 2-aminopropanoic acid.

**Cyclic compounds** (single ring, up to six carbons). Add "cyclo" to the stem. When the ring carries the
principal group, the carbon bearing it is carbon 1, and you number round the ring to give other substituents
the lowest locants: cyclohexanol, cyclohexanone, cyclohexene, methylcyclohexane, 3-methylcyclohexanol.

## Naming aromatic compounds

There are two patterns.

**1. The benzene ring is the parent and substituents are prefixes.** Chlorobenzene, nitrobenzene,
methylbenzene, 1,3-dinitrobenzene, 1,4-dimethylbenzene.

**2. A group on the ring gives a parent name of its own.** These names must be learnt:

| Structure | Parent name |
|---|---|
| C₆H₅OH | phenol |
| C₆H₅COOH | benzoic acid |
| C₆H₅NH₂ | phenylamine |
| C₆H₅COCl | benzoyl chloride |

The carbon carrying that group is **carbon 1**. Number round the ring, in whichever direction gives the
other substituents the **lowest set of locants**, then list prefixes alphabetically.

**When a ring is attached to a chain**, the ring can be named as the **phenyl** group, C₆H₅–: phenylethanone
(C₆H₅COCH₃), phenyl ethanoate (CH₃COOC₆H₅), methyl benzoate (C₆H₅COOCH₃), N-phenylethanamide
(CH₃CONHC₆H₅).

**Which group sets the suffix?** When two groups are on the ring, the higher-priority one becomes the parent:
carboxylic acid > acyl chloride > amide > ketone > alcohol or phenol > amine. So HOC₆H₄COOH with the groups
opposite each other is **4-hydroxybenzoic acid**, not "4-carboxyphenol".

## Worked example 1: naming from a structure

*Name the compound: a benzene ring carrying –COOH, with –NO₂ on the next-but-one carbon and –Cl on the carbon
after that.*

**Step 1: parent.** –COOH has the highest priority: **benzoic acid**; that ring carbon is C1.

**Step 2: number.** Counting one way, –NO₂ is on C3 and –Cl on C4: locants {3, 4}. Counting the other way
they would be on C5 and C4: {4, 5}. The lowest set is {3, 4}.

**Step 3: prefixes alphabetically.** chloro before nitro.

**Answer: 4-chloro-3-nitrobenzoic acid.**

## Worked example 2: from a name to every formula

*Give the structural, skeletal and molecular formulas of N-methylpropanamide.*

- "propanamide": three-carbon acyl chain ending in –CONH–, carbonyl carbon is C1.
- "N-methyl": one CH₃ on the nitrogen, replacing one H.
- **Structural:** CH₃CH₂CONHCH₃.
- **Skeletal:** a zigzag of two lines for CH₃CH₂, then a carbon with a double-bonded O, then a line to NH,
  then a line ending in the methyl carbon.
- **Molecular:** C₄H₉NO.

It is a **secondary amide**: one alkyl group on the N. Its isomer butanamide, CH₃CH₂CH₂CONH₂, is a primary
amide with the same molecular formula.

## A naming trap with methylbenzene derivatives

The syllabus names the ring-chlorinated products of methylbenzene **2-chloromethylbenzene** and
**4-chloromethylbenzene**: chlorine on the ring at position 2 or 4 relative to the methyl group. Do not confuse
these with **(chloromethyl)benzene**, C₆H₅CH₂Cl, where the chlorine is on the side-chain. The brackets matter:
they show that "chloromethyl" is a single substituent. If unsure, draw the structure next to the name.

## Common mistakes

- **Wrong suffix for acyl chlorides**, such as "ethyl chloride" or "ethanoic chloride". It is ethanoyl chloride.
- **Counting the nitrile carbon wrongly.** CH₃CH₂CN is propanenitrile: the C of –CN is part of the chain.
- **Numbering the ring from the wrong carbon.** The parent group's carbon is always C1.
- **Forgetting alphabetical order**: 4-chloro-3-nitro, not 3-nitro-4-chloro.
- **Drawing a displayed formula of benzene** with every C–H; not expected, and easy to get wrong.
- **Missing the N locant** for amides with groups on nitrogen.
- **Miscounting hydrogens on a ring corner** that carries a substituent: it has no H.

## Exam technique for Cambridge command words

- **Name** or **give the systematic name**: one name only; extra alternatives can cost the mark.
- **Draw the structural / displayed / skeletal formula**: give exactly the type asked for. A skeletal formula
  must show functional groups in full, with the O–H and N–H hydrogens.
- **Deduce the molecular formula**: count atoms methodically, ring corner by ring corner.
- **Identify** the functional groups: name them precisely ("acyl chloride", not "chloride").
