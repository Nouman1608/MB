These questions cover section **3.3.2.4 Chlorination of alkanes** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2. Learn the ideas first in the [Chlorination of alkanes study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-chlorination-of-alkanes/) and the [Chlorination of alkanes revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-chlorination-of-alkanes-revision-notes/). Use Mᵣ values CH₄ = 16.0, Cl₂ = 71.0, CH₃Cl = 50.5, CCl₄ = 154.0.

## Questions

**1.** State the conditions needed for methane to react with chlorine and write the overall equation for the formation of chloromethane. **[2]**

**2.** Name the type of mechanism for this reaction and explain what is meant by the term substitution. **[2]**

**3.** Write the equation for the initiation step and state the type of bond fission. **[2]**

**4.** Write equations for the two propagation steps and explain why they are called a chain reaction. **[4]**

**5.** Write equations for two different termination steps, one of which forms a hydrocarbon. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why a small amount of ethane is found in the products. **[1]**

**7.** Explain why dichloromethane, trichloromethane and tetrachloromethane are also formed. Write an equation for the formation of dichloromethane from chloromethane. **[3]**

**8.** Suggest how the proportion of chloromethane in the products can be increased. **[2]**

**9.** Write the propagation steps for the reaction of ethane with chlorine to form chloroethane. **[2]**

**10.** Propane reacts with chlorine in UV light. Explain why two monochloro products form and name them. **[3]**

**11.** Calculate the atom economy for the formation of chloromethane in the reaction CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl. **[2]**

**12.** 6.40 g of methane reacts with excess chlorine under UV light to form chloromethane only. The mass of chloromethane obtained is 15.0 g. Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**

**13.** Calculate the mass of tetrachloromethane formed when 0.400 mol of methane reacts completely with excess chlorine, and the number of moles of chlorine used. **[3]**

**14.** A student says "chlorine reacts with methane because methane is a polar molecule". Explain why this is incorrect and give the correct reason. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Ultraviolet light [1]; CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Free-radical substitution [1]; a substitution reaction is one in which an atom or group is replaced by another atom or group (here H is replaced by Cl) [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Cl₂ → 2Cl• [1]; homolytic fission [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Cl• + CH₄ → •CH₃ + HCl [1]; •CH₃ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + Cl• [1]; the chlorine radical is regenerated in the second step so it can start the cycle again [1]; so one initiation step can lead to many cycles of reaction [1]. **[4]**

**5.** Any two from: Cl• + Cl• → Cl₂; •CH₃ + Cl• → CH₃Cl; •CH₃ + •CH₃ → C₂H₆ [1 each, with one forming a hydrocarbon, which is C₂H₆]. **[2]**

**6.** Two methyl radicals combine in a termination step: •CH₃ + •CH₃ → C₂H₆ [1]. **[1]**

**7.** Chloromethane still contains C–H bonds, so chlorine radicals can attack it and replace further hydrogen atoms [1]; this can continue until all four hydrogens are replaced [1]; CH₃Cl + Cl₂ → CH₂Cl₂ + HCl [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Use an excess of methane [1]; so that chlorine radicals are more likely to collide with methane than with chloromethane [1]. **[2]**

**9.** Cl• + C₂H₆ → •C₂H₅ + HCl [1]; •C₂H₅ + Cl₂ → C₂H₅Cl + Cl• [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Propane has two different types of hydrogen atom (on the end carbons and on the middle carbon) [1]; substituting an end hydrogen gives 1-chloropropane [1]; substituting a hydrogen on the middle carbon gives 2-chloropropane [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Atom economy = Mᵣ of desired product ÷ sum of Mᵣ of all reactants (equal to the sum of the products) × 100, so 50.5 ÷ (16.0 + 71.0) [1]; = 50.5 ÷ 87.0 × 100 = **58.0%** [1]. **[2]**

**12.** n(CH₄) = 6.40 ÷ 16.0 = 0.400 mol [1]; theoretical mass = 0.400 × 50.5 = 20.2 g [1]; percentage yield = 15.0 ÷ 20.2 × 100 = **74.3%** [1]. **[3]**

**13.** CH₄ + 4Cl₂ → CCl₄ + 4HCl [1]; mass of CCl₄ = 0.400 × 154.0 = **61.6 g** [1]; n(Cl₂) = 4 × 0.400 = **1.60 mol** [1]. **[3]**

**14.** Methane is non-polar because carbon and hydrogen have similar electronegativities [1]; it reacts because UV light breaks the Cl–Cl bond to give reactive chlorine radicals that attack the C–H bond [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* A propagation step has one radical on each side. A termination step has none on the product side. Count the dots.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving the dot off radicals.
- Putting two radicals in a propagation step.
- Omitting UV light.
- Forgetting further substitution.
- Writing curly arrows or ions in a radical mechanism.
