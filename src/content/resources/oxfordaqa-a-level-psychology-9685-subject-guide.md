OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Psychology (9685) introduces the
scientific study of mind and behaviour, from how memory works to the
causes and treatment of schizophrenia and the psychology of the
workplace. It suits students who enjoy both evidence and argument,
since the course asks you to evaluate research as much as describe it.
It is a modular qualification of four written papers, each lasting 90
minutes and each marked out of 90. The two AS papers are each worth 50%
of the AS and 20% of the A-level; the two A-level papers are each worth
30% of the A-level.

## How the course is organised

The specification is arranged in four units, one for each paper.

**AS stage**

- **Unit 1, Introductory Topics in Psychology**: memory, social
  psychology and psychopathology. These three topics introduce core
  areas of the discipline and the kinds of studies psychologists use to
  investigate them.
- **Unit 2, Biopsychology, Development and Research Methods 1**:
  biopsychology, cognitive development and the first research methods
  topic. Here the course turns to the biological basis of behaviour, to
  how thinking develops, and to the foundations of how psychological
  research is designed and analysed.

**A-level stage**

- **Unit 3, Advanced Topics and Research Methods 2**: the psychology of
  sleep, schizophrenia, and the second research methods topic, which
  extends the methods work begun at AS.
- **Unit 4, Approaches and Application (Synoptic)**: scientific
  approaches in psychology, issues and debates in psychology, and
  applied psychology: work and the individual. This unit is synoptic,
  meaning it draws together ideas from across the whole course.

## How it's assessed

| Stage | Paper | Content | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AS | AS Paper 1 | Introductory Topics in Psychology (Memory, Social Psychology, Psychopathology) | 90 minutes | 90 | 50% of AS; 20% of A-level |
| AS | AS Paper 2 | Biopsychology, Development and Research Methods 1 | 90 minutes | 90 | 50% of AS; 20% of A-level |
| A-level | A-level Paper 1 | Advanced Topics and Research Methods 2 (Psychology of Sleep, Schizophrenia) | 90 minutes | 90 | 30% of A-level |
| A-level | A-level Paper 2 | Approaches and Application | 90 minutes | 90 | 30% of A-level |

All four papers are externally assessed written exams, and the course is
not tiered. Two points about the structure matter for planning:

- **The weighting is not equal.** Every paper has the same length and
  mark total, but the A-level papers carry more of the final A-level
  grade than the AS papers do.
- **Units can be resat.** Because the qualification is modular, each
  unit can be resat any number of times, and the best result counts.
  That makes it possible to improve an AS result while studying the
  A-level units, though each resit is extra workload to plan for.

The specification was first taught in September 2018. The first AS
exams were in May/June 2019 and the first A-level exams in May/June 2020.

## How the parts fit together

Psychology at this level is less a list of topics than a set of
questions approached from several directions. Several threads link the
units:

- **Research methods run through the whole course.** Research Methods 1
  in Unit 2 and Research Methods 2 in Unit 3 are taught as named topics,
  but every other topic depends on them, because evaluating a study
  means judging its design, sample, data and conclusions.
- **Biological explanations recur.** Biopsychology in Unit 2 supplies
  the background for the psychology of sleep and for biological
  explanations of schizophrenia in Unit 3.
- **Psychopathology leads to schizophrenia.** The AS work on defining
  and explaining abnormality is extended into the detailed study of one
  disorder at A-level.
- **Unit 4 pulls everything together.** Scientific approaches and issues
  and debates ask you to compare explanations from across the course, so
  examples from memory, social psychology, development, sleep and
  schizophrenia all become evidence in the synoptic paper.

The common skills are describing theories and studies accurately,
applying them to unfamiliar scenarios, analysing data, and evaluating
evidence in a balanced, well-supported way.

## Planning your study

The unit order is also a sensible teaching order. One way to plan the
two years:

- **First year:** Unit 1 and Unit 2. Treat Research Methods 1 as a
  thread you return to every week, rather than a block to finish once;
  each time you learn a study in memory or social psychology, identify
  its method and its strengths and weaknesses.
- **End of the first year:** sit the AS papers if your school enters
  you, or consolidate the AS content if it does not.
- **Second year:** Unit 3 first, with Research Methods 2 built on top of
  your AS methods knowledge, then Unit 4. Keep a synoptic notebook in
  which you record how each earlier topic illustrates the approaches and
  the issues and debates.
- **Final months:** revise Units 3 and 4 together, and decide with your
  teacher whether resitting an AS unit is worthwhile.

Start exam-style questions on each unit as soon as it is taught, and use
the course's exam preparation page for how to approach each paper and
how to use the resit option strategically.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers does OxfordAQA A-Level Psychology 9685 have?**
Four: two AS papers and two A-level papers. Each lasts 90 minutes and is marked out of 90.

**Are all the papers worth the same?**
No. Each AS paper is worth 50% of the AS and 20% of the A-level, while each A-level paper is worth 30% of the A-level.

**Can I resit units in OxfordAQA A-Level Psychology?**
Yes. The qualification is modular, and each unit can be resat any number of times, with the best result counting.

**What does synoptic mean for Unit 4?**
Unit 4, Approaches and Application, draws on the whole course. Scientific approaches, issues and debates, and applied psychology all ask you to use material from earlier units.

**Where does research methods appear?**
Research Methods 1 is part of Unit 2 at AS and Research Methods 2 is part of Unit 3 at A-level, but method and evaluation skills are needed in every topic.
