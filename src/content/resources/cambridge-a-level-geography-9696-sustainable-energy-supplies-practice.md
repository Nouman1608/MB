## Section A

**1.** Distinguish between the installed capacity of a power station and the electricity it generates. **[2]**

**2.** Explain why fuelwood can be a renewable resource but still be used unsustainably. **[3]**

**3.** The table shows data for three power plants in Nerith, a fictional country. One year has 8 760 hours.

| Plant | Installed capacity (MW) | Generation in one year (GWh) |
|---|---|---|
| Wind farm | 3 000 | 7 884 |
| Solar farm | 1 500 | 2 628 |
| Gas power station | 1 000 | 4 380 |

**(a)** Calculate the capacity factor of the wind farm. Show your working. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the wind and solar farms have lower capacity factors than the gas power station. **[3]**

**(c)** Suggest one consequence of this for planners of Nerith's electricity supply. **[2]**

**4.** Energy use per person is 160 GJ a year in Aldmark (a fictional HIC) and 12 GJ in Cotala (a fictional LIC). In Cotala, 70% of energy comes from traditional biomass.

**(a)** Calculate how many times greater energy use per person is in Aldmark than in Cotala. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain **two** reasons for the difference in energy use per person. **[4]**

**5.** Explain how concern about energy security can change the balance of energy sources in a country. **[4]**

**6.** Outline one local and one global environmental impact of energy use in large cities. **[4]**

## Section B

**7.** The table shows the percentage share of each source in electricity generation in three fictional countries.

| Source | Aldmark (HIC) 2010 | Aldmark 2025 | Benzaro (MIC) 2010 | Benzaro 2025 | Cotala (LIC) 2010 | Cotala 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Coal | 40 | 10 | 70 | 60 | 0 | 0 |
| Oil (diesel) | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 30 | 15 |
| Gas | 25 | 35 | 10 | 10 | 0 | 0 |
| Nuclear | 20 | 15 | 2 | 5 | 0 | 0 |
| HEP | 5 | 5 | 15 | 12 | 65 | 75 |
| Wind | 5 | 25 | 1 | 8 | 0 | 3 |
| Other renewables | 5 | 10 | 2 | 5 | 5 | 7 |

**(a)** Calculate the change in coal's share, in percentage points, in Aldmark and in Benzaro. **[2]**

**(b)** Compare the changes in the electricity mix of the three countries. **[4]**

**(c)** Suggest reasons for the differences you have described. **[4]**

**8.** Explain how climate can affect both the demand for and the supply of energy in a country. **[6]**

**9.** Explain the environmental impacts of hydroelectric power at local and global scales. **[6]**

**10.** "Level of development is the most important factor influencing a country's energy mix." With reference to examples, how far do you agree? **[20]**

## Answers

**1.** Installed capacity is the maximum power a station can produce at any moment, measured in MW [1]; generation is the amount of electricity actually produced over a period, measured in MWh, GWh or TWh [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Fuelwood regrows, so it is renewable [1]. If trees are cut faster than they regrow, for example around growing towns, local supplies are exhausted [1], so the resource is depleted and soils erode, making use unsustainable [1]. **[3]**

**3. (a)** Maximum output = 3 000 × 8 760 = 26 280 000 MWh, or 26 280 GWh [1]; 7 884 ÷ 26 280 × 100 = 30% [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Wind turbines only generate when wind speeds are within their working range [1]. Solar panels produce nothing at night and less on cloudy days or in winter [1]. A gas station can run whenever fuel is supplied, so its output is limited only by demand and maintenance [1]. **[3]**

**(c)** Planners need more installed renewable capacity than demand alone suggests [1], plus storage or flexible back-up such as gas or HEP for windless, dark periods [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** 160 ÷ 12 = 13.3 times [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** Aldmark has more industry, cars and household appliances, all of which use commercial energy [1], so demand per person is much higher [1]. In Cotala many households cannot afford, or have no grid access to, modern energy [1], so they rely on small quantities of fuelwood and charcoal for cooking [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* also credit climate (heating demand in a cold HIC), linked to higher demand.

**5.** A country that imports most of its fuel from one supplier is vulnerable to price rises or cut-offs [1]. To reduce the risk, it may develop domestic sources such as coal, renewables or nuclear [1]. It may diversify imports, for example building terminals to import liquefied gas from several suppliers [1]. It may also reduce demand through efficiency, so the share of imported fuel falls [1]. **[4]**

**6.** Local: vehicles and power stations release particulates and nitrogen dioxide [1], causing smog and respiratory illness in the city [1]. Global: burning fossil fuels for transport, electricity and heating releases carbon dioxide [1], adding to the enhanced greenhouse effect and climate change [1]. **[4]**

**7. (a)** Aldmark: 10 − 40 = −30 percentage points [1]; Benzaro: 60 − 70 = −10 percentage points [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** All three countries increased the share of renewables, but at different rates [1]. Aldmark changed most: coal fell from 40% to 10% while wind rose from 5% to 25% [1]. Benzaro remained dominated by coal (60%), with smaller rises in wind and nuclear [1]. Cotala depends mainly on HEP, which rose from 65% to 75%, while diesel fell from 30% to 15% [1]. **[4]**

**(c)** Aldmark can afford new wind farms and grid upgrades, and may have policy targets to cut emissions [1]. Benzaro may have large coal reserves, so coal gives security and low cost for a fast-growing economy [1]. Cotala probably has large rivers and HEP potential [1], while diesel is imported and expensive, so replacing it saves foreign currency [1]. **[4]**

**8.** Demand: cold winters raise demand for heating [1], and hot, humid summers raise demand for air conditioning [1]. Seasonal differences change when demand peaks, affecting the type of supply needed [1]. Supply: high, reliable rainfall and steep relief give HEP potential [1]. Strong, steady winds suit wind power, and high sunshine suits solar [1]. Drought can cut HEP output, so climate also affects reliability of supply [1]. **[6]**

**9.** Local: a reservoir floods valleys, farmland and settlements, displacing people [1] and destroying habitats [1]. The dam traps sediment, so downstream channels and deltas are starved and may erode [1]. Fish migration is blocked and water temperature and flow regimes change [1]. Global: HEP produces very little carbon dioxide in operation, so it reduces greenhouse gas emissions compared with fossil fuels [1]. In the tropics, decaying vegetation in reservoirs can release methane, reducing this benefit [1]. **[6]**

**10.** The real exam marks extended answers with levels of response, not point by point. The points below are indicative creditworthy content.

*Introduction:* define energy mix [1] and level of development [1].

*Agree:* HICs can afford the capital for nuclear, offshore wind and grid storage [1]. LICs rely on traditional biomass because many households lack grid access [1], as the energy ladder shows [1]. Industrialising MICs need large, cheap supplies and often turn to coal [1]. Development brings technology and skills that widen the choice of sources [1].

*Disagree:* resource endowment often matters more: river-rich countries use HEP at every level of development [1]. Coal-rich countries rely on coal, whether HIC or MIC [1]. Climate sets the potential for solar, wind and HEP [1]. Energy security drives change, for example diversifying away from a single gas supplier [1]. Policy can transform the mix quickly, as in phase-outs of nuclear or coal [1]. Pollution and climate commitments now push countries of all income levels away from coal [1].

*Analysis:* development often works through other factors, such as income affecting technology and policy choices [1]. Endowment sets the starting point; development decides how far a country can move from it [1]. The importance of factors changes over time as costs of renewables fall [1].

*Use of examples:* a named country where endowment dominates [1]; a named country where policy or security drove change [1].

*Judgement:* a reasoned conclusion on "how far", such as development mattering most for total demand and household fuels, but endowment and policy mattering more for the electricity mix [1]. A clear final view [1]. **[20]**

*Examiner insight:* top answers separate total energy demand from the mix of sources, and judge each.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using MW where MWh is needed, or forgetting to convert GWh and MWh.
- Describing shares without saying that totals may differ.
- Comparing countries one after another without direct comparison.
- Impacts not labelled local or global.
- Essays that list factors without weighing them.
