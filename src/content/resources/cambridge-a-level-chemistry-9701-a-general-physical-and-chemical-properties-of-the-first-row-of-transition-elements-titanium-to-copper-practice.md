## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define the term *transition element*. **[2]**

**2.** Which ion has the electron configuration [Ar]3d⁵?

A: Cr²⁺

B: Mn³⁺

C: Fe³⁺

D: Co²⁺

**[1]**

**3.** Which element of the d block, between scandium and zinc inclusive, has an atom with the
configuration [Ar]3d⁵4s¹?

A: vanadium

B: chromium

C: manganese

D: copper

**[1]**

**4.** Describe the shape and orientation of a 3d<sub>z²</sub> orbital. **[2]**

**5.** Describe the shape and orientation of a 3d<sub>xy</sub> orbital. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** Copper forms Cu⁺ and Cu²⁺ ions. Zinc forms only Zn²⁺.

**(a)** Write the electron configurations of Cu⁺, Cu²⁺ and Zn²⁺. **[2]**

**(b)** Use your answers to explain why copper is a transition element but zinc is not. **[2]**

**7.** Titanium forms TiCl₄, Ti₂O₃ and TiO.

**(a)** Give the oxidation state of titanium in each compound. **[1]**

**(b)** Give the electron configuration of titanium in its +3 oxidation state. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why titanium can form compounds in several oxidation states. **[2]**

**8.** Deduce the oxidation state of the transition element in each species.

**(a)** VO²⁺ **[1]**

**(b)** Cr₂O₇²⁻ **[1]**

**(c)** [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻ **[1]**

**9.** Calcium is an s-block metal that forms only Ca²⁺. Explain, in terms of ionisation
energies, why manganese can reach the +7 oxidation state, as in MnO₄⁻, but calcium never exceeds +2. **[3]**

**10.** Iron(II) or iron(III) ions catalyse the reaction between iodide ions and
peroxodisulfate ions.

**(a)** State which property of transition elements allows iron ions to act as catalysts in
this reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Write equations to show how Fe²⁺ is used and then reformed. **[2]**

**11.** Solid iron catalyses the formation of ammonia from nitrogen and hydrogen. Explain how
the electronic structure of iron allows it to bond to reactant molecules during catalysis.
**[2]**

**12.** Explain, in terms of electronic structure, why transition metal ions such as Co²⁺ readily
form complex ions with water and ammonia molecules. **[2]**

**13.** Give the full electron configurations, in terms of [Ar], of V³⁺, Ni²⁺ and Ti⁴⁺, and state
which of these ions has an incomplete d sub-shell. **[3]**

**14.** Manganese compounds include MnSO₄, MnO₂ and KMnO₄. Calcium compounds include CaSO₄, CaO and
CaCl₂. Using **three** characteristic properties of transition elements, describe how the
chemistry of manganese differs from that of calcium. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** A d-block element [1] which forms one or more stable ions with incomplete d orbitals [1].

**2.** **C** [1]. Fe is [Ar]3d⁶4s²; losing 4s² and one 3d electron gives 3d⁵. (Cr²⁺ is 3d⁴,
Mn³⁺ is 3d⁴, Co²⁺ is 3d⁷.)

**3.** **B** [1].

**4.** Two lobes along the z axis, one each side of the nucleus [1], with a ring (doughnut) around
the nucleus in the xy plane [1].

**5.** Four lobes lying in the xy plane [1], pointing between the x and y axes [1].

**6. (a)** Cu⁺: [Ar]3d¹⁰; Cu²⁺: [Ar]3d⁹ [1]; Zn²⁺: [Ar]3d¹⁰ [1].

**(b)** Cu²⁺ has an incomplete d sub-shell (3d⁹), so copper forms at least one stable ion with
incomplete d orbitals [1]. Zinc's only ion has a full 3d sub-shell, so zinc does not meet the
definition [1].

**7. (a)** TiCl₄: +4; Ti₂O₃: +3; TiO: +2 [1].

**(b)** [Ar]3d¹ [1].

**(c)** The 3d and 4s sub-shells are similar in energy [1], so successive ionisation energies rise
gradually and different numbers of electrons can be lost for similar energy, giving several stable
oxidation states [1].

**8. (a)** V + (−2) = +2, so V is +4 [1].

**(b)** 2Cr + 7(−2) = −2, so Cr is +6 [1].

**(c)** Fe + 6(−1) = −3, so Fe is +3 [1].

**9.** Manganese's 4s and 3d electrons are similar in energy, so all seven can be removed or used in
bonding with a gradual rise in ionisation energy [1]. For calcium, the third electron must come
from an inner shell (3p), much closer to the nucleus and less shielded [1], so the third
ionisation energy is very much larger and cannot be recovered from bonding or lattice formation
[1].

**10. (a)** Iron has more than one stable oxidation state (+2 and +3), so it can gain and lose
electrons [1].

**(b)** S₂O₈²⁻ + 2Fe²⁺ → 2SO₄²⁻ + 2Fe³⁺ [1]; 2Fe³⁺ + 2I⁻ → 2Fe²⁺ + I₂ [1].

**11.** Iron has vacant d orbitals that are energetically accessible [1], which can accept electron
pairs and form dative bonds with the reactant molecules, adsorbing them onto the surface and
weakening their bonds [1].

**12.** Co²⁺ has vacant d orbitals of accessible energy [1] that accept lone pairs from H₂O or NH₃,
forming dative covalent bonds [1].

**13.** V³⁺: [Ar]3d²; Ni²⁺: [Ar]3d⁸; Ti⁴⁺: [Ar] (3d⁰) [2 for all three, 1 for two]. V³⁺ and
Ni²⁺ have incomplete d sub-shells; Ti⁴⁺ has none [1].

**14.** Any three of: manganese shows variable oxidation states (+2 in MnSO₄, +4 in MnO₂, +7 in
KMnO₄) whereas calcium is always +2; manganese compounds are often coloured (KMnO₄ is purple)
whereas calcium compounds are white; manganese ions form complex ions with ligands such as
water; manganese compounds act as catalysts, for example Mn²⁺ in the manganate(VII)–ethanedioate
reaction. One mark for each of any three points [3].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing the definition in terms of atoms instead of ions, or leaving out "stable".
- Removing 3d electrons before 4s when writing ion configurations.
- Forgetting that the chromium and copper atoms have one 4s electron.
- Drawing or describing 3d<sub>xy</sub> lobes along the axes.
- Explaining variable oxidation states without referring to the similar energies of 3d and 4s.
- Giving only one of the two reasons for catalytic behaviour.
