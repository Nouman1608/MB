These questions cover subtopic 5.1 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417: the positive and negative effects of microprocessor-controlled and smart devices in the home and in transport. All scenarios are invented.

## Section A: short answers

**1.** Which **one** of the following is an actuator? **[1]**

A: Temperature sensor

B: Motor

C: Microprocessor

D: Touch screen

**2.** State what is meant by a *smart device*. **[1]**

**3.** Name **two** labour-saving devices in the home that are controlled by microprocessors. **[2]**

**4.** Describe the difference between a device that monitors and a device that controls. **[2]**

**5.** Which **one** of the following statements about autonomous vehicles is correct? **[1]**

A: They do not need sensors because they follow a map

B: They cannot be affected by bad weather

C: They use sensors, cameras and GPS so a computer can steer and brake

D: They remove all risk of accidents

---

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** An elderly man lives alone. His daughter installs a smart speaker with video calling and a smart oven that he can switch on by voice.

**(a)** Describe **one** positive effect of the smart speaker on his degree of social interaction. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe **one** negative effect of the devices on his physical fitness. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain **one** way the devices could affect the security of his data. **[2]**

**7.** A city installs smart traffic lights at a busy junction. Sensors in the road detect queues of vehicles.

**(a)** Describe how the system controls the lights. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain **one** positive effect of the system on transport safety. **[2]**

**8.** A new car records its location and driving data and sends it to the manufacturer's computer.

**(a)** Give **two** benefits of this data to the car's owner. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain **two** risks to the security of the owner's data. **[4]**

**9.** Compare a stand-alone microwave oven with a smart oven controlled by a phone app, referring to lifestyle and security of data. **[4]**

**10.** A family's smart heating app still uses the password set by the manufacturer.

**(a)** Explain why this is a risk. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe **two** other measures the family could take to protect their smart devices. **[2]**

## Section C: extended questions

**11.** Discuss the effects of microprocessor-controlled devices in the home on people's lifestyle and leisure time. **[6]**

**12.** A taxi company plans to replace its drivers with autonomous vehicles. Evaluate this plan, considering the company, its passengers and transport safety. **[8]**

---

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. A motor is an output device that the microprocessor switches on to cause movement; the sensor is an input. **[1]**

**2.** A microprocessor-controlled device connected to a network, so that it can be monitored or controlled remotely, for example by an app [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Washing machine [1]; robot vacuum cleaner [1] (also credit: dishwasher, microwave, bread maker). **[2]**

**4.** A monitoring device collects and reports data but does not act on it [1]; a controlling device uses the data to change an output automatically, such as switching on a heater [1]. **[2]**

**5.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** He can see and talk to his family and friends by video call [1], so he feels less isolated even though he lives alone [1].

**(b)** He can switch on the oven by voice instead of walking to the kitchen [1], so he moves less during the day and may lose strength or mobility [1].

**(c)** The devices are connected to the internet and linked to accounts [1], so a hacker could listen through the speaker or learn his routine, such as when he is out or asleep [1]. **[6]**

**7. (a)** Sensors detect the number or length of the queue [1]; the data is sent to the microprocessor, which compares it with preset values [1]; it changes the timing of the lights, giving longer green phases to the busier road [1].

**(b)** Queues are shorter, so drivers are less likely to become frustrated and take risks such as running a red light [1], which reduces the chance of collisions at the junction [1]. **[5]**

**8. (a)** The car can be located if it is stolen [1]; a garage can use the fault data to diagnose problems quickly [1] (also credit: emergency services can be alerted with the location after a crash). **[2]**

**(b)** The data could be intercepted while it is being transmitted [1], so someone could track where the owner goes and when their home is empty [1]. The manufacturer's server could be hacked [1], exposing the personal details and journey records of many owners at once [1]. **[6]**

**9.** Lifestyle: both save time, but only the smart oven can be switched on remotely, so a meal can be cooking before the owner gets home [1]; the stand-alone microwave must be operated in person [1]. Data: the stand-alone microwave is not connected to a network, so it has no data risk [1]; the smart oven and its app could be accessed by a hacker, who might learn the household's routine or interfere with the oven [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** Default passwords are the same for many devices or are published online [1], so a hacker could easily log in and control the heating or learn when the home is empty [1].

**(b)** Keep the device software updated so security weaknesses are fixed [1]; secure the home wireless network with a strong password and encryption [1] (also credit: turn on two-factor authentication on the account). **[4]**

**11.** Lifestyle positive: chores such as washing clothes are done automatically, so less effort is needed [1]; devices such as heating can be controlled remotely, so the home is warm on arrival [1]. Lifestyle negative: people become dependent on the devices and may struggle if they break [1], and may rely more on convenience food cooked by microwave [1]. Leisure positive: time saved on chores can be spent on hobbies or with family [1]. Leisure negative: much of the saved time may be spent watching streamed media rather than on active pursuits [1]. **[6]**

**12.** Company: no driver wages, so running costs fall over time [1]; vehicles can operate at all hours without breaks [1]; but the vehicles are very expensive to buy and maintain [1]. Passengers: journeys may be cheaper [1], but some passengers may not trust a vehicle with no driver or may lose help with luggage [1]. Safety: no crashes caused by tiredness, distraction or alcohol [1]; but sensors may fail in heavy rain or snow, and the vehicles could be hacked [1]. Judgement: the plan is likely to save money in the long term, but the company should introduce the vehicles gradually and keep some drivers until the technology is proven in local conditions [1]. **[8]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using a home dimension in a transport question.
- Writing "it could be hacked" without saying what the hacker could see or do.
- Giving only one side in a discuss or evaluate question.
- Leaving out a conclusion when the command word is evaluate.
