## Questions

**1.** Draw the general structure of an amino acid. Label the amine group, the carboxyl group and the R group. **[3]**

**2.** Two amino acids join to form a dipeptide.

**(a)** Describe how the peptide bond forms. **[3]**
**(b)** Name the reaction that breaks a peptide bond, and state what is needed for it to happen in a cell. **[2]**

**3.** (calculator allowed) The Mr values of three amino acids are glycine 75, alanine 89 and serine 105. The Mr of water is 18.

**(a)** Calculate the Mr of the tripeptide Gly–Ala–Ser. **[2]**
**(b)** State how many water molecules are needed to hydrolyse completely a single polypeptide of 250 amino acids. **[1]**

**4.** Name the level of protein structure (primary, secondary, tertiary or quaternary) described by each statement.

**(a)** Two lengths of a polypeptide lie side by side, hydrogen bonded backbone to backbone. **[1]**
**(b)** The order Met–Lys–Thr–Ala at one end of a chain. **[1]**
**(c)** A disulfide bond between cysteine 12 and cysteine 95 of the same chain. **[1]**
**(d)** Three polypeptide chains held together to make one functional protein. **[1]**

**5.** Parts of a folded polypeptide bring these R groups close together.

**(a)** Name the interaction most likely to form between: (i) two phenylalanine R groups (non-polar); (ii) the –OH groups of serine and threonine; (iii) an arginine R group (positively charged) and a glutamate R group (negatively charged); (iv) two cysteine R groups. **[4]**
**(b)** The protein is placed in a solution of much lower pH. Suggest which of the interactions in (a) is most likely to break, and explain why. **[2]**

**6.**

**(a)** Explain, in terms of R groups, why most globular proteins are soluble in water. **[2]**
**(b)** Classify haemoglobin, collagen, amylase and keratin as globular or fibrous, and state the type of role each class generally has. **[2]**

**7.** Describe the structure of a molecule of haemoglobin. **[5]**

**8.** (calculator allowed) In adult haemoglobin each α-globin chain has 141 amino acids and each β-globin chain has 146.

**(a)** Calculate the number of peptide bonds in one haemoglobin molecule. **[2]**
**(b)** Taking the Mr of haemoglobin as 64 500 and the Ar of iron as 55.8, calculate the percentage by mass of iron in haemoglobin. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** A red blood cell holds 2.7 × 10⁸ haemoglobin molecules. Calculate the maximum number of oxygen molecules it can carry. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why a person whose diet contains too little iron may become breathless during exercise. **[2]**

**9.** Describe the structure of a collagen molecule and how collagen molecules are arranged to form collagen fibres. **[6]**

**10.** (calculator allowed)

**(a)** The triple-helical region of each chain in a collagen molecule has 1023 amino acids in a Gly–X–Y repeat. Calculate the number of glycine residues in this region of one collagen molecule. **[2]**
**(b)** A collagen molecule 300 nm long appears 24 mm long on an electron micrograph. Calculate the magnification. **[2]**
**(c)** A mutation replaces one of the glycines with an amino acid that has a large R group. Suggest and explain how this could affect the collagen. **[3]**

**11.** Compare the structures of haemoglobin and collagen, and explain how each structure suits the protein's function. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Central carbon bonded to H, –NH₂, –COOH and R, with all bonds shown, including the –OH of the carboxyl group [1]. Amine group (–NH₂) labelled [1]. Carboxyl group (–COOH) and R group both labelled [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* A carboxyl group drawn as –CO₂ or –COO without the hydrogen usually loses the structure mark, even if the labels are right.

**2. (a)** A **condensation** reaction [1]. The –OH from the carboxyl group of one amino acid and an –H from the amine group of the other are removed as **water** [1]. A covalent **–CO–NH–** bond forms between the carbon of one and the nitrogen of the other [1].
**(b)** **Hydrolysis** [1]; water is added, catalysed by a **protease** enzyme [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (a), "water is lost" without saying which groups it comes from gains only the first mark; the groups must be named.

**3. (a)** Two condensations remove 2 × 18 = 36: 75 + 89 + 105 − 36 [1] = **233** [1].
**(b)** **249** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Subtracting 18 once instead of twice gives 251 and loses the accuracy mark; the method mark still needs the subtraction to be shown.

**4. (a)** **Secondary** [1] **(b)** **Primary** [1] **(c)** **Tertiary** [1] **(d)** **Quaternary** [1]

*Examiner insight:* (c) catches many candidates: a disulfide bond within one chain is tertiary; it is quaternary only when it links two different chains.

**5. (a)** (i) **Hydrophobic interaction** [1]; (ii) **hydrogen bond** [1]; (iii) **ionic bond** [1]; (iv) **disulfide bond** [1].
**(b)** The **ionic bond** [1]. Extra H⁺ ions make the negatively charged –COO⁻ of glutamate gain H⁺ and become uncharged –COOH, so there is no longer an attraction between opposite charges [1].

*Examiner insight:* For (b), naming the ionic bond with no reason scores one mark only; the second mark needs the change in charge on an R group.

**6. (a)** Hydrophobic (non-polar) R groups are folded **inside** the molecule [1]; hydrophilic (polar or charged) R groups are on the **outside** and form interactions with water molecules [1].
**(b)** Globular: haemoglobin and amylase; fibrous: collagen and keratin [1]. Globular proteins generally have physiological roles and fibrous proteins structural roles [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (a), "globular proteins are polar" is not enough; the mark needs the positions of hydrophobic and hydrophilic R groups.

**7.**
- Globular protein with **four polypeptide chains**, so it has **quaternary structure** [1]
- **Two α-globin and two β-globin** chains [1]
- **One haem group per chain**, four in total, each held in a pocket of the folded chain [1]
- Each haem contains an **iron ion, Fe²⁺** [1]
- Chains held together by hydrophobic interactions, hydrogen bonds and ionic bonds; hydrophilic R groups on the outside [1]

*Examiner insight:* "Four haem groups" and "one haem group per chain" are the same point and score once; you need five different ideas for five marks.

**8. (a)** Total amino acids = 2 × 141 + 2 × 146 = 574; four separate chains, so 574 − 4 [1] = **570** [1].
**(b)** 4 × 55.8 ÷ 64 500 × 100 [1] = **0.35%** [1].
**(c)** 4 × 2.7 × 10⁸ = **1.08 × 10⁹** [1].
**(d)** Iron is needed to make haem, so **less haemoglobin** is made and less oxygen is carried in the blood [1]; muscles receive less oxygen for **aerobic respiration**, so breathing rate rises [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (a), subtracting 1 instead of 4 gives 573: treating the molecule as one chain loses the accuracy mark. In (b), using one iron atom instead of four is the most common slip.

**9.**
- **Three polypeptide chains**, each a helix, wound into a **triple helix** [1]
- **Hydrogen bonds** between the chains [1]
- **Glycine at almost every third position**, small enough to let the chains pack tightly [1]
- Molecules lie **side by side**, with **staggered ends** [1]
- **Covalent cross-links** between R groups (lysine) of neighbouring molecules [1]
- Molecules form **fibrils**, which bundle into **fibres** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Calling the chains "α-helices" is a contradiction that can cost a mark; describe them as helices wound into a triple helix.

**10. (a)** 1023 ÷ 3 = 341 glycines per chain [1]; × 3 chains = **1023** [1].
**(b)** Convert to the same units: 24 mm = 24 000 000 nm [1]; 24 000 000 ÷ 300 = **× 80 000** [1].
**(c)** The large R group **cannot fit in the centre** of the triple helix [1], so the three chains **cannot pack closely** and the triple helix is disrupted [1]; the collagen fibres have **lower tensile strength** and tissues such as tendon or bone are weaker [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (b), magnification has no units; writing "80 000 nm" loses the accuracy mark. In (c), each mark needs a link: shape, then packing, then strength.

**11.**
- Haemoglobin is **globular**, with **four chains** (2 α, 2 β) folded into a compact shape; collagen is **fibrous**, with **three chains** wound into a long **triple helix** [1]
- Haemoglobin has **haem with Fe²⁺**, which binds O₂ reversibly; collagen has **no prosthetic group** [1]
- Each haemoglobin carries up to four O₂, for **oxygen transport** [1]
- Hydrophilic R groups outside make haemoglobin **soluble**, so it is carried at high concentration in red blood cells [1]
- Binding of one O₂ changes the shape of haemoglobin, so others bind more easily [1]
- Collagen: **glycine every third** amino acid gives tight packing of the chains [1]
- Collagen molecules are **staggered** and **covalently cross-linked** into fibrils and fibres [1]
- So collagen has high **tensile strength** and is **insoluble**, suited to tendons, skin and bone [1]

*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs matching points ("four chains but three chains"); two separate descriptions with no link can lose comparison marks, and every structural feature should be paired with its function.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Drawing amino acids with missing hydrogens on the amine group or the –OH missing from the carboxyl group.
- Saying water comes from the R groups during condensation.
- Forgetting to subtract one water per peptide bond in Mr calculations, or subtracting one too few.
- Counting peptide bonds for a multi-chain protein as if it were one chain.
- Classing a disulfide bond inside one chain as quaternary structure.
- Naming "bonds" without saying which: hydrogen, ionic, disulfide or hydrophobic.
- Using one iron, or one O₂, per haemoglobin molecule.
- Giving magnification with units, or failing to convert mm to nm first.
- Describing collagen strength without cross-links or staggered ends.
