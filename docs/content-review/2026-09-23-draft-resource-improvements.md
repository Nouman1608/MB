## 1. `igcse-mathematics-algebra-and-graphs` (Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580)

Reviewer: Mathematics reviewer (Muhammad Ghazali Siddiqui).

### Suspected error: corrected (D-294)

**Resolved 2026-09-23:** the owner approved the correction. The same sentence was
in seven 0580 topic guides; all seven now read "grades A* to B".

Original note:

The opening paragraph says: "Extended candidates are eligible for grades A* to
E and Core candidates for grades C to G, so grades A* and B need the Extended
tier". The grade ranges imply that A*, A **and** B all need Extended; "A* and
B" reads as if A were excluded. Suggested wording: "so grades A* to B need the
Extended tier". Please confirm against the 0580 2025–2027 syllabus.

### Proposed "Key points" box (after the introduction)

- Algebraic manipulation (C2.2) underpins the whole topic: expanding,
  factorising and simplifying must be automatic before equations and graphs.
- Core: linear equations, simultaneous linear equations, simple
  rearrangement, sequences (linear, simple quadratic and cubic), and graphs of
  linear and quadratic functions and a/x.
- Extended adds quadratic equations, algebraic fractions, proportion,
  functions, differentiation and more graph types.
- For a sequence, write out the differences first: constant first difference
  means linear, constant second difference means quadratic.

### Proposed "Common mistakes" section (after "How to approach it")

1. **Expanding a negative bracket.** −2(x − 3) is −2x **+** 6; the sign of the
   second term is often left unchanged.
2. **Dividing an inequality by a negative number** without reversing the sign:
   −3x > 12 gives x **<** −4.
3. **nth term from the first term.** In 5, 8, 11, … the nth term is 3n + 2, not
   3n + 5: check by substituting n = 1.
4. **Losing a solution.** x² = 9 has two solutions, x = 3 and x = −3.
5. **Changing the subject in the wrong order.** Undo operations in the reverse
   of the order they were applied to the subject.

## 2. `atomic-structure` (Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620 / O Level 5070)

Reviewer: Chemistry reviewer (Muhammad Nouman Ahmed).

No suspected errors found on reading. Proposed additions:

### Proposed "Key points" box

- Proton number defines the element; mass number = protons + neutrons.
- In an atom, electrons = protons, so the atom has no overall charge.
- Shells fill 2, 8, 8 for proton numbers 1–20; outer-shell electrons give the
  group (I–VII) and the number of shells gives the period.
- Isotopes: same proton number, different numbers of neutrons, same chemical
  properties (the Supplement explanation: same electronic configuration).

### Proposed "Common mistakes" section

1. **Mixing up proton number and mass number** when reading a symbol such as
   ²³₁₁Na: the top number is the mass number (23), the bottom is the proton
   number (11), so neutrons = 12.
2. **Configurations of ions:** Na⁺ is 2,8 (one electron removed), Cl⁻ is 2,8,8
   (one added). Students often give the atom's configuration.
3. **"Isotopes have different chemical properties."** They have the same
   chemical properties; only mass-related physical properties differ.
4. **Relative mass of the electron:** it is not zero, it is very small
   (about 1/1840).
