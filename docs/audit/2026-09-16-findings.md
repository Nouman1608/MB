# Marlbridge audit - findings of 16 September 2026

Batch 28: 42 resources read in full, covering Urdu across every board that carries it and
the IB language courses - Cambridge IGCSE Urdu as a Second Language 0539 (6 files),
Cambridge O Level First and Second Language Urdu 3247/3248 (6), Pearson Edexcel A Level
Urdu 9UR0 (5), OxfordAQA International GCSE Urdu 9264 (5), IB Diploma Language A language
and literature (5), IB Diploma Language A literature (5), IB Diploma Language B (5) and IB
Middle Years Language Acquisition (5).

76 findings: 38 confirmed errors, 13 inconsistencies, 18 questionable claims, 6 unverified
items, 1 duplicate or overlapping scope. Every file was read end to end, every tariffed
question was re-summed by hand, and every board document named below was opened directly
rather than through search results. Three adversarial passes were then run against the
highest-value claims with instructions to refute them; they overturned or rescoped twelve,
and those corrections are carried inside the findings themselves rather than left in a
separate note.

Audit state after this batch: 1,122 of 1,251 baseline resources fully read, 129 remaining,
1,440 grouped findings, 728 currently open. All nine validation steps pass on the
regenerated artefacts.

## READ THIS FIRST

**Four of the audit's own proposed corrections were wrong and would have made a resource
worse. They are corrected in place; read these four before editing anything.**

**E768 - do not write the film names in as directors.** The specification gives each film
as a name and a year with no role label, and at least one of those names is the film's
producer rather than its director. Adding the role would introduce a factual error the
board does not make. The same finding: the essay collection is not prescribed whole - the
specification names five specific essays from it.

**E782 - do not replace 32 with 24 wherever it appears.** In this qualification 32 is a
real and correct figure: four criteria at eight achievement levels each, for criterion-
related classroom assessment. 24 is the examination blueprint's per-criterion raw mark.
The defect is assigning 32 to *each* criterion, which matches neither scale. A blanket
replacement will introduce an error into any passage about classroom assessment.

**Q284 - the composition types are four, not three.** A correction listing narrative,
descriptive and discursive drops *argumentative*, which 3247 names alongside them.

**E761 - the board does not say that answers in the wrong language score zero.** The
requirement to answer in Urdu is real and is stated in the specification. The zero-mark
consequence appears nowhere in the specification, its marking guidance or the board's
teaching guide, and has been removed from the finding.

**U44 is the entry to read before applying any correction in this batch.** It records nine
places where correcting one qualification by another's rule would introduce an error. The
sharpest: one board's A Level in this subject *does* have a listening paper, so the finding
that another board's international GCSE has none must not travel; and translation direction
reverses between the two boards' first papers.

**Three standing method notes.**

First, a list introduced with *includes*, *such as* or *e.g.* is not exhaustive. Two
findings here are scoped as "the syllabus names no summary *task*" rather than "summary is
off-syllabus" for exactly this reason.

Second, absence is established by reading the complete relevant section and the appendices,
never by a string search. E769 - the largest finding in this batch - rests on reading five
separate sections of the specification, and says which.

Third, presence must be established in the specific file a finding is attached to. Where a
finding below names two files, that is because the finding is about the two disagreeing.

## E749 - Two qualifications' first papers are presented as one shared paper, with the wrong duration for one of them [Confirmed error, High]

- **Files:** `o-level-cambridge-urdu-first-and-second-language`
- **Where:** Opening description
- **Locate by:** `Cambridge O-Level Urdu Paper 1, Reading and Writing, is a 1 hour 30`

**What is wrong.** The guide opens by describing a single first paper of one hour thirty minutes shared across the first-language and second-language codes, differing only in the second paper and in difficulty. Both halves are false. The first-language paper is one hour thirty minutes with two parts, reading worth twenty-five and writing worth twenty-five. The second-language paper is one hour forty-five minutes with five sequential exercises of eight, nine, nine, ten and fourteen marks; its own syllabus states expressly that the paper is no longer divided into parts and that the exercises are presented sequentially. Different durations, different structures, different task types. This is the first sentence of the resource, and its sibling revision notes already carry the correct split, so the guide contradicts a file that is right.

**Fix.** State the two papers separately with their own durations and structures; only the component title and the fifty-fifty weighting are genuinely shared.

## E750 - A resource states the board offers no A Level in the subject, which is false [Confirmed error, High]

- **Files:** `o-level-cambridge-urdu-first-and-second-language`
- **Where:** Progression note
- **Locate by:** `  Cambridge does not offer an A-Level Urdu qualification`

**What is wrong.** The guide tells students the board does not offer an A Level in this subject, and on that premise routes them to another board as the natural progression. Both of the syllabuses the resource itself cites say otherwise in their progression sections, naming the board's own AS and A Level in the subject as what candidates achieving the higher grades are prepared for. Three notes for correction, and they matter more than the finding. First, the obvious citation is unsafe: the AS-level code's final June series is June 2026 and it appears in the board's published list of withdrawn syllabuses, from 2027 being available in the November series in one territory only. Second, the A Level code usually paired with it could not be confirmed as current from any official document: it is absent from the February 2026 syllabus-changes document that names every other code in this subject, and its qualification page could not be retrieved, although a control check showed that page behaviour is not itself evidence of withdrawal. Third, the code that is demonstrably current is the one for centres in that country, which the changes document records as being replaced by a new language-and-literature code for first examination in 2027. For a reader in that country the resource's practical claim is defensible even though its literal claim is wrong, because the general A Level code is not enterable there. The correction must therefore name the right code and the territorial restriction rather than simply asserting that an A Level exists.

**Fix.** Delete the false claim and name the progression route that applies to the reader's own centre, with the replacement code and its first examination year.

**Evidence.** Cambridge Assessment International Education, Syllabus Changes (International), February 2026, version 1 - https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/663781-syllabus-changes-international-.pdf

## E752 - The four listening assessment objectives are replaced by the subject-content bullets, in the wrong order [Confirmed error, High]

- **Files:** `igcse-cambridge-urdu-paper-2-listening`
- **Where:** Listening assessment objectives
- **Locate by:** `understanding factual information and ideas, identifying and selecting relevant details, identifying ideas, opinions and attitudes and the connections between them`

**What is wrong.** The guide takes the four listening bullets from the syllabus's subject-content section and presents them, in that order, as the definitions of the four listening assessment objectives. They are different lists. The real objectives are, in order: identify and select relevant information; understand ideas, opinions and attitudes; show understanding of the connections between ideas, opinions and attitudes; and understand what is implied but not directly stated, given with the examples of gist and the speaker's purpose, intention and feelings. The guide's ordering inverts the first objective, collapses the second and third into one item, and then maps exercises onto these wrong definitions, so a student revising the third objective revises the wrong skill. Two notes for correction. The fourth objective's example clause must be quoted in full, because it is what distinguishes it from the reading equivalent, which refers to the writer rather than the speaker. And the syllabus is itself internally inconsistent on numbering, heading the speaking block as the third objective in one place and the fourth in another; a correction must not silently tidy that.

**Fix.** Quote the four listening objectives verbatim and keep the subject-content bullets separate and unlabelled.

**Evidence.** Cambridge IGCSE Urdu as a Second Language 0539 syllabus for 2025-2027, version 2, September 2024 - https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/664633-2025-2027-syllabus.pdf

## E757 - A practice paper tests a discrete grammar component the qualification does not have, and totals two-thirds of the paper [Confirmed error, High]

- **Files:** `urdu-reading-and-writing-practice`
- **Where:** Sections A and B
- **Locate by:** `## Section A — Grammar`

**What is wrong.** The file correctly states that the paper has six exercises worth sixty marks, then presents a paper in two sections containing nine questions worth forty. The qualification has no discrete grammar exercise: its six exercises are short answer, multiple matching, note-making, summary, functional writing and extended writing. Nothing in the file corresponds to the first three, and there is no multiple matching and no note-making anywhere in it. Parsing parts of speech, transforming to the passive and forming plurals as separately marked questions is the shape of the other code's second paper. Two notes for correction. Grammar is assessed in this qualification, but as a quality of writing under a named writing objective and a subject-content bullet, not as a discrete exercise; a correction must not delete grammar teaching, only stop presenting it as an exercise of this paper. And porting the items to the other code's second paper without adjusting the word counts would import a new error, because that exercise is approximately two hundred words for twenty marks.

**Fix.** Re-scope the file to the six real exercises with their tariffs, or retitle and retag it as grammar support.
