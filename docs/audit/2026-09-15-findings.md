Batch 27: 42 resources read in full, covering ICT and computer science across every board
and code that carries them - Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 (9 files), Cambridge AS/A Level IT
9626 (6), Cambridge IGCSE Computer Science 0478 (6), Cambridge O Level Computer Science
2210 (6), OxfordAQA International GCSE Computer Science 9210 (5 newly read of 6 in the

66 findings: 37 confirmed errors, 15 inconsistencies, 8 questionable claims, 5 unverified
items, 1 duplicate or overlapping scope. Every file was read end to end, every tariffed
question was re-summed by hand, and every board document named below was opened directly
rather than through search results. An adversarial pass was then run against the
highest-value claims with instructions to refute them; it overturned or weakened six, and
those corrections are carried inside the findings themselves rather than left in a
separate note.

Audit state after this batch: 1,080 of 1,251 baseline resources fully read, 171 remaining,
1,364 grouped findings, 690 currently open. All nine validation steps pass on the

## READ THIS FIRST

**Six of the audit's own claims were wrong and are corrected in place. Two of them would
have made a resource worse if acted on in their original form.**

E722 originally gave the file-size answers in decimal symbols. The figures 1.37, 2.52 and
29.3 are right, but **the units must be the binary denominations the syllabus names** -
the syllabus uses kibibyte and mebibyte and never MB or KB, so the right number against
the wrong symbol is still off-syllabus. The same finding originally called the 1024 rule a
recent amendment; it has been in the syllabus since version 1, so there is no period in
which the decimal answers were defensible.

Q273 originally said a plotter cannot proof a full-size colour poster. **That is false.**
Several large-format devices sold and commonly called plotters are raster colour inkjets.
The defect in the resource is the stated *reason* - precision line work - not the device
name, and a correction asserting the absolute form would replace a loose claim with a
wrong one.

The other four: E713 originally framed a qualification mix-up as a cross-board error, when
0417 and 9626 are both Cambridge; E729 originally cited "the specification's command set",
which is published in separate teaching guidance rather than in the specification; Q274
originally treated material as off-specification when the specification says students "may
use these techniques if they wish to", making it not-assessed rather than incorrect; and
E740's weighting is reported from the board's table because the board's own prose
contradicts it.

**U39 is the most important single entry for a corrector to read before starting.** It
records six places where correcting one qualification's resource by another's rule would
introduce an error. The sharpest: 9210 restricts efficiency to time, while 9645 *requires*
efficiency of time and memory and names space-wise efficiency expressly. Carrying the 9210
correction into a 9645 resource would manufacture a false positive.

**Three standing method notes, all learned the hard way.**

First, a command-word table introduced with the word *includes* is not exhaustive. Quote
the preamble and check the subject content before calling a word off-list.

Second, absence is established by reading the complete relevant section and the
appendices, not by a string search.

Third, and new this round: **presence must be established in the specific file a finding
is attached to, not anywhere in the corpus.** Six markers in this batch were byte-exact
quotations from the sibling file rather than the named one, and were caught by the
pre-flight gate rather than by the audit's own checking. Where a finding now names two
files, that is because the finding is about the two disagreeing.

## E712 - Eight marks and two note sections teach effects of computing on employment, which this syllabus does not contain [Confirmed error, High]

- **Files:** `ict-computer-systems-practice`
- **Where:** Questions 8 and 9
- **Locate by:** `**8.** Explain **two** effects that increased use of ICT has had on employment. **[4]**`

**What is wrong.** The topic on the effects of using information technology consists of exactly two subtopics: microprocessor-controlled devices in the home and in transport, and health problems from prolonged use. A practice file sets two questions worth eight marks on the effects on employment and on teleworking, and the revision notes of the same family carry sections on job losses and creation and on working patterns. Neither employment nor any working pattern appears anywhere in the syllabus. Note for correction, twice over: the syllabus carries a preamble saying its lists are not exhaustive and other related aspects should also be studied, so teaching employment effects is permitted; what is not defensible is presenting them as this topic's content or setting exam-style questions on them, because the assessment section says questions are set on the subject content and there is no mark-scheme anchor for them. The study guide of the same topic covers the two real subtopics correctly, so the family contradicts itself and the guide is right.

**Fix.** Replace the two questions and the two note sections with the named home, transport and health content.

## E722 - Four file-size answers use decimal division where the syllabus requires binary, making the stated marks wrong [Confirmed error, High]

- **Files:** `igcse-computer-science-data-representation`
- **Where:** File size calculations
- **Locate by:** `megabytes  = 1 440 000 / 1 000 000 (or / 1 048 576 for MiB)`

**What is wrong.** The syllabus states in the notes to its file-size outcome that answers must be given in the units specified and that calculations must use the measurement of 1024 and not 1000. Four worked answers across three files divide by a million or a thousand instead. Recomputed independently: an image of eight hundred by six hundred pixels at twenty-four bits gives 11,520,000 bits and 1,440,000 bytes, which is 1.37 on the binary convention and 1.44 on the decimal one; a thirty-second sound file at 44,100 hertz and sixteen bits gives 21,168,000 bits and 2,646,000 bytes, which is 2.52 rather than 2.65; an image of two hundred by one hundred and fifty pixels at eight bits gives 240,000 bits and 30,000 bytes, which is 29.3 rather than 30. Two notes for correction. First, the rule is not a recent amendment: it has been in this syllabus since its first version, so there is no period in which the decimal answers were right. Second, the units must be the binary denominations the syllabus names, not the decimal symbols; a correction that writes the right number against the wrong symbol is still off-syllabus. One file states the rule correctly eight lines above breaking it, and the two prose files of the other code do the same calculation correctly.

**Fix.** Recompute all four answers on the binary convention and label them with the syllabus's own denominations.

**Evidence.** Cambridge IGCSE Computer Science 0478 syllabus for 2026-2028, version 6, published September 2026 - https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697167-2026-2028-syllabus.pdf
