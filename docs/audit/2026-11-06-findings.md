## E926 - Two repairs added an explanation that contradicts the 9701 syllabus [Confirmed error, Medium]

- **Files:** named in the observation below (not in the frozen baseline snapshot)
- **Where:** a-reaction-kinetics-rate-equations-and-catalysis; a-transition-elements-properties-complexes-and-redox-chemistry; a-chemistry-transition-elements-revision-notes (main at 040e0bd)

**What is wrong.** (1) E103's repair removes the intermediate from the rate equation correctly but justifies it at a-reaction-kinetics-rate-equations-and-catalysis L158-160: 'outcome 26.1.2(a) restricts a rate equation to species that appear in the overall equation'. 9701 (2025-2027) 26.1.2(a) says only 'understand and use rate equations of the form rate = k [A]m[B]n (for which m and n are 0, 1 or 2)'. As a rule it is false: a catalyst is absent from the overall equation and can appear in the rate equation, and 26.1.5(d) asks candidates to identify an intermediate or catalyst from a mechanism. The audit's own finding paraphrased 26.1.2(a) loosely, which this wording follows; the finding's paraphrase is corrected here. (2) E112's repair states the NaOH observations correctly but explains them with 'OH- acts only as a base here, not as a ligand that displaces water' (a-transition-elements-properties-complexes-and-redox-chemistry L159-160) and 'since OH- is acting as a base rather than as a ligand' (a-chemistry-transition-elements-revision-notes L93). 9701 28.2.7: 'explain qualitatively that ligand exchange can occur, including the complexes of copper(II) ions and cobalt(II) ions with water and ammonia molecules and hydroxide and chloride ions'; 28.3.5 names 'hydroxide and chloride ions as examples of ligand exchange affecting the colour observed'. The notes' own ligand table lists OH- as a monodentate ligand.

**Fix.** (1) Remove the attribution to 26.1.2(a); say the intermediate is replaced because its concentration cannot be set or measured, using the fast pre-equilibrium. (2) Keep the observations; delete the 'not a ligand' reasons, or describe the reaction as the syllabus frames it.
