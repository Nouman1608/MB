# Marlbridge audit — what is missing from the site, asked directly

*Cycle-coverage sweep. I355 recorded that a findings record indexed by file answers "what is
wrong with this page?" and cannot answer "what is missing?" — and that both coverage gaps
found so far were found by accident. This asks the question on purpose.*

---

## E901: six resources on a syllabus whose last examination was 2025

All six resources this corpus holds for **Cambridge O Level Islamiyat 2058** declare the
series covering examinations in **2024 and 2025**. That window closed before the present
examination year.

The board has published **two cycles since**, and its own syllabus page lists all three:

| cycle | status |
|---|---|
| 2024–2025 | what all six resources declare |
| 2026–2027 | published September 2023 |
| **2028** | cover reads *"Use this syllabus for exams in 2028"*; version 2, April 2026 |

The qualification is **not withdrawn** and the code has not changed — this is not a retired
subject the corpus is right to leave behind. (A neighbouring code, 2068 Islamic Studies, *is*
restricted — "The last June series for this syllabus will be 2026" — but that is a different
qualification.) **Nothing else in the corpus covers 2058.**

And unlike the environmental-management case in I355, **these six files carry no note at
all** telling a reader which edition they follow or that newer ones exist. The resources
simply describe a syllabus that no longer applies.

Verified against the board's own syllabus page and syllabus PDFs — not against the resources,
and not against the screen that flagged them.

## The more useful half: the screen was wrong twice before it was right

Both times toward a large false alarm, and both caught before anything was published.

**Rule one** took the largest year in a series string as the last examination year. That is
backwards for most of this corpus: *"For first teaching 2015"* is an open-ended start, not an
expiry, and so are *"2015-onwards"*, *"From 2014"* and *"first assessment 2018"*. It flagged
**110 of 161 codes**, including AQA 7408 Physics — a qualification plainly still running.

**Rule two**, after that was corrected, read a bare four-digit year as a single examination
year. That is true of one board and false of another. It flagged **Edexcel 9UR0 Urdu as eight
years stale**; the board's own qualification page shows the specification is current and 2018
is its *first-teaching* year. The correct rule is board-dependent — Cambridge numbers a
syllabus by the year it is examined, Pearson by the year it is first taught — which is not
something the string can tell you.

Both were caught because **every flag was checked against the board before anything was
claimed from it**. That was the rule the script was written with, stated in its own docstring,
rather than a check applied afterwards. Of the two flags actually verified in this round, one
was a real error and one was a false positive — a 50% rate on a two-item sample, which is why
the other 21 rows above are presented as a register to work rather than as findings.

## E901 - Six Islamiyat resources are built on a syllabus whose last examination was 2025, and the board has since published two further cycles [Confirmed error, High]

- **Files:** `o-level-islamiyat-paper-1`, `o-level-islamiyat-paper-2-practice`
- **Where:** Declared syllabus series in all six resources of the code
- **Locate by:** `title: "O Level Islamiyat: Paper 1 (Cambridge 2058)"`

**What is wrong.** Every one of the six resources this corpus holds for one O Level code declares the series covering examinations in 2024 and 2025. That window closed before the present examination year. The board has published two cycles since: one covering examinations in 2026 and 2027, and a further one for 2028 which its own cover instructs candidates to use and which reached a second version in April 2026. The qualification has not been withdrawn and the code has not changed, so this is not a retired subject that the corpus is right to leave behind; it is a live qualification whose resources stopped two cycles ago. Nothing else in the corpus covers this code. Unlike the environmental-management case recorded separately, these six files carry no note telling the reader which edition they follow or that a newer one exists, so a candidate has nothing to alert them: the resources simply describe a syllabus that no longer applies, and the papers, topics and content they set out may or may not match what the candidate will sit. Note for correction: this was established from the board own syllabus page and syllabus documents, not from the resources and not from the screen that flagged them. The same screen flagged a Pearson code as eight years stale on the strength of a bare year in its series field, and the board own qualification page shows that specification is current, so the flag was wrong and the check is what separated the two.

**Fix.** Retarget all six files to the cycle the candidate will actually sit, or, if that cannot be done at once, state on every one of them which series it follows and that newer cycles exist, as the environmental-management files already do. Establish from the board which cycle a candidate reading the site today is most likely to be entered for before choosing between the two.

**Evidence.** Cambridge O Level Islamiyat 2058 syllabus for examination in 2026 and 2027, the cycle immediately after the one these resources declare - https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697279-2026-2027-syllabus.pdf

## Q367 - A hundred and thirty-nine resources across twenty-two codes sit on examination windows that close this year, and the screen that found them was wrong twice before it was right [Questionable claim, High]

- **Files:** `o-level-islamiyat-paper-1`, `o-level-cambridge-environmental-management-rocks-and-minerals`
- **Where:** Declared syllabus series across the whole corpus
- **Locate by:** `title: "O Level Islamiyat: Paper 1 (Cambridge 2058)"`

**What is wrong.** The preceding finding and the environmental-management one were both found by accident, while looking for something else, and both are absences rather than defects, which a findings record indexed by file cannot surface. The corpus was therefore swept directly for the question. Grouping all resources by board and syllabus code gives a hundred and sixty-one codes. Twenty-two of them, all of one board, hold every one of their resources on a closed examination window that ends in the present year or earlier: one ended last year and is the confirmed error recorded alongside this, and the other twenty-one end this year and cover a hundred and thirty-three files. Those twenty-one are not wrong today. They become wrong on a known date, they have no companions on the following cycle, and the two cases already examined show what happens when that date passes without action: in one the files were relabelled and no companion was published, and in the other nothing was done at all. This is therefore a register of scheduled staleness rather than a list of errors, and it is the first thing this audit has produced that says when a problem will arrive rather than that it has arrived. Note for correction, and it is the more useful half. The screen was wrong twice before it was right, both times in the direction of a large false alarm. Its first rule took the largest year in a series string as the last examination year, which is backwards for most of this corpus, because a specification described as being for first teaching in a given year is open-ended rather than expiring; that rule flagged a hundred and ten of the hundred and sixty-one codes, including current qualifications of three boards. Its second rule, after that was corrected, read a bare four-digit year as a single examination year, which is true of one board and false of another, and it flagged a Pearson code as eight years out of date when the board own qualification page shows the specification is current and the year in question is its first teaching year. Both were caught because every flag was checked against the board before anything was claimed from it, which is the rule the script was written with rather than one applied afterwards.

**Fix.** Publish the register with its dates and work the twenty-one codes before their windows close rather than after. Keep the rule that a flag is a document to open: this screen has now been wrong in two distinct ways and right once, and the checking is what distinguished them.
