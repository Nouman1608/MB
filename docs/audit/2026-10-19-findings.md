## E903 - A practice answer says the propanone and hydrogen cyanide product has a chiral centre and forms a racemate; that product is achiral [Confirmed error, High]

- **Files:** `as-chem-carbonyls-practice`
- **Where:** as-chem-carbonyls-practice, question 3 stem and answer 3(c)
- **Locate by:** `title: "AS Chemistry: Aldehydes and Ketones — Practice Questions"`

**What is wrong.** Introduced by the repair of E88. Question 3 opens: the product in question 2 is optically inactive despite containing a chiral centre. Answer 2(b) names that product as 2-hydroxy-2-methylpropanenitrile, and answer 3(c) says the planar carbonyl carbon in propanone is attacked from either face, giving the racemic product. The central carbon carries two methyl groups, a hydroxyl and a nitrile, so it has no four different groups and there is no chiral centre and no racemate. The question teaches the concept on a molecule it does not apply to; it holds for an unsymmetrical carbonyl such as ethanal or butanone.

**Fix.** Set question 2 on an aldehyde or unsymmetrical ketone, for example ethanal giving 2-hydroxypropanenitrile, so that question 3 and its answer are true.

## E906 - Weight and the normal contact force are given as a Newton's third law pair [Confirmed error, High]

- **Files:** `edexcel-igcse-physics-forces-and-motion`
- **Where:** edexcel-igcse-physics-forces-and-motion, the third-law paragraph added for E207 (about lines 128 to 132)
- **Locate by:** `title: "Forces and Motion"`

**What is wrong.** Introduced by the repair of E207. The paragraph states the law correctly, forces on different objects, and then gives as its example that your weight pushes down on the ground and the ground pushes up on you with an equal and opposite normal contact force, calling these the two forces of a pair. Weight is the Earth's gravitational pull on you; its third-law partner is your pull on the Earth. The normal contact force on you is paired with your push on the ground. Weight and the normal force both act on you and are equal only in equilibrium, which is the first law. This is the textbook misconception the example should prevent.

**Fix.** Pair your push on the ground with the ground's push on you, and the Earth's pull on you with your pull on the Earth.

## E907 - Centrioles are said to give rise to microvilli [Confirmed error, High]

- **Files:** `a-level-biology-cell-structure`
- **Where:** a-level-biology-cell-structure, the organelle section added for E252 (about lines 210 to 213)
- **Locate by:** `title: "A Level Biology: Cell Structure (Cambridge 9700)"`

**What is wrong.** Introduced by the repair of E252. The added text says centrioles organise spindle fibres and give rise to cilia and microvilli. Centrioles, as basal bodies, give rise to cilia and flagella, which have a microtubule core. Microvilli are extensions of the cell surface membrane supported by actin microfilaments and do not arise from centrioles. A candidate taking this into the 9700 comparison of cilia and microvilli would give a false origin for one of them.

**Fix.** Say that centrioles form the basal bodies of cilia; describe microvilli separately as membrane folds supported by actin.
