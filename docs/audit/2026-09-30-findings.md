# Marlbridge audit - the last screened group, read through

*Correction audit, round six. No resources were read and no specification was opened. Both
findings come from reading the audit's own corrections one at a time, which is now the only
method this workstream uses.*

The correction audit screened open confirmed errors for seven signatures. Four of the
resulting groups have been read through: section structure (27), terminology (15),
over-deletion (24) and, here, enumeration (45). **The enumeration group was the last one
unread. Every screened group has now been read.**

## Q359: two unsafe corrections in forty-five

Enumeration fires when a correction hands a corrector a count or a closed list. The
confirmed cases behind it are real - a correction supplying four stages of massive-star
evolution where the outcome enumerates none, and one listing seven market-failure areas
where the syllabus phrases six.

| what the reading found | count |
|---|---|
| hand over a count the observation already took from the source | 34 |
| **unsafe, found here** | **2** |
| already on record as unsafe (one of the six section-structure corrections) | 1 |
| misdescribed: a closed list that is empirical, not published | 1 |
| under-specified: a count with no contents (see Q360) | 4 |
| screen misfires - not about enumeration at all | 3 |

**The two unsafe ones.**

- **E276** tells a corrector to *"list the five sub-topics the topic actually contains."*
  Nothing in its own observation establishes that the topic has five. The observation
  proves only that digestion belongs to the next topic. **The count is the audit's, not the
  specification's** - and the defect being corrected is a list of the wrong length.
- **E442** tells a corrector to remove a writing form from three files because it is not
  among the six the specification names. Its own observation records that the same form is
  a named writing type in **another board's syllabus audited alongside it**. The correction
  names no code. Applied across the corpus it deletes a required form from that board's
  resources.

E442 is the **second confirmed instance** of the cross-board hazard first proved on energy
stores. It was not predicted; it was found by reading.

**The misdescribed one.** E520 rebuilds two practice papers on *"the five tariffs the board
uses"*. Its observation derives them from nine live papers. The specification enumerates no
tariffs at all. An empirically observed set is good evidence and is not a published closed
list, and a correction should say which of the two it is handing over.

**The two models of the group,** worth copying:

- **E210** closes a list and says *why it may be closed*: the specification enumerates the
  prefixes as a requirement rather than as examples.
- **E786** does the opposite and is equally right: it names the three categories the
  syllabus specifies and then instructs that the examples beneath them be presented as
  **non-exhaustive**, because the guide says its own table is neither prescriptive nor
  exhaustive.

**Already well handled.** E789 carries three notes for correction, states that the sibling
code's list is open because it is introduced with *including*, says expressly that the
correction **must not travel** there, and scopes itself to *"the two files of this code
only."* It is the best-scoped correction in the audit on this axis.

## Q360: a count without its contents

Four corrections state how many items a list should have and do not say what they are - a
plate name for each of three examples, twelve core studies, eight topic areas, thirteen
items across three lists. A fifth of the same shape came out of the terminology group. In
every case the finding's own reading retrieved the items and none reached the correction.

Not unsafe. But a corrector must re-open the specification to redo work already done, and
one who fills the gap from memory reintroduces the error the finding was raised about.

## Q359 - Reading the enumeration group through leaves two unsafe corrections out of forty-five, and one closed list that is empirical rather than published [Questionable claim, High]

- **Files:** `igcse-oxfordaqa-biology-organisation`, `edexcel-igcse-english-language-revision-notes`, `aqa-gcse-sociology-families-education-practice`
- **Where:** Corrections flagged for handing over a count or a closed list
- **Locate by:** `title: "OxfordAQA IGCSE Biology: Organisation (9201)"`

**What is wrong.** The last unread signature group of the correction audit is the one that fires when a correction hands over a count or a closed list, and forty-five open confirmed errors carry it after reconciliation against the decision log. All forty-five have now been read individually. Three are not about enumeration at all, the screen having fired on an ordinary number in corrections about square roots, a mark tariff and a pair of dehydration temperatures. One is already on record as unsafe, being one of the six section-structure corrections that offer dropping headings their own observations establish as real, and nothing in this reading changes it. Thirty-four hand over a count the finding's own observation has already taken from the specification, in most cases naming the items one by one, and those are sound; the model of the group is the one on unit prefixes, which says why the list can be closed at all, namely that the specification enumerates the prefixes as a requirement rather than as examples, and the second model is the one on text types, which states the three categories the syllabus specifies and then expressly instructs that the examples beneath them be presented as non-exhaustive. Two corrections are unsafe. The first tells a corrector to list the five sub-topics a topic contains, when nothing in its own observation establishes that the topic has five; the observation shows only that digestion belongs to the following topic, so the count is the audit's own and not the specification's, and a corrector who trusts it will publish a list of the wrong length in a file whose defect is a list of the wrong length. The second tells a corrector to remove a writing form from three files because it is not among the six the specification names, and its own observation records that the same form is a named writing type in another board's syllabus audited alongside it; the correction names no code, so applied across the corpus it deletes a required form from the other board's resources. That is the cross-board shape already recorded against energy stores, and it is the second confirmed instance of it. One further correction is not unsafe but is misdescribed: it rebuilds two practice papers on five tariffs described as the ones the board uses, where the observation derives them from nine live papers and the specification enumerates no tariffs at all. An empirically observed set is good evidence and is not a published closed list, and a correction should say which of the two it is handing over. Note for correction: establish the sub-topic count from the specification before applying the first, and apply the writing-form removal only to files of the code the finding examined.

**Fix.** Establish the sub-topic count from the specification before applying the first correction, scope the writing-form removal to the code the finding examined, and restate the tariff set as observed across nine live papers rather than as published by the board.

## Q360 - Five corrections state a count and withhold its contents, which leaves a corrector to supply the list the finding was raised about [Questionable claim, Medium]

- **Files:** `igcse-geography-earthquakes-and-volcanoes`, `a-level-psychology-approaches-issues-and-debates`, `ib-dp-global-politics-peace-conflict-revision-notes`
- **Where:** Corrections that give a number without the items behind it
- **Locate by:** `title: "Earthquakes and Volcanoes: Plate Boundaries, Hazards and Case Studies"`

**What is wrong.** Reading the enumeration group also exposed a defect of a different kind, one that is not unsafe but makes a correction unusable without repeating the work behind it. Four corrections tell a corrector how many items a list should have and do not say what the items are. One instructs that the correct subducting plate be named for each of three examples and names none of the three plates, its own observation having deliberately described them without naming them. One instructs that the twelve compulsory core studies be listed grouped by approach and names none of the twelve, although the syllabus lists them and the groupings are themselves assessed. One instructs that an extension be described against eight named topic areas and that they be listed, and lists none. One instructs that four types of conflict, three named resolution terms and six named causes be used, and supplies none of the thirteen items. A fifth of the same shape was recorded in the terminology group, where a correction instructs that a heading be renamed and does not say to what. In every case the finding's own reading of the source retrieved the items and none of them reached the correction, so a corrector must re-open the specification to redo work the audit has already done. The risk is not that the correction is wrong but that a corrector who fills the gap from memory reintroduces the error the finding was raised about, which is how a count becomes wrong in the first place. Note for correction: these five should carry their lists before they are worked, and the general rule is that a correction which states a count must also state its contents.

**Fix.** Add the named items to each of the five corrections, and treat a correction that states a count without its contents as incomplete rather than as ready to apply.
