## E912 - A practice paper for the first OCR GCSE Business component examines break-even and added value, which are not in that component [Confirmed error, Medium]

- **Files:** `ocr-gcse-business-activity-practice`
- **Where:** ocr-gcse-business-activity-practice, Section B questions 20 and 22
- **Locate by:** `title: "OCR GCSE Business: Business Activity, Marketing and People — Practice Questions"`

**What is wrong.** The paper is tagged to J204/01, Business 1: business activity, marketing and people, whose content is topics 1 to 3. Question 20 [3] asks for a definition of added value and a way to increase it, and question 22 asks for the added value per loaf and the daily profit [3] and the break-even output [2]. Break-even is 5.4, in Business 2: operations, finance and influences on business. The phrase added value, and the word value, do not occur in the J204 specification. The specification places synoptic assessment only in Business 2, so Business 1 does not draw on topic 5. Eight marks of the practice paper therefore test content the component it is labelled for does not examine, with no label. The arithmetic itself is right.

**Fix.** Replace questions 20 and 22 with topic 1 to 3 content, or label them as Business 2 content.

## E913 - A 9702 practice question requires two pieces of Big Bang evidence the syllabus does not contain, while the same file labels its other beyond-syllabus items [Confirmed error, Low]

- **Files:** `a-physics-astronomy-cosmology-practice`
- **Where:** a-physics-astronomy-cosmology-practice, question 6(c) and its answer
- **Locate by:** `title: "A Level Physics: Astronomy and Cosmology — Practice Questions"`

**What is wrong.** Question 6(c) [4] asks for two further pieces of evidence for the Big Bang, and the scheme credits cosmic microwave background radiation and the relative abundance of hydrogen and helium. Syllabus 25.3 for 2025 to 2027 contains four outcomes: redshift of spectral lines, the redshift equation, why redshift implies an expanding Universe, and Hubble's law leading to the Big Bang theory. The microwave background and nucleosynthesis appear nowhere in section 25; the only occurrences of microwaves are in the superposition outcomes. The same file marks question 5(c) and question 8(a) as background beyond the 9702 outcomes, so 6(c) reads as syllabus content by contrast.

**Fix.** Label 6(c) as background beyond 9702, or replace it with a question on 25.3.

## I360 - Urdu themes two and three call the specification's topics sub-topics under invented numbers, and a practice question awards marks for classifying a sentence by them [Inconsistency, Low]

- **Files:** `igcse-oxfordaqa-urdu-local-national-international`, `igcse-oxfordaqa-urdu-local-national-international-revision-notes`
- **Where:** The 9264 Theme 2 and Theme 3 guide, notes and practice files; study-employment revision notes "Four sub-topics" and line 34; study-employment practice question 7 [3]
- **Locate by:** `title: "OxfordAQA International GCSE Urdu: Local, National, International and Global Areas of Interest (9264)"`

**What is wrong.** The 9264 specification, Version 1.1, gives Theme 3 as four topics (Topic 1: My studies, Topic 2: Life at school/college, Topic 3: Education post-16, Topic 4: Jobs, career choices and ambitions) and Theme 2 as four topics, with sub-topics shown as bullet points beneath some of them. The Theme 2 and 3 files call the four topics sub-topics and number them 2.1 to 2.4 and 3.1 to 3.4, which collides with the specification's own use of sub-topic for the bullets in Theme 2. The Theme 3 notes then say reading tasks test the distinction by asking which sub-topic a detail belongs to, and practice question 7 awards two of its three marks for saying a sentence belongs to "sub-topic 3.2" rather than 3.1. This is the pattern E215 recorded in physics. The Theme 1 file already uses topics and sub-topics correctly.

**Fix.** Use the specification's Topic 1 to 4 wording, drop the claim about what reading tasks ask, and rebuild question 7 on the comparative structure alone.

## Q393 - The 9610 guide measures genetic diversity by a method the specification does not list, and omits one it does [Questionable claim, Low]

- **Files:** `a-level-oxfordaqa-biology-diversity-of-living-organisms`
- **Where:** a-level-oxfordaqa-biology-diversity-of-living-organisms, the genetic diversity paragraph
- **Locate by:** `title: "OxfordAQA A-Level Biology: The Diversity of Living Organisms (9610)"`

**What is wrong.** The guide says genetic diversity can be measured as the proportion of polymorphic gene loci, or by comparing base or amino acid sequences. 9610 Version 5.1, 3.1.11.1, lists comparing the base sequence of DNA or mRNA, the frequency of specific base sequences or alleles in populations, and the amino acid sequence of encoded proteins. Polymorphic loci do not occur in the specification, and allele frequency, which the paired practice paper itself uses in answer 5(a), is missing from the guide. The biology is sound; the specification's list is not what the guide gives.

**Fix.** Give the three comparisons 3.1.11.1 lists, and mark polymorphic loci as background if kept.
