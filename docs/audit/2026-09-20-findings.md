# Marlbridge audit - absence re-verification round

*Follows the findings file of 19 September 2026. No resources were read in this round.*

## What this round did, and why it exists

The 19 September file opened with a warning about the audit's own method: a finding had
asserted that a paper has no lettered sections, recorded that absence as established by
reading rather than searching, and been wrong. The terms were on three pages of the guide.
The cause was that extracting a long PDF as linear text can **truncate silently** part of
the way through, so a complete search of the extracted text returns nothing while the
document itself contains the term throughout.

That warning applied to every absence finding in the audit - roughly three hundred of them -
and left them all provisional. This round begins retiring that provisionality. Two complete
specifications were loaded end to end, with page counts and per-block coordinates recorded,
and the eighteen absence findings resting on them were re-checked one by one.

**Seven findings are added, numbered U64-U67 and Q336-Q338. None of them is about a
resource. All seven are about the audit.**

## The two most important entries

**U64 measures the truncation defect instead of fearing it.** It bites on documents longer
than about fifty pages, and it bites hardest where the absent thing would live in a late
appendix or data table. On the ninety-eight page syllabus the extracted text passes the cap
at page fifty-three, which keeps the taught content and loses the assessment details, the
practical notes, the conventions appendix and the entire data section from page eighty. On
the fifty-nine page syllabus the whole subject content ends at page thirty-nine, so no cap
tested could have damaged eight of that document's nine findings. **Absence findings made
against short documents were never exposed.** This narrows the re-verification from three
hundred findings to the subset resting on long documents.

**U65 reports a second defect, which is worse, because document length does not protect
against it.** A two-column syllabus lays its lower-tier and higher-tier content side by
side. Extracted as linear text it emits both column headers before the body regardless of
which column the body occupies, so higher-tier content reads as lower-tier. This was proved
live, not hypothesised: on one page a whole sub-topic has an empty lower-tier column and
both its outcomes sit in the higher-tier column, and the extracted text reads header,
header, outcome. **Every tiered syllabus in the corpus is affected.** Tier must be resolved
by the horizontal position of the text block against the column rule, and the coordinate
recorded - not by reading order.

## The finding that should change how the other findings are used

**U66: the commonest failure was not a false finding but a false correction attached to a
true one.** Of eighteen re-checked, two were cleanly upheld with new evidence, five were
upheld only after their corrections were narrowed, one was overturned on its count, and
three could not be settled without the resource text. Five of eighteen carried a correction
that would have introduced a new error if applied as written - a compound called
off-syllabus that the syllabus names as a required example, a reagent called off-syllabus
that the syllabus names as a common alkali, a ligand the correction would exclude that is
expressly listed.

**Q338 names four such corrections and states the constraint each one needs.** The sharpest
is a page said to wrongly cover the extraction of a named metal: the syllabus describes that
extraction as electrolysis in its own words and requires the half-equations, so the defect
is topic placement, not subject matter. A corrector who deleted the material would be
removing required content.

**Q336 corrects one of the audit's own counts downward.** A finding states that six outcomes
require dot-and-cross diagrams; a complete read of all fifty-nine pages finds four. The other
two occurrences of the phrase are in the appendices and are not learning outcomes. The
finding's substantive half - that the diagrams are taught nowhere in the resources - stands
untouched.

**Q337 fixes two tier attributions by coordinate and flags that one was probably backwards.**
If the earlier finding named the copper compound as the lower-tier electrolysis example, it
has the tiers inverted, which would push content into a tier where those candidates are not
assessed.

**U67 lists three findings that cannot be closed** because they name a defect without naming
the thing that is defective - two assert a synthetic route is off-specification without
saying which route. The complete syllabus text is held with coordinates, so each closes
immediately once the missing detail is supplied.

## What is still outstanding

Two documents of eighty-one have been cleared. The remaining high-risk absence findings are
listed in `outputs/absence-triage.txt`, ranked by exposure. Until a finding's source document
has been read complete and its page count confirmed, **treat any absence finding in this
audit as provisional**, and check that the thing called absent is absent in the form the
correction asserts.

## Conventions

Each finding gives its files, the section concerned, and a single-line verbatim string to
locate it by. For these seven the located file is the resource the amended finding concerns;
the finding itself is about the audit's method, not about that file's content.

## Q336 - A count of outcomes requiring one diagram type is overstated by half [Questionable claim, Medium]

- **Files:** `ionic-covalent-and-metallic-bonding`, `ionic-covalent-metallic-bonding-revision-notes`
- **Where:** Dot-and-cross outcomes
- **Locate by:** `title: "Ionic, Covalent and Metallic Bonding: Revision Notes"`

**What is wrong.** An earlier finding of this audit states that six outcomes require dot-and-cross diagrams. A complete read of all fifty-nine pages finds four such outcomes, two in each tier of two sub-topics. The other two occurrences of the word in the document are in the presentation-of-data and conventions appendices and are not learning outcomes at all. The finding's substantive half, that the diagrams are taught nowhere in the resources, is unaffected and was never exposed to the defect; only its count is wrong.

**Fix.** Restate the count as four and keep the coverage claim.

## Q337 - Two tier attributions are now fixed by position and one is likely to have been backwards [Questionable claim, Medium]

- **Files:** `electrolysis-and-fuel-cells`, `ionic-covalent-and-metallic-bonding`
- **Where:** Tier attribution
- **Locate by:** `title: "Electrolysis and Fuel Cells"`

**What is wrong.** Two earlier findings concern which tier content belongs to. Both are now settled by horizontal position rather than reading order. In the bonding sub-topic the lower tier carries the formation of ionic bonds between elements of the first and seventh groups and the higher tier carries formation between metallic and non-metallic ions, the two sharing an identical vertical position in opposite columns. In the electrolysis sub-topic the lower tier names molten lead bromide, concentrated aqueous sodium chloride and dilute sulfuric acid, and aqueous copper sulfate is higher tier. If the earlier finding named the copper compound as the lower-tier example it has the tiers backwards, which would push content into a tier where those candidates are not assessed. Note for correction: the same sub-topic also requires inert electrodes of platinum or carbon, and the higher tier adds the copper-electrode case, so a correction must not flatten the two.

**Fix.** Set both attributions to the positions recorded and re-check any resource built on the earlier reading.

**Evidence.** Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620 syllabus for 2026-2028, version 2, August 2026, 59 pages - https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697205-2026-2028-syllabus.pdf

## Q338 - Four corrections would introduce errors of their own if applied as written [Questionable claim, Medium]

- **Files:** `alloys-and-extraction-of-metals`, `alcohols-and-carboxylic-acids`
- **Where:** Correction constraints
- **Locate by:** `title: "Alloys and Extraction of Metals"`

**What is wrong.** Four earlier findings are sound but carry corrections that would be wrong. One says an electrolysis page should not cover the extraction of a named metal; the syllabus describes that extraction as electrolysis in its own words and requires the electrode reactions and ionic half-equations, so the defect is that the material sits in the wrong topic, not that it is off-topic. One uses a term for a reaction that appears nowhere in the syllabus, which names the reaction differently, so a correction must not import the term as syllabus vocabulary nor call the reaction off-syllabus, since it is required in both tiers. One frames a preparation sub-topic as having four routes, where a fifth exists in the higher tier. And one asserts that a list omits two named items where the list can be parsed as four or five items depending on how one entry is split, so the claim is unfalsifiable until the parsing is stated.

**Fix.** Narrow all four corrections before they are applied.

**Evidence.** Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620 syllabus for 2026-2028, version 2, August 2026, 59 pages - https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697205-2026-2028-syllabus.pdf

## U64 - The truncation defect bites only on long documents, and the threshold is now measured [Unverified item, High]

- **Files:** `a-transition-elements-colour-stereoisomerism-and-stability-constants`
- **Where:** Absence-finding re-verification
- **Locate by:** `title: "Transition Elements: Colour, Stereoisomerism and Stability Constants"`

**What is wrong.** Re-verifying eighteen absence findings against two complete syllabuses establishes the shape of the defect. On a ninety-eight page document the extracted text passes one hundred thousand characters at page fifty-three, so a capped read keeps the taught content and loses the assessment details, the practical notes, the conventions appendix and the whole data section from page eighty onward. On a fifty-nine page document the entire subject content ends at page thirty-nine and every assessment detail by page fifty-three, so no cap tested could have damaged eight of that document's nine findings. The defect is therefore real but bounded: it threatens absence findings made against documents longer than about fifty pages, and it threatens them most where the absent thing would live in a late appendix or data table. Two of the nine findings against the long document turn on exactly that, one of them on a data table at page eighty-five. Findings made against short documents were never exposed. Note for correction: the page count itself must be established before relying on any absence finding, because the document proved longer than the audit assumed.

**Fix.** Re-check absence findings against documents over fifty pages, prioritising anything whose subject would sit in an appendix or data section.

## U66 - Half the re-verified findings were right about the defect and wrong about the remedy [Unverified item, High]

- **Locate by:** `title: "Carboxylic Acids and Acyl Chlorides"`

Of eighteen findings re-checked, two were cleanly upheld with new evidence, five were upheld only after their corrections were narrowed, one was overturned on its count, and three could not be settled without the resource text.

**Fix.** Before applying any absence finding, check that the thing it calls absent is absent in the form the correction asserts.
