families - International GCSE Mathematics 9260 (6 files), International AS/A-level
Mathematics 9660 (4 newly read of 5), International GCSE Psychology 9218 (5 newly read of

## READ THIS FIRST

**E822 is the most urgent finding in this round and should be fixed before anything else.**

Three biology resources describe OxfordAQA International GCSE Biology 9201 as split into a
core tier and a higher tier, name pacemakers, stents, valve replacement, artificial hearts,
blood groups, transplant rejection and mechanical ventilation as higher-tier-only, and
advise core-tier candidates to deprioritise them. **The qualification has no tiers.** Its
assessment section states that content from any part of the specification may be assessed
in either paper, and its awarding section gives a single nine-point scale with no tier
ranges. All of that content is required for every candidate. A sibling resource for the
same qualification correctly calls it linear and untiered. This is the only finding in the
round that actively steers students away from material they will be examined on.

**Three of the audit's own claims were wrong and are corrected in place.**

**Points of inflection are Extension-tier content in 9260, not Core.** An earlier draft
asserted the opposite. The entire calculus strand of that qualification - A13, A14 and A15
- sits in the Extension column with the Core column empty. Acting on the original claim
would have pushed calculus into Core-tier materials where none is assessed. The error was
reproducible by reading the specification PDF as linear text: extraction emits both column
headers before the body regardless of which column the body occupies, so an empty Core cell
reads as a Core entry. Every tier attribution in this round was resolved by position on the
page, not reading order.

**Milgram did run a variation in which the authority's status was manipulated** -
Experiment 13, in which an ordinarily dressed man takes over the ordering role and
obedience falls to a fifth. An earlier draft asserted that no such variation exists and
that the teaching point derives entirely from a different researcher. It does not.

**Zimbardo and minority influence are not named in 9685, but they are not off-specification
either.** Every bullet of the relevant sub-topic is introduced with the word *including*,
so none is a closed list. The defensible finding is that the topics are unnamed, not that
they are excluded.

**U55 is the entry to read before applying any correction.** It records nine places where
correcting one code, tier or unit by another's rule would introduce an error - including
the two mathematics codes differing on both calculators and points of inflection, and the
two psychology codes using *anonymity* and *unanimity* in otherwise parallel lists.

## On the corpus sweep

All 1,251 resources were compared line by line for text shared verbatim across exam-board
boundaries, which is the fingerprint of one resource having been built from another board's
file. 88 file groups share such text. Most is harmless - two boards teaching the same
subject will word it similarly. Three groups share scope-bearing text, and **two of those
three turned out to be genuine coincidence**: the accounting pair share closed lists that
both specifications carry identically, and the economics pair share a section numbering
that both specifications genuinely use. Those two are recorded here as clean.

The real cases are D08's two families. The detection method under-reports: the mathematics
case was caught through shared *task* text rather than shared *scope* text, so other scope
problems may be sitting in task-shaped lines and will need reading rather than matching.

## E822 - Three resources invent a tier split for an untiered qualification and tell students to deprioritise assessable content [Confirmed error, High]

- **Files:** `igcse-oxfordaqa-biology-bioenergetics`, `oxfordaqa-igcse-biology-bioenergetics-revision-notes`
- **Where:** Tier framing
- **Locate by:** `marked B (content examined only at the higher tier).`

**What is wrong.** Three resources describe this qualification as split into a core tier and a higher tier, name several sub-topics as higher-tier-only, and advise core-tier candidates to deprioritise them at the expense of compulsory content. The qualification has no tiers. Its assessment section states that content from any part of the specification may be assessed in either paper, its awarding section gives a single nine-point grade scale with no tier ranges, and the word tier appears nowhere in the document; the board's mathematics qualification, which is genuinely tiered, defines its two tiers and their grade ranges expressly, so the contrast is visible within the same board. A sibling resource for the same qualification describes it correctly as linear and untiered, so the family contradicts itself. The content the resources tell students to skip - pacemakers, stents, valve replacement, artificial hearts, blood groups, transplant rejection and mechanical ventilation - is required for every candidate. Note for correction: the letter markers in the specification margins that the resources rely on are not tier markers. One of them sits on a whole sub-topic heading covering plant transport and on a required practical, content the resources themselves treat as ordinary, so no tier reading of them is coherent; what they do mean could not be established from the document and should not be guessed.

**Fix.** Delete the tier framing from all three files; the named content is assessable for every candidate.

**Evidence.** OxfordAQA International GCSE Biology (9201) specification, version 4.3 - https://www.oxfordaqa.com/oaqaresources/specifications/oxfordaqa-international-gcse-biology-specification.pdf
**Evidence.** OxfordAQA International GCSE Mathematics (9260) specification, version 5.1 - https://www.oxfordaqa.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/oxfordaqa-gcse-mathematics-specification.pdf

## E827 - Circles are taught as content of a unit that does not contain them, in three resources [Confirmed error, High]

- **Files:** `a-level-oxfordaqa-mathematics-unit-p1`, `oxfordaqa-a-level-mathematics-p1-revision-notes`

- **Locate by:** `Given the expanded form, complete the square in both x and y to recover the centre and radius. Two circle properties recur: the perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects it, and the tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.`

**What is wrong.** Three resources teach the equation of a circle, completing the square to recover centre and radius, and the circle properties as content of the first pure unit. And do not delete the material - every candidate for the first unit also sits the other unit, so the content is required; but the corpus currently has no resource for that unit's pure section at all, so deleting rather than relabelling would leave the topic with no coverage anywhere.

**Fix.** Relabel or migrate the material to the other AS unit's pure section rather than deleting it.

**Evidence.** OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Mathematics (9660) specification, version 5.2 - https://www.oxfordaqa.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/oxfordaqa-a-level-mathematics-specification.pdf

## E823 - A trigonometry answer is wrong because an intermediate value was rounded before the inverse sine [Confirmed error, Medium]

- **Files:** `oxfordaqa-a-level-mathematics-p1-practice`
- **Where:** Question 6(c)
- **Locate by:** `**(c)** Sine rule: sin B ÷ 11 = sin 52° ÷ 8.75 [1]; sin B = 0.991 [1]; B = **82.3°** [1].`

**What is wrong.** The stated values are a sine of 0.991 and an angle of 82.3 degrees. Recomputing independently by both routes: the cosine rule gives the third side as 8.7546319, so the sine is 8.6681183 divided by that, which is 0.9901180, or 0.990 to three significant figures, and the angle is 81.93844 degrees. The cosine rule applied directly to the angle gives 81.93844 as well, and the three angles then sum to 180 exactly. The stated angle is the inverse sine of the stated rounded value, so this is one upstream rounding error rather than two independent ones, and the board's own guidance elsewhere tells students not to round during intermediate steps.

**Fix.** Give the sine as 0.990 and the angle as 81.9 degrees.

**Evidence.** OxfordAQA International GCSE Mathematics (9260) specification, version 5.1 - https://www.oxfordaqa.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/oxfordaqa-gcse-mathematics-specification.pdf

## E829 - Two resources teach techniques for cases the specification says will not be set [Confirmed error, Medium]

- **Files:** `oxfordaqa-a-level-mathematics-p1-revision-notes`
- **Where:** Integration and differentiation
- **Locate by:** `**Area:** the integral gives a **signed** value, so regions below the x-axis integrate negative. For a total area, **split at the roots and take absolute values** — simply adding the signed integrals gives the wrong answer.`

**What is wrong.** The resources teach splitting an area at the roots and taking absolute values, and assert that the inconclusive second-derivative case appears in the examination. The specification states that questions involving regions partially above and below the axis will not be set, and that questions will not be set requiring the determination of or knowledge of points of inflection. Two notes for correction. Both exclusions sit inside the sub-topics of this unit and scope to it, so neither may be cited as authority over a resource for a later unit. And the second-derivative point is a judgement rather than a clean hit: the specification does require second-order derivatives and their application to maxima and minima, so teaching that a zero second derivative is inconclusive is defensible; only naming points of inflection is excluded.

**Fix.** Keep the wholly-below-the-axis case, mark the split-area technique as background, and drop the claim that the inconclusive case appears.

## E830 - A self-test answer gives a false reason and misstates which bases two expressions have [Confirmed error, Medium]

- **Files:** `oxfordaqa-igcse-mathematics-algebra-manipulation-revision-notes`
- **Where:** Self-test
- **Locate by:** `4. Why can't x²y³ and x³y² be combined by adding exponents?`

**What is wrong.** The answer says two expressions cannot have their exponents added because their bases are arranged differently. Both expressions have the same two bases, and multiplying them does add the exponents of each matching base, giving a fifth power of each; checking numerically at two and three gives 108 times 72, which is 7776, and that is two to the fifth times three to the fifth. What cannot be done is adding them as like terms, because like terms must have identical powers of every variable. The question as written also contradicts the index-law outcome it is meant to test.

**Fix.** Ask why the two cannot be added as like terms, and note that multiplying them does add the exponents.

**Evidence.** OxfordAQA International GCSE Mathematics (9260) specification, version 5.1 - https://www.oxfordaqa.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/oxfordaqa-gcse-mathematics-specification.pdf

## E836 - Three resources set a learning objective they never supply the material for [Confirmed error, Medium]

- **Files:** `igcse-oxfordaqa-psychology-conformity-and-obedience`, `igcse-oxfordaqa-psychology-conformity-obedience-revision-notes`
- **Where:** Conformity
- **Locate by:** `- Learn the basic procedure and one key finding of Asch's conformity`

**What is wrong.** All three resources of this topic tell students to learn the procedure and a key finding of the named conformity study, and none supplies either: across the three files there is no line-judgement task, no confederates, no group size, no conformity rate and no date, and the only question on it asks the student to name the researcher for one mark. The treatment is asymmetric, since the obedience study's procedure is described with its figure. Two notes for correction. The figures are a mean of about thirty-seven per cent conformity on critical trials with about three-quarters conforming at least once, and the group-size effect levelling off at three opponents; the number of confederates was condition-specific rather than fixed. And the standard account is contested: one well-known critique is a content analysis of textbook accounts rather than a re-analysis of the original data, and the best-known failed replication had a second condition, with probationers and probation officers, in which conformity returned to the original levels. Quoting only the failed half misleads.

**Fix.** Supply the procedure and the figures, and characterise the critiques accurately.

**Evidence.** OxfordAQA International GCSE Psychology (9218) specification, version 1.1, teaching from September 2023, exams May/June 2025 onwards - https://www.oxfordaqa.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/oxfordaqa-gcse-psychology-specification.pdf
**Evidence.** Asch, Studies of independence and conformity, Psychological Monographs 70(9), 1956 - https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2011-16966-001

## D08 - Two resource families were built from another board's files and carry that board's scope [Duplicate / overlapping scope, Medium]
