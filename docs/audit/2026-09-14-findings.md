Batch 26: 53 resources read in full, covering three complete subjects across every board
and code that carries them - Law (18 files, AQA 7162, Cambridge 9084, Pearson Edexcel
YLA1), Islamiyat (17 files, Cambridge 0493 and 2058, OxfordAQA 9237) and Pakistan Studies
(18 files, Cambridge 0448 and 2059, OxfordAQA 9236).

92 findings: 41 confirmed errors, 17 inconsistencies, 24 questionable claims, 9 unverified
items, 1 duplicate or overlapping scope. Every file was read end to end, every tariffed
question was re-summed by hand, and every board document named below was opened directly
rather than through search results. An adversarial pass was then run against the
highest-value claims with instructions to refute them; it overturned or weakened five,
and those corrections are carried inside the findings themselves rather than left in a
separate note.

Audit state after this batch: 1,038 of 1,251 baseline resources fully read, 213 remaining,
1,298 grouped findings, 653 currently open. All nine validation steps pass on the
regenerated artefacts.

## READ THIS FIRST

**Five of the audit's own claims were wrong and are corrected in place. One of them
matters more than anything else in this file.**

An earlier draft of E677 proposed correcting a commencement date to 29 March 2014. The
correct date is **13 March 2014** - the date the commencement order brought section 1 of
the Act into force. 29 March is when the first ceremonies could be solemnised, a
consequence of the fifteen-day notice period. The finding as written now gives the right
date, but acting on the earlier phrasing would have replaced one wrong date with another.

The other four: E675 originally said the specification recognises no set of four elements
of actus reus, when it has exactly four bullets and the fault is *which* four; E676
originally asserted that reversing is not an avoidance technique at all, which contradicts
a published syllabus and is withdrawn, leaving the finding scoped to one board only; Q262
originally proposed correcting an institution's founding date, and is downgraded to a note
because 1875 and 1877 are both defensible and neither board specifies one; and E705
originally asserted that certain mark tariffs do not occur on a paper, which the live paper
disproves.

**U38 is the most important single entry for a corrector to read before starting.** It
records six places in this batch where correcting one board's resource by another board's
rule would introduce an error - including a hill station that two syllabuses misspell, so a
resource using the correct spelling must be left alone.

**Two standing method notes, both learned the hard way in earlier rounds.**

First, a command-word table introduced with the word *includes* is not exhaustive. Before
any finding asserts that a command word is off-list, quote the table's own preamble and
check whether the specification's subject content uses the word elsewhere. Two findings in
this batch are classed as questionable rather than confirmed for exactly that reason, and a
finding in the previous round had to be downgraded after the same mistake.

Second, absence is established by reading the complete relevant section and the appendices,
not by a string search. Where absence could not be established positively, the finding says
so.

## E671 - A practice paper teaches three civil tracks where there have been four since October 2023 [Confirmed error, High]

- **Files:** `law-english-legal-system-practice`
- **Where:** Question 3
- **Locate by:** `Explain the three tracks in the civil courts`

**What is wrong.** The question asks for the three tracks in the civil courts and the answer gives small claims up to ten thousand, fast track from ten to twenty-five thousand, and multi-track above twenty-five thousand. The intermediate track was created with effect from the first of October 2023 and is now the normal track for a claim that suits neither the small claims nor the fast track; the multi-track is residual. Note for correction: the intermediate track is not simply a band from twenty-five thousand to one hundred thousand. The rule also requires a trial of no more than three days, no more than two experts a party, and a limited configuration of parties, and several classes of claim go to the multi-track whatever their value. The sibling study guide of another board in this batch already states the four tracks correctly.

**Fix.** State four tracks and describe the intermediate track by its conditions rather than by a bare monetary band.

**Evidence.** Ministry of Justice, Civil Procedure Rules, Part 26, as amended by SI 2023/572 in force 1 October 2023 - https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1998/3132/part/26

## E675 - Three resources render the specification's actus reus content by dropping two of its elements and promoting a third [Confirmed error, High]

- **Files:** `aqa-a-level-law-criminal-liability-practice`
- **Where:** Question 2 and the actus reus section
- **Locate by:** `**2.** Name the four categories of actus reus recognised by the specification. **[4]**`

**What is wrong.** The specification's actus reus content is four bullets: conduct, with acts and omissions and states of affairs; voluntariness and involuntariness; causation; and consequences. Three resources give the content as acts, omissions, states of affairs and causation. That splits the first bullet into three, drops voluntariness and involuntariness and drops consequences, and raises causation from its own bullet into the list of what actus reus consists of. A question asking candidates to name the categories the specification recognises cannot be answered correctly from the specification. Note for correction: the fault is in which four, not in the number. The specification does have four bullets, so a correction asserting that it recognises no set of four would be wrong in the opposite direction.

**Fix.** Quote the four bullets as the specification writes them and reword the question to ask for the elements it lists.

**Evidence.** AQA A-level Law 7162 specification, subject content, criminal law - https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/law/a-level/law-7162/specification/subject-content/criminal-law
