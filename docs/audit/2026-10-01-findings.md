# Marlbridge audit - testing the caveat instead of leaving it standing

*Absence re-verification, round four. Two specifications pulled, both put through the
terminal-marker test before either was searched. One passed and settles a claim; one failed
and settles nothing, which is the point.*

The over-deletion read-through cleared 24 corrections on how they were built and then
recorded a caveat: several of them rest on **absence claims against Cambridge 9701**, the
syllabus where silent truncation was *proven* at pages 50-53. Publishing that caveat and
leaving it there would have been the easy move. This round tests two of the claims.

## Q361: the CFC claim holds - caveat lifted, for that one finding

E79 asserts that *ozone*, *CFCs* and *chlorofluorocarbons* return no matches anywhere in
9701. That is exactly the sentence shape a truncated read produces, which is why it was
named in the caveat.

The extraction was confirmed complete first — changes-to-this-syllabus page, Cambridge
Assessment address block and closing copyright line all reached, 177,151 characters.

| term | result |
|---|---|
| ozone | **absent** |
| CFC | **absent** |
| chlorofluorocarbon | **absent** |
| stratosphere | **absent** |
| free radical | present x2 |
| free-radical substitution | present x4 |

The control terms matter as much as the absences: *free-radical substitution* appears in the
alkane, halogenoalkane and mechanism outcomes, so the retrieved text demonstrably covers the
part of the document where CFC chemistry would sit. **E79 is sound and its correction is safe
on its own premise.**

That settles one claim, not the caveat. The other findings named in it are untested.

## U71: the biology syllabus could not be read complete

E250 asserts that *fractionation*, *homogenisation*, *centrifugation*, *artefact* and
*endosymbiosis* return no match anywhere in 9700. The extraction reached **124,817
characters and stopped mid-word** inside the mathematical-requirements appendix, in the
middle of the key to a diversity index. **It fails the terminal-marker test.**

All five terms are absent from what was retrieved, and the retrieved text clearly reaches the
right part of the document — the eyepiece-graticule and stage-micrometer outcomes appear
three times, the outcomes on viruses as non-cellular structures appear as well. Two of those
are among the three replacements E250 proposes, so **the replacements are confirmed to be
real outcomes** even though the absence is not confirmed.

It would be easy to argue that a mathematical appendix could not have held subject-content
terms, and therefore that the claim is safe. **That is precisely the step the terminal-marker
test exists to forbid**, and it is how a finding was produced and then overturned earlier in
this audit. So: **supported, and not established.**

## Why this round was worth running

The second document failed at 124,817 characters. A completeness threshold based on length
would have passed it — the one document proven complete in an earlier round was *smaller*, at
106,651. The test that caught it looks at the document's own closing matter and nothing else.

Two documents pulled, one absence claim settled, one recorded as unsettleable from the
available text. That is the yield rate this workstream was demoted on, and it is the honest
one.

## Q361 - The chlorofluorocarbon absence claim is re-established against a complete reading of the chemistry syllabus, and the caveat over it is lifted [Questionable claim, Medium]

- **Files:** `as-chem-halogenoalkanes-practice`
- **Where:** Question 6 with answer, and the closing guidance
- **Locate by:** `title: "AS Chemistry: Halogenoalkanes — Practice Questions"`

**What is wrong.** The over-deletion read-through cleared a group of corrections on the construction of their instructions while recording that several of them rest on absence claims against the AS and A Level chemistry syllabus of this board, which is the document where silent truncation was proven at pages fifty to fifty-three. The finding on chlorofluorocarbons was named in that caveat as the clearest example of the shape a truncated read produces, because it asserts that three terms return no matches anywhere. That claim has now been tested against an extraction confirmed complete by the terminal-marker test, the changes-to-this-syllabus page, the Cambridge Assessment address block and the closing copyright line all having been reached. Across the whole document ozone, chlorofluorocarbon, the abbreviation for it and stratosphere are all genuinely absent, while free radical and free-radical substitution are present four times over in the organic-chemistry and halogenoalkane outcomes, which shows the retrieved text covers the part of the document where the missing terms would sit if they were there at all. The finding is therefore sound and the correction attached to it is safe to apply on its own premise. Note for correction: this settles one of the claims behind the caveat, not the caveat itself, since the other findings named there have not been tested and remain unestablished.

**Fix.** Treat this finding as re-established and apply its correction. Leave the caveat in force for the other absence claims of the same group until each has been tested the same way.

## U71 - The biology syllabus could not be read complete, so the cell-fractionation absence claim remains unestablished although everything retrieved supports it [Unverified item, Medium]

- **Files:** `a-level-biology-cell-structure-revision-notes`, `a-level-biology-cell-structure-practice`
- **Where:** Notes cell-fractionation, artefacts and endosymbiosis sections; practice questions 3, 6 and 7
- **Locate by:** `title: "A Level Biology: Cell Structure — Revision Notes"`

**What is wrong.** The second claim tested in this round is the one asserting that fractionation, homogenisation, centrifugation, artefact and endosymbiosis return no match anywhere in the AS and A Level biology syllabus of this board. The extraction obtained for that document reached one hundred and twenty-four thousand eight hundred and seventeen characters and then stopped mid-word inside the mathematical-requirements appendix, in the middle of the key to the symbols of a diversity index. It therefore fails the terminal-marker test: the changes-to-this-syllabus page and the address block appear earlier in the document but the closing matter was never reached. All five terms are absent from what was retrieved, and the retrieved text demonstrably covers the relevant part of the document, since the cell-structure outcomes on the eyepiece graticule and the stage micrometer are present three times over and the outcomes on viruses as non-cellular structures are present as well. Those two are among the three replacements the correction proposes, so the replacements are confirmed to be real outcomes of this syllabus even though the absence is not confirmed. The honest position is that the finding is strongly supported and not established, and reasoning that the missing appendix could not have contained subject-content terms is exactly the step the terminal-marker test exists to forbid. Note for correction: the replacement outcomes the correction names may be relied on; the absence claim may not, until the document is obtained complete.

**Fix.** Obtain the syllabus complete, by another route if necessary, and re-test the five terms against it. Until then record the finding as supported but unestablished, and rely on the three named replacement outcomes, which are confirmed.

**Evidence.** Cambridge International AS and A Level Biology 9700 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027 - extraction FAILED the terminal-marker test, stopping mid-word inside the mathematical-requirements appendix - https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/664560-2025-2027-syllabus.pdf
