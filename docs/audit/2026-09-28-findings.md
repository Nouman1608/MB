# Marlbridge audit - the tier-blind repair does not work mechanically

*Correction audit, round four. No resources were read. This round produced no repairs — it
established that a repair I proposed last round would have caused damage.*

## What I proposed, and why it was wrong

Q354 said 59 tier-blind corrections needed only one thing: the tier carried down from the
finding's own observation into its fix. Mechanical, cheap, no source documents. It also held
back nine as unsafe twice over.

I generated those annotations and inspected them before publishing. **They were wrong**, for
two separate reasons.

### 1. Lexical collision — countable

The vocabulary that detects a tier is ordinary subject vocabulary elsewhere in the same
corpus:

| flagged as a tier | what it actually was |
|---|---|
| `extended` | **"extended response questions"** — a question type on nearly every paper |
| `extended` | **"extended writing questions"** |
| `core` | **"the argon core plus 3d5 4s1"** — an electron configuration |

**16 of the 68 (24%) contain no genuine tier word at all.** They are not tier findings in any
sense.

**Three of those sixteen were among the nine I held as unsafe twice over.** So that number is
**six, not nine** — E373, E494 and E520 come out. The six that remain are the chemistry
outcome on the two-column syllabus, a physics coverage claim, a computer-science coverage
claim, a history revision omission, a history practice-paper rebuild, and a mathematics aims
list.

### 2. The premise itself — not countable, and worse

Even where the tier word is genuine, **a tier appearing in an observation does not make the
correction tier-specific.**

E343 mentions the International AS and A-level numbering while establishing that a coverage
list is misnumbered. The repair is to renumber the list. That is not AS-specific in any way.
Annotating that fix with a tier would **add a restriction the finding never claimed** — a new
error, dressed as a repair.

This is exactly the mechanical-edit failure the section-heading pull request made two rounds
ago, and that I flagged there. I then proposed the same shape of thing myself.

`outputs/tier-carry-proposals.txt` is **marked VOID** and must not be applied. It's retained
only as the record of a method that was tried and rejected.

## Where this leaves the numbers

| claim | as published | corrected |
|---|---|---|
| genuinely tier-blind | 68 | ~51 |
| unsafe twice over, held | 9 | **6** |
| mechanically repairable | 59 | **0** |

The remaining tier-blind corrections need an individual read each. There is no pass that
does them.

## The pattern, stated plainly

This is the **fourth** screening defect found in this workstream, and the first where I
proposed *acting* on the output rather than just counting it:

1. `\bAS\b` case-insensitive, matching the word "as" — inflated a group from 89 to 301
2. a snapshot trusted as current without reading its commit — produced Q348, corrected by Q350
3. tier vocabulary too narrow — 21% false positives, Q355
4. tier vocabulary colliding with subject vocabulary — 24% false positives, and a premise that
   didn't hold at all

All four were caught by inspecting the output, none by the screens themselves. The common
cause is now clear enough to state as a rule: **a regex screen over a corpus whose domain
vocabulary overlaps the screen's own vocabulary cannot be trusted without item-level
inspection.** Every count this workstream has published rests on such screens.
