# Marlbridge audit - a stale snapshot, and a check on the open repair

*Correcting yesterday's correction, and reviewing PR #47 before it merges.*

## Q350: the file evidence was three days out of date

Q348 corrected an earlier count and made a point of it — file evidence, not narrative.

**The file evidence was a local directory called `repository-live` whose commit I never
checked.** It predates `d71bcda` of 11 September, which had already repaired two of the six
files Q348 lists as damaged. The diff described a repository that hadn't existed for three
days.

Reading those two files from `main` settles it. Both carry `## Section A — Short-Answer
Questions` and `## Section B — Extended-Response Questions`, a resource booklet naming a
fictional company, an explicit statement of which component the paper represents, and the
15-mark evaluative question the finding they close asked for. **Restoring the baseline text
over either would have stripped the resource booklet and component identification back out.**

A second defect in the same finding is interpretive. The diff detected the absence of the
word "Section", and I called that *losing a heading*. Six of the eleven had been **relabelled**
— to `Part One`/`Part Two` or `Question 1`–`Question 4` — not stripped. Four of those six have
since been checked against their specifications: **Cambridge 7115, Cambridge 0450 and Edexcel
4EC1 have no lettered sections at all**, so the relabelling was correct and restoring "Section
A/B" would have reintroduced exactly the invented structure this cleanup exists to remove.

Of the six files this audit called damaged, **only the two GCSE business papers were both
genuinely stripped and genuinely in need of restoration.**

This is the third correction in four days to a claim made at a width its evidence didn't
support, and the second one I've made myself while auditing others for it. The lesson is
narrow and practical: **record the commit a snapshot holds before using it as evidence, and
don't let a directory's name stand in for that.**

## Q351: PR #47 before merge

Most of it is right.

- **The two OCR J204 files are sound.** Headings genuinely deleted, now restored, with the
  mismatch between the restored Section A and the 15 marks of multiple choice it should
  contain flagged in the PR rather than papered over. That is the right handling.
- **The new Section C is better than sound.** It goes before the existing 20-mark evaluation
  question in the Edexcel file, which is exactly where the specification puts its single
  20-mark essay.

**One change should not merge as written.** On two files the PR restores Section A and B
headings whose specifications define those sections *by their source material* — and neither
file has any:

| | what the spec says the section is | what the PR puts under the heading |
|---|---|---|
| Cambridge 2281 | Section A: **one** compulsory 30-mark question on previously unseen source material; Section B: 20-mark questions each introduced by stimulus material | Section A over three short-answer questions totalling 9 marks |
| Edexcel WBS12 | Section A: questions **based on sources**; Section B: same format, **different sources**; Section C: essay based on sources | Section A over two questions worth 6 marks between them |

The heading asserts the one thing that isn't there. The PR's own analysis said this before the
change was made — that pasting the headings onto the current structure "would misrepresent it
the same way the original damage did", and that this needed an editorial decision rather than
a mechanical restore. The analysis was right.

The already-repaired OCR H431 papers show what a complete fix looks like: they gained a
resource booklet and a statement of which component they represent, along with their headings.

**Recommended:** merge the two GCSE business files and the new Section C; hold the four
Section A/B headings on the other two files until each has the source material its section
requires.
