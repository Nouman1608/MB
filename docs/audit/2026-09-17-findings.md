# Marlbridge audit - findings of 17 September 2026

Batch 29: 48 resources read in full, covering four Cambridge applied subjects, each as a
paired IGCSE and O Level or A Level code - Statistics 0479 and 4040 (12 files),
Environmental Management 0680 and 5014 (12), Commerce 0715 and 7100 (12), and Global

72 findings: 35 confirmed errors, 14 inconsistencies, 17 questionable claims, 5 unverified
items, 1 duplicate or overlapping scope. Every file was read end to end, every tariffed
question was re-summed by hand, every calculation was recomputed from first principles,
and every board document named below was opened directly rather than through search
results. Three adversarial passes were then run against the highest-value claims with
instructions to refute them; they overturned or rescoped twelve, and those corrections are
carried inside the findings themselves rather than left in a separate note.

Audit state after this batch: 1,170 of 1,251 baseline resources fully read, 81 remaining,
1,512 grouped findings, 763 currently open. All nine validation steps pass on the
regenerated artefacts.

## READ THIS FIRST

**Four of the audit's own proposed corrections were wrong. Two of them would have removed
content the syllabus requires. Read these before editing anything.**

**Q302 - do not carry the equal-class-widths rule across grouped data.** The syllabus
attaches "Groups will have equal class widths" to the *frequency polygon* outcome only.
The neighbouring histogram outcome anticipates the opposite, requiring the vertical axis
to be labelled frequency density when widths are unequal. A teacher told that grouped-data
questions use equal widths would drop unequal-width histogram practice, which is required
content. This was the audit's own over-generalisation, caught on the adversarial pass.

**Q308 - the insurance requirement has four parts, not three.** A rebuild covering
purposes, types of risk, and pooling/premiums/documents would still omit *the role of an
insurance broker*, which the syllabus names. Correcting to the audit's original three-part
list would reproduce a gap.

**E800 - do not assert that granite has no mantle derivation.** Granitic magma can arise
by differentiation of mantle-derived melt; the accurate correction is that it does not come
*directly* from mantle melt. Separately, the syllabus never uses the phrase "magma from the
mantle", so this finding must not be framed as a mismatch with the syllabus - the defect is
the resource's own.

**U51 - 7100 is not withdrawn.** The board's notice is headed "Withdrawal" but describes a
*zone restriction*: three zones for 2026 and 2027, one zone only from 2028. The code
continues. A correction stating that 7100 is withdrawn would be false.

**U50 is the entry to read before applying any correction in this batch.** It records
eleven places where correcting one code or series by another's rule would introduce an
error. The sharpest: the two Environmental Management series have different paper
structures, assessment-objective splits, ore concept, extraction list and biofuel
itemisation; and the second and third assessment objectives mean different things in
Commerce and in Global Perspectives, so an objective correction must not travel between
them.

**Three standing method notes.**

First, a list introduced with *includes*, *such as* or *e.g.* is not exhaustive; one
introduced with *limited to* is closed. Several findings here turn on that distinction, and
two were rescoped when the adversarial pass found the syllabus says neither.

Second, absence is established by reading the complete relevant section and the appendices,
never by a string search.

Third, presence must be established in the specific file a finding is attached to. Where a
finding below names two files, that is because the finding is about the two disagreeing.

## E813 - Personal liability is said to fall at every step of the ownership progression [Confirmed error, High]

- **Files:** `commerce-commercial-operations-revision-notes`
- **Where:** Forms of business ownership
- **Locate by:** `access to more capital and less personal liability. Also know: cooperative, public limited company,`

**What is wrong.** The file states that each step from sole trader through partnership to private limited company trades control for more capital and less personal liability. The first step does not reduce personal liability at all: a general partnership carries unlimited liability, and because that liability is joint, and in many jurisdictions joint and several, a partner's exposure can exceed a sole trader's. Liability falls only at incorporation, when the company becomes a separate legal person. The claim also contradicts the file's own self-test answer, which drops the liability clause, and the correct comparison table in a sibling file. Note for correction: another file's version of the same sentence is hedged and describes the whole progression rather than each step, and does not need the same edit.

**Fix.** State that control is traded for capital at each step and that liability falls only at incorporation.

## E799 - Metamorphic rocks are said never to contain fossils, on the one such rock the syllabus requires [Confirmed error, Medium]

- **Files:** `o-level-environmental-management-rocks-revision-notes`, `o-level-cambridge-environmental-management-rocks-and-minerals`
- **Where:** Rock types
- **Locate by:** `**Sedimentary rocks may contain fossils; igneous and metamorphic rocks do not**, because the heat involved would destroy them.`

**What is wrong.** Two files state absolutely that igneous and metamorphic rocks do not contain fossils, and one builds a self-test question on the premise. Low-grade metamorphic rocks do preserve fossils, and slate, which the syllabus names as one of only two required metamorphic rocks, is the standard case; one slate formation is a celebrated fossil site whose fauna includes preserved soft tissue. Three notes for correction. The word routinely overstates it: exceptional preservation in slate is rare and the fossils are typically flattened and distorted by cleavage, so the accurate form is that such rocks can and do preserve fossils. The igneous half of the original sentence is essentially sound, so a correction refuting the whole sentence would overreach. And the claim in the sibling file that sedimentary rocks alone contain fossils carries the same defect.

**Fix.** State that fossils are common in sedimentary rocks, usually destroyed by metamorphism but preserved in low-grade rocks such as slate, and absent from igneous rocks formed from melt.
