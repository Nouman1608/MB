## Q378 - A finding that a question misattributes a phrase to the specification is withdrawn: the specification uses that phrase in its own words [Questionable claim, High]

- **Files:** `oxfordaqa-igcse-computer-science-programming-practice`
- **Where:** E746, the six-mark question on the principles of imperative programming
- **Locate by:** `title: "OxfordAQA IGCSE Computer Science: Programming Fundamentals — Practice Questions (9210)"`

**What is wrong.** E746 recorded that a question asks for the three combining principles of imperative programming named in the specification, and that the specification does not name them by that phrase, so a candidate searching for it finds nothing. Read complete, the specification says in its own words that the three combining principles, sequence, iteration or repetition and selection or choice, are basic to all imperative programming languages. The question attributed the phrase correctly and the finding is withdrawn. It was produced by the failure the terminal-marker test exists for, a negative search over text not established as complete, and it is the first overturn in this round of eight documents, close to the rate the handoff forecast. The repair side has already reworded the stem under D-196, to the three basic constructs of imperative programming. That wording is also accurate, so the withdrawal asks for nothing to be reverted. Note for correction: do not reinstate or reverse either wording on the strength of this record.

**Fix.** Withdraw E746 and exclude it from the open count. No resource change.
