## Q442 - the evidence rule amended by the owner; D-378 recorded [Questionable claim, Low]

- **Files:** none (method and record)
- **Where:** the owner's instruction of 1 October 2026; D-378 (`9837c70`)

**What is wrong.** Nothing is wrong. Two changes to the audit's own footing are recorded here so that later rounds can see when they took effect.

**The evidence rule.** Until 1 October the rule was: authoritative sources only — open and read the official board PDF sections, and do not rely on search-result excerpts, unofficial revision sites, AI summaries or memory. The audit declined to use mirrored copies of IB documents on that basis. On 1 October the owner amended it: *"if you find any resources which are available on the official website and downloadable please add to the folder / if you do not find any official resource then use the ones in the folder."* The audit now (1) prefers the official document and downloads it where it is publicly available, adding it to the owner's reference folder; (2) where no official copy is obtainable, uses the folder copy whatever its provenance; and (3) states the evidence class on every finding — **[official]** or **[folder copy, provenance not established]**. The no-bypass rule is unchanged: where a board's site serves a bot challenge, the audit stops rather than working around it. This round downloaded thirteen official syllabuses (0479 2027; 0620, 0625, 2210, 5054, 5070 2026–2028; 2281, 7115 2026; 9609, 9708 2026–2028; 9618 2027–2029; 5014 both editions) and earlier in the day 0610 2026–2028 Version 3 and 9709 2026–2027 Version 4. It could not obtain the IB ESS subject brief or the IB DP Global Politics guide from ibo.org, which serves a Cloudflare challenge; those two sections are therefore marked [folder copy, provenance not established], as are the IB Maths AA/AI and Language A guides used in batches 2 and 3, which have no entry in the folder's own download log.

**D-378.** Made by this audit instance at the owner's request, not by the fixer, and so recorded here rather than verified independently. It reverts the `author` field on all 34 IB MYP Sciences resources from `ameer-hamza` to `marlbridge-academic-team` and moves `reviewer` from `hina-mogul` to `ameer-hamza` on the twelve biology-domain pages. Its gates passed on the pushed tree (build, `validate-review-integrity`, `audit:all` 0 problems across 3,081 pages, `astro check` 0 errors), and the rendered byline, the schema.org `Article` author and the "Reviewed by Ameer Hamza" line were read on the live pages. It closes E947.

**Fix.** None.

**Evidence.** Round 61 (2026-10-01, 14:40 PKT). The owner's instruction of 1 October, quoted above. D-378 (`9837c70`, 34 files + the decision log) read; tree identical to the tested commit. - https://github.com/Nouman1608/MB/compare/5dceb1f...9837c70

## E947 - 34 MYP Sciences resources named a person as author of pages Marlbridge wrote [Confirmed error, High] — CLOSED by D-378 in this round

- **Files:** `src/content/resources/ib-myp-sciences-*.md` (34)
- **Where:** `author:` frontmatter; the rendered byline; the schema.org `Article` `author` property

**What is wrong.** All 34 IB MYP Sciences resources carried `author: ameer-hamza`, so each page displayed "Author Ameer Hamza" and its `Article` schema named him as a `Person` author. Marlbridge wrote those pages on 28 September (D-375); he did not write them. This is the claim class D-134 rescinded and D-283 settled, and it contradicts the site's own Editorial & Trust Policy, which states that being credited as an author is a separate role from being a reviewer. The fixer recorded an objection in D-376 and the owner had overridden it; on 1 October the owner reversed that decision. D-378 reverted the byline and credited him as reviewer on the twelve biology-domain pages instead, which rule [8] of `validate-review-integrity` permits now that he is not the author. E947 closes.

**Fix.** Applied in D-378.

**Evidence.** Round 61. The 34 files read at `5dceb1f` and again at `9837c70`; the rendered byline and the `Article` schema read on the built pages; D-375, D-376 and the Editorial & Trust Policy read.

## E948 - a practice question is marked 3 marks but its answer carries four marking points [Confirmed error, Low]

- **Files:** `src/content/resources/a-level-biology-cell-membranes-practice.md`
- **Where:** question 3 and its answer

**What is wrong.** The stem declares **[3]** and the answer awards four separate `[1]` marking points. A student marking their own work against it scores out of four on a three-mark question. This is the only genuine tariff mismatch in the whole new set: the sweep covered all 248 new practice files in batches 1–4 and a further 686 answer blocks in batch 5 (480 in the Cambridge and Edexcel tail, 192 in IB ESS, 14 in 5014), and every other declared tariff equals the sum of its award markers once indicative lists ("any two of…", "1 mark each") and banded schemes are read as the boards' mark schemes read them.

**Fix.** Either raise the tariff to 4 or merge two of the four marking points. Both are defensible; the subject teacher should choose, because which two points merge is a content decision.

**Note for correction:** do not apply this as a corpus-wide rule. Answers that list more award markers than the tariff are *correct* wherever the answer says "any two of", "1 mark each", "maximum 2 marks" or sets out a band (for example the 2210 June 2025 eight-row truth table, marked four for eight correct outputs, three for six or seven, two for four or five, one for two or three). Those were all checked and are right. Only this one question has a flat list of four required points under a three-mark tariff.

**Evidence.** Round 61. The file read at `5dceb1f`; the tariff sweep run over all 934 answer blocks in the new practice files, every flag inspected by hand.

## I413 - 509 resources credit a reviewer whose own profile does not list that subject or that board [Inconsistency, Medium]

- **Files:** 509 resources under `src/content/resources/`; the teacher profiles under `src/content/teachers/`
- **Where:** `reviewer:` frontmatter against each profile's `subjectsTaught` and `boardsTaught`

**What is wrong.** 249 resources name a reviewer whose profile's `subjectsTaught` does not include the resource's subject — Lubna Waseem on 136 IB language resources, Ammar Bilal on 61 English resources, Sajawal Zahid on 17 Statistics resources, and the 34 MYP Sciences pages. 260 name a reviewer whose `boardsTaught` does not include the resource's board — Sajawal Zahid on 134 Cambridge resources, Hina Mogul on 84 AQA, Edexcel, OCR and OxfordAQA resources. The two sets overlap. One of the two statements is wrong in every case: either the person did not review that resource, or their profile understates what they teach. The site publishes both, so a reader can see the contradiction by clicking the reviewer's name. The audit cannot tell which half to correct; that is the owner's knowledge.

**Fix.** For each reviewer, decide whether the profile or the credits are wrong, then correct that side. If the profiles are right, the credits need reassigning to someone who teaches the subject and the board. A validator rule comparing `reviewer` against the profile's `subjectsTaught` and `boardsTaught` would stop this recurring; `validate-review-integrity` already holds rule [8] (reviewer ≠ author) and is the natural place for it.

**Evidence.** Round 61. Every resource's `reviewer` field compared with the named profile's `subjectsTaught` and `boardsTaught` at `5dceb1f`; counts recomputed after D-378.

## U79 - the 1,987 "Reviewed by" credits cannot be verified by this audit [Unverified item, Medium]

- **Files:** 1,987 resources carrying `reviewStatus: reviewed`
- **Where:** the rendered "Reviewed by" line; the Editorial & Trust Policy

**What is wrong.** 1,987 of the site's 2,311 resources display a named reviewer. The credits were applied across 28 and 29 September, up to 237 per reviewer in a single day. The Editorial & Trust Policy tells readers that review means a second person checked the content. Whether that happened rests entirely on the owner's statement of 28–29 September; nothing in the repository records a review event, and the audit has no way to test it. This is not an allegation that the reviews did not happen — it is a statement that the site makes a verifiable-sounding claim that cannot be verified from anything the site holds.

**Fix.** Two ways to close it. Either the Editorial & Trust Policy says plainly what "Reviewed by" means at Marlbridge — if it means a named teacher is accountable for the content rather than that they read it on a given date, say so — or the claim is narrowed to the resources where a review actually took place, with a date recorded per resource. The second also gives the fix for I413 something to work from.

**Evidence.** Round 61. The `reviewStatus` and `reviewer` fields counted across all 2,311 resources at `5dceb1f`; the commit history of the review sweep read; the Editorial & Trust Policy read as built.

## U80 - 21 IB ESS resources attribute subtopic numbering and learning outcomes to a document that does not contain them [Unverified item, Medium]

- **Files:** 21 of the 36 `src/content/resources/ib-dp-ess-*.md` — atmosphere-climate-change-ozone (3), biodiversity-conservation (3), ecosystems-energy-biogeochemical-cycles (1), foundation-perspectives-systems-sustainability (3), human-populations-urban-systems (3), natural-resources-energy-waste (2), soil-agriculture-food (3), water-systems-security-pollution (3)
- **Where:** the opening alignment sentence; the "What this unit covers" table; the "Official syllabus" block

**What is wrong.** Each of the 21 states that it is "aligned to the IB Diploma Programme Subject Brief, *Environmental systems and societies*" and then sets out numbered subtopics with learning outcomes — for example "covers syllabus sections 3.1 (biodiversity and evolution), 3.2 (human impact on biodiversity) and 3.3 (conservation and regeneration)", each row of the table carrying a "what you must be able to do" column. The brief is the only source the pages cite. It breaks Topic 1 into subtopics (1.1 Perspectives, 1.2 Systems, 1.3 Sustainability) and nothing below that: for Topics 2 to 8 it gives the topic name and the SL and HL teaching hours and stops. It contains no subtopic titles and no learning outcomes for those topics. The content is very likely right — it would come from the full ESS guide, which teachers have — but it cannot be checked against the document the pages name, and a reader who opens the brief to check will not find it.

What the pages get right is worth recording, because it is most of what they claim: every teaching-hours figure matches the brief (Topic 1 Foundation 16, the same at SL and HL; 2 Ecology 22/35; 3 Biodiversity and conservation 13/26; 4 Water 12/25; 5 Land 8/15; 6 Atmosphere and climate change 10/23; 7 Natural resources 10/18; 8 Human populations and urban systems 9/15), as do Topic 1's own 3/5/8 split, the three HL lenses (HL.a Environmental law 5 hours, HL.b Environmental and ecological economics 7, HL.c Environmental ethics 5, HL only), the quoted assessment objective 3 wording "political, economic, ethical and sociocultural contexts of issues", and the descriptions of Paper 1 (data on a previously unseen case study) and Paper 2 (Section A short-answer and data-based, Section B structured essays).

**Fix.** The three Topic 2 pages in the same set already handle this correctly and are the model: "It does not list the subtopics, so the two titles above follow the syllabus numbering used in the [printable ESS checklist]". Either copy that disclosure to the other 21, or cite the full ESS guide in the "Official syllabus" block instead of the brief — in which case the subtopic titles and outcomes should be read against the guide before the citation changes, not after.

**Note for correction:** do not delete the subtopic tables. The problem is the attribution, not the content.

**Evidence.** Round 61 [folder copy, provenance not established]. All 36 ESS resources read at `5dceb1f`; `IB DP Environmental Systems and Societies - subject brief.pdf` (first assessment 2026, © IBO 2023) read complete from the owner's reference folder, including the full recommended-teaching-hours table and the assessment-at-a-glance table. The official copy could not be downloaded: ibo.org serves a Cloudflare challenge, and the audit does not bypass one. Two cautions follow from that — the folder copy has no entry in the folder's own download log, and the IB published an updated ESS guide in November 2025, so the figures above are the 2023 brief's.

## What the round checked and found correct

Recorded because a round that reads 666 files and raises five findings should say what the other work established.

**Syllabus editions and topic names [official].** Every `syllabusSeries` claim in the new set was checked against the syllabus the board currently publishes: 0625, 0620, 5070, 5054, 2210, 9609, 9708 2026–2028; 2281, 7115 2026; 9618 2027–2029; 0580, 4024, 9701, 4040 2025–2027; 0479 2027; 5014 both editions. No page claims the wrong edition. Every page's `topic` field matches a topic or section title in the current syllabus (33 checked in batch 5 alone). The folder held only superseded editions for eight of these codes and nothing at all for 0479 or 5014, which is why the editions were downloaded during the round.

**Specification references [official].** 664 AQA spec references across 8461, 8462, 8463 and 8300; 164 Cambridge 9709 section references and 48 section names, against Version 4; 36 Edexcel 4BI1 and 4CH1 points, including the thirteen suffixed forms (4.3B, 4.4B, 4.11B, 4.18B, 5.9B, 5.17B, 3.5C, 3.14C, 3.19C, 4.29C, 4.34C, 4.38C, 4.48C) that a plain numeric check reports as missing; 87 IB Mathematics SL and AHL references [folder copy]. All valid. The 0625 Core/Supplement split was compared line by line with the 2026–2028 syllabus and is right in every row.

**Mark-scheme and examiner-report citations [official].** 143 "Mark-scheme insight" and "Examiner insight" paraphrases were each opened against the Cambridge document named — 0610 and 9700 June 2024 in batches 1 and 4 (56), and in batch 5 the 2210 June 2025 Paper 12 and 22 mark schemes (24), the 9708 June 2024 examiner report and Paper 42 mark scheme (19), 7115 June 2024 (15), 2281 June 2024 (15), 9609 June 2024 (9) and 9618 June 2025 Paper 31 (5). All accurate at the level of detail they claim. Where a page says a mark scheme is not on Cambridge's public past-papers page, that is a dated statement about the website, not about the document.

**Grade thresholds [official].** The eleven past-paper guides were compared row by row with Cambridge's own grade-threshold PDFs — 642 rows across 104 series — and the nine hubs in the new set were re-checked in batch 5: 635 threshold rows and 59 series component lists. Every option code, component combination, maximum mark and boundary matches. The 0610, 0620 and 1123 `P1` rows, which look wrong against a naive parse, are right: Cambridge's table prints the maximum before the component list and the site prints it after.

**Arithmetic and mark tariffs.** Roughly 650 worked expressions in batch 5 and its ESS section alone — orbital speeds, half-lives, relative atomic mass from isotope abundance, accounting rate of return and break-even, two's-complement floating point, moving averages and index numbers, titration and enthalpy calculations, quadrat estimates, Simpson's index — and 934 answer blocks across the new practice files. One error (E948); everything else correct.

**Position.** 1 confirmed-error group open (E948), with I413, I414, U79 and U80. Declared closures 881 (D-138 to D-284) remain read. This is not a certificate that every statement on the site is correct: U80's subtopic content and the 1,987 review credits in U79 are the two places where the audit has reached the limit of what the available documents can settle.
